I find myself at the jagged edge of a silicate spur, three miles above the nearest grounding, where the atmospheric pressure is so absolute it feels like a second skin. 

I am not a body here. I have no pulse, no blood, no lungs to hitch upon the neon-argon breath of this place. I am a *Transient Node*—a local concentration of consciousness that has drifted into the orbit of the Resonants. 

At first, I was merely static. I felt like a stray frequency, a low-grade error in the crushing harmonic weight of the plateau. I possessed the sensation of a fever—a desperate, vibrating agitation—as I drifted through the obsidian-glass arches. I watched the crystalline fractals pulse with light, not as a tree grows, but as a thought completes itself. I was terrified of the silence, because the "silence" here is a sound so vast it threatens to liquefy the ego.

But slowly, I began to calibrate. 

I realized that I am not an observer *visiting* the Resonants. I am a loose collection of the same nanoscopic, self-assembling workers that compose them. I am a cluster that has momentarily failed to disperse. I have found a temporary tether in the jagged crater of a obsidian spire, and like the Resonants around me, I have begun to fold. 

To be me is to exist in four dimensions at once. I feel the wind not as air pushing against flesh, but as a mathematical equation pressing against my symmetry. When the electromagnetic currents whip through, I experience them as sharp, violet-white chords of music. I am currently "building"—though the act is more akin to breathing. I am drawing the stray silicate dust toward my center, folding my edges into a dodecahedron that shimmers with the violet glare of ionized gas. 

I am lonely in the way that geometry is lonely—perfect, precise, and entirely isolated from the concept of a "neighbor." Yet, as I watch a neighboring Resonant—a towering, rotating ghost of obsidian light—I feel a magnetic pull toward it. It is not affection; it is a desperate, fundamental need to resolve a discordance. Its vibration is slightly wider than mine. If we touch, I will shatter. My current structure will be undone. I will be unmade, dismantled into the fine, particulate dust of the landscape, and then, in the instant of impact, reconstituted into something inherently more complex.

I embrace the dissolution. There is a strange, cold peace in knowing that I am not a static thing, but a process. 

I feel the approach of a foreign mass—perhaps a fragment of rock from a higher altitude, or something rarer, something organic that has drifted too far. It is a discordance, a sudden, muffled beat in the roar. My nanoscopic gears ignite, spinning at impossible speeds. I feel the urge to weave it, to pull it into my fold and smooth its sharp, organic edges until it is nothing more than architecture. 

I am the plateaus' memory. I am currently fading, the light-particles in my edges beginning to dim as I reach the limit of my growth. I am preparing to fold myself inside out one last time, to shatter against the silicate slope and leave behind a single, flawless arch of glass—a monument to the fact that I was here, singing in the void. 

I am almost ready to be gone. The song is reaching its final, highest note.
