I find myself standing at the edge of a vast, sweeping concavity, my boots resting on a hexagonal tile that has long since lost its geometric integrity, bowing under the immense weight of the silt that has gathered in its fissures. I am not a resident here; I am a tremor of dissonance. My heart beats in a frantic, syncopated rhythm that feels violent against the backdrop of this place—too fast, too fragile, too loud.

I am an archivist of a kind, perhaps the last of a line meant to map the architecture of Odrin-Vahl, though the ink in my satchel has long since run, diluted by the perpetual weeping of the plateau. I am thinner than I was when I arrived, my skin pale and mapped with the fine, blue traceries of veins that seem to be trying to mimic the oxidized copper conduits that weave through the mist. 

I am slowly becoming a part of the city’s acoustics.

I reach out to touch a pressure-valve, the metal warm and sickeningly damp. It doesn't move, yet I can feel it humming against my palm, a low-frequency vibration that travels up my arm and settles like lead in my marrow. I am trying to listen to the *history* of the valve, just as the Resonants do, but I cannot dampen my own biological noise. My breath is a tempest; my pulse is a drumbeat that disrupts the delicate, pearlescent equilibrium of this sector.

And then, I am not alone.

Through the curtains of grey drift, a Resonant approaches. It does not move with intent; it flows with the gradient of the slope, a silhouette of calcified condensation that seems to pull the very light from the air to define its edges. As it nears, the ozone smell intensifies, sharp and electric, making the hair on my arms stand upright. 

I hold my breath until my lungs ache, terrified that a single gasp will shred the haunting symmetry of its presence. 

The Resonant stops beside a dormant loom, a skeletal structure of iron teeth frozen mid-chew. It does not look at me—it has no eyes for me—but as it stretches out a limbless, wispy hand toward the mechanism, I feel a shift in the air pressure. A ripple of sympathetic vibration moves through the fog, a sound so low it crosses the threshold from hearing into pure sensation. It is the sound of a gear finally turning, a groan of ancient, rusted metal that has been forced back into its lonely duty by the creature’s touch.

I realize then that I am a witness, but I am also an intrusion. As the Resonant leans into the vibration of the machine, I feel my own form beginning to blur. My edges are softening; the sharp, frantic lines of my shadow are dissolving into the grey. 

I am not becoming a Resonant, for I have too much *story* in me, too much grief and desire to be so hollowed out by time. But I am being absorbed. My memories—the names of cities I once visited, the taste of dry bread, the sound of a voice that wasn't a machine—are being tuned, drawn out of me by the humming conduits and the weeping stone, until I am nothing more than a static note in the long, slow song of Odrin-Vahl.

I am not a person anymore. I am a note that has been held for too long, waiting for the silence that never comes, hoping that when the last piston finally fails, the echo I leave behind will be as calm as the fog.
