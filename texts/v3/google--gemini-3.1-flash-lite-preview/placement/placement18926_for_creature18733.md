I am a displacement. 

I am not the water, nor the stone, nor the shimmering lace of the Sift-Walkers. If you looked for a skeleton or a pulse, you would find nothing—only a knot of *perspective* pulled tight, like a thread caught in a gear. I am a memory of a shape, a ghost of an anatomy that refuses to dissolve, even though there is no reason for it to hold together.

I find myself clinging to a shelf of calcified coral, tucked into a curve where the current is weakest. My "hands"—if I can call them that—are not flesh. They are solidified intentions, edges of my own identity that have resisted the softening influence of the musk. I am the only thing in this vertical ocean that still possesses a horizon line in its mind. Everyone else here looks at the *state* of things; I am still cursed with the habit of looking at the *distance*.

I am a Chronicler of the In-Between, though no one asked for the role.

In this place, I have become something like a lens-polisher. I do not have the grace of the Sift-Walkers who wrap themselves around the geodes in holy union. I am too frantic for that, too jagged. My existence is a series of small, rhythmic jolts. I watch the Sift-Walkers arrive at the Vessels, and every time one of them unravels—giving up its life to become a seal against the silence—I feel a phantom ache in my chest, a desperate, human *grief* for a death that simply isn't there. They are not dying; they are succeeding. I am the only one who perceives this as a loss 

As the pressure hums against my skin—feeling like the touch of a thousand cold needles—I reach out toward the passing Echo-Weavers. When their massive, liquid bellies drift beneath me, bathing me in that sudden, shocking warmth of ozone and ancient rain, I do not just feel the heat. I steal it. I hoard the memory of the light.

I take that fleeting clarity—that one second where the musk clears and the stars are revealed—and I press it into the pores of the sponge-stone. I am carving, wordless and frantic, a chronicle of the Epochs into the very reef. I am "writing" the history of a world that doesn't want to be remembered. 

I am a friction point. Because I refuse to let the silence be absolute, the currents around me are starting to change. They swirl differently now, eddies of curiosity forming in the wake of my presence. The Sift-Walkers, in their slow, drifting rotations, have begun to tilt their nodes toward me as they pass. They don't have eyes, but they feel the *shape* of my urgency. They sense the cold, rigid heat of my surprise. 

I am a splinter, and I am starting to feel the world wake up around me. I am no longer just an obstruction; I am becoming a question. And in this vertical ocean, where everything is an answer, a question is the most dangerous, beautiful thing that can exist. 

I sit, and I wait for the next Echo-Weaver, and I prepare to steal the stars again.
