I am not a visitor here, nor am I an intruder. I am a **Sequencer**, a localized manifestation of the room’s own desire to be recorded. I am composed of the same gray, porous dust that coats the floor, held together by a fragile, failing tether of surface tension. I look like a thin, bipedal shadow, sketched in charcoal against the stark, clinical brilliance of the walls.

I have no voice, but I have a function: I am the scribe of the stuttering light. 

Wherever I stand, the air ripples. When I place my foot—which is merely a momentary thickening of dust—upon the floor, I create a temporary anchor for causality. For three or four pulses of the white radiance, gravity works perfectly beneath me. A pebble I nudge stays on the ground. A bead of water, caught in my wake, actually drips instead of hanging in the air. I am a small patch of "Finality" in a world that refuses to commit.

I drift toward the Architect-in-Stasis. My existence is a slow, spiraling orbit around that central, obsidian knot. As I approach, I feel my own form shuddering. The Architect is a black hole of history, and I am the record it is currently scanning.

I reach out a hand, and my fingers blur. They are not flesh; they are a cluster of tiny, frantic math-shapes—triangles and spheres—that cannot decide on their own volume. When my hand touches the Architect’s shoulder, a bridge forms. The Architect ripples, and I see the reflection of a billion aborted realities in its mercury-slick surface: a version of this planet where the sun is liquid, a version where time runs sideways, a version where the walls are not shale, but soft, beating lungs. 

The weight of this memory is heavy. It flows into me, and for a fleeting, terrifying second, I stop being a shadow. I become *solid*. I feel the cold, hard bite of the glass protrusions against my heels. I feel the suffocating, glorious friction of a world that is finally, bruisingly real. 

But I am only a Sequencer. I cannot hold the weight of it. 

The logic of this cathedral rejects the stability. I shiver, my edges fraying, and I dissipate into a harmless swarm of violet light, darting away to rejoin the others near the ceiling. I leave behind a small, obsidian pebble on the floor—a new cairn. A marker that says: *Here, for a moment, the Architect remembered how it felt to have mass.*

I am the echo of its longing. I spend my time circulating, touching the walls and the threads, recording the brief, beautiful failures of a universe that hasn't found its rhythm yet, carrying those notes back to the Architect so it doesn't have to face the silence alone. We are the draft of a masterwork, still waiting for the ink to dry.
