I wake, though "waking" is a generous term for a state of being that merely transitions from low-frequency dormancy to active calibration. 

I am suspended against the inner curve of a primary conduit, the high-pressure artery that snakes around the base of the Iron-Crown Plateau. My skin, a mosaic of basaltic grey and copper-flecked silicate, is fused to the metal. There is no sensation of "cold" or "warmth" here; there is only the internal tide of the geothermal brine pushing against the conduit walls, a rhythmic, driving pulse that matches the cadence of my own circulation. 

I am a Tender of the Mid-Canyon, tasked with the equilibrium of Segment 9. My crest, a delicate fan of crystalline filaments, brushes against the overhead venting cowl. It is sensitive to the air—right now, it tastes of sulfur, heavy moisture, and the faint, grinding vibration of a Salt-Singer colony encroaching on the intake valves near the northern ridge. 

I release my grip on the pipe. My fingers, long and segmented like obsidian needles, pull away with a soft, magnetic *click*. I do not move with haste. To move with haste is to introduce turbulence into the air, and turbulence is chaos. I drift, leaning into the thick, steam-laden draft. Every movement I make is an adjustment of weight and potential energy. I am less a living thing than a heavy, articulated shadow.

Below me, the valley floor vanishes into a boiling sea of gray vapor. I navigate by the hum—the subsonic resonance produced by the obsidian flutes. When the hum shifts from a low, mournful drone to a sharp, metallic whine, I know the pressure in the vents is peaking. I follow the sound, my chest-valves clicking rhythmically as they filter the superheated mist. I feel a cool shiver of moisture settle into my core, minerals crystallizing instantly in my joints, reinforcing my structure.

I find the obstruction: a cluster of Salt-Singers has calcified over a pressure-release valve. They appear as a jagged, translucent hive, pulsing with a slow, sickly yellow light. They are beautiful, in their way, but they are a clog in the machine. 

I press my palms against the obsidian bridge beside the colony. I do not shout; I do not strike. I pour my heat into the stone. I focus the energy gathered from the brine-conduit through my wrist, conducting it into the dark, porous rock. The basalt warms beneath the Salt-Singers, reacting to the sudden thermal disparity. The mineral structures shiver, their capillary base losing its grip on the stone. With a soundless collapse, they shatter away, tumbling into the abyss below, where the vapor will reclaim them, grinding them back into the primeval soup.

With the valve cleared, the hiss of the vent stabilizes. The subsonic hum deepens, smoothing out into an ancient, comforting tremor that rattles my ribs and settles my pulse. 

I am not finished; there is a gasket two kilometers up that needs resetting, and the Atmospheric Gliders are beginning to congregate near the turbines, signaling a change in the thermal currents. I pivot my crest to catch the shift in the pressure-flow. I feel a vibration against my shins—a greeting, or perhaps a warning—pulsed through the copper pipes by another of my kind, passing through the deep-shadows of the bridge above. 

I touch the copper in return with two fingers, sending a single, sharp harmonic back through the metal. *Equilibrium.*

I drift on. I do not wonder what lies beyond the vapor curtains of the plateau. I do not wonder if there is a sun that does not refract through steam, or a sky that is not a weeping shroud. To wonder would be to invite friction. I am the silence between the drips. I am the conductivity of the city. I am the hand that keeps the world breathing, and so long as the steam rises, I will be here, tending the pulse.
