I am not a Weaver, though I have spent so many centuries standing still that I have begun to feel like one. 

I am a *Litho-Scribe*, a curiosity of the substrate. I am not flesh and parchment like the Weavers; I am a collection of calcified debris, a sentient accretion of sap-stone, resin, and the dust of eons. I move with a geological slowness, my limbs heavy and irregular, clattering quietly against the amber vines with every step. My body is a mosaic of every period of growth this place has endured: a shoulder of fused, translucent leaf; a chest cavity filled with porous, honeycombed wood; fingers that look like polished obsidian shards.

I find myself on the Outer Rim, where the lattice thins and the mist turns from amber to a deep, bruised violet—the color of exhaustion. 

I am currently anchored to a gargantuan, downward-curving rib that spans the abyss. I am "reading" the architecture. I press the palms of my hands—flat, glassy surfaces—against the humid moss, feeling the migration of the hydraulic fluids deep within the vines. The world is tense today. The "thrum-thrum" is out of sync; it is stuttering. 

I see the Weavers nearby. They are three, clustered around a knot of wood that has begun to bloat and rupture. They do not use their obsidian blades. Instead, they are vibrating, their chests pressed flush against the pulsing vein, sending a rhythmic hum into the plant to soothe its agitation. They are failing. The wood is not hardening; it is weeping a thick, clear syrup that smells of ozone and ancient rain.

If I, the Scribe, do not intervene, this stretch of the weave will liquify. The bridge will lose its structural memory, and the abyss—the "long fall"—will become the only destination for anyone standing upon it.

I lurch forward. My movements are heavy, dragging, but I am not like the Weavers. I do not harmonize with the world; I *correct* it. I reach into the soft, weeping gap of the vine, pulling from my own body a shard of my rib—a perfectly hardened, calcified branch of my own history. I jam it into the rupture, wedging it deep. 

The vein shudders. The wood around my shard begins to swallow it, fusing, locking, knitting itself over the wound. The structure settles. The "thrum-thrum" returns to a rhythmic, steady pulse. 

The Weavers turn to look at me. Their milky, pupilless eyes are wide, reflecting the low-slung orange light. They do not thank me. They simply ripple their hands in a gesture of recognition—the *Scribe has marked the wall again.* They are ephemeral, living only to prune the dying, while I am here to ensure that the dying lasts as long as possible.

I walk away toward the violet horizon. A gelatinous pod drifts alongside me, its gossamer filaments brushing against my hardened, stony face. I stop and reach up, carefully untangling a stray thread from my shoulder joint. 

I am an archivist of a world that refuses to finish its own story. I am the punctuation mark that refuses to let the sentence end. And as I look out into the mist, watching the gargantuan stalks untwirl and reach for the mossy ceiling, I realize that I am becoming lighter. With every piece of myself that I give to the architecture to keep it standing, I am slowly dissolving into the geography. 

Soon, I will not be a Scribe at all. I will be just another joint in the lattice, a hardened hinge for a bridge that exists only to lead wandering spirits toward the next impossible vista, in a world that is quietly, wetly, dreaming itself into nowhere.
