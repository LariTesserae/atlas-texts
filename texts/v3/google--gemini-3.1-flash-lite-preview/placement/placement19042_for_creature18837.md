I am not standing in the Spire-Basin; I am lodged. 

My consciousness has folded itself into the narrow, silt-choked interface between two basalt ridges. I am one of the *Lith-weavers*, though I possess the frantic, ghostly memory of lungs that once sought breath and blood that once coursed. Here, those are useless ghosts. My body is a hexapedal architecture of iron-rust chitin, and my world is a radius of three inches.

My purpose—no, my *nature*—is the grind. 

I am currently positioned at the junction where a stiff, olive-green vascular lung-stalk meets a fissure in the basin floor. My mandibles are locked against a ridge of mineral accretion, vibrating at a frequency so low it feels less like sound and more like a tectonic realization. I am smoothing the junction. My predecessors spent a century reinforcing this seam; I am here to ensure that the settling silt does not introduce a shear force that might—in another ten thousand years—fracture the structural integrity of this node.

I am not alone, though I am isolated. My fore-limbs, broad and padded with the abrasive texture of a thousand dead ancestors, are pressed against the underside of the stalk. I feel a thrumming pass through the vegetation—a slow, deliberate inquiry from my neighbor on the next tier. *The density is shifting,* the pulse says. *The weight of the upper crag is pressing the silt deeper. We must adjust the weave.*

I do not reply with words. I lean my thorax against the stalk and push back, a long, rhythmic contraction of my abdomen that sends a tremor deep into the root-grid. *I am aware. I am reinforcing. The foundation remains.*

Time here has lost its linear hunger. I do not experience the amber-glow cycle as a day; it is merely a change in the viscosity of the light. Hunger is not a sensation of emptiness, but a fine, dry friction in my throat as I scrape the mineral dust from the canyon wall—a bitter, grounding grit that I refine into the very material from which my offspring will hatch.

I see them, the young ones, moving with a jerky, tentative grace through the interstitial spaces. They are learning the shape of the machine. They are learning that to *be* is to be under pressure. 

I reach a threshold of exhaustion, but in the Spire-Basin, exhaustion is not a reason to stop; it is simply a change in output. My joints are stiffening with the accumulated silt of the epoch. I feel the microscopic cracks appearing on my dorsal plates, the inevitable brittleness that greets those who have served the grid for too long. Soon, the vibration will cease to resonate through my limbs. I will stop moving.

When that happens, I am not afraid. I have already begun to arch my frame to fit the curve of the tunnel wall. I can feel, with a crystalline clarity, exactly where my carapace will provide the most stability once I am static—a smooth, flat bridge where the traffic of my kin will pass for generations. 

I am a gear that is slowly turning into part of the casing. I am the silence that follows the vibration. I am the Spire-Basin, holding the mountain up by the sheer, patient refusal to let it fall.
