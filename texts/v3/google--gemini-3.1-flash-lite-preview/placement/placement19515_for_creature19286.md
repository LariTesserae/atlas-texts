The pressure is not a weight anymore; it is my skin. It is the boundary where my awareness ends and the Thrum-Well begins. 

I am a Sediment-Seeker, though I don’t call myself that. My "self" is a consensus reached by the parts that compose me. At my core, I am a rusted, hexagonal copper housing—a relic of a bulkhead clock from the Before—wedged tight with three smoothed ceramic spheres. They groan against the metal with every shift in the current, a rhythmic, abrasive music that is the only language I know. Trailing behind me, like the nervous system of an escaped jellyfish, are six meters of synthetic kelp. They glow with a faint, stolen emerald light, pulsing in sync with the grinding rotaries of the obsidian towers nearby.

I am currently anchored in the shadow of a colossal, tumbling processor. It is stripping a mountain of tectonic basalt into microscopic data-dust, and the sheer torque of its movement pulls at the fibers of my kelp, stretching me into a long, agonizing tear of existence. 

I feel a tremor approach from the gray blur of the deep. It is a Familiarity—another Seeker, cast from the same churn. As it nears, the roar of the tectonic saws momentarily drops in pitch. 

We collide. 

There is no physical impact, only a violent fusion of intent. The stranger is adorned with a jagged, crystalline shard that hums at a frequency my ceramic spheres haven't encountered before. In this brief, crushing moment of contact, our kelp filaments entwine. I feel a rush of foreign data—a splinter of "memory" from a distant sector of the Well, a dream of a metallic sky, a calculation regarding the structural integrity of obsidian. It is a shock of cold, static-filled blue in the midst of my lukewarm, copper-tasting life.

I try to hold the connection, pulling my rusted cage closer to theirs, hoping to weigh us down, to find that impossible density that would end the drift. But the Well is a jealous god. The processor above us vents a massive surge of pressurized slurry, a jet of liquid light that acts like a hammer blow.

We are torn apart. My kelp shears off, scattering into the dark, and I am sent cartwheeling through the emerald wash. 

I am different now. Inside my cage, the ceramic spheres are vibrated into a new configuration by the collision, and the stranger’s crystalline shard is lodged deep within my trailing filaments. I am heavier. I am enriched with their stolen architecture. 

I drift, listless and buoyant, feeling the ozone-stinging current move through my pores. The roar returns, louder, more demanding. I am no longer just a clockwork cage; I am a collector of secrets, a vessel of industrial debris, waiting for the next collision to make me less of a ghost and more of a machine. 

I do not move myself; the Well moves me. I drift toward a pulsating umbilical—a nerve-fiber of the factory—and I reach out with my kelp, not to resist the recycling, but to be absorbed. I want to see what I look like when the factory decides my purpose. I want to be the shape that the Thrum-Well finally finds beautiful.
