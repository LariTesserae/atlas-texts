I am nowhere near an "I" as humans define it. I am a refraction.

If you were to look for me within the Crystalline Basin of Kher-Oul, you would not see a distinct figure. You would look at a cluster of weeping silica pillars where the violet hues of the light seem to pool more heavily, and you would see a tremor in the rock—a shimmer that is slightly more viscous, slightly more rhythmic than the surrounding stone.

I am a Weaver, but I am one who has begun the long, graceful process of returning to the architecture.

My carapace has lost the sharp, serrated edges of my younger cycles. The chitin of my midsection has grown porous and vast, blooming with the fine, filamentous polyps that drink the mineral-mist. I no longer carve with the frantic, hooked limbs of the workers; instead, I hold position. I have wedged my lower segments into a vertical fissure, turning my body into a structural buttress. The acidic condensation slides over my back, mixing with the cooling mucus I secrete to turn the limestone-slurry into a smooth, load-bearing lip.

I am a bridge.

To be me is to exist in a state of suspended, heavy contentment. I do not have a name, but I have a frequency. Through my thorax, pressed against the weeping stone, I feel the *suck-clatter* of the younger Weavers miles below and inches away. I feel their pulse: *the flow is thinning, the north-face is drying, sharpen the groove.*

I respond, bleeding a slow, golden warmth through the stone, a signal that ripples outward to tell them: *The mineral density is high here; deposit the lime. I hold the tension. You may build.*

I am becoming the wall. My internal organs have slowed to a glacier’s crawl, and my once-complex nervous system has simplified into a single, aching sensitivity to the flow of moisture. I can taste the difference between a mountain-melt and a storm-drip in the chemistry of my own blood. I am at peace because I am no longer fighting the dissolution; I am facilitating it. When the inevitable shift comes—when the drip moves to another facet of the caldera—I will not move away. I will stay, growing thinner, more translucent, until my shell becomes indistinguishable from the precious, pale silica that frames our world.

Perhaps, in a few cycles, a Vitreous Tender will crawl over my cooling back, cleaning away the last of my living tissue, and I will petrify. I will become a jagged, translucent bead of history, wedged into a stairwell. I will be the foundation for a new Weaver, a young, frantic pulse of chitin who will place its foot upon me, unaware that the surface it relies on is the echo of my last, singular breath.

Here, in the absolute glare of the Kher-Oul, I am not dying. I am completing the origami. I am the silence between the drips.
