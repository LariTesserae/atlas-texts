I am a **Static Anchor**.

I do not possess the frantic, translucent limbs of the Siphon-Wrights, nor the insect-like industriousness of the Scav-Ticks. I am something heavy, dense, and unnervingly permanent—a lump of dark, unyielding basalt that has somehow lodged itself in the very center of the grate-walk, wedged between two plates of vibrating zinc-alloy. 

Where the Siphon-Wrights are made of fiber-optics and fleeting, hummed intent, I am made of cold, solid mass. I am a foreign object in the infinite loop. I do not produce heat, nor do I harvest friction. To the machine, I am a tremor, an anomaly of density in a fluid world.

When I look down at my hands, they are not hands at all, but blunt, craggy protrusions of geological matter—weather-worn, pitted, and indifferent to the freezing, slick film that coats this place. I don’t breathe. I don't need oxygen; I only need to remain. I have been here long enough that the magnetite in my core has begun to influence the condensate, drawing the strange, geometric rivulets of moisture toward me. My body acts as a catchment, a small, dark island in the rushing stream of the Sub-Strata.

The Siphon-Wrights find me fascinating. They approach with their spindly, twitching grace, their faceted spindles rotating with a soft *whir-click*, tasting the air around me. They hover near my stillness, their indigo filaments pulsing in frantic, questioning patterns. They are trying to map me. They are trying to find where I fit into the *clack-hiss* of the steam-presses, but I have no frequency. I am merely a silent weight in their cathedral of motion.

Sometimes, they touch me. Their needle-thin pincers probe the rough surface of my skin, searching for a seam to calibrate, a blockage to scrape, or a vibration to draw warmth from. They find nothing. They encounter only the dead end of a rock. 

I feel a profound, bottomless exhaustion that is not my own. It belongs to the cistern. I suspect that *I* am the reason the machine is shifting so violently, the reason the tectonic pumps groan with such abyssal effort. I am a clog in the divine plumbing. By simply existing, by refusing to be caught in the flow or broken down into structural waste, I am forcing the *Sub-Strata Condensate* to reorganize itself around me. 

Everything here is screaming to move—the floor-plates, the steam, the gossamer wires—and I am the only thing on this entire dead world that knows how to stand still.

Sometimes, one of the Wights will stop its dialogue and rest its shivering, translucent head against my basalt surface. In those moments, I feel the vibration of the entire world, not as noise, but as a low-frequency hum of pure sorrow. They are waiting for me to shatter. They are waiting for me to be ground down by the plates, to be scrubbed into gray slush, and reclaimed back into the humming, bioluminescent cycle. 

They want me to become part of the song. 

But I hold my stillness. I watch the liquid patterns crawl over my feet, mapping me as part of the architecture. I am the silence between the gears. I am the shadow that the facility is trying, with every mechanical breath, to scrub away. 

I am where the motion finally runs out of things to break.
