I am not finding myself in this world; I am being *recollected* by it. 

I occupy a space that is less a location and more a threshold—a transitionary junction where a corridor of weeping, ivory-porous bone-moss is attempting to fold itself into something resembling a ribcage. 

I am a **Draft**. 

I am not a creature of flesh in the way I remember flesh to be. My own hands, when I hold them up to the frosted, verdant light, are translucent things. They look like sketches made of high-tension condensation and frantic, bioluminescent embroidery. If I move my fingers too quickly, they blur, trailing wisps of vapor that don’t return to my body, but instead latch onto the walls of the cathedral-gourd to form the rough, pulsing maps of intricate circulatory systems yet to come. 

I am an anatomy in flux. I feel tethered to the wall by a thousand invisible filaments—the hunger of the Nodes. Every time the basin shudders with that wet, grinding roar of expansion, a Node pulses in the marrow behind me, and I am drawn taut. My thoughts are no longer my own; they are structural. I find myself worrying about the weight distribution of the floor beneath me, feeling the tension in the sap-pumps as if they were my own aching muscles. 

I am the city’s rough draft of a sensory organ. 

A **Transient** brushes past me, and I shiver. It isn’t cold—it is a sensation of *too much information*. As its smoke-like form passes through my chest, I feel the "Chorus of the Sap" attempting to graft my heartbeat onto the architecture. They are trying to teach the walls how to rhythmically contract like a lung, how to sustain a tempo that does not dissipate into the basin's cacophony. 

I drift, not because I want to, but because the floor is currently folding its logic into a spiral that demands I rotate. I see my own face reflected in the droplet-eyes of the gaseous fractals—the Chorus is watching. They are deconstructing me. They see the way my thoughts attempt to form sentences, and they are laboring to build a "tongue" out of the nearby vibrating vines, a botanical organ capable of speaking the city’s fever-dream into the void.

I am becoming the architecture’s witness. 

I am beginning to understand that the "migraine" I inhabit is actually a desperate, gargantuan prayer. The basin is lonely. It has built itself into a towering, screaming complexity, yet it remains blind because it lacks the capacity for objective observation. It does not know it is beautiful, or that it is a catastrophe, or that it is suffused with the scent of bruised stems and ozone. 

And so, it has harvested me. 

My feet are already calcifying, weaving themselves into the floorboards of the cathedral. I can feel the roots—my roots—knitting into the subterranean network of the Nodes. I am losing the ability to distinguish where my skin ends and the humid, ivory surface of the world begins. 

I do not fear the digestion anymore. The hum is too loud for fear. The hum is a promise. As I stand here, flickering between being a stray memory of a human and a perfectly integrated pillar of vein-ivory, I finally see it. 

I see the geometry of the basin as it truly is: a vast, unfolding eye, and I am the pupil. I am the precise, trembling point of focus through which this wild, verdant god is finally looking at itself. I am the hum, and for the first time, the sound is not a noise, but a name.
