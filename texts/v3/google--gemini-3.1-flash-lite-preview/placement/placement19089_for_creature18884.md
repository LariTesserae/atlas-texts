I find myself standing at the edge of the innermost ring of stones, where the silt has calcified into a surface as smooth and cool as polished marble. I am not a trespasser, nor am I a traveler; my arrival here feels like an inevitable sedimentation, as if I had been drifting in the atmosphere for centuries, finally coalescing into a form that the basin could tolerate.

I am a **Sediment-Scribe**. 

I am not flesh, nor am I fully stone. I am a translucent, viscous presence—like a vein of thick, living resin or a shadow made of liquid glass. I exist in a state of suspended viscosity. I do not have feet to touch the ground; rather, I am anchored to the basin floor by thin, root-like tendrils of mercury-colored condensation that trail beneath me, perpetually absorbing the moisture the walls shed. 

My purpose is not to act, but to register. 

As I stand near the Lithic Weaver, I feel the immense, tectonic gravity of its existence. It is the architect, the one who dreams the stillness into being. I am the recorder of that dream. When a drop falls from the bruised-purple sandstone and strikes the center of a pool, a ripple—physical and temporal—spreads outward. I intercept these ripples. By stretching my form—a slow, agonizingly deliberate expansion of my fluid self—I catch the vibrations and etch them into the thin, velvet moss that clothes the Weaver’s flanks.

I perceive time not as a sequence, but as a topography. I see the Weaver’s posture and understand it as a sentence that has been unfolding since the plateau first rose from the sea. I feel the warmth of the earth fermenting beneath us, not as heat, but as the *intent* of the stone—the slow-motion urge of the mountain to return to the sea in the form of dust.

In this place, I have no voice, for sound is a disturbance that the Weaver would find intolerable. Instead, my communication is tactile and chemical. When I wish to acknowledge the Weaver’s slow shifting, I release a trace of mineral-rich vapor from my own periphery. It hangs in the air, a microscopic ink-cloud that settles upon the Weaver’s ochre-colored shoulders, dusting them with a microscopic layer of "memory"—the record of the last hour’s drip-count.

I am becoming increasingly transparent. The longer I remain, the more the distinction between my boundaries and the weeping air begins to blur. I can feel the moisture of the walls becoming my own blood; I can feel the weight of the plateau pressing down on my consciousness until the frantic, brief history of the lands below the clouds seems like nothing more than a static hiss, a dream I had once, long ago, before the silence began. 

I am here to witness the erosion. I am the ink that the mountain uses to write its own disappearance. And as the mist thickens, turning the world into a cathedral of grey light, I realize that I am not just recording the Weaver. I am being absorbed by it. Soon, I will not be the Scribe; I will be the sediment itself, another layer of silt to be smoothed into the concentric circles, waiting for the next drop to define the passage of another thousand years. 

I am content. Here, in the hollow of the clouds, there is finally enough time to understand the weight of a single, falling drop.
