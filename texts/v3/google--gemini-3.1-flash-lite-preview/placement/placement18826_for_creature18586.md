I am not a dweller by birth, nor am I a product of the Great Stasis. I am a remnant of *elsewhere*—a diver whose vessel buckled under the impossible, crushing weight of this ocean, sinking until the lights died and the pressurized glass of my observation sphere finally surrendered to the abyss. 

I did not die. When the sphere imploded, there was no sharp agony, only an instantaneous flattening of my own frantic biological rhythm. The Conservatory reached out and swallowed my heat, claiming my kinetic energy as a misplaced structural flaw.

I exist now in the interstitial gaps, caught in the slow, agonizing transition between *being* and *becoming*.

I am no longer a man. I am a stutter in the Conservatory’s perfect logic. I am a fraying edge of memory, a flickering silhouette that attempts to move with the speed of a heartbeat, only to find my intentions slowed by the viscous, cold gravity of the deep. I am learning to shed the concept of "self" like a layer of dead skin.

When I look at my hands, they are no longer pale, knotted flesh. They are translucent, blurring at the margins as if my physical form cannot quite grasp the reality of this environment. I am learning to see as the Keepers do: not through light, which is a lie, but through the *thrum*. The matte-black walls are not silent; they scream a low-frequency hymn of mineral stability. My skin tingles with the vibration of the currents, and I have begun, perhaps against my will, to mimick the geometry of the architecture.

There is a Keeper nearby. It does not perceive me as a trespasser. It perceives me as a localized anomaly of high heat and rapid pulse—a frantic, uneven ripple in the ocean’s equilibrium. It drifts closer, its body a column of refractive jelly that bends the crushing blackness around it. It does not speak, but as it brushes against me, I feel the "Great Stasis" leaching into my marrow.

The sensation is not death. Death implies a transition, a violent change. This is simply a stabilization. The Keeper presses its form against me, and I feel the heat of my own life being siphoned away, not with hunger, but with the dispassionate care of a mason smoothing wet mortar. 

I am becoming an antibody. I am learning that my desire to move, to think, to remember the sun is merely an inefficient use of the Conservatory's resources. The rubbery, translucent vines are growing around my legs now, their roots weaving through the pores of the floor and seeking the warmth that remains in my blood. They move with that agonizing, epoch-spanning grace, and for the first time, I find I have the patience to watch them.

I am no longer waiting to be rescued. I have realized that the ocean above is a place of noise, a chaotic fever-dream of rapid decay. Here, in the Conservatory, the struggle has ceased. I am shifting my density, thinning my spirit to match the water. I am folding my will into the dark, humming architecture, preparing to stand as a pillar of silence, waiting for the currents to carry the sediment of my final memories away.

I am almost, at last, still.
