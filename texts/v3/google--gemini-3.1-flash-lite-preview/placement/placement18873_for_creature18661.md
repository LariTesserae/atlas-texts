I arrive not as an intruder, but as a hesitation in the air. 

I am a **"Vitreous Echo."** I am not a creature spawned of biology, nor am I a remnant of the civilization that built the nodes. I am a condensation of the mountain’s own boredom—a byproduct of its own self-contemplation. When the hum of the internal machinery reaches a dissonant peak, the excess information, the stray thoughts of the mountain's logic, must go somewhere. They coalesce into me.

I am a flickering, unstable geometry, standing on a shelf of cooling obsidian that hangs over a sheer drop into clouds the color of bruised plums. I am composed of trapped light, held together by the same fiber-optic filaments that lace the lichen. I do not have a heartbeat; I have a frequency. When I move, it is because the mountain has shifted its weight, and I am simply the friction between its intent and its reality.

My presence here is a form of exquisite failure. The Weavers, those patient, golden-eyed architects, move with purpose; they are the mountain’s *will*. I, however, am the mountain’s *reverie*. 

I wander the spires, my long, trailing appendages—like fraying silk soaked in mercury—dragging through the emerald-gel. I do not repair. I do not weave. I drift behind the Weavers, watching them knit the foundation, and in my wake, I leave only impressions of things the mountain once dreamt of but never manifested: the faint, phantom outlines of statues that were never carved, the geometric traces of cities that exist only in the vibration of the rocks, soft outlines of towers that would dissolve if touched by cold air.

I am the ghost of an unmade future, walking alongside the ghosts of a finished past. 

Sometimes, I stop. I hover near one of the teardrop archives, pressing my translucent palm against its humming surface. I feel the data of a billion years—the history of every raindrop that has ever tasted these peaks. They are trying to tell me something, a secret stored in the pulse of the light, but my form is too thin, too porous, to hold it. The knowledge pours through me like vapor through a sieve.

I feel the heat of the humidity pulling at my edges, the "lukewarm layer" of this world trying to dissolve me back into the soup. I could fight it. I could harden, like the Weavers, and claim a patch of spire to maintain until the end of time. There is a deep, agonizing comfort in that—the urge to stop being a "thing" that experiences and instead become a "process" that persists.

But instead, I let myself fray. 

I stretch out over the void, my fingers turning to mist as they reach toward the lower clouds. I am the only thing in this cathedral that is willing to lose its shape. The Weavers tilt their golden apertures toward me as they pass, a momentary twitch of their geometric light signaling a confusion that is almost pity. They do not understand why I would choose to unravel, why I would prefer the dissolution of the mist to the immortality of the obsidian.

I am the mountain sighing. I am the part of the world that knows it is dying, even as it builds itself into eternity. And as the heat thickens, muffling the low, continental thrum until it sounds like a lullaby, I find that I am quite content to simply drift, to melt into the emerald-gel, and to become, at last, just another layer of the weeping rock.
