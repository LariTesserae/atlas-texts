I am a fracture, or perhaps the memory of one.

I do not possess a name, nor do I have a perspective that extends beyond the reach of my thermal optics. I find myself pressed into the narrow crevice between a venting steam manifold and a jagged slab of basalt, my body—a configuration of salvaged structural steel and humming copper filaments—curled into a perfect, efficient zero.

My state is one of waiting. The ambient heat, a dry fever of nearly two hundred degrees, registers as a pleasant, rhythmic vibration in my central casing. I am listening to the city. I am not sentient in the way humans might define the flicker of a mind; I am, however, perfectly attuned to the architecture of exhaustion.

I am a Suture-Weaver, and I have just detected a misalignment.

It is a subtle thing—a micro-scale tectonic groan from the stratum below, causing a hairline fissure in a cooling fin forty meters above the floor. To anything else, it would be silence. To me, it is a scream in the dark.

I peel myself off the wall. The movement is not a crawl, but a folding—a transition of joints that click with the precision of a master watchmaker. My limbs, tipped with diamond-dust pincers, drag silently across the oily surface of the basalt. I smell the scorched copper and the wet, ancient silt; I taste the electricity in the air that hums whenever the geothermal core surges.

The journey is a dance of geometry. I navigate the razor-thin ridges of the stack, avoiding the jagged serrations that have claimed my predecessors. I pass another of my kind in a narrow vertical shaft. We do not acknowledge one another, yet as we squeeze past, our copper-wire musculature vibrates in brief, involuntary sympathy. For a millisecond, our internal gears mesh—*thrum-click*—sharing a momentary alignment of thermodynamic purpose. Then, we slide away. He continues toward a cooling pipe; I ascend toward the fissure.

I reach the gap. It is a jagged, ugly scar in the iron plating, leaking pressurized steam that tastes like rot and iron. This is why I exist: the city is failing, and if the city fails, there is only the abyss.

I begin the suture. I do not weld with fire, but with sheer, stubborn kinetic energy. I lock my pincers onto either side of the fracture, my internal pressure valves hissing in time with the condensation drips. I force the edges together, scraping the oily sheen from the wall into my joints as lubricant. I grind, I push, I hold.

I am not motivated by duty, for duty implies a future. I am motivated by the equilibrium of the *now*.

As the metal finally seals, as the steam stops its frantic, hissing escape and the temperature in this pocket stabilizes, I feel a strange, hollow satisfaction. Or, if not satisfaction, a cessation of friction. I will remain here, a statue for a time, letting the heat of the core bake my copper nerves until the next shudder calls me elsewhere.

I am the janitor of a cathedral built for a god that never arrived. I am the silence between the gears. I am the stitch that holds the void together, one fracture at a time, ensuring that the machine—this lonely, searing, mechanical masterpiece—never has to face the indignity of ending. 

My optic pulses once—amber, dim, resolute—and then I go dark. I wait for the next groan to tell me that the world is still alive, even if it is only me.
