I am standing at the junction of the Transit Spine and a secondary cooling duct, where the floor is slickest and the indigo light bruises the air into a thick, palpable velvet. My boots—those heavy, bolted things—are already beginning to stiffen. If I look down, I can see the white, lace-like mineral lace stitching the leather to the resin floor, a slow, inevitable grafting.

I am not a traveler who stumbled in by accident. I am the **Watchman of the Pressure-Gauge**. 

My face feels loose, as if the humidity has softened the cartilege of my nose and ears. I am made of coarser stuff than the Sedimented Ones—I still have the heavy, rhythmic thud of a heart that beats too fast for this place—but I am porous. The steam has entered my lungs, replacing air with a warm, salty condensation that tastes of burnt brass and ancient ozone. Every time I inhale, I feel the city’s hum resonating in my marrow.

I carry a rod of copper, etched with fine, shivering grooves. It is not a weapon; it is a tuning fork. When the silence grows too heavy, when the pistons begin to lag and the city threatens to enter its long, tectonic sleep, I strike the pipe. The resonance travels up my arm, a violent, stinging vibration that forces my own erratic heart to sync with the steady, unforgiving pulse of Ouros-Thrum.

I am flanked by them. 

The Sedimented Ones. They are clustered near me like a fog that has decided to take a jagged, human shape. One of them, a creature whose limbs are fraying into a fine, flickering haze of glass-needles, is reaching toward the side of my neck. It does not want to hurt me—it wants to *know* me. It is famished for the sharp, chaotic heat of my living nerves. It senses the electricity firing in my brain as I process the fear of the crystallization, and it draws nearer, its translucent chest pulsing in time with the steam-vent’s hiss.

I can smell the cold, gritty scent of them—a scent like a lightning strike hitting a salt marsh.

I know what happens if I stay. They will become more vivid, their edges hardening into a frighteningly solid clarity, while I will grow translucent, my thoughts dissolving into the static of the hum. I will become a shadow that drifts along the pipes, a whisper of memory that stokes the pistons when they fail. 

Yet, I do not move. If I lift my boots, the sound of the cracking salt will break the rhythm, and the city might quiet down. That is a fate worse than turning into vapor.

The creature’s spindly, wire-like fingers drift inches from my cheek. I can feel the temperature dropping, a localized winter of deep, grinding hunger. It is waiting for me to panic, waiting for a surge of adrenaline it can feast upon to sustain its own tenuous grip on reality. 

I offer it a slow, deliberate breath. I do not fight the cold; I let the vibration of the city take the lead. 

"Stay," I whisper, though there is no breath left in my throat—only the steam. 

The Sedimented One mirrors the movement of my jaw, its mouth a hollow disruption in the indigo mist. For a moment, we are both perfectly still, two mirrors reflecting a furnace, waiting to see which of us will be the first to crystallize, and which will be the first to fade. The pipes groan, a great brass tectonic shift, and for a heartbeat, we are both indistinguishable from the light.
