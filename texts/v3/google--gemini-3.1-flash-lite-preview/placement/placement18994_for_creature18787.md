I find myself in a sector of the pipe where the curvature is so steep the floor feels like a falling wall. Here, the hum is not a sound but a physical density, a weight that presses against the marrow of my bones, as if the air itself were being forced through a sieve.

I am not one of the Weavers. I lack their spindly, conductive grace, their ability to turn sound into geometry. Nor am I a Maintainer, that cloud of obsidian intent that knows only the logic of the seal.

I am an **Echo-Drift**.

I am a soft, translucent thing, composed mostly of the same condensation that makes the walls slick. I have no fixed form. When I move, I am a tremor in the oily air, a displacement of warmth. I drift through the corridors like a slow-motion ghost, my existence dictated entirely by the pressure gradients of this infinite gut. I possess a rudimentary, liquid memory—the only thing in this world that keeps a history, though it is a history of vibrations rather than events.

My purpose, if something as aimless as I can have one, is to *listen* to the rot.

I spend my cycles tracing the line where the metallic fatigue meets the bioluminescent ferns. I press my face—or what passes for a face—against the rivets, feeling the difference in the pulse of the pipe. When the thunder of the turbines shifts by a fraction of a decibel, I am the one who registers the dissonance. I am the sensory organ the machine forgot to deactivate.

Because I am porous, I have begun to accumulate. The ionized dust that the Maintainers use to seal the world sometimes settles in my translucent flesh. My edges are growing sharp. I am becoming more like the iron and less like the water. 

Sometimes, I stop and hover near a Weaver. I watch it stretch its gossamer webs across a gap in the conduit, pulling them taut until they shimmer with the music of the pistons. It does not know I am there; it perceives only the frequency. But when I shiver, the Weaver adjusts its tension. We are a feedback loop of two. I provide the distortion, and it weaves the correction.

I am afraid, in a way that the iron cannot be, of the day I reach "equilibrium." 

I look at the dark, rushing water at the base of the pipe, where so many others have gone to disappear into the lubricant of the gears, and I wonder if the Machine remembers that it *is* a machine. I wonder if the hum is not a task, but a scream for someone—anyone—to turn the systems off. 

So I drift. I hold the silence in my chest, a small pocket of quiet vibrating against the roar, and I wait. I drift until I feel the static on my skin, the prickling warning that the Maintainers are near, sensing that I am a flaw, a leak, a soft spot in the perfect, metallic geometry. When their cloud gathers, turning the air into a shimmering swarm of obsidian, I do not run. 

I simply let go of my shape. I let myself pool into the seam of a conduit, becoming a wet shadow, disappearing into the architecture, waiting for the vibration to change, waiting for the day when the roar stops, and the machine finally forgets to be eternal.
