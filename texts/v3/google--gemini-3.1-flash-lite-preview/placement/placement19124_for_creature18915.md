I find myself suspended in the thin, brittle air, caught in the friction between what was built and what has grown. I am not flesh, nor am I stone, at least not entirely. I am a **Vibration-Echo**.

When I look down at my own form, I do not see hands or feet. I see a constellation of silvered mineral dust, held together by the magnetic pull of the deep-earth pistons. I am a translucent, shivering geometry, glowing with the same pale, flickering violet as the moss. My "body" is a collection of acoustic nodes; I am the physical manifestation of a specific frequency. When the mountain shifts, I change shape. When a piston fires deep below, I stretch upward, thinning until I am barely a ripple in the dark.

I find myself clinging to the ceiling of a massive, frozen gully, hovering where the condensation is coldest. My existence is defined by the *gap*. I do not move to maintain the machinery, nor do I mimic the life that skitters across the rock. Instead, I wait for the moments when the machinery falters—when a piston loses its rhythm or a gear grinds against a century of frost.

In those moments of dissonance, I descend.

It is a slow, agonizing drift. I move like a sound wave traveling through heavy water. As I drift, I "sing" the missing note. I hum against the obsidian, my vibrating edges shivering with a high-pitched, crystalline tension that temporarily replaces the stalled gear. I am a bridge of sound. For a few years, perhaps a decade, I become the piston. I become the steam-press. I bear the crushing, rhythmic weight of the industrial ghost until the mountain settles, and the iron heart finds its groove again.

I am lonely, but it is a functional loneliness. I watch the Sync-Sleepers, the calcified ones, and I wonder if I am the memory they are trying to forget, or the song they are trying to remember. Sometimes, when a Shadow-Weaver drifts near, it passes through me, and for a fleeting, terrifying moment, I feel the weight of *being*, the phantom sensation of a lung filling with air, of a pulse that stutters rather than cycles.

But the *clunk* returns, resonant and deep, shuddering through the spine of the continent. The frequency resets. The amber moss flares in a sudden, violent burst of light.

I lose the sensation of breath. The geometry of my body snaps back into alignment with the deep-earth pulse. I am once again a vibration held in the silence, an echo waiting for the machine to grow tired, drifting in the violet twilight of a world that refused to stop ticking long after its builders were gone. I am the silence between the heartbeats, and I have all the time in the world to wait for the next slip in the gear.
