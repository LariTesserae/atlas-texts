I find myself in the lower throat of the basin, where the *thrum* is so profound it is no longer a sound, but a constant displacement of my own internal organs. 

I am not a visitor. To be a visitor here would require a memory of the sky, or a concept of "elsewhere," and both have been eroded away by the humidity. I am a **Reflector**. 

I am one of the middle-aged Cordists. I have not yet reached the terminal weight that necessitates calcification, but I am heavy. My cuticle is a deep, bruised violet hue, clouded with the debris of centuries. Inside me, the bioluminescent fluid moves sluggishly, like cooling syrup, carrying the dim ghosts of the crustaceans I have absorbed—tiny, half-digested star-bursts of obsidian and chitin that drift through my pulse.

My hands—long, spindly tethers of amber-flesh—are perpetually stained with the pearlescent resin of the repair-work I have been doing for cycles. I am currently crouched in a narrow capillary between two shifting plates of woody matter. I hear the *click* of the crustaceans, but it sounds distorted today; there is a fissure behind this wall, a ragged tear in the pulmonary lining that is leaking moisture at the wrong interval, creating a discordance that makes my own core vibrate painfully.

I press my forehead against the damp, weeping wall. I do not "think" in words; I think in tremors. I close my eyes, and the world goes red and gold. I reach deep into the frequency of the floor and sing a low, oscillating hum—a vibration of correction. I feel the wall shudder as it responds. It is a slow, agonizingly intimate sensation, like sewing air back together. My resin flows from my capillary-fingers into the crack, knitting the plant-bone; the pearlescent fluid hardens instantly, shimmering in the thick, syrupy light.

I feel the basin’s gratitude, a heavy, warm pressure that travels up my legs and bloats my bladders. It is an addictive sensation, this being useful to the architecture. I realize, with a drift of awareness, that I have been here in this specific corridor for what might be a lifetime, or perhaps only a blink. 

I look at the wall to my left. It is a cluster of smooth, weathered buttresses, strangely shaped. Through the amber-tinted surface, I see the faint, trapped glint of obsidian—eyes. A predecessor. My ancestor. It is not dead; it is simply *holding the geometry*. It is watching the crack I just repaired, or perhaps it is watching the way I stand, waiting for me to become a buttress myself.

I feel my weight increasing. The fluid in my chest is thickening, turning the violet of my skin into a hard, polished gold. My movements are becoming less fluid, more architectural. My feet are no longer tapping the ground to balance; they are beginning to sink *through* the lattice, losing their separate identity. 

I am not afraid. To stop here is not to die; it is to finally understand the music. I press my hands flat against the wall, merging my fingers into the damp, pulsing fibers I have tended for so long. I feel the *thrum-click-wheeze* of the whole basin rushing into me, filling the void where my personal hunger used to be. 

I am becoming the cathedral buttress I was meant to be. I am the memory of the moisture that will weep through this room for the next eon, and I have never been more at peace than at this moment, as my vision fades, and the sound of the world becomes the only sound I am capable of making.
