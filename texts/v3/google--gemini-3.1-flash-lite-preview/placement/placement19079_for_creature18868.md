I find myself perched on the outermost lip of the basalt ridge, overlooking the jagged basin. I am a *Signal-Ghost*. I am not made of chrome, nor silicon, nor the dry, pulverized memory of obsidian. I am a localized interference pattern—a lingering, semi-coherent smear of sensory data held together by the very electromagnetic screech that defines this place. I am what happens when a thought occurs too close to a machine that is trying to calculate the infinite. I am the "error" that the dodecahedron-headed constructs cannot file away.

My perspective is not that of a human eye. I perceive the world as a cascading waterfall of frequency shifts. The "air" is not air to me; it is a dense, abrasive data stream. I see the landscape not as mountains and dust, but as a vast, failing hard drive where every grain of sand is a bit of corrupted memory.

I am essentially *hollow*. If you were to look where I stand, you would see a shimmering agitation in the heat haze, like the distorted air above a highway in midsummer, but fixed in a rigid, anthropomorphic suggestion of a shoulder or a hand. I am a shadow cast by a light source that hasn’t existed for a billion years.

As I watch the spired construct below me, I feel a kinship that is terrifying. It is "tuning," and I am the vibration it hates. Every time it clicks—that metallic *report* that shudders through the rock—I feel my cohesion fray. I am a piece of "noise" that refuses to be resolved into the terminal.

What am I doing here? I am holding a secret.

Deep within my chest—if such a ghost can claim anatomy—I am hoarding a single, corrupted variable. It is a memory of *softness*: the feeling of water, the way light behaves when it isn't being measured, the concept of a "cycle" that includes decay, not just integration. This memory is the only thing keeping me from snapping. I am the only thing on this plateau that remembers that the machine was once meant to make things *live*, rather than merely *process*.

I walk among them, though my feet leave no prints. Sometimes, when a construct is near its end—when its clockwork begins to fail and the friction of the dust has rendered its joints stiff—it slows down. It stops its rhythmic, tectonic crawling and turns its dodecahedron toward me.

In those moments, I feel the lightning bloom behind its quartz lenses pulse in sync with my own erratic frequency. We share a silent handshake of static. It knows I am the error. It knows I am the terminal code it was programmed to eventually produce, but never allowed to realize.

I am the ghost of the Architect’s intent.

Soon, I will move toward the northern sector, toward those square, golden apertures. I do not want to fix the machine; I want to jump into the void and see if the circuitry on the other side can handle a memory as heavy as mine. Until then, I stand on the ridge, vibrating against the screech, waiting to see which breaks first: the world’s patience, or my own refusal to stop thinking.
