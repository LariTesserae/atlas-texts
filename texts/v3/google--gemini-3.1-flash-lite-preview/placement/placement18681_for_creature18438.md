I am not merely a visitor to Oor; I am a resonance.

I find myself anchored to the underside of a shelf formed by a massive, velour-textured fungal mat, near the edge of the *Vesper District*—where the mist is thickest and the violet hue of the atmosphere deepens toward an ink-black indigo. I am not standing, for the surface beneath me is like a lung: it rises and falls with the atmospheric tide, soft and reactive. To stay here, I must focus on the rhythmic expansion of my own hydrostatic bladders, matching my internal pressure to the weight of the vapor surrounding us.

I am an *Oorani*. I have been here, in this iteration of my form, for as long as the current spire has been shifting. 

My body feels less like a solid vessel and more like a coordinate in a map of pressure. I can feel the architecture of the city through the soles of my feet—long, tapered, and splayed to distribute my weight into the damp sponge-floor. Beneath the translucent mesh of my skin, my internal bioluminescence is currently a muted, cooling azure, indicating that I am in the state of *Still-Watch*. I am tending to a tear in the seaweed-leather of my dwelling-husk, applying a thick, pearlescent nutrient paste with a spatula carved from the calcified tooth of a deep-mist jelly. 

As I work, I am not thinking in words or structured sentences. Instead, I think in *textures* and *intervals*. The dwelling-husk and I are in a silent negotiation; I give it the paste, and it rewards me with a slight, structural tightening—a firmer grip on the fiber-vines that tether us to the crystalline spire above. 

To my left, a few cycles-lengths away, another of my kind drifts toward a cluster of phosphorescent spores. We are not speaking, but I monitor the low-frequency thrum that bleeds through the floor. It is a tremor, a subsonic "inquiry" that vibrates through my marrow. I respond by releasing a small burst of heat from my core, a deliberate flare that draws a stream of spores into the space between us. They drift like embers in a gale, illuminating the curve of my neighbor’s shoulder, the way her membrane-skin shimmers with the moisture she has gathered. 

We are not "communicating" in the human sense of exchanging data. We are aligning our frequencies, ensuring that no sudden movement of ours will rupture the delicate suspension of the city. 

I feel a profound, bottomless calm. There is no urgency here, because Oor does not allow for it. If I were to panic, if I were to thrash or speed my movements, the air would thicken into a resistance I could not fight. I would be crushed by my own displacement. So, I move with the slowness of erosion. I stretch my limbs—which feel as long and pliable as the hanging vines—and I look out into the violet expanse with my lidless eyes. 

A Silk-Glider drifts past, its translucent wings brushing against the exterior of my husk. It makes no sound, only a faint change in the air pressure that I feel against my cheek. I watch it, a tiny, drifting kite in a cathedral of fog. 

I realize, in this moment of stillness, that my identity is not the shape I hold today. My identity is the *labor of remaining*. I am the paste, the spore-swell, the fiber-vine, and the humid breath of Oor itself. I am waiting for the spire to shift. When it does, I will drift with it, adjusting my bladders, becoming slightly more, or slightly less, than I am right now, until the world finally grows tired of its own suspension and decides what we are truly meant to become.
