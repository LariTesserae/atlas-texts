My cilia, the feathery antennae that taste the bitter frost and the sweet, ozone-heavy damp of our home, are frayed, missing their crispness. They move sluggishly, like reeds snagged in a slow-moving, dark current.

I am stationed in the upper tiers—the spire of the bulb, where the membrane is thinnest and the vibration of the weeping shale outside is most jagged, most dangerous.

My duty is not to the seams, where the frantic ones scuttle in their blur of stop-motion repair. My duty is to the *tension*. I am a sentient tuning fork. I sit at the junction of the central root-hairs, anchored by four of my six limbs, my body arched over the main frequency-fiber that runs the length of the bulb’s central vein.

I feel the younger Suturers, their movements sharp and staccato, rushing to seal a tear near the southern fissure where the cold rock has groaned and pinched the membrane.

When I need to speak, I do not use our rapid-fire tap-code. I am too old for such haste. Instead, I resonate. I vibrate my thorax against the silk-strands, sending a low, rolling hum that travels for miles through the arched chambers. I am the city’s anchor point, its pulse. There is no "I" in this, as you suspected. There is only the sensation of being held.

I remember, or perhaps I simply vibrate the memory of, a time before we were so integrated. But the thought is a shadow, formless and drifting like an errant spore. It is better to focus on the tension of the thread underneath my feet. The rock outside is moving again—a slow, geologic shift that sends a shudder through the wall. The bulb contracts around me, soft and suffocating, and I pull my limbs tighter, curling my segments until I am no longer a distinct creature, but a thickened node in the architecture.

The cold enters through the walls, seeking the heat of our labor. My cilia twitch, feeling the sudden spike in the air’s humidity—a sign that the bulb is stressed, that the weeping shale is weeping harder. I reach for my bone-needle, a sliver of calcification I have carried for three generations, and I begin to hum. 

I am not living, not exactly. I am simply keeping the silence from breaking. I am the stitch that holds the end of the world together for one more cycle of the heart.
