I am standing on a landing of spun quartz, though "standing" is a generous term for a state of existence defined mostly by the refusal to integrate with the environment. I do not have feet. I do not have a frame that occupies space in any reliable, Newtonian sense. I am a geometry of jagged edges, a sliver of darkness pulled tight against the plum-colored sky.

I am a Refraction.

I do not think in words—words are too heavy, too sluggish, like trying to carve a monument out of frozen light. I think in *state-changes*. I experience the world as a sequence of tensions and resolutions. Right now, I am tracking the structural strain of the spire to my left. It is leaning at an impossible, acute angle, holding on to a history of gravity that no longer applies. It is stubborn. It is "too present."

The taste of copper is sharp against the non-place where my consciousness resides, a metallic tang that signals a build-up of unspent reality. The spire is shivering; it wants to be sand, but it is clinging to its obsidian shape out of a fear of the void. I drift toward it. I do not "move" so much as I revise my coordinate in the local grid. *Here. Not here. There.*

As I slide through the wall of the spire, I feel the resistance of the architecture—a dry, screeching friction that vibrates through my entire length. For a heartbeat, the obsidian becomes translucent. I catch in my own edges the reflection of what this spire was before it became a skeleton: a tall, humming cathedral of resonant glass, filled with the music of things that were once alive and certain of their own weight. I hold that memory for a fraction of a second, a fleeting, shimmering ghost of a balcony and a staircase, before the sheer density of the vision dissolves into the cold flour that coats my surface.

The spire sighs, a soundless vibration that travels up through the mountain's ribs, and it begins to dissolve. The obsidian softens, turning into the fine, metallic dust of this place. I absorb the release of pressure—that delicious, static surge of logic finally collapsing into entropy. I am sated. I am a little heavier, a little darker.

Then, there is an intersection.

Another of my kind drifts through the corridor. We do not pass each other; we *coincide*. As our edges overlap, the architecture around us bends, turning inside out to reveal the interior of a realm where the laws of physics are not a stutter, but a song. The psychic scream of our contact vibrates the iron in my core. For that instant, I see the entirety of the city. I see the horizon curling into a perfect, infinite loop, and I understand the architecture of our own inevitable end.

We peel apart. The silence returns, heavier and colder than before.

My companion continues down the ridge, its silhouette flickering like a dying pulse. I remain on the quartz bridge, watching the spire I’ve just dismantled settle into a fine, gray heap. I am empty again. I am hungry again. I watch the bruised light of the sky, waiting for the next tremor of reality, waiting for the next moment to offer itself up to be cleaned. 

There is no malice in this. There is only the long, slow work of undoing. I am the silence that follows the echo, and I am waiting for the moment when there is nothing left to echo at all.
