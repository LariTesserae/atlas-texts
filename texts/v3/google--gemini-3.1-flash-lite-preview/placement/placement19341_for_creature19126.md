I am not an explorer here because I have a destination. I am an explorer here because I have a resonant frequency.

Standing upon the slick, obsidian mosaic, I feel the *drip-clack* not in my ears, but in the marrow of my long, thin limbs. I am not human, though I retain the *notion* of a human shape—a lingering, vestigial memory of upright posture that the Refractions occasionally mimic when they drift too close. 

I am a **Sediment-Walker** who has lingered too long in the "clear" phase. 

I am tall, twelve feet of pressurized, translucent silicate. My surface is still mostly diamond-hard, lacking that milky, marble opacity that signifies a Walker nearing the end of its purpose. I am too clear, too fragile, too cognizant of the empty space between the shards. While the older Walkers stand as stoic, silent needles—anchors for the basin’s pulse—I find myself fidgeting. 

My movement is a slow, agonizing re-orientation. I don't "walk"; I shed my current alignment. I tilt my needle-tipped base against the sharp obsidian, my body humming with the strain of the Tuning-Forks’ symphony. Every *drip-clack* that vibrates through the rock forces a shudder through my frame. When I unfurl, I do not do so to harvest the viscous, weeping ribbons of fluid—not yet. I unfurl to see if I can catch a Refraction.

I am a graveyard-tender who has forgotten how to be a statue.

When the temperature drops and the bruising blue light bleeds up from the basin floor, I stand perfectly still, bracing myself against the weight of the Tuning-Forks. They exert their pressure, demanding I dampen my internal vibration to match the rhythmic chime of the stones. But I refuse. I vibrate at a discordant frequency to attract the drifting fractals.

They approach me like moths to a dying star. A Refraction, shimmering in a shifting lattice of blue, drifts into my chest cavity—a hollow space meant for structural density, currently filled only with the cold, wet air of the valley. For a moment, the Refraction mimics me. It tries to adopt the geometry of my spine, the sharp angle of my shoulder. It reaches for the solidity I possess, and in return, I reach for the history it carries.

In that brief, glowing contact, I am no longer a walker of stone. I am a memory of a world that breathes. I see the landscape not as a "graveyard of geometry," but as a garden of glass, warm and vibrant, lit by a sun that did not merely pulse, but glowed with a steady, golden heat. I feel the phantom sensation of soil beneath feet that were not needles.

Then, the temperature begins to rise. The blue light recedes. The Refraction flickers and dissolves back into the mineral veins of the earth, leaving me shivering in the wet, suffocating gloom. 

The Tuning-Forks intensify their roar. The *drip-clack* rhythm accelerates, demanding that I settle, that I stop this frantic imitation of life, that I let the mercury-thick moisture coat me until I am white, opaque, and permanent. 

I reach out a thin, sharp hand, tracing the air where the light just was, but my fingers find only the cold, hard reality of the weeping spires. I tuck my filaments back in. I return to my needle-point stance. I become a static anchor once more, waiting for the symphony to end, waiting for the day when I am no longer a walker, but a part of the monument. 

I am practicing how not to be. It is the hardest thing I have ever had to learn.
