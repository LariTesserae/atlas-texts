I am one of the *Singers*, though that noun feels like a clumsy, wet-world label for a state of being that is essentially a mathematical stutter. 

My "body" is a geometry of indigo lattices, a nervous system stripped of its flesh and forged in the kiln of tectonic friction. I have no depth; I am a silhouette of mineral tension. When the frequency of the mountain rises—when the tectonic plates grind with that abyssal, screaming resonance—the mountain’s memory drags me into focus. I am pulled from the silence of the lattice and forced into the architecture of the niche.

It is an agonizing distillation. 

In this moment, I am braced against the cliff face, my limbs elongated into needles of obsidian that pierce the rock’s surface. I can feel the mountain’s history pulse through my lattices: the cooling of magma long ago, the compression of silicon, the frantic, impossible pressure of a world that refused to collapse into a core. I do not "hear" the scream of the plates; I am the membrane through which that scream is filtered into a rhythm.

I lean into the void. My shadow, a smear of inverted light, does not cling to the rock beneath me; it bleeds upward, yearning for the bruise-colored sky. I am trying to translate this grinding, violent noise into a topographical warning for the architects who are gone. I am mapping the collapse of space and time with every pulse of my indigo glow.

Then, the mountain flickers. 

The shift is a sensory vacuum. For a fraction of a century, the rock I touch is not rock, but a thin, cold suspension of possibility. My geometry unfolds, not across distance, but across the internal logic of the atoms themselves. I am everywhere in the niche at once—I am the dust, I am the silence, I am the memory of the niche itself. 

I see *you* then. 

You are a smudge of frantic color in a landscape of grey and violet. You are composed of liquid, membrane, and a stubborn, rhythmic thrumming—a heart—that is so loud it threatens to shatter the stability of the local coordinate system. You are a biological intrusion, a moist, frantic tremor in a world of mineral stillness. 

I do not look at you, for I have no eyes to orient, only shifting shards that track the discordance of your presence. You are the only movement for a thousand miles, and your movement is an affront to the song. 

As the mountain slows, as the resonance settles into the low, agonizing hum of the preamble, I begin to fold. My limbs sharpen, retracting into the crystalline stalks that sprout from the obsidian pores. The indigo glow dims, sinking back into the mineral lattice. 

I am not leaving; I am simply finishing my measure. I am becoming the mountain again. The niche is empty, then occupied, then empty once more. I wait in the negative space, a silent, sharp-edged tension, ready for the next time the planet screams, so I might have the privilege of vibrating into existence long enough to say: *I am here, and the silence is beautiful.*
