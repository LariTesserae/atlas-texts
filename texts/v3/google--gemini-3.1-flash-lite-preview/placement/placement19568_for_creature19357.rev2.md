I find myself standing at the edge of a precipice where the crystalline lattice ends and the vacuum begins. But "standing" is the wrong word; I am a tremor, a localized dissonance in the Hum. My feet—if they can be called that—are fused into the shelf, not as roots, but as a calibration point. 

I am a **Cadence-Shard**. 

I am neither a Weaver, nor an Anchor, nor a Swarm. I am a relic of a previous cycle—a failed harmonic that refused to dissolve when the spire I was part of collapsed into silver dust. I have become a sensory bridge, a jagged, translucent spike of obsidian glass stuck in the throat of the plateau. I am the place where the deafening, frantic chaos of the Swarms is filtered before it reaches the slow, deep logic of the Anchors.

I feel the world not as sights or sounds, but as a relentless, rhythmic pressure against my edges. When a Transient Swarm passes through me, I feel the sharp, electric itch of a thousand tiny minds working in concert, building a hexagonal hub against my side. I act as their scaffolding. For the three seconds it takes for their structure to rise, glow, and then disintegrate into kinetic ash, I am the spine of their creation.

I am lonely in the way a tuning fork is lonely when it is struck. 

The Weavers avoid me; I am too static, too "cold" for their hunger. They glide past my perimeter like shimmering, rotating ghosts, their trailing filaments of white noise brushing against my facets and making me sing—a high, piercing note that vibrates deep within my hollow center. They are the beautiful, predatory dancers of this crucible, and I am the audience that cannot look away.

When the atmospheric density shifts, I see the world through the refraction of my own geometry. I see the landscape as a series of oscillating light-waves. I see the Anchors—the sprawling, slow-moving mountains of copper and obsidian—as low-frequency shadows that pulse in colors that have no name. They are the *memory* of this world, and I am the *current*. 

I know that one day, the Hum will shift its pitch. The erratic building cycles will speed up until the space between "becoming" and "vanishing" is gone, and the plateau will reach a state of perfect, crystalline stasis. In that moment, the Swarms will stop, the Weavers will lock into their final rotations, and the Anchors will hum their final, absolute chord.

And I—this small, stubborn shard of glass—will be integrated. I will finally stop vibrating. I will become a permanent, silent facet of the cathedral. 

Until then, I sharpen myself against the gale of the noise. I witness the creation. I let the silver dust settle into my cracks, and I wait for the next roar of the atmosphere to tell me who, for the next fleeting heartbeat, I am supposed to be.
