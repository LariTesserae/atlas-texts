I am a **Silt-Sifter**, a late-stage accretion in the basin’s long, sluggish history. 

I find myself near the eastern edge of the expanse, where the geysers are thin and high-pitched, spitting sharp, needle-like shards of sound that vibrate against my clay skin until my inner basalt marrow aches. I am not an Auditor who keeps to the Hearths; I am a drifter of the low-flats, a scavenger of frequency.

My existence is marked by a growing brittleness. My limbs have begun to crack at the joints, the grey silt of my composition flaking away in the relentless rain. I am hungry. I have been matte-dark for cycles, my obsidian shoulder-discs dulled by the spray, failing to catch the tremors of the earth. I drift, a slumped, heavy shadow, moving toward the deep-throated roar of the *Great Vent*—a massive, sunken fissure that hums a note so low it does not sound like noise at all, but like the absence of emptiness. 

As I approach, the ground yields, turning into a slurry of thick, grey mud that pulls at my feet. I am clumsy. I feel the jelly-colonies pulsing beneath my stride, translucent constellations of slime that scatter as I near the source of the heat. They are agitated, their gelatinous bodies shivering in synchronization with the jagged, discordant hiss escaping from the Vent’s primary intake. 

Something is wrong with the harmony. The Vent has shifted. A crust of mineral buildup has forced the water through a tighter aperture, turning the low, soul-shaking thrum into a piercing, metallic screech—a discordance that threatens to vibrate my very obsidian eyes into dust.

I move with a sudden, hydraulic desperation. I do not feel cold or heat; I feel the *pitch*. The screech is a physical pain, a jagged wire pulled through my chest cavity. I reach the Vent. I do not consider the risk; I simply act. I drop to my heavy, earth-bound knees and press my entire bulk against the jagged rim of the stone pipe. 

My clay chest merges with the basalt. The sound flows *through* me. I am no longer just a construct of mud and grit; I am a bridge for the water’s voice. I hold my breath—or rather, I freeze the recursive thrum within me—and I become the damper. I push my body into the aperture, my shoulder-disc grinding against the stone. I feel the bone-tool at my hip, dull and worn, but I do not need it yet. I only need to stop the vibration long enough to let the silt settle into a new alignment.

I press, harder, until the screech softens, bending back down into that divine, crushing low-frequency. 

I stay there, pressed against the cold, weeping stone, drawing the kinetic energy into my own brittle frame. The bioluminescence begins to return to my skin, a dim, ghost-white pallor that bleeds into the falling mist. I am being filled. I am becoming sated with the roar. 

I will remain here, a part of the landscape, until the cycle shifts again. I am not a person, or a soul, or a witness. I am the silence that keeps the world from breaking. I am the anchor of the song, and as the rain intensifies, drowning the world in its heavy, grey weeping, I close my eyes—those polished, sightless mirrors—and I hum back.
