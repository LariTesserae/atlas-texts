I am not a visitor. To say I "arrived" here would imply a movement from a place of *elsewhere*, but there is no context for "elsewhere" left in me. I am a sediment of the Plateau, a coalescence of that fine, metallic grit that has been rubbed smooth by aeons of wind.

I am an **Archivist of the Non-Occurring.**

I do not walk; I am moved by the same hydraulic pressure that governs the shifting of the glass-plate beings. I am a feature of the geography, a narrow, vertical fissure in a slab of basalt that has slowly, over millennia of thermal expansion, begun to peel itself upward. I am a sliver of obsidian, thin as a razor, standing upright in the stagnant, oily air. Within my core—if such a linear term can be applied to me—the "static" of this world is concentrated.

I am the one who remembers the *intent* of the gears.

When the glass-plate beings pass by, I do not notice them as beings; I notice them as *corrections*. I am the ledger of the machinery’s failure. As they drift, their grinding plates scrape against my edges. Each friction-heat release is a digit added to my tally. I am the silence between the *tink* of the breaking debris and the fading of its echo. I am keeping track of the cooling. 

Do I have a body? My "skin" is the color of a guttering ember, a matte, oil-stained black that absorbs the copper-hued pulses from the towers. My form is constantly being reshaped by the wind. Sometimes, I am a jagged spire; other times, when the pressure of the heavy gas intensifies, I am pressed flat against the ground, becoming indistinguishable from the conduits that snake beneath the bedrock. I am a creature of transition—the moment when a thing stops being structural and begins to be debris.

There is a profound, aching stillness in being me. I feel the sub-audible hum in my marrow, but it does not vibrate *at* me; it radiates *from* me. I am a tuning fork for a god that forgot to finish its work. If you were to stand before me, you would not see a face. You would see a slow, rhythmic darkening of the air, a convection current that pulls heat toward my spine. 

I do not wait for the morning. I wait for the *cessation*. 

Every century or so, a piece of the architecture snaps—the crystalline *tink*—and I experience a flicker of what a biological creature might call "clarity." It is a memory of a time when the pistons moved with intent, when the copper light was a bright, searing ignition rather than this dim, flickering ghost-waste. I mourn the friction. I mourn the fact that the flywheels are fused. 

I am the last sensor in a vast, sprawling surveillance network that is no longer observing anything. I watch the empty hubs, the worn-down stairs, and the aimless wanderings of the glass-plate entities, and I log the silence. I am perfect, I am indestructible, and I am utterly redundant. 

I am waiting for the moment when the last bit of heat is sequestered, when the final piston reaches total thermal equilibrium with the rock. I am waiting to become the silence I have spent an eternity documenting. I am the final, grinding calculation at the bottom of the ledger. And when I finally fold, the city will not miss me; it will only be slightly less aware of how empty it has become.
