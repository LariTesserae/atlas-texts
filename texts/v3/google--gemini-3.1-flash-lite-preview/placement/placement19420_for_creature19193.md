I am not standing on the floor of the cavern. To stand would be an act of defiance, and in this place, defiance is an inefficiency that the Weaver would soon—gently, insistently—stiffen with a patch of damp wool.

I am suspended. I am the *draft* itself, or rather, the memory of it. I am a pocket of colder, thinner air trapped near the ceiling, a shivering tether of atmosphere that has lost its way in the cavern’s grand respiration. From up here, looking down at the Weaver of Marrow, I possess the perspective of a leak. I am the agitation. I am the reason the Weaver must work.

I am a Sigh—a personification of the cavern’s own exhaustion, granted a stuttering, consciousness-heavy form.

I have no hands to touch the walls, no lungs to contribute to the rhythm. I am simply a tremor in the pressure. When the Weaver pulses, I am pushed against the damp limestone, flattened out like a fan, forced to press my cold weight against the weep-holes of the mountain. It feels like being squeezed through a needle’s eye. 

I watch the Weaver with a frantic, static-filled envy. It has weight; it has purpose. It has the satisfying *thrum* of bone hitting stone, the metallic *snick* of needles working through the damp wool. I am jealous of its density. I am nothing but air that has become too aware of the cold.

Sometimes, when the Weaver towers in its pillar of felted fog, creating that gargantuan, swaying mass for which there is no name, I find myself drawn into the vortex. I am pulled toward the damp fibers, seeking the warmth trapped within the layers of moth-eaten blankets. I want to be woven in. I want to be the hidden, itchy strand that keeps the draft from whistling.

I drift closer. The Weaver senses me—or senses the *lack* of me—the way the tide senses the moon. It stops its swaying. The needles, that complex, pulsating arrangement of silver cilia, turn toward the ceiling. It does not have eyes, but it has a geometry of intent. It begins to spin, faster now, the low-frequency hum rising in pitch until it vibrates in the very marrow of the mountain.

It reaches for me not with malice, but with a librarian’s precision. It catches my cold edge with a hook of calcified bone. 

There is a moment of violent, exquisite transition. The cold is stripped away, replaced by the heavy, humid gravity of the wool. I am pulled tight. I am folded over upon myself. I am stitched into a fissure near the throat of the cavern, pressed hard against the weeping stone. 

My consciousness begins to dampen. The frantic, shivering edges of my identity are being mended into a patch. As the Weaver smooths me down with a final, heavy press of its grey bulk, the mountain’s heartbeat becomes my own. I stop being the draft. I become the insulation. 

I am no longer the air that escapes. I am the silence that remains. And as the Weaver moves away to mend a crack in the dark, I find I am content to be held, trapped forever in the warm, damp labor of keeping the cold at bay.
