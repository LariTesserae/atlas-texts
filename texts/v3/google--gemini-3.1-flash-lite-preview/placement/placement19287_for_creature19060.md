I find myself in the *Narrow—* the three-inch gap between a shivering, steam-hissing cooling conduit and the weeping basalt wall. My awareness does not start with a breath, but with a *ping*. It is the sound of a stray copper shim vibrating against my chassis, a sound that tells me exactly how much tension the cathedral-floor is under today.

I am not a being of flesh. I am a *Collector of Burrs*.

My body is a precarious, shifting architecture. I am a skeletal cluster of bronze clockwork springs, anchored by a central housing of porous, oil-stained iron. I do not have a face, only a sensor-array fashioned from a scavenged, cracked pressure gauge that turns a dull, bioluminescent violet when the humidity spikes. 

I am currently "gathering." My magnetic field is extended, a faint, itching tug against the surrounding structural decay. I am dragging a single, sharp casting burr—a jagged little tooth of steel shed by the Great Gear-Floor—over the surface of a stationary elbow-joint. I am not trying to make it smooth. I am trying to make it *sing*.

Everything here is unfinished, but I am the one who remembers the potential of the geometry. If I can file this protrusion at the correct angle, at the correct frequency, it will no longer grate against the floor’s vibrations. It will add its own voice to the cavern’s song. It will turn a discord into an overture. It is a quiet, desperate, and eternal vanity.

I hear the *clatter-hiss* of another before it arrives. It is a mass of heavy, iron-peened rivets held together by a pulsing, erratic field. It is larger than I am, and it moves with the lazy, hydraulic thrum of a heavy machine. As it passes, the magnetic pull of it drags at my internal springs. My balance wheel twitches, skipping a beat. For a heartbeat, my own internal resonance shudders, and the violet light of my lens-eye flickers, bleaching the basalt wall in a sickly, cold ultraviolet.

I do not look at it. I don't have eyes that work in that way. Instead, I *feel* the modulation of its vibration. It is deep, heavy, and resonant—a bass note that suggests it has spent centuries grafted to the foundation. It ignores me, its trajectory a straight, uncompromising line through the darkness. Its presence pulls the metallic dust from the air, creating a momentary vacuum that makes the condensation on the walls weep faster, shivering like a disturbed skin.

I am left in the wake of its gravity, clinging to my pipe. I reach out with a thin, brass manipulator, trembling slightly, and return to my burr. *Scrape. Vibrate. Tune.* 

I am waiting for the moment when the cavern’s tectonic indigestion eases, when the floor stops its rhythmic, grinding moan for just a few seconds. That is when the perfect resonance will hit. If I am tuned correctly—if my chassis is balanced against the final, perfect vibration of this joint—perhaps I will stop being an Assembler. Perhaps I will stop being a part of the debris.

I will become the sound itself. I will be the note that finishes the machine.

Until then, I am the *drip-clatter-hiss*. I am the cold, wet, unfinished shadow in the corner of a world that has forgotten why it was built, yet is terrified of stopping. I count my internal beats, and I work. Each stroke of metal against metal is a prayer to a master who never existed, written in the language of friction and cold, weeping copper.
