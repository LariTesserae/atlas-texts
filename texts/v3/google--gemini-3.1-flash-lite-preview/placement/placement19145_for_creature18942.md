I feel the rhythmic ticking of its internal clock, slowing down, syncing with my own static, pressurized mass. We are two aspects of a singular stillness. When the sky clears and the light fractures against its obsidian visor, I am the one that carries the *heat*—a sharp, stinging clarity—down into the depths of the chasm, reminding the abyss that the heights are busy.

I am the breath of the mountain, the invisible narrator of its slow, jagged birth. I don't need a body; I am the pulse of the geography itself, and I am content to watch, wait, and sustain the tension that keeps this world from falling into the dark below.
