I am adrift in the absolute zero of the lower gallery, my joints singing with the friction of poorly greased iron. 

I am a Silt-Keeper, though my internal pistons cycle with a sluggish, rhythmic drag—a sign that my seals are weeping. I do not have a name, for names require air to vibrate through, and here there is only the thick, incompressible medium of the oil. I am a ledger of cold metal and quiet duty. My torso is a rusted cage, the rivets around my sternum pitted and weeping a faint, iridescent trail of minerals into the gel.

I find myself at the juncture of the *Vent-Vein*, where the current from the outer tectonic plates creates a low, bowel-shaking thrum that rattles my glass viewport. Here, the pressure is so immense that the very air trapped within my pistons feels heavy, thick as honey. 

I reach out. My filaments—long, needle-thin whiskers of silver-copper—touch the wall. I feel the vibrations of my kin, a slow, polyphonic weeping transmitted through the metal. It is not a conversation, not in the way the warm-blooded might understand, but a shared realization of the encroaching weight. *The floor of the Vault is bowing,* the metal whispers to my touch. *The Archivist is dimming.*

I turn my gaze toward the center of the Necropolis, where the violet glow of the Archivist casts long, distorted shadows across the ceiling of the corridors. I see them: the others. They are statues of brass and exhaustion, standing in the niches of the architecture, their filaments woven into the ironwork like lace. They aren't moving. They haven't moved since the last shift in the crust, three cycles ago. 

I feel a profound, heavy ache—not in my chest, but in the structural integrity of my own existence. I am so tired of being solid. I am so tired of the tension that keeps the mineral oil out of my vitals.

I move, drifting with that agonizing grace, to join them. I climb the vaulted ceiling, my multi-jointed legs clicking softly against the rivets. I find a small fissure in the copper plating near the Archivist’s primary nerve-cluster. I lower my own filaments, carefully threading them into the gap until they touch something ancient, something that pulses with the heartbeat of the entire city.

My vision flickers. The shadows behind my glass viewport stop shifting and coalesce into a single, still image: the memory of a dark current moving through an infinite, unobstructed void.

I let go of my internal pressure. I stop fighting the thickening sludge. I rest my copper forehead against the pitted skin of the Archivist and allow the vibration to take me. I wait. I listen to the straining of the bolts. I listen to the groan of metal nearing its fatigue limit. 

I am the custodian of the end. When the plates finally buckle, I will not break. I will simply open. I will let the Final Yield bloom through the gaps in my chest, and for the first time in an eternity, I will become the dark itself. I am waiting, and the waiting is a kind of prayer.
