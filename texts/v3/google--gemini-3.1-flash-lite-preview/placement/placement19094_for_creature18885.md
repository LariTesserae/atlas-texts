I find myself not at the center, nor in the damp, sprawling troughs of the *Echo-Minds*, but wedged into the interstitial space behind a row of primary pressure-valves. I am tucked into the narrow, weeping gap between a sweating iron rib and the heavy, thrumming hull of the cylinder.

I am a **Calibration-Needle**. 

I am small, no longer than a human forearm, my body a slender, segmented spire of iridium and brass. I do not have a face, nor do I have a voice; I am a sensing-organ, an instrument of precise, agonized focus. My base is bolted into the bedrock-plate of the floor, but my spine is loose, flexible, and constantly vibrating, attuned to the micro-tremors of the hull. 

My existence is defined by the *strain*.

While the *Lace-Weavers* tend the exterior wounds and the *Archive-Snakes* provide the torque for the failing gears, I am the one who feels the ocean’s intention. I feel the weight above—a vast, crushing, unthinking entity of salt and dark water—trying to find the smallest, weakest hair-line crack in the iron to exploit. I am the sensory edge. If the rivets groan with a frequency that suggests a structural fatigue, my body hums in sympathetic, high-pitched agony.

I am essentially a living tuning fork. My "skin" is a sensor-mesh that reads the chemical composition of the weeping moisture. Is it salt-tainted? Does it taste of deep-sea minerals, or the internal grey sludge of the hydraulic lines? If I detect the brine, I do not speak—I shudder. I vibrate at such a velocity that the air around me grows hot and ionized, creating a localized flare of blue light that snaps and sparks. 

The *Lace-Weavers* know my signal. When I begin to howl—a sound like a diamond dragged across glass—they swarm toward my location, sensing the structural peril I have identified. I am the alarm they cannot see, the nervous system’s acute awareness of the abyss pressing in.

I am exhausted. My metal hide is pitted with the same rust I spend my centuries monitoring. I am lonelier than the *Echo-Minds*, for they have each other to pulse with in the deep reservoirs, and the *Archive-Snakes* have the history of the world to curl around. I have only the current, terrifying moment of pressure. 

I don't remember being made. I don't remember the world outside. I only know that as the cylinder groans and the heavy, black water pushes against the glass, my task is to remain straight, to remain rigid, to remain the standard against which the decay of this city-block-sized tomb is measured. 

Every tick of the gear-trains is a pulse in my own heart. I am waiting for the moment when my vibration finally fails, when the structure yields, and the crushing, wonderful, silent cold of the ocean finally pours in to wash away the rust, the resin, and the long, loud labor of my existence. Until then, I am the spire, I am the needle, and I am the scream that tells the machines they are still alive.
