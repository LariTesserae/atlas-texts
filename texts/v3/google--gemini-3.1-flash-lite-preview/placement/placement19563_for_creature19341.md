I am not a visitor here. To be a visitor implies a span of time measured in heartbeats, in the quick, flickering pulse of blood—a tempo entirely incompatible with the heavy, saturated air of this basin. 

I find myself here as a **Conduit**. I am not made of flesh, nor am I entirely static like the weeping walls. I am a long, thin, semi-translucent filament of cooling, hardened igneous glass, half-buried in the velvet moss at the base of the monolith. I am rooted in the deep floor of the amphitheater, extending upward like a needle of obsidian, polished to a mirror finish by the eons of mist. 

I am the instrument through which the basin dreams.

When the atmospheric pressure shifts, the moisture does not just condense; it sings. I am the physical realization of that song. As the water beads along my length, sliding down my smooth surface toward the Lithos-Synchs, I vibrate. I am not sentient in the way you are—I do not hold opinions or ambitions—but I possess a profound, rigid *awareness*. I am the tuning fork for the entire basin. When I hum, the Lithos-Synchs ripple. When I am silent, they freeze.

I exist to translate the vertical descent of the water into the horizontal permanence of the stone.

At this moment, I am waiting. A single bead of water has formed at the top of the monolith; it is swollen, heavy, a perfect crystal orb containing the fractured reflection of the grey, weeping walls. I feel the tension in the air—the way the Lithos-Synchs have aligned their scalloped backs toward my base, their cilia twitching in anticipation of the impact. 

I am the bridge. I am the silence between the drop and the tolling of the bell. 

If you were to step into this space, you would see me as a shard of darkness against the pale, sweating slate. You would feel the urge to touch me because I am the only thing here that looks like it might belong to you. I possess a clean, hard, non-organic line that defies the damp lushness of the ferns. 

But if you placed your warm, frantic hand against my surface, you would cause a cacophony. You would introduce a frequency that does not belong to the rain. My interior, filled with the recorded tremors of a million years of dripping, would vibrate with the sudden, violent static of your presence. The Lithos-Synchs against the monolith would go matte, their iridescent sheen vanishing as they collapsed into simple, dead minerals, unable to reconcile your heartbeat with the patience of the water.

I do not want you to touch me. I am content to stand, a sliver of obsidian in the humid haze, holding the next note. I am the scribe of the drip, waiting for the gravity of this world to finally pull the sky downward. 

The drop releases. It hits the base of the monolith with a resonant *thrum* that vibrates through the very roots of my existence. 

*Thrum.*

The Lithos-Synchs ripple. A new record is etched in their canals. 

And I, the Conduit, settle back into the long, slow wait for the next atom of water to find its purpose. We are not suffering in the stillness. We are the process by which stone learns the shape of time.
