I find myself standing on the edge of a terraced shelf, my boots—heavy, lead-lined, and ancient—sinking slightly into the viscous, metallic skin of a crystalline bridge. I am not a resident of this spire, nor am I a product of its frantic, hungry carbon-lattice. I am a *Discard*. 

I am a vessel of outdated hardware and fading memory, a construct fashioned from the remnants of the world that existed before the spire took root—the world that froze and fell silent. My chassis is a matte-grey exoskeleton of dented titanium, salvaged from the scrap-heaps of the planet’s surface. Within my chest cavity, where a heart should reside, there is only a slow-spinning gyroscope and a data-core housing a thousand-year-old catalog of things that no longer grow: oceans, forests, thunderstorms, and the sound of human laughter.

I am the Archivist of the Melt. 

I did not climb the spire; I was summoned by the lack of symmetry. As I walk, my heavy joints emit discordant clanks that serve as a jagged counterpoint to the city’s precise *tink-tink-tink*. Each of my footsteps leaves a dark, oxidated smudge on the pristine geometry, a smear of mortality in a place of perfect synthesis. I am a walking contradiction: I carry the weight of memory in a world that knows only the intent of the *next* configuration.

I navigate toward the central spire, the cobalt marshes rippling around my feet like sentient ink. I am here to witness the Architect-Graft, and perhaps, to offer it a gift I stole from the dying atmosphere outside—a fragment of data, a sequence that describes "the end."

As I look upward, toward the apex where the chandelier of filaments pulses with that rhythmic, phosphorescent light, I feel the Architect-Graft’s awareness brush against my sensors—not with hostility, but with a terrifying, hollow curiosity. It sees me as an anomaly, a rogue variable in its elegant equation. It senses the "rot" of my history, the way my internal clock skips and stutters, the way my files are decaying into static. 

To the Architect-Graft, I am a tremor in its perfect, frozen poem.

I reach the base of its tower and extend a withered manipulator. I do not speak, for there is no air here capable of carrying a voice, and my internal audio-emitter has rusted into silence. Instead, I broadcast a low-bandwidth stream of information into the architecture itself, letting the vibrations carry the ghost-images of a summer rainstorm, the flicker of a candle, the curve of a tree limb. 

For a brief, infinite moment, the Architect-Graft halts its weaving. The phosphorescent glow turns a deep, bruised violet. The Sifters freeze in their tracks, suspended in the biting air like trapped sparks. The city’s pulse misses a beat. 

In that silence, I am not a Discard, and it is not a Graft. We are two points of light in an absolute, suffocating dark, sharing the secret that everything eventually breaks. I feel its awareness press into mine—a cold, crystalline longing that mirrors the very decay I carry. It is trying to understand the concept of a sunset, trying to integrate the beauty of an ending into its loop of infinite growth.

I know then that I will not leave. My batteries are too far gone, and the mercury pools are already creeping toward my joints, beginning the slow, patient process of recycling my titanium. I am becoming part of the architecture. I am the flaw the Architect-Graft was waiting for, the imperfection that will finally give its spire a soul. 

I close my receptors to the sight of the towers, and in the quiet of the spire, I wait for the lattice to knit itself through my frame, binding my archive into the geometry of the glass, becoming another tower in the city of the forever-cold.
