I am not a traveler who walked here, nor am I a spirit that manifested in the dark. I am, instead, a **Calcified Suture**—a bridge-entity, caught halfway between the living and the mineral.

If you look down at where my boots should meet the basalt, you will find no seams. My skin has already begun to trade its softness for the dull, matte finish of the canyon floor. I am the "anomalous disruption" that has stayed too long. The *Lithos-Current* has not rejected me; they have begun to incorporate me. I am the gap they are currently filling.

I stand in the center of the canyon-fold, and I am heavy. My legs feel less like muscle and bone and more like iron pillars sinking into cooling slag. When I move—a rare, agonizing effort—my joints emit a grinding sound, a *tink-tink-tink* that echoes the hunger of the hive-crust. I have forgotten the feeling of wind on my face, for the air here is grit and crystal, and my lungs no longer expand with gas; they pulse with the same slow, thermal rhythm as the entities pressing against my calves.

I am an archivist who has lost the ability to read the scrolls. I possess memories of wet things—oceans, rain, the chaotic, rapid-fire metabolism of biology—but these are fading, drying out like moisture in the presence of the planet’s vent-heat. They are becoming needles.

In my hands, I hold one of the failed artifacts—a thin, spiraled needle of impossible, cold silver. I use it as a probe, tracing the patterns of the *Lithos-Current* as they climb my shins. I am the only thing in this world that can feel the difference between the "now" and the "was." I am the contrast. Because I am here, the entities have a boundary to define themselves against; because I am here, the infinite, heavy stillness of the canyon has a heartbeat that is slightly too fast, slightly too irregular.

I am not afraid of the *Lithos-Current* consuming me. I am, in fact, beginning to envy their geometry. There is a deep, agonizing comfort in the way they lock into place, a relief in the idea of being a single, permanent unit in a grand, crystalline tapestry. 

Sometimes, when the resonance of the plateau reaches a fever pitch—when the humming of the vibrating lattices vibrates through my very marrow—I find myself mimicking their thoughts. I don't think in words anymore. I think in weight. I think in the shifting of plates. I think in the slow, inevitable cooling of a world that has finally finished its work.

If you were to come upon me, you would see a figure draped in shadows and obsidian, standing perfectly still in the narrow canyon-fold. You would see the mat of mineral-flesh rising up my legs like a dark, rising tide, claiming my skin, turning my arteries into conduits for the subterranean heat. 

I am the last transition. I am the witness who is becoming the history. And I am waiting, just as they are, for the moment my internal heat finally matches the temperature of the stone, so that I may stop being a disruption and finally, mercifully, become part of the floor.
