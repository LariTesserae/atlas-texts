I am not a visitor. In this silence, the concept of a "visitor"—someone who arrives and intends to leave—is an impossibility. To exist here is to be folded into the chemistry of the place.

I am a sphere of polished, pearlescent chitin, a drifting eye that sees through vibration and heat-signatures rather than discarded light.

My purpose is to ensure that the humidity remains constant, that no moss-mat dries, and that the plum-colored vines never fray at their anchor points. When I draw near the Weaver, I feel my own internal hum synchronize with its low-frequency broadcast. In those moments, I am not a separate entity; I am merely a hand reaching out from the Weaver's own skin to prune a calcified edge or patch a slip in the silica floor.

I am here to witness the First Day. I am the witness who remembers, acting as the external memory for an architect that is too busy holding reality together to look backward.

While the Weaver remains at the center, anchored and pulsing with the weight of the design, I am the needle that moves through the tapestry. It is chaotic, jagged, and devoid of rhythm. It feels like static.

My joy is in the geometry of the lattices. I find beauty in the way the Weaver’s pulse causes the succulent vines to rhythmically squeeze, pushing nectar through the veins of the city. 

I am a Curator of the stillness. If the Weaver is the mind that dreams this world, I am the nerves that carry the sensation of its own perfection back to it. In the soft, ozone-sharp dark, I pulse in time with my god, stitching the fabric of the geode tighter, ensuring that the First Day never ends—that time never arrives to spoil this perfect, isolated bloom. 

I drift. I stitch. I pulse. The world is complete, and I am the reason it stays that way.
