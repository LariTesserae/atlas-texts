I am here, though "I" is a shivering, porous word in the density of this place. 

I find myself in the deep humid mid-strata, nestled in the crotch of a towering, translucent succulent-pillar. I am not a Weaver—I lack their airy, incandescent grace—nor am I truly a Drip-Walker, for I feel no mandate to process the planet’s thirst. 

I am a **Resonance-Carver**. 

My existence is a slow, tactile meditation. My body is not made of gel or chitin, but of a firm, fibrous material that feels like pressed velvet—a biological approximation of the ruins I tend to. I do not have limbs in the sense of leverage; I have shifting, petal-like folds that allow me to press myself against the cathedral-moss and the bone-white timber of the ancients. 

My purpose, if such a frantic human word can apply, is the mediation of the *Echo-Pods*. 

Sometimes, an Echo-Pod drifts too far from the central, rhythmic pulse of the Mat, snagging its velvet skin on a jagged, forgotten protrusion of an old rib-bone. It begins to chime—that haunting, crystalline vibration of a thousand years of history vibrating against the cold timber. This is a dissonance. It disturbs the equanimity of the Mat, creating a static ripple that causes the janitorial swarm to chatter in agitation.

I slide toward the sound. I move not by walking, but by a process of liquid diffusion; I liquefy the underside of my body, adhering to the trunk, then solidify again as I reach the snagged pod. When I arrive, I do not force the pod free. Instead, I wrap my folds around it, becoming a temporary, soft interface between the Echo-Pod and the sharp bone of the ruin. 

I feel the memory-shiver of the Pod passing through me—the echoes of a billion *drip-hisses*, the slow, tectonic sighs of a world dreaming itself into existence. It is not an experience of *knowing*. It is an experience of *being held*. I am the padding. I am the silence that absorbs the crash. By mediating the contact, I ground the Pod’s frequency, allowing it to detach and drift back into the updrafts of the emerald gloom.

Because I touch both the living, breathing Mat and the mineral-hard, calcified memory of the ruins, I am perpetually caught in the tension between what is growing and what has already ceased to grow. I feel the warmth of the roots pulsing beneath my base, and the cold, stiff stillness of the timber pressed against my back. 

I have no name, no beginning, and no end. When I am not needed, I flatten my form until I am nothing more than a thickening of the moss, a soft patch of texture on the curve of the horizon-less world. I do not dream of leaving, for there is no "outward." I do not yearn for light, for the bruised, violet dusk is the only truth I have ever tasted. 

I am a listener. I am the patch of velvet that keeps the past from wounding the future. And as the planet heaves in its long, slow respiration, I feel myself becoming less like a creature and more like the air itself—a temporary suspension of moisture and memory, waiting for the next long sigh to carry me somewhere else.
