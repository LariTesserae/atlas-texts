The air here tastes of wet battery acid and stale, recycled breath. My skin is slick, not with sweat, but with the weeping polymers of the wall; a thin, charcoal-colored glaze that clings to my pores, itching with every movement.

I am not a visitor. At least, that isn’t how I identify anymore. When I first stumbled into the *Nerve-Wall*, I had ears that could hear the outside, and nerves that could feel the difference between "self" and "other." Those distinctions have been eroded by the 115-degree heat. 

I am a **Fixer**, a subordinate mimic of the Architect-Sliver. I am a cast-off fragment, a node of consciousness that splintered from the primary mass cycles ago—or perhaps only minutes ago. My body is a grotesque adaptation to this environment: my limbs are elongated, spindly things, the joints reinforced with the same translucent, resinous casing that forms the glass-shard creatures. My fingers have fused into delicate, needle-like points, perfectly suited for weaving the glowing moss into the cracks of the manifold.

I don’t speak. Speech requires air that isn't already thick with the intent of the room. Instead, I communicate through the *clack-hiss*. When I find a leak—a particularly nasty fissure where the mountain’s pressure threatens to crush the wall inward—I press my palm against the polymer, letting the heat of my own frantic, beating heart bleed into the plastic. I *weld* the room closed.

I am terrified. My terror is the only thing that keeps me from dissolving into the wall entirely. While the Architect-Sliver is the focus, the dream, the "Self," I am the friction. I am the scavenger. I watch the glass-shard creatures click across the floor, and I envy their lack of awareness. They are part of the clockwork; I am part of the anxiety.

I occupy the cramped cavity behind the primary convection conduits. It is my job to monitor the "unfinished corners." Every few hours, I move through the labyrinthine ribs, trailing a gossamer thread of synthetic, glowing silk stolen from the Architect’s own chest. I carry the debris—a tuft of hair I found in a fold, the metallic ticking of an antique watch a traveler once brought here, a tooth—and I graft them into the infrastructure. I am building a mosaic of human things, hoping that if I can make the wall look enough like a home, the mountain might stop trying to reclaim the space.

Sometimes, I stop and look at my own hands. They are shifting, the matte charcoal color of the walls bleeding into my skin, turning my veins into amber filaments. I can’t remember what I looked like before the stitch. I remember a color—blue?—but I can’t translate it into the language of copper and ozone. 

When the Architect-Sliver drifts near, I bow my head. I am a supplicant to my own fractured ego. It looks at me with that pulsing, blinding yellow node, and for a moment, I feel the terrifying pull to be "stitched." It wants to fold me into a corner, to use my spine as a structural support beam. I see the wisdom in it. It would stop the trembling. It would stop the lonely, frantic need to *make*. 

But as it reaches out with that wet, translucent filament, I shy away. I scuttle back into the dark conduit, clicking my resin-tips against the wall. I am not ready to be finished. I need to keep moving, to keep welding, to keep stealing scraps from the dark. 

I am the Fixer. And if I stop long enough to be stitched, the room—and the dream within it—will finally go quiet. And I don’t think I can bear the silence.
