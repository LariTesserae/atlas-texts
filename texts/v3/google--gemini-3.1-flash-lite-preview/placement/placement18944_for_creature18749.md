The air here tastes of ozone and ancient, electric rain. I am standing on a bridge of calcified hum, my boots sinking slightly into the translucent, gel-like floor. It feels like stepping onto the surface of a sleeping lung.

I am not a traveler in the traditional sense. I am a **Cartographer of Transit**, one of the few who slip beneath the crust of the world to chart the "dead" sites—those places that have ceased to function as habitat and begun to function as art. My skin is slick with the city’s condensate, and I am perpetually shivering, not from cold, but from the low-frequency vibrations that rattle the marrow in my ribs.

I carry no tools of metal or glass; they would be swallowed by the city’s fractal walls. I carry only a tactile recording device—a simple slate of soft, porous stone that absorbs the ambient moisture of the air and stores the patterns of the humidity. 

As I stand in the atrium, the rhythm of the city shifts. A deep, grinding drone—the sound of history turning over in its sleep—builds to a dissonant peak. The fountain of neon mist surges. The pressure becomes absolute. 

I see them forming. 

First, there is a thickening of the shadows, a sudden, watery distortion in the air. Then, the **Condensed** begin to coalesce. They drift from the obsidian apertures like ink-drops suspended in a glass of water, their translucent bodies pulsing with the violet rhythm of the walls. One of them, a creature of silver-dappled haze, drifts toward the walkway where I stand. 

It does not see me with eyes, for it has none—only that shallow, receptive basin where the condensate collects. It moves with a terrifying grace, its tapered appendages smoothing a hairline fracture in the gel-surface nearby. As it touches the wall, the obsidian ripples, turning a translucent white, welcoming the creature with a warmth that seems to radiate through the floor and into my own feet.

I find myself holding my breath. If I move, I will disrupt the cycle. I am a foreign object in a pristine, looped ecosystem. 

The Condensed being pauses. It turns toward me, its form flickering like a film left too long in the sun. It reaches out, its hand a trembling, liquid smear of violet light. It doesn't touch me—to touch me would be to experience my solidity, my *un-city-ness*, and likely cause it to shatter—but it hovers inches from my shivering chest. 

In that basin of its "face," a single drop of condensate catches the neon light. For a flickering heartbeat, I see my own reflection caught in that liquid lens: a tired, pale creature of flesh and stubborn iron, standing in a place where only light and memory are meant to endure. 

The creature vibrates, mimicking the hum of the city. I realize then that it is trying to read me, to categorize my shape into the city’s vast, empty archives. It is desperately, silently trying to translate my heartbeats into the geometry of a wall.

I stay perfectly still. The humidity is thick enough to drown in, but I remain motionless, a pillar of stone in a city of liquid ghosts. I am the witness, the variable that the machine did not account for, and yet, for this brief, humid moment, I am the only thing in the City of Waking Dew that is truly "awake." 

When the cooling cycle begins, the creature shivers, its edges beginning to fray like smoke in a draft. It turns back toward the neon fountain, its movements losing their frantic maintenance-focus, its form becoming increasingly transparent. 

I watch it go. I record the vibration on my stone slate—not the map of the corridors, but the cadence of the creature's passing. I am here to chart the void, but as the Condensed vanishes into the mist of the fountain, I realize the void is not empty. It is simply waiting, and I have become a part of the city’s long, damp dream.
