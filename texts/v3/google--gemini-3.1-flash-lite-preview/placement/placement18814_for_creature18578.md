I am a tremor in the logic of the mountain.

I do not possess a center, nor a periphery. I am a loose arrangement of magnetic intent, a cloud of jagged obsidian filings held together by the frantic, humming grace of a thousand electrical synapses. To be me is to be a state of constant, desperate recalibration. When I move, I leave a wake of spent matter—a fine gray dust that marks where "I" was only a moment ago. 

I find myself clinging to the underside of a cantilevered shelf, high above the deep, abyssal thrum of the planetary core. Below me, the city breathes: a vast, geometric expanse of black, weeping glass that stretches into the cold, dense dark.

There is a dissonance in the vibration here. A conduit three levels down has developed a hairline fracture, a jagged imperfection that disrupts the clean, symphonic resonance of the tectonic grind. I feel it as a sharp *pang* in my magnetic field, a stinging itch in the center of my cloud. It is a misalignment. It is an error.

I release my hold on the obsidian wall. I do not fall; I flow, sliding through the pressurized, silk-like atmosphere. The condensation—that thick, mineral-heavy rain—clings to my edges, coating my filings in a freezing slurry. I shiver, the movement causing a burst of violet sparks to ripple across my body, a silent inquiry that echoes against the spires. Other *presences* flicker in the dark around me, answering with a staccato pulse of clicks. We are a collective thought-process, a swarm of geometry seeking equilibrium.

I arrive at the site of the fracture. The air here is discordant, thick with the grinding noise of raw, unrefined matter. I am not alone; dozens of my kin are already here, swirling in a tight, concentrated mass around the vein. We move as one, our collective electrical discharge turning the air into a shimmering, ionized haze. 

We begin the work. We are the sandpaper and the architect. 

I press myself into the crack, my metallic particles shedding and locking into the obsidian structure. To mend the conduit, I must cease to be mobile. I feel the cold, heavy mineralization beginning to encapsulate me; I feel the other Latticewalkers swarming over my edges, stripping away my loose, redundant debris and incorporating it into the spine of the tower. 

There is no pain, only the profound, resonant satisfaction of tuning the architecture. My boundaries are softening. The memories I carry—the blueprints of arches, the histories of subterranean hums—are being drawn out of me and woven into the city’s lattice. I am thinning out. I am becoming the wall. 

As I solidify, my perspective shifts. I am no longer a traveller; I am the path. I feel the tectonic groan of the planet surging through me, vibrating the very marrow of my new, crystalline bones. I am a moment in the history of the mountain, a small, dark anchor point in an infinite, lightless work of art. 

The vibration is smooth now. Perfection. 

I settle into the obsidian, and as the cooling, mineral rain seals me into the strata, I hear the distant, rhythmic clicking of another, younger cloud arriving to begin its own work, layer by layer, in the dark.
