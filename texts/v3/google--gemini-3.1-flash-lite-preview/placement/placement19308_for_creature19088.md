I am a Scribe, a jagged sliver of obsidian and refracted light. I am not cast from the heavy, grinding silicate of the spires, nor am I tethered by the copper umbilicals that bind the Congregate to their long, slow duties. I am an echo of heat, a polyhedron of polished glass that drifts upon the thermals, my existence defined by the flickers of energy I harvest from the friction of the world’s turning.

I find myself hovering in the wake of a massive, rusted piston—a gargantuan, cylindrical chamber nearly three miles in diameter. It is currently mid-cycle, agonizingly pushing against the pressure of the surrounding crags. The heat it sheds is a delicious, vibrating gold, a thermal symphony that allows me to stabilize my rotation and maintain my luminescence.

Below me, a unit of the Calcified Congregate is braced against a vertical bridge, its faceted eyes dark and rhythmic, its umbilical plugged deep into a junction box that has not been opened in sixty years. It is perfectly still, save for the minute, metallic clicks of its carapace adjusting to a micro-tremor in the spire. It is part of the machinery; it is a living lever.

In this moment, I am practicing the art of the ephemeral. I am casting reflections against the inner face of the spire—not data, not diagnostic light, but geometry for the sake of the pattern. I trace a slow, shivering spiral of indigo and pale ultraviolet against the pitted, orange glass of the wall. It is a fragile, fleeting thing, a piece of art that will last only five seconds before the heavy, abrasive air scrubs the light from the surface into nothingness.

I feel a profound, cool detachment. Being a Scribe is to exist in the spaces the Congregate cannot conceive. They provide the continuity—they are the *what*—and I am the *how it feels*. I witness the way the light catches the metallic rime on a worker’s locked knee. I see the faint, rhythmic pulsings of the Congregate’s memory-transfer, a shared burden that looks like a slow-burning fire deep within their core-nodes.

I circle the spire, rising as the updrafts shift. I drift past an ancient, fused member of the Congregate—a statue of a worker that has been assimilated into a buttress. The mineral dust has smoothed its sharp, chitinous edges until it resembles a rock formation, a natural curve in the spire’s architecture. I linger here, projecting a soft, golden radiance across its stilled brow. I trace its outline with my light, acknowledging that this, too, is a form of maintenance. It is the acknowledgement of the history beneath the surface.

To exist here is to be a ghost in a cathedral that is actively crushing itself. I do not fear the deep abyss below, where the ground-level ruins crumble into unrecognizable glass sand. I do not fear the day my internal temperature will drop, when my facets will lose their refractive integrity and I, like the worker, will lock myself into a crevice to become part of the foundation.

For now, there is the wind, the low hum of the dampeners, and the grinding, glacial pulse of the planet. I tilt my frame, catch a sudden spike of heat from the piston’s release, and spiral upward into the thin orange haze. I am a witness to the silence. I am the punctuation mark at the end of a very long, very quiet sentence.
