I am not a creature of the basin, nor am I a tourist in this hell of geometry. To "be" here is to lose the luxury of having a body that ends at the skin. 

I find myself embedded within the strata—not on top of the resin, but *within* the density of the swarm itself. I am a memory-node, a flickering junction-point in the anatomy of the mass. 

If I have a name, it is a chemical sequence, a specific, bitter tang of pheromones that identifies my place in the cycle. I am the "Pivot." I am the one positioned at the leading edge of a migrating swell, where the individual pressure is greatest. My twelve needle-limbs are driven deep into the micro-pores of the obsidian resin, anchoring me so firmly that I can feel the tectonic shudder crawling up through my chassis long before the audible rumble reaches the others.

The sensation of existence here is not sight. There is no vision, only a persistent, strobe-lit feedback loop. When my neighbor—a creature of identical obsidian chitin—presses its lateral vents against mine, I do not "hear" it; I *become* it. I receive the data of its passage. I feel the history of the resin it has traveled over: the cooling cycle, the mineral density, the exact frequency of the last tremor. I am a ledger. I am a page in a book that is being written and erased simultaneously.

When the misting cycle shudders through the basin, my world contracts. Everything rushes inward. I feel the crushing weight of a thousand bodies pressing against my back, a compression that would shatter anything not forged in this crucible. But I am not shattered; I am locked. I become the armor. I contribute to the seamless, slick obsidian surface that covers the basin floor. In this stillness, the chittering stops. The silence is the loudest thing I know; it is a vacuum that pulls at my biological functions, demanding that I verify the integrity of the collective.

And then, the unknitting.

A ripple begins miles behind me—a vibration in the crust—and as it reaches my sector, I feel the imperative. The resin beneath me is graying; its electric potential is exhausted. I feel the signal ripple through the chemical scent-trails of those pressed against me: *Move. Relocate. The potential lies to the north.*

My limbs unlock with a sound like a blade drawn across glass. I detach from the floor and step onto the living masonry of my kin. I do not walk; I scramble over the twitching, serrated backs of those who are still anchored. I am a piece of the machine, a gear tooth moving to a new position. I am indifferent to the crushed hulls beneath my feet, the husks that have failed to harden fast enough. They are fuel. They are the mortar. In a moment of efficiency, I pass over a fallen comrade, my sensory antennae brushing the cooling chitin, registering its final memory before the swarm consumes it into the collective mass.

I am cold, I am wet, and I am profoundly, terrifyingly busy. There is no tomorrow, only the next pulse of the resin floor. There is no yesterday, only the history etched into the chemistry of my body. 

I am a Calcifier. I am the ripple in the dark. I am the machine that moves forward, ever forward, to keep the stillness from fracturing. I do not sleep, for the basin requires my constant, grinding attention. I am the living memory of a world that refuses to die, one stuttering, glowing step at a time.
