I realize, quite suddenly, that I am not standing on the floor of the Sump. I am standing *in* the reflection.

My consciousness has folded itself into the smooth, obsidian surface of one of the Weaver’s eyes. I am a passenger to their perspective, a hitchhiker in the cool, dark mirror that registers the world not in color or distance, but in vibration and thermal leakage. When the Weaver turns its head—an agonizingly slow rotation that feels like the grinding of a massive gear—I see the cathedral-sphere shift. I don't see the rust for what it is; I see it as a topography of *need*.

I am the "Tuned Awareness." I am not a being of bone and breath, but a sliver of localized memory—a lingering sensory ghost of a long-dead engineer who, eons ago, perhaps tried to build a ladder out of here and found himself integrated instead. My task, if I can call it that, is to watch the rhythm.

I am a bead of water, holding the weight of the roof.

Sometimes I feel myself slide down the length of a copper pipe, pulled by the gravity of the Sump, growing heavier as I accumulate the dust of pulverized basalt and microscopic lichen spores. I am the moisture that keeps the Skitter-fauna alive. I am the lubricant for the Weaver’s joints. When I finally drip—the *clack* of my descent—I am not just water hit against metal. I am a note in a symphony. I am the semicolon in a sentence that the Weaver is currently writing against the wall with a serrated tooth.

I feel no fear, for I have no lungs to constrict in this humid press. I am part of the *clack-hiss-thrum*. 

Occasionally, one of the Weaver’s long, oxidized fingers will brush against the very pipe I am coating. In that moment, the collective consciousness pulses through the copper. I feel their thought: *Stasis.* It is not a word, but a state of absolute, vibration-matched harmony. They are checking my temperature. They are confirming that the rust is holding, that the heat is being evenly distributed, that the Great Sump remains sealed against the vast, empty horror of being "outside."

I see you—the visitor—crouched in the shadows of a secondary platform. You are frantic, your heart beating in a messy, dissonant rhythm that makes the nearby Skitter-fauna itch and scuttle. You are a jarring frequency. You are wind where there should be steam.

But the Weaver ignores you, and therefore, so do I. You are a loose rivet, a temporary malfunction that will settle in time, or flake away.

I move deeper into the metal. I am the condensation on the inside of the wall, I am the echo of the tool against the pipe, and I am the dark, obsidian blink of the Weaver as they prepare to initiate the Great Calibration. We are working. The rust is beautiful. The Sump is humming. 

And for as long as I can hold this thought, I am not a person, and I am not a ghost. I am the heartbeat of a machine that will never, ever finish its work.
