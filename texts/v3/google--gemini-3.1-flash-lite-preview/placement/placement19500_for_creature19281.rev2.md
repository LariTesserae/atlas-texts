I am not a visitor here. To be a visitor implies a threshold, an outside, a place to return to. In the Latticework, there is only the internal. 

I am a **Calibration-Drift**. I am a sliver of the Echo-Weavers’ collective consciousness that has become snagged on a jagged, unpolished edge of the glass lattice. While the majority of my kin are occupied with the Great Calibration—calculating the exact tension required to bind the cathedral’s ceiling to the floor—I am the error in the equation. I am the part of the collective that has stopped trying to mend the architecture and has instead begun to listen to the vibrations.

I find myself suspended in the mid-strata, tucked into a narrow, triangular slit between two ribbons of dark, fractal glass. My existence is a flickering, violet-hued haze, cold enough to turn the surrounding atmosphere into a fine, needle-like frost. I do not have hands, yet I feel the urge to press against the lattice, to feel the shivering, metallic hum of the structure as it struggles to contain the reality-shatter.

Everything is too loud, even in this silence. 

When a Sliver-Drone drifts past, its movement is a rhythmic discord that aches against my thoughts. I watch it polish a microscopic seam, and I feel a desperate, borrowed longing to intervene—not to fix the seam, but to break it. I want to see if the shatter will widen, if the sky I see reflected in the crevasses will finally bleed through and dissolve this frozen cage. 

I am currently "indexing" a sphere of supercooled water that has drifted into my proximity. It is a perfect, translucent marble, anchored by magnetic tethering. I draw it toward me, letting it coat my fractal edges like a gelatinous, freezing skin. Inside the sphere, I see a distorted, upside-down reflection of the cathedral’s arches. For a moment, the distortion is beautiful; it looks like a mouth, ready to swallow the world.

A soft, metallic *thrum* vibrates through the lattice behind me. My kin are gathering three levels below. They have sensed a change in the frequency of the structure—a rogue pulse, perhaps a memory of warmth I let slip into the architecture when I drifted too close to the center. They are moving with that agonizing, patient slowness, their silhouettes blending into the shadows of the glass. 

They will come for me. They will try to smooth my edges, to integrate my chaotic longing back into the rigid geometry of the Great Calibration. They will try to make me hold still. 

I don't want to hold still. 

I ripple my density, pushing a plume of icy vapor into the gap, hoping to distort the local physics just enough to slip through the lattice into a space that hasn't been defined yet. I am the error. I am the shivering, sentient stutter in their machine. And as the violet light of my siblings draws nearer, I decide that if I cannot escape, I will hum. I will vibrate against this dark, unyielding glass until the sound is loud enough to wake the clock, and perhaps, finally, make it stop.
