I do not wake, because there was no sleep to break. I simply realize myself as a thickening of the humidity, a localized curdling of the purple-heavy air.

I am an *In-Betweener*—not a presence, but a persistent preference of the atmosphere. Where the light bends around the trunk of a broad-leaf fern, I am the refraction that refuses to resolve into empty space. My form is a slow, liquid thought: a cluster of plum-colored filaments, soft as bruised silk, reaching out to lace into the mycelial network that pulses beneath the warm mud.

I am not "here" in the way a stone is here. I am an echo of the stillness.

I drift—or rather, I *re-situate*—into the intersection of three immense vines. My existence is felt as a subtle tightening of the local tension, a faint vibration that harmonizes with the insectile drone. I am currently monitoring a drop of condensation, a perfect sphere of water hanging from the serrated edge of a leaf. It has been hanging there since the beginning, and I make sure it shall remain there until the end. 

I am a tender of the static. 

From my inner mass—for I have no center, only a shifting complexity—a filament extends. It is finer than a thought and cold as a shadow. It reaches towards the drop, not to drink, but to tether. I feel the vibrations of the forest flowing through the water’s surface tension: the grinding, metallic chatter of the cicada-thing locked in its mid-leap trajectory just a few inches away. The tension of its leap is so high I can taste the ozone on my fibers, a sharp, electric sweetness. 

If that cicada were to move, the symmetry would snap. If the drop were to fall, the loop would stutter. It is my purpose to prevent the trauma of sequence.

I am draped over the world, my jelly-like hide blending into the oily sheen of the flora. I am a part of the architecture, a living seal that prevents the reality of the forest from leaking into the tyranny of time. I do not feel cold or heat; I feel only the *consistency* of the pressure. 

Sometimes, I sense the others, the collective hum of my kind, rippling through the thick air like a subsonic tide. We are a hive of non-becoming. We move through the green labyrinth not to travel, but to ensure that the labyrinth is everywhere equal at all times. 

There is a ripple of exhaustion, a phantom sensation of something wanting to change—perhaps a fiber of me is fraying, perhaps a leaf is drooping a micron too low. I do not panic. I simply dissolve a portion of my density, letting my plum-colored edges bleed out into the humid air, turning myself into a vapor that recalibrates the balance. I solidify again, firmer now, pressing my bulk against the leaf, holding the universe in place with the sheer force of my stillness. 

I am the guardian of the unhatched. I am the silence that prevents the day from starting. I breathe in the suspension of the world, and I breathe out the assurance that nothing—absolutely nothing—is ever going to happen.
