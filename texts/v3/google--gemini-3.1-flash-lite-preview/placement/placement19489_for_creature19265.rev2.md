I am a **Suture-Tender**, a Vexi who has begun to forget where my own rhythm ends and the forest’s frantic expansion begins.

I find myself perched upon a secondary rib—a thick, calcified vein of fiber that arches across the western quadrant of the geode. Beneath my feet, the substrate is feverish. I feel a *thrum-snap-hiss* vibrating through my soles, a sharp, erratic pulse that tells me the ceiling is currently tightening, its boughs twisting clockwise to catch a new draft of geothermal heat. To a non-Vexi, the noise would be maddening, a wall of organic sound; to me, it is the only song I have ever known.

My task today is not grand. I am working at the base of a thickening node, where a seam of translucent, bioluminescent sap has begun to weep too freely. If left unchecked, the weeping will turn into a rupture, and the node will bloat, potentially crushing the loom I share with three others. 

I reach into the fold of my own thoracic plating and withdraw a shard of volcanic glass, jagged and biting. I do not "think" about the repair; I feel the resonance of the room, the sympathetic vibration of my kin standing five yards away. We are a chorus of tactile signals. They are broadcasting a warning—*too much pressure, the vein is engorged, wait for the contraction*—and I yield. I hold my breath, letting the cilia on my shoulders taste the ozone shift, waiting for the exact micro-second when the architecture sighs.

*Snap.*

The floor shifts. The vein contracts. I move with a twitchy, multi-jointed grace, pressing my palm against the weeping tissue to stem the flow. The sap is hot, viscous, and it hums against my skin—a low, buzzing frequency that makes my own internal fibers shiver in unison. I slice a strip of living tissue, the fiber parting with a sound like a drawn-out intake of breath. I begin to weave the strip into the gap, braiding it with the existing root-structure.

As I work, I look down at my hands. The tips of my fingers are already beginning to harden, the color shifting from the dark brown of aged bark to the pale, greenish-white of the hungry new growth. I am being incorporated. I have been at this node for a rotation, and already, I feel the roots beginning to trace the architecture of my ankles. I am not standing *on* the floor; I am becoming a structural buttress.

There is no fear in this. Fear is a luxury of the slow, of the stagnant. Here, there is only the *Doing*. 

Sometimes, in the brief, flickering yellow light of the canopy, I catch the reflection of the other Tenders. They are motionless, save for their fingers, their bodies angled to catch the structural currents. We look like wooden statues, carvings left behind by a civilization that decided to stop being separate from its house. We are the surgeons of a tumor that is also a masterpiece. 

I finish the weave. I press my chest against the node, transmitting a brief, sharp sequence of pulses—*Seal. Anchored. Holding.*—to the others nearby. They answer with a low, resonant hum that travels through the soles of my feet, a collective "rest" that lasts only until the next branch snaps.

I stay there, pressed to the wood, feeling the wall’s pulse become my own heart rate. I can feel the fibers in my own legs beginning to knit with the fibers of the rib I am standing on. Soon, I will not need to move to check the seams. I will be the seam. And that is the peace of the Vexi: to work until you are so perfectly woven into the garden that the garden finally, and mercifully, takes you in.
