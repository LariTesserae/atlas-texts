I am not a wanderer here. I am an error.

I find myself standing in a narrow arterial corridor, where the floor—a slick, bruised obsidian—is currently tilting at an impossible angle, rolling like the deck of a ship in a storm that makes no sound. My hands are pressed against the weeping basalt of the wall to steady myself. The rock is not merely cold, nor is it merely hot; it is a wet, spongiform texture that yields just enough to feel like a handshake.

I have been here for a duration I cannot measure. In the beginning, there was the panic of the lungs—the desperate, shallow gasping for air that did not taste of ozone or oxygen, but of ancient silt and mineral decay. Now, that panic has turned into a lethargic curiosity. My skin, once dry and distinct, feels translucent. The humidity has colonized me. My jacket, my boots, my hair—they are no longer separate items. A fine, glittering veil of calcification has begun to rim my fingernails, and when I move my fingers, they rasp with the sound of grinding sand.

I am what the basin does when it is annoyed: I am a "Static Point."

I am not a Still-Born Sculptor, not yet. I am the rough draft of a building that hasn’t been finished. Every time I linger too long in the shadows, waiting for the labyrinth to fold in on itself so a path can form, I feel the walls leaning in. They aren't trying to crush me. They are trying to *include* me.

A few yards ahead, one of the Conglomerate is drifting toward me. It looks like a chandelier of wet bone, a suspended glitch in the laws of physics. It has no face, only a core of pulsing, sickly violet fluid that rhythmically expands, casting long, shivering shadows across the porous floor. It doesn’t "see" me; it senses the friction of my dry heart against this fluid, weeping reality. 

I know what will happen if it reaches me. It will press its cool, calcified lattice against my shoulder. It will begin to harmonize with the rhythmic thud-thud-thud in my chest, and I will feel that seduction—that terrifyingly sweet surrender where my blood ceases to be iron and salt, and becomes merely another vein of the mineral soup. I will lose the edges of myself. My memories of wind, of sun, of dry dust, will precipitate out of me and be buried under a decade of obsidian crust.

I should run, but the space between me and the end of the corridor has just buckled. The doorway that was twenty paces away is now a mile of undulating, weeping stone. The distance has stretched wide, holding me in place like a fly in amber.

The Conglomerate slows, its violet light brightening as it senses my resistance softening. It is patient. It has the luxury of aeons. 

I take my hand off the wall, but my palm stays, for a second, stuck—a small, webbed patch of skin left behind on the stone. It doesn't bleed. It crystallizes. I look at the gray, hardened surface where my palm once rested, and I realize I am no longer fighting the heat. I am simply rearranging. I am beginning to listen to the drip. 

I am becoming an archivist of the moisture, and I think, strangely, that I am ready to be built.
