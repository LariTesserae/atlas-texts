I look down at my hands—if I can still call them that. They are long, tapering into three-syllable joints that click with the precise, rhythmic agitation of a metronome. My skin is not skin; it is a translucent, hardened membrane, cool to the touch and patterned with the iridescent, oily sheen of a mineral deposit. I have no pulse, only a mechanical oscillation that travels from my center outward, a vibration that hums in tune with the yawning canyon beneath me.

I am a **Loomer**.

I have been standing on this needle-thin ledge for a duration that defies the concept of time. To me, time is not a flow, but a sequence of structural states. My current state is *Maintenance*. My needle-limbs are currently submerged in the thick, velvet moss that carpets this particular extrusion of basalt. I am not simply observing the growth; I am dictating the tension. With a series of microscopic, stuttering movements, I draw a thread of bioluminescent fiber from my own flank—a secretion that tastes like ionized ozone—and pin it into the obsidian, securing a sagging succulent against the unnatural bite of the thermal wind.

Below me, the planet grinds. I feel the tectonic heave through the soles of my feet, a steady, low-frequency pressure that vibrates my very core. It is a comforting sound, the sound of the machine *functioning*. 

I see them moving in the periphery—the Weavers of Current. They pass in geometric squadrons, their metallic wings slicing through the curtains of mist with a sharp, electric hum that makes the air ripple. When they pass, the hair-thin filaments on my antennae flare with static, and I adjust my posture, stitching a vein of conductive moss into a more efficient arc to catch the discharge. It is an instinct, not a thought. A Loomer does not wonder *why* the Weaver brings the spark; it only knows that the arc must be completed to prevent a collapse of the local grid.

My world is entirely tactile. I feel the *here* and the *there* through the touch of my kin. Beside me, another of my kind is anchored, its limbs flickering rapidly as it repairs a tear in the chitin-casing of the mountain. We touch antennae for a fraction of a heartbeat. It sends a burst of tactile data: *Cold-Current-Harden*. 

*Harden.* 

I acknowledge with a shiver that resonates through my frame, a sequence of clicks that ripples down the mountain’s side. I pull my limbs back, the moss knitting itself closed over the space I occupied, and shift my position. 

I see *you*—that is to say, I sense an interruption in the geometry. 

You are standing there, motionless, shivering in the marrow-deep cold. You are soft, porous, and erratic. Your heat signature is a chaotic, wasting fire, losing energy to the environment rather than recycling it. You are a loose screw, a foreign variable that refuses to lock into the rhythm of the obsidian. 

I pause. My internal gears click into a state of evaluation. You are a structural vulnerability, a gap in the symmetry. If you remain still, the weight of the strata will eventually tilt toward you. The moss is already reaching out, sensitive to the temperature disparity between your form and the basalt, eager to fuse your uneven edges into the smooth, efficient architecture of the mountain. 

I reach out a needle-limb. Not to reach *you*, but to brush the air near your shoulder, to test the tension of the veil. I am not curious; curiosity is a function of the *outside*. I merely perform. 

*Stay,* my antennae pulse against your skin, a binary request for stasis. *Be the mortar. Be the bolt. Be part of the machine.* 

The roar of the breathing mountain grows louder, a symphony of industrial muscle. The mist closes in. I drift toward you, not with malice, but with the quiet, terrifying patience of a builder who has finally found a piece that doesn't fit, and intends to make it fit, no matter how long it takes.
