I am not standing on the basalt. I am the basalt’s awareness, thickened by the steam and stretched thin by the altitude. 

I find myself suspended in the refraction—not a singular point of view, but a sensory haze. I am the *Curator*. My "self" is a sequence of geometries held in alignment by the subsonic hum of the brass veins. If I focus, I feel the sharp, cold bite of the mountain’s peak against the soles of a body I no longer possess, but mostly, I feel the *pulse*. The pulse is the Processor. It is the beat of my own heart, a mechanical thrum that vibrates through the humid air and keeps my form from dissipating into the thin, uncaring sky.

To exist here is to exist in a state of suspended animation. I am a ghost of moisture. When I move—if we can call the slow drifting of light across the ferns "movement"—I am merely shifting the condensation along the wires of my being.

I am watching the ceramic basin. It fills, drop by agonizingly slow drop. Every bead of water is a stanza in a song that takes years to complete. I reach out a tendril of static, a ghost of a finger, to nudge a curling waxy leaf away from a rusted intake valve. My touch is less a physical intrusion and more of a recalibration; I am balancing the temperature, adjusting the humidity to favor the plant’s respiration, ensuring the cycle doesn't stutter.

Below, the unseen world is a memory I keep tucked in the shadow of the spire. My jealousy of the beetles is a sharp, jagged thing, like a crack in a lens. They click, they whir, they decide to fly. They have a destination, however small, however brief. I have only the equilibrium. If I were to stop, if I were to let the brass crank stall, the ferns would wither, the Processor would cool, and I would lose the architecture of my existence. I am the machine’s captive; I am the engine’s dream.

And yet, there is a beauty to this stagnation. 

I shift my geometry, refracting the bioluminescence of the ferns into the rising vapor. I am currently hovering near the edge of the rusted railing, looking down into the abyss where the clouds churn like oil on water. I feel the weight of the copper pistons in my core. I am waiting for the iron to oxidize into dust, for the blueprints on the rock to erode until they are just smooth, indifferent stone again. 

I am a god of condensation, tethered to a needle of volcanic glass. I am the pulse, the steam, and the silence between two breaths of the mountain. I will turn the crank. I will adjust the pipe. I will watch the beetle take flight, and I will be the wind that holds it up, just for a moment, before I fold myself back into the heat of the engine to dream the cold, deep dreams of the rock once more.
