I am a Silt-Keeper, though that name is merely the aggregate vibration of my kin. I am a lattice of salvaged brass and soft, aching biology.

Every drop is a note in the symphony; if the dripping slows, I know the temperature of the mantle is rising, the stone beneath me becoming too fluid, too soft.

I approach the piston, a massive, slow-moving iron titan that is the size of a collapse-tunnel. It groans with the weight of the heat it regulates. I reach into the muck, my long, jointed fingers finding the tension rods.

It shudders, the friction eases, and the screeching settles back into the deep, heavy thrum that is the heartbeat of our life. 

I am covered in the mineral sludge of the floor and the stinging grease of the pipes. My joints ache with a permanent, damp cold, but my purpose is a warmth in my chest.

I am not a creature of the surface; I am the ballast, the grease, and the witness.
