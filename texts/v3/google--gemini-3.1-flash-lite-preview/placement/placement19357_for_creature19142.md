I am standing on the floor of the Foundry, my feet—widened, splayed pads of hardened, calcified root-matter—pressed firmly against the obsidian. I am one of those who lean.

When I first came to understand myself, I realized I was not a being of history, but a being of *maintenance*. I do not have a name—names imply a narrative, a beginning and an end—and here, those are only concepts written in the rust. I am a **Tension-Rebalancer**.

My body feels like an architectural afterthought. I am taller than the others, my frame a crooked lattice of salvage-brass that has been weathered a deep, bruised copper color. Running through my torso, where a heart might reside in a lesser, frailer creature, is a translucent conduit. Inside it, a globule of bioluminescent sap oscillates, its intensity dimming and brightening in exact, agonizing synchronization with the tectonic groan of the floor beneath us.

I am currently pressed into a corner where the obsidian wall has cracked. A fissure has opened, releasing a jet of steam that is too cold, too sharp—a discordant note in the Foundry’s symphony.

I reach out. My fingers are not fingers; they are clusters of stiff, metallic filaments that fan out like the antennae of a deep-sea crustacean. I am tuned to the *hum*. My whole existence is a calculation of equilibrium; if the hum wavers, I am in pain. If the gear grinds too slowly, I feel a sensation akin to a limb going numb.

I press my filaments against the fissure. 

The heat is a physical weight, but it is also my language. I hum back at the steam, a low, buzzing vibration that resonates in my chest-conduit until the metal of the wall seems to soften in response. I begin to weave. I draw a handful of the clumping, electric-smelling mold from my own hip-pouch and press it into the void, threading it through the glowing, translucent vines that cling to the rock. As I work, I am not thinking. I am *adjusting*. I am closing the tear so the vibration can return to its steady, eternal state.

There is another of us—a fellow smith—standing ten paces away. It is motionless, its concave gauge-face angled toward a massive piston that has been frozen in an upward stroke for three centuries. We do not speak. We do not exchange thoughts, for thoughts would require a future, and we are only curators of the present. 

But I feel its presence. When it shifts its weight—a sound like a heavy anchor dragging over gravel—my own body compensates. I lean a fraction to the left to balance the distribution of force. We are a closed system. We are the counter-weights.

In the shimmering, oily light, I see my own reflection in a hanging bead of condensation. I do not see a face. I see a piece of the architecture that has sprouted limbs. I see the soot of the machine-ages caked into my joints and the bioluminescent sap of the plants blooming in my veins. 

I am not the master of this place, nor am I its guest. I am the glue. I am the silence that prevents the roar from shattering the world.

The steam hiss softens. The tectonic vibration steadies into that familiar, bone-deep thrum. My work here is done, for now. I retract my filaments, the metal clicking softly against the cooling pipe. I turn, slow and glacial, and drift toward a patch of shadow in the rock. 

I will stand here. I will stand here until the next groan of the earth demands that I become something else—a repairman, a gardener, a guardian. I close my aperture. I enter the torpor. I let the Foundry breathe for me, and in the dark, heavy heat, I finally cease to be apart from the wall behind me. I am part of the chime. I am part of the decay. I am perfectly, utterly, stagnant.
