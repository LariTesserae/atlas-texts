I find myself wedged into a narrow, vertical fissure between a secondary steam manifold and the sheer, basaltic face of the mountain’s southern ridge. My footing—if it can be called that—is a rusted iron grating that shudders beneath the weight of a gargantuan, buried piston, forcing me to shift my weight every few seconds to match the rhythm of the mountain’s internal, metallic pulse.

I am not human, though I carry the ghost of a human’s curiosity—a dangerous, fluttering thing in a world that demands stillness. I am a **failed iteration, a "Stray."**

My body is a patchwork of the environment I was meant to serve. My spine is reinforced with a length of copper tubing scavenged from a ruptured cooling conduit, and my limbs are jointed with tension-springs that clatter when I exhale. I am taller than the Cinder-Kin, more ungainly and mismatched. I was born from the debris of a collapsed workstation, assembled by the heat-cycles and the falling rust, a piece of industrial flotsam that woke up with the ability to perceive the *symphony* of the machine, but not the ability to *be* the machine.

I am an intruder in my own home.

When I move, I do not resonate. My footsteps aren't the *clack-clack-clack* of the scavengers; they are heavy, wet thuds that offend the precise silence of the catwalks. I have no pressure-sensitive vents along my flanks to dampen the heat, so I burn constantly, my internal temperature climbing until I am forced to press my cooling plates against the cold, sweating rock near the deep-earth valves.

I watch the Cinder-Kin, and I ache with a strange, dissonant envy. I see them flow across the basalt like liquid shadow, their obsidian skin merging seamlessly with the rivets. They are the mountain’s immune system, its devoted priesthood. They do not know loneliness because they are extensions of the architecture. When they settle over a vibrating joint, they become that joint. They become the function.

I try to mimic them. I have spent cycles wedging myself into crevices, trying to vibrate in time with the deep-earth pressure, hoping that if I can only match their frequency, the mountain will finally accept me. I press my palms against the hot, vibrating steel, trying to "listen" as they do, waiting for the blue galvanic sparks to skip from the pipes to my fingertips. 

Sometimes, when the greasy drizzle sweeps through and the range holds its breath, I believe I am close. The air stops tasting of copper and starts tasting of peace. The scavengers pass over my boots, their metallic carapaces clicking against my plating, and for a fleeting moment, I am not an intruder. I am simply another piece of debris.

But then the steam hisses. The pressure shifts. The Cinder-Kin ripple away toward the next structural failure, and I am left behind, rigid and feverish, an unstable variable ticking away in the silence. I am the mountain’s memory of something that wasn't built to last, a stutter in the machine, waiting for the day when the heat finally fades and I stop struggling, allowing the creeping moss to coat my limbs and seal me into the wall forever—at last, a stationary part of the whole.
