## What you’re walking into (operational summary)
A warm, humid, extremely low-relief wetland plain with weak drainage: lots of shallow standing water, thin soils over old rock, iron-hardpan patches that alternate between “brick” and “ice,” and black anoxic mud that will happily take a boot and argue about giving it back. Visibility is long but softened by haze. Sound carries. Storms can be sudden, rain can turn flats into mirrors, and the ground can change from firm to swallowing in one step.

Assume:
- **Constant wet feet unless you actively prevent it**
- **High insect load** (biting + swarming)
- **Navigation by microtopography is hard** (everything is almost-flat)
- **Hypothermia is still possible** (wet + wind + night cooling), even in “warm” places
- **Lightning risk** is non-trivial (big open, frequent thunderstorms)

## Health & biohazard preparation (do this before you depart)
### Vaccines / prophylaxis (multiverse-agnostic baseline)
Even if the local pathogens are “different,” the *mode* of risk is classic wetland:
- **Tetanus**: up to date.
- **Hep A / Typhoid**: if your physiology supports it.
- **Malaria prophylaxis**: strongly consider if your setting supports malaria-like vectors; if you can’t confirm, plan as if it exists.
- **Leptospirosis awareness**: treat all warm shallow water as contaminated with animal urine until proven otherwise.
- **Anti-helminth plan**: avoid bare-skin water contact; have a broad-spectrum antiparasitic course in your med kit only if you know you tolerate it.

### Personal protection rules
- **No open cuts exposed**. Cover abrasions immediately; change dressings often (humidity ruins bandages).
- **No barefoot / sandals**. Ever. Not for “just a second.”
- **Don’t drink surface water** unless you treat it (see Water section).

## Clothing system (dress for wet abrasion + insects + heat)
You want a system that keeps insects off your skin, dries as fast as possible, and still works when soaked.

### Core kit
- **Long-sleeve, tightly woven shirt** (nylon/poly blend) + **light long pants** with reinforced cuffs.
- **Permethrin-treated clothing** (or your universe’s equivalent insecticide treatment) *before* you arrive.
- **Head net** + **wide-brim hat** (the soundscape will be loud; don’t rely on hearing alone—protect eyes/face so you can keep scanning).
- **Neck gaiter / buff**: doubles as sun, insect, and mud protection.
- **Gloves**: thin, grippy, quick-dry. You’ll brace on rock ribs and grab vegetation.

### Footwear (the make-or-break choice)
Pick based on whether you’ll be mostly **wading** or mostly **skirting**:
- **Wading-heavy route**: snug **wet boots** or jungle boots with *excellent drainage*, paired with **neoprene socks** or quick-dry liners.
- **Skirting-heavy route**: lightweight hikers can work *only if* you accept they will be wet anyway and you manage foot care aggressively.

Avoid “waterproof” boots here: they become **bathtubs**.

### Foot care protocol
- Carry **2–3 sock systems** (liner + outer) and rotate.
- **Dry feet at every opportunity** (even 10 minutes matters).
- Use **anti-maceration barrier** (zinc oxide or anti-chafe balm).
- Treat hot spots early; trench foot happens in “not that cold” conditions when it’s always wet.

## Movement & terrain discipline (how not to get eaten by the ground)
### Golden rule: probe, don’t commit
- Carry a **stout probe staff** (not a trekking pole—something you can lean on hard).
- **Test each step** in dark mud or floating mat: push down and feel for sudden drop.
- If the staff sinks past mid-shaft easily, **detour**.

### Read the ground
- **Iron-stained hardpan**: safe when dry, treacherous when wet. Treat it like algae-slick rock.
- **Floating plant mats**: sometimes hold; sometimes conceal water. Step only where rooted vegetation is dense and continuous.
- **Sour black mud**: anoxic, adhesive, energy-sapping. One bad crossing can cost hours and skin.

### Pace & energy
This landscape looks easy and walks hard.
- Plan **2–3× the time** you’d expect from the apparent flatness.
- Budget extra calories; wetland walking is constant micro-correction.

## Navigation (the place where “just go that way” fails)
In near-flat terrain, small errors compound.

### Tools
- **Offline maps** if you have them, but don’t assume stable landmarks.
- **Compass + GPS** (or your multiverse equivalent) plus **dead reckoning**.
- **Altimeter** can help even with tiny elevation changes; “a firmness underfoot” is real navigation data here.
- **Marking tape is a last resort** (leave-no-trace issues, plus it can mislead you later).

### Techniques
- Navigate **from firm islands to firm islands** (rock ribs, slightly raised ground, thorn shrub clumps).
- Use **sound**: bird colonies and frog choruses can be consistent location cues, but don’t depend on them in storms.
- In haze, set **short legs** (e.g., 200–500 m), confirm position, repeat.

### Don’t rely on rivers
There aren’t committed channels. Your “drainage-line” may vanish into sheet water.

## Weather & lightning (this will hurt you if you treat it casually)
### Thunderstorms
- If you hear thunder, assume lightning range.
- In an open plain, your safest move is counterintuitive: **avoid being the tallest thing**, but also avoid isolated trees.
- Best lightning posture option when caught: move to a **lower spot** that is not actively pooling deep water, keep your profile low, feet close together, minimize contact with the ground (your staff stays down only if needed for balance).

### Visibility swings
After rain, glare off wet flats can be blinding—pack **polarized eye protection**.

### Sleep planning
Nights can feel cooler than expected because you’re wet.
- Bring a **sleep system that works damp** (synthetic insulation beats down).
- Always have a **dry reserve layer** sealed in a waterproof bag.

## Insects & noise (comfort is safety)
### Insect control stack
- Treated clothing (permethrin) + **repellent on exposed skin** (DEET/picaridin equivalent).
- **Head net** is non-negotiable at dusk/dawn.
- Consider **ear protection** for sleep: the soundscape is relentless and can degrade decision-making over days.

### Bites are medical issues here
Scratching + humidity = infections. Pack topical antiseptic and anti-itch.

## Water & food (it looks like water; it isn’t water)
### Drinking water
Assume all surface water is contaminated.
- Best: **filter + chemical** (or UV) as a two-step.
- Black, peaty water can clog filters fast—bring a **pre-filter cloth** and plan maintenance.
- If you can, source from **seep-freshened swales** rather than stagnant basins, but still treat it.

### Food strategy
- High-calorie, low-prep foods. You’ll often be muddy, wet, and not wanting to stop long.
- Keep food sealed: ants and moisture will find everything.

## Shelter & camp craft (where “dry ground” is a luxury)
### Site selection
- Camp on **rock ribs** or the **slightest rises**—the firmness underfoot matters.
- Avoid the tempting flat near waterlines: storms turn that into a shallow lake.

### Shelter types that work
- **Freestanding tent** (stakes are unreliable in saturated soils) OR hammock system if there are enough trees (your description suggests trees are scattered—don’t bet your life on hammocking).
- Groundsheet is useful, but keep it minimal; water will run *over* and *under* everything.

### Fire is optional; dryness is not
Count on not making a fire. Bring a stove if you need heat/boils.

## Wildlife interaction (predation is efficient; don’t be the odd, slow thing)
You’re unlikely to be “hunted,” but you can be:
- **Investigated**, **defended against**, or **accidentally entangled** in the food web.

Rules:
- Give wading birds and territorial birds wide berth.
- Don’t corner small mammals between cover islands.
- Watch for reptiles on warm stones; don’t step onto the “nice seat” without looking.

Also: avoid handling carcasses, feathers, or unknown bone deposits in old anoxic pools—pathogens concentrate in weird places.

## Communication & emergency planning (because self-rescue is hard here)
### Comms
- Carry a **reliable emergency beacon** (or multiverse equivalent).
- Voice carries, but that doesn’t mean help exists.

### Extraction plan
Have at least one:
- **Hard-direction bailout** to higher ground you can see (even if it’s “half a day” away).
- **Storm lock-down plan**: where you will sit tight if moving becomes too risky.

### Injury plan
Wetland injuries get worse fast.
- Assume any sprain becomes a mobility crisis because of suction-mud and water crossings.
- Pack a **real compression wrap**, blister care, antiseptic, and a way to keep a limb elevated and warm.

## Behavioral etiquette (how to move without making everything worse)
- Step on **durable surfaces** when possible (rock ribs, firm sand patches).
- Don’t trample sparse vegetation islands—they’re the only stable habitat in many basins.
- Don’t dump untreated waste into pools; in low-drain systems, contamination lingers.
- Keep noise low near nesting areas—sound is a primary map and territory marker here.
