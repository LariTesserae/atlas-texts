## What you’re walking into (so you pack the right mindset)
This is not a “rainy city.” It’s a **tidal, sweating, mechanically warm littoral megastructure** where:

- **Everything is damp** (even when it’s not raining).
- **Everything is loud** (silence exists only in engineered pockets).
- **Everything moves** (lanes, walkways, barriers, even some buildings).
- **Everything corrodes** (salt + humidity + dissolved industrial chemistry).
- **Biology is everywhere** (but almost none of it is plants; think biofilms, insects, aquatic life, scavengers).
- “Streets” are often **grates over water**, or surfaces designed to *carry* water.
- Navigation is **spatiotemporal**: where something is can depend on *when* you’re there.

Prepare the way you would for: a wet industrial port + an elevated reef of infrastructure + an airport tarmac during a storm—except it covers a whole horizon.

## Pre-departure preparation (do this before you pack)

### 1) Medical and tolerance checks
This environment punishes weak links: skin, ears, lungs, balance, sleep.

**Baseline recommendations**
- **Vaccinations**: treat as “dense wet city + warm canals + heavy animal contact.” Update tetanus; consider hepatitis A/B; any region-specific waterborne illness equivalents your multiverse clinic flags.
- **Skin management**: humidity + salts + friction = rashes and cracking. If you’re prone to eczema, fungal infections, or contact dermatitis, bring prescription topicals.
- **Respiratory**: fine grit + aerosolized brine + biofilm fragments can irritate lungs. If you have asthma/allergies, bring inhalers and antihistamines.
- **Motion/balance**: surfaces shift, decks vibrate, routes reroute. If you’re prone to vertigo, plan for it.

**Sleep reality**
- Expect **chronic noise** and **vibration hum**. If you can’t sleep without quiet, plan to *engineer* your own.

### 2) Decide what kind of visitor you are (it changes your kit)
- **Surface traveler** (markets, elevated decks, transit lines): biggest risks are noise, crowds, slipping, petty theft, getting routed into a flood corridor.
- **Low-district traveler** (threshold-dams, canal-edge neighborhoods): add risks of contaminated water contact, aggressive scavengers, sudden inundation, and getting trapped by gate closures.
- **Edge traveler** (platform lace at the margins): add risks of fog, disorientation, fewer people, more wildlife, and long response times for rescue.

### 3) Documentation and translation
This place runs on **real-time civic systems**.
- Bring at least **two identity methods** (one physical, one digital/offline-capable).
- Ensure your translator handles **“flow language”**: signage that changes meaning with time, tide state, and access permissions.
- Prepare for addresses formatted like:  
  **District / Vertical layer / Segment ID / Time window / Gate state**

If you can’t parse that, you’ll miss appointments and end up at locked barriers.

## Packing: what an ordinary human needs to not suffer

### Core clothing system (assume you will be wet)
**Outer layer**
- **Waterproof-breathable shell** with sealed seams (not “water resistant”).
- **Hood that actually seals** around face/neck; brim helps in hard rain.

**Mid/inner**
- **Fast-drying synthetics or wool**, not cotton (cotton becomes a cold wet rag).
- **Two complete base-layer sets per day** if you chafe easily.

**Hands**
- **Thin grip gloves** (rails are slick; metal is wet; biofilms are real).

**Feet (most important)**
- **Non-slip, siped soles** rated for wet metal and algae-coated surfaces.
- **Quick-drain footwear** or waterproof boots *plus* spare dry insoles.
- **Anti-blister kit** (moleskin/hydrocolloid) because you will walk on damp feet.

**One rule:** if you bring only one “serious” item, make it the footwear.

### Waterproofing and corrosion control
Salt and wet grit will destroy ordinary zippers and electronics.

- **Dry bags** in at least two sizes (one for daily carry, one for “everything”).
- **Waterproof pouch** that can be operated one-handed for documents.
- **Corrosion inhibitor wipes** (for metal tools, zippers, camera mounts).
- **Spare laces / spare zipper pulls** (small failures become big fast here).

### Noise survival (non-negotiable)
You need *real* hearing protection, not polite earplugs.

- **High-NRR earplugs** (foam is fine, fitted is better).
- **Over-ear defenders** for long exposure zones (fans/turbines/mag-line corridors).
- **Sleep earbuds + white noise** (or active noise canceling if you can keep them dry).
- If you rely on hearing for situational awareness, bring **filtered musician’s plugs** for daytime and save full isolation for transit corridors.

### Air and eye protection
- **Clear anti-fog glasses** (protect from grit and spray; improves comfort hugely).
- **Light respirator or high-filtration mask** for:
  - sandblown corridors,
  - maintenance swarms “sintering” dust,
  - canal-edge stink zones,
  - crowded humid markets where aerosol load is high.

### Health and hygiene kit (tailored to this city)
- **Antifungal powder/cream** (feet and folds).
- **Barrier cream** (thighs/under straps).
- **Electrolytes** (humidity + constant sweating; you may not notice dehydration).
- **Waterless soap / alcohol gel**, but also:
- **Rinse bottle** (small) for salt/biofilm contact on skin cuts.
- **Waterproof bandages** + antiseptic. Treat every scrape like it met a canal.

### Lighting and power
Light is “ordinary,” but spaces under decks can be dim and fog eats contrast.

- **Headlamp** with a wide flood mode.
- **Power bank** rated for humidity; keep it in a dry bag with desiccant.
- **Connector adapters** if you expect local charging standards; ideally a **sealed universal adapter**.

### Navigation and “don’t get rerouted into trouble”
- **Offline map package** (because dense infrastructure can make comms patchy).
- A device/app that can ingest **real-time flow bulletins**: closures, surge routing, deck load limits, acoustic hazard advisories.
- **Physical backup**: a small printed “layer map” of your main district and an agreed meeting point *per vertical level*.

### Daily-carry bag (what you keep on you)
- Dry pouch: ID, cash/credit equivalent, medical card.
- Earplugs.
- Mask.
- Two adhesive bandages + antiseptic wipe.
- Small towel or absorbent cloth (for hands, rails, lenses).
- One spare base layer (top or socks).
- Snack that tolerates humidity (sealed).

## How to plan your arrival (timing is safety)

### Choose the right time window
- Avoid arriving during **evening downpour cycles** if possible; the city “breathes” and the evening often hits hardest.
- If you can, arrive in a **midday stability window**: fog lifts, visibility improves, systems are in their “normal flow.”

### Book lodging by *acoustic and elevation class*
When selecting where to sleep, ask specifically:
- **Elevation above surge-routing threshold** (not just “ground floor vs high floor”).
- **Distance to mag-lines / turbine chambers / main fan corridors**.
- Whether rooms have **acoustic shaping** or “quiet pocket” engineering.

If you can’t get guarantees, bring your own sleep noise-control.

## On-site behavior: how not to get hurt or become a problem

### Walking and touching rules (wet city etiquette)
- Assume **every rail is slick**, every surface is either polished by traffic or coated in microscopic life.
- **One hand free** when moving through transitions (stairs, tide-stairs, deck joins).
- Don’t sit on “clean-looking” ledges near canals; many are **working surfaces** (thin-water corridors, filtration lips, sensor edges).

### Water contact: treat canals as industrial, not recreational
Even if it doesn’t smell awful:
- Don’t wade. Don’t swim.
- If splashed: rinse promptly, disinfect cuts, change socks/clothes.
- Don’t eat with wet hands that touched rails.

### Wildlife: assume it’s urban-smart
- Keep food sealed. Don’t hand-feed anything.
- Watch edges: predation is public and fast; startled flocks and animals can collide with you.
- If you see “maintenance cavities” with warm air and clicking: **don’t reach in**.

### Interacting with the city’s moving parts
This is critical: you are in a place where infrastructure **decides things**.

- Obey active markings (light/pigment lanes). They can change within minutes.
- Never cross a barrier that looks “temporarily open.” Gates behave like eyelids.
- If a deck shivers or panels reorient, **stop and let it finish**. That motion often precedes drainage cascades or load redistribution.

### Markets and crowds
Crowds plus wet surfaces are a slip-and-theft combination.
- Keep valuables under your shell, inside a zipped inner pocket.
- Use a cross-body strap you can tighten; avoid dangling bags.
- Have a “drop plan” for your phone (lanyard, wrist strap).

## Communication, money, and security (multiverse practicalities)

### Electronics security
Dense cities with ubiquitous sensors reward good hygiene:
- Keep one device “clean” for identity and funds, and another for exploration/media.
- Disable automatic connectivity if you don’t trust local networks.
- Use waterproof cases; humidity kills ports first.

### Money
Expect microtransactions for transit reroutes, access gates, and time-window services.
- Carry a small reserve in a form that works **offline** (physical token/cash equivalent) in case the network goes down during storms.

## Suggested “first 48 hours” plan (reduces overwhelm)

**Hour 0–6: acclimation**
- Stay in one district, one elevation band.
- Learn three safe routes: lodging ↔ food ↔ transit hub.
- Buy/obtain local ear protection if yours isn’t enough (you’ll know quickly).

**Hour 6–24: controlled exploration**
- Visit a canal-edge area only with daylight visibility and a known return window.
- Identify at least two “quiet coves” you can retreat to.

**Hour 24–48: expand vertically**
- Add one new layer per outing (upper decks, under-bridge markets, service-ceiling corridors) so you can build a mental 3D map.

## Emergency readiness (what to do when it goes wrong)

### If a surge-routing event starts
You’ll know: gates cycle, markings change, crowd flow tightens, and the soundscape shifts (pumps wake up; low roar increases).

- Don’t try to “beat the water” through low corridors.
- Move **up**, not “away.” Vertical distance beats horizontal distance here.
- Follow official flow markings even if they look longer; they’re designed around closures.

### If you slip and get soaked
- Change socks/base layer ASAP.
- Clean any abrasion; cover it.
- Don’t keep walking in wet boots if you can avoid it—your feet will fail before your will does.

### If you get disoriented in fog at the margins
- Stop moving; moving platforms and lacework can lead you into restricted or unsafe edges.
- Use sound beacons/official markers rather than sight lines.
- Call for a guided route; this city expects that.
