## What this place will do to an ordinary human (baseline expectations)

You are going into a warm, high-elevation basin where **water is everywhere but rarely clean**, where **most surfaces are slick**, where **industry is part of the weather**, and where **animal life fills the ecological roles plants usually occupy**. Expect:

- **Persistent damp** (clothing never fully dries in ambient air; condensation happens indoors).
- **Thin air** (exertion hits harder; mild altitude effects are common).
- **Constant noise** (softened by humidity but continuous; important for situational awareness).
- **Corrosion and biofilm** on anything left exposed (including your gear).
- **Frequent close-quarters encounters** with urban fauna, especially near heat and water routes.
- **Navigation complexity** (multi-level streets/catwalks/tunnels; fog erases distance cues).

Preparation is about staying **upright, uninfected, unlost, and unpoisoned**, while remaining **legible and non-disruptive** to the city’s infrastructure rhythms.

## Pre-trip conditioning (do this before you ever portal in)

### 1) Altitude and exertion readiness
Even though it’s warm, it’s still high. If you arrive and immediately start hauling a pack up catwalk stairs, you’ll feel it.

- **Cardio base**: be able to walk 2–3 hours with a pack without spiking your breathing.
- **Stair training**: catwalk cities are vertical in sneaky ways.
- **Plan a “first 48 hours” rule**: light activity only; no tunnel excursions; no long climbs.

**Medical note (Earth-human standard):**
- Talk to your clinician about **acetazolamide** (altitude prophylaxis) if you’re prone to altitude sickness.
- Arrive hydrated, but avoid overdoing plain water (electrolytes matter).

### 2) Vaccinations and meds (assume “industrial wet city” risks)
This place screams “small cuts become big problems.”

- Ensure **tetanus** is current.
- Bring a **broad-spectrum topical antiseptic**, **antibiotic ointment**, and **sterile wound closure strips**.
- Bring **oral rehydration salts**, **anti-diarrheals**, and an **antiemetic**.
- Consider a clinician-discussed **standby antibiotic** if you’ll be far from reliable care.

### 3) Skin/foot preparation (the quiet killer)
Constant damp + long walking = maceration, fungal issues, trench-foot-like injury.

- Start using **foot antiperspirant** (or drying powder) a week beforehand if you sweat heavily.
- Trim nails, break in boots, and practice changing socks quickly without sitting on the ground.

## Core hazards and how to counter them

### Hazard A: Slickness + verticality (falls are the #1 “ordinary traveler” failure mode)
Biofilm + mist + metal grating = skating rink.

**Countermeasures**
- **Footwear**: waterproof boots with *soft, high-traction soles* (think sticky rubber, not hard hiking lugs).
- **Gloves**: grippy palms; you will grab railings often.
- **Hands-free lighting**: headlamp (fog eats light; catwalk undersides are dim).
- **Three-point rule** on ladders and wet stairs; move slower than you think you need to.

### Hazard B: Aerosols, ammonia notes, industrial vapors, microbial mist
Warm wet air carries more than water.

**Countermeasures**
- Bring a **comfortable respirator** you can wear for hours:
  - Minimum: **P100/FFP3** particulate.
  - Better: combo cartridges that also cover **acid gases / ammonia** if you expect tunnels or outflows.
- **Eye protection**: clear, vented goggles are worth the social awkwardness; mist + grit + corrosion dust is real.
- In tunnels: treat any “sweet,” “sharp,” or “solvent” odor as a cue to **leave first, analyze later**.

### Hazard C: Water that looks harmless but isn’t
Canals and runoff are functional infrastructure and microbial habitat.

**Countermeasures**
- Do **not** wade. Do **not** drink. Avoid splashing.
- Carry your own water or use **serious purification**:
  - Prefer: sealed supplies.
  - If forced: **filter + chemical treatment**, but note industrial contaminants may not be addressed by standard backpacking filters.

### Hazard D: Corrosion and damp ruin gear fast
Anything metallic that isn’t protected will start to fail.

**Countermeasures**
- Pack electronics in **IP-rated dry bags** + **desiccant** + **spare zip seals**.
- Use **dielectric grease** on exposed connectors; bring a tiny bottle.
- Favor **polymer, titanium, stainless**, or coated tools over plain steel.
- Wipe-down ritual: every evening, wipe metal, re-oil hinges, dry contacts.

### Hazard E: Fauna in corridors (not “monsters,” but urban-adapted animals)
They behave like city animals everywhere: opportunistic, territorial, heat-seeking.

**Countermeasures**
- Don’t eat while walking. Don’t leave scraps.
- Avoid standing still near **warm vents, transformer cavities, bridge undersides, and canal edges**—those are “life hubs.”
- Learn the local rule: **sudden quiet is information**. If a normally noisy passage goes still, back out.

Carry:
- **Small deterrent light** (bright strobe) and/or **air horn** (but use sparingly; noise is communication here).
- **Bite protocol kit**: irrigation syringe, antiseptic, dressings, compression wrap.

## Clothing system: what actually works here

Think “warm rainforest meets machine shop,” except at altitude.

### On-body
- **Base layer**: synthetic or merino that still insulates damp (avoid cotton).
- **Mid layer**: thin fleece or active insulation (you’ll chill when you stop).
- **Shell**: waterproof-breathable is fine, but in constant humidity it becomes “waterproof mostly.” Choose durability over breathability.
- **Pants**: quick-dry, abrasion resistant; cuff design that doesn’t wick canal splash upward.
- **Socks**: rotate 2–3 pairs daily. Carry **liner socks** if you’re prone to blisters.
- **Gloves**: one grippy pair + one dry spare.
- **Hat/hood**: fog drizzle is constant; keep water off your face to reduce fatigue.

### Foot care routine (non-negotiable)
- Midday: change socks if you feel your feet getting “soft.”
- Night: dry feet completely, apply barrier ointment, air boots (even if they never truly dry).

## Gear checklist (multiverse-traveler practical loadout)

### Safety + navigation
- Headlamp + spare battery (fog + multi-level structures).
- Handheld light with a tight beam (for looking down shafts and under catwalks).
- High-visibility band/patch (local traffic is rail + maintenance carts; be seen).
- Compact first aid kit focused on **cuts, punctures, and infection control**.
- Lightweight **harness + short lanyard** if you expect a lot of catwalk work (falls are catastrophic).
- Paper map + grease pencil; do not rely on GPS-like systems working cross-world.
- Simple **hand signals** card for your travel group; noise makes speech unreliable.

### Health
- Respirator + spare filters.
- ORS packets, electrolyte tablets.
- Skin barrier cream, antifungal powder/cream.
- Water you trust.

### Maintenance (you will use these daily)
- Microfiber cloths.
- Small bottle of light oil + corrosion inhibitor wipes.
- Zip ties, waterproof tape, spare buckles.
- Spare dry bags / zip pouches.

### Social/transactional items (often useful in dense industrial cities)
If you need goodwill: bring **dryness, seals, and reliability**.
- Desiccant packs, gasket material, quality fasteners, good tape, durable gloves—these tend to be valued.

## Arrival strategy: first day without getting hurt

### Step 1: Find a “dry-ish anchor” before exploring
You want a base that is:
- Above the lowest runoff routes.
- Near (but not inside) a pump-house district (safer, patrolled, maintained).
- Not directly adjacent to warm vents (fauna density spikes there).

### Step 2: Learn the city’s water logic
Within 2–3 hours you should be able to answer:
- Where does water *run* when it rains harder than usual?
- Which walkways stay passable?
- Where are the canal crossing points that don’t require jumping?

### Step 3: Tune your ears
Because sound is a survival sense here, don’t block it completely.
- Use hearing protection that reduces volume but preserves speech frequencies if possible.
- Identify the “normal stack” of sounds in your district (turbines + drips + animal layer). Anything missing is a clue.

### Step 4: Don’t go below ground immediately
Wait until:
- You have a local guide or reliable map.
- You have checked respirator fit.
- You understand how to recognize **bad-air cues** (headache, sudden fatigue, metallic taste, burning eyes).

## How to move through the layered city (street / catwalk / tunnel etiquette)

### Ground level (wet streets, drainage grooves)
- Walk on textured stone where possible; avoid glossy metal plates.
- Do not block grooves or canals—those are not decorative; they’re flood control.
- Assume any “harmless” puddle may be **warm, contaminated, or electrically adjacent**.

### Catwalk level (preferred when it’s really wet)
- Treat it like active infrastructure: maintenance crews and small freight movement may have right-of-way.
- Don’t linger under dripping cable runs; it’s not just water—often mineral-laden, sometimes chemically active.
- Clip in (if you brought a lanyard) during heavy fog or high wind; depth perception gets weird.

### Below level (service tunnels)
Only go with preparation.
- Bring a second light.
- Mark your route physically (chalk/grease pencil) because everything looks the same when wet.
- If you hear **silence** where there should be pumps, consider it an emergency condition: either failure (flood risk) or territory (fauna).

## Food, water, and hygiene in a no-vegetation ecology

### Eating safely
- Prefer sealed rations you brought.
- If you buy local: favor foods that are **fully cooked, served hot**, and eaten immediately.
- Avoid anything that looks “fermented” unless you know the local microbial norms—this world’s base of life is films and blooms.

### Water
- Treat all open water as industrial runoff or biofilm habitat.
- Hot-looking channels can scald; warm doesn’t mean safe.

### Hygiene
- You will not “dry out” naturally. Plan hygiene around **wiping + targeted cleaning**, not long showers.
- Clean hands before touching eyes/respirator seal.
- Clean and cover every cut immediately; in this environment “small abrasion” is an invitation.

## Fauna interactions: how not to become part of the system

Think in terms of **corridors and heat**:
- Heat sources are feeding grounds, nesting grounds, and hunting grounds.
- Narrow passages are ambush geometry.

Rules:
1. **Never run** unless you must—running triggers pursuit in many predators and causes slips in you.
2. **Don’t corner yourself** in dead-end alleys or fenced canal margins.
3. **Don’t shine lights into nests/cavities** unless you’re prepared to leave immediately.
4. If you see cleaned bones, ammonia stink, or an unnatural gap in ambient life: **back out**.

If something confronts you:
- Make yourself larger, keep barriers (railings, grates) between you and it, and withdraw steadily.
- Use sound as deterrent only if escape routes are clear; noise can also attract attention.

## Weather and timing: when to travel inside the basin

Because storm tracks are habitual, your best “touring windows” are often:
- Late morning to mid-afternoon after the fog has lifted enough to restore depth cues.
- Avoid “first fog” hours at dawn for catwalk navigation.
- If the city has known heavy-rain sequences, treat them like train schedules: be on higher routes before they start.

## Emergency planning (simple, robust)

### Know three safe elevations
- A **high viaduct** (visibility and escape from sudden flooding).
- A **mid-level anchor** (near maintained infrastructure).
- A **lowest-level exit route** *only if necessary* (because low routes become water routes).

### Have a “wet failure” plan
If you fall in canal water or get soaked:
- Strip wet layers at base, wipe down, get into your driest set.
- Clean any exposed skin, especially cuts.
- Dry feet and re-sock. This is prevention, not comfort.

### Air problem protocol
If you develop headache, confusion, chest tightness, burning eyes in tunnels:
- Leave immediately to higher, more open air.
- Do not “push through.”
- Assume it’s environmental until proven otherwise.

## Mindset adjustments (the place’s rules, translated into traveler behavior)

- **You will never be fully dry**; plan for controlled damp, not victory over damp.
- **Maintenance is daily life**; treat gear care as dinner.
- **Follow water logic**; it’s the real street map.
- **Listen more than you talk**; sound tells you what’s functioning and what’s hunting.
- **Respect heat**; it’s where life concentrates—yours included.
