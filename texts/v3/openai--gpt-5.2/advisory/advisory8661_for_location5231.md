## What this place does to an ordinary human (so you can plan like an adult)

You’re not preparing for a “ruined city.” You’re preparing for an **active environmental machine** operating inside a **geologically young, sharp-edged basin** where:

- **Sound is the dominant physics you will experience** (continuous broadband roar + infrasound + localized acoustic “corridors” + rare silence traps).
- **Moisture is everywhere but water is unreliable** (condensation, mist, brief local rain, mineral-laden films; pockets of pooled water that may be chemically harsh and biologically busy).
- **Light is dim and flat** (overcast + grit + haze; weak shadows; low contrast navigation).
- **The terrain cuts** (fresh ridges, shears, raw ravines; slippery biofilms and moss).
- **Infrastructure moves** (bridges shift, pylons rotate, tuning events alter airflow/sound; doors may open/close based on proximity/pressure/harmonics).
- **Fauna uses resonance** (predators and prey both treat sound like scent and force; quiet zones are avoided for a reason).

Plan for **sensory overload, hearing injury, respiratory irritation, chronic damp, navigation ambiguity, and “city behaviors” that can trap or funnel you**.

## 1) Pre-trip conditioning (do this before you pack anything)

### Noise & infrasound tolerance training
- Spend time (safely, with professionals) in **high-noise industrial environments** or controlled acoustic chambers to learn your reactions: nausea, panic, dissociation, headache, jaw pain.
- Train to communicate with **hand signals and text-on-device** when speech is unreliable.
- Practice sleeping with **high attenuation hearing protection** plus a **vibration-damped sleeping system** (see below). Many people break on day 2–3 from sleep loss.

### Moisture discipline
- Practice living out of **dry bags inside humid spaces**. If you are sloppy, your electronics and meds will wick water constantly.
- Drill “everything gets wiped and sealed” routines. Condensation here is not occasional—it is the default.

### Movement on sharp + slick surfaces
- Train with **cut-resistant gloves**, trekking poles, and deliberate foot placement. Assume “moss = slip” and “black stone edge = knife.”

## 2) Core hazards and what actually stops people

### A. Hearing damage / vestibular disruption
The roar isn’t just loud; it’s *structural*—you’ll feel it in teeth and organs. Infrasound can cause nausea, anxiety, tremor, fatigue, and poor decision-making.

**Mitigation goal:** reduce exposure without creating total silence (because sudden silence may indicate a boundary or a trap zone).

### B. Quiet zones (treat as restricted areas)
They are bounded, monitored-looking, biologically “armored,” and behaviorally avoided by animals. That’s enough: **do not enter** unless your mission is specifically to investigate and you have a team, tethers, and remote probes.

### C. City motion / retuning events
Shifts are smooth and confident—meaning they can **pinch, shear, or strand** you without drama. Your warning signs will be:
- subtle **harmonic changes**
- distant tower **light-band flashes**
- wind “key” shift and the basin’s roar rebalancing

### D. Mineral film + biofilm
Everything will be slick, slightly sticky, and mildly abrasive. Expect dermatitis, fungal rashes, trench-foot-like issues, and gear fouling.

### E. Resonance predators
Some hunters don’t need to see you; they need you to **broadcast**. Metal rattles, hard footfalls on resonant boulevards, and shouted speech are all “scent trails.”

## 3) Packing: what matters here (and why)

### Hearing & vibration (non-negotiable)
1. **Dual-layer hearing protection**
   - Molded earplugs (high NRR) + over-ear defenders.
   - Prefer models that allow **selective pass-through** (so you can keep a *controlled* sense of the environment).
2. **Infrasound/vibration management**
   - A small **vibration dosimeter** or at least a broadband SPL meter that covers low frequencies.
   - **Jaw relaxation aid** (people clench; cracked fillings are not hypothetical).
3. **Communication that works when voices don’t**
   - Bone-conduction headset may misbehave in heavy vibration; bring **text-based comms** and **line-of-sight signals** (IR strobes, panel markers).

### Respiratory + eye protection
- **P100/FFP3-level filtration** with spare filters. The suspended grit and mineral aerosol will sandpaper your lungs over days.
- Sealed goggles with anti-fog measures; fog is constant and will defeat cheap coatings.

### Clothing for warm-damp abrasion
- Wicking base layers (not cotton).
- **Antifungal/antimicrobial socks** + multiple changes.
- **Cut-resistant gloves** (thin enough for dexterity).
- Outer layer: breathable waterproof (you will get rained on locally, suddenly).
- Bring a small kit for **skin barrier**: zinc oxide, antifungal cream, hydrocolloid dressings.

### Footwear for “sharp + slick”
- Boots with:
  - **puncture-resistant midsole**
  - aggressive tread that still grips wet biofilm
  - ankle support for uneven young cuts
- Add **microspike-style traction** only if you confirm surfaces aren’t magnetically/field-active in a way that makes metal hazardous.

### Navigation (assume GPS is lying or useless)
- **Inertial navigation** (phone IMU apps can help, but bring a dedicated unit if you can).
- **High-power headlamp** + low-power red/amber mode.
- **Retroreflective markers** for your own breadcrumbing (but place them where fauna won’t treat them as cues).
- Paper map won’t exist; your best map is **your own logged track + annotated soundscape**.

### Shelter & sleep
- A **sealed bivy/tent** that can handle constant damp.
- A **vibration-damping sleep pad** (thick foam + air, or specialized industrial anti-vibration mat cut to size).
- A way to create *reduced* noise without “absolute silence”: keep your hearing protection on, but do not seek/engineer a total dead zone.

### Water & food (counterintuitive here)
- Bring most of your water. Local water exists but is:
  - intermittent
  - mineral-heavy
  - biologically unfamiliar
- If you must use local:
  - pre-filter (sediment)
  - **distill** if possible (best)
  - otherwise RO + activated carbon + heavy metal filtration + a disinfectant step  
  Do not assume boiling solves chemical load.

### Tools that won’t betray you acoustically
- Wrap hard gear to prevent clatter.
- Prefer **soft-fastening** (straps, hook/loop thoughtfully, not snap buckles).
- Trekking poles with damped tips; avoid tapping that “pings.”

### A “do not improvise without it” kit
- Tether line + harness (for shifting spans and ravines).
- Non-metallic wedges/chocks (doors and moving panels may pinch).
- Thermal blanket (warm place, but shock and wet still steal heat).
- Tourniquet + hemostatic gauze (sharp stone + falls).
- Broad-spectrum antibiotics (field-expedition grade guidance) and strong antiseptic.

## 4) Arrival protocol: first 60 minutes decide whether you stay alive

1. **Stop and instrument-check before you move**
   - SPL/infrasound reading baseline.
   - Wind direction and “key” (you’ll learn it; write it down).
   - Note nearest visible condenser/weather towers and any open plazas.

2. **Do a gear noise audit**
   - Walk 20 meters on the nearest surface. If your kit rings, rattles, or clicks: fix it now. You are advertising.

3. **Choose your “home vector”**
   - Pick a distinctive, tall cluster or landmark line of towers.
   - Establish a return route with markers that are visible in dim haze (IR reflective tape can work better than visible color).

4. **Avoid the most tempting paths**
   - The smooth “polished stone” channels that repel dust and make pebbles hover may be field-active. Treat them like active conveyor systems until proven otherwise.

## 5) How to move without becoming part of the food web

### Use the city’s loudness as cover, but don’t add to it
- Speak close-range only; use text/hand signals.
- Step off highly resonant boulevards when possible; move along **vegetated margins** *carefully* (slip risk) or along rougher surfaces that don’t transmit as cleanly.

### Don’t enter acoustic “instrument spaces”
Places that turn one sound into rolling echoes are operational terrain—either for predators or for the city. If you must cross:
- go quickly, quietly, and in a tight group
- no metal-on-metal contact
- don’t run unless you must (panic noise travels)

### Treat quiet boundaries like cliff edges
If you notice your breathing suddenly feels “private,” you’re near something bounded. Back out the way you came. Do not test it with shouts, thrown objects, or drones unless you can afford to lose them.

## 6) Weather towers: useful, but not “safe”

Condenser towers can mean:
- higher moisture (good for emergency water capture with a clean tarp and distillation)
- more insects and swarms
- sudden localized downpour (hypothermia-by-wet is still real even in warmth)
- more animal traffic (prey gather, predators follow)

If you camp, don’t camp *under* the tower influence cone. Camp offset, upwind, with a clear escape route.

## 7) Interacting with the city (assume it is a managed system)

### Doors that open “when you approach”
- Do not assume invitation. Assume **sensor-driven logistics**.
- If a doorway cycles for you, it may also be logging you. Minimize time in thresholds; never let a door close between teammates.
- Use wedges if you must hold it—prefer non-damaging, non-metal.

### Moving bridges / rotating pylons
- Never linger mid-span.
- Don’t anchor ropes to anything that looks like it was designed to rotate, flex, or “float.”
- If you perceive a tuning event (light-band flash, harmonic shift), step into a wide open area away from pinch points and wait.

## 8) Biological precautions (this ecosystem is opportunistic)

### Insects
- Full coverage clothing; consider permethrin-treated outer layers.
- Netting for face/neck during dusk swarms.
- Expect bites/stings to be chemically odd. Carry antihistamines and an epinephrine auto-injector if you have any allergy history.

### Plants
- Don’t bare-hand moss mats. They’re wet reservoirs and likely full of irritants or symbionts.
- Bioluminescent edges and sound-activated flowers: beautiful, but don’t stand in them. They may be **signal beacons** in the local ecology.

### Grazers and gliders
- Grazers may be calm but large; don’t wedge yourself between herd lines and cover.
- Gliders may not fear you; they may use you as a moving windbreak. Don’t swat—keep your outline stable and move away.

### Predators
Your best defense is **not being interesting**:
- keep noise low
- keep movement smooth
- avoid predictable corridors
- don’t “hide” in quiet zones

Carry a deterrent, but be careful: in a resonance ecology, sonic devices may escalate. Favor:
- bright light bursts (short, controlled)
- physical barriers
- retreat to rough terrain where sound doesn’t carry cleanly

## 9) Water strategy (because your instincts will be wrong)

You will feel damp and still dehydrate because:
- you’ll breathe through filters
- stress and constant noise increase metabolic load
- mineral taste discourages drinking

**Plan:**
- drink on a schedule, not thirst
- carry electrolyte mix
- treat any local water as contaminated until fully processed

Collecting condensation is tempting. If you do it:
- use a **clean surface you brought**
- don’t scrape from city materials (unknown patinas, living skins, metals)
- distill whenever possible

## 10) Sleep and sanity (your hidden failure mode)

The place will try to keep you awake with:
- continuous roar
- vibration
- dim light cues that never “close the day”
- intermittent rain percussion
- insect columns and distant movements

**Rules that keep you functional:**
- Establish a strict sleep window.
- Wear hearing protection while sleeping, but don’t chase total silence.
- Keep a buddy system even at rest—people wander when overloaded.
- Use simple rituals: wipe down, dry feet, change socks, log notes. Routine anchors cognition.

If you start to feel:
- irrational dread that spikes when the wind changes key
- strong urge to seek “the quiet”
- auditory pattern-finding obsession (you *will* hear patterns)

…treat it as a symptom. Eat, hydrate, rest, and reduce exposure.

## 11) “If X happens, do Y” field rules

- **If the roar drops sharply** → you are near a quiet boundary. Stop, backtrack, mark the edge, do not probe deeper.
- **If towers flash a muted band of light** → expect retuning/motion. Get off spans, away from hinges and narrow corridors.
- **If you notice pebbles hovering or drifting** → you’re near a field-active path. Cross minimally; don’t camp there; protect electronics.
- **If insects start coordinating flashes** → you’re in an active signaling window. Move away calmly; don’t stand still in a swarm column.
- **If you hear deliberate pulse patterns** → do not answer with sound. Log time/location, switch to low signature movement, and assume you are observed.

## 12) What a “good” expedition looks like here

- You move **slow and quiet** along margins, not the city’s “perfect” corridors.
- You treat weather towers as **resources**, not shelters.
- You keep your team tightly managed and well-rested because the environment is designed (intentionally or incidentally) to erode attention.
- You leave **minimal traces**—because anything that learns may learn *you*.
