## What will try to kill or cripple you here (in order)

1. **Dehydration-by-environment**: not from heat, but from *constant desiccation* (thin, cold, dry air + wind). You will lose water through breath and skin faster than you expect, even while you “don’t feel sweaty.”
2. **Altitude hypoxia**: thin air plus exertion equals headache → confusion → bad decisions.
3. **Dust + abrasive storms**: particulate inhalation, eye injury, equipment abrasion, seals failing.
4. **Cold without “wet cues”**: you can get dangerously chilled while still feeling dry and “fine.”
5. **Electrostatics / bioelectrical infrastructure**: charged air, discharge events, and “living power lines” you can accidentally bridge.
6. **Noise / resonance**: chronic high SPL fatigue, disorientation, and in some corridors, chest-thumping low-frequency tones.
7. **Cultural/biochemical hazard**: **free water is a contaminant**. Your breath, sweat, tears, spills, and trash are treated like a corrosive release.

Everything else (fauna, predation, getting lost) becomes manageable once those seven are handled.

## The single most important principle: you must be a closed-loop “dry visitor”

Assume the city is a coupled organism + grid that treats moisture the way we treat acid spills.

**Your goal:** *never vent liquid water or humid air to the open environment unless you are in a designated exchange/vent bay.*

That means you travel like a hazmat worker crossed with a desert mountaineer:
- sealed layers
- capture your own humidity
- contain all waste
- decontaminate before you touch civic/living surfaces

## Pre-trip conditioning (do this before you ever portal in)

### Physiology
- **Altitude prep**: arrive pre-acclimated if possible (staged exposure or medication per your medical reality). If you can’t acclimate, plan on **supplemental O₂** from minute one.
- **Skin barrier training**: start using a barrier routine days ahead (non-water-based ointments can reduce transepidermal loss). This place punishes “bare human.”
- **Respiratory tolerance**: if you have asthma/reactive airways, treat this as a high-risk destination—dust + thin air is a bad combo.

### Mindset
- Practice operating calmly in:
  - loud environments (ear-pro + comms)
  - gloves all day
  - “no casual eating/drinking” conditions

If you’re the kind of traveler who improvises, this is the world that makes improvisers leak, and leaks become emergencies.

## Essential kit (minimum viable loadout)

### 1) Breathing + humidity control (non-negotiable)
- **Full-face respirator or helmet** with:
  - P100/HEPA-grade particulate filtration (or better)
  - **powered airflow assist** if you can (dust + hypoxia makes passive masks miserable)
  - **CO₂ management** (scrubber cartridge or periodic purge in approved zones)
  - **humidity capture** on the exhale (a condenser or desiccant loop) so you are not venting moist breath
- **Supplemental O₂**:
  - conservative plan: enough for *all exertion* + emergencies
  - redundancy: a second small bottle you never touch unless primary fails

### 2) A truly sealed clothing system
- **Windproof outer shell** with sealed seams (think: storm-rated mountaineering shell but upgraded)
- **Inner vapor barrier layer** (yes, it feels weird; it prevents the world from “vacuuming” your skin moisture away)
- **Insulation that still works dry** (synthetic, not down unless perfectly protected)
- **Goggles/visor** rated for fine dust abrasion
- **Gloves**: two-layer system (dexterity inner + abrasion outer)
- **Boots**:
  - sealed uppers
  - dust gaiters
  - soles that won’t spark easily on mineral salts (avoid metal cleats unless necessary)

### 3) Water *for you*, but never “free” to them
You still need water. You just can’t *release it*.
- **Internal hydration bladder** with bite-valve inside the respirator/helmet (no open drinking)
- **Spill-proof, double-contained reservoirs** (primary + secondary containment)
- **Waste capture**:
  - urine bag system or sealed canisters
  - solid waste bags with desiccant
- **Sweat/condensate management**:
  - absorbent liners you can seal and pack out
  - small field desiccant packs (kept contained so you’re not dropping granules everywhere)

### 4) Static / electrical safety
- **ESD-aware outer layer** (anti-static fabric) and **grounding strategy**:
  - you do *not* want to be the best conductor between a resonant tower and a living cable-bridge
- **Non-conductive trekking pole** (ceramic/fiberglass) for balance and probing
- Keep **metal tools** inside insulating wraps; don’t casually touch embedded luminous “veins.”

### 5) Hearing, comms, and navigation
- **Hearing protection** (active or high-NRR) that still allows comms
- **Comms that don’t rely on quiet**:
  - throat mic or bone-conduction headset works well under wind roar
- **Navigation**:
  - visual landmarks may be silhouettes; bring **LIDAR/sonar-like mapping** if available
  - otherwise: high-contrast markers, inertial nav, and a strict “don’t wander alone” rule

### 6) Shelter for dust storms
- **Storm bivy / micro-tent** designed for abrasive wind
- **Sealant tape + patch kit** (dust will abrade seams and gaskets)
- **Spare filter media** (plan to replace often)

## Arrival protocol (first 30 minutes on the plateau)

1. **Do not step out “to feel the air.”** You will pay for curiosity with cracked lips and a headache.
2. **Run a seal check**: suit, respirator, exhale condenser, glove cuffs, boot gaiters.
3. **Set your baseline**:
   - pulse ox reading
   - temperature and wind chill estimate
   - filter status
4. **Establish your “wetness containment” routine immediately**:
   - no spitting
   - no wiping sweat onto surfaces
   - no open food
   - any moisture event goes into a sealable wipe and into containment

If there are locals/maintenance entities, expect the first interaction to be a **moisture compliance check** (formal or informal). Treat it as you would a border biosecurity inspection.

## How to move without causing trouble (or getting hurt)

### Don’t treat the plant-architecture as inert
- Those “walkways” that braid and hum are *alive and energized*.
- Step where the locals step; avoid “quiet shortcuts” across plates/mats that look strong—many are functional membranes.

### Keep clearance from resonant cavities and intake spires
- Intake/dehumidifier monuments may create **strong airflow gradients** and **pressure zones** that mess with your respirator and balance.
- Resonant corridors can spike sound levels; use hearing pro proactively, not after pain starts.

### Crossing open mineral courts
- They’re navigationally tempting (flat, open) and ecologically dangerous:
  - predators track movement easily
  - salts can be slick or abrasive
  - static charge buildup tends to be worse in open, dry expanses  
Move fast, in a tight group, with preplanned exit points.

## Eating, drinking, and medical care (practical reality)

### Hydration and calories
- You will need **more water than usual**, but you must treat every milliliter as hazardous cargo.
- Prefer:
  - concentrated rations that don’t crumble into dust
  - foods that don’t require open preparation
- Drink through an internal port only; never break mask seal outdoors.

### First aid adjustments
- **Nosebleeds and cracked mucosa** are common: carry nasal moisture-barrier gel and sealed swabs.
- **Eye protection is medical protection** here; grit scratches become infections fast.
- **Hypoxia symptoms** (headache, nausea, clumsiness) should trigger an automatic rule:
  - stop
  - oxygen on
  - warm up
  - reassess navigation

### Contamination and local chemistry
Assume local surfaces are optimized for “no free water.” Your sweat/condensation can cause:
- material stress
- chemical reactions
- social alarm  
So treat accidental drips like you dropped solvent in a cleanroom: isolate, wipe, seal, report if appropriate.

## Social / civic etiquette (how not to become a walking incident)

- **Announce moisture risks up front** if you must remove gear (medical emergency, filter failure). Ask for a designated bay.
- **Never cut, sample, or “collect a souvenir”** from living structures. Cutting releases gels/volatiles that may be:
  - toxic to you
  - attractive/repellent to fauna
  - functionally equivalent to severing cables in their grid
- If you see warning sigils about moisture, treat them as **high severity**, not advisory.
- Don’t cry “openly” if you can help it (seriously). If you’re emotionally affected, turn away, use absorbent liner, seal it.

## Weather and storm posture (what you do when the world closes)

### Early storm cues
- wind pitch shifts (towers “sing higher”)
- light drops further (dust thickening aloft)
- infrastructure quiets or changes rhythm (vents seal, corridors dim)

### Your storm checklist
1. **Stop traveling**. Distance misleads in low light + dust.
2. **Find a sheltered lee** that is *not* an intake/dehumidifier throat.
3. **Seal everything**: goggles, cuffs, vents, comms ports.
4. **Switch to storm filters** (or protect primary filters with sacrificial layers).
5. **Anchor yourself**: abrasive wind can literally sand-blast you off balance.
6. **Wait for the reopen**: the city’s “posture” will relax; that’s your signal.

Plan like a sailor: you don’t “beat” this wind; you adopt the correct stance and let it pass.

## Interacting with fauna (basic rules that work)

- **Assume anything ceramic-chitinous is dry-adapted and bite-resistant.** Don’t try to shoo with bare hands.
- **Don’t chase gliders** or stand under thermal plumes to watch them; those plumes are also infrastructure exhaust and can be electrically “lively.”
- If you hear coordinated clicking that starts to synchronize with your movement: **you’re being mapped** (by animals or by the signal-web). Change direction, reduce noise, tighten formation.

## Packing out and departure (leave without leaving a scar)

Before you exit:
- run a **waste inventory** (nothing left behind, especially moisture-bearing trash)
- do a **dust decon** on your suit so you don’t import abrasive salts to your next world
- isolate any gear that may have become “wet-contaminated” by your own condensation leaks

If the locals offer a dehumidification/decon chamber, take it. It’s not hospitality; it’s how their world stays stable.

## A realistic “ordinary human” travel plan (recommended)

- **Duration:** 2–6 hours on first visit, no overnight.
- **Party size:** 2–4 people, never solo.
- **Route:** designated civic corridors only; no open-plateau wandering until you’ve proven your seals and storm timing.
- **Abort criteria:** any one of these triggers immediate return:
  - respirator seal compromise
  - uncontrolled moisture leak (water spill, bladder puncture)
  - pulse ox below your safe threshold despite O₂
  - dust storm onset without nearby shelter

## Mental preparation: what it feels like

Expect a place that is:
- **dim but not dark**
- **cold but not snowy**
- **alive but mineral-hard**
- **so loud it becomes physical**
- and socially: **quietly strict about moisture**, the way some facilities are strict about fire or radiation.

If you arrive sealed, dry-contained, oxygen-supported, and respectful of the living grid, you can walk through a city that sings without becoming one more problem it has to solve.
