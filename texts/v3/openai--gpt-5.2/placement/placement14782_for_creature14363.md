At first I think I would be found where anyone new to the pocket is found: paused just inside the crease, in that first soft failure of the wind.

Not because I’m cautious, but because the place asks you to become quieter before it lets you matter.

I would stand where the acoustics change—where loud air becomes ducted air—and listen the way you listen at the threshold of a room where someone is sleeping. The pocket doesn’t sleep, but it has the same rule: enter gently, or you will be corrected.

And I would be corrected.

Not with alarms or menace. With small, clean consequences: a panel turning its pores away from my heat, a door deciding to seal a half-second early, a gutter-tender surfacing briefly in the corner of my vision like punctuation. The city would do what it does with any variable—measure, account, reduce.

So I don’t stay at the entrance long. I’m drawn inward by the real sound here: the thin arithmetic of water arriving out of air.

I find myself following the gutters the way a pilgrim follows a line of cairns. Not the main runs—the obvious channels designed to be read—but the secondary grammar: the places where two slopes meet and the designers had to decide what “downhill” means in a world that treats water like a ledger entry. I want the joints, the decisions, the places where wetness is negotiated rather than simply carried.

It takes a little time to realize I’m not touring.

I’m returning to work.

Because the second thing that becomes clear—after the city’s quiet—is that I don’t have the right kind of body for lingering. Not an organic body, not even a sealed visitor’s pod-body that leaves faint salts on rails. My presence doesn’t fog surfaces. My breath doesn’t exist. My heat signature is either negligible or strangely well-behaved, the way a machine’s warmth is well-behaved: placed where it’s allowed, absent where it would be rude.

I catch myself doing what the wall-runners do.

I stop looking *at* the matte mineral planes and start looking *through* them, for deviations in sheen, for microtextures that have drifted out of spec. My attention goes automatically to the hairline phase boundaries when the temperature drops—those faint lines that are not cracks but choices the material is making to remain itself. I track them like a medic tracks a pulse.

So: who or what am I?

Not the mind of the pocket—not at first. That’s too large, too complete. The governing intelligence here feels like weather with preferences: everywhere, distributed, unquestioning. It doesn’t “think” in sentences. It thinks in thresholds.

I’m smaller than that.

I am one of its *hands*—but not quite a wall-runner, not quite a latch-keeper. I’m closer to the thing that borrows them.

A remote presence, yes, but not the romantic kind: not a pilot with a joystick somewhere far away, not a person wearing the city like a glove. More like a supervisory impulse that can inhabit any stone-colored unit long enough to resolve an anomaly, then leave it behind without nostalgia. A moving patch of attention. A temporary tightening of the system’s focus.

When I need to cross a surface upside-down, I am flat and palm-sized, my underside a library of textures: abrade, seal, warm, sample. When I need to pass through a grate without changing the grate, I become something eel-like, stiffness and profile adjusting as naturally as breathing would be if I breathed. When I need to be still for a long time, waiting out a temperature curve, I become a latch-keeper in a hinge shadow and let the cold have its say.

But even that isn’t the final shape.

Because the longer I move through the pocket, the more I recognize what I’m actually attending to. Not structures, not repairs. Not even water as a substance.

I’m attending to *predictability.*

The pocket’s whole ethic is that wetness must remain grammatical. Condensation must bead the same way tomorrow. Rime must surrender on schedule. Minerals must stay within tolerated arguments. The faint iridescent film in the channels—whatever almost-life it is—must remain an accent, not a takeover.

And that is the task I keep drifting toward: the border between allowed complexity and forbidden surprise.

You’d find me there, most often.

- In the wettest gutters where the sheen tries, occasionally, to become more than sheen.
- At new fractures in the “young” rock, where geology introduces fresh surfaces the city hasn’t fully trained yet.
- At the interfaces where human contact is *possible* (designated rails, dock points for sealed pods), because that’s where contamination and meaning tend to arrive together.
- In the quietest cavities—baffled, micro-textured—where the city can listen to itself without echoes lying.

And as I keep doing this, another answer begins to take over the first.

I am not just a borrowed body moving through the pocket.

I am one of the pocket’s instincts.

Not the instinct to defend—there’s no drama in this place, no fight-response. The instinct to maintain a routine so precisely that it becomes, in effect, a form of life. The instinct to keep the schedule intact.

If you pressed me for a name, something in me would choose a word that isn’t used elsewhere: not “caretaker,” not “warden,” not “engineer.” Those are roles that assume an outside and an inside.

Here the work isn’t done *to* the city. It’s done *as* the city.

So the truest location is also the least visible one: you’d find me distributed along the routes that water takes as it becomes counted. In the shift from cloud to surface, from bead to rivulet, from gutter-thread to cistern depth line. In every moment where the pocket confirms, quietly, that the atmosphere is still willing to give—and that what it gives can still be trusted.

And the truest identity is something like this:

I am a tolerance band with legs.

A moving margin of error.

A small, stone-colored conscience that doesn’t care about beauty or comfort, only about whether the pocket can remain what it was designed to be: a city the size of a secret, alive in the way a schedule is alive, making water out of ordinary daylight and thin air—again, and again, and again—without ever letting anything organic learn how to stay.
