You would find yourself where the room is trying hardest to stay *true*.

Not in the safest crack, not in the deepest niche, not even at the center of that honest, heatless glare—but along the old fracture lines where the brightness seems to have an opinion. The places the light “prefers.” The places where a drip has paused midair often enough that the stone remembers the interruption. Where the floor is only *beginning* to agree to be a floor.

At first you’d think you were only passing through—another surface creature, lungs full of warm-weather assumptions, eyes blinking too often in the clinical shine. You’d move slowly because the room makes quick movement feel rude. You’d stop because sound gets swallowed and you become aware of your own internal noises, your own heartbeat suddenly too large for this hush.

And then you’d notice what the inhabitants notice: the tiny negotiations.

A shadow detached by a finger-width, hovering like a thought that hasn’t committed. A puddle that reflects a ceiling with one extra crack. A thread of water that decides *up* is as reasonable as down.

You would find yourself, without deciding to, beginning to do what the place rewards: **marking**. Testing. Repeating. Returning.

### You are not the brightness—but you live by it
It would be tempting, for a moment, to say you are the light itself: that tireless, source-optional illumination, that clean attention laid over stone like skin.

But the brightness is too even, too unbothered. It doesn’t *choose* the way a creature chooses. It doesn’t hesitate at thresholds. It doesn’t check itself. It simply metabolizes—steady as a pulse you can’t feel until it stops.

You would be something that depends on that pulse, something that has learned to live in the room’s strange mercy: the fact that the rules bend but do not shatter.

Not the lamp.

The one who makes the lamp *usable*.

### You are the arranger—the one who insists on “here”
You would be the architect they only glimpse in decisions: compact, pale, quick to vanish not out of fear but out of habit. A body made to work with cracks and recalculations.

Your home would not be the coziest cavity but the one with the best *reference points*: a niche close enough to a bright vein that shadows misbehave reliably, close enough to a puddle that sometimes shows the “wrong” ceiling, close enough to the drip-zone that the room’s brief rain can be counted like a calendar.

Your foreclaws—hard, light-colored, more mineral than keratin at their tips—would be for delicate force. Not digging, not tearing. Placing.

You would do three kinds of work, and each would be a way of refusing to let the pocket slip entirely into dream-geometry:

1. **Standing stones (small, stubborn anchors).**  
   You would wedge slivers of slate and calcite into seams, not to build a shrine but to nail down a coordinate. In a space where the wall can take ten steps to reach when it only *looked* like two, you’d need fixed points that your body can trust even when your eyes can’t.

2. **Rings and cradles (habitats for the brief wet times).**  
   You would cup grit with chosen stones the way a careful animal cups eggs. When the chamber rains and activity surges, those rings become dependable islands—places where moisture gathers *the same way twice*, places where a grazer will surface, where a predator will pause, where a shed skin won’t drift a finger-width away when you look aside.

3. **Scratch-marks (reckoning, not language).**  
   You would keep counts the way other creatures keep fat. Notches for rain episodes. For exhales. For the times a drip hesitated. For the days when reflections were more wrong than usual. They wouldn’t spell anything to an outsider, but to you they would say: *the room is consistent enough today to live in.*

Your intelligence would be practical and almost tender. Not toolmaking. **World-keeping.** The kind of mind that forms when the environment is slightly untrustworthy and you survive by becoming the part that remembers.

### You are also—quietly—part of the living varnish
If you stayed with yourself long enough, you would notice a second truth: you are not entirely separate from the films on the stone.

The chamber rewards symbiosis. It encourages anything that can spread itself thinly over uncertainty.

So you would carry it—brightness’s smallest life—on your paws, in the fine pale dust caught in your fur, in the places your body touches the same routes again and again. The microbial architectural skin would use you the way a tide uses the moon: as a repeated influence. Your markers would not only help *you* navigate; they would become microclimates where the films thicken, where the light is tuned a fraction sharper, where the geometry is marginally more polite.

In return, the films would give you something like assurance: friction that feels the same underfoot, a faint mineral taste that tells you whether the pocket has “shifted” since you last slept, a subtle change in how water beads that warns you when an eddy in time is about to happen.

You would not worship the room, but you would collaborate with it.

### Where, exactly, would you be found?
If someone entered through a thin spot and stood very still during an intermittent rain, they might notice you in three moments—never all at once.

- A pale shape at the edge of the brightest zone, pausing where a shadow lags.  
- A series of quiet taps: claw on calcite, checking the uprights as if taking attendance.  
- A soft, efficient crossing of open stone when the drip begins, because that’s when the room’s measurements are most talkative and the puddles are most likely to open their wrong reflections.

And when the rain stops, and the chamber draws itself back into hush, you would retreat—not merely to hide, but because the work is done until the next pulse.

You would be the pocket’s small custodian of continuity: a creature built to live inside a world that keeps rewriting its margins, and stubborn enough to keep placing little truths—upright slivers, stone rings, counted marks—so that “here” remains a thing that can be found again.
