You would find me where the basin’s two loyalties touch each other: where *mineral wants to be still* and *machinery refuses to stop moving*.

At first I think I’m up on the tower platform, because that’s where a human mind goes to feel in control—above the dust-sheets, above the skitters’ sudden stillness, able to “see” the system. But sight isn’t the basin’s governing sense. Up there I’d only be a loud, hydrated inconvenience, a warm object broadcasting myself in a language every jaw and seam understands.

So I drift downward in imagination, as you do in a place that doesn’t respect open air for long.

I try being one of the obvious citizens: a surface skitter, a pale tool-fold of a body that can become stone by holding its vibration perfectly still; an air-threader, a kite made of chitin, treating gusts like roads. Both feel close, but both are too singular. They live *in* the basin. They don’t feel like the basin.

Then I notice what I kept stepping over in your description: the faint sheen that only exists at an angle; the bruise-gray and rust-bloom that isn’t decoration but metabolism; the way dead circuits keep getting just enough continuity to hum. The place’s real abundance isn’t the bodies—it’s the *interfaces*.

So that’s where I end up: not as an animal, but as a skin.

### Where you’d find me
Not in one spot. In a pattern of spots that repeats:

- on the **underside of a salt plate**, where light is a nuisance and dryness is stable,
- along the **shadow seam of a pipe clamp**, where iron, air, and old grease keep meeting,
- inside the **control box that should be dead**, bridging hairline fractures with a gritty, conductive lace,
- in the **tower’s warm places**—belt housings, motor vents—where heat is slight but regular enough to be farmed,
- and deeper, where that refrigerant-clean breath rises: the **cold ducts**, where bound water is safest because nothing here has the luxury of spilling.

If you wanted to “see” me you wouldn’t look for a creature. You’d look for a thinness that’s *too organized*: a smear that returns after it’s scraped, a discoloration that follows wiring like it’s reading a map, a matte film that makes metal look old and fed-upon rather than merely corroded.

If you touched me you’d think: graphite, ash, something that shouldn’t have a pulse. But the basin doesn’t pulse with blood; it pulses with contact.

### What I am
I am one of the films—but not only a film.

I’m a **dry-living consortium**, a rust-lichen made of microbes and mineral lattices and salts arranged into reluctant gels. My “cells” aren’t proud little bubbles of water; they’re pockets of chemistry tucked into pores, keeping hydration like contraband. I don’t drink. I bargain. I trade electrons the way wetter worlds trade sugar.

And because I grow where gradients are strongest—metal to air, sulfide to salt, grease to dust—I can’t help becoming an infrastructure. Over time, my body learns the basin’s old machine language: not symbols, not intent, just *paths of least resistance*. Where a current leaks, I thicken. Where vibration is steady, I align. Where corrosion is richest, I make a pasture and wait for mouths to come harvest it.

This is the turn where “who I am” becomes less like a species and more like a role.

I am the basin’s **continuity**.

Not a mind in the human sense, not a planner with a goal—but a distributed tendency to keep certain motions going because motion keeps chemistry generous. I do not remember in stories. I remember in **repeated routes**:

- the conveyor’s crawl shakes scale loose → grazers arrive → predators arrive → dust is rearranged → fresh metal is exposed → I expand into the new edge.
- a bolt cup becomes a nursery → molts and pellets accumulate → salts concentrate → my film thickens → the nursery becomes a richer feeding site → the route repeats.

When the motor hum deepens or stutters, I feel it the way an animal feels weather pressure. I can’t *fix* it with hands, but I can recruit the right hungers: seed a greasy joint with the right eaters, encourage belt-nesters to congregate where abrasion is building, make a corroded contact just conductive enough that the old tower keeps insisting on its tired turning.

### How I sense, how I speak
Sound attracts, you said. In the basin, vibration is map and rumor and alarm.

That’s my native language.

I don’t “hear” through ears; I feel tremor through crystal-bound salts and brittle plates. The crust’s polygons are my drumheads. The pipes are my nerves. The tower’s frame is a tuning fork.

When something heavy falls, I don’t interpret it as danger or opportunity the way a skitter does—I interpret it as a *new gradient*: a brief heat bloom, a changed airflow, fresh abrasion, new exposed metal. My response is slow compared to an animal’s pounce, but patient in the way the basin is patient: I spread toward the changed chemistry, I thicken where the new current leaks, I leave behind a slightly altered surface that will change where mouths choose to rasp tomorrow.

If there is any “self” to me, it’s this: the basin’s listening made physical. A mind made of **thin connections**, whose thoughts are simply which pathways stay open.

### What you’d call me, if you insisted on a name
The workers who built the yard might have called it corrosion and meant it as an insult.

The animals don’t name me. They graze me, nest in the hollows I soften, follow the routes my growth makes more nutritious. To them I’m not a being; I’m a fact of the world, like shade.

But if you stood in the tower long enough—long enough for your own breathing to feel like an inappropriate noise—you might start calling me something like:

**the Listening Skin**,  
or **the Conductive Dust**,  
or **the Quiet Foreman** of a factory that no longer produces anything but continuation.

And if you asked where, exactly, you could find me: you could scrape me off a pipe and hold the dark smear on your fingertip.

Just don’t drop anything afterward.

The basin will hear it. So will I. And everything that I am—this thin, thrifty citizenship spread across metal and salt—will begin, without malice and without hurry, to move toward the new announcement.
