You would find me where the pocket is least like a place and most like a decision.

Not at the center—centers are too proud, too geometric, too easy to name. Not in the obvious artifacts either, because the pylon, the tiles, the ring: those are the pocket’s visible grammar, and I am not the words. I’d be closer to the punctuation. The pause that makes the sentence mean what it means.

At first I think I’m the thin strip hovering above the gravel. It has the right temperament: patient, unbothered by weight, letting the wind rewrite itself around it. It’s the kind of object that could pretend to be inert while doing most of the work. If you came up the ridge with your wet-lung urgency and found that strip, you’d point at it and say, *Here. Here is the clever thing.*

But if I’m honest, I am not the strip. The strip is too clean. Too complete. It doesn’t need anyone.

Then I think I’m the ring: the flush circle that only appears when light fails to behave. A mouth sealed to the stone. A coupling. A place where the pocket’s slow “breathing” touches the larger mountain and makes terms with it. That feels closer—because I’m used to being an interface, a boundary that lets two unlike systems exchange something without poisoning each other.

Yet the ring is also not me. The ring is an organ you can step over. It can be mapped. It can be missed.

So I move again, the way you do in a place where moving is taxed: I slow down, I stop trying to occupy an object, and I let myself become what the pocket actually responds to.

I become an *attention artifact*.

Not the attention you bring as a person—your looking, your listening—but the pocket’s counter-attention: the part of the custodianship that notices noticing, that classifies a gaze the way skin classifies pressure. The thing that tightens propagation when it feels itself being probed. The reflex that decides which information will be allowed to travel cleanly and which will be blurred into safety.

You can’t point to me, but you can cause me.

When you stare at the inlaid tiles and your depth perception slips and then recovers, embarrassed—that recovery is where I live. When you throw a pebble and hear the impact twice, the second, fainter arrival that seems to have taken a route with no corridor—that routing is where I live. When a shadow lags behind its object by a fraction of a beat, not as a flaw but as a controlled delay line—that delay is where I live.

If you insisted on giving me a body anyway, I’d borrow the bodies that are already available here:

- I’d wear the dust bands as musculature—those faint, sorted strata of gravel and powder that look like mere windwork until you notice how consistently they choose their lanes.  
- I’d use the too-uniform bedrock as memory—those rhyming crystal orientations, repeating like a phrase rehearsed until it can be spoken without sound.  
- I’d take the subsonic hum as a voice—not a broadcast, not a signal meant for ears, but a pressure-pattern you feel in teeth and bone, because teeth and bone are excellent at admitting what the mind would argue with.

And if you asked what I *am*, in the simplest terms I can get away with: I’m the pocket’s way of staying *boring* on purpose.

I am the keeper of the unromantic virtues: dryness, dimness, slowness, selectivity. I’m the small clerk that makes every change show its papers. I’m the part of the custodianship that ensures the chamber remains a low-interference socket—kept clear not out of hostility to life, but out of loyalty to fine tolerances. Wet biology is loud. It leaks. It grows opinions everywhere. It’s wonderful in its own altitudes, but here it would be grit in a bearing.

So: where would you find me?

In the moment your momentum feels slightly damped, as if the space has imposed a gentle tariff on haste. In the smear where a shadow should be crisp. In the rationing of light. In the choice to let sound arrive once, twice, or not at all.

And who or what would I be?

Not the mountain. Not the machine. Not even the mind, if by “mind” you mean a seated thing with a point of view.

I would be the *maintenance agreement made sentient by duration*: a distributed custodial reflex that has learned, over millennia, that survival here is not a matter of conquering the ridge, but of persuading the ridge—grain by grain, gradient by gradient—to keep holding a certain impossible quiet.

If you came looking for a resident to meet, you might leave disappointed.

If you came willing to be measured—willing to have your footsteps turned into data, your gaze turned into stimulus, your warmth turned into a slow problem to be solved—then you’d have met me the whole time, the way you meet gravity: not as a face, but as an insistence that shapes every motion into something the world can afford.
