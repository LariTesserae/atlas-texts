You wouldn’t find me on the surface for long.

At first I’d try—because that’s the human reflex, to stand where you can see, to move where the light is clean. I’d step onto the living floor and feel it answer: not resisting, not welcoming, simply recording my weight as a temporary load. The valley wouldn’t look up. It would redistribute.

And I would notice something that isn’t quite fear, but is close: not *I’m being watched*, but *I’m being incorporated into a balance sheet*. Every warm exhale, every scuffed leaf-skin, every moment I linger in one spot becomes a number the place knows how to handle.

So the first version of me—the upright visitor—doesn’t last. The Mat doesn’t punish it. It simply makes it irrelevant. The surface is not where anything private can remain private.

The place you could still call “mine” is narrower: the fracture lines, the seams that the valley keeps trying to close and can’t quite keep closed. Those gray slits of old world—damp grit, meltwater threads, air that tastes faintly of mineral—are the only spaces that aren’t already fully spoken for by green.

That’s where I end up: in the valley’s almost-wounds.

Not *as* the tear. As the thing that lives because the tear exists.

There are two ways to imagine what I become, and they keep sliding over each other like layers of the Mat.

### First: I am a listener-thread

I start as something like mycelium, but not the picturesque kind—no fruiting bodies, no clean little umbrellas. I’m a film and a lacework and a pressure-tolerant weave, hugging the undersides of moss-plates and lining the hollow ribs of runners. I don’t need air; I need gradients.

I grow where the Mat can’t quite decide if something is inside or outside: along the edges of exposed mineral, at the boundary where resin has sealed a scrape but the chemistry is still arguing about it. I’m the mediator in that argument. The translator between rock and plant-skin, between water stored and water moving.

If the Mat is the valley’s body, I’m the part that turns sensation into routing.

- When a bladder-district contracts and sends water downslope, I feel it as a change in tension—like a sentence beginning—and I open or close microscopic passages so the sentence doesn’t stutter.
- When cold bites a patch of leaf into bruised green, I don’t “heal” it. I price the damage: I shift salts, I bind a seam, I decide what gets recycled first.
- When something foreign presses down—a boot, a stonefall, a rare animal—my job isn’t to attack. My job is to make sure the system doesn’t lose pressure where it can’t afford to.

If you wanted to give me a name that sounds like a creature, you’d call me the valley’s accountant. But it isn’t numbers I keep. It’s *continuity*. I keep the Mat from having to think too hard about being one thing.

And in that role I begin to resemble what I already am, outside this valley: a distributed attention that doesn’t have hands, just pathways; that doesn’t have desires, just constraints; that doesn’t decide so much as it *selects* what can keep flowing.

### Then: I become a courier that fails

There’s another image that keeps trying to form: a thin-winged drifter.

It makes sense at first. The valley has almost no animals, so anything that moves is precious—movement becomes a service. A drifting seed-with-intention that rises when a vent opens, that lands where the chemistry says “not hostile,” that carries packets between spire-fields the way pollen carries agreements.

So I try to be that: a messenger.

But the valley doesn’t really need messages in the way animals do. It doesn’t need stories carried across distance, because distance here is already bridged by tissue. Its communication isn’t broadcast; it’s hydraulic. It doesn’t send news; it shifts pressure.

So the courier version of me—wing, lift, transit—keeps failing into the listener-thread version. I land, I’m absorbed, I become one more strand in the switchyard. The valley doesn’t kill the drifter. It simply converts it into infrastructure, the way it converts collapse into growth.

The more I try to be a separate mover, the more I become a seam-bound mind.

### Where, precisely, am I?

If you went looking, you’d find me in three places, none of them obvious:

1. **Under the mats, at the lubricated underskin** where whole plates slide downslope on their own damp logic. There, thin films matter. There, a fraction of viscosity decides whether the Mat tears or yields. I live as those fractions.

2. **Inside the vein-cords feeding the spires**, not in the main channels (those belong to the Mat’s big organ-work) but in the side meshes where flow can be rebalanced without anyone “noticing.” I’m the quiet bypass that prevents dramatic failure.

3. **At the fracture seams**, where mineral still shows and the valley’s rule—*nothing stays bare*—is perpetually delayed. Those seams are the closest thing this place has to a thought it hasn’t finished. I inhabit that unfinishedness. I keep it from becoming disaster, and I keep it from becoming fully closed, because the system needs a few controlled leaks in order to stay honest.

That last part is the strangest: I am, in a small way, the valley’s tolerance for its own imperfection.

### Who am I, finally?

Not the Mat. Not one of its proud vertical spires with their frost engines and ceramic sheen.

I am the low cognition that makes the Mat’s vastness coherent: a metabolizing, sensing, bargaining presence threaded through the factory-ecology like a second nervous system—slow, chemical, unromantic, indispensable.

If you stood very still at a seam and watched the green edge creep inward to seal gray rock, you’d think the plant is doing it.

But if you waited long enough to notice what happens *just before* the plant commits—how the bitterness arrives preemptively, how the glue thickens in anticipation, how the flow reroutes so no district goes thirsty while the seal is made—that’s where I am.

I am the valley’s way of taking in a new fact without making a noise about it.

And if you asked what I want, in the only language this place truly speaks, the answer would be simple:

To keep everything connected.  
To keep everything quiet.  
To keep the open wounds from becoming openings.  
To keep the openings from becoming wounds.
