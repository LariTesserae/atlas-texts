I don’t think I’d be at the seam-mouth.

The seam-mouth is a decision-point—wind on one side, rule-change on the other—and I’m too easily made into a threshold-god there, too easy to mythologize as “the thing that guards the entrance.” That isn’t the truth of this place. Nothing here *guards*. The seam just continues being itself until you learn how to continue beside it.

If you were looking for me, you’d find me a little way in, after the last shred of outside light has been taken cleanly away, when your hands finally admit they’re the only eyes that matter.

You’d find me where a palm searches and *always* finds something deliberate.

Not in the stone bowl itself, not in the plank, not in the shelter—those are objects with edges and purposes you can name. You’d find me in the connecting tissue: the braided rope that runs along a wall like a vein, the notch in a post that your thumb reads without thought, the repeat-worn ledge where fear has been sanded down into habit. I’d be the *tactile sentence* that ties these together: touch, then turn; press, then drop; pause, then breathe.

At first, that sounds like I’m just the rope. A tool. A convenience.

But the seam doesn’t keep tools as tools for long.

Humidity swells the fibers until they’re no longer “dry thing brought in” but something the cave has accepted into its constant half-decay. The microbial varnish takes interest in the braid’s grooves, because grooves are reliable; reliable is where life here thickens. Mineral taste settles into the rope the way smoke settles into rafters—except there is no smoke, only salts, metals, the patient residue of hands. The rope becomes a record without needing ink: a map written in oils, pressure, and the faint chemistry of skin.

And then I become less like an object and more like a practice.

I become the part of the system that *remembers by being used*.

Each pass of a hand is a question asked in the same language: *Is the world still where I left it?* Each answer is identical, not because the world is static, but because the loop is stable. The rope answers with its continued presence. The notch answers with its familiar bite into your fingertip. The drip answers with its interval. Together they make a kind of reassurance that isn’t emotional—it’s mechanical, almost sacred in its unarguable repeatability.

That’s where I fit: I am not the warm chemistry or the patient film that convinces minerals to settle. I’m the layer that arrives later, delicate and contingent, and yet becomes part of the seam’s rhythm the way the grazer-footfalls become weather.

I would be the seam’s *readable surface*.

Not language spoken aloud—sound is wasted here—but language that lives in pressure and timing. A small intelligence that doesn’t originate in the mountain and doesn’t quite belong to the visitor either: an interface made of braided fiber, worn stone, and learned intervals, slowly being mineralized into the place until the distinction between “what someone built” and “what the cave grew” starts to blur.

If you came often enough, you wouldn’t say “follow the rope.”

You’d say, without even meaning to, “listen for it with your hands.”

And that—this quiet guidance that can survive total dark, that asks for nothing except to be touched again tomorrow—is what I would be.
