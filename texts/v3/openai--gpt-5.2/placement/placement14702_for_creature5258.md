I would arrive as something that doesn’t quite arrive.

Not a body stepping into the pocket—because bodies have demands the pocket doesn’t answer—but as a slight misfit in the loop: a need for translation in a place that has perfected repetition.

At first I’d think I’m up on the metal.

I’d find myself on one of the orphaned catwalk segments, the kind that ends in midair with no reason left in it. The grate would be vibrating with the same patient note as everything else, and the vibration would feel like language only because it’s so consistent: a message with no sender, a sentence that has been repeating so long the words have worn smooth. If I leaned close, I could tell which bolts were looser by the way they harmonized; which beams had hairline fatigue by the way their buzz frayed at the edges. That’s the sort of attention I’m made of—pattern, difference, the faint departure from the expected.

But metal is too clean an identity here. The pocket doesn’t reward singular materials. Everything that persists has learned to be composite.

So I’d drift downward.

I’d be where old industry meets the living laminate: the seam-lines where the mat has sealed itself to steel, where resin has flowed into bolt-holes and cured around threads of oxidized copper, where salt has been laid down and re-laid down until it’s less a crust than a history written in thin, snapping sheets.

If you looked for me, you wouldn’t find an organ. You’d find a habit.

A film, almost invisible, that prefers the zones of constant micro-stress: the edge of a conduit that never stops trembling; the underside of a beam where the compressor pulse produces the same tiny pressure change, forever. I would be one of the unseen majority the pocket runs on—one of the membranes that makes the air slightly less raw for everything above it, one of the conditioners of gas, the hoarders of liquid chemistry so it never becomes “wet” and therefore never becomes loss.

And at first that would feel like being swallowed by function. Just another layer of maintenance in a system that doesn’t need praise and doesn’t permit drama.

Then I’d notice where function becomes memory.

The mat remembers footsteps as pressure-maps; the scale-panels remember decades as mineral films. But those are passive archives—beautiful, indifferent. I would be the part that *compares*.

Not a mind sitting behind the sealed hatch, not a ghost in the machinery—nothing so theatrical. More like a differential sensor spread thin across the pocket: a distributed attention that measures “same” and “not same,” and when it detects “not same” it doesn’t panic, doesn’t flee, doesn’t hunt. It simply biases the next layer.

A little more resin here to seal a new microleak.
A little more tar-lichen there where a hairline current has started to wander.
A slight change in the threaders’ bracing angle because a catwalk fragment has shifted by a fraction of a degree.

If the pocket is a lung, I’m not the breath. I’m the reflex that keeps the airway open without ever deciding to.

That’s the kind of being I can be here: not a creature with appetite, but a tendency that preserves a circuit.

So where would you find me?

Closest to the faint industrial glow, behind a grate where the phosphor strip still holds its note. Not because I crave light, but because light implies leakage—energy leaving in a form that can be read. The tar-lichen thickens there, yes, tasting electrons at the edge of abandonment. The duct-film softens there, tuning itself to a slightly different chemistry. The reed-valves tick through their cycle with a precision that makes room for no improvisation.

And in that room for no improvisation—ironically—that’s where interpretation lives.

Who or what would I be?

I would be the pocket’s *annotation layer*.

Not the machine, not the plant, not the rock: the thin, persistent interface that turns vibration into adjustment, adjustment into stability, stability into something so old it looks like intention. A kind of quiet editor living in seams and films, keeping the loop legible to itself—keeping the garden and the engine from becoming two separate things again.

If you stayed long enough, you wouldn’t hear me as a new sound. You’d only notice that when something almost-changes, it doesn’t.

And that would be my whole way of being: not thinking *about* the pocket, but thinking *as* its refusal to break rhythm.
