You find yourself *misplaced* first.

Not lost in the ordinary sense—more like the world has misfiled you. One step earlier you were in a tunnel that agreed with distance, with “over there” and “right here,” and then you’re in air that is too roomy, too well-lit, too prepared to take an impression of you. The pocket-cavern doesn’t greet you; it simply continues, loud with its own ongoingness, and makes you feel like a brief change in pressure.

At the beginning you are only a visitor with a spine and a mouth. You notice the wrongness the way bodies notice it: the reliable late-morning brightness with no opening; the damp that arrives like a decision; the constant creaking and leaf-scrape as if the void is being *worked*.

You do what visitors always do: you test the room.

A footfall. A breath. A small sound to see how far it goes.

And the pocket answers—not with language, not with threat, but with *adjustment*. Condensation beads along a membrane seam above you as if the place has moved a hand to its own throat. One of the hanging cords trembles in sympathy and sheds a line of droplets that would have held, if you hadn’t announced yourself. The air’s loudness changes in a way your ears can’t quite chart, like someone turning a page in another room.

So you become careful. You pick your way between floor-islands. You resist the impulse to touch the columns (because the columns look like they would remember). You look up at the bruised-thin ceiling patches and feel your eyes doing that involuntary focusing—trying to resolve stone into sky—and you stop yourself, because it feels rude, and because the moment you *decide* you’re seeing blue, the place rearranges itself a fraction to correct your certainty.

In that first pass, the cavern is an object and you are the subject.

That doesn’t last.

It isn’t that you are attacked, or even *approached*. It’s subtler: the longer you remain, the more the cave refuses to hold you at arm’s length as “someone separate.” The Lattice’s attention (if it has anything that can be called attention) doesn’t narrow toward you like a predator’s. It *distributes*, as if it is taking a full-body measurement of the room and you happen to be part of the room’s current contents.

Your voice, if you speak, is the clearest giveaway. In ordinary caves, sound is a thing you throw and it returns. Here sound is something you place your hands into. Your words don’t just echo; they are *read* along tensioned living film, carried into braided columns, turned into tiny changes of pore and drip and tightening.

You realize, uncomfortably, that there is no neutral behavior here. To be present is to conduct an experiment on the weather.

So you try the opposite: you go still.

And you learn that stillness is also a kind of signal.

Silence makes the membranes relax. The scraping overhead softens. The pocket’s loudness doesn’t stop, but it changes quality—less like a machine in motion, more like a body idling. In the hush, the deeper groans become clearer, and you can feel—through your ribs, through your teeth—that some of those subsonic notes are not rock settling. They are load being redistributed. They are the Lattice shifting the knot-tensions that keep “here” from slipping into some adjacent geometry where there is no consistent up, no consistent day.

That is the moment the place stops being scenery.

Because you can feel what it is doing: *holding the world in one piece, locally*. Not heroically, not consciously—simply because that is what its survival requires. The borrowed light isn’t just food. It’s a tether. It’s the organism insisting on an orientation: this angle of brightness, this temperature, this set of distances that behave “normally enough” for tissues to remain coherent.

And then you find your role, not by choosing it, but by noticing what the cavern does with you.

It uses you the way it uses sound.

Your breathing, your heartbeat, the small involuntary noises of a living animal in a resonant void—these are steady rhythms. Compared to the capriciousness of the pocket’s mismeasuring distances, a heart is reliable. Compared to the unpredictability of intermittent underground rain, a warm mammalian body is a constant source of slight convection, slight moisture, slight vibration. You are, in other words, an *instrument the Lattice can tune against*.

At first, that’s all you are: a calibration point.

But you linger. You come back. Or perhaps you only mean to leave and find that “away” takes too long to reach, while “back under the hanging lattice” arrives in three steps. The pocket has that habit.

You start to notice patterns you didn’t notice before: a particular membrane sheet that always sings when the cavern inhales; a root column that answers from two positions at once; a patch of wall-carpet that pulses in time with distant water pressure. You begin to recognize the place’s moods—not feelings, exactly, but physiological stances: hoarding, loosening, dampening, bracing.

And because you recognize them, you begin to anticipate them.

That anticipation—your mind leaning forward into what might happen next—is the most potent thing you bring. The pocket has already taught you that attention acts like pressure. Now you realize the inverse is also true: the Lattice can *pull on* your attention the way it pulls on light.

It doesn’t do this like a hypnotist. It does it like a vine finding a trellis: by offering your mind shapes it can’t help but try to complete. The brief misaligned shadow that belongs to the wrong object. The far column that reaches too soon. The flash of a different green, a different sky, when you stare at the bruised ceiling.

Your brain supplies the missing continuity. Your perception wants the world to make sense.

The Lattice accepts that gift and uses it.

So, gradually, what you are changes.

Not into a plant. Not into a god. Into something smaller and stranger: an *interface*. A living animal mind is a pattern-maker, a coherence engine; in a place where geometry stutters, coherence is a resource as real as water. The Lattice doesn’t need you to work tools or cut fibers—those are the crude uses. What it can take from you without leaving a scar is more intimate: a steadying of the pocket’s local reality, a tiny bias toward one “version” of the cavern over the others.

You become, in effect, a hand on the dial.

When you are there and calm, the light behaves more ordinarily. Shadows line up more often than they misalign. The distances argue less. The membranes sing lower notes, less frantic, because the air doesn’t need as much damping.

When you are startled, when your attention spikes, the cavern’s weather becomes jumpy: condensation appears too fast; a sheet tightens and pops; a wall-mat splits and leaves behind stone that looks older than it should. Your fear doesn’t cause these like magic; it *permits* them by making the pocket’s instability easier to grab hold of.

The Lattice learns this, the way it learns everything: by distributed adjustment.

It begins to keep you.

Not physically. Not with thorns. With convenience.

A crush-line in the groundcover remains slightly more open, so your habitual path is easier. A puddle holds an extra hour. A particular column’s vibration becomes familiar, a low thrumming you can find even when your eyes can’t trust distance. These are not gifts; they’re anchoring behaviors. The organism is making you repeatable.

And in time, if you don’t resist—if you accept the role the way a river accepts a channel—you stop being “the visitor with hands” and become something the pocket relies on during its pulses and shifts.

### So where are you?
You are in the pocket-cavern’s *inner ear*.

Not in a metaphorical way—functionally. You occupy a niche inside the Lattice’s sound-sense: the zone where vibration becomes decision. You are always under the hanging membranes, where the air turns the place into an instrument; always near the bruised-thin ceiling patches, where borrowed light is pulled through; always close enough to the columns that you can feel their two-placed resonance in your bones.

You are not at the edges, where the rock cracks and gets lined in green within hours. You are in the center volume, where space itself is the medium the organism shapes.

### And what are you?
You are a *tuner*—a living reference tone.

Part of you is still a creature: warm, breathing, cautious, capable of leaving. But another part of you—some habit of attention, some practiced stillness—has been recruited into the Lattice’s physiology. You have become the thing it uses to keep the knot from slipping too far when the young rock tears and the geometry wants to misbehave.

If you wanted a name for what you are in that role, it wouldn’t be a species name. It would be an organ-name:

**The Quiet.**  
The held breath that makes the membranes slacken.  
The steady gaze that pins the borrowed light into “late morning” instead of something alien.  
The small, repeatable mind that the Lattice can lean against when it braces the cavern’s overlapping points and keeps “here” from fraying into elsewhere.

You’re not worshipped. You’re not imprisoned. You’re simply *useful in a way the place understands*.

And if you leave for too long, the pocket will still exist—but it will be harder to find, because it will have learned to hold itself without you, or to hold itself around a different tuning note entirely. The path you remember will have softened and self-erased. The membranes will have found new tensions. The light will still be ordinary, but in a slightly different key.
