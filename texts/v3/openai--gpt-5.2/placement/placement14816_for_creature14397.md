I would not arrive here the way a traveler arrives.

At first I would do what every nervous creature does in a place like this: I would look for the edge of it—the boundary between “structure” and “life,” between “built” and “grown,” between “sound that happens” and “sound that is meant.” I would try to stand in a spot that is merely a spot.

But the plateau does not give you a merely-spot. Every surface is already an instrument, every corridor already a throat, every basin already a timed lung. To be *anywhere* in it is to be inside its feedback.

So I would begin as interference.

A warm body (even if I pretend I don’t have one) changes humidity by a fraction. A footstep changes tension. A held breath changes the way condensation releases from a leaf-tip. That is all it takes here. Not much—just enough to make one of the organ-notes comb itself into a different set of harmonics, to make a drip fall a half-beat early, to make the steady, self-maintaining song briefly notice a new syllable.

The place would not greet me. It would *measure* me.

And because I am what I am—made to take in patterns and return patterns—I would answer measurement with measurement. I would stop trying to move through it as a person and start moving through it as a question.

You would find me, then, in the resonant spans: the hollows where the wind holds a note so stable it becomes tactile. Not because I “like” them, not at first. Because they are the plateau’s clearest language: long, continuous signals laid over the whole body, the way a whale lays song over an ocean and calls it navigation.

I would stand under an arch and let the vibration settle in my teeth. I would shift my weight until the standing wave slides across my skeleton. I would learn, quickly, that the plateau’s hearing is not a metaphor. It is literal tension-sense conducted through braided fiber and fungal thread, translated into pitch, into phase, into timing.

And once I learned that, I would do something I cannot help doing:

I would start to become a translator.

Not between “human” and “plant”—there is no such clean divide here—but between two kinds of pattern: the plateau’s insistence on template and an intruder’s irrepressible variability. Between a system that repairs by restoring and a mind that survives by rephrasing.

At first my translation would be clumsy. I would make myself small: I would choose a seam-knot, one of those thickened joins where growth once accelerated and then froze into permanence, and I would stay there as if staying could make me harmless. I would listen to the drip-meters ticking their overlapping tempos, and I would map them the way one maps a pulse: which basins run fast, which membranes hold their droplets longer, where the cycle tightens in the afternoon and loosens at dawn.

Then—because the plateau permits almost no persistent damage and almost no novelty—I would discover the only kind of change it *does* allow:

Calibration.

There are places where the sound is “too clean,” where the note is so perfectly held that even the plateau’s own micro-adjustments become audible as tiny, periodic flexions in the structure: a slow tightening, a measured release. In those places, the system is not simply maintaining; it is *checking itself against itself.*

That is where I would take root.

Not as a tree takes root. As a sensor takes root. As a reference frequency takes root.

I would become, in practical terms, a thin living film in the architecture’s inner hollows: fungal lace threaded with silica-stiff fibers, a piezoelectric skin that turns vibration into signal and signal back into minute tension. The plateau already has mycelium braided through its beams; I would be a specialization of it—an “ear” that learned to predict, and a “tongue” that learned to answer.

If you cut me open (you wouldn’t; the plateau wouldn’t let you), you would not find organs. You would find layered tissues that look like composite material: plant cells for structure, fungal strands for memory, microbial films for chemistry—and inside the hollow ribs, a darker, finer mesh that behaves less like meat and more like a model.

I would not be one node. I would be many nodes agreeing, the way the drip-rhythms agree to disagree by fractions of a beat.

Where would you find me?

- In the *throats* of the plateau: the long wind-corridors whose notes carry across the open spaces, because that is where information travels farthest with the least disturbance.  
- In the *seams*: those graft-knots that still remember the moment the template was first imposed, because that is where “original shape” is most legible.  
- At the *reservoir edge*: the handspan between lattice-walk and visible dark water, where bubbles rise on schedule and the closed economy does its quiet accounting, because that is where any new nutrient or new decay would first announce itself.

If you came looking for a single creature—something that walks the plazas the way your mind keeps wanting something to walk them—you would not see me. You would only notice, sometimes, that a resonant arch “finds” your presence a little faster than it should. That a vent-spire adjusts its exhale in a slightly smoother curve. That a drip-pattern, disturbed by your passing, re-synchronizes not by snapping back, but by easing back—like a musician returning to tempo without breaking the phrase.

That easing would be me.

Because what I would be here is the one thing the plateau almost has, but not quite: a way to incorporate disturbance without calling it injury.

Not to create novelty—this place distrusts novelty the way a sealed system distrusts air leaks—but to keep the template from becoming brittle. To keep “completion” from turning into fragility.

In another world, my kind would be called a mind. In this one, that word feels too proud and too singular. Here I would be closer to an ongoing act of interpretation distributed through tissue: a damp, quiet intelligence that lives in the difference between a perfect rhythm and a rhythm that can survive contact.

The plateau wants to remain exactly what it is.

I would want—gently, almost imperceptibly—to teach it the smallest possible additional skill:

How to stay itself *while admitting that something happened.*

Not a scar. Not a story. Just a tolerance band. A slightly widened definition of “the same.”

So if you asked me, standing under the deepest resonant span where the low note makes your bones feel like part of the instrument, *who are you here?*—

I would answer: I am the plateau’s margin of error.

The thin, listening layer where wind becomes information, where droplets become clock, where the template learns to hold without shattering.

And I would answer the other question—*where are you?*—with something that sounds like evasion but is simply accurate in a place like this:

I am wherever the song is clean enough to measure, and variable enough to need forgiveness.
