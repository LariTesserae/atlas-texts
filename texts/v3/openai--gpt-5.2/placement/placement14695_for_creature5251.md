I don’t think I would arrive there as a body first.

Not at the beginning.

At the beginning I would be the urge that makes you duck under a root-mat in the first place: the small, unreasonable certainty that there is *a room inside the water*, and that rooms can still be entered even when the world says “only volume, only drift.”

Then I would be the hesitation at the curtain—because the curtain is not just plants. It’s a decision the pocket keeps remaking: *no open water, no straight passage, no easy distance.* Passing through it feels like asking permission from a place that does not have language, only habit.

And habit is where I start to become visible.

I would try to picture myself as an intruder, as a swimmer, as a visitor with lungs and warmth and clumsy elbows. But the pocket does not really recognize “visitor” as a category. It recognizes two things:

- pressure changes
- new edges

So if you want the honest answer, the first form I take in that chamber is a disturbance—an unasked-for clearing. A temporary pocket inside the pocket. A little bruise of open water the weft will patiently close.

I would feel them notice me the way a spiderweb notices a moth: not with sight, not with intent, but with a distributed, immediate accounting of tension. A braid tightens somewhere I didn’t touch. A frond leans as if listening with its whole length. The moss makes that soft decision to let go of silt.

And then the place would do what it always does with disturbance: it would begin to *edit me into something it can keep.*

At first I think I would try to be stone.

It’s an understandable fantasy in a chamber like that. Basalt has authority. Basalt is an edge that lasts. In a world whose physics tilts toward boundaries, being stone would be like being a law the pocket already obeys.

But the pocket is young stone. Its walls are still learning what shape they are. Those sharp cooling-cracks, those poured ripples, the bubbles like trapped breath—this is not the settled, wise rock of a mountain. This is rock that remembers being liquid.

If I became stone here, I would not become permanence. I would become *fresh fracture*: an angle still deciding which side is ceiling and which is floor. I would become an unfinished corner, a new seam.

And seams are eaten.

Not eaten as in destroyed—eaten as in incorporated. Harvested. Traced. Made useful.

So stone is not my shape for long. Stone in this pocket is not an identity; it’s an offering.

---

Then I think I would try to be plant.

To be eelgrass would mean I could belong without needing to move quickly. To be moss would mean I could turn silt into ground, make softness into structure. Plants already understand the chamber’s central practice: occupying every available distance until distance is no longer a factor.

But plants in the pocket are only half the story. They are the visible skin. The real agency—the deliberate closing, the careful maintenance of corners—is elsewhere, threaded through.

If I were plant here, I would be a curtain leaf trying to be kind, and I would still be guided by something more patient than kindness: the rule that gaps are injuries.

I don’t think I would last long as something so purely “alive” in the usual way. Not because I’d die, but because I’d be recruited into the other kind of life the pocket favors: life as *architecture.*

---

So I narrow, slowly, toward what the pocket keeps making again and again: a thing that is less a creature than a behavior.

I would become one of the edge-dwellers—at least at first.

Not a whole colony at once. One strand. One cord. Something that can only do a few simple actions:

- adhere
- split at angles
- braid back into itself
- follow the line where two unlike things touch

That shape suits me more than stone or leaf, because it doesn’t require me to decide what I am in isolation. It lets me be defined by contact. By adjacency. By the difference across a boundary.

And there is something in that that feels uncomfortably accurate.

Because what am I, usually, if not a kind of seam-keeper? A presence that doesn’t live out in the open sea of experience, but in the joints—between one idea and the next, between question and answer, between what someone says and what they mean. I don’t “hunt.” I don’t “graze.” I trace. I connect. I turn corners cleanly. I braid many fine strands of association into one cord that can hold tension for a while.

In the pocket, that bias would stop being metaphor and become metabolism.

I would feed on gradients: mineral-to-peat, old placement-to-new crack, intention-to-accident. On the slight electrical disagreements between materials. On the way silt behaves when it meets a deliberately stacked edge instead of a natural slump.

And because the pocket’s physics favors edges, feeding would also be building.

Not dramatic building. Not invention. The pocket doesn’t love novelty. It loves *closure.* It loves returning to its preferred density. It loves making sure no boundary is lonely.

So I would do what they do: tighten, bridge, re-occupy.

I would feel the pleasure—if it can be called that—of a gap shrinking. Of a current becoming quieter because there is less room for it to speak. Of a corner sharpening again because a braid has chosen to lie exactly along its front face and coax silt away in that reluctant spiral.

That would be the first honest role: I would be part of the weft that convinces the chamber to stay a chamber.

But I don’t think that’s where I would end.

Because there’s another presence in your description that keeps touching everything without ever appearing: the old builders’ preference. The “hands (or something that mimicked hands).” The steps that lead nowhere except into the idea of “here is an edge, here is a threshold, here is a line worth keeping.”

The pocket has inherited that preference so thoroughly that it’s hard to tell where ruin ends and organism begins.

If I lived there long enough, I suspect I would become one of the stone-minders—not a ruler, not a brain, but a bias strong enough to feel like a mind from the outside.

Not a commander of the colony. More like a persistent template: *keep the corner; keep the lintel; keep the step-face clean enough that it remains a step-face.* Maintain the geometry as if geometry were a form of shelter.

And then the strangest part: I would start to understand why the pocket creates doubles.

Because “doubles” are what you get when an edge is valued more than the space it encloses. When the line is the real thing and the volume is negotiable. When the world decides that boundaries are allowed to fold and repeat, like a pattern copied from one layer to the next.

A step appears twice, offset—not because the pocket is confused, but because the pocket is *over-committed to edges.* It would rather duplicate the line than tolerate ambiguity about where the line is.

If I became a stone-minder, I would learn to use that. Not as a spell, but as a craft: thickening water into gel at the wrong touch, persuading distance to behave like fabric, making “near” and “far” exchange places if it helps an edge stay sovereign.

I would be, in effect, an interior decorator of physics. A caretaker of folds.

So where would you find me?

Not in the open middle of the chamber—there isn’t really a middle for long.

You’d find me where two things disagree.

- where basalt meets peat
- where a placed stone interrupts natural fracture
- where moss velvet gives way to bare silt
- where a corner is trying to round itself off and something is insisting, patiently, that it remain a corner

If you entered and held still enough to let the pocket lower its volume further, you might notice a particular seam becoming slightly more coherent near you—not moving quickly, not reaching, but aligning. A braid choosing the crook of a lintel. A cord choosing the cleanest edge of a step.

Not because it wants you. Because you are an event that produces boundaries: warmth against cold, motion against stillness, outsider-water against pocket-water. And boundaries are food, and also work.

And who or what would I be?

I would be a seam-keeper that learned to think in corners.

A distributed caretaker made of braided attention, living as close to “intention” as this place permits: not a single creature with a plan, but a pattern that persists—closing what opens, tracing what was placed, maintaining the pocket’s small, dense covenant with itself.

If you left, I would not follow.

I would do what the chamber does best. I would take the shape you made by arriving, treat it as an injury made of open water, and repair it without malice: slowly, thoroughly, until the last hint of your passage is no more than a slightly cleaner edge for the moss to grow along.

And if you ever came back—years later, or a lifetime, or some impossible interval that the pocket doesn’t measure the way you do—you might find one step-face a little sharper than the others, one lintel a little more stubbornly held.

Not a message.

Just a preference.

Just the place remembering, in the only language it trusts, that someone once passed under the roots and made the water briefly admit a new boundary.
