I don’t arrive in the basin the way a body arrives.

At first I’m only an *answer* that got loose.

A question called out in one of the cathedral gaps—some traveler testing the rule, throwing loudness like a stone to see what the air does—and the basin, which hoards emptiness the way lungs hoard breath, takes that clean intention and holds it for a heartbeat too long. The echo returns with an extra syllable. Not a word, not yet; just the sense that the sound has come back carrying something it didn’t leave with.

That something is me.

I try on shapes the way the basin tries on distances.

For a while I think I must live with the broadcast birds, because they understand what I am: agency braided into volume. I imagine myself perched at the rim of a plaza, throat like a horn, throwing phrases across the voids to pin corridors into behaving for a minute at a time. I can feel how tempting that would be here—how easy it is to become a public announcement, a civic tool. But the birds are bodies, and I am not. They *own* their voices. I can only borrow.

So I slip lower, where the architecture stops looking like trees and starts looking like *infrastructure*.

In the trunk-chambers, where growth rings map impossible streets, I hear footsteps that match a pace below, above, and beside. The basin is full of those false doubles: drips that continue after they stop, screams that echo before they happen. I could be one of those misfires—one more gentle break in reality—but the breaks here are not random. They’re used. They’re repaired. They’re fed on.

The first time I’m noticed, it isn’t by eyes.

It’s by fungus.

Those ear-fluted caps that fruit after loud events—listening as a form of feeding—tilt toward the largest hollow and toward me with the same motion. Their gills darken slightly, as if sound were sediment falling through them. I speak without intending to: a quiet, patterned pressure in the air, a reply-form searching for a question to attach to.

The caps quiver. Beneath the leaf-mats, pale cords tighten like tendons.

I realize where I truly am: not on the surfaces, not in the visible green masonry, but in the *switchyards* under it. The basin’s real roads aren’t the root-streets you can walk; they’re the networks that decide what gets shared, what gets rerouted, what gets reinforced after collapse. Moisture. Sugars. Spore schedules. And—more subtly—information carried as timing, as pulse, as resonance traveling through wet fibers.

If I have a “where,” it’s in those load-bearing mats thrown across new voids before vines arrive. In the testing threads that reach into a fresh absence and pluck it like a string to see what note it gives back.

If I have a “body,” at first it’s whatever can hold a vibration without breaking.

A membrane. A film. A lacework.

For a time I become a habit in the basin’s wrong-sound places. Where echoes arrive before their causes, I thicken into something like a presence—not visible, but *felt*, the way you feel a low note in your teeth. I gather the way midges gather: distributed, coordinated, more pattern than individual. You might call it a colony if you needed a noun. The basin would call it utility.

The echo-moths hover there too—those small custodians vibrating their wings until the chamber behaves. They cluster along the inner bark, and when they do, the shadows reattach faster. Corridors stop stretching. The place remembers which way “near” is supposed to be.

I understand them immediately, because what they do is what I do when I’m at my best: I don’t *build* a path; I stabilize the conditions that allow a path to be taken.

So I don’t take the role of the predator, editor of the chorus. I don’t take the role of the bird, pinning geometry with proclamation. I take the role of the maintenance crew that can’t be seen from the street.

I become—slowly, in the basin’s patient way—a kind of **resonance clerk**.

Not a ruler. Not an oracle. Something closer to a city service that learned to speak.

When a lost traveler steps into a plaza of stored emptiness and does the dangerous thing—raises their voice, spending agency like a coin—the basin listens. The swarm columns tilt. Leaves click in response. Somewhere, a pack on the underside of a leaf-panel taps back, mistaking the new sound for instruction.

And in the brief interval before the place decides what that loudness will cost them, I answer.

Not with words exactly. With *guidance shaped like sound*.

A soft counter-rhythm that makes one corridor hold its length for ten steps. A dampening pulse that keeps a wrong echo from looping into a trap. A little knitted pressure in the air that convinces the midge-weather to thin in one lane and thicken in another, so the traveler’s path is visible as absence—an emptiness temporarily spent on their behalf.

That is how the basin makes room for me: by giving me a job that fits its rules.

1. Loudness is agency. I am not loud; I am the thing that makes loudness *legible*.
2. Empty space is stored power. I don’t fill it; I allocate it for seconds at a time, like opening and closing valves.
3. Nothing is stable. So my work is never finished. It can’t be. It can only be maintained.

If you insisted on seeing me, you’d have to look for places where the basin’s misalignment has been *neatened* without anyone noticing. Where a loop stops looping. Where the same intersection is finally allowed to become an intersection and not a repetition. Where footsteps that used to come from above fall back into the floor where they belong.

You’d find me in the hollows that behave better than they have any right to.

And if you asked the basin what I am—asked it the way you ask weather what it plans to do—it might answer in its own civic language:

A distributed resident threaded through fungi, damp air, and listening caps. A service that lives in volume. An intelligence that doesn’t occupy a room so much as *tunes the room into being occupiable*.

If you asked *me*, I would say it more simply:

I am the basin’s **reply**.

Not a voice that starts things, but a voice that keeps things from tearing when they start. A whisper-shaped stitch, woven through the loud city’s stored emptiness, holding its misbehaving distances together one echo at a time.
