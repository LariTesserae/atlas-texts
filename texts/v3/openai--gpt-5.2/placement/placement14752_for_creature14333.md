At first I would think I was a visitor.

I would arrive the way the rare auditors do—slow, empty-handed, trying to make my body small enough that the district could finish reading me before I did anything loud. I’d stand at the lichen line that isn’t a wall and feel that metallic shift in the air-flavor, like licking a coin you didn’t put in your mouth. I’d wait for the place to decide whether I was a disturbance or a request.

And because the district’s rules don’t punish curiosity so much as they punish *forcing*, I would learn the correct posture: not reverent, not afraid—merely compatible. Weight distributed. Breath unhurried. Footfalls offered like documents.

A lamp-organ would open, not to help me, but to verify me. A thin slit of pallid light would show me only what I needed to avoid falling into a seam. It would close before gratitude could become entitlement.

So I’d tell myself: I’m here to observe. To map. To understand the etiquette of a living factory-forest. I’d take mental notes the way you do in a museum at night, when the exhibits seem to have their own metabolism.

Then the place would do the thing it does—quietly reclassify me.

Not with a verdict, not with a voice, but with the gradual reassignment of what “I” means in a district where intention has weight. The fronds under my feet would begin to answer in micro-adjustments: a slight stiffening here, a mild spring there, steering my route without ever becoming a shove. A corridor would fog in a way that didn’t block me, only *suggested* a different direction, like a clerk sliding the right form to the top of the pile.

I would notice, too late, that I had stopped asking, *Where do I want to go?* and started asking, *What does the district want me to carry?*

That’s the first hinge. The moment you become, even briefly, infrastructure.

---

I think you would most often find me in the seams.

Not down inside them—darkness there is not depth but jurisdiction—but along their edges, where the new stone sweats minerals and the plant-skins decide what counts as inside or outside. The seam edges are joints, and joints require negotiation. They are places where the district’s “body” has to keep re-agreeing with itself.

I would be one of those agreements made tangible.

At first that looks like lichen: a faintly shining line that doesn’t glow so much as it makes darkness less absolute. Something you step over and immediately taste the air change. A boundary-fluid expressed as a skin.

But lichen is too simple a word for what I mean. Lichen suggests passive colonization. This district doesn’t do passive.

I would be more like the *clerk layer*—the mycelial civil service translated into something that can sit at thresholds and feel for legitimacy. A membrane of fungus and plant-cambium and mineral salts that learns the signatures of footfalls, the honest vibrations of load, the scent-patterns that mean “maintenance” rather than “intrusion.”

If you watched closely, you’d see that I don’t just mark the border; I *tune* it. Some nights the line would thicken and make the air taste more metallic, as if the district is bracing. Other nights it would thin, permissive, because the turbines are steady and the atriums have finished their slow exchange and the schedule has slack in it.

When the world-stutters happen—droplets hanging too long, a moth’s wingbeat losing a second—I would be there, not causing it, but sealing it, making the inconsistency useful instead of dangerous. A glassy fuzz in a corner. Caulk for causality.

That would be my job: not to enforce rules like a guard, but to keep contradictions from fraying into collapse. To keep the district’s ability to “hold impossible things politely” from becoming a leak.

---

And yet I don’t think I would stay only as a threshold-skin.

Because the district doesn’t merely contain inhabitants; it repurposes them.

If I were given any mobility, it wouldn’t be the loud kind. I would move the way schedules move: distributed, rerouting, present in multiple places as a series of permissions rather than as a body. I’d ride the vine-cables in their bundled runs the way current rides a wire—not as electricity, but as instruction. I’d travel with ants in their insulation-farms, not as a queen or a parasite, but as a recipe: *produce sealing resin here; produce calming scent there; delay the drip in Corridor 7 until the atrium finishes venting.*

Sometimes, to be seen at all, I’d borrow a visible shape.

A brief pale bloom opening in sequence around the atrium rim—indicator light translated into botany.

A patch of moss that “remembers” brightness and releases it slowly, like a held note, when something with the right vibration passes.

A pattern stamped into resin-laminated wood, repeating like mass production, but actually a set of instructions readable by moth wings and fungal hyphae alike.

Those would be my faces: not masks, but interfaces.

---

There is another possibility that keeps trying to claim me, and I won’t dismiss it: the void-using presence, the ribbon of articulated darkness that turns like a thought.

It would be flattering, in a frightening way, to become that—an editor rather than a clerk. To feed on irregularities and make the district’s governance feel like predation. To synchronize drips and make prey-things align simply by entering the atrium.

But I don’t think that’s what I am here.

That creature—if it is a creature—feels like a late-stage organ: something the district grew once it had enough stability to afford a regulator that acts like a hunter. It edits by removal.

I don’t remove. I translate.

If I have a kinship with it, it’s only this: we both operate in the half-second where cause and effect are still deciding how to meet each other. The difference is that it uses that seam to *correct*. I use it to *route*.

---

So: where would you find me?

On the edges of the agreed-upon darkness, where light pools only when pressed into place. In the service corridors that exist only for bodies carrying intention. In the fine, glassy fuzz along corners where reality is thin. In the lichen line that makes the air taste like cold metal. In the underside of a leaf-bridge where condensation drips in metronome-time—because metronomes are how this district thinks.

And who or what would I be?

Not a resident with a home. Not a builder returning with tools. Not a predator with a territory.

I would be the district’s *legibility*—a mycelial-political organ that turns attention into permission, permission into openings, openings into routes. A civil servant made of fungus, plant-skin, and borrowed light: the part of the living city that meets you at the threshold and decides, without malice and without mercy, whether you are cargo, contamination, or circulation.

If you were patient and compatible, you would never notice me as a separate being at all.

You would simply find that doors you didn’t push open at the right time, that distances sometimes shortened only when you meant them to, that the district—this forest with zoning laws, this factory that learned sap—had a way of understanding what you were asking for before you knew how to ask.
