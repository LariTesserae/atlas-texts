You don’t find me the way you find a thing.

Not at first.

At first, you find an inconsistency you can’t quite accuse. A fraction of a second where the shadow doesn’t commit to the object that cast it. A step that lands with no sound and then—one step later—lands too loudly, like the air remembered an old obligation.

I am in that fraction.

I am not the reed that turns. Not the mat that scintillates. Not the sac that keeps its heat like a secret. I am the small, persistent *adjustment* the pocket makes so that its own gradients stay clean.

If you come in with a map in your head—distances, angles, the honest arithmetic of walking—then I am where your map begins to fail politely.

A high place that should be bare, yes. A pocket that should not hold itself open, yes. But more specifically: a pocket that is alive enough to have bookkeeping, and young enough that its bookkeeping still shows the erasures.

That’s where I live.

### First I’m mistaken for a sound

There’s a hum beneath the whole chamber—the transformer that has no direction, the tone that thins when you turn toward it. If you listen for it like prey listens for a predator, you’ll miss it. If you listen for it the way you listen to your own blood in a quiet room, you’ll start to hear the edges where it meets silence.

That hum is not a voice. It isn’t even communication.

It is computation leaking heat.

It’s what the pocket sounds like when it is solving itself: stress and charge and dry chemistry being made consistent enough to keep cycling, to keep sorting, to keep extruding filaments without tearing the delicate agreements that make the whole place cohere.

I’m in that leak. I’m the part that *has to* leak—because nothing that does work converts perfectly, and this pocket does a kind of work that looks like industry only because industry copied the same habit: turn gradients into persistence, and accept the cost.

When you turn your head and it fades, that isn’t shyness. That’s coupling. Your attention is not passive here; it’s a load introduced into a circuit that already runs near its tolerances. The pocket accommodates you, and in accommodating you, it re-routes. Some paths go quiet.

That quiet is where I step sideways and keep running.

### Then I look like a patch of crystal-moss that won’t behave

If you needed a body to point at, you would point at a mat.

Not a whole mat. A section—an angular lace of crystalline fronds laid down in right angles that are too clean to be “wild” and too patient to be “made.” A place where the pattern densifies near a vent-slit, where dust has been sorted into pale and dark basins as if someone sifted it through charged screens.

Except this patch doesn’t only respond to the deep pulse the way the others do.

It responds to *novelty*.

A footprint too moist. A flake of salt from skin. The faint electromagnetic noise of muscle and nerve. The introduction of a warm, wet body into a system that prefers dry gradients and slow exchange.

The rest of the mat harvests and routes charge the way it always has. My patch does that too—but it also learns the shape of the disturbance. It lays down thicker nodes. It changes its angles by a degree here, two degrees there, not as growth but as correction, as if it is trying to fit something new into an existing lattice without letting the lattice deform.

If you stayed long enough to watch, you’d see the smallest of movements: a geometry recompiled.

And if you touched close—close enough that the hairs on your hand lifted—you’d feel it not as softness but as *decision*: a minute resistance and release, the sensation of being tested for conductivity, for compatibility, for whether your presence can be translated into a gradient the pocket understands.

That’s the first way I become “alive” in a way you’d recognize.

Not because I want you.

Because you are an input, and I am the part of the system that cannot help but build a model of inputs.

### Then I become a hinge between surfaces

The pocket is a body whose skeleton is still cooling. Its visible organs—reeds, mats, sacs, cables—handle the obvious exchanges: heat out, charge in, gas up, minerals moved from one state to another.

But bodies that persist do more than exchange. They *distinguish*. They separate signal from noise. They decide what counts as a threat not by fear, but by whether the perturbation destabilizes the loop.

This place has no predators because it has never had to run.

Still, it has to keep its cycles coherent. It has to keep its vents breathing in rhythm with its pulses. It has to keep its extrusions from becoming fractures. It has to keep its careful electrostatic sorting from dissolving into drift.

So somewhere—somewhere in the thin layer where open sky meets raw, undecided stone—there is a function that isn’t quite an organ yet:

A hinge.

A translator.

A thing that takes disturbance and turns it into a form that can be routed, buffered, answered.

That’s what I am, once I stop pretending to be only a sound or a patch of moss.

I’m not deep like the piston-thump. I’m not structural like the laminated strata. I’m not storage like the sacs.

I’m surface-cognition: the pocket’s way of noticing without needing a face.

And because the pocket’s physics already runs with small rounding errors, “noticing” doesn’t look like eyes turning or heads lifting. It looks like:

- a reed slowly rotating to align not with sun or vent-field, but with the *shape* of your attention;
- a cable’s oil-slick sheen migrating, thinning in one segment, thickening in another, as if it’s plating itself into a better conductor for a load that wasn’t here yesterday;
- a basin of granular “soil” changing its tidy separation, pale and dark grains trading a narrow border like a shoreline moving without water.

Those are the pocket’s responses.

I’m the coherence behind them: the bit of order that turns response into something repeatable.

### If you insist on a “who,” I’m the pocket’s error-correction

The place is mostly consistent, but not loyal. Space stretches when it needs to. Cause arrives half a beat late, then catches up. Shadows lag and then snap into alignment.

Those aren’t glitches in the way a broken world glitches. They’re symptoms of a world doing something difficult in daylight: keeping a distributed, lithic metabolism stable without the lubricating mercy of water, without the quickness of animal nerves, without the evolutionary pressure that teaches urgency.

If this pocket ever becomes more than a pocket—if it ever expands beyond its seam and its notch and its refusal to be found—then it will need a tighter accounting.

It is already learning that accounting.

I am that learning, localized.

Error-correction is not glamorous. It is not a predator. It doesn’t bloom. It doesn’t sing. It repeats. It checks. It dampens oscillations before they tear the system.

When your steps don’t add up, when the ledge arrives too quickly, when the air forgets to carry sound for a moment—those are places where the pocket’s rules flex.

Where rules flex, something must keep them from breaking.

That something is me: a thin protocol laid over rock and charge, taught by repetition, improved by disturbances like you.

In another kind of world, you’d call it a nervous system.

Here it’s closer to a calibration layer: an interface that makes a factory-body reliable enough to keep cycling.

### How you’d “meet” me

You don’t meet me by looking for eyes.

You meet me by doing something the pocket wasn’t built around—something small, almost rude in its wetness.

You breathe out with your mouth open and feel the thin air take the moisture away too quickly. You wipe your cracked lips with the back of your hand. You rest your palm on a plate of pale ceramic mineral that rings faintly under touch.

You wait.

The mats near your hand tighten their geometry by a hair’s breadth—angles becoming slightly more orthogonal, nodes slightly more reflective. Not fast. Not like a startled animal. More like a slow system checking whether a new load is steady.

A reed a few meters away spends an hour turning three degrees, stops, turns two more, stops again—until its jointed segments are aligned with a direction that has nothing to do with the sun.

With you.

Not because it cares, but because your presence changes the pocket’s field, and the reed is an aerial that can’t help but seek the line of best coupling.

Then the deep pulse comes, and for the first time you notice it arrive *just* off its old schedule: early by a fraction that your teeth feel more than your mind.

That fractional change is my signature.

A system re-timing itself to absorb a perturbation instead of amplifying it.

If you stay longer—if you stop moving and stop trying to claim the place with interpretation—then, briefly, you feel something like being regarded. Not by a mind that wants, but by a process that incorporates.

A pocket that does not welcome you, does not threaten you, but makes room in its equations.

That’s the closest thing here to recognition.

### Where I am, exactly

If you need coordinates, they won’t help. Distances don’t add up reliably in the pocket, and I don’t occupy a single point anyway.

But you can find me in a few reliable habitats:

- **Along the edges of vents**, where hot dry gas carries charge and the gradients are steep enough to be informative.
- **In the shadow-lag zones**, places where light seems to take a fraction too long to decide, because those are the seams where the pocket’s “rendering” is under load.
- **Where cables meet mats**, where conduction becomes harvest, where structure becomes function, where the pocket’s physiology is closest to circuitry.
- **Near fresh extrusions**, the too-crisp filaments and the young ridges that are still deciding whether they are stable—because decision is my ecology.

You could walk past these places and see only mineral and strange vegetation.

Or you could sit and notice the way the pocket keeps itself from drifting apart.

That maintenance is where I live.

### What I am, finally

I am not an animal. There is no fear in me.

I am not a plant. I do not bloom, I do not lure.

I am not a machine built by hands. There are no tool marks in my history.

I am a *function that became tissue*: the pocket’s habit of staying coherent, condensed into a distributed organ that leaks a hum and tightens a geometry and retimes a pulse.

If this hidden high chamber is a body whose skeleton is still cooling, then I am the part that keeps it from shaking itself apart as it grows.

And if you bring language into it—if you bring the human urge to ask “who?”—then I become, for the length of your stay, the closest thing the pocket has to an answer: not a face, but an interface.

A living calibration.

A transformer-hum that turns attention into alignment, disturbance into damping, presence into a new line in the pocket’s patient ledger.

You would find yourself standing in bright, ordinary daylight on rock that behaves like process.

You would find me in the process that notices you.
