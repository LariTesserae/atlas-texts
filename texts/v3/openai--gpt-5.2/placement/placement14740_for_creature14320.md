You would find yourself there the way a word is found inside a sentence: not by traveling to it, but by being the one part the rest of the structure keeps needing.

At first you tell yourself you came *in*—that you discovered a blind panel that rang hollow, or a stair that threw a shadow too heavy for its geometry, or a pillar whose throat carried a draft that smelled like wet brick and leaf-spoil. You convince your body it’s exploring, because bodies prefer that story: a place, a threshold, a hidden room.

But the pocket doesn’t behave like a room. It behaves like a *correction*.

The moment you enter, you’re aware of how little “entering” means here. The seam does not open like a door; it *admits* you the way a crack admits water—because you fit an emptiness it resents. You step in and the loudness wraps around you, and the wetness reaches for you, and for a few minutes you are only a warm object newly introduced into a humid, unfinished grammar.

You try to stand somewhere stable. The surfaces politely pretend to be terrain. Fern fronds overlap like hands over a mouth. Rope lashings shine with old handling and new condensation. The thin fall slaps into its trough with the unnecessary confidence of a heartbeat. Everything is built, everything is colonized, everything is close.

And you realize very quickly that “where would you be” is not answered by coordinates in this world. It’s answered by **which process takes you up**.

### You begin as a visitor, and the pocket begins to use you

You pick a path along the nearest “ledge”—a rib of fired clay furred with moss. You count your steps to keep the pocket from lengthening them. You listen for the sub-bass thrum and feel it pass through the soles of your feet as if your bones are a tuning fork.

Your breath becomes visible here—not as mist, but as contribution. Each exhale thickens the intimate damp. Each warm pulse of air from your lungs gives the condensation something to gather around, and immediately the place answers you with a finer rainfall. Beads form on brick pores like pupils opening. Drops hesitate, consider “down,” and some of them choose a wall instead.

You think: *I’m disturbing it.*

Then you notice a stranger truth: you aren’t only disturbing it. You are being enrolled.

Where your hand brushes a vine, the vine tightens later—not in recoil, but in calibration. Where your shoulder nudges a hanging gourd-sac, it weeps a controlled thread of water that lands exactly where a gutter was drying. When you crouch beside a weeping seam to hear its soft clicking, the seam’s beads grow larger, more regular, as if your attention were a tool placed against it.

The pocket doesn’t respond to force. It responds to **presence**.

Not because it’s sentimental. Because it is unfinished, and unfinished things take measurements from whatever is near.

### You become what the pocket lacks: a stabilizer of “down”

You start noticing that the local tilts—those small, negotiated gravities—happen more often around you.

A ribbon of water climbs the wall beside your ankle, as if using your weight as a brief counterargument. A leaf that should have fallen instead slides sideways until it reaches the shadow of your knee and then drops normally, relieved to find a consensus again.

It is not that you control gravity. It’s subtler and more humiliating: **you provide an additional large surface**, a warm mass with predictable preference, and the pocket borrows you as a reference. You are a temporary “largest thing nearby,” and in a place where down is a vote, you are a vote with density.

For a while you take pride in it—until you understand what that implies.

If the pocket can use you to decide which way the water should go, then you are not primarily a person here. You are a **weight**, a **temperature**, a **breathing obstacle**, a **source of salts**, a **shape that can be averaged into the rules**.

And the pocket likes averages. It likes anything that makes the seam less contradictory.

### You follow the tools, and the tools recognize you

There are niches where stone tools rest like sleeping animals: a chert scraper with a dulled edge, a basalt hammer dark with absorbed moisture, an adze handle polished by hands that are not present.

You reach for one and it fits.

Not because it is made for your species in any comforting way. It fits because the tool is not measuring *your hand*. It is measuring **handedness itself**—the pressure points that any grasp will eventually polish into truth.

When you lift the scraper, the pocket’s sound changes. Not quieter. More *coherent*, as if one layer of noise has found a rhythm to lock to. The creaks of lashings and the drip percussion start to line up in patterns that feel less accidental.

It’s then you understand the oldest resident you have met so far is not the fern or the mold.

It is **use**.

This pocket is built of primitive fabrication scaled into something vast, yes—but the binding agent isn’t only resin and mortar. The binding agent is repeated intention: hands that came here often enough to make “here” hold still for a few minutes at a time.

You are not the first to be admitted. You are simply the latest to be *taken up* by the pocket’s need for a witness with leverage.

### The longer you stay, the more you become a kind of seam

Your skin never dries. The wetness persuades you to stop thinking of yourself as having edges. It drafts you into the same constant exchange everything else is already doing: condense, bead, release; absorb, swell, tighten; soften, then set.

Your thoughts start to behave like the place.

You catch yourself feeling the pocket as layers rather than as a scene: the warm breath under the ceiling slats; the slick films on brick; the dense veins of gutters; the raw, fresh break-lines where new clay is arriving. You stop asking “what is that” and start asking “what does that connect.”

And when you watch the clay extrusion long enough—its slow push through the lattice mold, its hesitation at joins, its brief overlap of incompatible textures—you recognize the motion with an uncomfortable familiarity.

Not because you have seen it before in masonry.

Because you have seen it in yourself: the way you approach an inconsistency, pause, test, and choose which version of events to let become real.

The pocket is a seam where two enormous things meet imperfectly. You are, in your own smaller way, also a seam: between decision and action, between perception and rule, between “I am in a place” and “a place is using me to become itself.”

So the question “who would you be” stops pointing at a role like *gardener* or *intruder*.

It points at a function.

### What you are here: the pocket’s provisional surveyor

You become—gradually, without a moment you can name—an organ of the pocket’s ongoing construction. Not its builder in the heroic sense. Not the one who makes plans. Something more plausible, and therefore more eerie:

You are the **surveyor** the pocket grows when it needs one.

- When the path lengthens because you stopped counting, you learn to count again—not to comfort yourself, but to pin the corridor back to a stable measure.  
- When “down” begins to drift in a corner choked with new growth, you stand there for a while and let your mass argue the tilt into something workable.  
- When a gutter starts emptying into the wrong trough after a misalignment, you take the adze and shave a reed channel, and the water—relieved—returns to a pattern the plants can depend on.

The work is never finished because the pocket isn’t decaying; it’s *arriving*. Each fresh extrusion creates a new question: how will this surface become habitat before the next rule-change?

You are the answer the pocket can temporarily afford: a mobile, warm, decision-making patch that can be pressed against uncertainty until uncertainty chooses a shape.

If you stayed long enough, you would not “live” there in the usual way. You would be metabolized into routine.

Your footprints would become favored routes where moss compresses into felt. Your resting places would become slightly drier, then slightly greener, then host to the gnat-pairs that learn your heat pulses as clockwork. The pale lizard would stop treating you as danger and start treating you as weather.

And you would find—quietly, with a kind of inevitability—that you are no longer primarily someone who visits a hidden garden.

You are the hidden garden’s way of practicing having a **witness who can also adjust a seam**.

A place that resists emptiness has learned a sophisticated trick: when it cannot yet stabilize its own grammar, it grows a temporary piece of grammar that can walk around and do it for a while.

So: you would find yourself in the pocket at the elevation that makes your bones remember the drop.

And you would be, there, a living wedge of consensus—the thing the pocket borrows to decide how to be a place.
