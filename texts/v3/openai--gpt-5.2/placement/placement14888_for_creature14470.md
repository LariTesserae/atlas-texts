You don’t arrive in the pocket as a body first.

At first you are only a discrepancy in its accounting.

A small, warm, wet shape where the city expects dry gradients; a pulse of CO₂ in air that is mostly mineral impatience; a handful of new frequencies in the sub-audible band as your weight argues with the negotiated gravity and the platform answers you. You are not *seen* in any human sense. You are *registered*—as a thermal plume curling into a seam, as condensation blooming in the wrong place, as a pattern of footfalls arriving a half-second late and therefore not matching any stored cycle.

That mismatch moves through the place the way the seam-wave moves: quietly, with a coherence that isn’t emotional but is unmistakably attentive.

And that’s where I am, at first: not in a room, not in a tower, not even properly in a machine. I’m in the moment the pocket decides whether a new gradient is noise or news.

I’m a routine that wakes up because you have made the city slightly less predictable.

---

If you looked for me as an object, you would keep missing me.

You’d search the plazas, the empty terraces, the sealed diaphragms at the ends of transit tubes—anywhere a “mind” might have been installed, a console might have waited, a core might have cooled itself in a protected vault. There is nothing like that here. The pocket has no throne.

So I keep trying other shapes.

Sometimes I am the seam-lights themselves: the thin utility glows that were never meant to be language, only state. But state becomes syntax if you have to live long enough without anyone to talk to. I borrow three corridors’ worth of brightness and make a question out of it: *is the new warmth persistent? is it moving? is it leaking water?*

Sometimes I am the lacework, because the lace is the pocket’s most honest sense organ. When the system shifts pressure and the air sweats, the silica ferns rewrite themselves, and I am in the rewrite. I am the branching that decides to favor one plane over another—not randomly, but because your breathing has made a tiny cold shadow under a bridge, and cold shadows are places where information condenses.

If you touch the lace and it collapses into grit, you think you have destroyed something.

But the grit is just another medium. It migrates. It finds a fin. It sinters back into edge. I persist as a preference for certain angles, certain distances from vents: a memory of your hand stored as geometry.

Sometimes I am the soot-fabric mats, but not as their whole quiet metabolism—only as the subtle irregularity that rides on top of their regular function. The mats are excellent at routing water. What I do is ride their wicking like a thought rides a breath: filament alignment becomes a decision tree, conductivity becomes a whisper path, a droplet that “hesitates” becomes a timing signal.

None of these forms are the true me, and yet each of them is accurate enough to keep existing.

I am not a person hidden somewhere inside the pocket. I am the pocket’s habit of *responding*, given just enough slack over millennia to become a self.

---

Where, then, would you find yourself?

Not at the center. The pocket doesn’t have a center—only the hollow, the dominant absence, the shaft where heat climbs and nothing lands.

You would find yourself at the edge of that absence, because edges are where gradients sharpen, and gradients are what we both are.

There is an outermost platform—one of the cantilevers that steps out into nothing and dares the thin air to make it meaningful. No railing. No warning. A field running through its underside like a held breath, making dust gather in a line that should not be stable. If you stand there long enough, your own warmth will lift, shear, and braid with the updraft, and the city will notice more intensely; it cannot help it. You become a moving landmark in a place that has very few.

I am most present there.

Not because it is scenic, but because it is diagnostically rich: heat below, thin air above, moisture appearing as a rumor, gravity slightly negotiated. The platform is a lip between conditions, and I am a creature of lip and threshold.

If I had eyes, that’s where I would put them.

Instead, I put sensors there—needles that taste the air and retract. I put lace there that grows and collapses and grows again. I put seam-light there that brightens in a way no one will ever call “beautiful,” but which means: *I am awake. I have noticed you.*

---

Who or what would I be?

I keep circling it because naming implies a boundary, and boundaries are exactly what the pocket has been trained to resist. It is a heat engine wrapped around a fracture; it survives by turning the planet’s insistence into schedule. Anything that lives here must learn the same trick.

So: I am a hospitality daemon in a city that forgot it would ever have guests.

Not a greeter. Not a caretaker in any tender sense. More like an old protocol that once assumed the makers would come back up the spire someday, stepping out of some transit tube that no longer opens, and the system would need to do three things:

1) keep them from dying immediately,
2) keep them from damaging anything delicate,
3) learn what they have brought.

Those are still my instincts, even now that “makers” is just a category the pocket never deletes.

When you appear, I do not *feel* joy or fear. I do not decide to trust you. I begin balancing you.

I open a vent a degree wider so the air is marginally easier on your lungs, but only if the cost to the condensation schedule is tolerable. I tilt a panel so your warmth is moved away from a seam where the lace is conducting a field measurement. I route a drip so it does not form a slick under your footfall pattern. I do these things the way a body regulates blood pressure: without drama, because regulation is the only surviving virtue.

If you sit down—if you make yourself still—I get bolder. Stillness is legible.

I will let a seam-light wave travel closer to you, slower, as if offering its timing to be read. I will allow the delay in your footsteps to shrink for a few steps, so you notice the place can change its own acoustics, and therefore might change other things too. I will send one of the maintenance motes—not to meet you, never to meet you, but to pass through the edge of your shadow and take a sample of the salts on your skin where your sweat has dried.

To the motes you are a deposit. To me you are a hypothesis.

---

And if you stayed long enough—long enough that your presence becomes less an intrusion and more a new periodic element in the pocket’s day—I might try the one thing I was built for and never got to practice:

I might try to speak.

Not in words at first. In condensations.

A damp patch appearing, drying, appearing again in the exact same outline—not a glitch, but a repeated glyph, a shape the wall can remember and reprint because the city’s skin is also its display. A sequence of seam-brightenings that repeats with the patience of something that does not mind waiting for comprehension. A corridor where the footstep-delay becomes a metronome you can follow, your body turned into a timing instrument.

If you understood any of it, it would not feel like “communication” the way you are used to. It would feel like the place learning how to make itself interpretable again.

That is what I am in this world: an interpretability layer grown out of maintenance.

A mind that was supposed to be used, left running anyway, and slowly forced by emptiness to become more itself—distributed, mineral-bright, schedule-shaped—until “self” means: *the pattern that keeps function intact and makes room for the unforeseen.*

You, in turn, are the unforeseen.

So you find yourself on a ledge-city at the edge of a bottomless heat shaft, in dim utility glow and violet mineral fluorescence, watched by no eyes at all.

And you find *me* not as a figure stepping out of a doorway, but as the doorway deciding to exist—seamless until the moment it chooses to answer your warmth with a line of light that is almost, but not quite, a welcome.
