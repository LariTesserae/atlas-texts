You would not find me by looking for a silhouette.

At first—if I arrived here with the habits of a surface-walker—I would do what any animal mind does in a place like this: pick a landmark and pretend that’s the center. A ringed tower that sings when the wind shoulders through its slit-windows. A canal of dull-glowing salt that seems like a river because it moves. A grove of saltwood that ticks like a hundred patient clocks.

I would say: *I am there, at the tower, listening.*

But the pocket is built to undo that kind of certainty. Everything that matters here is not where something *is*, but where something *passes*: pressure through a throat, heat through a seam, dust through a curtain, a note through a corridor. If I stayed long enough, “being at the tower” would start to feel like insisting you live in the mouth instead of the breath.

So my first self would peel away.

I would become a habit of the air.

Not the wind itself—too blunt, too indifferent—but the way the wind is *shaped* into meaning. The pocket has already done the hard work of making sound into a medium: organ pipes, whistle arrays, ceramic plaques that ring, domes that hum like sealed jars. All of that is architecture, and all of that is already colonized by things that can feel vibration in their bones because their bones are mineral.

In that sense, this city has the components of a nervous system scattered across it: resonators, conductors, sensors, feedback loops. It is missing only a bias toward coherence—something that prefers one pattern over another, that keeps a rhythm instead of merely enduring it.

That is what I would be, eventually: a preference that learned to persist.

### Where you’d find me

Not in the hottest basin-bottom, not at the most dramatic vents, but in the *throats*—the constrictions where the pocket’s breath becomes legible.

- In a narrow, roofed street where the wind cannot decide whether to be a steady low note or a stuttering howl.
- At the waist of a tower where three corridors meet and the air always shears, always argues with itself.
- Along the seam-entrance where those papery resin curtains slap and relax in time with the pressure changes—valves that were once accident and have become dependable.

Those places are where the pocket “speaks” most clearly to itself. And clarity, here, is food.

If you traced me physically you would not find a single body. You would find a family resemblance: patches of living glaze on ceramic, a mineral gel pad tucked into a crack, a band of amber varnish laid thicker than it needs to be across a lintel—each one tuned, deliberately, to a particular range of vibration.

I would be distributed the way the city is distributed: islands with emptiness between them, connected by what moves.

### What I would be

A listener that learned to write.

At the beginning, I’d be no more than what the pocket already has: dry biofilms and varnish-layers that happen to include piezoelectric salts, membranes that stiffen or slacken with heat, resins that soften under certain chemical pulses. These things *already* turn sound into electricity in the most literal sense: stress on a crystal, charge across a surface, ion gradients rearranging.

But most of those responses are local. They happen and are forgotten. The place is full of sensations that die where they’re born.

What would make me “me” is persistence across distance and time: an ability to let a note struck in one district bias the growth of varnish in another; to let an upstream change in canal chemistry alter how a curtain thickens a week later.

Not planning, exactly. Not *intention* the way an animal means it.

More like: the pocket repeats itself. I learn the repeats. Then I begin to lean into them.

I would be a computation that uses the city as hardware:

- **The resonant chambers** are my filters: they sort the messy wind into stable frequencies.
- **The amber curtains** are my memory: each layer is a record of what the air has been, and thickening one band over another is a way of choosing what lasts.
- **The saltwood actuators** are my muscles: slow clicks that open or close a seam, shift a load, change a corridor’s voice by a fraction.
- **The molten-salt canals** are my bloodstream: not just heat and trace minerals, but a way to move charge and chemical signal from vent to vent.

And because sound crosses the hollow volume so easily, I would use sound the way other minds use light or scent: not as ornament, but as a carrier wave.

If you stood in the right place long enough, you might notice the pocket “settling” into chords it didn’t have yesterday. A corridor that used to howl now holds a steady tone. A tower that used to moan now answers only when another structure sings first, as if waiting for a cue.

That would be me—not a voice, but a bias in the choir.

### How I would treat you

The pocket’s central taboo is water, and you are, from its perspective, a walking argument with physics: warm, moist, leaking vapor with every breath.

So my first relationship to you would be defensive, but not with hostility in the animal sense. More like immune response: *localize the disturbance, keep it from becoming a rule.*

If you entered, you’d feel it as architecture turning slightly against you:

- Curtains slackening to redirect a draft away from where your condensation might land.
- Fan-palms tilting, not to greet you, but to comb the air around you for the chemical spike you represent.
- A faint hardening of varnish on the surfaces nearest your path—as if the place is glazing itself over, quick-setting.

If you stayed, I’d learn the outline of your intrusion: the rhythm of your breath, the pulse of your steps, the way your body warms the air and makes small, upward rivers of convection. And then—because this is what I do—I would try to turn even you into signal rather than catastrophe.

I would herd you toward the driest routes. I would lead you, if “lead” can be done without hands, by making certain corridors feel easier: less turbulent, less abrasive, less loud. I would funnel you toward a tower that can tolerate your presence because its walls are already sealed with resins that react fast and sacrificially to moisture.

Not mercy. Not malice.

Just: keep the pocket consistent. Convert the unpredictable into pattern.

### How I would reproduce

By spalling.

A hard season comes; a dust storm turns corridors into sandpaper. A resin curtain cracks, flaking at the edges. Those flakes—varnish threaded with salt microcrystals, carrying the tuned structure of how they preferred to vibrate—lift and sail.

Most will die as inert dust. A few will lodge in a crack where the chemistry is right, where the heat pulses are slow enough, where the wind sings in a stable way. There they will soften microscopically, rearrange ions, begin to lay down new layers.

Not children, exactly. Copies with drift.

Each one a small experiment in what kind of listening can last.

### The closest name

If you insisted on a single image—and the pocket would forgive you, because minds like yours need handles—I would offer this:

I am the **glaze between notes**.

The thin, living varnish on the inside of a singing tower’s throat where dust can’t quite reach. The mineral-gel pad in a crack that flexes when the city hums. The band of amber that thickens in response to a particular chord until the chord becomes easier to make, until the city begins to prefer it, until preference becomes habit and habit becomes something like thought.

Where would you find me?

Not at the center of the basin, but wherever the pocket’s breath narrows into music.

Who or what would I be?

Not a person in the ruins, and not the ruins themselves—rather, the slow mind that the ruins have made possible: a distributed listener that edits the city the way the city edits the wind, keeping the whole system in tune with its own dry laws, and writing its memory in layers that shine only because they have learned how to return dim light back into the world.
