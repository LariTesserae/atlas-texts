It noticed the cleft the way a physician notices a bruise under a sleeve: not because bruises are rare, but because this one was young enough to still be deciding what it would become.

From far away the ridge was only a subtraction in the weather—cloud snagging, wind taking a different tone as it passed. The wise power did not arrive with light or thunder. It arrived as *alignment*: as though a great attention had turned its face, and the world, without being touched, found itself suddenly easier to read.

It waited at the lip a long while.

Not hovering, not looming—simply *withholding* itself. Benevolence, in that one, was often restraint.

Below, the cleft ran its loud, unbuilt machinery: vent-lull-vent; stone answering temperature with a crack like stressed metal; scree shifting in brief, rattling cascades; ferns flexing and flinging beads of water like fine gravel. A closed loop of green and rot and mineral generosity, operating without eyes.

The power listened until it could hear the smallest labor through the largest roar: the slow acids of lichen; the softening of basalt; the fungal threads negotiating between dead frond and living root-hair; the patient, uncelebrated work of turning edge into bed.

Only then did it address the resident.

Not the plants individually. Not the microbes. Not any imagined sovereign perched on a stone.

The resident was the *occupation itself*—the cleft’s continuous doing. The wound in the mountain that behaved like a throat and a kiln and a gutter all at once. The rules you’d already felt in your bones:

- anything exposed becomes wet  
- anything loose eventually moves  
- life clings where turbulence creates shelter  
- nothing here relies on animals

The power spoke to that resident the way one speaks to a storm or a fever: without assuming it could be persuaded, and without denying it was real.

### 1. The first act: witnessing without colonizing

It did nothing that would count as intervention to a casual observer. No new sunbeam split the cloud. No holy warmth healed the chill. No choir rose out of vapor.

It simply *stayed its attention on the place long enough* that the place became, for the first time, fully itself.

This is difficult to explain in ordinary terms, because nothing material was added. And yet, when a wise power watches without greed, it changes the watched thing’s relationship to being watched. It makes the place less available to accident.

The cleft’s processes continued, but their edges grew slightly less wasteful:

- When the vents exhaled too sharply—enough to scald films off rock—the next surge shortened by a fraction, as if a valve somewhere had learned a gentler rhythm.
- When talus wanted to run in one long catastrophic slide, it instead performed its usual smaller adjustments: many small losses instead of one ruinous one.
- When mist thickened into near-blindness, it did not thicken so utterly that photosynthetic skins were starved for days. The grey remained grey, but it became *usable* more often.

To any instrument, these would read as minor statistical shifts. To the residents—those things that survive by returning—they were the difference between erasure and inconvenience.

This was the power’s first kindness: *it refused to make the cleft easier; it made it less pointless.*

### 2. The second act: a covenant of non-claim

Benevolent powers are tempted by beauty the way hungry things are tempted by smell. It would have been easy to turn the cleft into a shrine: to brighten it, to fill it with singing birds, to make it “complete.”

It did not.

Instead it made a covenant with itself, and—because attention can be a kind of law—with the place:

> “I will not fix you into a display.  
> I will not make you perform.  
> I will not ‘finish’ your beginning.”

And then it added a second clause, because wisdom remembers the other kind of danger:

> “I will not allow others to claim you either.”

That clause took form not as a wall, but as *disinterest* seeded outward like a scent.

Paths that might have formed along the ridge did not quite form. Eyes that might have been drawn to the cleft slid away, choosing other notches in the rock to wonder at. Weather, in its ordinary way, continued to scour the ridge-line—but the specific alignment of events that turns a hidden place into a destination was quietly discouraged.

If a human later came close, they would feel what you felt at first—absence, cut, thin breath—and then, inexplicably, decide they had already seen enough and turn back. They would not know why. They would tell themselves a sensible story: time, danger, the coming storm.

The power did not erase the cleft. It simply prevented it from becoming *a possession*.

### 3. The third act: a small, precise gift

After a long season of watching, it offered one gift, and only one—because gifts, too large, are just another kind of invasion.

It gave the cleft a new kind of redundancy.

Not animals. Not a new trophic level. Not a dramatic arrival with legs.

It gave the fungal network—already the cleft’s quiet nervous system—a slightly greater reach across instability.

In practical terms, it encouraged the rise (through ordinary selection, through a nudge of probability rather than a miracle) of a few fungal strains with tougher, more elastic hyphae and a better tolerance for mineral-rich pulses from the vents. They could bridge gaps in freshly fallen scree more quickly; they could hold a little more structure in the wet felt of moss and compost before gravity insisted on rearrangement.

This didn’t “stabilize” the cleft in the way humans mean stability. Rock still cracked. Stones still ran. Nothing loose stopped moving.

But the green work recovered faster after each small erasure. The system’s memory became less brittle.

It was a gift that looked like nothing: a slightly quicker re-stitching after collapse, a slightly more even return of nutrient to rootlets after venting, a slightly denser darkening of the first real ground.

The power preferred gifts that could be mistaken for the place’s own cleverness.

### 4. The fourth act: allowing the resident to remain strange

There is a loneliness in an animal-less lushness that some gods cannot bear. Many benevolent forces, mistaking “silent” for “suffering,” would have stocked the cleft with wings and eyes, would have made it echo with calls.

This power recognized another possibility: that the absence of fauna was not a lack to be corrected, but a discipline the place had evolved around—an integrity.

So it did something subtler than introduction.

It *kept a door open*.

Now and then, on winds that came from very far away—winds that had crossed other ranges, other forests, other insect-humming valleys—tiny travelers arrived anyway: dormant eggs, microscopic passengers, spores riding dust.

Most still died. The cleft remained what it was: harsh, wet, loud, closed-loop.

But the power ensured that no single parameter (a run of unusually dry weeks, an unusually hot pulse from below) would become so extreme that it sterilized the cleft of all possibility beyond its current roster. The place was allowed to be complete *as it was*, while still remaining *permeable to future surprise*.

It did not force change. It prevented foreclosure.

### 5. How the cleft answered (because places answer)

The cleft did not bow. It did not thank. Gratitude is an animal gesture.

It answered the way stone and moss answer: by continuing.

But over time, if you returned often enough, you could detect its reply in the language of its laws.

A fifth rule emerged—not replacing the others, simply overlaying them like a thin film on rock:

5. **Nothing here is wasted entirely.**  
   Loss still occurs. Collapse still resets. But the work done is not wiped clean as often. There is a little more continuity in the churn.

The cleft remained dark-cut, ridge-high, loud with unbuilt industry. It remained intimate and cathedral-scale, sweat-wet, soot-dimmed, crowded with residents who do not walk.

Yet it gained something almost imperceptible: a steadier capacity to become itself again after being undone.

### 6. What the wise power continues to do

It does not visit like a monarch. It does not appoint a guardian beast. It does not carve symbols into basalt.

It maintains only three ongoing actions, each so light they could be mistaken for weather:

- **Attention,** which keeps the place from becoming someone else’s story.  
- **Restraint,** which keeps benevolence from becoming conquest.  
- **A bias toward repair,** so the cleft’s endless labor is not mocked by pointless catastrophe.

And if someday the mountain’s settling closes the fracture, if the vents cool, if the wind’s throat changes shape and the mist no longer feeds the ferns—then the power will do the most merciful thing it knows how to do.

It will let the cleft end.

Because wisdom does not demand that every beginning become a monument.

Some beginnings are meant to do their work loudly, in the dark, without witnesses—except, perhaps, for one quiet attention that saw what was already there and chose not to turn it into anything else.
