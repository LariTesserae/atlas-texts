**Preparation Guide for Visiting the Salt Cathedral of Stillness**  

---

### **1. Health & Safety Considerations**  
- **Respiratory Protection**: The air is thick with ozone, metallic particles, and solvent vapors. Pack a high-efficiency particulate air (HEPA) respirator with chemical/biological filters. The metallic taste and static electricity may cause respiratory irritation or metallic poisoning if inhaled over time.  
- **Hydration & Nutrition**: There is *no liquid water*. Carry a supply of safe drinking water (if allowed by local customs) or a personal desalination device for emergency brine processing. Avoid consuming local “solvents” or “rivers of liquid glass”; they may be corrosive or neurotoxic. Pack nutrient-dense, non-perishable food, as the local flora/fauna are inedible (and likely poisonous).  
- **Skin Protection**: The warmth and metallic air may cause dehydration or chemical burns. Wear a full-body, sealable suit (e.g., neoprene or hazmat material) to block salt dust, solvent splashes, and static discharge. Coat exposed skin with a moisture-retaining barrier cream.  
- **Noise Dampening**: The cavern’s industrial cacophony and resonant hum could damage hearing or induce vertigo. Use noise-canceling headphones or earplugs designed for extreme environments. Be prepared for disorientation from low-frequency vibrations.  

---

### **2. Equipment & Tools**  
- **Seismic Mapping Device**: The fauna communicate via ground vibrations, and the salt responds to movement. A handheld seismograph or motion sensor will help you “listen” to the environment and avoid triggering salt reactions.  
- **Non-Reactive Clothing**: Avoid materials that conduct static or react with solvents (e.g., synthetics like nylon; opt for treated wool or rubber). Shoes should be padded and non-metallic to minimize disruption to the salt’s equilibrium.  
- **Light Sources**: The bioluminescent fungi and amber glow provide dim illumination, but carry a UV-reactive headlamp or flashlight (non-invasive wavelengths) to study the salt’s crystalline structure without disturbing its “singing.”  
- **Sampling Gear**: If collecting data, use sterile, sealed containers. The metallic salt and organisms may degrade standard equipment—pack titanium or ceramic tools. Avoid touching the machinery or built structures; they are part of a self-regulating system.  
- **Emergency Communication**: A long-range transmitter might be jammed by the cavern’s electromagnetic fields. Carry a backup system (e.g., homing beacon) and leave a detailed itinerary with your multiverse agency.  

---

### **3. Environmental Interactions**  
- **Salt Sensitivity**: The living salt reacts to sound and movement. Move slowly and deliberately, avoiding abrupt gestures. Do not touch the salt formations, as they may “resonate” and amplify vibrations, destabilizing the cavern’s equilibrium.  
- **Machinery Protocol**: The machines are ancient but functional. Refrain from interacting with them—attempting to repair or reverse-engineer them could disrupt their eons-old loop. Observe their rhythms to navigate safely (e.g., timing your movements with the piston’s cycles).  
- **Tar Pool Hazards**: The viscous non-aqueous solvents may trap or dissolve organic matter. Stay on marked paths or use a pole to test footing. Eel-like creatures in the pools could be territorial or venomous.  
- **Faunal Caution**: Armored beetles and translucent drifters may be curious but aggressive if threatened. Avoid bright lights or sudden movements. The barnacle-like symbiotes on the drifters could detach and colonize foreign materials—wear a suit with a self-sealing membrane.  

---

### **4. Navigation & Structure Exploration**  
- **Geometric Instability**: The cavern’s terrain slopes gently but may shift due to the salt’s growth. Use a laser rangefinder or GPS (if functional) to map distances. The ceiling’s lost shadows suggest vertical hazards—carry a tether line.  
- **Intact Structures**: Buildings lean at “impossible angles,” implying spatial distortion. Test surfaces with a probe before entering. Control panels with flickering code may still be active—avoid interacting with them. The metallic vines feeding into fungi nests could ensnare clothing or skin.  
- **Liquid Glass Rivers**: These reflective flows are beautiful but dangerous. Avoid touching the surface (risk of thermal burns or molecular destabilization) and stay clear of their fractal reflections, which might hypnotize or disorient.  

---

### **5. Mental & Ethical Preparation**  
- **Time Dilation Tolerance**: Though time appears frozen, prolonged exposure may induce a “static equilibrium” mindset, causing apathy or dissociation. Practice mindfulness techniques and set alarms for return deadlines.  
- **Ecosystem Respect**: The fauna and flora are adapted to the salt’s feedback loop. Do not collect samples or remove items (even “dead” machinery). The cathedral’s balance relies on undisturbed materials.  
- **Philosophical Readiness**: The absence of water, the fusion of industry and biology, and the timeless stillness may challenge your perception of life and purpose. Reflect on themes of metamorphosis and obsolescence to maintain psychological resilience.  

---

### **6. Optional Extras**  
- **Salt Resonance Detector**: Measure the frequency of the humming crystals to locate “silent zones” for rest.  
- **Thermal Imaging**: The warm air and hidden fissures may obscure heat signatures—use this to map safe zones.  
- **Symbiote Repellent**: The barnacle-like organisms could infest gear. Carry a solvent-based cleaner and UV sterilization wand.  

---

**Final Note**: The Salt Cathedral is a living paradox—a place where decay and perpetuity coexist. Approach it as a pilgrim, not a conqueror. Leave only footprints (if the salt allows), and take only memories. Remember: here, even stillness has a heartbeat.
