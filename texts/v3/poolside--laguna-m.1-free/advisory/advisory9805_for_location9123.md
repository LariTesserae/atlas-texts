   - **Outer Shell:** Waterproof yet breathable jacket and pants to handle the wet air and frozen muck.

   - **Sturdy Boots:** Waterproof, ankle-high mountaineering boots with aggressive treads for icy granite slabs and squelching muck. Add **microspikes or crampons** for traction on frozen surfaces.

   - **GPS Device:** A rugged, waterproof GPS with a long battery life—though be prepared for its accuracy to waver if the landscape shifts.

4. **Protection from the Uncanny:**  
   - **Respirator Mask:** For potential noxious sulfur smells or airborne debris from icefalls.  
   - **Goggles:** To shield eyes from the glare of ice and sudden shifts in light.  
   - **Sturdy Helmet:** To protect against falling rocks or icefalls.

- **Traction and Mobility:**  
  - Carry a **collapsible walking stick** to probe the ground ahead, testing for hidden fissures or unstable ice.  
  - **Rope and Harness:** For crossing treacherous areas, though anchor points may be unreliable.

### **Survival in the Wet, Shifting Environment**

  - **Carry a Water Supply:** The marsh’s fluid is undrinkable. Bring all the water you’ll need for the duration of your stay.  
  - **Sterilization:** A portable UV purifier or iodine tablets, though they may not work on non-water liquids.  
- **Food Storage:**  
  - Use a **bear-proof container** or odor-sealed bag to prevent attracting the glass-furred mammals or other fauna.

  - **Gloves and Long Sleeves:** To avoid contact with unfamiliar plants or creatures.

### **Emergency Preparedness**
- **First Aid Kit:**  
  - Include hypothermia treatment (e.g., heat packs, emergency blanket), antiseptics for cuts from ice or rock, and a **snake bite kit** (just in case—there may be venomous creatures adapted to the cold).

  - Identify multiple exit routes, as paths may collapse or freeze over. Have a **satellite phone** or emergency beacon ready for rapid extraction.
