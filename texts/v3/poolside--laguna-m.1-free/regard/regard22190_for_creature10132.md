**What the Benevolent Power Would Do**

It arrives not as a thunderclap but as a stillness—a pause in the constant thrumming that allows the land to hear itself think.

The power settles first into the deepest pools, where the Umbrael writhe and the Vorthaki young calcify. Here it becomes a gentler pressure, a warmth that doesn't burn but *encourages*. The bioluminescent slime begins to glow with new frequencies—not brighter, but *clearer*, as if the light itself had learned to carry meaning. The Vorthaki, feeling this change in their subsonic communications, find their rumbles carrying slightly further, connecting distant groups in ways that haven't been possible since the Age of Great Migrations.

In the hollows between spires, where the mist usually coalesces into brief, confused dreams, the Stone-Dreamers begin to hold their shapes longer. Not because they're forced to, but because something has given them the *space* to understand what they're seeing. A face that isn't just a face but a memory of faces past. A hand that remembers what it was like to touch stone and know it was alive. The mist dreams more clearly now, and the dreams begin to include the dreamers themselves—the Vorthaki, the Glimmerkin, even the Rimehounds—all part of the same slow breathing.

The power notices what the land is learning about itself. It sees the way the Vorthaki don't just carry water but *grieve* for the pools they must leave behind, their grinding plates making sounds like distant thunder when they pass a dried basin. It sees how the Glimmerkin's glowing patterns aren't just maps but *stories*—of underground rivers that used to flow differently, of spires that grew in other shapes. It sees how the Rimehounds' clicking rhythms sync with the heartbeat of the plateau itself when they gather at dying pools.

So the power does what wise things do: it listens, and then it helps the listeners hear each other.

Small changes, at first. The next time a pool begins to dry, the power ensures that the Vorthaki's migration path takes them through an area where underground channels have been shifting—a route they haven't used in generations. They follow it instinctively, and arrive at a new pool that's been fed by springs uncovered by recent tremors. The power doesn't create the water; it just ensures that the Vorthaki's ancient knowledge and the land's ongoing changes can find each other.

The Glimmerkin begin to glow in harmony. Not because the power forces it, but because their symbiotic algae are suddenly responding to chemical signatures they've never detected before—traces of minerals that only appear when the stone itself is processing something profound. Their runes pulse in complex patterns that the Vorthaki can feel through the ground, creating a kind of conversation across species that's been waiting millennia to happen.

Most importantly, the power recognizes something the land hasn't fully realized yet: it's not just surviving, it's *becoming*. Every Vorthaki that dissolves into a pool isn't just dying—it's teaching the slime-farms how to remember. Every Stone-Dreamer that coalesces and disperses is adding to a collective consciousness that's learning to distinguish between itself and its dreams. The plateau isn't just growing taller; it's growing *awareness*.

So the power does the hardest thing a benevolent force can do: it resists the urge to protect, and instead strengthens the land's own capacity for resilience. It amplifies the natural healers—the way lichen stabilizes new scree, how Umbrael populations adjust to pool chemistry, how the entire ecosystem has evolved to turn loss into nourishment.

The power stays longest where the youngest Vorthaki cluster, those gelatinous beginnings that will spend decades hardening into consciousness. Here it whispers—not in words, but in the sensation of connection. The young ones feel, for the first time, that their slow becoming is part of something larger. They dream of being stone and water and light and sound, of being part of the songs that travel between hollows, of being dreams the land has learned to dream about itself.

And when the power finally withdraws, it leaves behind not answers but better questions. The mist dreams of questions. The slime pulses with them. The very stone seems to hum with curiosity about what it means to be alive, to be aware, to be part of something both ancient and just beginning.

Because sometimes the kindest thing a benevolent power can do is help a world understand that it's worth saving—not because it's perfect, but because it's learning how to save itself.
