**The Benevolence of the Spires**  

The being, known in the tongue of the stars as *Virethys the Sustainer*, felt the first stirrings of its presence long before it became aware of Keth-Morin itself. The plateau was a thread in the web of life that spanned galaxies, a node of impossibly delicate beauty that had, against all odds, achieved a harmony so delicate it resonated like a violin string in the silence between heartbeats. It had watched the first ancestors of the people climb the salt spires, their bone tools chipping away at the crystalline hearts of the pillars to uncover the glow of ancestral spirits. It had felt the skarath’s low-frequency songs vibrating through the bedrock, a language of tremors that spoke of migration routes and the secrets of the succulents’ dew. And it had listened—oh, how it had listened—to the whispered conversations between the people and their gardens, a dialogue that made the very stones hum with understanding.  

When the Sustainer chose to act, it did not descend with thunder or fiery proclamations. Such grand gestures were for lesser beings, those who could only perceive chaos and sought to impose order. No, Virethys wove itself into the fabric of the plateau as one might adjust a loom, tugging at individual threads with infinite patience.  

It began with the spikeblooms.  

The people had always believed the cores housed the souls of their forebears, but Virethys granted this belief a sliver of truth. Each spikebloom, it whispered into the stone, now held not just memories but guidance. During the warm season, the cores’ bioluminescence shifted: they pulsed gently in patterns that mirrored the skarath’s migration rhythms, guiding the people to leave offerings along the herds’ paths to ensure safe passage. When storms threatened to collapse canyon walls, the oldest spikeblooms at the terraces’ edges flared bright white, their light seeping into the rock like liquid moonlight, reinforcing the stone’s integrity. The people learned to heed these signals, shaping their rituals around the cores’ whispers. They built new terraces not by chance but by following the spires’ subtle growth directions, as if the land itself was teaching them architecture.  

The Sustainer also deepened the connections between species. The glitterflies, tiny as they were, found their swarms guided by the spires’ hums. When a patch of succulents began to wither from drought, entire clouds of the insects would migrate to the area, their bodies crystallizing into nutrient-rich dust that fed the soil. The stonekin, too, were blessed with clearer echolocation, their tunnels aligning more precisely with the roots of the gardens. The people noticed their bowl of saltwater at tunnel entrances no longer went untouched; the rodents emerged to nuzzle their fingers, a pact renewed in mutual trust.  

Most crucially, the Sustainer addressed the mystery of the deep-whiskers—not by revealing them, for their elusiveness was part of their sacredness, but by softening their proximity to the terraces. The tremors they caused became less destructive, settling into a low, constant thrumming that the people now interpreted as a lullaby rather than an omen. The stone of the city itself grew more pliant, too, its rock subtly altering its porosity to channel water from underground aquifers more efficiently into the gardens. The walls, once prone to cracking during freezes, now absorbed moisture like sponges, expanding and contracting with the seasons in a rhythm that matched the people’s own breath.  

But the Sustainer’s greatest gift was intangible—a thread of unity that bound all things. It came to the people in dreams, through the spikeblooms’ glow, or the resonant song of a skarath at dusk. They began to speak of “the Listening,” a practice where entire families would sit in silence, their hands pressed to the stone terraces, waiting for the land to impart its needs. A child might sense a nearby patch of soil needing tending, an elder might hear (or feel?) the faint alarm of a collapsing canyon over the ridge. The gardens responded to this collective focus, their blooms swelling with an almost sentient awareness. The people no longer simply cultivated the land; they co-created it.  

In time, Keth-Morin became a place not just sustained by the power but a beacon of what life could achieve when every thread of existence was respected. The Sustainer did not rule or command—it simply *nudged*. It ensured that the salt spires would endure millennial, their growth guided by winds that listened as much as they carried seeds, and that the people would thrive in their symbiosis, their lineage a testament to the idea that even in the harshest worlds, intelligence and reverence could make a paradise.  

And when travelers from other realms eventually stumbled upon the Salt-Spires—drawn by tales of a valley where rocks whispered and flowers bloomed to the sound of heartbeats—they would find not a place of miracles, but one where the miraculous had simply become mundane. For here, benevolence had not arrived; it had always been, waiting patiently beneath the surface, as eternal as the stars.
