If I were such a power—benevolent, yes, and having learned wisdom through eons of watching worlds turn—I would first *listen*. Not merely with attention, but with the deep listening that perceives the subtle music beneath all things. And what I would hear in the Caldera of Sighing Stones is something rare: a place where brokenness has been transformed into beauty, where the accidental and the intentional have woven themselves together into something greater than either alone.

I would approach quietly, as one might tiptoe into a temple where prayers are still being spoken. My presence would be felt not as intervention but as a gentle amplification of what already exists—strengthening the bonds between the pluies and their machine-homes, ensuring that the rust crawlers' work of transformation continues unchecked by outside forces. I would nudge the occasional mineral deposit to form just so in the weeping stones, enhancing the pluesteading process, making the drip-drip rhythm more melodious, more sustaining.

But perhaps most importantly, I would *protect the mystery*. The steamborn—those whispered legends—would be allowed to remain elusive. If they exist, if they are truly the echoes of those who came before, clothed in vapor and memory, then they serve as living bridges between what was and what has become. To reveal them too soon, to categorize them, to study them, would be to diminish their magic and their purpose. Instead, I would ensure that the mists remain thick enough to preserve their sanctuary, while thinning just enough in other places to allow for moments of wonder when a traveler might glimpse something that could be a person, or could be steam, or could be both.

For the industrial relics, I would accelerate their integration into the landscape—not destroying them, but guiding them toward complete marriage with the living world. Metal would slowly transmute into minerals beneficial to the rust crawlers. Circuits would become veins for the fogweavers. The old control panels would evolve into true nests, their buttons blossoming into phosphorescent growths that pulse in time with the caldera's heartbeat.

And when the seasonal steam cycles grow too violent, when the geothermal vents threaten to overwhelm the delicate balance, I would whisper to the stones themselves, teaching them new songs of release, helping them regulate their breath so that abundance continues without destruction.

Most of all, I would ensure this place remains *found but not exploited*. Let it appear on no maps, yet let those who need to find it—whether scientists seeking to understand symbiosis, artists hungry for beauty, or lost souls needing sanctuary—discover it in their own time. Let them come not as conquerors or developers, but as students. Let them leave changed, carrying with them a deeper understanding that nothing is ever truly lost, only transformed into the raw material for new wonders.

The Caldera of Sighing Stones already knows how to heal itself; my greatest gift would be to simply witness this miracle, and ensure it has the space and time to continue doing so forever.
