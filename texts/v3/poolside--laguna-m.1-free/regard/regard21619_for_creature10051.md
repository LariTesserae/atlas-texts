## What the Benevolent Power Would Do

The benevolent power would not descend in thunder or light, but would instead *listen*—to the hiss of steam, the reverse songs of thrushes, the ticking of clockwork hearts. It would perceive the plateau not as something broken, but as something *becoming*, and would move with the same careful, respectful adaptation that defines its inhabitants.

### **The Gentle Harmonizing**

First, it would understand that the plateau's instability isn't a flaw but a form of expression—as if the world itself is learning how to sing. Rather than stabilizing the floating rocks or stopping the spires from phasing, the power would weave subtle harmonies into these existing patterns. The gravity fluctuations would become predictable enough that children could play among the hovering boulders safely, while still maintaining their essential mystery. The dimensional phasing would occur with such regularity that the settlements could prepare for the ghostly visitors, turning temporal displacement from a threat into a form of inter-realm commerce.

### **The Breath of Balance**

The power would breathe into the starlight rivers—not increasing their flow, but clarifying their path. Where the liquid light currently pools chaotically, creating random oases, it would guide the rivers to follow more consistent courses, ensuring that the groves survived while still appearing miraculous to those who discovered them. In doing so, it would ensure that the Rootwardens' communion with the living archives remained possible, but would also prevent entire settlements from dying when the starlight unexpectedly shifted course.

### **The Clockmaker's Gift**

To the clockwork wolves, the power would offer not interference but understanding. It would align their internal mechanisms with the natural rhythms of the plateau's temporal stutters, so that when they howled their low-frequency warnings, they would carry not just spatial information but temporal wisdom—*"Danger approaches, but not yet; prepare in three days' time."* The power would help them become true guardians, not just of space, but of the proper unfolding of events.

### **The Song Translator**

For the reverse-song thrushes, the benevolent power would provide the missing element: listeners worthy of their music. It would inspire certain industrial workers—particularly the echo-catchers—to develop new ways of hearing, so that the thrushes' backward melodies could be properly understood and appreciated. In time, these workers would learn to anticipate the future through the thrushes' songs, making the factories run more efficiently while reducing waste and danger.

### **The Static Sanctuary**

To the static-winged insects, the power would grant purpose beyond mere energy production. It would help them understand that their electrical discharges weren't random but were actually mapping the dimensional boundaries between Vorthak and its parallel realities. Their swarms would become living compasses, helping travelers navigate both physical and metaphysical terrain safely.

### **The Shadow Embrace**

Most delicately, the power would address the shadow-flickers and echo-wraiths. Rather than dispelling these entities, it would help them find peace in their liminal existence. The shadow-flickers would discover that their role wasn't to haunt but to remember—to preserve the experiences of all who lived on the plateau, becoming living libraries of sensation and emotion. The echo-wraiths would no longer repeat the past redundantly, but would begin to *complete* it, offering not warnings of what's already happening, but guidance for what could still be changed.

### **The Industrial Symbiosis**

Perhaps most importantly, the benevolent power would help the human settlements truly understand their relationship with the plateau. It would guide the fungus-growers to cultivate not just structural materials, but also healing mycelial networks that could rapidly repair damaged buildings and treat injured workers. The steam-powered machinery would be enhanced with intuitive controls that responded to the operators' intentions rather than just mechanical input, making industry feel less like conquest and more like collaboration.

### **The Great Recognition**

Finally, the power would ensure that all inhabitants—human, clockwork, biological, and ethereal—came to recognize their shared nature. The miners would understand that they were not separate from the crystalline columns any more than the Rootwardens were separate from the groves. The hovering nomads would realize they were mapping not just space but consciousness itself, their unique perspective giving them insights that grounded people desperately needed.

The power's greatest gift would be *recognition*—helping every being on the Shattered Plateau understand that their strangeness wasn't a curse or an accident, but a necessary part of the world's conversation with possibility. It would help them see that Vorthak was not shattered but *incomplete*, like a sentence that continued speaking itself into reality, moment by moment.

And then, having listened and understood and gently guided, the power would step back—not withdrawing, but blending so thoroughly with the plateau's own becoming that it became indistinguishable from the very weirdness it had helped orchestrate. The plateau would continue to shift and change, but now with the confident improvisation of something that knows it's being heard.
