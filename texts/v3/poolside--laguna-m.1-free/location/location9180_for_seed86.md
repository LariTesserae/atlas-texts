**The Cavernous Hollows**  

Beneath a sky of unbroken stone, the Hollows stretch in endless, echoing darkness, their ceilings lost to shadow. The air is cool and still, carrying the mineral tang of damp earth and the faint, sweet rot of decaying organic matter. Pockets of moisture seep through cracks in the rock, pooling into shallow, stagnant pools that reflect the dim, amber glow of bioluminescent fungi clinging to the walls. These fungi, sparse and stunted, form the region’s only vegetation—a patchwork of pale, ghostly clusters that pulse faintly with the rhythm of some unseen heartbeat.  

The terrain is a jagged mosaic of sharp-edged stone, carved by ancient waters now reduced to whispers. Stalactites and stalagmites jut like broken teeth, their surfaces slick with condensation, while the ground is a chaotic expanse of loose gravel, smooth boulders, and pits where the earth has collapsed into hidden voids. The rock here is young in geological terms, freshly exposed by some long-ago upheaval, its edges unsoftened by time. The passage of eons has left the landscape in a state of frozen dynamism—stable, yet raw, as if the world itself paused mid-breath.  

Sound dominates this realm. The constant drip of water, amplified by the cavernous space, creates a rhythmic percussion. Deeper still, the low rumble of underground rivers vibrates through the stone, their sources and destinations unknown. But it is the fauna that drowns all else in noise. Flocks of echolocating creatures—bat-like in form but with translucent wings veined like leaves—swarm through the air, their shrieks and clicks forming a cacophony that never ceases. In the shadows, something larger moves: the scrape of claws on stone, the wet slap of tails, the low croaking of amphibious masses clustered around the water pools. These creatures are legion, their shapes indistinct in the gloom—some scaled, some furred, all adapted to the damp dark. They breed and feed in the absence of predators, their numbers unchecked, their presence a roiling carpet of life that shifts and churns without pause.  

The light here is borrowed from the fungi and the pale, sourceless luminescence that seeps through certain rocks. It casts everything in a hazy, otherworldly twilight, enough to see by but never enough to dispel the sense of being watched. The absence of built structures or artifacts is absolute; this place has never known the touch of tool or hand. What exists here is pure and undisturbed, though the fauna’s primitive tools—sticks, stones, the sharpening of teeth and claws against the rock—hint at a rudimentary intelligence, or perhaps just desperation in a world of endless night.  

Despite the chaos of sound and motion, the Hollows are static, unchanging. The rivers below flow to no sea, the fauna breeds to no future, and the light never shifts. It is a world paused in equilibrium, its vastness and cacophony contained within a bell jar of time, where life claws at the edges of existence and finds, impossibly, a way to overflow.
