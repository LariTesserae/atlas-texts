The **Cathedral Depths** stretch in vast, echoing chambers, their ceilings lost to shadow and light. This is a world where the earth’s bones have been sculpted not by time alone, but by eons of stillness, leaving walls of **primordial glass**—smooth, obsidian-like formations that hum faintly with residual heat from the planet’s core. The air is cool and damp, carrying the mineral tang of condensation that beads on every surface, pooling in shallow basins carved by ancient waters. These pools are few and far between, but their presence is felt: sudden mists that bloom in the stillness, swallowing sound, and leaving behind a film of moisture that slicks the stone.  

Here, **vegetation is alien or extinct**. The landscape is instead a tapestry of **fungal filaments** that carpet the ground, glowing faintly in hues of blue and green—a bioluminescent network that pulses like a slow heartbeat. These fungi grow in concentric rings around the ruins of an **urban sprawl**, where structures of carved stone and petrified wood rise in terraced layers. The buildings are rudimentary yet deliberate: low arches, tunnels lined with smooth clay tiles, and amphitheaters sunken into the earth. They speak of a civilization that thrived here before the great stillness, their tools simple—flint blades, woven fiber ropes, and vessels molded from compressed clay.  

The fauna is **abundant but unseen**. Massive, eyeless creatures slither through the fungal forests, their bodies leaving faint trails in the dust. Smaller beings—crystalline insects, perhaps, or segmented arthropods—skitter along the walls, their carapaces reflecting the fungi’s glow. Yet the dominant sound here is **not life**, but the low, resonant **tremor of the earth itself**. The **dynamic turbulence** stems from deep below: geothermal vents release bursts of steam and tremors, causing the glassy walls to crack and reform in erratic patterns. Entire sections of the city have been swallowed by sudden fissures, only to be reborn as new caverns.  

Light comes from the fungi, yes, but also from **veins of quartz** embedded in the rock, which catch and refract whatever ambient glow exists, casting prismatic flashes across the caverns. The light is **bright**, almost oppressive in its constancy, as if the world itself rejects darkness. Yet there is an eerie stillness to it—**no wind stirs the mists**, no voices echo. The silence is absolute, save for the occasional drip of water and the far-off rumble of shifting stone.  

This is a **mineral world in flux**, where the rules of stability no longer apply. The erosion has settled into a **primordial stability**, but the turbulence suggests something is constantly unraveling and rebuilding. The fauna adapts with frenetic energy, but their movements are ghostly, almost spectral. The ruins speak of a society that once thrived on ordered construction, but now the city is a **layered ruin**, half-buried in sediment, half-reclaimed by the fungi. Somewhere in the depths, the earth’s chaos stirs anew, and the Cathedral Depths await their next transformation.
