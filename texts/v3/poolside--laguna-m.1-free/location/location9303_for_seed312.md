# The Fractured Plateau of Veyl

## Terrain and Scale

The land sprawls across an immense, elevated plateau that stretches beyond the horizon in every direction—a vastness so complete it seems to possess its own weather systems. This is the Vastness of Veyl, where the very air carries the weight of scale. The plateau sits at a crushing altitude, its edges dropping away into mist-shrouded abysses below, but here on the tableland itself, the land is relatively flat yet scarred by the raw violence of recent geological birth. Sharp ridges and spires of newly-formed stone jut upward like the broken teeth of some primordial leviathan, their surfaces still gleaming with the freshness of recent eruption or tectonic upheaval. The very crust here feels young, as if the land has only just begun to find its shape after some cosmic convulsion.

## Water and Vegetation

Despite the cold that bites at everything, water is present but stingy with its gifts. Seasonal rains fall in brief, intense bursts, creating temporary streams that carve shallow channels through the rocky surface before freezing solid overnight. Pockets of water collect in natural basins, forming icy mirrors that reflect the harsh sky. These pools are the lifeblood of the sparse vegetation—patches of hardy, low-growing lichens and mosses, their dark green and gray forms clinging stubbornly to sheltered cracks and crevices. Here and there, clusters of stunted shrubs huddle together, their twisted branches cradling tiny, waxy leaves that minimize water loss in the thin, cold air. The vegetation forms a patchwork quilt across the landscape, dense enough in places to support life, but generally sparse and struggling.

## Atmosphere and Cold

The atmosphere is thin and sharp, carrying the crystalline clarity that comes with extreme elevation. Every breath burns slightly in the lungs, each exhale puffing white in the dim light. The temperature never rises above freezing, and the cold is a constant companion, seeping into bones and metal alike. Snow falls occasionally, but more often the precipitation comes as freezing rain that coats surfaces in a treacherous glaze of ice. The air carries the scent of stone and frost, with undertones of wood smoke from the scattered settlements that dot the landscape.

## Urban Settlements

Scattered across this harsh realm are the settlements of Veyl's inhabitants—cities and towns that cling to the landscape like barnacles on an ancient ship. These are not the grand metropolises of legend, but rather dense aggregations of primitive construction. Buildings are hewn from the living rock itself, carved directly into cliff faces and canyon walls where they can find shelter from the relentless winds. Others are built from massive stone blocks fitted together without mortar, their surfaces rough-hewn and functional. The density of these settlements is remarkable given the harsh conditions—they press close together, with buildings nearly touching, creating a maze of narrow streets and alleyways that wind between structures like frozen rivers. Smoke rises from countless chimneys, carrying the scent of cooking fires and the tang of metal-smithing, for even in this primitive age, the inhabitants have learned to work the scarce metals they can find.

## Materials and Technology

The technology here is primitive but effective, born of necessity rather than sophistication. Tools are forged from meteoric iron and native copper, worked by hand in small, communal forges that glow with communal fires. The stonework shows evidence of careful craftsmanship, with walls built to precise specifications despite the lack of advanced tools. Bridges and aqueducts span chasms with engineering that speaks to generations of accumulated knowledge, passed down through oral tradition. The materials are largely local—granite and basalt for foundations, timber hauled from distant valleys below, and wool from the hardy mountain goats that provide both sustenance and clothing.

## Ecology and Fauna

Despite the harsh conditions, life thrives here in ways both expected and surprising. The fauna is abundant, though specialized for survival. Massive herds of mountain sheep with thick, shaggy coats traverse the plateaus, their hooves clicking against stone as they seek out the sparse vegetation. Packs of wolf-like creatures with white winter pelts hunt in coordinated groups, their breath forming clouds in the frigid air. In the deeper valleys, populations of hardy deer browse on the few available plants, while overhead, massive birds with wingspans that dwarf a man circle lazily, riding the thermal currents that rise from the sun-warmed rock faces. Small mammals—mice, voles, and lemmings—populate the spaces between rocks, serving as food for the many predators of the realm.

## Sounds and Atmosphere

The sounds of Veyl are mostly quiet but pregnant with meaning. Wind whistles through gaps in the rock formations, sometimes rising to a mournful howl, other times dropping to an almost imperceptible whisper. In the settlements, the rhythmic hammering of metalworkers provides a constant backdrop, punctuated by the calls of vendors hawking their wares in the market squares. From the surrounding wilderness come the distant calls of the large birds, the rustling of small creatures in the undergrowth, and occasionally the haunting howl of wolves on the hunt. At night, the silence becomes profound, broken only by the occasional crack of settling ice or the soft footsteps of nocturnal hunters.

## Rules of the World

Time moves slowly here, measured not in hours but in seasons that arrive gradually and depart with reluctance. The dynamic nature of the landscape is patient—glaciers creep forward millimeter by millimeter, rock formations are slowly carved by wind and ice, and entire settlements may rise and fall over the course of centuries without dramatic change. Yet life persists, adapts, and finds ways to thrive. The inhabitants have learned to read the subtle signs of their world—the way the wind shifts before a storm, how the animals behave when danger approaches, and the precise moment when the brief growing season begins. Their relationship with the land is intimate and respectful, born from generations of coexistence with a place that gives little but demands much.

The very rocks themselves seem alive with potential, as if the young land is still dreaming itself into being. Strange magnetic properties in some regions cause compass needles to spin wildly, forcing navigators to rely on traditional methods. Occasionally, the ground trembles with minor earthquakes that remind everyone that this is still an evolving landscape, where the line between geology and biology sometimes blurs in ways that hint at deeper mysteries yet to be understood.

This is a world of contrasts—harsh yet nurturing, vast yet intimate, young yet ancient in ways that defy simple understanding. It is a place where humans have carved out existence not through dominance but through adaptation, becoming part of the landscape rather than separate from it, their lives woven into the slow, patient rhythms of stone and ice.
