**The Sunken Reaches of Vorthak**  

The world here is an ocean, but not the ocean of our knowing. Its depths sprawl across what was once a highland valley, its ridges and peaks now drowned beneath kilometers of brackish water, the land transformed into a labyrinth of underwater mountains, terraces, and canyons. The elevation is deceptive—towering seamounts rise like drowned cathedrals, their slopes etched by time into terraces of sediment and black coral, while valleys plunge into abyssal plains where sunlight is a forgotten myth. The water itself is cold, bitterly so, carrying the memory of ice ages in its currents.  

**Terrain & Materials**:  
The seafloor is a patchwork of textures. In the shallows, where light still filters pale and green, fields of giant kelp undulate in slow motion, their stipes thick as ancient tree trunks, their fronds broad and gelatinous, shimmering with bioluminescent speckles. These "forests" cling to the remnants of bedrock, which are pitted and eroded into smooth, rounded shapes—proof of eons of liquid weathering. Further down, the terrain shifts to vast plains of silt and volcanic rock, their surfaces carved by unseen forces into smooth, wave-like patterns. In the deepest trenches, the ground gives way to fields of hexagonal basalt columns, their geometric regularity unnerving in the perpetual gloom, as though the landscape itself might be artificial.  

**Atmosphere & Light**:  
Darkness is absolute here, save for the faint phosphorescence of the kelp and the occasional flicker of deeper creatures—if they exist. The water is thick with minerals, giving it a greenish opacity, and the temperature sits perpetually at a knife-edge chill, just above freezing. Currents move with a slow, deliberate intensity, carrying sediment and the whispers of the deep.  

**Ecology**:  
There is no animal life, but the vegetation thrives in strange symbiosis with the ruins. The kelp grows from submerged patches of soil and debris, their root-like holdfasts anchoring to the skeletons of old ships and collapsed stone piers. In the trenches, forests of tube worms cluster around hydrothermal vents, their plumes glowing faintly blue. These vents also spew mineral-rich fluids, fostering microbial mats that coat the basalt columns in iridescent streaks of rust and jade. The ecosystem is sparse but active—microbes and extremophiles driving processes unseen, their metabolic heat and chemical discharges shaping the landscape.  

**Built Environment**:  
Scattered across the seafloor are remnants of a civilization, their origins obscure. Stone circles stand half-buried in silt, their alignments suggesting some long-forgotten astronomical purpose. Crumbling wharves jut from the kelp like the bones of a leviathan, their wooden pilings encrusted with barnacles and coral. In the deeper valleys, what appear to be houses or temples rest tilted on the seabed, their windows dark and their doorways gaping like mouths. These structures are primitive in design—rough-hewn stones and timbers, evidence of a people who relied on simple tools and raw materials. Some bears faint carvings of spirals and jagged lines, symbols that seem to shift when viewed peripherally.  

**Sounds**:  
The water thrums with sound. The creaking of ancient timbers under pressure, the hiss of vent emissions, and the low-frequency groan of tectonic shifts echo through the depths. The kelp forests churn with a wet, rhythmic rustling, like pages of a book being turned by invisible hands. Farther out, there are noises that do not belong—the grinding of stone against stone as the terrain reshapes itself, and a distant, rhythmic pulsing that might be the heartbeat of the planet itself.  

**Weirdness & Dynamics**:  
Time behaves oddly here. Sediment layers show evidence of sudden, violent shifts—as if the landscape has been sculpted in bursts rather than gradually. Some of the stone ruins seem to be *moving* in slow increments, their positions altering imperceptibly over years. The kelp forests bloom and wither in cycles, their glow dimming and brightening with no apparent cause. Most unsettling are the "breathing" formations—columns and arches of rock that expand and contract rhythmically, as though the ocean itself were inhaling and exhaling through them.  

In the shadows between the kelp, there are places where the water feels *thicker*, resistant to movement. Dive into these zones, and your vision blurs as if reality is a thin membrane. Some who have tried claim to see flashes of the world before the Flood—or perhaps what lies beyond it.  

**Rules of the World**:  
- **The Deep is Alive**: The ocean here is not empty but a vast, slumbering entity. Its movements are deliberate, and its memory stretches back to eras before humanity.  
- **The Ruins Remember**: The structures are not merely abandoned; they are *waiting*. Their carvings and alignments suggest they serve a purpose, though that purpose remains dormant.  
- **Darkness Breeds Light**: The deepest trenches, where light never reaches, are paradoxically the most luminous, their bioluminescence a defense against something lurking in the abyssal dark.  

This is a place of contrasts: cold and heat, silence and cacophony, decay and growth. It is a tomb and a womb, a frontier and a riddle.
