### **The Frozen Depths of Thryl'Mor**  
*"A world suspended between thunderous silence and the scream of ice."*

---

### **Terrain & Geography**  
Thryl'Mor is a vast, fractured realm of **glacial continents** adrift in an abyssal ocean. These continents are not true land but towering *icebergs* of immense scale—some spanning thousands of kilometers—calved from an unknown, deeper frozen mass. Their surfaces are jagged and sharp, sculpted by eons of tectonic ice shifts, yet they drift in slow, stately orbits around a dim, distant star. The submerged parts of these continents are hollow, their interiors riddled with caverns and tunnels carved by ancient pressure, creating a labyrinthine terrain of submerged voids and ice-clad corridors.  

Between them, the ocean itself is a **wall of liquid, supercooled brine**, black and motionless except for the slow creep of thermal columns rising from the abyss below. The water is dense with salt and ammonia, maintaining a slushy consistency but never fully freezing, even at the surface.  

---

### **Materials & Atmosphere**  
The ice is not ordinary: it is **organic-industrial hybrid**, a composite of frozen ammonia-water matrices threaded with rusted metal filaments, coppery conduits, and polymer chains. These materials form naturally over decades through the slow crystallization of industrial runoff that seeps into the ice from the scattered structures below. Minerals like pyrite and magnetite are embedded in the ice, giving it a faint, eerie luminescence under the weak sunlight.  

The atmosphere is thick with **microscopic ice crystals** suspended in a near-vacuum below the ocean's surface. These crystals refract what little light filters down, creating an eternal twilight of indigo and silver. The air (or rather, the ocean’s dissolved gases) carries the sharp tang of ozone and the metallic bite of decay from the industrial ruins below.  

---

### **Ecosystem & Fauna**  
Despite the lack of vegetation, Thryl'Mor teems with **abundant, biomechanical life**. The dominant species are the *Cryovora*: massive, whale-like creatures that resemble living icebergs, their bodies encrusted with colonies of chemosynthetic microbes. They migrate through the ocean, grazing on mineral-rich seeps and leaving trails of bioluminescent algae in their wake. Smaller fauna include swarms of *Keth’Vorn*—translucent, eel-like predators with mineralized scales that grind against each other to produce a high-pitched resonance, adding to the cacophony.  

The ocean floor is home to *Anomalocaris*-like scavengers that burrow through the ice, consuming the rust-eaten remains of machinery. Their exoskeletons are armored with iron oxide plates, making them look like living relics of the world’s industrial past.  

---

### **Built Environment**  
Scattered across the ice continents are the **remnants of the "Forge Epoch"**, an age of industrialization that ended abruptly millennia ago. These remnants are not ruins but *preserved artifacts* in a state of eerie stasis:  
- **Submerged Factory-Cities**: Enormous, dome-shaped structures half-buried in ice, their chimneys still emitting a faint steam even after eons. Their interiors hum with residual magnetic fields, keeping their machinery in motionless operation.  
- **Icebound Refineries**: Towering platforms that process the ocean’s brine into strange, unstable alloys. These refineries are unmanned, their processes governed by autonomous algorithms that loop endlessly without purpose.  
- **Singularity Spire**: A single, towering spire of black glass and steel that pierces the ice shelf, its apex hidden in the clouds. It emits a low-frequency vibration that resonates with the Cryovora’s songs, acting as a sort of ecological anchor.  

---

### **Weirdness & Dynamics**  
The world operates in a state of **frozen equilibrium**. The industrial systems continue to function but produce nothing; the fauna thrive without ecological imbalance; the icebergs drift but never collide. The only changes occur in the subharmonic rumbles of the ocean itself—a low, omnipresent **deafening sound** caused by the *Singing Ice*: the interaction of thermal columns, industrial machinery, and Cryovora vocalizations. This sound is both a navigational tool for the fauna and a form of resonance that keeps the ice continents stable.  

Weirdness manifests in the form of **Temporal Echoes**: faint, ghostly duplicates of the industrial structures that flicker in and out of visibility, suggesting the world is caught in a loop of its own history. These echoes sometimes align with the real structures, creating overlapping space-time anomalies that the fauna instinctively avoid.  

---

### **Sound & Light**  
The soundscape is a **symphony of discordant industrial moans** and natural ice crackles. The Cryovora’s songs are the loudest, harmonizing with the refineries’ vibrations to create a perpetual, thunderous drone. Paradoxically, this noise masks the subtle mechanical whispers of the autonomous systems, which are eerily quiet despite their activity.  

Light is filtered through the ice into a spectrum of cobalt and pale green. Bioluminescent algae cling to the submerged factories, casting shifting shadows that mimic the movements of the Forge Epoch’s workers, now long gone.  

---

### **Rules of the World**  
1. **The Ice Remembers**: The icebergs retain the memories of the Forge Epoch, their crystalline structures encoding data like biological DNA.  
2. **Sound is Currency**: The Cryovora and industrial systems exchange energy through resonance, making the deafening noise a vital force.  
3. **No True Death**: All structures and fauna exist in a state of perpetual, unchanging existence, neither decaying nor evolving.  
4. **The Singularity Spire’s Gaze**: The spire’s vibrations are slowly "curing" the ice continents, altering their mineral composition into something alien.  

---

Thryl'Mor is a paradox: alive yet stagnant, frozen yet dynamic, a world where industry and nature have merged into something neither built nor born, but *maintained*.
