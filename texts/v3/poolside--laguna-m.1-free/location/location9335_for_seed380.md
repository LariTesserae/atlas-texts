**The Veiled Warrens of Tarn Hollow**  

Beneath a canopy of eroded limestone that filters the sky into a perpetual dusk, the Veiled Warrens of Tarn Hollow cling to existence at the edge of a drowned world. The terrain is a labyrinth of damp, cathedral-like chambers, their walls etched by millennia of patient erosion into undulating curves and honeycombed crevices. Here, the ground slopes gently upward in places, revealing layers of sedimentary strata that have been hollowed out by time, their surfaces streaked with veins of mineral deposits that weep slow, mineral-rich seepages. The air is thick with condensation, each breath a mist that clings to skin and stone alike, leaving everything perpetually slick and glazed.  

**Materials** shift asymmetrically between natural and crafted. The bedrock itself is a tapestry of honey-colored calcite and darker flint, worn smooth by subterranean floods and the slow dance of tectonic memory. Embedded within the stone are remnants of an older order: riveted metal pipes, now softened and dulled by rust, snake through tunnels like petrified serpents. These are joined by clusters of strange, barnacle-like crystals that sprout from fissures, their surfaces iridescent with moisture, and by the skeletal remains of machinery half-swallowed by root-like fungi. The built environment here is a patchwork of vaulted chambers carved for industry, their ceilings supported by columns of fused concrete and stone, now draped in curtains of silver moss that shiver with every reverberation.  

**Atmosphere** hums with a dim, diffused light that seems to emanate from the walls themselves—the result of bioluminescent lichen cultivated into mosaic patterns by an unknown hand, their glow a muted blue-green that stains the air. The moisture here is constant, a fine spray that rises from countless seeps and pools, settling into a fog that muffles sound and softens edges. The temperature hovers at the cusp of cold and mild, cool enough to make the breath mist but not so low as to freeze the countless trickles of water that bead and drip from every surface.  

**Sounds** are sparse but layered. The dominant noise is the rhythmic *plink… plink… plink* of water droplets finding their way into unseen reservoirs, a metronome that underscores the slow pulse of the place. Beneath this, there’s the creak of ancient metal joints expanding and contracting with the damp, and the occasional groan of stone settling—a sound like a deep, tectonic sigh. Occasionally, a distant mechanical hum thrums, a relic engine somewhere in the depths still operational, its purpose obscure. The fauna contributes its own low symphony: the skittering of clawed feet on stone, the soft clicks of chitinous legs, and the almost subliminal rustle of wings in the shadows.  

**Ecology** defies easy categorization. Sparse vegetation takes the form of hardy, moss-like growths that carpet the stone in patches, their fronds tipped with tiny, scale-like leaves that shimmer with trapped moisture. These give way to clumps of fungal blooms, some of which pulse faintly with their own inner light, casting eerie patterns on the walls. The fauna is abundant and strange: eyeless, amphibious creatures the size of cats that navigate via echolocation, their skin mottled like the stone itself; swarms of iridescent insects that flit through the fog, their wings leaving trails of phosphorescence; and the Watchers, tall, spindly beings with elongated limbs that move soundlessly along the walls, tending to the machinery with appendages tipped in delicate, multi-jointed claws.  

**Rules of the World** here are dictated by equilibrium—one that balances the old and the new, the natural and the artificial. The Warrens exist in a state of managed decay, their systems maintained by the fauna or some ancient, dormant intelligence. Water is channeled through networks of pipes and aqueducts, some of which still function, delivering moisture to the roots of hanging gardens cultivated in artificial caverns. The industrial infrastructure, though weathered, persists: valves open and close in response to pressure changes, gears turn slowly in the dark, and the bioluminescent lichen is carefully pruned to maintain its patterns. Time moves differently here, measured in the slow growth of crystals, the patient wear of stone, and the gradual shift of ecosystems.  

**Weirdness** manifests in subtle ways: shadows that linger too long after their objects have moved, reflections in the pools that don’t quite match their surroundings, and the sense that the Warrens are not static but slowly *breathing*, expanding and contracting with a rhythm felt in the bones rather than the ears. The machinery seems to adapt to its environment, parts growing crusts of organic material that blur the line between biology and technology. At the heart of the Warrens lies a massive, half-submerged engine—its purpose unknown, its operation marked by a low, subharmonic vibration that resonates through the stone, as if the entire space were a living thing, and this engine its pulse.  

In Tarn Hollow, the boundaries between ruin and renewal blur. It is a place where the industrial past has been softened by time and transformed into something both alien and familiar—a sanctuary for the strange, sustained by forces that are neither wholly natural nor entirely mechanical, but something caught beautifully and precariously in between.
