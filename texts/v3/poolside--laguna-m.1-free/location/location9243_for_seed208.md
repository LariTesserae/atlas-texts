**The Weeping Expanse**  

The land here is ancient—older than memory, older than the slow grind of tectonic plates. It is a low-lying realm, a sprawling basin where the earth has settled into its final, flattened form. The terrain is a mosaic of slick, obsidian-like rock formations, their surfaces glazed by millennia of condensation. Water seeps from countless pores in the stone, weeping into shallow pools that mirror the dim, diffused light filtering through a sky choked with slow-moving mists.  

This is not a world of plants. Instead, the ground is carpeted with vast networks of *mineral mycelium*—crystalline filaments that grow in fractal patterns, pulsing faintly with bioluminescent blues and purples. These structures, neither fully organic nor inorganic, absorb moisture and minerals from the air and rock, forming fragile, glassy shelves and towers. Some resemble the bleached remains of coral, others the chaotic latticework of frost, but all hum with a low, subsonic resonance that thrums through the soles of your feet.  

The air is cool and damp, tasting of iron and ozone. Sound here is a living thing—a constant, layered cacophony. The weeping rocks drip in rhythmic, orchestrated patterns, their drops striking stone or water with hollow, metallic tones that echo for seconds. Wind channels through fissures in the bedrock, creating haunting whistles and booms that mingle with the distant, resonant calls of the *shard-hunters*: flightless, six-legged creatures with carapaces of fused quartz, their legs clicking in staccato rhythms as they scuttle between the pools. Their cries are sharp, almost mechanical, adding to the din. Overhead, something massive stirs in the mist—its wingbeats (if they are wings) generate shockwaves felt in the chest, though it remains unseen.  

Scattered across the expanse are the remnants of structures, built by hands long gone or evolved. Crude stone circles stand half-buried in mineral mycelium, their purpose unclear—ritual sites, perhaps, or markers. Rusted, simple tools lie half-embedded in the silt: picks with hafts of petrified wood, blades of unknown metal, their edges still sharp despite the damp. These artifacts seem recent compared to the timeless landscape, suggesting the presence of a sparse, itinerant people who come to harvest the mycelium’s resonant properties or to listen to the stones.  

Life here is abundant but strange. In addition to the shard-hunters, there are the *glassworms*—translucent, serpentine beings that tunnel through the mineral networks, leaving behind trails of iridescent slime that harden into new filaments. Colonies of eyeless, eel-like *mist-drinkers* glide through the pools, their bodies undulating in hypnotic synchrony. The sheer number of creatures ensures constant motion: predators and prey chase through the pools, wings clatter against rock, and the mycelium itself quivers with activity. Yet the density remains sparse—a deliberate balance with the emptiness, as if the land actively repels overcrowding.  

The light is perpetually dim, as if the sun exists only as a rumor. The sky glows a bruised, greenish-gray, and shadows cling to the landscape like oil. At intervals, the ground trembles—not from earthquakes, but from the slow, tectonic-like pulsing of the mycelium itself, a deep, rhythmic undulation that suggests the entire expanse is a single, slumbering organism.  

This is a place of cycles: the weeping of the rocks, the resonance of the structures, the migration of the shard-hunters. It is a world that has forgotten growth and only knows maintenance, a realm where life persists in strange forms, and the most profound sounds are the ones that shake the bones.
