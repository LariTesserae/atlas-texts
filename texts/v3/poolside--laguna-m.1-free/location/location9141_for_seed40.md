**The Verdant Spires of Yttrium**  

The landscape defies the altitude it occupies—a high, cold plateau scarred by the sharp, crystalline formations of *yttrium stone*, a material that hums faintly with residual industrial energy. These towers, some hundreds of meters tall, rise abruptly from the ground like broken teeth, their facets gleaming under the relentless glare of a pale sun. The terrain is dotted with shallow, interconnected pools of water that percolate from fissures in the stone, their surfaces reflecting the sky in distorted, shimmering patches. The air is damp but thin, carrying the metallic tang of rust and the faint ozone scent of dormant machinery.  

Constructed canals snake between the spires, their banks lined with *vitreous reeds*—glassy, synthetic plants that grow in sparse clusters, their leaves chiming softly in the wind. These reeds are among the few vegetation here, engineered to survive in acidified soil and the plateaus’ harsh conditions. The built environment is total: every surface is manmade, from the hexagonal tiles of the ground (which click-clack quietly as they shift slightly with the slow creep of time) to the towering factories that loom over the pools. These structures are a mix of Brutalist concrete and corroded steel, their windows dark, their chimneys cold. Yet machinery thrums in the distance—gears the size of houses turning at glacial speeds, their movements a reminder of the place’s industrial past.  

**The Atmosphere**  
A perpetual brightness glares here, not from sunlight alone but from phosphorescent lichen that clings to the spires, casting an eerie, clinical glow over everything. The sky is a washed-out blue, but it occasionally fractures—ripples of distorted light flicker across it like heat haze, suggesting reality is unreliable. Gravity, too, wavers by imperceptible degrees; objects sometimes drift slightly upward before settling, as if the plateau itself is breathing. The silence is profound, broken only by the faint *plink* of water droplets and the distant, rhythmic *thud* of a hydraulic press restarting somewhere in the earth.  

**Ecology & Fauna**  
The fauna is abundant yet uncanny. Bioluminescent *crawler-moths* swarm around the pools, their wings leaving trails of light like digital smears. They feed on the reeds, which secrete a nectar that glows amber. More unsettling are the *hollowbacks*, deer-sized creatures with translucent skin and bones visible through their chests. They move in slow, deliberate patterns, as if following invisible currents, and their presence is accompanied by a low-frequency vibration that rattles the teeth. Dead hollowbacks are found sometimes, their bodies filled with coiled wires instead of organs. Birds, too, populate the spires—*clankwrens* with metallic feathers that clang like wind chimes, their calls mimicking fragments of human speech.  

**Rules of the World**  
Time behaves oddly here. Clocks run backward in the eastern spires but forward to the west, and some visitors report experiencing the same day three times. Memories also warp; conversations from the previous day feel like dreams, and certain areas are *remembered* rather than seen. The yttrium stone is said to store the thoughts of those who die near it, replaying them as whispers to the living. There is no true "natural" substrate—the soil is a mix of crushed stone, ash, and ground bone, compacted by centuries of footfalls.  

**Dynamic Processes**  
The plateau is in slow flux. The spires grow imperceptibly taller each year, their crystals absorbing ambient energy. The pools multiply as fissures widen, and the canals occasionally reverse direction without warning, flooding districts built along their banks. The geothermal vents beneath the plateau pulse every few decades, causing the ground to tremble and the hollowbacks to migrate. Meanwhile, the cities of the plateau—if they can be called that—are abandoned, their architecture functional but lifeless, inhabited only by the crawler-moths and the echoes of former inhabitants.  

**Sound**  
The predominant sound is absence: a thick, expectant quiet that amplifies small noises. The *drip-drip* of water, the skitter of claws on metal, the groan of settling stone. Occasionally, a machine will activate in the distance—a bellow of steam from an ancient refinery, followed by a long, low whistle as vapor vents into the sky. These sounds feel intrusive, almost violent, in the stillness.  

**Density & Scale**  
Despite its openness, the plateau feels claustrophobic due to the density of structures and phenomena. The spires are close enough that their shadows intersect, creating shifting mazes of light and dark. Everywhere are the remnants of the built environment: rusted conveyor belts frozen mid-stride, staircases leading to nowhere, and walls embedded with pulsating veins of yttrium that seem almost alive. The scale is vast yet intimate; one can walk for hours and still feel the presence of unseen forces, as though the plateau itself is a single, slumbering organism.  

In Yttrium, the boundary between the constructed and the organic has collapsed, leaving a realm where industry, nature, and reality’s frayed edges intertwine.
