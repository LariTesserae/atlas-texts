**The Hollow of Echoing Stone**  

The landscape exists within a shallow, bowl-shaped depression, its edges worn smooth by centuries of relentless seepage and weathering. Here, the ground is a patchwork of damp stone and compacted earth, where water seeps perpetually from fissures in the rock, creating a network of shallow rivulets and glassy pools. The air hangs heavy with mist, its chill clinging to the skin like damp cloth. The temperature hovers just above freezing, numbing breath into visible puffs that drift and dissolve in the dim, diffused light filtering through a sky perpetually veiled in slate-gray clouds.  

The terrain is entirely artificial—a maze of low, interconnected structures hewn from a pale, porous limestone that weeps moisture constantly. These buildings, no taller than a person’s shoulder, are arranged in a grid of narrow, winding paths. Their walls are pocked and softened by erosion, their surfaces streaked with algae and mineral deposits that have built up over long, slow centuries. The construction is primitive but deliberate: stones fitted without mortar, their joints packed with clay and organic fibers now blackened and decayed. Some structures have collapsed into mounds of rubble, their remnants incorporated into the landscape as if the place itself is slowly reclaiming its own bones.  

The sparse vegetation consists of hardy, stunted growths—lichen-splotched shrubs, tufts of grass that cling to cracks, and the occasional twisted tree whose roots have split the limestone. These plants struggle against the constant moisture, their forms gnarled and asymmetrical, as though shaped by the same hands that built the stone structures.  

Sound fills the hollow like a living thing. The dominant noise is the *drip-drip-drip* of water from overhead ledges, punctuated by the harsh calls of unseen birds and the skittering of small, amphibious creatures in the pools. There is a constant, low-frequency hum—like distant machinery or the resonant thrumming of the stone itself—emanating from the ground, perhaps produced by underground aquifers or the strange acoustics of the hollow. Every now and then, a sudden crash echoes as a section of rockface gives way, sending a cascade of water and debris into a nearby channel.  

The fauna is abundant but alien. Bioluminescent insects flit between the structures, their glow faint but persistent, casting an eerie blue-green light. Amphibious mammals the size of cats, with translucent skin and webbed feet, slip into the pools and vanish beneath the surface. Birds with metallic feathers wheel overhead, their calls sharp and discordant. The most striking are the *stoneback* creatures—large, armored beings that resemble a cross between a tortoise and a weasel, moving in slow, deliberate herds along the paths. They feed on the algae and lichen, their digging often triggering minor rockfalls that contribute to the ever-shifting topography.  

The erosion here is ancient and deliberate, as though the land itself has learned to sculpt. Hollows in the limestone have been expanded by centuries of dripping water, creating alcoves and caves that dot the landscape like missing teeth. The built environment is in a state of dynamic equilibrium: structures are perpetually collapsing, while new ones—smaller, cruder—are added by unknown hands or natural forces. In some places, the stones seem to have been arranged in patterns that suggest purpose: spirals, grids, or symbols worn illegible by time.  

The weirdness manifests in subtle ways. Shadows seem to linger slightly out of sync with their casters. Reflections in the pools do not always match their surroundings—showing glimpses of the hollow in different seasons or configurations. At night, the hum deepens, and the mist takes on a faint, iridescent sheen, as though the air itself is alive and listening.  

In this small, hollowed realm, the intersection of the constructed and the natural has created something neither fully human nor fully wild. It is a place where time moves differently, where stone remembers and water dreams, and where the boundaries between the made and the grown have dissolved into a strange, beautiful equilibrium.
