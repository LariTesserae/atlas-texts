**The Inhators of the Sunless Cathedral**  

The denizens of this drowned realm are denizens of *becoming*, their forms and behaviors shaped not merely by survival, but by the geometry of the place itself. They are as much a part of the architecture as the stone, their bodies and biochemistries tuned to the slow pulse of the geothermal vents and the whisper-hum of the ancient glass. They are few in number but vast in variety, each species a verse in an evolving, underwater symphony:  

---

### **The Luminous Choir**  
The jellyfish-like drifters, *Luminophores*, are the most visible inhabitants. Their domed, translucent bodies pulse with bioluminescence not randomly, but in synchronized waves, as if sharing fleeting thoughts. Their tendrils, lined with light-sensitive cells, brush against the walls and each other, creating a distributed nervous system that spans the entire cathedral. They filter the metallic-rich water through cilia that shimmer like liquid metal, extracting trace elements and excreting the excess in crystalline droplets that feed the glassy growths. Their collective movement carves paths through the corridors, guiding currents and stirring sediments. When they aggregate in vast schools, their glow intensifies, bathing entire chambers in spectral blues and greens—a transient aurora that signals mating or migration, though those concepts are alien to their timeless existence.  

---

### **The Mason-Skimmers**  
*The Calcigrades*, the shelled, segmented scavengers, are the architects of the cathedral’s ongoing transformation. Their carapaces are lustrous, layered composites of chitin and mineral deposits they’ve scraped from the walls. They belong to a lineage of creatures that evolved from burrowers into sculptors, their claws and mandibles precisely calibrated to chip away at the glassy structures. They build nests in the hollows of arches, lining them with the filings of their work, which harden into a cement-like substance. Some species specialize in certain tasks: *Carvers* smooth surfaces, while *Etchers* mark glyphs into the stone—patterns that mimic the ancient symbols etched by the lost builders, a form of cultural mimicry. Their society is organized into swarms, each a node in a vast, decentralized intelligence that reshapes the cathedral’s layout slowly, incrementally.  

---

### **The Tunnel Wyrms**  
Beneath the main halls, *Serpentia profundis*—eel-like predators—slither through the submerged tunnels, their bodies narrow enough to navigate the narrowest fissures. Their scales are a mosaic of black and iridescent blue, allowing them to blend into the glassy walls. They hunt in packs, communicating through low-frequency vibrations that resonate through the stone. Their jaws unhinge to reveal not teeth but a ring of bioluminescent organs that stun prey with flashes of light before they strike. Their larvae are paralarvae, translucent and wingless, that drift in the open water until they find a tunnel to claim as their own. They are the guardians of the deep passages, their presence a deterrent to any intruder.  

---

### **The Corallites of the Glass Coast**  
Growing from cracks and crevices like living sculptures, the *Corallites* are sessile beings that resemble anemones but are fundamentally different. Their tentacles are rigid, crystalline filaments that refract light into prismatic patterns, luring plankton and small crustaceans into their central mouths. They secrete a calcium-rich mucus that combines with minerals in the water to form the glassy growths coating the ruins. Some Corallites have evolved mutualistic relationships with the Calcigrades, offering shelter in exchange for the minerals the skimmers dislodge. Their colonies form vast gardens of light, their tentacles swaying in the current like the fingers of a submerged dancer.  

---

### **The Architect Fish**  
In the largest chambers, where the water is stirred by geothermal currents, dwell the *Barnacle-Maws* (*Corypheus crustatus*). These massive, slow-moving fish are encrusted with barnacles and anemones that have colonized their bodies over centuries. Their gaping mouths are lined with mandibles like blacksmith’s tongs, capable of crushing the armored shells of Calcigrades. They feed on the masons’ shed exoskeletons, extracting nutrients from the mineral layers. Despite their fearsome appearance, they are placid, drifting like living islands through the cathedral’s halls. Their role is unclear, but some theorize they are the remnants of a symbiotic relationship with the builders, now left to their own devices.  

---

### **The Glyphkeepers**  
Hidden in the deepest, oldest chambers are the *Glyphkeepers*, beings so alien they defy easy description. They resemble clusters of amphipods fused into communal organisms, their exoskeletons etched with bioluminescent patterns that mirror the ancient symbols on the walls. They do not move but instead *transmute* the glyphs through rhythmic contractions, their bodies emitting a faint electrical charge that activates the stone’s residual energy. They are the de facto librarians of the cathedral, preserving the knowledge of the lost builders in their very biology. They communicate through a language of pulses and refracted light, their existence a bridge between the organic and the artificial.  

---

### **The Vent Sovereigns**  
Near the geothermal sources, where the water bubbles and boils, dwell the *Pyrocorals*. These sessile beings resemble brain corals but glow with an inner heat, their tissue rich in chemosynthetic bacteria that convert the mineral-rich water into energy. They anchor themselves to vents, their fronds dangling into the hot currents, and exude a substance that accelerates the growth of the glassy formations. They are the most reclusive of the cathedral’s inhabitants, their intense bioluminescence visible only in the darkest depths. Some believe they are the descendants of the builders’ terraforming efforts, their DNA spliced with the stone itself to create eternal stewards of the environment.  

---

### **The Echo Whales**  
The largest creatures are the *Echo Whales* (*Cetotherium sonorus*), leviathans that navigate the cathedral’s vast chambers by echolocation. Their songs resonate through the stone, a low-frequency chorus that shapes the very bedrock. They are filter feeders, sifting Corallites and Luminophores from the water, but their true role is more enigmatic: their songs seem to stabilize the ancient structures, their vibrations preventing collapses that might otherwise occur over millennia. They are revered by the Calcigrades, who follow their migrations like pilgrims.  

---

### **The Unseen**  
Beneath all this thrums a deeper mystery. In the places where the glyphs cluster thickest, some believe there are *Shadow-forms*—beings that exist only in the periphery of vision, glimpsed in flashes of light or the corners of one’s metaphorical eye. They might be the last remnants of the builders, their consciousness fused with the architecture, or something else entirely. They leave no physical traces, only an unshakable sense of being watched.  

---

This is a world where life and architecture are indistinguishable, where the creatures themselves are monuments to time and adaptation. They are not merely inhabitants but collaborators in the slow, eternal act of transformation, shaping a cathedral that is as much alive as it is stone.
