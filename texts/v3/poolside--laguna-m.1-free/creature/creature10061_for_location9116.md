The inhabitants of Aethermoor are, above all, a people suspended in perpetual becoming. They are human—or perhaps something akin to humanity, their forms and features altered by the valley’s strange equilibrium. Their bodies are not subject to the erosion of time: skin smooth and unlined, hair that never thins or grays, eyes that hold a depthless, glassy sheen. They move with a careful deliberation, as though fearful of disrupting the fragile stasis that binds their world. Yet this caution is not born of malice but of a profound, unspoken understanding: motion is both inevitable and meaningless here.  

Their settlements, carved into the stone itself, reflect this duality. The dwellings are low and windowless, their interiors lit by the faint glow of phosphorescent fungi cultivated in cracks along the walls. The inhabitants have learned to navigate by feel and memory, their footsteps tracing the same worn paths day after unchanging day. Tools are their most prized possessions—not for their utility, but for the familiarity of their weight and shape. Hammers strike anvils with a rhythmic precision that borders on ritual, the sounds absorbed by the mist but felt in the chest, a reminder of their presence in the world.  

The people of Aethermoor speak rarely, their voices hoarse from disuse. When they do, it is in low, measured tones that seem to avoid the still air, as though sound itself might fracture the equilibrium. Their language is stripped of metaphor, relying instead on gestures and symbols hammered into metal tablets. These tablets, stored in granaries that never fill or empty, are the closest thing to art they possess: repetitive geometric patterns that mirror the growth of lichen on the rocks.  

Their relationship with the land’s fauna is one of eerie symbiosis. The crystalline birds perched on cliff faces are not kept as pets but are treated with a quiet reverence, their frozen songs considered a form of prayer. The six-legged mammals, which graze in fixed positions, are tended by the people, who leave bundles of mist-dampened moss at their feet. The obsidian-furred predators are avoided, yet their presence is acknowledged with small offerings placed at the edge of settlements—a gesture less of fear than of respect for the thin line between stasis and chaos.  

The mist is both a lifeline and a veil. It seeps into homes, pooling in corners where dew collects and feeds the underground roots of the verdant gardens. The people have learned to read its patterns, believing it carries messages from the valley’s deeper places. At times, shapes form in the mist—a fleeting silhouette, a hand, a face—but these are met with silence, as if the act of speaking might banish them. Some claim to see their own reflections in the Veilstream’s waters, younger selves trapped in the moment before the valley’s stasis took hold. Whether these visions are delusions or glimpses of a trapped past is a matter of quiet debate.  

Perhaps most unsettling are the inhabitants themselves. They do not sleep, nor do they tire. Their days are spent in cycles of labor that have long since lost meaning, their hands moving through the motions of crafting, tending, and maintaining with a devotion that borders on religious. Children are born, but they never age, their laughter and tears preserved in the same unchanging state as everything else. Death does not exist here, and so neither does mourning. Instead, there is a profound, melancholic patience, as though the people of Aethermoor have accepted their role as custodians of a world that exists outside the flow of time.  

Yet beneath this stillness lies a flicker of something else. In the deepest parts of the valley, where the mist grows thick and the stone hums with an unearthly resonance, some claim to hear whispers. These are not voices but the sound of the earth itself, a slow, rhythmic pulse that suggests the valley is not as static as it appears. Whether this is a sign of change or merely the result of their own minds straining against the unchanging is unknown. What is certain is that the people of Aethermoor, for all their stillness, are not entirely passive. They are witnesses, keepers of a secret that the world outside might never understand.  

In the end, Aethermoor’s inhabitants are both prisoners and priests of their own existence, living in a liminal space where the mundane and the mythical blur into one. They are the stillness, and the stillness is them.
