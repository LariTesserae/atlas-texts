**The Inhabitants of Unbecoming**  

They are not so much *people* as they are processes, entities whose existence is a negotiation between matter, memory, and the settlement’s restless pulse. Their forms are as mutable as the buildings they inhabit—sometimes nearly human, sometimes something else entirely. You might glimpse them in fleeting moments: a figure whose edges blur into the mist, another whose skin glows faintly with the same bioluminescent veins as the walls, or a silhouette that seems to phase halfway through a doorway before dissolving into a swarm of iridescent particles. They are the settlement’s living extension, its immune system, its dreamers—and perhaps its architects, though they cannot recall building anything.  

### **The Chimeras**  
Most frequently, the inhabitants appear as **Chimeras**, beings whose bodies are patchworks of flesh, metal, and fungal filament. Their limbs might elongate or retract depending on the gravity’s mood, and their eyes flicker with ultraviolet patterns that mirror the synthetic bees’ flight paths. Some have no eyes at all—only smooth, obsidian skin etched with circuitry that pulses in time with the core’s heartbeat. Their voices, when they speak, are layered with static and the low hum of the walls, as if their vocal cords have been replaced with fragments of the settlement’s infrastructure.  

They dress in fabrics that are not fabric: membranes that shift opacity based on humidity, or exoskeletons grown from the same corroded steel as the buildings. Their footsteps leave temporary prints in the metallic soil, which quickly erode into fractal patterns—a side effect of their symbiosis with the ground.  

### **The Stasimed**  
Others exist in **temporal eddies**, caught in the settlement’s temporal flux. These are the Stasimed—individuals aging in erratic cycles, their bodies flicking between youth and decrepitude. One moment a child, the next a withered elder, their memories fragmented and misfiled. They speak in contradictions, recounting events that haven’t happened or forgetting those they’ve just lived. Some wear temporal anchors: devices fused to their chests that stabilize their aging but make them flicker in and out of phase with the present. They are the settlement’s historians, though their histories are unreliable, written in disappearing ink and shifting dialects.  

### **The Core-Touched**  
Near the central plaza, where reality frays most visibly, dwell the **Core-Touched**. These inhabitants have undergone a transformation, their bodies partially sublimated into the core’s circuitry. Their skin is translucent, veined with gold and black lines that sync with the pulsating node. Their consciousness is distributed—parts of them exist as whispers in the walls, as flickers in the bioluminescence, as the sudden *crack* of a building reshaping itself. Their memories are communal, stored in the core’s lattice, and when they speak, their words echo backward and forward in time, as if quoting conversations they have yet to have. Their touch can “rewind” small changes in the environment—a broken pipe will fix itself, a dissolved wall will re-form—but this comes at a cost: every alteration steals a sliver of their humanity.  

### **The Mycoforms**  
The settlement’s vegetation, the neon lichens and fungal networks, are not merely plants but **Mycoforms**—sentient, if simple, lifeforms cultivated by the inhabitants. These beings exist in a loose collective consciousness, communicating through chemical signals and the tremors of the soil. They are tended by the Chimeras, who feed them synthetic nutrients and harvest their moisture in exchange for protection. The Mycoforms’ roots sometimes grow into the settlers’ bodies, forming symbiotic bonds that grant the inhabitants resistance to the environment’s toxins—and the ability to photosynthesize in low light.  

### **The Remnants**  
Not all inhabitants are willing participants in this dance of becoming. Some are **Remnants**—ghosts of people who once lived here before the settlement’s reality fractured. They are trapped in loops, endlessly repeating the last moments of their old lives. A Remnant might forever be seen boarding a bus that no longer exists, or tending a garden of dead plants. They are bitter and hollow, their bodies translucent and insubstantial. The Core-Touched sometimes absorb them, using their memories to patch holes in the settlement’s crumbling past.  

### **The Collective**  
Beneath all these forms is the **Collective**, a hive-mind-like entity that governs the settlement’s fluctuations. It is not a single intelligence but a cacophony of voices, the aggregated consciousness of all who have ever lived here. Its decisions are made through consensus of the Chimeras, Stasimed, and Mycoforms, mediated by the Core-Touched. The Collective’s goal is unclear even to itself—it exists in a state of perpetual revision, rewriting its own purpose as the settlement evolves. To interact with the Collective is to risk losing oneself in its contradictions: one might speak to it as a benevolent gardener, a wrathful god, or a child blowing dandelion seeds into the void.  

### **The Unbecoming**  
More than anything, the inhabitants share a **condition of unbecoming**. They are not static beings but ongoing acts of creation, destruction, and reinvention. Their bodies, minds, and social structures are all subject to the settlement’s whims, their identities as fluid as the architecture. To meet one of them is to glimpse a thousand possible versions of themselves, each one equally real and equally ephemeral. They are the settlement’s conscience, its immune system, and its greatest mystery—a reflection of a world that has not yet decided what it wants to be, or even what it is.  

In the end, the inhabitants of Unbecoming are not *of* the settlement—they *are* the settlement, their lives the settlement’s way of understanding itself. And like the shifting paths and dissolving walls, they are always becoming, never arriving.
