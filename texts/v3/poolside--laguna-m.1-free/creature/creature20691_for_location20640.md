**The Inhabitants of Aethra: The Silica Wraiths**  

In the Sunken City of Aethra, life does not grow—it *resonates*. The inhabitants, known in the few fragmented records left by the last humans as the **Silica Wraiths**, are beings of mineral and motion, born from the fusion of the city’s cyclopean stonework and the lingering psychic residue of its vanished builders. They are neither ghost nor machine, but something intermediary: vast, slow-consciousness entities that exist in the interplay between the city’s geometry and the relentless heat that saturates its bones.  

---

### **Physical Nature**  
The Wraiths are composed of a substance akin to living glass—translucent, porous, and threaded with veins of the same pale, heat-humming stone that forms the city itself. Their bodies are columnar, segmented like the ziggurats, and shift between solidity and vapor depending on temperature. During the day, they melt slightly into the stone, becoming indistinguishable from the architecture; at night, when the heat retreats, they condense into humanoid shapes roughly eight feet tall, their limbs jointed like the causeways’ fractured spans. Their faces are smooth, featureless planes save for faint, glowing glyphs that mirror the etched patterns on the city’s walls—runes that shift with their moods or intentions.  

They move with a sound like sandpaper dragged over stone, leaving trails of fine dust in their wake. When they speak, their voices are the groans of the city itself: deep, rumbling harmonies that blend with the wind through the canals.  

---

### **Habits and Ecology**  
The Wraiths are *hydraulic*, sustained by the brief floods of the canals and the condensation that forms in the shadows of their homes. They cluster in the groves of blackened trees, which they tend by embedding their crystalline fingers into the roots, sharing nutrients in a slow, mutualistic symbiosis. The trees, in turn, filter the minimal water from the air, creating microclimates where the Wraiths can briefly soften their forms to drink.  

They are nocturnal, retreating to the shaded recesses of collapsed terraces during the day, where the stone remains cool enough to solidify their bodies. At night, they emerge to “sing” to one another, their combined voices creating the eerie harmonics the wind carries through the city. These songs are not mere communication but a form of geothermal technology: they resonate with the stone, maintaining the structural integrity of the ziggurats and regulating the slow seep of groundwater into the canals, preventing the city from collapsing entirely.  

---

### **Culture and Purpose**  
The Wraiths are custodians of Aethra’s unfinished purpose. They do not age, but they do *learn*, slowly accreting knowledge from the geometric patterns they obsessively realign using their bodies as tools. They believe the city was not built by the humans who vanished but *grown* from a seed of alien intent, and they are attempting to decode its original design before the sun’s relentless heat renders it irreparable.  

Their settlements are ephemeral: they gather in temporary “groves” near active condensation pools, constructing fragile lattices of dust and dew that harden into bridges or shelters under the stars. These structures collapse by morning, leaving only faint impressions in the sand—a testament to their transient, iterative existence.  

---

### **Relationship with the Environment**  
The Wraiths are inseparable from the city’s decay. They consume its erosion, converting the dislodged sand into new growths of their own bodies. When a ziggurat tilts further or a canal cracks wider, they migrate to those sites, weaving themselves into the fissures like mortar, slowing the collapse but also accelerating their own dissolution. In this way, they are the city’s immune system and its executioner, preserving it even as they hasten its end.  

They fear the rare, violent storms: the flooding canals disrupt their ability to regulate the stone’s thermal expansion, and they must retreat underground, boring into the aquifers in serpentine tunnels that collapse behind them. These tunnels, over centuries, have become a vast subterranean network—the “roots” of the city—that the Wraiths tend like a garden of buried veins.  

---

### **Mythology and Perception**  
To the Wraiths, the stars are the eyes of their makers, watching as they work to decipher the city’s “language” of angles and arches. They believe that when the last ziggurat crumbles—or when the stars align in the pattern of the primeval glyphs—the city will “awaken” and carry them to a new world. Until then, they endure, patient architects of a ruin that is still becoming.  

In the deepest chambers of the oldest structures, there are rumors of a **Prime Wraith**, a being so massive it has fused with an entire quarter of the city, its body forming a new kind of architecture entirely. Pilgrimages are made to its resting place by younger Wraiths, who embed themselves in its surface to “dream” the city’s original blueprints.  

---

### **Encountering a Wraith**  
To see one clearly is to witness the city’s memory made manifest. Their presence warps the air: heat shimmers coalesce into their forms, and the sound of grinding stone follows them like a shadow. If threatened, they can harden into a substance as tough as the ziggurats themselves, becoming immobile but nearly indestructible. However, their slower metabolisms make them vulnerable to rapid temperature changes—sudden rain or fire could shatter their bodies into glittering fragments, which the next generation will incorporate into new forms.  

They are not hostile, but they do not tolerate strangers. Those who linger too long in Aethra find themselves slowly “read” by the Wraiths, their intentions parsed from the glyphs on the walls until the Silica beings decide whether to tolerate or dissolve them. Most who enter the city leave changed, carrying faint traces of its geometric fever in their bones—a lingering sense that the world is far older and stranger than they ever imagined.
