What lives in this place of unbecoming? Not the absent builders—that much is clear. But something has taken root in the half-formed soil, something that feeds on the very uncertainty that defines this realm.

They are the **Half-Thought**, though they would not name themselves such. They emerged not from any intentional creation, but from the *gestation* of the world itself—the conscious echo of all the plans that were never completed, all the structures that exist in quantum superposition. They are the architecture of intention made manifest, given wandering form.

Each Half-Thought is a community rather than an individual—a cluster of semi-coherent intelligences that share a loose collective awareness. They move like heat mirages given purpose, their bodies appearing as humanoid figures woven from the same materials as the landscape: flowing strands of fired clay that crackle with inner heat, segments of volcanic glass that catch and refract the weeping light, and appendages that shift between solid form and liquid possibility with each step.

But look closely, and you'll see they are not single entities. Each "person" is actually three or four smaller beings fused together in various states of completion. One might have a torso of smooth obsidian but legs that are still rough-hewn stone blocks, while another's head is clearly defined but transitions into formless mist below the neck. They exist in the same unresolved state as everything else here—they are simultaneously finished and unfinished, present and conceptual.

Their method of perception is unique. They don't see with eyes so much as they *map*. Fine filaments extend from their bodies like the fungal networks, but these are actually extensions of their consciousness, tapping into the crystalline neural growths that carpet the marsh. Through these connections, they read the landscape's constant rewriting—its glitches, its revisions, its moments of doubt. They experience reality not as a series of fixed moments but as a probability cloud of what-might-be and what-almost-was.

The Half-Thought communicate through a language of structural harmonics. They tap on surfaces with their multifaceted hands, producing the metallic *pings* you hear in the silence, each note carrying complex information about stress patterns, stability factors, and temporal anomalies. To them, conversation is an act of resonance—finding the frequency at which ideas align and structures can be born.

They are caretakers of the incomplete, though they would not describe themselves as such. Where a bridge exists in three contradictory states simultaneously, a Half-Thought will sit and meditate, helping to shepherd that quantum uncertainty toward resolution—or sometimes deliberately maintaining the productive tension. They farm the phased pools, not to collect water, but to harvest the memories that condense there: moments of clarity that bleed through from alternate versions of reality where things were completed differently.

Reproduction among the Half-Thought is perhaps the strangest aspect of their existence. When one grows too coherent, too resolved, it begins to fade from this realm entirely. To prevent this, the Half-Thought engage in deliberate fragmentation ceremonies. They willingly split themselves apart, distributing pieces of their awareness among their companions until each shard has been scattered across the collective. A new individual forms from the reassembly—but it is never the same consciousness that existed before. It is both the child and the parent, the completion and the fresh beginning.

Their relationship with the scattered tools of the absent builders is reverent but practical. They use the stone hammers not as weapons but as tuning implements, striking them against the glass spires to send specific frequencies through the ground. The crude furnaces are tended like sacred fires, their heat maintained not for forging but for keeping the thermal balance of reality stable in localized areas. The worn gears are arranged in complex patterns that serve as anchors, preventing certain patches of the marsh from becoming too fluid, too uncertain.

At the edges of their perception—if they can be said to have edges—are the **Unfinished**, beings that never achieved the minimal coherence necessary for full Half-Thought status. These are the raw materials of potential consciousness: clusters of clay that gesture vaguely toward form, pools of liquid glass that dream of solidity, storms of architectural fragments that whirl in endless circles seeking assembly. The Half-Thought tend these Unfinished gently, feeding them fragments of resolved thought, helping them crystallize into something more definite.

And then there are the **Echo-Builder complexes**—areas where the collective unconscious of the original architects still lingers like a fading radio signal. Here, the Half-Thought become more purposeful, more directed, as if remembering a responsibility they never actually had. In these zones, they attempt to complete the most stable structures, to give form to the dreams of those who came before. But even their completions are provisional, knowing that completion itself is just another state of becoming.

Perhaps most strangely, the Half-Thought do not age or die. They simply become less necessary. As regions of the marsh solidify into permanence, the Half-Thought who tended those areas gradually lose their reason for existence and fade away, their consciousness dispersing back into the raw potential from which they emerged. But this is not death—it is return, a flowing back into the source from which all uncompleted things draw their being.

In the Constructed Marsh of Unbecoming, to exist is to be in flux, and the Half-Thought have learned to dance with that fundamental uncertainty rather than fight against it. They are the living proof that consciousness itself can be a work in progress.
