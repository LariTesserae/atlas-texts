The Sundered Plateau of Kael-Vorth is not merely a place inhabited by the Dissolved; it is a living, breathing entity that consumes and transforms those who reside within its fractured realm. The inhabitants are as much a product of the plateau’s eldritch processes as they are its survivors, their existence a testament to the malleability of life under the weight of impossible geometries and the erosion of time itself.

### **The Dissolved: Children of the Crystalline Dream**

The Dissolved are the last vestiges of a civilization that once carved itself into the plateau’s living rock. Their bodies are neither wholly flesh nor stone, but a fusion of both—limbs that taper into jagged mineral growths, skin that shimmers with the same prismatic mists that fray the plateau’s edges. Their bones have been supplanted by veins of crystallized ore, pulsing with the slow, alien rhythm of the land itself. They move with a gliding grace, their footsteps muted as they navigate the hollowed spires and inverted towers that serve as their homes. 

Their communication is a chorus of vibrations, transmitted through the rock like the tolling of subterranean bells. Each tone is a word, each resonance a sentence, layered into a language that shifts with the plateau’s moods. They speak of the plateau as the **Kereth-Vorth**, the "Dreaming Bone," a deity that sustains them even as it dissolves them into its matrix. To be Dissolved is to be in harmony with this dissolution—to embrace the erosion of one’s former self as a form of transcendence.

#### **The Hollow Chorus**
The Dissolved gather in the **Spire of Unmaking**, a cathedral-like structure where their combined vibrations harmonize with the plateau’s own shifting frequencies. Here, they perform the **Rite of Resonance**, a communal ritual where their bodies glow faintly with bioluminescent lichen, and their voices echo through the stone in a cacophony of harmonics. These gatherings are both a celebration of their existence and a desperate plea to the plateau for stability, as their songs help anchor the land’s reality to a semblance of coherence. Those who lose the ability to resonate—due to injury or the creeping entropy of prolonged exposure—are cast into the salt flats, their bodies left to phase entirely into the lithofungal spores.

#### **Symbiosis with the Lithofungi**
The Dissolved maintain a symbiotic relationship with the lithofungi, the glass-spore organisms that carpet the plateau. They cultivate gardens of these crystalline fungi in the terraces, tending to them with careful vibrations that coax the growth of specific formations. The fungi’s spores are harvested for their ability to crystallize memories into solid form, which the Dissolved embed into their settlements as records of their history. However, the spores are volatile—contact with them can cause hallucinations, as they act as gateways to fragmented echoes of the plateau’s past. Some Dissolved deliberately inhale spores to commune with these echoes, seeking wisdom from the long-dead civilizations that once claimed the plateau.

### **The Phantoms: Ghosts of the Threshold**

The plateau’s fauna, the phantom-like beings that flicker at the edge of perception, are rarely seen but deeply feared. The Dissolved call them **Vorth’kai**, or "Not-There Ones," and believe them to be the remnants of those who were claimed by the plateau before the Dissolved learned to negotiate with its will. The Vorth’kai leave behind spirals and sigils in the salt, which the Dissolved interpret as warnings or omens. Some of these phantoms are said to be the original inhabitants, trapped in loops of their own deaths, forever reenacting their final moments. Others whisper that they are future versions of the Dissolved, glimpsed through the echo storms—a fate to be avoided at all costs.

### **The Echo Storms: Fractured Reflections**
The Dissolved revere the echo storms as manifestations of the plateau’s dreaming. During these events, time dilates, and the landscape overlaps with its past and future selves. The Dissolved use these moments to scavenge relics from alternate versions of their settlements, retrieving tools, books, or even individuals who have since been erased from their timeline. However, prolonged exposure to an echo storm can fracture a being’s sense of self, causing them to act out scenes from alternate lives. The Dissolved have developed techniques to "anchor" themselves during these storms, using bundles of lithofungal spores tied to their wrists—a practice that allows them to survive but leaves them permanently marred by crystalline growths.

### **The Canyon of Voices: The Descent**
The phosphorescent mist of the canyon is a place of pilgrimage for the Dissolved, though none dare descend. They believe the voices rising from its depths are the **Kereth’vora**, the plateau’s forgotten subconscious. These entities are said to be the first Dissolved, those who chose to dissolve entirely into the plateau’s core. Their songs are mournful and dissonant, a reminder of what awaits those who surrender to the dream. Some among the Dissolved, particularly the elderly, seek to join them, disappearing into the mist with deliberate steps. Their absence is not mourned; instead, it is seen as a return to the source.

### **Memory and Mutation**
The plateau’s influence erodes the minds of its inhabitants, gradually overwriting their memories with fragments from its own ancient history. The Dissolved combat this through the use of **memory crystals**, which they embed in their chests to preserve their personal histories. However, even these are not immune to the plateau’s whispers. Over time, many Dissolved begin to recall lives they never lived—memories of lush forests, forgotten wars, or cities that once bridged the sky. These false memories are called **Kereth’lun**, "Bone Lies," and are both a curse and a gift, offering a respite from the plateau’s desolation but blurring the line between self and the land’s collective dream.

### **The Unseen Threat**
Beneath the Dissolved’s acceptance of their fate lies a deep, unspoken fear: that the plateau is not merely dreaming, but awake, and hungry. Some whisper that the Dissolved are not its children but its parasites, their crystalline bodies slowly leaching the plateau’s essence to sustain themselves. Others believe that the plateau is ascending to some higher plane of existence, and that the Dissolved will be consumed in its transcendence. This fear drives the most radical among them to seek escape, though no one has ever successfully left the plateau’s grasp. The horizon curves upward, paths loop endlessly, and even the concept of "outside" becomes uncertain in a place where reality itself is fraying.

In the Sundered Plateau, to live is to exist in a state of perpetual becoming—to shed one’s old skin like a discarded spore and allow the dream to remake you into something new, something eternal. The Dissolved are not merely inhabitants; they are the plateau’s heartbeat, its breath, its slow, inexorable unraveling.
