In the Weeping Warrens of Keth-Mor, the inhabitants are as much a product of the land’s fevered contradictions as its towering forges and weeping stones. They are **the Keth’vanni**, a people shaped by the interplay of water, rust, and the relentless hum of machinery. Their existence is a testament to adaptation, both physical and cultural, in a place where the very air seems to pulse with latent energy.

---

### **Physical Traits**  
The Keth’vanni are a hardy folk, their bodies marked by the plateau’s harsh embrace. Their skin bears a muted, ashen tone, as if dusted with the red ochre of the Mourns’ mineral-rich waters. Many have developed **thickened epidermis** on their hands and feet, an evolutionary response to decades of navigating the slick, condensation-crusted surfaces of their world. Their eyes are wide and pale, adapted to the dim, green-tinged glow of the bioluminescent pools that dot the landscape. Some elders among them are nearly blind, their vision dulled by years of squinting through the mist—but their other senses compensate. They navigate by the vibrations of the forges, the rhythm of the waterwheels, and the subtle shifts in the air’s moisture content.  

A peculiar trait among the Keth’vanni is their **resonance sensitivity**: they can feel the low-frequency hum of the weeping rocks in their bones, a trait that sets their machinery and architecture apart. Builders often speak of "listening to the stone" when designing structures, aligning aqueducts and waterwheels to harmonize with the plateau’s natural vibrations. This sensitivity has also made them impervious to the Deafening Hour’s cacophony; they communicate through **hand gestures, rhythmic footfalls, and flickering light signals** using shards of bioluminescent glass.  

---

### **Culture and Society**  
The Keth’vanni live in a society governed by **tides of water and steam**, their livelihoods dictated by the ebb and flow of the Mourns. They are divided into three castes:  
- **The Veythari** ("Stone-Singers"): Engineers and architects who interpret the plateau’s hum, shaping the forges and channels. They are the spiritual leaders, conducting rituals to "soothe" the rocks and ensure their weeping continues.  
- **The Mourn’keth** ("Water-Keepers"): Maintenance workers who tend the aqueducts, aquifers, and bioluminescent pools. They are the most superstitious, believing the Mourns to be veins of an ancient god buried beneath the plateau.  
- **The Sarnul** ("Forge-Borns"): Laborers who stoke the fires and tend the pistons. They are pragmatic and resilient, their bodies calloused from the heat, and their lungs perpetually lined with soot.  

Their settlements are **sparse and modular**, built high on terraces to avoid the Mourns’ flood season. Homes are constructed from rusted metal sheets and salvaged stone, their roofs sloped to channel condensation into cisterns. Windows are rare; instead, walls are embedded with fragments of bioluminescent algae, cultivated to provide light.  

---

### **Fauna and Symbiosis**  
The Keth’vanni share their world with creatures as peculiar as their own nature:  
- **Glimmer Moths**: Insects that feed on the algae of the pools, their wings refracting the bioluminescent light into prismatic patterns. The Keth’vanni use them in rituals, releasing swarms to "paint" messages in the air.  
- **Stoneback Tortoises**: Massive, slow-moving reptiles that burrow into the plateau’s cracks, their shells etched with the red mineral deposits of the rocks. They are revered as symbols of patience and endurance.  
- **Whisper Eels**: Blind, serpentine fish that inhabit the deeper Mourns, their bodies slick with algae. The Mourn’keth harvest their secretions, which are used to lubricate machinery and etch protective runes onto stone.  

---

### **Conflict and Innovation**  
The plateau’s weeping is finite, and its depletion has bred both innovation and conflict. The **Veythari** have developed "resonance cannons"—devices that vibrate at the same frequency as the rocks—to stimulate new weeping in dry zones. Meanwhile, the **Sarnul** have built floating forges that drift on the Mourns, following seasonal currents to new mineral deposits.  

Yet tensions simmer between the castes. The Mourn’keth accuse the Veythari of "enslaving" the rocks with their machines, while the Sarnul resent the Veythari’s refusal to let them mine the plateau’s deeper veins. Bandit clans, known as **Rustkin**, prowl the gullies, stealing water and scrap to survive.  

---

### **The Unseen**  
Beneath it all, there is a creeping **unnaturalness** to Keth-Mor. The bioluminescence is spreading, creeping up the stones like a living stain. Some say the Mourns are evolving, their pools growing deeper and more sentient. Others whisper that the rocks’ hum is a **call**, summoning something from the plateau’s core—a slumbering entity that the Keth’vanni’s ancestors once worshiped as a god of rust and rain.  

In the Deafening Hour, when the forges roar and the Mourns swell, the Keth’vanni don their **resonance masks**—helmets embedded with tuning forks that let them "sing" back to the stones. It is both a prayer and a plea, a ritual to keep their world weeping, their lives sustained by the uneasy truce between water, stone, and steel.  

To live in Keth-Mor is to exist on the edge of a knife, where every drop of water is a battle won and every breath of mist a gift from a land that may one day decide to stop crying.
