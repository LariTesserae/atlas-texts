**Inhabitants of the Weeping Marches of Vorthak**  

The Weeping Marches are not merely a landscape—they are a living contradiction, their inhabitants as much a part of the terrain’s fractured essence as the weeping minerals or the mercury that flows like lifeblood through their veins. To dwell here is to exist in the tension between dissolution and genesis, where the act of survival requires a surrender to chaos.  

**The Chitterclank Architects**  
The chitterclanks are perhaps the most overt sign of the marches’ industrial past—and its future. These arthropod-like constructs, with their corroded iron carapaces and sinewy wire limbs, move in skittering packs that converge around the weeping stones, their mandibles scraping mineral-rich deposits into their internal crucibles. Their purpose is not mere survival but *creation*. They are artisans of a forgotten science, assembling hybrid structures from the refuse of collapsed realities: gears fused with bone, pipes grown from fungal mycelium, and gears that spin without axles. Some theories suggest they are the remnants of a civilization that once tried to mechanize the marches themselves, while others say they are the marches’ immune system, corralling the chaos of interdimensional debris into coherent form. Their "industry" is a vast, incomprehensible project that reshapes the landscape nightly, leaving behind alien geometries and corroded monuments that sing with the resonance of the fungal hivemind.  

**The Glasswyrms’ Pilgrimage**  
The glasswyrms, frozen at the molecular level by their own ammonia-based metabolisms, are neither wholly alive nor dead. They burrow through the brine-soaked earth in slow, seismic undulations, their bodies leaving trails of frost that crystallize into temporary ecosystems. These serpentine entities are pilgrims of a sort, drawn to the Maw in cycles that mirror the marches’ erratic geological heartbeats. Their calcium carbonate scales are etched with runes that shift and fade, remnants of an ancient code that once governed the flow of mercury and the rhythm of gravity. Some glasswyrms are said to carry fragments of the past within their cores—preserved moments of the world before the marches, glimpsed in the iridescent sheen of their scales. They are both destroyer and architect, eroding the land with their passage to make way for new growths of fungal mats.  

**The Mycoelectric Choir**  
The fungal mats—those geometric, bioluminescent growths—are not merely flora but the neural network of the marches themselves. Their electric-blue pulses synchronize with the hissing brine and the grinding gears of the chitterclanks, creating a symphony of communication that stitches the realm together. Within this choir, individual entities arise: the *Sporethinkers*, floating clusters of fungal matter that drift in the fog, their thoughts transmitted through bioelectric storms. They are the philosophers of the marches, debating the nature of time and entropy in harmonic droning. Others, like the *Rootmothers*, anchor themselves to the calcified husks of microbes, their hyphae threading through the stones to extract memories from the mercury. These beings are neither plant nor animal but something older, a relic of the world that existed before life learned to distinguish itself from the elements.  

**The Maw’s Dwellers**  
The sinkhole known as the Maw is a throat of black metal and pulsing light, its tunnels alive with the clangor of unseen machinery. Here dwell the *Gearwhales*, entities of living alloy and liquid mercury that migrate through the tunnels like leviathans, their bodies inscribed with the same flickering symbols as the levitating terrain. They are the guardians of the marches’ digestive system, breaking down the remnants of dead realities into raw material for the chitterclanks. The guttural voices from the Maw are not speech but the resonance of these beings’ metallic hearts, a language of vibrations that warps the air into shapes and meanings. Some say the Gearwhales are the last fragments of a god that once tried to eat the world, only to be consumed by it in turn.  

**The Levitating Remnants**  
Not all of the marches’ inhabitants are native. The chunks of red sandstone etched with shifting symbols and the moss-covered concrete humming with stellar heat are prisoners of the gravity fluctuations, trapped in loops of motion that baffle even the chitterclanks. These remnants—perhaps refugees from other dimensions or failed experiments in reality-engineering—have become semi-sentient over time. The symbols on the sandstone form a hive-mind of fractured consciousness, its members reliving the final moments of their home world in an endless cycle. The concrete slab, meanwhile, houses a microcosm of life that evolved inside a dying star, its heat and radiation birthing crystalline organisms that feed on entropy itself. Both are refugees in a land that chews up and reconstitutes the very idea of "home."  

**The Time-Bent Wanderers**  
The fluctuating time fields of the marches have given rise to beings who exist in multiple temporal states simultaneously. The *Chronoclasts* are humanoid figures—if they can be called that—whose bodies flicker between youth and decay, their memories a jumble of past, present, and potential futures. They move like sleepwalkers, planting seeds in the fungal mats that will not sprout for centuries, or dismantling chitterclank machinery that has not yet been built. They are the marches’ historians and revisionists, editing the narrative of the realm with each step. Some Chroniclasts are benevolent guides, helping lost travelers navigate the marches’ temporal eddies; others are malevolent, erasing entire sections of the landscape with the force of their disbelief in its stability.  

**The Unseen Current**  
Perhaps the most pervasive inhabitants of all are the *Currents*—non-corporeal entities that flow through the mercury like rivers through space. They are the accumulated psychic residue of every mind that has ever died in the marches, their thoughts and regrets given form by the weeping minerals. The Currents whisper to the fungal mats, influencing their growth patterns, and occasionally coalesce into temporary bodies of liquid metal and brine to interact with the physical world. They are the marches’ subconscious, a collective haunting that ensures no secret is ever truly buried.  

In the Weeping Marches, to be alive is to be a contradiction: a fleeting arrangement of matter and meaning in a place where both are perpetually unraveling. The inhabitants know this, and it shapes them. They are not survivors but collaborators, stitching themselves into the chaos to create something new from the wreckage of everything else.
