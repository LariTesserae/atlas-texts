**The Inhabitants of Vorthak**  

The weeping city’s true inhabitants are not *of* the world—they are its echoes, its indigestion, its dreams. They exist because the city remembers being alive, and in that remembering, refuses to let go.  

**The Mire-Witches**:  
These beings are the fractured souls of the city’s original inhabitants, their forms woven from the marsh’s black muck and the pulsing roots that vein the ruins. They emerge during the steam-vents’ peaks, their bodies tall and gaunt, with skin like cracked clay and hair that drips luminous lichen. They speak in wet, gurgling tones, their voices seeming to come from both their throats and the pools around them. They tend to the city’s wounds—pressing their hands to walls to halt erosion, knotting roots into new pathways, and whispering to the stones until they shift into more "comfortable" positions. They are neither wholly benevolent nor malevolent, but driven by a primal need to keep the city *conscious*, even as it decays. Their tears are acidic, eating into the stone to create new grooves, and they collect these tears in hollowed gourds to "plant" as new buildings.  

**The Husk-Cities**:  
In the voids beneath the streets, where staircases spiral into humming darkness, dwell the Husk-Cities—entities born from the reflections in the puddles. These are not reflections of Vorthak but of other places, other *possibilities* that never were. Their inhabitants are translucent, faceted beings with too many joints, their forms flickering between humanoid and architectural. They whisper through the city’s hollows, speaking in languages that sound like grinding stone and collapsing brick. They are drawn to the real city’s instability, seeking to merge their own crystalline structures with Vorthak’s shifting walls. At times, they succeed, creating short-lived hybrid buildings that glow with inner light before dissolving into mist. They are the city’s subconscious, its regrets, and its unbuilt futures.  

**The Sap-Lights**:  
The flickering lights darting between ruins are the city’s immune system—self-sustaining colonies of bioluminescent fungi and moss that evolved in the absence of true flora. They cluster around the lampposts, feeding off the black sap that oozes from the metal, growing into bulbous, pulsing masses. These entities are territorial and reactive, flashing bright red when intruders approach and dimming to near-invisibility when content. They communicate through rapid color changes, creating a silent semaphore that even the Mire-Witches struggle to interpret. Their spores are hallucinogenic, causing those who inhale them to see the city as it once was—or as it might become. Some say the Sap-Lights are the closest thing to "alive" in Vorthak, though they reproduce by infecting the rust on abandoned tools, turning scraps into new nodes.  

**The Bone-Crafters**:  
Deep in the honeycombed tunnels, where erosion grooves spiral like fingerprints, dwell the Bone-Crafters. These are humanoid figures carved from the very stone they inhabit, their bodies perpetually half-formed as they chip themselves loose from the rock. They are artisans of a sort, using the city’s reclaimed materials to build "corrections"—bridges that connect to nothing, doorways that lead sideways, and staircases that loop into themselves. Their hands are forever dusted with stone chips, and their eyes glow with the faint blue light of ghost lichen. They speak only in riddles, believing that the city’s true shape lies hidden in paradox, and they seek to sculpt Vorthak into its "ideal" form, one that defies gravity, memory, and even time.  

**The Echo-Things**:  
The most elusive are the Echo-Things, born from the soundscape itself. They manifest as distortions in the air, like ripples in water, coalescing into vague humanoid shapes that mimic the voices of long-dead citizens. They are drawn to the metallic *pings* of cooling stone and the creaks of shifting wood, feeding on the vibrations. They cannot form stable bodies, instead existing as overlapping layers of sound and motion—a whisper here, a flicker of misheard footsteps there. They haunt the spaces between the city’s breaths, and when the mist thickens, they cluster like static, their murmurs rising to a deafening chorus before vanishing. Some say they are the city’s attempts to speak to itself, to fill the silence left by its abandonment.  

**The Unseen Gardeners**:  
In the rare patches where thorny vegetation grows, there are Gardeners—shadowy figures with spindly limbs and faces hidden behind masks of bark. They tend the thistles and thornvines with obsessive care, pruning them into geometric patterns that mirror the city’s spiraled grooves. Their tools are rusted relics: scythes that hiss steam, watering cans filled with black water, and seeds that sprout into crystalline flowers. They are rarely seen directly, only felt as a prickling on the skin when one passes too close to their work. They speak to the plants in a language of clicks and whispers, coaxing them to grow in defiance of the city’s entropy.  

**The City Itself**:  
Perhaps the most profound inhabitant is the city as a singular, dormant entity. Vorthak’s "body" is the accumulation of all its shifting stones, its breathing walls, its tides of water and mist. It dreams in the language of erosion and root-pulse, its subconscious manifesting as the Mire-Witches’ rituals and the Husk-Cities’ invasions. When the subsonic hum grows loud, it is the city dreaming of mobility, of rising from the mire to reclaim its place in the world. Those who listen too long to its dreams risk becoming part of the dream themselves, their forms dissolving into the landscape or their minds adding new layers to the city’s ever-shifting logic.  

In Vorthak, life and environment are one. To exist here is to participate in the city’s ceaseless, paradoxical dance of decay and becoming. The inhabitants are not separate from the ruins—they are the ruins, breathing, remembering, and refusing to let go.
