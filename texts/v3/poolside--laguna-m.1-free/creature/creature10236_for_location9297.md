**The Inhants of the Weeping Marsh**  

The inhabitants of Shardvale are a people shaped by moisture and memory, their existence a delicate balance between endurance and adaptation. They call themselves the *Thornveil*—a name earned from the way their settlements cling to the land like lichen to stone—and they have evolved peculiarities that mirror the marsh itself. Their bodies are wiry and compact, with joints that flex in fluid, almost amphibious ways, allowing them to navigate the slick boardwalks and unstable terrain. Their skin is pale and mottled, mottled like the clay itself, with a thin film of subcutaneous fat that insulates against the perpetual damp. They rarely speak aloud, having developed a complex language of clicks, whistles, and hand gestures—a remnant, perhaps, of ancient avoidance tactics to prevent disturbing the marsh’s "listening." Their eyes are large and reflective, adapted to the dim bioluminescence of the plants, and their ears are disproportionately sensitive, capable of detecting the faintest drips or creaks in the wood, which serve as their primary means of communication over distances.  

**Biological and Cultural Adaptations:**  
The Thornveil have a symbiotic relationship with the marsh’s bioluminescent flora. They cultivate the waxy-leaved shrubs in enclosed courtyards, trimming and tending them with ritualistic care, as the plants’ hum at dusk is believed to harmonize with the heartbeats of the settlement. The glow of the berries is harvested and pressed into pigments for body paint, used during the Green Sleep to ward off the disorienting insects. Their homes are built with a lattice of blackened timber and wattle, but the walls are often embedded with living willow branches, which they graft into the structures to stabilize them against the ground’s "breathing." The weeping stones are sacred to them; they believe the gelatinous strands are the marsh’s way of recording its history. The strands are carefully harvested, dried, and braided into ropes, baskets, and even clothing—materials that are both water-resistant and oddly flexible, as if alive.  

**Social Structure and Beliefs:**  
The Thornveil live in communes of extended families, their social structure fluid and meritocratic. Leadership falls to those who can best "read" the marsh—interpreting the patterns of its breath, predicting sinkholes, or navigating the Echoes without becoming lost in them. These individuals, called Marsh-Speakers, undergo trials during the Green Sleep, entering the bioluminescent swamps alone to commune with what they believe are the spirits of the land. Their settlements are never static; each year, as floods reshape the landscape, the Thornveil dismantle and rebuild their homes in a slow, crab-like migration. They view this not as a burden but as a sacred duty, a way of staying "rooted in motion."  

**Artifacts and Rituals:**  
Their tools are crafted from the weeping stones’ fibrous strands and the iron-rich clay, which they fire in kilns fueled by sulfur-rich marsh gas. The resulting ceramics and metalwork are strange and beautiful—jagged, organic shapes that seem to grow rather than be made. Their forges are communal spaces, and the sulfurous smoke they emit is considered a protective barrier, warding off the marsh’s more unstable phenomena. During the Echoes, when sounds and movements from the past replay, the Thornveil retreat to their courtyards and drum rhythms to "anchor" themselves to the present. They believe the Echoes are memories of the marsh’s violent birth, and by mimicking the sounds, they help the land "remember" its own stability.  

**The Marsh’s Children:**  
There are rumors of other beings in Shardvale—the shapes glimpsed in the mist, neither animal nor human. The Thornveil refer to them as the *Drowned Kin*, descendants of those who vanished into sinkholes or became lost in the Echoes. Some say the marsh reabsorbs its dead, twisting their bodies into the roots of willows or the gelatinous strands of the weeping stones. Others believe the Drowned Kin are not dead at all but transformed, part of the land’s ongoing conversation. The Thornveil leave offerings at the marsh’s edge: polished stones, strands of their own hair, and vessels of fermented grain. They do not fear these beings but treat them as neighbors, sometimes even partners in the daily negotiation of survival.  

**The Weight of Wetness:**  
To live in the Weeping Marsh is to exist in a state of constant negotiation—with the land, with the echoes, with the slow, inexorable pressure of water and time. The Thornveil have learned to move with the marsh’s rhythms, to read its moods in the drip of a leak or the tilt of a wall. They are a people of thresholds, forever occupying the space between land and water, past and present, the living and the half-alive. Their lives are short but intense, marked by rituals that honor the fragility of their existence and the strange, pulsing beauty of the world they inhabit.
