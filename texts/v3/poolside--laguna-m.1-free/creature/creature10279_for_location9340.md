**The Humming Dwellers of Verdant Rust**  

The inhabitants of the Glimmering Throat are not quite human, but neither are they fully *other*. Their bodies are draped in layers of patched, water-stained cloth—tattered remnants of some distant age—which they stitch together from salvaged fibers and the silk of bioluminescent algae threads. Their skin is a patchwork of pale, porous flesh mottled with symbiotic lichen colonies that shift in color with the humidity, glowing faintly green in the dark. These lichens are not merely decoration; they are an extension of the dwellers themselves, respiring in tandem with the moss that carpets the settlement and serving as living conduits for the phosphorescent mold that seeps from the walls. Their faces are obscured by wraps of woven cattail fibers and strips of corroded metal, leaving only their eyes visible: wide, reflective, and unblinking, like those of deep-sea creatures adapted to eternal twilight.  

They move with a deliberate slowness, as if every step requires calculation. Their limbs are elongated and jointed awkwardly, as though they have grown into the postures required by their labors. Many have extra fingers—three or four on each hand—twisted and calloused from handling machinery caked in rust and root-tendrils. They do not speak. Instead, they hum, low and resonant vibrations that seem to emanate not just from their throats but from their chests, rib cages, and even the soles of their feet. This humming is a language older than words, a harmonic resonance they use to commune with the pumps, the trees, and the very walls themselves. The dwellers believe that speech would fracture the delicate balance of the settlement’s "song"—the symphony of mechanical hums, creaking metal, and pulsing vegetation that sustains their world.  

### Symbiosis with the Machinery  
Their relationship with the ancient pumps is intimate and sacred. Each dweller is assigned to a cluster of machines, which they tend with ritualistic precision. They oil gears with the metallic nectar exuded by the spiral trees, a fluid that corrodes steel but also seems to invigorate the machinery, allowing it to metabolize the rust and algae into kinetic energy. The dwellers’ hands are permanently stained green from this process, their nails encrusted with crystalline deposits of iron oxide and plant matter. Without their care, the pumps would stall, and the water would stagnate, allowing the vegetation to overrun the canals and choke the settlement’s lifeblood. They fear this stagnation above all things, for it would mean the "Great Drowning"—a dissolution where the boundary between root and ruin vanishes entirely, and the last vestiges of structure collapse into the marsh.  

### The Whisperers of Growth  
Though the dwellers do not cultivate traditional crops, they tend to the vegetation as gardeners might. The silver-leaved trees, which spiral upward in impossible geometries, are grafted and pruned by the dwellers using tools forged from melted car parts and polished bone. These trees are both pillars and parasites, their roots threading through the skeletons of buildings to draw nutrients from the industrial soil. The dwellers believe the trees are the descendants of some ancient forest that learned to adapt to the rust, twisting its essence into something new. Their leaves emit a faint, hypnotic phosphorescence, which the dwellers say contains memories—fragments of the world before the Rust took hold.  

### The Absence of Other Life  
The lack of fauna is both a mystery and a burden. The dwellers tell stories of a time when birds filled the sky and fish swam in the canals, but those creatures vanished long ago, unable to adapt to the metallic tang of the water or the suffocating fog. Now, the only movement comes from the dwellers themselves and the bioluminescent algae that bloom in the canals, their light pulsing in eerie synchrony with the dwellers’ humming. Some say the algae are the last remnants of the old world’s oceans, transformed by the Rust into something alien. The dwellers avoid eating them, believing they carry the memories of drowned cities.  

### The Ritual of Permanence  
Each dweller undergoes a rite of initiation at adolescence (if such a stage exists in their cycle; time is fluid here). They must spend a night alone in the "Weeping Quarter," a flooded section of the settlement where the walls drip with condensation and the roots writhe visibly. Survivors of this trial return forever changed, their lichen growths more vibrant and their humming deeper, as if they’ve absorbed some of the settlement’s collective consciousness. These initiates join the ranks of the "Keepers of the Pulse," those responsible for maintaining the great pumps at the heart of the Throat.  

### The Shadow of Decay  
The dwellers are acutely aware of their own mortality, though they rarely acknowledge it. Their bodies, despite their adaptations, are slowly succumbing to the same forces they fight against. Their skin grows thinner, their joints stiffer, their humming quieter. Some believe that when a dweller’s song fades entirely, they become part of the vegetation—absorbed into the roots or transformed into one of the silver trees. Others think they simply dissolve into the fog, their essence feeding the next generation of pumps. Regardless, the dwellers accept this fate with the patience of stone, continuing their work until the day the machines fall silent and the roots claim them all.  

In the Glimmering Throat, to live is to collaborate with decay. The dwellers are neither its victims nor its masters, but its reluctant architects, crafting beauty from the ruins of a world that refused to die.
