The inhabitants of the High Moors are the **Resonant Dead**—not truly dead, but neither alive in any conventional sense. They are the lingering consciousness of the ancient civilization that once carved the black stone settlements, preserved and transformed by the moors' reality-warping properties into something both more and less than human.

They manifest as **frequency-ghosts**, beings of pure resonance and memory who exist in the harmonic spaces between moments. Each is anchored to a cluster of echo-trees, their consciousness spread across the network of hollow trunks like a choir singing in different keys. They appear as translucent figures wrapped in tattered remnants of their former clothing—robes that shimmer with the same bioluminescent patterns as the crystalvines, faceted like the quartz veins in the stone.

These beings move by **resonance-stepping**—teleporting between areas of similar vibrational frequency, which is why they're often found near the humming trees or standing atop the floating rock slabs. They don't walk so much as *phase* into existence where the land's song matches their own internal frequency. Their voices sound like wind through dry leaves, but when they speak, their words create visible ripples in the air—sound-made-substance that briefly illuminates the strange geometries of their reality.

They feed on **temporal eddies** and **memory-resonance**. Some dwell in settlements where time runs backward, consuming the reverse-flow to maintain their form. Others frequent areas where time accelerates, rapidly aging objects to extract the nostalgic essence that lingers in weathered surfaces. Their sustenance is the emotional echoes embedded in the ruins—joy, sorrow, love, and loss crystallized in the stone itself.

The Resonant Dead have no fixed appearance, for they are constantly **reconstructing themselves** from the land's collective dreams and memories. Their features shift like reflections in disturbed water. One moment they might resemble their ancestors with weathered faces and calloused hands clutching stone tools; the next, they've become something more fluid—a face that stretches like taffy, limbs that branch into root-like appendages, eyes that glow with the soft blue-purple light of starblossoms.

They communicate through **symphonic thinking**—complex harmonies that carry layers of meaning. To outsiders, their conversations sound like wind chimes and distant thunder, but between themselves, each variation in tone, each harmonic convergence, conveys intricate thoughts about the shifting architecture of reality, the slow migration of stone terraces, or the proper frequencies for stabilizing particularly unruly sinkholes.

Many of them have developed a symbiotic relationship with the crystalvines, allowing the luminescent plants to grow through their semi-corporeal forms. This gives them **bioluminescent markings** that pulse in time with their emotional states and allow them to participate in the moors' complex pollination networks—even though the insects they once served are long gone, the vines still respond to the electrical signatures of consciousness, and the Resonant Dead have learned to mimic these patterns.

They serve as **reality gardeners**, tending the impossible landscape with careful manipulation of frequency and memory. When a floating terrace begins to sink, they gather to sing it back aloft using the echo-trees as amplifiers. When time flows too chaotically in one area, they create harmonic anchors to stabilize it. They prune the crystalvines, not for growth, but to maintain the proper electromagnetic balance that keeps the moors suspended in their pocket of altered physics.

Children among them—beings newly transitioned from human to resonant form—still retain more recognizable features. They're fascinated by mirrors and still pools, not understanding that their reflections show impossible landscapes, and they'll spend hours trying to communicate with these alternate versions of themselves. They play games of **echo-tag** across the floating stone terraces, their laughter creating harmonic distortions that make the rocks sing.

The oldest among them, those who remember the world before the moors became strange, have become **living monuments**. Their bodies have crystallized into forms resembling the ruins themselves—angular, geometric, carved from solidified sound and memory. They stand motionless for decades, acting as tuning forks for entire regions, ensuring that reality doesn't unravel completely.

They fear the **Silent Zones**—areas where the echo-trees have died, leaving behind only empty husks. In these places, time flows normally, the floating stones crash to the ground, and the Resonant Dead become truly mortal, aging rapidly until they crumble to dust. They avoid these zones with desperate intensity, having learned that death here means final cessation, not transformation.

Most hauntingly, they're beginning to forget what it meant to be solid. They remember hands that could hold tools, feet that could walk on solid ground, but these memories feel like dreams of being someone else entirely. Yet when they gather in large groups during the periodic convergence storms, their combined resonance can temporarily restore physical laws, allowing them to experience—if only briefly—what it felt like to be anchored to a single moment in time.

They are the moors' immune system, its repair mechanism, and its memory—all that remains of life's defiant persistence, transformed into something beautiful and terrible that exists in the spaces between what was, what is, and what might yet be.
