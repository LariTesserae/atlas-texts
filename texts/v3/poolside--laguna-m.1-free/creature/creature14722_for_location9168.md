The inhabitants of this valley are known as the **Symbionts**, a people born from the collision of humanity and the land’s restless rebirth. They exist in a tense, cyclical harmony with their environment, their lives a blend of reverence and utilitarian pragmatism. Physically, they are a diverse lot, shaped by the valley’s extremes. Many have developed a faint bioluminescence, a side effect of prolonged exposure to the glowing moss and fungi that blanket their homes. Their skin bears faint traceries of silver or green light, most pronounced on their faces and hands, which they often paint with symbolic patterns to mimic the moss’s natural filaments. Some have taken to wearing cloaks woven from the iridescent fur of local rodents, their hoods lined with moss to filter the valley’s miasma. 

### **Physiology and Adaptations**  
The Symbionts have evolved—or adapted through generations of necessity—to the valley’s conditions. Their lungs are more efficient at processing the thick, mineral-laden air, and their eyes, though human, reflect the amber hues of gas lamps and the eerie blue of fungal blooms, granting them better sight in the perpetual dusk. Many have calloused, scarred hands from handling corroded machinery and thorny vines alike. A subset of the population, the **Tinker-Druids**, have developed a unique symbiosis with the electric vines that drape the ruins. Tiny, static-charged hairs on their arms allow them to safely interface with the vines, channeling their energy to power lights or grind grain, though accidents are frequent and sometimes fatal.  

### **Culture and Society**  
Their settlements are clusters of low, moss-covered dwellings built into the skeletons of old factories. Walls are patched with tarps infused with fungal spores to regulate humidity, and roofs are layered with ferns to catch rainwater. The Symbionts live in extended family groups called **Rootlines**, named for the way their homes interconnect via root systems and corridors of ivy. Leadership rotates annually among the Rootlines, with each leader—called a **Verdant Keeper**—responsible for maintaining the fragile balance between the organic and mechanical.  

The Symbionts have a complex relationship with the remnants of the old world. They salvage parts from buried machinery to craft tools and art, but they also perform rituals to "appease" the land’s disquiet. At dusk, they gather at the **Convergence Pools**—shallow, salt-crusted lakes where steam rises in ghostly tendrils—to offer small sacrifices of metal or fruit to the unseen forces they believe shape the valley. These offerings often vanish into the depths, taken by the luminescent eels that lurk beneath the surface, creatures the Symbionts consider sacred.  

### **Technology and Daily Life**  
Their technology is a hybrid of scavenged parts and bioengineered solutions. Waterwheels and gears are kept turning by teams of both humans and large, domesticated insects called **clickflies**, whose buzzing wings are harnessed to power air pumps that ventilate the underground aqueducts. The Symbionts craft tools from rusted metal and hardened resin, shaping them with fire stolen from the ever-burning pilot lights of old furnaces. They farm in the braided streams, cultivating algae that grows in the salt-crusted pools and using the iridescent rodents to aerate soil in their gardens.  

Children are taught from an early age to read the "language" of the steam vents, which they believe tell stories of the valley’s past. They learn to navigate the maze of pipes and overgrown foundations, avoiding areas where the electrical vines grow too dense and the metallic birds gather in ominous flocks. The birds, called **scrapperjays**, are both allies and nuisances; their metallic feathers are highly prized for crafting reflective surfaces to ward off the valley’s shadowy predators—creatures never seen but often felt, lurking in the mist.  

### **Beliefs and Mysteries**  
The Symbionts do not speak of the valley’s origins, only that it is "the Becoming." They believe the land is alive, reshaping itself through cycles of destruction and growth. Their myths speak of the **Deep Movers**, entities they claim dwell in the bedrock, pushing the terrain upward and carving new streams. Some say the Symbionts themselves are not entirely human, but hybrid beings—born from the first people who merged with the land’s strange energies. This is why their skin glows and their children sometimes sprout leaves instead of hair, a phenomenon they call the "Green Touch."  

In the heart of the valley lies the **Nexus**, a half-submerged factory where the oldest machinery still thrums. The Symbionts avoid it, believing it to be a tomb of the past, yet they leave offerings of water and song at its threshold. At night, the sounds of the valley shift—the groaning of gears becomes almost melodic, and the mist takes on shapes that might be faces or hands reaching up. The Symbionts say these are the land’s dreams, and to disturb them is to invite misfortune.  

### **Conflict and Resilience**  
Despite their adaptability, the Symbionts face constant challenges. The valley’s terrain shifts unpredictably; buildings collapse, new streams cut through old paths, and the bioluminescent flora blooms and dies in erratic cycles. Recently, a faction called the **Purists** has emerged, advocating for the complete purging of industrial relics to accelerate nature’s reclamation. They clash with the **Keepers**, who believe the machines must be preserved as part of the valley’s identity. These tensions simmer beneath the surface, like the steam that seeps through cracks in the streets.  

Yet the Symbionts endure, their lives a testament to the valley’s duality. They are neither fully of the wild nor the old world, but something new—creatures of clay and rust, moss and metal, forever walking the line between collapse and regrowth.
