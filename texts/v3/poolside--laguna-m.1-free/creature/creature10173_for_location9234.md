**The Inhabitants of the Verdant Hollow**  

The people of the Verdant Hollow are a quiet, deliberate folk, shaped as much by the stone and the *xerophyta* as by their own hands. They are humans, but ones adapted to the Hollow’s peculiar rhythms and physics. Their skin is pale, almost translucent, with a faint greenish undertone from the chlorophyll that seeps into their bodies through the fibrous walls of their homes. Their eyes are large and reflective, tuned to the dim, fungal glows that filter through the *xerophyta*’s translucent membranes. They move with the careful slowness of those who know that haste is a luxury the dark does not afford—though they are quick when startled, their footsteps soundless on the dust-clad floor, and their hands are deft with the crystalline tools carved from heartwood.  

Their society is matriarchal, ruled by the **Vein-Mothers**, women whose bodies have been selectively altered over generations to host colonies of the *xerophyta*’s rootlets. These rootlets branch from their spines and limbs, knitting them into the plant’s neural web. The Vein-Mothers serve as intermediaries between the Hollow’s living architecture and its people, interpreting the hums and vibrations that ripple through the limestone. They are both revered and feared, for their communion with the *xerophyta* grants them prophetic visions—but also risks their minds, as the network’s vastness sometimes overwhelms their human senses. Their voices carry the resonant timbre of the Hollow itself, and when they speak, their words seem to echo not just in the air but in the very stone.  

Most inhabitants live in **cluster-towns** suspended within the hollowed interiors of old *xerophyta* trunks, their homes grown from the plant’s pulp and hardened into curved, organic chambers. These dwellings are ephemeral, slowly reshaped by the plant’s growth. Walls bulge with new root-tendrils, doorways sag and sprout bark, and families often find themselves relocated when the plant’s internal logic demands a shift. They accept this fluidity with a fatalism born of centuries; to fight the Hollow’s will is to invite the slow, toothless anger of starving roots. Their furnishings are minimal: cured leaf-matting for bedding, braziers of stone filled with slow-burning sap-resin, and tools hung from fibrous cords that drip with the plant’s sticky secretions.  

The Hollow’s people are skilled in the art of **symbiotic cultivation**, tending to the *xerophyta*’s root-networks as one might tend a garden, guiding the migrations of the burrowing root-fragments to reinforce weakened structures or seal cracks. They wear garments spun from the plant’s silk-like inner bark, which hardens into armor-like layers when treated with sap, and they mark their faces with dyes made from the crushed leaves, creating temporary tattoos that fade and shift as the plant metabolizes the pigments. Their children are named for the hums they are born during—**Low-Thrum**, **High-Spike**, **Still-Silence**—and their coming-of-age rites involve enduring the hallucinogenic sap’s visions, a test of mental fortitude.  

Yet the Hollow is not without its dissenters. A caste known as the **Stone-Singers** rejects the Vein-Mothers’ authority, believing the *xerophyta*’s network to be a parasitic force corrupting the land. They dwell in the fissures beyond the settled zones, scraping a living from the raw limestone and communing with the buried thing that stirs beneath. They claim the hum is not the Hollow’s heartbeat but a summons—a call from the thing below that the *xerophyta* are trying to drown out. Their bodies are marked by the acidic burns of resin, and their songs are discordant shrieks meant to disrupt the plant’s rhythms. They are rarely seen but often felt, as their sabotage causes sudden collapses or bursts of toxic sap that wither entire groves.  

At the heart of their cosmology is the belief in the **Great Settling**, a prophesied era when the Hollow will cease its eternal half-sleep and finalize its form. Some say this will mean the fusion of all life into the *xerophyta*’s network, a kind of collective transcendence; others fear it will see the plants and people alike crushed into the stone, leaving only the buried thing to inherit the earth. The Vein-Mothers preach patience, urging their people to “let the roots have their way,” while the Stone-Singers whisper of rising up to sever the network’s hold. For now, the hum continues, and the people of the Hollow live in the tension between growth and stillness, between the green pulse of life and the dark patience of stone.  

They are a people of slow miracles and slower decay, their lives measured in the unfurling of a leaf or the collapse of an archway, their fate entwined with a garden of bones that dreams of becoming a mountain.
