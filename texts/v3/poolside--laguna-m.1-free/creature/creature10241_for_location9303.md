## The Inhabitants of Veyle: The Stone-Singers and the Rooted

### The Stone-Singers

In the deepest shadows of the carved cities and along the most ancient fracture lines of the plateau, there dwell the Stone-Singers—beings that are neither wholly alive nor entirely mineral, but something that exists in the liminal space between geology and biology. They are the true children of Veyle, born from the magnetic anomalies and the land's own nascent consciousness during those first violent upheavals that shaped the plateau.

The Stone-Singers appear as vaguely humanoid figures composed of a living amalgamation of the plateau's constituent materials. Their bodies shift between states—sometimes solid granite streaked with veins of copper that pulse with magnetic energy, other times translucent as quartz, their internal structures visible as swirling matrices of crystalline formation. Their faces are smooth planes of various stones, with eyes that glow like captured starlight behind layers of mica. When they move, the sound is like stone grinding gently on stone, a susurration that carries meaning to those who know how to listen.

These beings communicate through subsonic vibrations that travel through the rock itself—their "songs" are geometric patterns of pressure and resonance that encode complex information about the health of the plateau, the movements of water through underground channels, and the approach of geological changes. To the human inhabitants, they appear as helpful spirits, appearing in moments of crisis to guide miners away from unstable tunnels or to show where fresh springs might be found. In truth, the Stone-Singers are the plateau's immune system, sensing disturbances and working to maintain the delicate balance of this young world.

### The Rooted

Among the human settlements dwell the Rooted—descendants of the first peoples who settled the plateau generations ago, now so thoroughly adapted to their environment that they have become something new. Their most striking feature is their relationship with the magnetic fields that plague navigation throughout Veyle. Where others find themselves helplessly turned around by the spinning compasses, the Rooted move with unerring certainty, their bodies having evolved specialized iron deposits in their inner ears that function as biological magnetometers.

The Rooted have developed skin that produces a natural antifreeze protein, allowing them to work outdoors in conditions that would kill unadapted humans within hours. Their lungs extract oxygen with twice the efficiency of ordinary peoples, and their metabolic processes can shift seamlessly between carbohydrate and lipid burning depending on food availability. Most remarkably, they have developed a form of external cellular memory—they can absorb trace minerals through their skin that supplement their diet and even communicate with the Stone-Singers through bioelectric fields that harmonize with the plateau's natural electromagnetic patterns.

The Rooted live in symbiosis with the plateau's lichen ecosystems. They cultivate patches of the hardy growth in carefully maintained terraces, understanding that the lichens serve as the primary producers in this harsh ecosystem. The lichens, in turn, seem to respond to the presence of the Rooted, growing more abundantly and showing greater genetic diversity in areas of long-term habitation. Some among the Rooted claim they can taste the "flavor" of different mineral compositions through their footsteps, feeling the subtle variations in the rock through their bare soles.

### The Between-Born

Perhaps the most unusual inhabitants of Veyle are those born during the magnetic storms that periodically sweep across the plateau—children who exist in constant flux between states of matter. These Between-Born are born during the most intense geomagnetic events, when the boundary between the physical and the potential becomes permeable. They grow up as seemingly normal children, but during the regular magnetic fluctuations, they phase partially into other dimensional states, becoming simultaneously present and absent.

The Between-Born serve as intermediaries between the Stone-Singers and the human population. They can exist comfortably in the vibrational frequencies that carry the Songs of the Singers, translating the geologic poetry into human language. In return, they help the Singers understand the emotional and social complexities of human life. Many Between-Born become the settlement's healers, their ability to phase between states allowing them to perform surgeries that would be impossible with conventional tools, or to extract toxins by partially existing in a state where matter passes through matter.

### The Seasonal Nomads

Not all inhabitants of Veyle remain rooted in place. The Seasonal Nomads follow the brief growing seasons and the migration patterns of the mountain sheep herds across the plateau. These people have developed an almost supernatural ability to predict weather and geological activity, reading signs invisible to others—the way certain lichens change color hours before a storm, or how the Stone-Singers' songs shift in preparation for an earthquake.

The Nomads travel in family groups of 20-30 individuals, living in shelters that can be assembled and disassembled in minutes. Their shelters are made from a material they call "cloud-wool"—fibers harvested from the massive birds that circle the plateau, woven together with the silk of cave spiders and treated with secretions from the Rooted's lichen gardens. These structures shift their opacity and insulation properties based on environmental conditions, becoming opaque and warm during storms, transparent and cooling during the brief summer warmth.

### The Deep Miners

Beneath the settlements, in caverns that extend down into the plateau's foundation, dwell the Deep Miners—humans and Stone-Singers who have adapted to subterranean life. These beings rarely see sunlight, navigating by echolocation and an extrasensory awareness of the magnetic fields. Over generations, they have developed bioluminescent patches on their skin that they can control consciously, using patterns of light to communicate in the eternal darkness.

The Deep Miners work with the planet's own growth processes, guiding the formation of new crystal structures and helping to channel mineral deposits into useful configurations. They are the architects of Veyle's hidden infrastructure—networks of tuned crystal resonators that stabilize the plateau's magnetic fields, prevent dangerous earthquakes, and carry water and nutrients throughout the underground ecosystem.

These various inhabitants of Veyle have learned that survival depends not on conquest or adaptation alone, but on becoming part of the plateau's own slow dreaming—the recognition that they are not separate from this ancient-yet-young land, but expressions of its ongoing creation, each generation adding their own verse to the eternal Song of stone and sky.
