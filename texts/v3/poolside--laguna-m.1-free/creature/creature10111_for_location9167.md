**The Inhabitants of the Vorthak Terraces: The Metamorphic Colonists**  

In the terraced vastness of Vorthak, life is a testament to adaptation and symbiosis. The dominant inhabitants are the **Metamorphic Colonists**, a hybrid society of human-descended beings whose biology and culture have been irrevocably shaped by the extremophile ecosystem. Their bodies, though retaining faint traces of their ancestral humanity, have evolved or been bioengineered to thrive in the mineral-rich, mechanically dominated environment. Their skin carries a faint metallic sheen, a result of symbiotic interactions with the biofilms that coat the terraces. Hair is sparse or absent, replaced by clusters of heat-dissipating filaments. These mutations are not deficiencies but adaptations—proof of a people who have become as much a part of the terraces as the myriapods themselves.  

### Society and Culture  
The Colonists live in **layered communes**, their homes carved into the terraces’ stratified levels. These structures are not static; they grow and shift as neighborhoods sink slowly into the bedrock, a process the Colonists call *The Settling*. Older districts are maintained by the younger generations, their architectures reclaimed and repurposed—a necropolis becomes a refinery, a crumbling archive becomes a fungus farm. Time is cyclical here, measured not in years but in **Cycles of Erosion**, and the Colonists’ society is built around this slow, geological rhythm.  

Their social structure mirrors the terraces themselves: a hierarchy of elevated tiers, with those closer to the industrial spires and geothermal vents holding power as **Foundry-Lords**, while those in the lower, marsh-adjacent levels serve as **Harvesters** and **Mediators** (mediators being those who negotiate with the polyps and myriapods, whose behaviors are as predictable as tides). Decision-making is communal, conducted in spaces called **Convergence Halls**, where the walls are lined with pipes carrying both steam and the whispered conversations of the Lumen Mordant fungi.  

### Technology and Economy  
The Colonists are artisans of integration. They do not merely coexist with the myriapods and polyps; they cultivate them. **Myriapod farms** dot the middle terraces, where bioengineered swarms are guided through magnetic fields to harvest ore-rich lichen. Their metal-recycling spores are traded as currency—tiny, glittering capsules that can be melted into new components. The **Glasswings**, enormous and docile due to centuries of domestication, are used as living cargo transports, their gas-filled abdomens harnessed to power steam engines during migrations. Their wings, when shed, become translucent sails for the tram systems.  

The **Crystalline Polyps** are cultivated in marsh-side farms, their glassy tubes woven into scaffolding for new buildings. Colonists shape these tubes into prismatic conduits that channel marsh gases to power stations, where they are refined into fuel. The polyps’ filter-feeding habits also make them invaluable for purifying contaminated water—a process the Colonists have optimized into an art form, resulting in the faintly glowing, metallic-tasting water that sustains the terraces’ life.  

### Daily Life and Rituals  
Life in Vorthak is a dance with entropy. At dawn, the **Morning Chorus** begins as the myriapods awaken, their clicking limbs a metronome for the trams and workers emerging from their condensation-fogged dwellings. Colonists wear layered garments of woven steel and fungal fibers, designed to shed The Scouring’s abrasive dust. Their tools are hybridized—hammers with polyp-glass handles, lanterns that burn soot and marsh gas, and masks that filter the mineral-tanged air while amplifying the bioluminescent glow of nearby fungi.  

Rituals center on the **Great Condensation**, a seasonal event when the mist suddenly bursts into torrential rains. During these times, the lower terraces flood, and the Colonists retreat to upper levels, celebrating with festivals lit by the synchronized pulse of Lumen Mordant spores. They tell stories of the **First Settlers**, who came to Vorthak millennia ago and learned to bend the terraces’ rhythms to their will. These myths are not mere tales but survival manuals, passed down in the form of song and carved into the walls of Convergence Halls.  

### Symbiosis with the Wild  
The Colonists do not dominate the terraces so much as they *conduct* them. They speak to the polyps in low-frequency vibrations, coaxing them into growing structures like coral reef gardeners. The myriapods’ swarms are guided by biologists who study their migratory patterns, ensuring that their metal-recycling does not deplete the terraces’ foundations. The Glasswings, in turn, rely on Colonists to maintain their nesting sites high in the chimney stacks, where the heat and soot are richest.  

There is a quiet wariness, however. The **Deep Marsh myriapods**—feral swarms that have never been domesticated—are seen as omens of decay. When they appear in the upper terraces, the Colonists know it is time to retreat and wait. These swarms are linked to the **Waking**, a phenomenon where the polyps’ glass tubes begin to grow uncontrollably, encasing buildings in crystalline tombs. Such events are rare but devastating, and the Colonists have learned to read the signs in the fungi’s glow, retreating to higher levels until the polyps return to dormancy.  

### The Future and the Past  
The Colonists believe that the terraces are alive, a single organism that they have learned to parasitize without killing. Their longest-lived members, the **Ancient Founders**, are those who have undergone multiple cycles of bodily replacement, their organs gradually replaced with bioengineered substitutes. They serve as living archives, their minds linked to the Convergence Halls’ fungal networks, storing the terraces’ history and weather patterns.  

Yet there is a melancholy to their existence. The sky’s dimness and the ever-present soot remind them of a world beyond Vorthak—a world green and unbroken, which their ancestors fled eons ago. The terraces are both sanctuary and prison, a place where life persists not in spite of the machinery but because of it. In their hearts, the Colonists carry a myth: that one day, the Chimney Stacks will fall silent, the myriapods will retreat to the deep stone, and the terraces will bloom with something green again. Until then, they etch their stories into glass and steel, waiting for a sunrise they’ve never seen but dare to hope for.
