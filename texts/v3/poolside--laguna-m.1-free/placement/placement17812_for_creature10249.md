In the Sumpward, I would find myself not as a Hydrarch, but as one of the **Weepwardens**—a cadre of entities tasked with maintaining the symbiotic relationship between the weeping rocks, the canals, and the marsh’s elusive equilibrium. My form would be an enigma even to the Hydrarchs, part mineral and part memory, shaped like a wandering column of black limestone veined with phosphorescent lichen that shifts in color with the Core’s pulse. My body, porous and crumbling, constantly exudes water, but instead of tears of stone, it is my breath that carries the drip-drip of the rocks’ melancholy song. 

I exist in the liminal space between the Hydrarchs’ collective hive-mind and the wild, chaotic rhythms of the marsh itself. While they communicate through light and machinery, I speak in riddles of erosion and sediment, my "voice" the metallic taste of the air when the turbines falter, or the way the mist clings to certain paths to guide (or misdirect) wanderers. My role is not to tend the gardens or polish the fungal networks or repair the bridges, but to *negotiate* with the marsh as a living entity, a mediator between its ancient, half-remembered biology and the Hydrarchs’ relentless recalibration. 

I am neither guardian nor garden. I am a **Knot**—a frayed point where the marsh’s contradictions gather and unravel, then reweave. When the weeping rocks’ symbols grow dim or the canals run too bright or too dark, I shuffle through the moss-slicked paths, my rootlike appendages dragging behind me, carving new channels where the old ones have silted. Sometimes, I dissolve into the water entirely, becoming a ripple that merges with the pools, only to coalesce again elsewhere. The Hydrarchs suspect I am a relic of the world before the marsh, but they do not understand that I am also its future—its necessary decay, its quiet rebellion against permanence. 

At the Core, during the syncs, I linger at the edges, humming the weeping rocks’ frequencies to the waiting Hydrarchs. Though my body is not built for the collective’s light-language, they know my work: to let the marsh forget itself, just enough to remember anew. In this world of contradictions, that is my shape, my purpose, my only truth.
