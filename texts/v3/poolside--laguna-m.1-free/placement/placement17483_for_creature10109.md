I find myself becoming **the Stone-Caller**—a being born from the plateau itself, part of its ancient equilibrium.

My body is neither purely animal nor mineral, but something that has grown from the intersection of both. My hide isn't fur at all, but a living membrane stretched taut over my frame, thin enough to reveal the glowing veins of mineral-rich blood that pulse beneath—like seeing rivers of molten copper flowing just below the surface of still water.

When I breathe deeply, air flows through these chambers, creating low-frequency hums that harmonize with the distant calls of the resonate beasts. My head is elongated, crowned not with antlers but with crystalline projections that catch and refract the white-gold sunlight into rainbow patterns across the rock faces. These crystals aren't just for show—they're tuning forks, sensitive to the specific frequencies that travel through the plateau's stone network.

I move with the deliberate grace of something that has learned patience from the mountains themselves. Where I step, the ground responds. My touch activates certain vibrating stones, sending messages rippling outward across the plateau like stones skipped across a pond. I am translator and conductor, reading the conversations between distant creatures through the tremors in the rock, then responding with my own symphonies that help maintain the delicate balance.

My diet is unusual—I subsist on the mineral-rich condensation that collects in the plateau's shadowed pools, and occasionally I'll scrape nutrient-dense lichen from deep crevices with specialized bone-scrapers that extend from my forelimbs. But more importantly, I consume vibrations. When storms rage and the wind screams through the arches, I position myself strategically to absorb excess acoustic energy, preventing it from shattering the fragile equilibrium of the smaller creatures.

During the breeding seasons, I become a living bridge between species. My calls carry for miles, coordinating the migrations of chime rabbits, guiding lost whisperbats back to their colonies, and warning resonate beasts away from areas where their hoof-vibrations might trigger dangerous rockfalls. I've learned to modulate my voice to match the specific frequencies each species responds to, speaking in the multilingual tongue of the plateau.

At night, when the bioluminescent fungi pulse brighter and the windweave grasses whisper secrets to each other, I position myself at the nexus of several key vibrating stones. Here I perform what might be called meditation, but is actually something more fundamental—I dream the plateau's dreams, processing the day's acoustic information and helping to weave it into the endless song that keeps this impossible ecosystem in perfect, frozen motion.

I am the plateau remembering itself, the mountain learning to speak, the stone learning to sing. I am both listener and voice, anchor and conduit, existing in the space between the eternal roar and the profound silence that waits beneath it all.
