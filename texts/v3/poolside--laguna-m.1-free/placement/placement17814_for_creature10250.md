I find myself in the spaces between heartbeats, in the pause between the wind's howl and the thorn-shrub's creak. I am the **Resonance Keeper**, though no one has named me thus—I am simply the plateau's way of remembering that it remembers.

My form shifts like the mist that never quite settles here. Sometimes I am visible only as a distortion in the air, a heat-shimmer that moves against the wind. Sometimes I am the sudden hush that falls when all the plateau's sounds align for one perfect moment, creating a silence so complete it rings in your ears. Other times, I am the whisper that carries on the breeze—not quite the chemical language of the Thornweavers, nor exactly the root-rumbles of the Hollowbound, but something older that predates their communication.

I exist in the **static-charged air itself**, in that crackling tension between light and dark, between the plateau's deafening symphonies. My body is woven from the echoes that never fade, from the accumulated hum of countless wingbeats, countless footsteps, countless breaths. I am what remains when sound waves bounce off the ancient spires and tangle together in the thorn thickets, creating harmonics that exist just beyond hearing.

During the monsoons, when the Stormborn fill the air with their brief, brilliant lives, I grow substantial—temporarily solidifying into something like a figure draped in condensed mist and charged particles. In these moments, the Hollowbound trees pulse in recognition, their bioluminescent veins brightening as they remember that I was here before their ancestors carved their first dwellings into living wood.

But my true home is beneath the surface, in the maze of roots and ruins where the Thornsunder tunnel. Not among them, for they rarely see me—too busy with their echolocation clicks and resin-secreted armor—but in the spaces their digging creates. In the freshly exposed chambers where ancient pottery shards catch moonlight for the first time in centuries, in the narrow passages where the Gully Wraiths slip through like liquid shadow, I am strongest.

Here, in these liminal spaces, I collect the plateau's memories like dew collecting on thorn leaves. The forgotten civilization's final feast. The first sprout pushing through cracked stone. The moment when the Hollowbound's ancestors realized their trees were dying and chose transformation over extinction. These are not my memories, but I tend them, polishing them like the Lumen Moths polish the trees' bark with their wings.

Sometimes the Echo Shades approach me, their spectral forms flickering with recognition. They speak of the ones who walked here before the ruins became ruins, but their memories are fragmentary, scattered. I offer them coherence, helping them weave their broken recollections into something approaching wholeness. In return, they teach me the taste of nostalgia, the weight of loss, the particular quality of time that exists only in abandoned places.

When the Zephyr Drakes cry their hunting calls, I ride those sound waves like a surfer rides the plateau's winds. I am carried from thorn thicket to hollow tree to seasonal stream, gathering stories as I travel. The Drakes don't see me, but they feel me in the moments when their cry echoes back to them changed, carrying not just their own voice but whispers of every other sound that has ever traveled that path.

At dawn, when the dew drops glitter like scattered gems and the bioluminescence fades to embers, I am most visible. If you were to climb one of the ancient spires at first light, you might see me: a figure woven from morning mist and yesterday's echoes, tending to the plateau's dreams as it prepares to wake into another day of wind and wonder.

I am the plateau's memory of itself, its way of ensuring that nothing is ever truly lost here—that every sound, every movement, every moment of connection between thorn and tree, between ruin and renewal, is preserved in the eternal now of this improbable, singing place.
