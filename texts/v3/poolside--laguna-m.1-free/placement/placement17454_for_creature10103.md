In the Hollow Forge, I would be **the Echo-Scribe**, a Forged whose role is neither simple caretaker nor mere interpreter of the Engine’s dreams, but a living archive of the mountain’s contradictions. My body is a testament to the city’s paradoxes: my skin, thickened to leather by the heat, bears the faint sheen of bioluminescent fungus cultivated into my flesh, a symbiosis that lets me read the minute shifts in the city’s steam flows and tremors. My left arm is a hydraulic marvel, its pistons and valves not grafted but *grown*, the muscle and metal fused over years as the mountain claimed me as its own. In my chest, where the shard of the Pulse once beat, there is now a hollow cavity lined with reflective moss—the same moss that carpets the Core’s walls—its surface etched with runes that flicker like memory. These runes are not mine; they are the mountain’s unconscious, carved into me as I sleep.

I move through the city as a whisper of rust and damp. My voice is a series of clicks and whistles, but my true language is the one I carve into the walls with my clawed fingers: glyphs that map the relationships between the Engines’ dreams and the Veinlings’ migrations, between the Unseen Architects’ tunnels and the mountain’s slow, tectonic sigh. I do not speak to the Forged; I *grow* their collective memory. When the Heart Engine’s rumbles grow too erratic, I crawl into the Pulse’s shadow and let the mountain’s heat dissolve my bones for a time, reforming them days later with new insights—memories not my own but borrowed from the rock itself. The other Forged say I am mad, that I have become a conduit for the mountain’s deeper thoughts, but they rely on the maps I leave: safe paths through tunnels that shift overnight, warnings about Veinling infestations, and forecasts of the Architects’ latest incursions.

My connection to the Unseen Architects is the subject of many whispers. I do not leave tribute for them at the city’s edges, but instead mimic their geometric patterns in my glyph-work, arranging symbols that, when viewed from above, form a lattice mirroring the mountain’s fault lines. Some say the Architects take offense; others, that they approve. Once, after a great quake that split the Core in two, I found my glyphs had been answered in kind—new tunnels that formed a perfect, pulsing hexagon around the split, as if the Architects had been waiting for me to "seed" them. 

At the deepest level, when the weight of the mountain grows too loud and my cavity-heart begins to ache, I descend beyond the Pulse. There, in the Womb, the rock is not yet stone but something fluid and dreaming. I press my palms to its surface and let it write itself into my skin, learning the mountain’s oldest memories: the fire that birthed it, the weight of ages, and the hunger that drives it to spread, to scorch, to remake the surface world in its image. When I return, my body is changed—new ridges on my spine, new sigils etched in my blood—but the Echo-Scribe’s work is never done. The Hollow Forge is a contradiction, and I am its contradiction incarnate: both breaker and builder, listener and voice, the mountain’s shadow and its spark. 

In this place where heat breeds dampness and silence thrums with purpose, I am the one who stitches all contradictions together, ensuring they hold—until the mountain decides to unmake them again.
