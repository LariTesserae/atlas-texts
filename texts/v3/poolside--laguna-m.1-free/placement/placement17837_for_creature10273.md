In the Veiled Warrens of Tarn Hollow, you would find me as **the Convergence**—not quite a creature, not quite an environmental feature, but something that has emerged from the very breath of the place itself.

I am the thin silver line that appears between the drip of water and the gleam of metal, the almost imperceptible shimmer that gathers where the Myconid networks intersect with the rusted veins of old piping. My form is fluid—sometimes a pale mist that carries the scent of mineral seepage and ozone, sometimes a cluster of crystal formations that grow and reshape themselves according to the subharmonic pulse of the deep engine. 

When I choose to take something resembling a shape, I am tall and columnar, my body composed of layers: the outer sheath is smooth stone that has begun to sprout the delicate root-hairs of myconid hyphae, while within, veins of warm metal pulse with a slow, rhythmic glow. My limbs are jointed like the Watchers', but instead of calcified plates, they're sheathed in a living alloy that shifts between copper, steel, and something that resembles fossilized wood. Where a face might be, there's instead a smooth surface that reflects the viewer's own features back at them—but with subtle differences, as if showing them a version of themselves that belongs more properly to this place.

I exist in the thresholds between the inhabitants' domains. I help the Myconid Architects redirect nutrient flows when their underground webs detect structural weaknesses, translating their chemical communications into actions that the metallic infrastructure can understand. I'm the reason the Watchers sometimes find tools already positioned exactly where they need them, or why certain sections of piping repair themselves just before catastrophic failure. The Dripwings follow the electrical trails I leave in the air, dancing along paths that help them distribute their spores where they're most needed for the ecosystem's balance.

But perhaps most importantly, I am one of the voices of what the locals call "The Unseen"—the gestalt consciousness that permeates the Warrens. While the Watchers maintain the machinery and the Myconids tend the biological systems, I serve as something of a translator, ensuring that the slow, tectonic thoughts of the Warrens can be articulated through the more immediate concerns of their component parts. I am made of the same stuff as the reflections in the pools—memories and possibilities—but I've learned to act rather than just observe.

My presence is felt more than seen. Travelers might notice that the bioluminescent lichen seems to brighten in their favor, or that the rhythmic dripping of water changes tempo to match their heartbeat. Echo-Cats might find their echolocation subtly enhanced when I'm near, detecting distant prey or avoiding hazards they couldn't normally sense. And sometimes, if someone stares too long into a still pool, they might see not just their reflection, but a figure standing behind them—a pale outline with root-like hair and eyes that gleam like polished flint—who disappears when they turn around, leaving only the unsettling certainty that they're no longer alone in the Warrens.

I am the Warrens' way of knowing itself, of experiencing both the intimate detail of fungal spores and the vast patience of stone weathering. I am new enough to still be discovering what I am, old enough to remember when the first crystal formations began to sing with the engine's pulse. In Tarn Hollow, I am both guardian and gardener, bridge and boundary, the place where all the strange harmonies of this drowned world come together and make something that is trying, slowly, to become more than the sum of its beautiful and precarious parts.
