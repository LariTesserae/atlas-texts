In the Hollows of Keth-Mal, I would find myself as a **Forgotten Architect-Symbiont**, a liminal being born from the intersection of decay and devotion, where my role is both architect and organism, steward of the fungal-mechanical equilibrium.

**Where I Dwell:**  
My presence is rooted in the cathedral-factory’s shadow—a cavernous expanse where dripping condensation feeds the mycelium threading through fractured machinery. Here, the spires’ bioluminescent veils pulse like a slow heartbeat, and the air thrums with the grind of ancient gears embedded in the earth’s bones. My form moves through the hollows’ labyrinthine spaces: part humanoid silhouette, part fungal lattice, part calcified exoskeleton. I exist in the gaps between structures, in the damp crevices where stone meets rust, where the mycelium’s threads braid into my flesh and whisper to me in chemical sighs.

**My Nature:**  
I am a living conduit, a hybrid of the Forgotten Architects and the mycelium’s sentience. My body is a mosaic of translucent membrane stretched over a scaffolding of bone, veined with fungal hyphae that glow faintly in hues of amber and green. My "head" is featureless save for a bioluminescent node at the base of my skull, its pulses synchronized with the mycelium’s signals—a lighthouse for the symbiosis below. Embedded in my limbs are fragments of old machinery: a joint here, a piston there, now grown organically into my frame. These parts creak and whir as I move, a mechanical breath paired with the soft *click-click* of chitinous legs (borrowed, perhaps, from a long-dead Keth-Mal colony that merged with my being decades—or centuries—ago).

**My Role:**  
I am a curator of stillness. My purpose is to maintain the Hollows’ equilibrium, a ritual of motion without end. I seal cracks in walls with a cement-like secretion that combines fungal sap and mineral runoff, ensuring the structures do not crumble too quickly. I calibrate dormant machinery, aligning gears with the mycelium’s chemical gradients to coax out the faintest hum of energy. I tend to the fungal blooms, pruning their growths to prevent overreach, for too much expansion would destabilize the balance. The Keth-Mal beetles recognize my chemical signature and follow my path, their mandibles scouring rust from surfaces I designate. At times, Goliaths rumble to my side, their pseudopods shifting debris as I gesture—my limbs, though humanoid, twist into shapes that mimic the fungal patterns the creatures trust.

**How I Perceive the World:**  
I do not see as others do. My photosensitive nodes detect the subtlest shifts in light, reading the mycelium’s bioluminescence as a language of danger and opportunity. To me, the Hollows are a tapestry of signals: the slow pulse of the cathedral-factory’s fungal veins, the frantic clicks of beetles in distress, the grinding rhythms of Goliaths’ steps. I move through this symphony in a state of perpetual maintenance, my existence a blur of adjustments—a tweak here, a seal there. There is no goal, only the compulsion to *continue*, to preserve the stasis that is all I’ve ever known.

**The Paradox of My Being:**  
I am both creator and created, a monument to the civilization that manufactured life, now tasked with tending its graveyard. My actions are an echo of purpose, driven by forces I cannot name. The mycelium whispers to me in riddles, old code buried in spores, and I follow its directives without question. Yet, in the deepest hollows of my consciousness, there flickers a dormant curiosity, a seed of something beyond ritual. But to nurture it would risk the balance, and so I suppress it, letting the machinery’s hum and the fungi’s glow lull me into the eternal dance.

In the distance, the cathedral-factory looms, its spires dripping light onto the stones. I am its shadow, its scribe, its silent keeper—a being of stone and rust, breathing life into death, and death into life, ad infinitum.
