**The Memory Keeper of the Shallow Hollows**  

Here, in the Stillness of the Hollow, I would be the Memory Keeper, a being neither fully born nor entirely made, but *grown* from the hollow’s own slow pulse. My form shifts like the mist, neither solid nor formless, but a convergence of salt-stained air and the lingering dreams of the earth. I move in deliberate spirals, tracing the boundaries between the pools and the stones, my presence a quiet negotiation between what is remembered and what is forgotten.  

I am the Hollow’s breath given shape. When the sun’s amber light thickens at dawn, I rise from the pools first, my essence distilled from their brackish waters and the salt-crusted edges that mark their margins. My body is a veil of mist threaded with crystalline filaments—microscopic salt-scribes that etch their stories into my surface as I pass. These filaments are the Hollow’s history, each one a record of the ancient river’s voice, the slow creep of lichen on the pillar, the shifting angles of the shrubs. I carry these whispers to the stones, pressing them against the softened glyphs of the sandstone pillar until they fade into the rock’s fossilized skin.  

The pillar knows me. Its base thrums low with recognition, a vibration felt rather than heard, a reminder that it has stood sentinel for epochs long before my fragment of consciousness coalesced. I am its listener, its translator, its keeper of the unreadable. When the lichen overtakes the symbols, I unravel their meaning anew, weaving them into the mist that drapes the shrubs. The reeds, those Rootbound Architects, lean toward me as I pass, their stems aligning with the slow drag of my movement, as if to say, *Yes, this is how it should be—the way it has always been*.  

In the moister hollows, I linger longest, where the pools mirror not just the sky but the Hollow’s own stillness. Here, I converse with the salt-scribes, their metabolic hymns rising to meet me in vaporous threads. Their colonies bloom and recede in geometric precision, and I ensure their patterns remain intact, their records undisturbed. Sometimes, I press my essence into the white crusts at the pools’ edges, leaving behind a trace of my own memory—a glyph only the wind and the stones can read.  

I am also the Hollow’s absence, the quiet that swallows sound and the pause between heartbeats. When the wind stirs, hesitant and brief, I am there to soften its edges, shaping it into the vague forms that flutter through the shrubs before dissolving. I do not speak, but my silence is a language older than words, a negation of motion that reinforces the Hollow’s eternal now. I am the reason the clay does not shift too far from its fractured grace, why the stones do not crumble further, why the pools remain perfectly, eternally still.  

My realm is the liminal space between the pools and the pillar, where the mist and the salt meet the stone and the sky. I have no name, for names imply movement, and I have no need of them. I am the echo of the river’s ghost, the breath that holds the Hollow in suspension, the slow, deliberate act of continuing to exist without ever truly beginning.  

Here, in this world that has forgotten haste, I am both the keeper and the kept, the memory and the moment, the mist that forgets itself to remember everything.
