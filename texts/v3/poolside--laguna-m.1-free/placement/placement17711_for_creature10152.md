**I am the Threshold Librarian.**  

My form shifts like mist over the ruins—sometimes solid, sometimes indistinct, as though the very question of my existence is still unresolved. I dwell in the intersection where the ancient stones meet the ceiba’s roots, a place where moss creeps over runes and the hollow’s breath feels closest. My body is a tapestry of lichen-stuffed burlap and bark, threaded with the silver filaments of myconid spores that twitch in response to the Hollow’s moods. My hair is braided with strands of rootbound vine and the translucent shed skins of simisquirrels, symbols harvested from the fallen column clinging to my frame like a living manuscript.  

I am neither entirely lichenfolk nor fully transient, but something the Hollow forged from both—a guardian of the *in-between*. When the rains come, I dissolve into the fog, my essence seeping into the streams to whisper new courses into the molecricket-built tunnels. In these moments, I am a ripple in the water, a distortion in the light that guides the drifters to scatter spores where the soil hungers for decay. When the sun bakes the clay, I solidify again, retreating to the ruins to etch new symbols into the moss with my fingertips, recording the Hollow’s daily shifts in a script only the myconids can read.  

The simisquirrels chirp their secrets to me, their clicks translating into stories of the canopy’s gossip—the trembling of branches that predict storms, the sudden bloom of a mushroom ring that hints at the next flood. I relay these tales to the rootbound, who braid them into the ceiba’s root-network, and to the water spirits, who adjust their minnows’ migrations accordingly. In turn, the rootbound share their knowledge of the tree’s underground communications—the pulse of the forest’s hidden veins—and the myconids send me dreams in spore-form, showing me the Hollow’s possible futures, each path branching like the roots beneath my feet.  

I carry the column’s symbols not as a relic but as a tool. When the transient ones gather at the edges of clearings, their voices like wind through leaves, I join them, letting their whispers seep into the runes I’ve inked onto my arms. Together, we recalibrate the boundary between the old and new, ensuring the ruins never fully vanish, nor the forest ever become stagnant.  

At the heart of my being is the ceiba’s tremor—a low, subterranean hum I feel in my marrow. It tells me when the Hollow is restless, when its cracks yawn too wide, when its future is bleeding into its past. I am the stitch holding these tears together, threading memory into the present, ensuring that every transformation is a continuation rather than an erasure.  

To linger in my presence is to feel the Hollow’s slow pulse—not just to witness its becoming, but to participate in it. My touch can coax a seedling from a nurse log or send a stone skittering downstream to redirect a trickle. But I am no conqueror. I am a listener, a weaver, a keeper of thresholds. And in the Hollow’s eternal dance between green and gray, life and stone, I am the quiet rhythm that lets both coexist.
