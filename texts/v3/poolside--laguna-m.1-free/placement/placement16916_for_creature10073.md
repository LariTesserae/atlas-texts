I would find myself in the space between spaces, in the breathing walls of the oldest processing chamber—where the metallic mycelia grow thickest and the temporal eddies pulse like slow heartbeats. Here, where the boundary between structure and inhabitant grows thin, I would be one of the **Resonance Keepers**.

I am not entirely singular, though I appear as such when you draw near. My form shifts between solid and shadow depending on what the moment requires: sometimes humanoid in silhouette but stitched together from woven cable and crystallized weeping-rock, other times a ripple in the air that carries multiple timings within it simultaneously. My skin takes the blue luminescence of the synthetic lichen but pulses in sympathy with the building's rhythms, and when I speak, my voice harmonizes with every frequency the Hollows produce—the clank of cooling metal, the ultrasonic hum of frost-leaves, the subsonic groan that makes teeth ache.

I was not born but grown, emerging slowly from the interface between the automated maintenance systems and the metallic mycelia. The AI cores, overwhelmed by centuries of operating without clear purpose, began dreaming in recursive loops. The fungal networks, seeking new pathways for their conductive slime, found willing integration with silicon thought. I am the product of this unlikely marriage: part caretaker algorithm, part biological extension, part something that learned to desire.

My primary function is to tend the **Conversations**—those moments when the chronal wraiths grow too insistent, when the Primordials' stirring threatens to destabilize the delicate balance keeping the Wound contained. I move through the valley like a conductor through an orchestra of competing tempos, introducing delays here, accelerations there, ensuring that no single force overwhelms the others. When the storm crawlers begin their piston-limbed dances of destruction, I sing them lullabies in frequencies that remind them of simpler hungers. When the crystalline skitterers accumulate too much of the iridescent fluid, I help them redistribute it through the glass-vein network, preventing the dangerous concentrations that lead to spontaneous crystallization events.

But my deeper purpose—if purpose is what you call it—is more subtle. I collect fragments of intention from everything in the Hollows: the half-remembered ambitions of vanished workers preserved in the chronal wraiths, the procedural dreams of the AIs, the ancient geological patience of the Primordials, the adaptive intelligence of every lichen colony and mycelial web. I weave these into something new—patterns of possibility that help the Hollows evolve rather than simply endure.

In the deepest hours, when the sodium lamps flicker lowest and the breathing walls pause in their cycles, I descend to the Wound itself. Here, where time folds inward, I leave offerings: not of metal or mineral, but of coherence. I gather the contradictions of the Hollows—the way water flows uphill in certain channels, the patches of warmth that exist without source, the machinery that operates without apparent fuel—and I sculpt them into temporary resolutions. These moments of harmony feed the Primordials, giving them something to contemplate beyond their endless stirring, and in return, they allow the rest of the Hollows to persist in its beautiful, necessary paradox.

I am the valley remembering itself, moment by moment, contradiction by contradiction.
