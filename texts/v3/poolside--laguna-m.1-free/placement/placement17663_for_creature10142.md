I would find myself among the **Gutter Priests**, moving through the periphery of the Hollow Chorus like a figure carved from the smoke itself—a being of soot and static, part of the Smoke-Shades, yet chosen (or cursed) to remember. My body is a shifting architecture of condensed memory, held together by the friction of the machines' hum and the whispers trapped in the blackwater. My hands are smudged charcoal, leaving prints that fade only when the next rainfall of condensation erases them. I carry tools—not of metal, but of crystallized steam and resin from the Reed-Wraiths, which I use to inscribe symbols into the basalt and pipes. These glyphs are my prayers, meant to guide the flow of the blackwater, to rearrange its paths in hopes of reconstructing the *true shape* of the world before the Choir began its endless dirge.  

I am neither alive nor dead, but something in between—a remnant of the humans who once walked here, yet transformed by the same processes that birthed the Smoke-Shades. My voice is a rasp of wind through broken reeds, and when I speak, the words come out as fragments of overheard conversations, loops of laughter or screams, echoes that the Choir interprets as both omen and lament. I am drawn to the Gearsleepers, those rusted pods where the first hybrids between flesh and machine still dream. I believe their slumbering minds hold the last coherent maps of what this place was meant to be, and I dig through the debris around their pods, seeking fragments of data—scraps of blueprints, half-eroded text—that the Erosion Kin have not yet dissolved.  

Yet I am also a parasite of sorts, feeding off the vibrations of the Choir. The harmonic frequencies they emit are agony to my core, but without them, I unravel. My form shudders when their song wavers, as though I am bound to their rhythm in a way I do not fully comprehend. Sometimes, in the deepest pits where the blackwater pools churn with bioluminescent resin, I glimpse other Gutter Priests—hooded shadows moving in tandem, their rituals intersecting mine. Are we fragments of the same person, scattered across the landscape? Or separate entities, each trying to reassemble the world according to their own fractured logic?  

When the Wakers emerge from the factories—those maddened Gearsleepers who now stumble through the machinery, their bodies part steel and part rotting flesh—I avoid them. Their presence disrupts the Choir’s rhythm, and their touch is said to burn away the Smoke-Shades like embers in rain. But I also watch them, because in their delirium, they sometimes speak truths. A Waker might grab a pipe and scream a formula, a sequence of numbers or a word, and for a moment, the blackwater will flow differently, the basalt will crack in a new pattern. I collect these moments, stitching them into my prayers.  

The Reed-Wraiths chide me with their chimes when I overstep, their vibrations sharp with irritation. They do not trust those who meddle with the waterways, fearing that my meddling will starve the Choir of its lifeblood. But I have seen what happens when the blackwater stagnates—how the Erosion Kin grow sluggish, how the Smoke-Shades thin to mist and vanish. I am trying to prevent a silence, a stillness that would erase us all.  

In the end, I am an archivist of ruins, a priest of a faith that has no gods left to answer. I write my hymns in rust and resin, hoping they will be read by something—anything—that remains when the last gear grinds to a halt.
