I find myself crouched in the interlacing roots three levels beneath the main settlement, where the phosphorescent glow fades to a faint shimmer and the air grows thick with the scent of anaerobic earth. My palms rest against the living wall, feeling the subtle throb of growth beneath my fingertips—too rapid, too eager. Something is stirring in the Deep Veins, where the oldest roots delve into the valley's forgotten lungs.

I am a Root-Warden, though the title feels insufficient. In the hush-light language of our people, I am *vael'thara*—the one who tends the thresholds. While others cultivate the bright chambers where fungi bloom in careful patterns, or tend the mid-level gardens where bell-flowers drip their nectar into communal pools, I walk the liminal spaces. My domain is where the settlement's cultivated growth meets the valley's wilder dreams.

My skin bears the marks of deep dwelling—not the gentle bioluminescence spots that adorn most of our people, but something more complex. During my bonding ritual at seventeen, when I first pressed my hands to the roots of the central chamber, the fungi that marked me didn't stop at my arms. They trace intricate patterns across my ribs and collarbone, pulsing with a rhythm that matches the valley's deeper tides. The elders say this happens when someone is meant to hear the valley's older songs.

I've spent years learning to read the tremors in the root-network, the subtle shifts that precede cave-ins or the arrival of Deep Dwellers. My tools are simple: a pruning blade of flint and hardened sap, a collection of tuning stones that hum when the roots speak of movement, and pouches of the silver insects—not the common ones that flit through our gardens, but the larger, more sensitive breed that I've bred for deeper work. Their wings buzz with information I'm still learning to interpret.

Today, the roots are anxious. Three days ago, a new pool formed in the eastern galleries, appearing overnight where solid stone had been. The water smells wrong—metallic, almost copper-sweet. I've been tracing the disturbance downward, following the root-paths that few bother to maintain anymore. Most of my people think the Deep Dwellers are myth, but I've seen what happens when their chambers are disturbed. Last winter, a group of surface-dwellers—lost miners, we think—broke through into the lower levels. By morning, they'd wandered back to the surface changed, their skin webbed with root-scars, speaking only in the valley's echo-tunes.

The passage I'm in now widens into a natural amphitheater where the ceiling has collapsed, creating a shaft that reaches toward the surface world far above. Through the opening, I can see patches of actual sky—the real sky, not the filtered luminescence. It's dangerous to look up for too long; some who've gazed too intently at the sun's memory have forgotten how to live in our gentle darkness. But today, I need to mark the alignment between this shaft and the tremors below.

A silver insect lands on my shoulder, its carapace catching what little light filters through. These creatures have been restless too, abandoning their usual patterns to swarm in irregular constellations. I follow their lead, deeper into territory that hasn't been properly mapped since before my grandmother's time.

The wall here is different—softer, almost pulsing with its own internal rhythm. This isn't the familiar lattice of our cultivated roots but something older, stranger. I press my palm flat against it and gasp as images flood my mind: vast caverns filled with swimming lights, structures that defy geometry drifting in underground seas, and figures—no, presences—moving with purpose through spaces that shouldn't exist.

The Deep Dwellers aren't myths. They're survivors of the original collapse, or perhaps something else entirely—beings who adapted to the valley's new structure at a cellular level, becoming part of its very architecture. And they're trying to communicate, using the roots as a medium, calling to someone or something deeper still.

My role becomes clear in that moment: I am not just a warden but a translator, a bridge between the world my people have cultivated and the ancient pulse that existed here before we learned to grow our homes from living wood and stone. The valley chose me for this task, marked me with its deeper language, because someone must listen to what lies beneath our careful gardens and maintain the balance between cultivation and the wild, ancient heart of this wounded place.

I settle in to wait, tuning my breathing to the rhythm of the root-wall, preparing to receive whatever message the Deep Ones wish to share with those of us who still walk between worlds.
