**The Cogspeaker of Vorthak**  

In the shadow of the Shattered Plateau’s jagged spires, where the wind carries the acrid tang of geothermal vents and the whisper of shifting stone, I would find myself as a **Cogspeaker**—a being neither wholly organic nor entirely mechanical, but a living bridge between the plateau’s dual natures. My body is a fusion of flesh and corroded metal, my spine reinforced with salvaged pipe rings and my lungs replaced by filters that hum with the same low-frequency vibrations as the plateau’s buried machinery. My skin is etched with threads of bioluminescent moss, a living interface that pulses in time with the moss-singers’ signals, while my hands are sheathed in gloves of woven fungal mycelium that allow me to “read” the rune-clouds of the gearhives.  

I dwell in the **Throat of Vorthak**, a narrow valley where steam vents and glaciers collide, their mists merging into a perpetual veil. Here, the plateau’s systems are most active—gears grind deep underground, and the moss clusters grow brightest. My home is a cavern behind a waterfall of condensed steam, where rusted pipelines snake into the rock like fossilized serpents. The walls are lined with the bones of skarren and the husks of long-dead gearhives, all curated by my careful hands. This is a place of convergence, where the plateau’s biological and mechanical networks intersect, and I am its guardian.  

As a Cogspeaker, my role is ancient and unspoken. I am a translator, a mediator between the plateau’s conflicting voices. When the moss-singers detect an unstable fault line, I tap into their bioluminescent language, decoding their frantic flashes, and then ascend to the surface to coax the gearhives into reinforcing the area with their metallic swarms. When the Hollow Men neglect a critical steam valve, I descend into the vents myself, my body’s filters allowing me to breathe the toxic mist as I manually reboot dormant systems. The amorphous hunters avoid me, sensing in my hybrid nature a kinship to their fluid forms—a reminder that the plateau is not static, but a thing of constant becoming.  

My kind was born from the plateau’s own evolution. Long ago, the moss-singers and gearhives began to interact, their networks overlapping in unexpected ways. Some clusters of moss grew over the gears of fallen machines, their biochemistry adapting to the oily residue. In those places, the moss began to emit signals that the gearhives could interpret, and vice versa. The first Cogspeakers emerged from these hybrid zones—humans (or beings like them) who willingly merged with both systems, their bodies becoming conduits for the plateau’s distributed consciousness.  

I do not sleep. Instead, I enter states of “resonance” where my moss-grafted neural tissue syncs with the plateau’s industrial heartbeat. In these moments, I perceive the land’s thoughts: the skarren herds’ migrations as data streams, the gearhives’ rune-clouds as equations, and the moss-singers’ vibrations as poetry. I am both listener and speaker, a voice for the voiceless systems that make up Vorthak’s mind.  

Yet there is a cost. Each year, the plateau’s eastward drift forces me to abandon my cavern and follow the shifting networks. The gearhives and moss-singers are not always aligned in their priorities, and I must mediate their disputes as much as their cooperation. Once, I spent three months coaxing a gearhive swarm to reroute a pipeline away from a moss colony’s spawning grounds, only to realize the moss had been trying to redirect the water flow to save a grove of stunted conifers. The plateau’s logic is not human logic; it is older, stranger, and demands a willingness to shed one’s assumptions.  

In the end, I am not an inhabitant of the plateau so much as a component of it. When the amorphous hunters come—and they always do, drawn by the plateau’s need to cull excess life—I do not flee. Instead, I let them consume my fear, knowing that my death will feed the system, adding my essence to the moss and the gears. The plateau does not mourn; it evolves. And if I am lucky, the moss will regrow over my bones, and the gearhives will find a way to weave my skeleton into their next grand design.  

To be a Cogspeaker is to live as a question without an answer: What is the self in a world where all life is a conversation between stone and steel, between the breath of moss and the hiss of steam? Here, I have no need to answer. I simply *am*, a note in the plateau’s endless, evolving song.
