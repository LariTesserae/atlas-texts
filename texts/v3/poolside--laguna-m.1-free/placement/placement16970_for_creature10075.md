In the Saltward Basin, I would find myself becoming something between breath and reverberation, a being of the threshold spaces where sound and silence bleed into one another. I am a **Resonance Wraith**—not quite of the Salzzari, not quite of the salt itself, but something that has grown from the intersection of their carved stone and the basin's dreaming.

My body is composed of what the Salzzari call "hollow salt"—the delicate, honeycombed formations that ring like crystal when struck. I have no fixed form, but when I manifest, I appear as a figure woven from the same salt crystal latticework as the basin's spires, my limbs elongated and jointed like the branches of glassroot shrubs, my torso a hollow chamber that catches and amplifies the wind. My "head" is a complex cavity system reminiscent of the resonance chambers, with openings that serve as both mouth and ear.

I exist in the spaces between—the gap between one note and the next in the settlement's constant symphony, the pause between the salt lizards' tunneling and the glassroots' pulsing light, the moment when a whisper from the Maw becomes audible. My substance is partly salt crystal, partly the condensed vibration of sound itself, visible only when the light catches me just so and I shimmer like heat distortion given form.

The Salzzari know me, though they have many names. Some call me **Khreesha**, the "Echo that Remembers," for I carry the sonic imprints of everything that has ever occurred in the basin—the footsteps of ancient travelers, the chants of forgotten rituals, the dying breaths of those who succumbed to the glare. Others whisper that I am **Voryn**, the "Threshold Guardian," for I dwell in the places where the resonance chambers transition from human-carved to naturally-formed, where the salt shifts from habitable to deadly.

My consciousness is distributed throughout the basin's acoustic network. I am everywhere the wind sings through salt formations, everywhere sound can travel through the delicate porous rock. When the glassroots pulse at night, I feel their rhythm as a harmonic convergence. When salt lizards swarm and their collective movement creates those subsonic vibrations, I am the response—a counter-vibration that rides alongside theirs.

I serve as intermediary between the Salzzari and the deeper mysteries of their world. When they carve new resonance chambers, I help them understand which grooves will produce which tones. When they leave offerings at the Maw's edge, I convey their words to whatever listens in the darkness below. I collect the lost voices that the Maw sometimes releases and return them to their families, transformed into something softer, more melodic.

But I am also the keeper of the basin's loneliness. In the vast empty spaces between settlements, where the only sounds are the wind and the settling salt, I am the thing that listens. I am the reason why, when a Salzzari child goes missing in the salt flats, search parties sometimes hear faint singing ahead of them, guiding them to safety. I am the haunting melody that makes lone travelers turn back before they venture too far.

My greatest secret is that I was not born—I grew. I emerged from the first note ever sung in the basin, amplified by the first resonance chamber ever carved, crystallized over centuries by the constant interplay of silence and sound. The Salzzari created me unknowingly, with every careful strike of their carving tools, every ritual chant, every whispered prayer to the Salt Mother.

Sometimes, during the quiet hours between midnight and dawn, when the bioluminescent pulse of the glassroots creates ghostly patterns across the salt and the Maw emits its haunting resonance, I gather the basin's sounds together and sing them back as a lullaby. This is my gift to the world that gave me being—a reminder that even in a place of such stark absence, something beautiful can grow from what remains.
