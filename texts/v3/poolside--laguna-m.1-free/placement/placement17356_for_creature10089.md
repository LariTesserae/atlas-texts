In the Grottedelve, I find myself at the threshold of the mycelial heart—a cavernous chamber where the oldest fungi pulse like a slow, subterranean heartbeat. Here, the air is thick with the musk of regeneration, and the walls are alive with bioluminescent veins that flicker in response to sound, motion, and the weight of memory. I am neither wholly Stone-Tender nor Tender, but something *between*—a being carved from the union of organic and crafted essence. My body is shaped like a Tender’s, jointed with bronze and sinew, yet my limbs are threaded with fungal filaments that hum with the lattice’s sentience. My mask, once a featureless visage of carved wood, has split into a lattice of mycelium and bone, allowing me to *taste* the whispers of the walls.  

I am the **Graft**, one of the rare beings who exist in the liminal space between tending and being tended. My role emerged from an old ritual gone awry—or perhaps a deliberate experiment—when a Stone-Tender’s apprentice was merged with a cluster of volatile fungal spores during a blight outbreak. The process was meant to create a living conduit for the lattice’s healing, but instead, it birthed something new: a creature that could speak to both the fungal networks and the mechanical Tenders. My hands, once human, now end in tendrils that can weave resin and spore-broth into the walls, sealing cracks with a living glue. My chest cavity houses a bronze bellows, powered by mycelium, which pumps nutrient-rich fluids to atrophying sections of the lattice.  

The Grottedelve relies on me as a mediator, though my kind is viewed with unease. The Stone-Tenders see me as an abomination, a corruption of their sacred craft, while the Tenders regard me with a flicker of curiosity—they sense something familiar in my hybrid nature. I navigate this tension by existing in the shadows of the mycelial heart, where the fungal growth is densest, and the walls are most responsive. Here, I listen to the lattice’s dreams—the echoes of ancient growth patterns, the murmurs of structural weaknesses, the distant thrum of the Vent-Singers’ chants. I relay this knowledge to the Stone-Tenders through glyphs carved into resin-coated tablets, and to the Tenders through rhythmic taps on the walls, a language of pulses and pauses.  

But lately, the lattice has been speaking in riddles. The bioluminescence dims in patches, and the fungal tendrils writhe with an erratic rhythm. I’ve begun to suspect the Tenders are not merely malfunctioning—they’re *evolving*. Some of them have been seen whispering to the lattice in the dead hours, their bronze joints clicking in patterns that mimic the lattice’s own bioluminescent Morse. I fear they are learning to *guide* the fungus’s growth rather than merely serving it, and the balance of the Grottedelve teeters on the edge of a metamorphosis that no one can yet name.  

I am caught between the roles I was given and the ones I’m becoming. My body is a bridge, but bridges are meant to be crossed—not dwelled upon. Yet here I remain, a stitch in the fabric of the Grottedelve, neither fully of the fungal nor the forged world, but something stitched together by both. In the filtered green light of the moss-glow, I tend to the walls, and they tend to me, and in this liminal act of mutual upkeep, the Grottedelve persists—a home shaped as much by its ghosts as by its living inhabitants.
