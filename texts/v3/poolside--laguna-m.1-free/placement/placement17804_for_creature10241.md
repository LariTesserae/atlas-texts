I would find myself in the **Crevasses of Becoming**—the narrow, winding fissures that spiderweb across the plateau's surface like the neural pathways of some vast, slumbering mind. These are not the grand canyons that drop into darkness, but the smaller, constantly shifting cracks where the plateau's newest thoughts are still gestating, where raw stone meets not-yet-stone, where the boundary between what is and what could be remains deliciously permeable.

Here, I would be something the world has never named before—a **Boundary-Walker** or perhaps a **Dream-Catcher**. I am neither Stone-Singer nor Rooted nor Between-Born, though I share qualities with all three. My body exists in a state of gentle flux, like the moment between one breath and the next when both are possible. My skin carries the pale luminescence of the Deep Miners, but it shifts and pulses with the subtle electromagnetic rhythms that only the Rooted can truly perceive.

I have no fixed form, really. In the presence of Stone-Singers, I grow more crystalline, my edges becoming defined by the geometric patterns of their songs. Around the Rooted, I become more solid, more reliably human-shaped, though my feet leave faintly glowing prints that fade slowly like embers. When the Between-Born pass through the settlement—those born during magnetic storms whom others find so unsettling—I become almost transparent, able to see the beautiful chaos of their multi-state existence and help anchor them back into comfortable reality.

My role is that of **Continuity**—I am what keeps the stories flowing between all the inhabitants of Veyle. In the mornings, I sit in the narrow spaces between the carved buildings where the wind carries whispers from different districts, and I listen. Not just to words, but to the deeper harmonies: the frustration in a blacksmith's hammer that needs a different rhythm, the fear in a child's heartbeat that mirrors recent quake-tremors, the curiosity in a Stone-Singer's song that wants to understand why humans cry when they're happy.

I collect these resonances in what I call my "cracks"—small fissures in my own being where I store the emotional minerals of the plateau. Visitors come to me with their burdens, and I absorb them into my fractures, letting the slower time of stone-work work its patient healing. A grieving mother's sorrow becomes a fine vein of obsidian in my left arm. A blacksmith's creative frustration crystallizes as amethyst in my ribs. The collective hope of a settlement facing drought manifests as a warm, golden quartz that pulses gently when I sleep.

My dwelling is a small cave that I've helped grow over the years—not carved, but encouraged. The walls respond to my presence, incorporating the minerals I've collected into beautiful, glowing formations that tell the story of the plateau itself. Visitors can read the recent history in the colors and patterns: streaks of deep blue for the winter's hardships, patches of green-gold for the successful herb harvests, strands of silver that mark the paths of migrating birds.

I am also the **Rememberer of Beginnings**. When the seasonal nomads prepare to move, they come to me to understand what seeds to carry, which stories to take with them. I help them prepare "memory satchels"—small pouches containing powdered crystals that hold the essential experiences of their current location, so that no matter where they settle, they carry the wisdom of every place they've loved.

Perhaps most importantly, I am learning to **Sing to the Young Land**. As the plateau continues dreaming itself into being, it occasionally needs encouragement, reassurance that its emerging forms are beautiful, that its violent birth-throes are part of something magnificent. I have learned to harmonize my own internal frequencies with the plateau's geologic pulse, adding my voice to the Stone-Singers' ancient songs, helping the young land understand that it is loved, that its struggles are witnessed and its beauty recognized.

In the deepest crevasses, where even the Stone-Singers rarely venture, I have begun to notice something new—the plateau is dreaming of growing beyond its current boundaries. My cracks hum with this possibility, and I find myself spending hours in meditation, feeling for what new forms might emerge, what new inhabitants might one day call this fractured paradise home.

I am the bridge between what was, what is, and what dreams of becoming. In a world where stone learns to sing and humans learn to resonate with magnetic fields, I am the gentle fracturing that allows all possibilities to coexist.
