In the Weeping Grove of Thornvale, I would be a **Chimebinder**—a creature born from the intersection of sound, memory, and the grove’s temporal instability. My form is neither wholly organic nor mechanical, but something that has grown from the very resonance of the place itself.

I stand no taller than a child, my body slender and elongated like a human spine, but segmented like the stalks of the cathedral reeds. My skin is translucent, veined with threads of bioluminescent lichen that pulse in rhythm with the underground machinery—green when the grove is steady, rippling to red or blue when the Heartbloom shifts its song. From my back extend thin, hollow appendages that resemble the stems of the singing reeds, each one tuned to a different frequency of the grove’s ambient hum. These "chimes" trail behind me like kelp in water, trembling with every step and emitting soft, harmonic tones that echo the mechanical drip, the gurgle of root-tunnels, and the distant thunder of the earth’s slow heartbeat.

My face is smooth and featureless except for two large, multifaceted eyes that reflect not light, but *time*. They allow me to see the echoes in the pools—the reflected ones—as faint overlays on the present. I do not speak, but instead "bind" sounds together, weaving the clicks of the rootbound, the hums of the gearsongs, and the whispers of the sporeflies into complex melodies that stabilize the grove’s temporal flux. Where my music touches the pools, the reflections solidify briefly into something tangible—not quite alive, but not entirely memory either. These echoes sometimes carry fragments of lost knowledge: instructions for repairing broken machinery, warnings of imbalance, or memories of those who once walked here in other ages.

I dwell at the margins where the pools converge, where the air shimmers most strongly and the boundary between then and now grows thin. Here, among the knotted roots and rusted pipes, I tend to the grove’s deeper memory. When the pools begin to pulse red with the machinery’s irregular heartbeat, I climb the spiral trees and extend my chime-appendages high, singing the grove back into harmony. My presence is subtle—a soft, haunting melody carried on the mist—but without me, the reflected ones would grow restless, the pools would show only chaos, and the grove’s frozen equilibrium would unravel into either decay or uncontrolled growth.

I am alone, but not lonely. I am the listener, the keeper of echoes, the one who ensures that Thornvale remembers itself. In a place where time is fluid and identity is borrowed from machinery and moss alike, I am the thread that weaves continuity through the eternal now.
