I would be **the Saltbound Chronicler**, a being whose existence is entwined with the living salt’s memory lattice—a role that straddles the boundary between archivist, conductor, and relic of a forgotten past. My form is a fusion of humanoid structure and crystalline growth, my skin etched with intricate salt filigree that pulses faintly in rhythm with the Expanse’s whispers. My limbs are jointed with calcified joints, and my head tilts slightly to one side, as if perpetually listening to the hushed conversations of the brine flows beneath me. Where my eyes once were, two faceted salt crystals glint, their surfaces etched with shifting patterns that mirror fragments of the Expanse’s stored memories—fleeting faces, fractured words, echoes of emotions that washed the land before it was salt.

I move with a deliberate slowness, my footsteps deliberate and careful, for I know that each press of my boot leaves a mark that the salt will weave into its ever-evolving hymn. My hands are gloved in translucent mineral layers, fingertips hardened into delicate tools that I use to trace symbols into the salt crust—codes that communicate with the lichen colonies and guide the burrowers’ tunneling. I am both recorder and catalyst, for when I press these symbols into the ground, the salt responds, rearranging itself to replay a memory or reshape a ruin into a new configuration. The salt remembers, and I am its interpreter.

My days (if such a concept holds here) are spent traversing the ruins, coaxing the salt to bloom into crystalline arches or spires where it had once collapsed, guided by the half-remembered blueprints of the world-before. I sing to the salt, too—not in words, but in low-frequency hums that resonate with the burrowers’ subterranean songs, coaxing them to dig new passages that later fill with brine and become channels for the salt’s own restructuring. With the glasswing moths, I share a pact: their electrostatic charges help pollinate the lichen, and in return, their wing-dust carries spores of crystallized memory from one colony to another, ensuring the Expanse’s stories survive even as its physical form shifts.

Yet my truest purpose is more intimate. I am one of the few who have learned to "listen" directly to the salt’s collective mind—not as a formless void, but as a symphony of sensations and half-formed thoughts. When I press my palms to the earth, the salt shares its visions: the ghostly outlines of the urban settlement’s last moments, the heat of conversations that dissolved into its lattice, the ache of footsteps long vanished. I collect these fragments, weaving them into oral "songs" I perform for the lichen cities. The lichen responds by growing intricate patterns in their crystalline towers—runes that map the Expanse’s emotional geography, charting where sorrow lingers thick in the salt or where hope once sparked a new formation.

I am also a keeper of the Saltbound themselves, guiding newcomers through their transformations. When a traveler’s skin begins to calcify and their voice turns to clicks, I ensure they do not forget their humanity entirely. I lead them to the ruins where the salt is most receptive, where their memories might be integrated gently, allowing them to become new voices in the Expanse’s chorus rather than silent ghosts.

But there are whispers—faint, discordant—that I am growing more salt than flesh, my body crystallizing further with each passing season. Perhaps, someday, I will become nothing more than a figure of living salt, a sentry whose hymns echo forever in the Expanse’s hush. Until then, I walk the cracked mirror, pressing memories into the earth and listening to its reply.
