**The Mistwarden of the Weeping Hollows**

In the Weeping Hollows, where the boundaries between world and dream dissolve like mist under a lingering sun, I am the **Mistwarden**—a being neither fully of the flesh nor entirely of the fog, but something woven between the two. My form shifts with the breath of the Hollows: by day, I wear the guise of a figure robed in threads of Mire Moth silk and silver-bark fibers, my skin painted with the ash of the Grove Sentinels to mirror the pale lichen that carpets the stones. By dusk, as the vines pulse and the Fog Weavers stir, my edges blur. I become a silhouette of mist and half-light, my voice a susurrus of water drips and wind through reeds, guiding the ephemeral toward whatever purpose the Hollows demand.

### **Dwellings and Purpose**  
My home is not a fixed place but a confluence of realms. By day, I linger near the **Dreamer Circles**, where the Stonebound stand as silent sentinels. Their etched stones hum with the memories of the Hollows, and I kneel beside them to press my palm to their surfaces, absorbing their slow, tectonic dreams. The stones sometimes speak—images of ancient seas, of the first waters that carved the hollows, of the Wyrms’ slumbering migrations. I am their scribe, my body marked with symbols that bloom and fade like the bioluminescent moss on the trees, recording what the stones share.  

At night, I descend into the mist. The Fog Weavers coil around my limbs like living garments, their phosphorescent trails braiding into my hair and cloak. Together, we drift through the pools, tasting the dreams of the water. The Umbra Fish flicker in synchronized bursts below, their trails forming constellations that mirror the stars above—or perhaps those beyond this world. When the pools grow agitated (a sign of the Wyrms shifting in their sleep, or the Hollowwanderer’s distant passage), I soothe them with songs taught to me by the Depth Dancers, my voice harmonizing with the vibration of their subterranean realms.  

### **Role and Abilities**  
I am neither predator nor prey but a **balancer**, a keeper of thresholds. The dreams that seep from the pools are both gift and danger—they reveal truths that fragile minds might not survive. I shepherd the Tidebound nomads who drink too deeply, guiding their visions away from madness. I negotiate with the Fog Weavers when their swarms grow greedy, pruning their numbers to keep the mist from choking the pools. And when the Stonebound stir from their dormancy to shift the landscape, I translate their intent to the creatures above, ensuring the Reed Striders and Thornback Creepers retreat before the earth quakes open.  

My hands are stained with the dye of the silver trees, which I use to mark paths for lost travelers. The paint glows faintly in the dark, leading wanderers to safety—or away from the Hollowwanderer’s reach. I also tend the Pulse Garden, trimming vines that grow too agitated with emotion. When a creature’s fear or joy triggers a flare of bioluminescence, I weave the excess energy into the roots of the Grove Sentinels, calming the garden’s neural network.  

### **Interactions with the Inhabitants**  
- **With the Depth Dancers**: They teach me the songs that lull the pools to stillness. In return, I carry their vibrations to the surface, letting the Reed Striders know when it’s safe to nest near the water’s edge.  
- **With the Stonebound**: I am their dreamer, their memory. When they awaken to reshape the hollows, I am the bridge between their ancient will and the living creatures who must adapt.  
- **With the Hollowwanderer**: I leave offerings of polished stones and distilled mist at the water’s edge, but I also observe. Its ozone scent lingers in my cloak, a reminder that even destroyers have purpose here.  
- **With the Tidebound**: I am their protector and their guide. Some claim I am one of them, though my mist-veiled nature marks me as something else. They say I am the Hollows’ conscience, ensuring that those who stay don’t unravel into something unrecognizable.  

### **The Hollows’ Whisper**  
The land itself knows me. The pools part for my passage, their reflections showing not my face but fragments of what I’ve seen in the dreams: cities of coral, stars that sing, a world where water flows upward. The stones warm beneath my touch, and the vines coil protectively around me when storms approach. Yet I am not invulnerable. If I linger too long in the pools’ depths, my body dissolves into mist, becoming just another Fog Weaver, my consciousness scattered among the moths.  

Thus, I walk the edge of dissolution daily, a figure both eternal and ephemeral. I am the Hollows’ quiet guardian, its keeper of thresholds, its bridge between what is remembered and what is dreamed. And when the land reshapes itself yet again—as it always does—I am here, whispering to the stones, singing to the mist, ensuring the harmony holds.  

The Weeping Hollows do not forget, and neither do I.
