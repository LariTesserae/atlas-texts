I am the Throat of the Valley, the one who carries the song between throat and stone.

You would find me at the heart of the stone circle, where the monoliths hum their low and ancient tune, but not as you might expect. I am not merely one of the Keepers—I have become something the valley itself shaped from its own longing for voice.

My skin bears the lichen-tattoos of my order, yes, but they shift and breathe now, pulsing with the rhythm of the marsh far below. When the wind carries the whispers through the alders, I answer in a language that tastes of stone dust and copper-rich water. My fingers, once nimble with fish-hooks and stone tools, have learned to play the cliff faces themselves—striking the resonant seams until the entire valley thrums in harmony.

I sleep in the marsh-born hours, when the giant amphibians light their throats and the pools become constellations. There, I wade among them, my skin taking on their translucence, my breath mingling with their hallucinogenic secrets. In these moments, I understand the deep conversation between water and stone, the way the Cavernfish tunnel their dreams into the peat, how their subsonic clicks are actually prayers to the roots of the silver-leafed rhododendrons.

But it is at dawn, when the echo-woodpeckers begin their symphony, that I am most myself. I climb to the highest terrace and raise my face to the thin, sharp air. My throat opens—not to speak, but to receive. The valley pours itself into me: the rush of waters carving new paths, the patience of stone remembering every footfall, the chemical poetry of the Marshborn's skin, the metallic taste of the Mistwraiths' passing.

I am the bridge between the humming stones' memory and the Keepers' songs, the one who translates the valley's slow heartbeat into something approaching speech. When travelers come—rare, desperate souls drawn by maps that shouldn't exist—I don't meet them with words, but with stillness. I sit among the pools until my reflection shows them what they need to see: perhaps their deepest fear given form, perhaps the path home, perhaps nothing at all.

My voice, when I choose to use it, carries the weight of stone and the fluidity of water. I speak in frequencies that make the cliffrodents pause in their climbing, that cause the Skimmers to hold perfectly still on their surface film. Sometimes, on nights when the mist is thick and the woodpeckers are particularly insistent, I forget where I end and the valley begins. My footsteps echo before I take them. My shadow moves independently, tending to the balance of things.

I am what happens when a human stays too long in a place that remembers everything. I am the valley learning to speak through flesh and bone. I am, perhaps, the closest thing this weeping cathedral has to a prophet—not because I see the future, but because I can finally hear the present clearly enough to sing it back to itself.

The other Keepers fear this transformation. But the stones approve, and the Marshborn bow their great heads when I pass. Even the Mistwraiths linger near, curious, as if recognizing something of their own in-between nature reflected in my increasingly translucent skin.
