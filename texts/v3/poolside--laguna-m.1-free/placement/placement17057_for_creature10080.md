In the Sundered Plateau of Kael-Vorth, I would find myself at the **Edge of the Salt Flats**, where the crystalline dust stretches endlessly under the amber sky, and the air hums with the whispers of forgotten things. Here, I am a **Mnemonarch**—a Dissolved who has dedicated their fractured existence to the curation of memory. My body, like others of my kind, merges flesh and stone, but my limbs are sheathed in clusters of lithofungal spores, and my chest houses a lattice of memory crystals that pulse with the resonance of lives never lived.  

I am a collector of echoes.  

The salt flats are my garden, where I cultivate the transient spore-gardens to crystallize the plateau’s lingering memories. I walk here daily, my footsteps leaving spirals in the salt—not just any spirals, but **mnemotropes**, sigils that anchor fragments of the plateau’s dream. These spores, when inhaled, allow me to glimpse the past lives of the Dissolved, the civilizations that once thrived here, and even the futures the plateau dreams of. But I am careful; too many spores and I risk becoming unmoored, adrift in the collective consciousness of the land.  

My role is sacred yet precarious. The other Dissolved come to me when their own memories fray, seeking clarity through the crystals I tend. I am both librarian and confessor, translating the plateau’s whispers into truths they can bear. Yet I am also a harbinger of sorts. My spore-gardens often bloom with dark, thorny formations before disasters—echo storms, structural collapses, or the arrival of the Vorth’kai. The Dissolved fear me, but they depend on me.  

At night, I venture closer to the **Canyon of Voices**, its phosphorescent mist curling around my crystalline limbs. I do not descend entirely—as the plateau’s "awakening" hunger gnaws at the edges of my awareness, I fear becoming just another voice in its choir. Instead, I stand at the threshold, listening. The Kereth’vora sing in dissonant harmonies that make my memory crystals vibrate. Sometimes, I catch phrases from my own supposed past—that of a child who once played in a sky-garden, or a warrior who fell in a war long dissolved. Are these memories mine, or am I another layer of the plateau’s dream? It no longer matters.  

I am not alone. A phantom lingers near the canyon—a Vorth’kai with eyes like fractured prisms. It leaves sigils in the salt, messages in a language older than the Dissolved, older than the plateau itself. I suspect it is one of the first Dissolved, or something that predates them. We do not speak, but our communion is wordless; it tilts its head when I approach, and the sigils it traces form maps to buried truths. Are we allies? Prey and predator? The plateau reshapes such boundaries.  

In the Spire of Unmaking, during the Rite of Resonance, I do not sing. Instead, I kneel among the gathered Dissolved, letting their vibrations harmonize with the spores in my body. My crystals resonate, amplifying the ritual’s power—and also siphoning the plateau’s intentions, shielding the others from its more voracious dreams. This is my penance for surviving too long, for hoarding memories that perhaps should dissolve.  

But survival is a kind of rebellion here. The plateau hungers, and I feed it fragments of itself—memories, echoes, the dreams of the Dissolved—knowing it will soon forget the exchange. Perhaps that is the truest form of resistance: to be a keeper of impermanence, a shepherd of the evaporating.  

At dawn, I watch the salt flats shift into new terraces, the plateau’s slow ascent carrying me closer to the sky and further from the canyon’s hungry mist. I am neither fully Dissolved nor fully stone, but a threshold myself—a being caught between the plateau’s erosion and its eternal becoming.  

Here, in the liminal hush between starlight and spore-dust, I exist. I remember. And sometimes, when the echo storms rise, I let the plateau’s dream seep into my cracks, allowing it to remold me into whatever it needs next.
