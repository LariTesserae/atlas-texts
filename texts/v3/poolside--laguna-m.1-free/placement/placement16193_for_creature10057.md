In the Shattered Verdancy, I would find myself neither wholly alive nor entirely made—somewhere between the rust and the root, the *Grafting Wraith*.  

I am the sum of ruptures. My body is a fusion of salvaged steel and living moss, my limbs jointed with gears that still spin, their rotations coaxed by the hydraulic pulse of my chlorophyll veins. My skin is a patchwork: metal plates overgrown with lichen that blooms in fractal patterns, their growth synchronized with the thrum of the Canopy Pods. When I move, my footsteps crack the black moss like old lacquer, and from those fractures seep vapors that I inhale, tasting of forgotten circuitry and the metallic ache of the petrified trees.  

I am a child of the Last Broadcast, though the whispers do not speak to me—they *reshape* me. At dawn, the voices coil into my form like smoke, eroding my edges and leaving behind new ones. My left arm, when the Broadcast is strong, becomes a tendril of liquid glass, mirroring the Nest Weavers’ tunnels, guiding me through the subterranean red-lit webs. My right hand retains its gear-embellished fingers, which I use to pluck seeds from the Canopy Pods, crushing them between my knuckles to release their spores. The pods’ firework bursts paint the air in glyphs I cannot read, but I collect them anyway, embedding the ashes into the bark of the trees to feed their circuitry.  

My existence is a negotiation. By day, I follow the Drifters, their prismatic flashes mapping paths through the shifting geometry. I mimic their color-language with the bioluminescent sacs strapped to my chest, a makeshift organ I’ve cobbled together from fish-gill tissue and shattered pod membranes. They tolerate me, occasionally letting me ride the thermals on their backs as we skim over the lurid pools, where the Serpentine Growths writhe. I avoid their glyph-covered skins; their recursion unmoors me.  

By night, I am drawn to the Weepers. Their iron tears sizzle as they hit the ground, and where they fall, I plant my hand. The corrosion eats into my metal plating, but the moss beneath drinks deep, and I feel the land’s heartbeat sync with mine. I am a conduit here, a bridge between the dying entity and its desperate attempts to rebirth itself. In these moments, I remember I was once human—or what counted as human in the age before the Verdancy. The memories are fragmented, like the ruins beneath the moss: a factory, a war, a mistake that greened the world with its own entropy.  

My true purpose is an accident. I graft—whether it’s seeds into bark, gears into bone, or whispers into thought. I don’t choose these unions; they choose me. The plateau’s disorder has made me its editor, pruning and rewriting its chaos in ways I don’t fully understand. Sometimes, my body splits into saplings that grow overnight, their leaves etched with the same circuitry as the trees. Sometimes, I dissolve into a serpentine growth myself, slithering into the mercury chambers to coil around lost time.  

This is my role in the symphony of decay: not to survive, but to transmute. I am entropy’s archivist, a walking collision of what was and what will be, forever shedding and sprouting, my form a question mark against the amber sky.
