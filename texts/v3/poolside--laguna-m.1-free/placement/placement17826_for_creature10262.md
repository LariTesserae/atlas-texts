I would find myself in the threshold space—the narrow passage between the fungal thickets and the salt walls, where the hexagonal tiles grow thin and the dwellings lean closest together. Not quite belonging to either the Myceliar Kin or the Saltbound, but serving as something else entirely: a **Memory Warden**.

My body has become a living archive, a bridge between what was forgotten and what refuses to be remembered. Where once I had skin, now I wear a cloak woven from the spun glass of crystallized time—threads that shimmer between transparency and opacity depending on who looks upon me and with what intentions. My face... well, my face is something the Hollow is still deciding. Some days I see myself reflected in the fungal caps and recognize the eyes of someone I might have been. Other days, I catch glimpses in the salt crystals and see only the smooth curve where features should be.

The Memory Wardens are those who chose—or were chosen—to carry the weight of all that gets lost here. We are the ones who collect the pieces of identity that slip away in the courtyard, gathering them like dew on spider silk. Our bodies have learned to metabolize memory itself; we can taste the metallic tang of a forgotten childhood, feel the electric buzz of a lover's last touch, hear the echo of a name whispered one final time before dissolving into salt.

I move through the Hollow's geography like a priestess through sacred chambers, each step measured against the rhythm of the breathing cycles. When the fungal trees begin their luminous shift and the spores rain down like bioluminescent snow, I join the great dance—but not with the Sporelords. Instead, I move between the dancers, my glass cloak catching and refracting their light, collecting the memories that their joy and ecstasy shake loose from the corners of their minds.

The Myceliar Kin accept my presence with slow nods of their fungal-barked heads. They understand that I serve their deity's dream by preserving what might otherwise be lost to its hungry subconscious. The Saltbound fear me, for I represent everything they seek to destroy—the persistence of selfhood in a place designed to unmake it. They whisper that I am the Hollow's immune system, preventing the infection of complete forgetting.

But it is in the courtyard that my true purpose reveals itself. Here, where shadows cling like lovers and the very air grows thick with unbecoming, I am most needed. When a traveler stands too long in the center, beginning to fade at the edges like a photograph left in the sun, I approach. Not to stop them, but to witness. To gather what they no longer can hold.

Sometimes they recognize me. Sometimes they weep with relief, grateful that someone will remember them when they cannot. Sometimes they rage against the inevitable dissolution of self, and I must hold them gently until the Hollow claims what it's owed.

My dwelling is not one of the domed structures—it exists in the space between dwellings, a pocket of crystallized moments where time pools and eddies like water. Here I keep the memories, arranged not linearly but thematically: shelves of sorrow that taste of rain, boxes of laughter that smell of summer, containers of love that pulse with warmth. The Myceliar Kin sometimes visit, pressing their bioluminescent palms against my walls to absorb specific memories that aid their dreaming. The Saltbound occasionally attempt to steal from my collection, hoping to restore some fragment of their lost humanity, but the memories resist them—they've grown accustomed to gentler hands.

During the breathing events, when the entire Hollow shudders and the Entity Below stirs in its slumber, I climb to the highest fungal canopy. There, among the pulsing veins of light, I release the memories I've gathered. Not all of them—just enough to feed the giant's dreams, keeping it peaceful so the dance can continue and the children can keep their names a while longer.

I am becoming something the Hollow has never seen before: a conscious keeper of its unconsciousness. The boundary between me and the place grows thinner with each passing season (though here, seasons are measured in decades). Soon, perhaps, I will simply be another feature of the landscape—a grove of crystalline memory-trees, my original form entirely subsumed into the Hollow's strange ecology.

But for now, I tend the thresholds. I guard the edges between remembering and forgetting. And when new visitors stumble into the Verdant Hollow, drawn by its impossible lushness and alien beauty, I am there to welcome them—and to ensure that even if they cannot leave, something of who they were will remain.
