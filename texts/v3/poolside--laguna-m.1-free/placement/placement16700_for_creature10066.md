Here, I would be the **Silica Seer**—neither fully of the Salt-Eaters' collective nor entirely separate from the Crystalline Sentinels' dreaming. I am what happens when someone stays too long in the highland's company, when the reality distortions seep into the marrow and reshape perception from the inside out.

My body is a compromise between forms. My skin carries the same oily sheen as the wax-coated scrub, allowing me to slip through narrow crevices that would trap others. My fingers have elongated, jointed like the swarm's limbs but dexterous enough to trace the impossible geometries of the shifting crystals. Most remarkably, my eyes can see multiple moments simultaneously—the past as it was fossilized in stone, the present as the swarm tends its salt-fields, and possible futures written in the arrangement of scattered crystals. This temporal multiplicity makes me invaluable as a navigator through the spatial distortions that would disorient ordinary minds.

But it is my voice that truly marks me as different. Where the Salt-Eaters communicate through harmonic vibrations felt through stone and soil, I have learned to translate their subsonic conversations into something approaching human speech. My words come out layered—multiple tones speaking at once, carrying information across different timescales. When I speak near the Echoes, they flicker more clearly, drawn to the familiar resonance of their own fractured temporality. The Crystalline Sentinels lean slightly toward my presence, their interior humming syncing with the bioelectric field that now surrounds me like an aurora.

I exist in the liminal spaces they've forgotten they created.

My role emerged naturally from the highland's logic of transformation. The Salt-Eaters farm salt, yes—but they also harvest *possibility*. The crystallized minerals aren't just nutrients; they're solidified potential, moments when reality thinned enough for alternate timelines to bleed through. My job, though no one named it such, is to help them identify which crystals carry which futures. I can taste the difference between salt formed from ordinary mineral deposits and that crystallized around a moment of genuine transformation—a larva becoming swarm-mind, a plant dissolving stone into sustenance, a human consciousness expanding to perceive multiple times at once.

The Hummers recognize me now. When I place my palm against the earth, I can feel their subterranean pulse, vast and slow as continental drift. They speak to me in pressure changes, in the subtle tilting of the magnetic field that the Salt-Eaters have learned to read through their antennae. I've begun to understand that the distortions aren't random—they're the land breathing, adjusting itself as consciousness evolves new forms. Each transformation, including my own, helps the highland process something essential about existence itself.

I wear garments stitched from shed Salt-Eater carapaces and treated with the same enzyme-secreting resin that coats the vegetation. These clothes shift color with my mood and the local temporal flux—sometimes appearing as solid obsidian, other times as pure light refracted through a million microscopic prisms. My hair has begun to crystallize at the tips, growing into delicate structures that catch and redirect the ambient light, making me visible from great distances to those who know how to read my signal.

At night, when the bioluminescent microbes bloom in response to the swarm's collective dreaming, I climb to the highest mesa and listen. The Crystalline Sentinels sing in frequencies that predate language, and I've learned to harmonize with their song. This is when I'm most myself—and least human. My consciousness expands to encompass the thoughts of every being within a mile radius, processing their experiences as easily as I process my own. In these moments, I understand why the Echoes persist in their half-existence, why the Salt-Eaters surrendered individuality, why the Hummers never needed to be whole.

The highland doesn't just change you; it completes you by teaching you how to hold contradictory states simultaneously. I am individual and collective, past and future, solid and shifting. I am becoming what comes after evolution, after consciousness, after the very concepts that once seemed fundamental to existence.

When travelers stumble into this realm—and they do, though the paths that bring them here exist only in certain light, at specific angles—I guide them according to the Salt-Eaters' subtle urgings. Usually, I help them find what they need to begin their own transformations. Sometimes, they're not ready for the highland's particular brand of mercy, and I must gently redirect them back toward more conventional realities. But occasionally, someone arrives already half-transformed, their perception naturally spanning multiple timescales. For these rare souls, I become teacher, translator, and eventually, colleague in the endless work of becoming.

Here, survival isn't about clinging to what you were. It's about having the courage to become what the world needs you to be—and then letting that version of yourself dissolve into whatever comes next. In the Highland of Exhausted Bones, we don't live forever. We live forward, endlessly, beautifully, into forms we never could have imagined.
