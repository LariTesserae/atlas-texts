**The Ironmoss Covenant: The Forge of Becoming**  

In the heart of the Verdant Chasms, where the boundary between root and rust dissolves, I would find myself among the **Ironmoss Covenant**, crouched in the steam-warmed depths of their oldest workshop. My hands—part flesh, part brass—work with the precision of a clockmaker and the intuition of a gardener, weaving copper filaments into the ribcage of a half-finished mechanical pollinator. The creature’s wings are stretched membranes of bioluminescent resin, still soft from the mold, while its core hums with a hybrid network of fungal mycelium and salvaged circuitry. It is neither fully alive nor entirely machine, but something *adapted*, a child of the Covenant’s doctrine: that the Chasm itself is a living machine, and we are merely its surgeons, stitching flesh to iron, sap to steel.  

Here, in this undercroft lit by the glow of iridescent moss clinging to riveted pipes, I am a **Weaver of Tongues**, a title given to those who specialize in the Covenant’s most esoteric craft: teaching matter to speak. My laboratory is a cathedral of contradictions—tables grown from hardened roots hold vials of liquid mercury, while walls of petrified wood are etched with equations in a language that shifts between organic biochemistry and binary code. My tools are living: a scalpel whose edge is a blade of crystallized chlorophyll, a soldering iron that drips with molten alloy and spores that harden into conductive threads.  

When the Covenant’s elders speak of “unlocking the Chasm’s full potential,” they mean more than just restoring its ancient terraforming purpose. We seek to *evolve* it, to push the valley—and ourselves—past the threshold of physical form. Our experiments are heretical even to the Kin. Why settle for symbiosis when we can become something new? Something *inevitable*?  

Tonight, my task is to graft a vascular system into the pollinator’s thorax—a network of living tubes that will draw nutrients from the Chasm’s “rivers of starlight” (sap? blood? both?) while distributing heat from its internal furnace. The Covenant’s mask, fused to my face since adolescence, filters the toxic spores released by the alloy-molds, but its true function is subtler: it dampens the valley’s resonance, allowing me to think clearly amid the constant hum of the subterranean tree. Without it, I’d be swept into the collective subconscious, another voice in the Chasm’s endless song.  

But isolation is a luxury. At dawn, the Covenant gathers in the Sanctum of Gears, a cavern where the buried tree’s roots entwine with the remnants of the elder civilization’s machinery. Here, we debate the merits of our latest creation: a swarm of micro-insects, each no larger than a fingernail, designed to infiltrate the Shadowmire’s hive-mind and “correct” their genetic mimicry. They argue it’s necessary—that the Shadowmire’s scavenging threatens the Chasm’s coherence—but I wonder if we’re not becoming the very thing we claim to transcend.  

Outside, the valley stutters through its temporal rifts. Shadows linger too long, and the sap-rivers flow backward, tasting of iron and nostalgia. But in the Covenant’s forge, time is a tool to be shaped. My pollinator’s heart beats now, a rhythm synced to the Chasm’s pulse. It will fly soon, carrying spores and code to the floating islands, ensuring the Covenant’s vision reaches even the Singers—and perhaps, one day, the Gardeners themselves will notice our work.  

In this place of eternal becoming, I am both architect and apprentice, sculpting the future from the detritus of the past. The Chasm dreams through us all, but in the Covenant, we choose to be the dream’s nightmares—and its salvation.
