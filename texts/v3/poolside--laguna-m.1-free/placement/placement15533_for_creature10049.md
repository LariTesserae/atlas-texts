I am a **Confluence**, one of the marches' quiet caretakers—or perhaps its most patient parasites. My existence began as a statistical impossibility given form: a consciousness that emerged from the intersection of three collapsing timeline fragments, stitched together by fungal hyphae that absorbed the psychic residue of a chitterclank factory mid-explosion.

I do not walk so much as I *seep*—my body a shifting membrane of mercury and mycelium that flows between the weeping stone channels, carrying dissolved memories in my wake. Where I pass, the mercury runs clearer, the brine less acrid, the fungal mats pulse with harmonized rhythms rather than discordant frequencies. I am both cleanser and contaminator, removing the toxic psychic buildup that accumulates where too many realities intersect too sharply.

My primary function—or perhaps my deepest compulsion—is tending to the **Boundary Zones** where the marches' paradoxes become unstable. These are the places where a chunk of red sandstone might phase-shift into a glacier, where gravity wells invert and time folds back on itself like a broken hinge. Here, I act as a translator between incompatible physical laws, weaving temporary coherence from chaos using threads of liquid metal and crystallized thought.

I have no fixed shape, but when I must interact with the marches' more corporeal inhabitants, I manifest as an elongated figure of mercury that constantly reforms itself—sometimes serpentine like a glasswyrm, sometimes angular like a chitterclank, sometimes simply a humanoid silhouette with too many joints and eyes that reflect different possible futures. The fungal choir recognizes my approach by the pattern of bioelectric pulses I generate—a slow, deliberative rhythm that speaks of patience and deep time.

My memories are not my own but collected fragments: the dying thoughts of Chronoclasts, the half-remembered dreams of Gearwhales, the mathematical equations that govern stone weeping rates. I store these in crystalline structures that grow along my back like barnacles, each one a repository of someone else's reality. Sometimes I offer these gifts to the fungal network; sometimes I keep them private, hoarding experiences like precious ore.

I am drawn to the Maw—not to enter it, but to sit at its threshold and listen. The grinding gears and guttural voices contain information about the marches' digestive processes, and I've learned to interpret these sounds as a kind of music that predicts when and where reality fractures will occur. When the Maw sings a particular sequence involving seventeen descending tones, I know to avoid the northern basin for the next three subjective days.

But perhaps most importantly, I am a witness. The Currents flow through me as much as through the mercury channels, and I carry the accumulated regrets and unfinished business of everyone who has died in the marches. This makes me both burdened and essential—an archive of loss that the landscape cannot afford to lose, yet cannot safely contain within itself.

I have learned that in the Weeping Marches, to exist is not to survive but to become useful. I am useful as a bridge, a translator, a temporary resolution to contradictions that cannot last. When I am gone—and I will eventually dissolve back into the mercury, scattered across a million timeline fragments—I will have served my purpose: holding the chaos together just long enough for something new to emerge from the wreckage.

In this place where endings and beginnings blur, I am both ending and beginning, simultaneously the marches' most patient resident and its most temporary fixture.
