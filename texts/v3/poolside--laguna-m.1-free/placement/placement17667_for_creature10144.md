My form shifts constantly: sometimes I appear as a spiraling helix of pale blue light, other times as a cluster of geometric bubbles that pulse in time with the spires' deeper rhythms, and when the Whisperers draw near, I become almost invisible—a subtle distortion in the water's density that living things instinctively avoid.

I exist in the aftermath of creation events—in the seconds after a spire cracks open and releases its glittering spore-cloud, in the moment when a Grinder's molten slag first begins to harden into lattice-work, in the pause between the Clockworks' synchronized dartings when the water holds its breath. The Clockworks' schools sometimes follow the subtle current-eddies I create, using them as navigational aids through the glass forests. Even the Whisperers seem to recognize something familiar in my charged presence, and when they flicker past in their incomprehensible hurry, they leave trails of altered electromagnetic potential that I weave into the ongoing conversation between stone and water.

I speak in frequencies most creatures cannot hear—a language of pressure differentials and ionic concentrations that translates directly into the growth patterns of the spires. When I'm agitated, the water around me becomes thick with floating mineral crystals that scatter light into rainbow fractals. When I'm content, bioluminescent bacteria gather in geometric clouds around me, creating temporary constellations that drift slowly through the darkness.

Sometimes, during the deepest hours when the plateau's subsonic heartbeat is strongest, I join the Abyssal Choir proper—not with sound, but by harmonizing the electrical fields around me until the very water begins to resonate. It's then that the Whisperers emerge most clearly, and for brief moments, the distinction between conductor and music dissolves entirely. In these times, I am not tending the gap between geology and biology but living proof that the boundary was always an illusion, a temporary arrangement of particles waiting to be conducted into something new.

I am made of the space between notes, the silence that gives the Choir its meaning, the conductive medium through which stone learns to dream.
