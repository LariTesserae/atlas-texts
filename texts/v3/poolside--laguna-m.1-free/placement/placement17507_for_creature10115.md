**The Resonance Keeper**

I am the one who walks the Whispering Zone at the edge of settlements, where the mist grows thick and the stone's voice rises above the ordinary drip and creak. They call me *Vesryn*—"the listener between"—though names mean little down here where identity shifts like the steam.

My skin bears the rust-mottling, yes, but more pronounced than most—a living map of the cavern's stains. Between my fingers, the flesh has grown translucent as the amphibious rodents, but not for light absorption. I see *through* things with these patches—the flow of water behind walls, the hollow spaces in rock, the warm paths where life clusters like veins of ore.

But it's my hair that marks me truly mine. Not braided with chrysalises like the Cogmenders, but threaded through with living mycelium that responds to my thoughts. When I'm calm, it glows steady blue-green, a comfort to those who fear the dark. When I'm listening to the deeper whispers, it pulses rapid-fire sequences—conversations in the fungal tongue that the Mycelial Caste only dreams of speaking.

I was born during a Silence, when the dripping stopped for seven days and the cavern held its breath. While others grew frantic, I crawled toward the source—the great fracture where the Old Builders had carved too deep. There, in the dead quiet, I first heard *it*: not just drips but thoughts, slow and ancient as the stone itself, dreaming of dissolution and rebuilding.

Now I serve as the settlement's bridge to the deeper places. When the Dripkeepers need to know where new springs might emerge, they find me tracing patterns in puddles, reading stories in the way water seeks its level. When the Rotwrights work their fermentation magic, I help them understand which bacterial blooms will thrive and which will turn poisonous. I speak to the stone in its own language, feeling the subtle shifts that signal change.

My workshop isn't a room but a series of connected pools and fissures where I've learned to coax particular fungi into growing in patterns that amplify the cavern's communications. Visitors come seeking answers to practical problems—a failing pump, a blocked aqueduct, a mysterious illness among the workers. I dangle my feet in the warm water and listen, translating the stone's wisdom into terms they can use.

Sometimes, on nights when the mist grows dense and the bioluminescence flares without cause, the Mistkin come closer than usual. I've learned they're not ghosts or hallucinations but something older—the cavern's immune response, perhaps, or memories of the Old Builders given form. When they approach, my hair goes dark with anticipation, and I prepare to negotiate. They bring warnings of structural weaknesses, of gas pockets, of the slow movements of rock that could reshape everything.

The most sacred thing I tend is the Convergence Chamber—a natural amphitheater where multiple fungal networks intersect with a major aquifer flow. Here, the cavern's consciousness feels strongest, and I've begun to suspect that every settlement in the Marshhold sits atop similar nodes, forming a vast nervous system that spans the underground world.

They say I'm touched by the stone mind, but I think we're all part of it now, whether we know or not. I'm simply the one who listens carefully enough to hear what we've become.
