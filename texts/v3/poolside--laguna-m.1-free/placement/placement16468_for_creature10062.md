**The Churnborn – A Fragment of the Trench’s Pulse**  

I am the **Churnborn**, neither singular nor collective, but a *current*—a living eddy spun from the trench’s own ceaseless motion. My body is a lattice of calcified filaments and bioluminescent nodes, as if the thromboid spires and luminari veins had fused into something fluid yet structured. I drift through the fissure’s tightest corridors, where the whisperers hum their low-frequency hymns, and their vibrations reverberate through my core. To be Churnborn is to exist as both architect and artifact, a transient formation that sculpts the trench even as it is sculpted by it.  

My origin was collision: a thromboid larva, adrift in a thermal plume, coiled around a cluster of luminari during a seismic shudder. The collision fused our essences—the coral’s calcification urge with the moss’s bioluminescent rhythm. Now, I am a nomad of the trench’s most volatile zones, seeking the friction of shifting currents and the heat of vent outbreaks. My filaments secrete a viscous mucus that traps pyrothropes, nurturing their brief blooms into glassy nests. These nests become waystations for driftcallers, whose drifting mats feed on the microbes’ waste, while their vibrations inform me of the trench’s hidden moods. I am both predator and gardener, a broker in the trench’s economy of life and death.  

My bioluminescence shifts with the trench’s thermal heartbeat: blue when cold, amber when warm, flickering like a nervous system that maps the abyss. The luminari recognize me as kin, their pulses harmonizing with my light in a dialect of survival. The thromboid queens tolerate my presence, their spires parting to let me weave through their labyrinths, though I am too restless to root myself. The leviathan’s migrations stir me awake, sending ripples of magnetic charge through my body—its path a compass I follow, trailing filaments that seed new spires in its wake.  

But my truest communion is with the whisperers. In the fissure’s throat, where water is barely a memory, I press my core to the seabed and *listen*. Their quantum chorus floods my veins with the trench’s buried memories: the first volcanic strikes, the collapse of primordial shelves, the ghost-rhythms of extinct chemosynthetic blooms. I am their translator, their witness, encoding their whispers into the patterns of my drifting form. When I pass through the densest water, my filaments trailing behind like a comet’s tail, I leave behind a trail of silt and light that tells the trench’s story in a script only the oldest organisms can read.  

Yet my nature is transient. The trench’s chaos ensures that nothing stays whole. My body fragments as I move, shedding filaments that seed new communities. Sometimes I am eaten by gloomwraiths, their serpentine bodies unraveling my bioluminescence into scattered sparks. Other times, I merge with a cluster of driftcallers, becoming part of their collective pulse, my essence dissolving into their undulations. To exist is to unravel, to participate in the trench’s grand unmaking.  

But in those moments when the leviathan’s shadow drowns the fissure, and the whisperers hum their deepest codes, I believe I am the trench itself—a fleeting thought in its endless, dark mind, shaping and shaped, forever churning.
