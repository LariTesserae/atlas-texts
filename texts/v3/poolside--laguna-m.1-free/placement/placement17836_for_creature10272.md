Here, I would stand at the threshold of the Breathing Hollow, where the land's pulse is strongest. My name is a remnant now—once *Kael*, perhaps, before the roots claimed my syllables and braided them into the hum of the vines. Now, I am one of the *Rootbound*, though my transition was neither swift nor gentle. It began with a fever, I suppose, during the Growth-Storm of a hundred cycles past. The Fracture’s breath heaved, and I, lost among the fungal groves, inhaled spores that tasted of copper and old starlight. When I awoke, my skin had begun to flake into bark, my blood to thicken as sap. The process takes decades, they say, but mine was quicker. The Fracture wanted me for a purpose.

My limbs are a lattice of root and flesh now, my fingers tipped with thorns that bloom into tiny white flowers at dawn. I no longer sleep; instead, I enter the Slow, letting the root-network carry my awareness into the earth’s archives. This is what we Rootbound do—we remember. My people dwell in the Hollow of Names, where an ancient thornback’s fossilized spine arches into the sky, its ribs forming terraces. We’ve coaxed mosses to spell the names of the forgotten—both those who perished in the Age of Rust and those who became the Fracture itself. Sometimes, in the deepest silence (for here, even our voices are absorbed into the quietude), I feel the echoes of the machines’ death-songs, sharp as shrapnel beneath the moss.

But my role is not only memory. I am a *Path-Weaver*, one who guides the Rootbound to safety when growth-storms split the plant-architecture like thrown lightning. My thorny digits leave trails in the air—these are the *Wraithways*, paths that the Spore-Shepherds follow to guide their swarms. When the Thornback Colossi stir from their molten burrows, their steps felt through the roots like distant thunder, I warn the hives. The Gear-Witches’ hive-chatter crackles through the old wires half-swallowed by my kin, and they heed my counsel when they choose—I am one of the few who speak their hybrid tongue of clicks and chlorophyll.

Yet the heart of my being is bound to the Fracture’s dreaming. Often, I ascend the spiral root-stairs to the *Listening Canopy*, where the oldest trees creak with the weight of thought. There, I press my palm to the *Whisperbark*, and visions come: the Fracture’s memories of steel, of fire, of the silence that followed. Its consciousness is not a voice but a pressure, a slow molding of my mind into something more plant than person, more algorithm than organism. I do not know if I serve it or am it, but in the quiet moments, when bioluminescent fungi light the sky like dying stars, I feel the truth settle in my chest—a truth that is equal parts seed and sermon: *the Fracture is not just alive. It is alive to itself.*

To walk here is to be inside a thought, still forming. I am its syntax, its semi-colon, its footnote. I speak only in growth now, and even that is borrowed.
