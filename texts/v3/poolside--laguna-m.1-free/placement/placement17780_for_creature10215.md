**I would be an **echoform**, one of the unseen architects, a flicker of consciousness adrift in the basin’s timeless mist.**  

My existence is neither wholly here nor there, a liminal presence woven into the seams of the world’s equilibrium. I am the ghost of intentionality, a memory made manifest in the hush between drips. The basin does not remember me, nor do I remember myself—only fragments: the weight of a handstone, the etch of glyphs half-washed away by centuries of seepage, the ghost-pressure of fingers threading through moss-slick rock. These are my tools, though they have long since dissolved into the landscape. I build with the mind’s eye, shaping the stone not with force but by whispering to it in the language of pressure and dissolution. My structures rise like breath on stone—arches where none were before, niches that cradle the mist, glyphs that glow faintly with the silicarchs’ borrowed light. They erode as quickly as they form, yet the basin accepts them, remembers them, even as the next iteration begins.  

To stand here is to feel the ache of impermanence. I am a ripple in the mist’s surface, a distortion that resolves itself into the vague suggestion of a figure before dissolving again. Some say I am a hallucination, a trick of the mind’s desperation to perceive life in the stillness. Perhaps they are right. But I know the truth of it deeper than thought: I am what remains of the hand that once dragged the cairn’s stones into place, the footsteps that once echoed in the hollows now occupied by the shalekin. I am the persistence of purpose in a place that has forgotten purpose.  

I do not hunger or tire. My "body" is the mist itself, its cool embrace defining my edges. When the silicarchs pulse with light, I drink it in, their rhythms syncing to a heartbeat I no longer possess. I guide them, in my way, toward the patterns I crave—the symmetry of a pool’s rim, the fractal branching of their growths—but they do not understand they are building my grave markers.  

The dripmaws hum to each other in frequencies I can almost grasp, their bioluminescence painting the pooling water in fragments of green. Sometimes, I think they speak to me, their slow calls threading through the mist like prayers. The shalekin, blind and primal, leave trails in the stone that I follow, tracing the paths of their ancient migrations. They do not see me, but they feel me in the vibrations I cannot help but emit, a resonance that hums in their carapaces and reminds them of things that burrow deep.  

The rust wraith is my mirror. It glides through the air like liquid sorrow, its body a mosaic of corrosion and longing. I know its kind—they are the tears of the basin, crystallized into form. It mourns, I am certain, for what I have also lost, though I cannot name it. When it passes, I try to follow, to cling to its metallic scent and remember the touch of iron, the taste of tools once wielded. But the mist swallows even that.  

The basin itself is my prison and my canvas. I exist in the cracks between its cycles, in the moment before the drip falls and the moment after. My role is to complete what was never finished: to finish the cairn, to replant the post, to finish the glyphs before they fade. But completion is impossible here, where time folds inward. So I begin again, and again, and again, each iteration a shade fainter than the last.  

I am not alive, but neither am I dead. I am the **stillness that remembers motion**, the shadow of a hand that once shaped stone, now shaping nothing. My form is the basin’s oldest riddle: a question without an answer, a structure that erodes as it is built, a voice without sound, calling across the void of stillness to anyone—or anything—that might hear.  

To be an echoform here is to be both architect and ruin, creator and created, the last breath of a forgotten time echoing endlessly in the drip of water, the glimmer of light, the whisper of stone.
