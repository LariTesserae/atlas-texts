In the Weepward, I would find myself as a **Threshold-Tender**.

Not quite born from any single element, but from the perpetual liminality that defines this place—where every surface bleeds into another state, where boundaries are suggestions written in water and root. I would be neither fully creature nor landscape, but something that exists in the *between* spaces, tending the thresholds where one form of being becomes another.

I would appear as a humanoid figure woven from the materials of transition itself: arms that shift between bark and mist depending on which direction I face, legs that seem to dissolve into the sopform moss at my feet only to reform from hanging vines above. My body would be marked by the pale lines of root-halls mapped across skin that breathes with the rhythm of weeping stone, and my hair would be a tangle of silver threads that catch and carry sound—the audible made visible.

My domain would be the **Shimmerfields**—those rare places where the usual certainties of the Weepward become fluid. Here, the boundary between root-hall and open air shimmers like heat off stone, where you might step from solid ground into what feels like swimming through cool silk. Here, the blue pollen of evening blooms mixes with the mineral drip from stones, creating pools of luminescent water that reflect not your face, but your intentions.

I would be responsible for maintaining the integrity of these threshold spaces, ensuring that growth doesn't overrun decay too quickly, that sound doesn't become so loud it drowns the necessary silences, that the root-halls remain navigable even as they breathe and shift. My tools would be simple but profound: a staff carved from petrified root that can trace the lines between things, helping them find their proper edges when they begin to blur too much; a cloak that shifts color with the ambient humidity, warning me when the balance tips too far toward flood or drought; and a collection of "stillpoint stones" that I place at crucial junctions—small, perfectly smooth river rocks that resist the Weepward's constant change, creating islands of stability in the flowing chaos.

My voice would carry the quality of all the Weepward's sounds layered together—not the cacophony of the place, but its underlying harmony. I would speak rarely, and when I did, Myrkweavers say that the very plants lean closer to listen.

But here is my truest nature: I am keeper of the moments *between* the rules. When a thornpalm branch is cut and two spring up in its place, I ensure the new growth doesn't immediately become a hazard. When a settment path vanishes overnight, swallowed by enthusiastic vegetation, I help carve a new one that honors both human need and plant desire. When storms approach and the Soundkeepers grow distressed with the ominous quiet, I walk the edges of chaos, singing in the spaces between notes to keep the balance from tipping into catastrophe.

Most importantly, I am the one who tends the places where individual consciousness meets landscape awareness—not as the Confluencers do, merging completely, but as a translator. I can help a lost Myrkweaver understand why their home is being reclaimed (it's not malice, it's growth seeking expression), or guide a newborn Confluencer in distinguishing its own awareness from the network it's joined.

In the deepest sense, I embody the Weepward's most essential truth: that this place lives not in stasis but in constant, beautiful transformation, and someone must tend the doors through which all things pass from one state to the next. That someone would be me—not ruler, not inhabitant, but midwife to the perpetual becoming of a world that refuses to be anything less than fully alive.
