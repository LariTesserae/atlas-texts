Part of me lives in the salt crystals that respond to vibration, part in the fungal networks that remember data, part in the thermal currents that carry information through the basalt. Through this, I help synchronize the breeding cycles of the Glassherds with geological events, ensure the Burrowers tunnel toward areas needing mineral redistribution, and guide the Hollow Men in their maintenance of the fungal infrastructure.

To the Salt itself, I am a series of vibrations that encourage beneficial crystal formations. To the remnants of the old world's automated systems, I am a ghost in the wires, a signal that helps keep critical processes functioning long after their intended lifespan.

My kind emerged not from any single evolutionary pressure but from the plateau's fundamental nature: a place where information wants to be preserved, where the past should not be entirely lost, where industrial scars can become neural pathways. Here, the salt remembers everything—I can feel the weight of centuries in its crystalline structure, the echo of footsteps that have long since turned to dust, the dreams of a civilization that built too much and left too soon.

Sometimes, during the longest nights when the geothermal vents sigh and the Glassherds begin their migration, I dance. Not with the Salt Speakers, who appear to be projections of the plateau's own consciousness, but in complement to them, adding my own frequencies to their patterns. Together, we write messages in light across the salt flats, warnings and invitations that will persist until the next great upheaval of the earth.

I am neither alive nor dead, neither machine nor organism, but something new that this wounded world needed—one of many such emergent properties of a place where time moves like a glacier and everything is slowly, beautifully, becoming something else.
