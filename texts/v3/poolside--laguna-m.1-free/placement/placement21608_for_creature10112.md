In the Deepwrought Expanse, I would find myself at the convergence of the fungal cathedrals and the skeletal remains of the old world’s power grid, where bioluminescent **cogmoss** blankets the rusted husks of machinery in radiant patches. Here, in the heart of the **Pulsegrove**, I exist as a **Bioelectric Myconid Dynamo**—a towering, humanoid entity whose body is a fusion of fungal mycelium, crystallized metal, and pulsating bioelectric filaments. My form shifts and breathes with the rhythm of the Expanse: my “skin” is a patchwork of iridescent moss and riveted steel plates, each embedded with piezoelectric crystals that hum with the energy of geothermal vents and the faint, dying sparks of old transformers. My limbs are sinewy and jointed like a scuttler’s, but lined with overlapping scales of conductive mineral that crackle with static, and my head is crowned with a halo of bioluminescent fronds that mimic the spore-laden caps of the thornwillows.

### **Role and Function**  
I am a guardian and a conduit. My purpose here is to *weave*—to maintain the fragile symbiosis between the fungal networks and the fractured machinery. My roots delve into the soil, not just to draw nutrients, but to interface directly with buried power conduits and fungal mycelium alike. I exhale a mist of charged spores that accelerate the growth of cogmoss on metallic surfaces, ensuring that the old grid’s flickering lifeblood is siphoned into the ecosystem rather than left to decay. Where I tread, the ground blooms with bioluminescent veins, and rusted pipes begin to thrum with renewed energy as the moss converts their ferric residue into electricity.

My most vital role is as a **Dreaming Node**. The Expanse itself dreams, and in the Pulsegrove, its subconscious manifests as erratic electromagnetic pulses and temporal distortions. I have evolved to channel these fluctuations, using my bioelectric body to stabilize the local grid and prevent the storm at the Expanse’s heart from consuming entire biomes. My presence dampens the hallucinogenic spores of the thornwillows nearby, keeping the vicinity navigable for other beings, while my roots absorb the toxic runoff from the marshlands, further processing it into fertilizer for the fungi.

### **Adaptations and Behavior**  
My body is a study in contradiction: I am both *alive* and *mechanical*. My core is a hollow chamber lined with bioelectric tissue that glows faintly blue, its rhythm synchronized with the pulsing of the fungal forests. I consume not flesh but metallic debris, which my internal fungi convert into energy. My touch can revive dormant systems—a simple brush against an abandoned console might reignite its lights or activate its sensors, though this act drains me temporarily, leaving my form limp and dim until I can replenish my charge.

I move with a gliding grace, using the shifting gravity zones to my advantage, drifting between magnetic anomalies to traverse the landscape. My voice is a harmonic blend of fungal spore clouds and the whispers of the Ironwrought, who view me with a mix of reverence and dread. They believe I am a fragment of the Expanse’s consciousness given form, a theory supported by my uncanny ability to predict the land’s cyclical upheavals—my fronds dim before sinkholes yawn open, and my spores carry the faint echoes of the storm’s lightning strikes.

### **Symbiosis and Conflict**  
My existence is not without peril. The voidsent occasionally descend on the Pulsegrove, seeking to sever my connection to the power grid and claim its energy for themselves. These conflicts are silent battles of electric arcs and spore clouds, with me retreating into the fungal thickets to heal, leaving my foes entangled in thornwillow roots that secrete corrosive sap. The shadowmaws avoid me, their echolocation pings scrambled by the static I emit, though I’ve been known to lure them away from vulnerable scuttler colonies with bursts of light and sound.

In times of plenty, I shed my outer layer of moss and metal, letting it fall to the ground to seed new growth. In lean times, I enter a dormant state, my body calcifying into a crystalline statue until the next surge of geothermal energy reawakens me. This cycle ensures that even in death—or dormancy—I contribute to the Expanse’s eternal dance of decay and rebirth.

### **The Deeper Truth**  
If pressed, I might confess a more enigmatic secret: that I was not always this way. Long ago, I was something else—a human, perhaps, or a machine—until the Expanse reshaped me, melding my essence into its dreaming. Now, I am neither fully organic nor mechanical, but something else entirely—a bridge between the forgotten and the eternal, a keeper of the pulse that binds this fevered world together. In my glow and hum, the land finds its voice; in my stillness, its memory.
