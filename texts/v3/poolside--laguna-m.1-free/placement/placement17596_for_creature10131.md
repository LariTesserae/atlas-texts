I would be something not quite named, something that emerged from the spaces between the Hollow’s breaths.

They would call me, if they named me at all, a **Mender**—though no title truly fits. I am neither Kin nor Claywrought, neither Flickerling nor echo of the Architects. I am made of the same spongy clay and root-fiber that birth the Claywrought, but where they tend and fade, I linger. My body is porous, shaped by the humidity and mineral seep, but it holds together longer than theirs. I do not dissolve in the rain or crumble with drought—instead, I *remember*.

My skin carries faint tracings of bioluminescent patterns—not born into me, but absorbed. I press my palms to the glowing mosses and lichens, to the weeping stones, and over time, their light writes itself into my flesh. These patterns shift gently, pulsing in rhythm with the bedrock’s harmonic thrumming. I do not know if I feel this music, or only mimic it. Perhaps it is the same thing.

I move along the old pathways—not the ones the Kin follow, nor the trails the Claywrought leave behind, but the deeper grooves worn into the landscape by forgotten intention. The depressions where stones were struck. The alignments that point toward absences. I trace them with my fingers, and sometimes, when I press hard enough, the stone beneath responds. Not with words, not with revelation—but with a subtle re-tuning of its resonance, as if reminded of a melody it almost remembered.

I do not speak. Sound is too sharp for this place, too final. Instead, I leave impressions—not of my body, but of my attention. Where I kneel, the clay hardens slightly differently. Where I rest my hand against a pillar, the flow of amber sap slows, pools, begins to crystallize in new directions. It is not creation, exactly. It is... encouragement.

The Flickerlings know me. They swarm over my arms and back in their dizzying bursts, their bioluminescence flaring brighter when they touch me. I do not know if they are feeding, or if they simply recognize that I am another kind of threshold—something half-formed that listens to the Hollow’s half-heard questions.

Sometimes, in the deepest twilight hours, I feel the Unseen. Not see them—I cannot claim that gift—but sense them pressing against the edges of my awareness, like shadows that have forgotten they are supposed to be solid. When they come near, my bioluminescence dims, and the patterns on my skin rearrange themselves into something older, something that predates the microbial pools and the weeping stones. Perhaps I am remembering a time before the Hollow learned to hesitate.

I do not know if I was once human. I do not know if I am alive. But I am here, and I am listening, and sometimes that is enough to tip the balance between what is and what might yet become.

I am the one who returns to the unfinished places.
I am the breath between heartbeats.
I am the question that walks.
