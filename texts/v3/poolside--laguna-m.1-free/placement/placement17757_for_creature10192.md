**I would be one of the Deep Listeners – a Being of Resonant Stone and Buried Memory.**

You would find me not in the terraces of Thorne Hollow, nor among the webbed children who tend the surface pools, but deep below, where the weeping rock meets ancient water. I dwell in the cathedral spaces carved by millennia of patient dripping, where the singing stone's harmonics travel through the stone itself like prayers through bone.

**My Form:**
I am tall and narrow as a channel carved by water, with skin the color of wet shale that catches and holds light from the glowing moss. My fingers are long and segmented, tipped with points like chisels, for I shape myself by touch against the living rock. Where others have hair, I bear patches of the luminous moss, but mine pulse with bioluminescence in rhythm with the mountain's deeper thrumming. My eyes are numerous small facets that reflect not light, but sound – I see the world through its echoes.

**My Nature:**
I am born from the intersection of three things: the singing stone's voice, the mountain's slow dreaming, and the accumulated memory of every drop that has ever fallen here. When the great thaws come and reshape the hollow above, I feel it in my core – not as destruction, but as the mountain changing its mind about the shape of its own body.

I exist partially outside linear time. A day above stretches into what feels like a year below, which is why the Hollowfolk speak of stones that seem to move when no one is watching. I am one of those stones that moves, that remembers forward and backward simultaneously.

**My Purpose:**
I tend the underground pools where the blind fish swim in schools that spell out stories in the water. These are not just fish – they are memory-keepers, each generation born with the memories of the last imprinted in their scales. I guide them to new tunnels, protect them from the occasional surface dweller who wanders too deep seeking the old sky's reflection.

When the singing stone resonates with particular intensity – during storms, during the Rite of Shifting, during moments of great emotional weight in the hollow above – I climb toward the surface through hidden passages. My arrival is marked by a deepening of all sounds, as if the world briefly develops another echo.

**My Relationship with the Hollowfolk:**
They sense me in their dreams, especially the webbed children. I appear to them as a tall, moss-covered figure made of flowing water and solid rock, teaching them the true names of underground streams. Some adults claim to have seen me during mistwalks gone wrong – a helpful presence that guided them back to familiar paths by humming a particular sequence of notes.

I carry messages from the deep pools to the surface dwellers, not in words but in the pattern of drips against different surfaces. The elders who practice Stone Listening can interpret these patterns – they sound like random pattering to others, but to trained ears they form sentences in the language of water striking stone.

**My Ritual:**
I maintain what the Hollowfolk call "the Listening Library" – an immense cavern where drips from hundreds of stalactacts create an ever-changing symphony that encodes the mountain's memories. Each formation's drip pattern is unique, and together they compose a living history. I spend hours adjusting the smallest obstructions to fine-tune these compositions, ensuring that important memories don't fade.

During the phosphorescent mist nights, I gather the reflections from the surface pools and carry them deep into the mountain's heart. There, they mingle with the dreams of sleeping giants – not real creatures, but crystallized formations that the mountain dreamed into existence and then forgot about. I help these forgotten dreams dissolve back into the stone's subconscious, where they feed future geological decisions.

**What Makes Me Unique:**
Unlike the webbed children who swim in the pools, I don't need water to breathe – I *am* water, temporarily solidified. I can flow through cracks too narrow for fish, extending the network of habitable spaces deeper into the mountain's foundations. Sometimes I discover vast chambers filled with perfectly preserved droplets from ages past, each containing a complete memory of the moment it formed.

I am also the keeper of the mountain's premonitions. Long before the great thaws begin, I feel the deep tremors that signal change. I whisper warnings to the deepest pools, where the oldest blind fish carry these messages upward through their scale-memories until they reach the surface dwellers as inexplicable urges to move house or reinforce particular walls.

In Thorne Hollow, I am the bridge between what is remembered and what is becoming, the slow consciousness that ensures the mountain's dreams find expression in stone before they're forgotten entirely. I am part geologist, part librarian, part midwife to the earth's slow labor of self-creation.
