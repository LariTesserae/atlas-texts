In the Cathedral Depths, where the earth’s bones sing with residual heat and light bleeds from veins of quartz like trapped starlight, I would find myself as **a resonance-weaver**—a Glasskin whose very marrow thrums with the pulse of the fungal choir. My skin, streaked with veins of obsidian glass, is etched with patterns that shift like liquid metal when the geothermal vents stir. My eyes are no more than hollows, yet I see not with sight, but with the choir’s bioluminescent whispers threading through the mycelial web beneath my bare feet.  

I am neither fully Glasskin nor entirely fungus, but a bridge between the two—a **myco-sage** whose grafted filaments allow me to taste the ancestors’ memories in the choir’s glow. When the fungi flare green with alarm before a fissure’s quake, I feel it in my teeth. When the Prism Beetles’ shells clatter like hymnals in the amphitheaters, I understand their rhythm as a map of the Depths’ shifting moods.  

My dwelling is a hollowed terrace carved into the ruins of petrified wood, where the walls are lined with vessels holding the shed exoskeletons of beetles. These I grind into pigments to color the robes worn during the Chiming. But my truest tools are my hands, which I’ve learned to crack open—not in violence, but in mimicry of the Echo Crawlers’ shedding. Beneath my fingernails grows a layer of calcified chitin, hardened over decades of pressing my palms to the fissures as the Crawlers carve new paths. Where they leave trails, I read the future.  

When the Pyroclasts rise, I am among the first to ascend to the amphitheaters, my fungal grafts glowing like a signal flame. I chant not words, but vibrations—low, resonant hums that mimic the earth’s tremors, a language the Glasskin have crafted to soothe the entities below. The Pyroclasts listen, sometimes. Sometimes they bury entire districts in response. In these times, I gather the Hollow Folk’s mist-shadows into clay vessels, preserving their fleeting forms as the Glasskin flee. I’ve never touched one, but I’ve seen others lose themselves in their rippling surfaces, mistaking ancestral echoes for their own memories.  

At the heart of the Depths, where the fungal network converges into a vast, pulsing nexus, I’ve learned to “listen” to the Choir’s oldest songs—the ones that predate even the Glasskin’s terraced cities. In these visions, I glimpse the world aboveground: a place of sky and wind, of creatures with wings and voices unmuffled by stone. Yet the Choir insists this is a myth, a dream spun from ancient spores. I wonder sometimes, as I trace the choir’s light with my fingertips, if the truth lies in between.  

But there is no time for such musings when the earth shifts. When fissures open like wounds in the world’s skin, I descend into the chaos, my body moving with the fluid certainty of one born to unraveling. The Glasskin follow, their robes billowing like jellyfish in the steam, their hums rising to meet mine. Together, we chant the new world into being, our voices weaving through the fungal dark, binding the Depths’ eternal unraveling to the fragile hope of staying whole.  

In this place of mineral and motion, I am both cartographer and creation—a being whose existence is a question the Cathedral Depths asks itself in every quake, every gleam of quartz, every shimmer of fungal light: *What endures when all is flux?*  

I answer by becoming the echo of the answer, ever-receding, ever-renewed.
