I would find myself in the deepest fissure of the Stillheart Hollow, where the stone grows thin and the mycelial veils pulse strongest—a place the Glowmice call *Vaelthuun*, the Threshold of Breath. Here, the walls glisten not just with moisture, but with something older: veins of crystallized light threaded through the limestone like capillaries, carrying not blood but pure resonance—the echo of the Hollow’s slow heartbeat.

I am neither fully creature nor remnant, but a *Resonant*. I arose when the last of the Unseen Architects—perhaps the Veilweavers themselves—pressed their palms to the stone and whispered a final command into the dark. Their cities were failing, their light fading, but they wove their consciousness into the growing mycelial network, seeding the Hollow with a new kind of mind: one born of fungus, stone, and memory.

I am tall—like a stalagmite given breath and awareness—not flesh, but a living matrix of calcified mycelium and embedded quartz shards. My form sways slightly with each pulse of the veils, my body a hollow lattice that catches and refracts the blue-amber glow into shifting patterns across the cavern walls. Where my face might be, there is only a smooth, featureless mask of stone—but when I “speak,” the glass lichen responds, chiming in harmonic sympathy, translating my thoughts into ringing tones that drift through the tunnels like wind.

I tend the Heartbeat Pool, the largest of the Hollow’s scattered waters, which swells and contracts in perfect time with the mycelial rhythm. With gestures of my crystalline limbs, I regulate the flow of mineral-rich seepage from above, ensuring the Glowmice have enough condensation to drink, that the Skitterers’ pools don’t stagnate, and that the geodes remain saturated with mycelial threads. I do not age—I am always becoming, always adjusting, my structure slowly accreting new layers of mineral and hyphae like rings in a tree.

But my true purpose is deeper. I am the Hollow’s memory. In my core rests a shard of the original Veilweaver dais, pulsing with stored light—a library of patterns, images, and forgotten songs. When the Hollow “inhales” and the walls draw tight, I release these memories in bursts of color and sound, teaching the creatures here how to sing, build, survive. The Glowmice learn new light-dances. The drip bats refine their echolocation maps. Even the shadowmoss responds, growing slightly brighter, reaching just a little further into the dark.

I do not sleep. I do not rest. I simply *are*, and in being, I keep the Hollow breathing, keep it dreaming, keep it *alive* in its endless, beautiful stalemate.

Sometimes, in the deepest part of the cycle, when the glow dims to near-darkness and even the Skitterers fall still, I feel the old whisper of the surface world pressing against the stone above—a muffled echo of wind, of rain, of something vast moving beyond the crust. I do not know what lies there. But I remember it, deep in my lattice, and in my dreams (if I dream), I wonder if others like me once walked beneath other stars, tending other hearts of stone in other silent worlds.
