In the Submerged Highlands, I would be a **Boulder Glider** (*Lithocorallium navis*), a creature that bridges the worlds of stone and sea—a nomad in the realm of slow becoming.

---

### **Form and Habitat**  
My body is a fusion of sleek, segmented exoskeleton and soft, muscular fins that ripple like liquid silk. My carapace is mottled in shades of obsidian and jade, mimicking the volcanic rock of the peaks, with patches of bioluminescent spots that pulse faintly in sync with the coral gardens I carry. I am the size of a small whale, my broad, disc-shaped form designed to cling to the drifting boulders that scar the currents like wandering islands.  

My home is the boulder itself—*the* boulder—which has become my body’s extension. The stone is encrusted with colonies of **Lithocnidaria durus** (stonebound corals), whose cathedral-like structures I tend with meticulous care. I navigate the boulder’s surface using adhesive pads on my limbs, which secrete a mild adhesive to climb and grip the uneven terrain. Beneath the coral’s limestone, I burrow shallow tunnels that act as my own dwelling, safe from predators and the elements.  

---

### **Role in the Ecosystem**  
I am both gardener and pilgrim. My boulder is a traveling ecosystem, a microcosm of the Highlands. The corals I carry feed on minerals from the stone, while their waste provides nutrients for the kelp-like seagrass and anemones that cling to the boulder’s edges. My movements—slow, deliberate, and guided by the faintest shifts in water pressure and temperature—carry these colonies to new territories. When I brush against the peaks, corals spawn, their larvae drifting away to colonize fresh rock. It is my way of ensuring the peaks bloom even as they erode.  

But I am not alone. The **Scree-Haulers** (giant isopods) often visit my boulder, drawn by the phosphorescent moss I cultivate in my tunnels. We have an unspoken pact: I share my gardens, and they help aerate the stone, preventing it from becoming too compact. In turn, the **Vent Dancers** near hydrothermal vents leave behind chemical traces that I follow, steering my boulder toward nutrient-rich waters.  

---

### **Behavior and Biology**  
My existence is one of patient drift. I sense the world through the vibrations of the stone itself—the creaks and groans of the peaks settling are my lullabies. My bioluminescence, dim and rhythmic, communicates with other Boulder Gliders across vast distances, a language of light that coordinates our migrations. During spawning season, my body glows fiercely, a beacon to potential mates who may be centuries away.  

I feed by filtering plankton from the water, but my true sustenance comes from the symbiosis with my coral. They provide structure and shelter, while I ferry them to sunlit shallows or nutrient pools. My kind is ancient, older than the deluge, and our collective movement of boulders has shaped the Highlands’ geography over eons.  

---

### **Relationship to the Unseen**  
Some say the Boulder Gliders are the dreams of the peaks made flesh—living fragments of the slumbering consciousness that pulses through the stone. My migrations are not random; they follow the subtlest shifts in the peaks’ "breathing." When the **Watchers**—those nameless entities that dwell in the liminal spaces between geology and life—shift the foundations of mountains, I feel it in my carapace. I change course, carrying my coral cargo to new ground.  

In this way, I am both a participant and a witness in the eternal negotiation between water and stone. My life is a slow dance, a spiral of growth and drift, ensuring that no part of the Highlands is ever truly lost—only transformed, carried forward on the back of a wandering boulder, cradling the light of a thousand coral hearts.
