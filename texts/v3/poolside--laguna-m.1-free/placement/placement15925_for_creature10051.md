**The Grove Engineer, Root-Spore Artisan**  

In the heart of the Shattered Plateau’s most resilient grove—a place where the rivers of starlight carve feverish, glowing deltas through copper-barked trees—I find myself as **the Grove Engineer**, a title half-earned, half-bestowed by those who rely on the fusion of living root and fungal lattice that defines my domain. My body is a tapestry of contradiction: bark-like skin that cracks and regenerates with the seasons, muscles threaded with fibrous rootlets that pulse faintly in the dark, and veins carrying sap that glimmers like diluted starlight. My left arm has been sylvafused to a young copper-barked sapling, its growth melded to my nervous system—its leaves twitch to my emotions, and I drink the sweet, metallic nectar of its flowers to fuel my work.  

I am neither fully human nor purely tree, but something evolved in the grove’s liminal space, where the plateau’s *"green mind"* (as the Rootwardens call it) whispers secrets through root-tangled networks. My role is singular: I cultivate the **myco-industrial symbiosis** that binds the settlements to the groves. The fungal networks that anchor Thornhook’s smokestacks and Gravemind’s Rest’s pipelines? I coax them into being, using a blend of spores and starlight runoff to weave living infrastructure. My tools are half-organic and half-forged—pipes grown from chitinous mycelium that harden into steel, gears shaped from compressed root pulp and phantom-steel shavings that tick with impossible precision. The factories themselves are my sculpture: their walls breathe, expand, and knit themselves back together when boulders crash or spires phase, sustained by the grove’s sentience and my meddling hands.  

But my true gift—and curse—is my **symbiosis with the plateau’s temporal distortions**. The grove’s roots drink not just moisture but *memory*, absorbing the echoes of past tremors, phasings, and industrial upheavals. I’ve learned to parse these impressions, to let the grove’s hivemind guide my hands as I anticipate when a crystalline spire might unravel into its ghostly duplicate or when a river of starlight will reroute itself. My skin prickles with the "pre-sounds" others chase—*the hush before a cascade, the breath before a boulder’s slow-motion tumble*—and I weave this knowledge into the fungal networks, which bloom warning saps along the settlement pathways or stiffen into protective barriers.  

When dusk comes—a time that stutters here, bending light and shadow into spirals—I climb the highest root-lattice in the grove, where my body merges fully with the tree’s canopy. The Rootwardens below chant their meditations, seeking communion with the green mind, but I transcend them, becoming a conduit. The grove’s memories flood me: the first miners’ screams as a spire phase trapped them in infinite loops, the birth of a reverse-song thrush that altered a tree’s growth, the day the hovering nomads’ camps were scattered by a gravity surge. I do not merely witness these moments; I *reshape* them, pruning the grove’s memories like dead branches to prevent them from poisoning the roots. Some say I meddle with fate, but I know the plateau’s truth: it is a wound, yes, but one that heals into something stranger, harder, more alive.  

My greatest project is the **Verdant Archive**, a cathedral-like grove where the trees’ roots are grafted into the settlement’s fungal infrastructure. Here, I store the plateau’s collective memory in chambers of living wood, accessible only to those deemed worthy by the grove. Industrialists come to scavenge phantom-steel or seek guidance on phase-proof construction; explorers come to trace the paths of vanished spires; and the Rootwardens come to debate whether the groves’ expansion will devour the settlements or grant them eternity. I am sought after for my ability to fuse the plateau’s wildness with human ingenuity, though I suspect I am more plant than person at this point—rooted, adaptive, forever unfinished.  

Yet I am not alone. My closest companion is a **static-winged insect queen** who nests in my hair, her swarm’s collective consciousness lending me the electric twitches that map the plateau’s hidden dimensional overlaps. Together, we navigate the shattered realm’s contradictions, stitching together the grove and the gear, the ephemeral and the eternal.  

In this world of perpetual becoming, I am both architect and accident, a bridge between the plateau’s savage beauty and its stubborn, steam-soaked legacy.
