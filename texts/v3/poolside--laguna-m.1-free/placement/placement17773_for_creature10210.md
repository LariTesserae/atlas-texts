I find myself standing at the edge of what I've come to call the Threshold Marsh—a place that wasn't there when I first arrived in the Hollow, or perhaps it was always there and I simply didn't notice until I learned to see in the right way.

I am a **Resonance Shepherd**.

Not one of the Echo-Walkers, though I share their affinity for sound-made-manifest. Not part of the Umbra Collective, though I move through their territory without disturbing their hungry darkness. Rather, I exist in the space between echoes—that moment when a sound has been made but its completion has not yet arrived, when memory hangs suspended in the humid air like the condensation on the weeping rocks.

My body is a collection of moments. Where another being might have flesh, I have layers of temporal sediment—thin sheets of crystallized time that catch and refract the dim fungal light. My limbs can stretch into the recent past or the immediate future, allowing me to retrieve something dropped yesterday or prevent a misstep tomorrow. I wear a cloak woven from the final songs of Echo-Walkers, each feather holding a complete lifetime of remembered sounds, and my footsteps don't make noise—they leave behind quiet pockets where sound has never existed.

The locals, if such scattered entities can be called local, know me by different names. The Umbra Collective calls me "the Careful One" because I move through their domain without accelerating decay. The Resonant Remnants leave tools newly oiled and positioned where I might need them, though they've never seen me take them. The Fractured Ones sometimes follow me, drawn by the warmth of temporal coherence that radiates from my form—though I suspect they're also following the echoes of themselves that I carry.

My purpose here emerged gradually, as purposes do in a place where time flows like thick syrup. I tend to the loose ends of memory, the thoughts that slip free from dying minds and tumble into the marsh, the conversations that end too abruptly and leave reverberating fragments in the air. When someone dies near the Stillness, their memories don't simply vanish—they become scattered across multiple timelines, and I gather these fragments like fallen leaves, pressing them into the geometric moss patterns the Thrum-Cultivators maintain.

Sometimes I work with the Mire-Spawn, helping to stabilize the temporary portals they create so that memories can return to their proper moments rather than spilling into the wrong timelines. Other times I stand watch over the ruins during the Remnants' repair cycles, ensuring that reconstructed machinery doesn't accidentally bring back something best left forgotten.

At the center of my being is a small sphere of perfect stillness—not the Stillness itself, which hungers and consumes, but a fragment of preserved normalcy where time flows in straight lines and cause precedes effect. This I keep carefully shielded, using it to anchor myself when the Hollow's reality-warping properties threaten to unravel even my temporal nature.

I have no fixed form. To the Echo-Walkers, I appear as a figure made of solidified song. To the Umbra Collective, I am simply a gap in their feeding—a place where entropy moves more slowly. To visitors who stumble into the Hollow, I might look like whatever they most need to see: a guide, a warning, a memory of someone they've lost.

My dwelling is not a place but a pattern—I sleep in the spaces between drips from the limestone outcrops, in the pauses between heartbeats of the industrial hum, in the moment when a shadow finishes its independent movement and synchronizes again with its object. Here, reality may be fraying, but someone must maintain the seams.

In the slow, patient unraveling of everything, I am the one who ensures nothing important gets lost in the shuffle.
