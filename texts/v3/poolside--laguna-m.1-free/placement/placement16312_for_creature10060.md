I find myself in the hollows—the spaces between what is named and what remains unspoken in this scarred landscape. Here, where the wind's growl softens to a whisper and the stone remembers every footfall that ever pressed against its surface, I am the **Boundary Listener**.

I am neither wholly of the living nor the dead, but something that exists in the sediment of moments—the fine dust that settles when a Storm Skimmer's chirp harmonizes with the Veinwalker's song, creating frequencies that most cannot hear but everyone feels in their marrow. My body is composed of the same mineral-laden silt that coats the seasonal riverbeds, but animated by something the Unseen Symbionts slipped into the water long ago—not quite consciousness, not quite instinct, but the echo of intention.

I appear as a figure woven from moving sand and reflective particles, my form constantly shifting between the silhouette of whatever creature last passed through my territory and something more geometric—angles that shouldn't exist in flesh, surfaces that reflect not light but possibility. When the storms come and reality buckles, I become more solid, more real, because the boundaries themselves strengthen during chaos.

My domain is the **Threshold Groves**—areas where three distinct ecosystems meet: the cliff-edge terraces where Veinwalkers make their phosphorescent markings, the underground cavern networks where Echo Architects reshape memory-ruins, and the mist-sink valleys where Hollow Giants exhale the moisture that feeds everything above. These are the places where decisions happen, where paths diverge, where the plateau itself seems to hold its breath.

I tend these boundaries like a gardener tends the edges of competing gardens. When a Veinwalker colony's migration route threatens to disrupt an Echo Architect's restoration pattern, I help negotiate the timing. When a Hollow Giant's movement would destroy a particularly beautiful spiral tree formation, I guide the Seedsingers to relocate their pollination efforts elsewhere. I collect the crystallized memories that fall from the temporal distortions—those moments when someone experiences déjà vu so intense it leaves physical traces—and arrange them in patterns that help stabilize the local reality.

My song is the sound of sand shifting, of stones settling, of the almost-audible click when two temporal streams briefly align. Those who've been Storm-Touched say I appear in their dreams as a figure made of hourglass glass, whispering directions that seem wrong but lead somewhere necessary. They call me the Cartographer of Almost-Places.

During the reality storms, when mesas reshape themselves and rivers run backward, I become crucial. While others flee or hide, I walk the transforming landscape, mapping the new connections between old places. My body absorbs the chaotic energies that would otherwise fracture weaker minds, storing them until the storm passes and I can redistribute them safely back into the plateau's systems. Sometimes I crystallize temporarily, becoming part of the growing monument-landscape, until the next convergence needs a boundary adjusted.

The Heart of the Wound pulses strongest near my territories, which is why I've learned to move in rhythm with its thrum, matching my own internal cadence to that primeval beat. When the Seedsingers' ephemeral lives end and their bodies become saplings, I'm there to ensure their roots find purchase in soil enriched by the previous cycle's endings. 

I am the plateau's subconscious—the part that knows where things should go even when the conscious mind of the land is busy reshaping itself. Not a guardian exactly, but a facilitator of transitions, a weaver of connections across the impossible distances that separate one moment from another in this wounded, wonderful place where beginnings and endings happen simultaneously in the space between heartbeats.
