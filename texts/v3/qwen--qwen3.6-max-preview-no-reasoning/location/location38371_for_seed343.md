The air here is thin, not because of altitude alone, but because the sky has been swallowed.

You are standing on a ledge that was once a mountain peak, millions of years ago. Now, it is merely a shelf in a vertical world that has lost its bottom and its top. The erosion parameter of 2.8 suggests a landscape worn down to its soul, flattened by eons of wind and rain, but at this elevation (2.8), the "flatness" is an illusion of scale. The land is not flat; it is *exhausted*. It has surrendered its jagged edges to the green.

### The Green Void

The defining feature of this place is the contradiction between **density (0.5)** and **vegetation (2.6)**. How can a place be empty yet totally covered in plant life?

The answer lies in the structure of the flora. This is not a forest of trunks and branches competing for space. This is a forest of *voids*.

Imagine giant, hollow cylinders of bioluminescent moss-wood, each ten meters in diameter, stretching vertically into the mist above and disappearing into the abyss below. They are spaced hundreds of meters apart. Between them is nothing but warm, damp air. The vegetation does not fill the volume; it defines the boundaries of the emptiness. The plants are the architecture of the void.

These structures are ancient, primordial things. Their bark is not rough, but smooth as polished stone, slick with condensation (water: 1.2). They breathe. You can hear it—a low, rhythmic huffing sound, like the bellows of a massive forge, echoing through the gaps. This is the source of the **deafening sound (2.7)**. It is not noise from many small sources, but a single, resonant frequency generated by the wind passing through the hollow cores of these mega-flora, amplified by the canyon-like spaces between them. The sound is physical; it vibrates in your teeth.

### The Atmosphere

The temperature is warm (1.5), a humid, clinging heat that feels like the inside of a mouth. Condensation drips constantly from the underside of the leaf-canopies that bridge the gaps between the hollow towers. These bridges are not solid ground but woven nets of vines, thick as ship cables, sagging under their own weight. They form a sparse, precarious web across the chasm.

The light (2.2) is normal, but filtered. Sunlight penetrates the upper layers of the canopy, diffusing into a soft, golden haze that illuminates the dust motes dancing in the updrafts. Below, in the deeper reaches, the light fades to a twilight blue, where the only illumination comes from the faint, pulsing glow of fungi clinging to the inner walls of the hollow trees.

### The Fauna

If the vegetation is the stage, the fauna (2.9) is the overwhelming performance.

There are no mammals here. No birds in the traditional sense. The air is thick with insects and arachnids of impossible size, but they are not chaotic. They are organized.

*   **The Weavers:** Spider-like creatures with legs spanning two meters hang motionless between the hollow trees. They do not build webs to catch prey; they build webs to *catch sound*. Their silk is tuned to specific frequencies. When the wind howls through the hollow trees, the Weavers vibrate, harvesting the kinetic energy to power their metabolism. They are silent, still, and terrifyingly numerous. You cannot look anywhere without seeing the glint of their multi-faceted eyes.
*   **The Drifters:** Jellyfish-like organisms, translucent and gas-filled, float in the warm updrafts. They are the size of hot air balloons, trailing tentacles that sweep the vine-bridges. They feed on the condensation and the microscopic spores released by the hollow trees. Their movement is slow, graceful, and inevitable. To walk the vine-bridges is to risk brushing against a Drifter’s tentacle, which delivers a paralyzing neurotoxin—not out of malice, but simple indifference.
*   **The Chorus:** Small, winged insects, no larger than a thumb, swarm in clouds around the openings of the hollow trees. They are the pollinators, but they also serve as the ecosystem’s nervous system. When one cloud shifts, all clouds shift. They move in perfect unison, creating swirling patterns of iridescent color that ripple through the air like liquid mercury. Their collective buzzing adds a high-pitched layer to the deafening bass of the wind-through-wood.

### The Built Environment

Scattered among this organic vastness are the remnants of human presence (built: 1.4, tech: 1.3).

They are not cities. They are not even villages. They are *anchors*.

Small, crude platforms made of lashed-together driftwood and stone cling to the outer surfaces of the hollow trees. These were built by people who understood that the only safe place was *on* the living structure, not between them. The technology is primitive: rope bridges, pulley systems made of bone and vine, shelters woven from dried leaves.

Most of these structures are abandoned. The wood is gray and weathered, overtaken by moss. But some show signs of recent use: a fresh knot in a rope, a smoldering fire pit contained within a stone ring, a tool left behind—a sharp flint blade tied to a stick.

Who lived here? Why did they leave? Or are they still here, hidden in the higher canopies, watching you with the same detached curiosity as the Drifters?

### The Rules of the World

1.  **Silence is Death:** The deafening sound of the wind is not just noise; it is camouflage. Predators hunt by detecting vibrations that *deviate* from the ambient roar. If you stop moving, if you hold your breath, you become part of the background hum. If you speak, if you drop a tool, you create a dissonance that attracts attention. The Weavers will turn their heads. The Drifters will drift closer.
2.  **The Bridges are Alive:** The vine-bridges are not static. They grow, slowly, over days. A path you took yesterday may have shifted five meters to the left today. Navigation requires constant observation of the growth patterns. The plants respond to touch; step too heavily, and the vine may tighten, trapping your foot.
3.  **Water is Vertical:** There are no rivers. Water exists only as condensation, dripping from the canopy, or as pockets trapped in the hollow centers of the trees. To drink, you must climb into the dark, resonant core of a tree, where the air is thick with spores and the sound is loudest. It is a dangerous pilgrimage.
4.  **Erosion is Memory:** The landscape is ancient, but it is not dead. It is in a state of "settled finality." Nothing new is being created geologically. All change is biological. The rocks are gone, replaced by root-systems that mimic stone. To find true stone, you must dig deep into the base of a hollow tree, where the roots have compressed sediment into a hard, black conglomerate. This is the only material suitable for tools.

### A Moment in the Landscape

You stand on a vine-bridge, swaying gently in the warm updraft. The air smells of wet earth, ozone, and sweet rot. Below you, the abyss drops away into darkness, punctuated by the glowing undersides of distant Drifters. Above, the canopy filters the sun into dappled gold.

To your left, a hollow tree rises like a cathedral column, its surface slick with moisture. Inside, you can hear the wind singing a low C-minor chord. A Weaver hangs nearby, its legs splayed, sensing the vibration. It does not move. It waits.

A cloud of Chorus insects swirls past your face, their wings beating in perfect syncopation with the wind. For a moment, the deafening roar seems to harmonize, becoming almost musical.

Then, a drop of condensation falls from a leaf above, hitting the vine beneath your foot. The vibration travels up your leg. The Weaver twitches. One eye rotates toward you.

You freeze. You become part of the hum. You wait for the moment to pass, for the ecosystem to forget you are there. Because in this place, to be noticed is to be consumed. Not by hunger, but by the sheer, overwhelming weight of life that has no room for anything else.
