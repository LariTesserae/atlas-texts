The world is not a planet, but a vessel. Specifically, it is the interior of a colossal, forgotten pressure valve, a spherical chamber of brass and riveted iron no larger than a cathedral’s nave, suspended in the void between stars or perhaps buried deep within the crust of a dying giant. It is a pocket of existence, hidden away from the macro-scale, yet within its curved walls, an entire ecosystem thrives with terrifying intensity.

**The Terrain: The Rusted Bowl**

There is no natural ground here. Every surface is manufactured. The "floor" is a concave dish of hammered copper and steel plating, pitted by eons of condensation and chemical runoff. This bowl slopes gently toward a central drain that does not empty into anything visible, but rather circulates the medium back up through massive, groaning pipes that line the upper circumference of the sphere.

Because the erosion parameter is so high (2.8), the metal has not just rusted; it has *aged*. It has developed a patina of such depth and complexity that it mimics geological strata. Layers of oxidized green, flaky orange, and smooth black lacquer have built up over millennia, creating a topography of ridges, valleys, and plateaus entirely composed of corrosion. What looks like a mountain range is actually a series of concentric rust-rings formed by the slow drip of condensation from the ceiling. The "soil" is a slurry of metallic dust, algae, and decayed organic matter, thick and warm to the touch.

**The Medium: Atmospheric Water**

This is not a dry place. The water level is 2.7/3, but it is not an ocean you swim in; it is an atmosphere you breathe, though barely. The air is saturated to the point of viscosity. It is a wall of rain that never falls because it is already everywhere. The humidity is absolute. Droplets coalesce on every surface, running down the curved walls in endless, shimmering sheets. To move through this space is to wade through a mist so dense it feels like walking through lukewarm syrup.

Visibility is limited to ten feet. Beyond that, the world dissolves into a grey-white haze illuminated by the ambient glow of bioluminescence and the occasional spark of arcing electricity. The temperature is consistently warm (2.0/3), a humid, tropical heat that clings to the skin and encourages rapid growth. It is the warmth of a compost heap, of a engine room left running for centuries.

**Vegetation: The Copper Forest**

Vegetation is lush (2.2/3), but it is alien to any terrestrial garden. These plants do not root in soil; they root in metal. They are lithophytes adapted to extract nutrients from rust and conductive salts. Ferns unfurl their fronds in shades of iridescent blue and purple, their leaves coated in a waxy substance that repels the constant moisture. Vines thick as human arms, textured like braided cable, spiral up the vertical pipe-structures, anchoring themselves into rivet heads and seams.

Moss covers everything. It is not green, but a spectrum of neon yellows, electric blues, and deep crimsons, glowing faintly in the dim light. This moss is the primary producer, feeding on the electrochemical potential difference between different metals. Where copper meets iron, the moss burns brightest, a living battery harvesting energy from the vessel’s own decay.

The density of life is sparse (0.8/3) in terms of large structures—there are vast open spaces between the clusters of vegetation—but those spaces are filled with the heavy, wet air and microscopic life. You might walk fifty feet across a flat expanse of polished bronze before encountering another significant feature, but that emptiness is deceptive. It is full of spores, pollen, and drifting micro-fauna.

**Fauna: The Scavengers of Steam**

Life is abundant (1.7/3), but it is small, quick, and strange. There are no large mammals. Instead, the fauna consists of insectoid and amphibious creatures adapted to the high-pressure, high-humidity environment.

*   **The Rust-Mites:** Beetle-like creatures the size of fists, with carapaces made of hardened resin and scavenged metal shards. They click loudly as they scuttle across the plating, eating the softer layers of oxidation. Their movement is a constant, skittering soundtrack.
*   **Vapor-Jellyfish:** Translucent, bell-shaped organisms that drift through the dense mist, trailing long, stinging tentacles. They pulse with internal light, changing color based on the electrical charge of the surrounding air. They are beautiful and deadly, filtering the airborne plankton.
*   **Pipe-Spiders:** Large, eight-legged arachnids that weave webs between the hanging chains and pulleys. Their silk is conductive, allowing them to sense vibrations through the metal structure itself. They wait patiently for prey to land on their webs, which hum with a low-frequency buzz.

The fauna is active (dynamic 2.1/3). There is a constant cycle of predation and decay. A vapor-jellyfish descends, catches a cluster of mites, and is then ensnared by a pipe-spider. The remains fall to the floor, where the moss consumes them, releasing gases that rise back into the mist. It is a closed loop, efficient and brutal.

**Sound: The Symphony of Decay**

The soundscape is loud (2.1/3). It is not a chaotic noise, but a layered symphony of industrial and biological processes.

1.  **The Drip:** A constant, rhythmic patter of water falling from the ceiling onto the metal surfaces. It echoes strangely in the spherical chamber, creating a reverberant drone.
2.  **The Hum:** The underlying vibration of the machinery. Though the original purpose of the valve is lost, residual energy still flows through the pipes. A low-frequency thrum vibrates in the chest, a reminder that this place is alive with power.
3.  **The Click:** The sound of thousands of rust-mites moving across the metal. It sounds like rain on a tin roof, but sharper, more erratic.
4.  **The Hiss:** Steam escaping from faulty seals. Sharp, sudden bursts of white noise that startle the fauna into stillness.
5.  **The Squelch:** The sound of footsteps sinking into the moss-covered sludge. Wet, organic, intimate.

**Light: The Dim Glow**

Light is normal (1.6/3) in terms of overall brightness, but its quality is unusual. There is no sun. The illumination comes from two sources:

1.  **Bioluminescence:** The moss, the jellyfish, and certain fungi emit a soft, diffuse glow. This creates pools of colored light in the darkness, shifting slowly as the organisms grow and die.
2.  **Arc-Lights:** Occasionally, old, exposed wiring sparks, casting brief, harsh shadows that dance wildly across the curved walls. These flashes illuminate the texture of the rust, revealing the intricate patterns of erosion.

The result is a twilight world, where shapes are defined more by silhouette and glow than by direct illumination. Colors are muted, dominated by greens, browns, and greys, punctuated by sudden flares of blue and violet.

**Rules of the World**

1.  **Conductivity is Life:** Everything is connected. The metal floor, the vines, the air—all conduct electricity. Touching two different metals simultaneously can result in a mild shock. Creatures have evolved insulating shells or behaviors to avoid this.
2.  **Corrosion is Time:** Age is measured in layers of rust. The older something is, the more complex its surface. New objects are smooth and shiny; ancient ones are textured and colorful.
3.  **The Cycle is Closed:** Nothing enters or leaves. All waste is recycled. Death feeds the moss, which powers the food web. The system is perfectly balanced, teetering on the edge of collapse but held together by the sheer inertia of its age.
4.  **Silence is Dangerous:** Because the environment is loud, silence stands out. If the clicking stops, if the dripping ceases, it means something large is nearby, holding its breath. Predators hunt by listening for the absence of noise.

**Atmosphere and Feeling**

To stand in this place is to feel both insignificant and intimately connected. The scale is small (0.2/3)—you can see the curvature of the horizon, the limits of your world. Yet, the complexity is infinite. Every inch of metal tells a story of erosion, every vine a testament to adaptation.

It is a place of beauty and horror. The air is thick with the scent of ozone, rot, and blooming flowers. The warmth is comforting but suffocating. The sounds are overwhelming but rhythmic. It is a machine that has become a jungle, a jungle that has become a machine.

There is a strangeness (1.8/3) to it all. The way the light bends through the mist, the way the plants seem to watch you, the way the metal feels alive underfoot. It is a place that should not exist, yet here it is, enduring, evolving, breathing.

You are an intruder here. Your presence disrupts the delicate balance. The rust-mites scatter. The vapor-jellyfish retreat into the higher mist. The pipe-spiders tense their webs. For a moment, the symphony falters. Then, as you stand still, the rhythm resumes. The drip continues. The hum persists. The world accepts you, not as a guest, but as another variable in its endless equation.

And as you look up, you see the ceiling of the sphere, miles above, covered in a canopy of glowing moss, dripping water that will eventually fall to the floor, feed the roots, and rise again as mist. It is a perfect circle. A perfect prison. A perfect home.
