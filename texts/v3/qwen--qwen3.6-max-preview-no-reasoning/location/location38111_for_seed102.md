The air does not flow; it hangs.

You are standing in the **Throat of the Geode**, a pocket-cavity buried deep within the crust of a world that has forgotten how to rain. Here, the concept of "wet" is an ancient myth. The humidity is zero. The dryness is so absolute it feels like a physical pressure against the eardrums, a vacuum trying to suck the moisture from your very cells. Yet, you do not parch. You cannot. This place is sealed, hermetic, preserved in a bubble of amber-colored gas that smells faintly of ozone and crushed cinnamon.

### The Terrain: Sharp and Young
The walls of this cavern are not smooth. They are jagged, crystalline teeth of fresh formation. The erosion parameter is low because this space was *created* yesterday, or perhaps a million years ago—it doesn’t matter, for time here is static. The rock is obsidian-black but shot through with veins of translucent quartz that glow with a dull, internal heat. There are no rounded stones, no sedimentary layers worn soft by wind or water. Every edge is razor-sharp, every facet a mirror reflecting the dim, sourceless light.

The floor is not soil. It is a carpet of **silica-sponges**. These are not plants in any terrestrial sense, but they mimic vegetation with terrifying fidelity. They are lush (2.1), dense mats of porous, glass-like structures that rise in spirals and fans. They are warm to the touch, radiating the 2.5 temperature of the earth’s deep heart. They do not photosynthesize. They feed on thermal gradients and trace minerals in the air, growing slowly, silently, eternally.

### The Atmosphere: Warm, Dry, and Loud
It is warm, but not humid. Think of the inside of a kiln after the fire has died, but the bricks still hold the heat. The air is thick, not with vapor, but with **micro-particulates**—tiny shards of crystal dust that float in suspension, catching the dim light (0.7) and scattering it into a hazy, golden gloom. You can see only ten feet clearly before the world dissolves into this sparkling fog.

And then, the sound.

It is deafening. A constant, low-frequency **hum** vibrates through the soles of your feet, up your spine, into your skull. It is not a mechanical noise. It is the sound of the crystals growing. As the silica-sponges expand, micro-fractures occur in the surrounding rock, releasing tiny bursts of acoustic energy. Multiply this by millions of organisms per square meter, and you get a roar—a continuous, resonant chord that never changes pitch, never fades, never swells. It is the sound of perfect equilibrium. It is loud (2.2), yet it is also silent, because it has always been there. Your brain begins to filter it out, until you notice the silence beneath it—the absence of breath, of wind, of change.

### Ecology: Abundant Fauna in a Sparse Space
Despite the density being sparse (1.1)—meaning there is vast empty space between the major features—the fauna is abundant (1.5). How? Because the creatures are small, numerous, and invisible to the casual eye.

They are **Glass-Striders**. Imagine insects made of living crystal, no larger than your fingernail, with legs like spun sugar. They scuttle across the sharp terrain, feeding on the mineral exudates of the silica-sponges. They are everywhere. If you look closely at the "empty" spaces between the lush fungal-glass forests, you see them shimmering, moving in coordinated waves. They do not make sound; their movement is frictionless. They are the only things that move in this frozen world.

Occasionally, larger forms emerge from the shadows—**Shard-Stags**, quadrupedal beasts with bodies of fused basalt and antlers of pure quartz. They move slowly, deliberately, as if swimming through heavy syrup. They do not hunt; they graze on the older, harder parts of the silica-sponges. Their eyes are black voids, absorbing the dim light. They ignore you. In this place, predation is obsolete. Everything is sustained by the earth’s heat.

### Built Structures: Scattered and Primitive
Scattered throughout the cavern are artifacts of a previous presence. Not cities, not ruins, but **shrines**. Small, hand-carved niches cut into the rock face, each containing a single, perfect geode. These are the work of primitive tech (0.9)—stone tools, bone chisels, careful manual labor. Who built them? Why? There are no bodies, no bones, no signs of struggle. Only these scattered offerings, placed with reverence in the hollows of the wall.

Some shrines are empty. Others hold geodes that pulse with a faint, rhythmic light, synchronized with the ambient hum. These are not power sources; they are **resonators**. The builders understood the sound. They tried to tune it. But the world is too stable, too eternal. Their efforts were swallowed by the equilibrium.

### The Rules of the World
1.  **No Water Exists:** Any liquid introduced here evaporates instantly, sublimating into the dry gas. Tears vanish before they reach your chin. Sweat disappears before it beads. You are forever dry, forever thirsty, yet never dehydrated. The body adapts, becoming more like the stone.
2.  **Time is Circular, Not Linear:** Nothing decays. Nothing ages. The Glass-Striders die, but their bodies do not rot; they shatter into dust that becomes part of the floor, which feeds the sponges, which grow new crystals, which birth new Striders. It is a closed loop. You could stay here for a thousand years, and you would not age. You would simply become part of the pattern.
3.  **Sound is Structure:** The loud hum is not just noise; it is the force holding the cavern together. If the sound stopped, the walls would collapse. The crystals vibrate at a frequency that counteracts gravity. To speak loudly is dangerous; your voice might disrupt the resonance. So you learn to whisper, or better yet, to think.
4.  **Light is Memory:** The dim light does not come from a sun or a lamp. It is bioluminescence from the deepest roots of the silica-sponges, filtered through layers of rock. It is a cold, blue-white glow that casts long, sharp shadows. Shadows here have weight. They feel solid.

### The Experience
You walk through the Throat of the Geode. Your footsteps make no sound; the soft, porous floor absorbs them. The Glass-Striders part before you like a tide of diamonds. You pass a shrine, its geode pulsing softly. You reach out to touch it, and the vibration travels up your arm, into your chest, syncing with your heartbeat. For a moment, you are not separate from the place. You are a note in the chord.

You realize why the fauna is abundant but the density is sparse. The creatures are not crowded; they are *distributed*. Each one occupies its own precise coordinate in the resonant field. To move is to disrupt the harmony. So they move slowly, carefully, respecting the geometry of the sound.

There is no exit. Or rather, the exit is irrelevant. The world outside is chaotic, wet, changing, noisy in the wrong way. Here, everything is exactly as it should be. The sharp rocks, the lush glass, the warm dry air, the endless hum. It is a prison of perfection. And as you stand there, listening to the song of the stone, you find yourself unwilling to leave.

You sit down on a patch of silica-sponge. It is warm. You close your eyes. The sound fills you. You begin to crystallize.
