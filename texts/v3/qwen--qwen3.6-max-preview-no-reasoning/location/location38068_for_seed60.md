The air here does not move; it hangs, heavy and sweet, thick with the scent of crushed chlorophyll and wet iron. You are standing in a hollow that feels less like a geographical depression and more like a lung held in a deep, suspended breath. The scale is intimate—no larger than a cathedral nave, perhaps thirty meters across at its widest, bounded by walls of living matter that rise vertically into a canopy so dense it filters the normal daylight into a bruised, emerald twilight.

This is the **Sump-Garden**, a place where the boundary between soil, plant, and machine has dissolved into a single, breathing substrate.

### The Substrate: Plant-as-Architecture
There is no ground beneath your feet. There is only the **Weave**. It is a carpet of vegetation so total (2.9/3) that it functions as geology. Mosses, lichens, ferns, and flowering vines have intertwined over centuries into a mat three meters thick, spongy yet firm enough to walk upon. It undulates slightly underfoot, responding to pressure with a slow, hydraulic sigh. Roots do not dig down; they spread sideways, locking together in a tension-compression network that holds the shape of this low-elevation basin.

Because the elevation is low (1.4/3) and the water table is high (1.6/3), the Weave is perpetually saturated. Water weeps from the pores of broad, waxy leaves and condenses on the underside of giant fronds, dripping steadily into hidden pools within the root-mass. The atmosphere is a misting chamber; every surface glistens. To touch anything here is to feel the cool slickness of dew and the warm pulse of sap.

### The Industrial Intrusion
Scattered through this green density are the bones of industry (tech 1.8/3, built 1.3/3). These are not ruins in the traditional sense, for they are not decaying—they are being *consumed* and *repurposed*.

A rusted boiler, once part of some distant factory, lies half-submerged in the mossy floor. But it is not empty. Thick, translucent vines have penetrated its corroded seams, using the metal shell as a trellis. Inside the boiler’s belly, bioluminescent fungi glow faintly, powered by the chemical reactions of rotting wood and oxidizing iron. A pipe, severed long ago, juts out from a wall of ivy, but instead of steam or smoke, it exhales a steady stream of warm, humid vapor, regulated by a valve choked with creeping jasmine. The technology here has been metabolized. The engines are silent, but their shapes remain, fossilized within the vegetative mass.

### The Soundscape: Loud and Wet
Despite the stillness of the forms, the place is loud (sound 1.6/3). It is not a roar, but a cacophony of micro-events amplified by the acoustics of the enclosed space.
*   The **drip-drip-drip** of condensation falling from leaf-tips into water-filled hollows creates a rhythmic, percussive beat.
*   The **creak** of woody stems shifting under their own weight.
*   The **hiss** of escaping vapor from the industrial vents.
*   And underneath it all, a low-frequency **hum**—the sound of millions of capillaries moving water upward against gravity, a vascular thrum that you feel in your teeth more than you hear with your ears.

It is a loud silence, filled with the busy noise of growth.

### The Ecology: Rare Fauna, Total Flora
Fauna is nearly absent (0.6/3). This is a kingdom of plants. No birds nest here; the air is too thick for flight, the branches too entangled. Insects exist, but they are microscopic, unseen architects of decay and pollination. The ecosystem is closed-loop, self-sufficient, and fiercely competitive. Every square inch of surface area is occupied (density 2.7/3). If a leaf falls, it is immediately covered by spores before it hits the ground. If a branch breaks, two others twist into the gap within days.

The weirdness (2.3/3) manifests in the **mimicry**. Some plants have evolved to resemble the industrial artifacts around them. A cluster of pale, tubular flowers mimics the shape of exhaust pipes. A patch of lichen grows in perfect concentric circles, resembling a gear mechanism. The line between the natural and the manufactured has blurred until neither term applies. This is a hybrid entity: a bio-mechanical organism.

### Atmosphere and Light
The temperature is warm (1.8/3), maintained by the decomposition of organic matter deep within the Weave and the residual heat of the buried machinery. It is a tropical warmth, sticky and enveloping. Light (1.8/3) penetrates in shafts, cutting through the canopy like solid beams. Dust motes dance in these columns, but they are not dust—they are pollen, spores, and tiny fragments of flaking rust. The light is green-tinged, filtered through layers of translucent leaves, giving everything an underwater quality.

### Rules of the World
1.  **Stillness is Strength:** Movement is dangerous. The Weave reacts to rapid motion. Step too quickly, and the vines may tighten, sensing vibration as a threat. Move slowly, and the path opens.
2.  **Water is Memory:** The water that drips from the rocks and leaves carries trace minerals from the old industrial site. Drinking it tastes of copper and sweetness. It induces vivid, dream-like states in those who consume it, visions of gears turning and roots spreading.
3.  **No Decay, Only Transformation:** Nothing rots away completely. Everything is absorbed. A dropped tool will be wrapped in roots within a week, incorporated into the structure. The landscape is eternal because it recycles itself perfectly.
4.  **The Hum Guides You:** The low-frequency vascular hum changes pitch depending on the health of the surrounding flora. Follow the lower tones to find stable ground; avoid the higher, sharper frequencies, which indicate areas of rapid, unstable growth or hidden voids.

### Emerging Details
As you stand there, you notice something peculiar about the "walls" of the grove. They are not just trees. Embedded in the bark of the largest central trunk is a control panel, its glass face cracked but intact. Buttons are pressed not by fingers, but by the swelling of knots in the wood. The tree has grown around the interface, interpreting the electrical signals as nutrients. Occasionally, a light flickers behind the bark—a red LED pulsing in time with the drip of water. It is monitoring the garden. Or perhaps, the garden is monitoring it.

A single drop falls from a hanging vine above you. It lands on your shoulder with a audible *plip*. The sound echoes, bouncing off the curved walls of foliage, multiplying until it seems like rain. But it is not raining. It is merely the garden breathing.

You realize you are not standing on earth. You are standing inside a machine that forgot it was ever mechanical, dreaming it is a forest. And the forest, in turn, dreams it is a machine. The distinction is irrelevant. Here, life and industry are the same verb, conjugated in the present tense, slowly, loudly, forever.
