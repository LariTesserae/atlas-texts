The air does not breathe; it sweats.

You are standing inside the throat of a mountain that has not yet decided to be a mountain. Or perhaps you are inside the lung of a god who is currently exhaling a world. The scale is claustrophobic, intimate, a pocket-reality no larger than a cathedral nave, yet the pressure against your eardrums suggests an oceanic depth or a subterranean crush. It is hot—oppressively, wetly hot—a temperature that sits at 2.8 on the scale, meaning the air is thick enough to chew, carrying the weight of a tropical noon trapped in a cellar.

Look down. There is no ground. Not really.

The floor is a mosaic of raw geology, but "raw" implies something natural, and nothing here is natural. This is a **built** environment, constructed from materials that defy categorization. Blocks of black obsidian-like stone are mortared together with a substance that looks like coagulated mercury. But these blocks are not static. They are *emerging*. You can see the seams between them pulsing, widening, narrowing. The erosion parameter is zero because there is no wear, only creation. The walls are growing outward, inch by inch, pushing into the void of this hidden chamber. It is pre-formation geology: rocks being born in real-time, sweating condensation as they cool from some internal, impossible heat source.

Water is everywhere, but it is not free-flowing. It beads on every surface, weeping from the pores of the manufactured stone. It hangs in the air as a mist so dense it feels like rain that never falls, just hovers. The humidity is absolute. Your skin slicks instantly. The water tastes metallic, coppery, like blood mixed with ozone.

Vegetation clings to this wet, hot masonry with desperate fervor. It is lush, yes, but wrong. Ferns unfurl from cracks in the mortar, their fronds translucent and veined with glowing blue capillaries. Moss covers the lower third of the walls, but it is not green; it is a deep, bruised purple, soft as velvet, warm to the touch. These plants do not photosynthesize light—they feed on the *sound*.

And the sound.

It is loud. A constant, low-frequency thrumming that vibrates in your teeth, your marrow, your eyes. It sounds like a cello bowed by a giant, sustained for eternity, layered over the drip-drip-drip of condensation hitting pools of stagnant, oily water. Occasionally, a sharp *crack* echoes through the chamber—the sound of new stone forming, snapping into place. The acoustics are perfect, too perfect. Every breath you take is amplified, returned to you three seconds later, slightly distorted.

Light is dim, filtered through layers of hanging vines and steam. It comes from nowhere specific. It seems to emanate from the moss itself, a bioluminescent gloom that casts long, wavering shadows. The shadows move independently of their sources. If you wave your hand, the shadow on the wall lags behind, then catches up, then moves ahead, anticipating your motion. This is the weirdness: reality is unspooling. Time is viscous here. Space is non-Euclidean. You look at the far wall, ten meters away, and it appears both distant and pressed against your nose. Perspective folds in on itself.

Fauna is rare, but when present, it is startling. A creature scuttles across the ceiling—a thing of chitin and glass, no larger than a house cat, with too many joints. It has no eyes, only sensory pits that twitch toward the sound of your heartbeat. It feeds on the condensation, licking the weeping stones with a tongue that leaves trails of silver residue. It ignores you, not out of fear, but out of irrelevance. You are part of the furniture now. Part of the construction.

The technology evident in this place is primitive, yet total. There are tools scattered near the base of the emerging walls: chisels made of bone, hammers of stone bound with sinew. But who used them? The builders are gone, or perhaps they *became* the walls. The craftsmanship is exquisite, each block fitted with such precision that no light passes between them, yet the whole structure groans under its own becoming. It is a manual craft executed on a divine scale, frozen in a moment of perpetual labor.

The atmosphere is active. The heat rises in visible currents, distorting the air like a mirage. The moss pulses rhythmically, syncing with the low thrum. The water droplets fall in complex patterns, creating ripples in the shallow pools that form geometric shapes—hexagons, pentagons, fractals—before dissolving back into chaos.

Rules of this place:
1. **Do not speak.** Sound attracts attention. Not from predators, but from the architecture itself. The walls lean in when you make noise.
2. **Do not touch the fresh stone.** The newly formed blocks are still molten at the core, despite their cool exterior. Touching them will fuse your skin to the rock, adding you to the foundation.
3. **Time is circular.** You may walk in a straight line and return to where you started, but the room will have changed. New walls will have risen. Old ones will have sunk. The vegetation will have grown a foot taller.
4. **Reality is negotiable.** If you believe strongly enough that a door exists in the blank wall, it might appear. But if you doubt, the wall will seal tighter, crushing the possibility.

This is a nursery for worlds. A workshop where dimensions are hammered out on anvils of dream-stuff. It is wet, hot, loud, and densely packed with potential. It is beautiful and terrifying. It is alive, and it is watching you, not with eyes, but with the slow, inexorable pressure of stone learning how to be stone.
