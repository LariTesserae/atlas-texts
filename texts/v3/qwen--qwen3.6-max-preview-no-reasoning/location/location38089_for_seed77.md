The horizon does not curve; it stretches into a flat, blinding infinity of beige and ochre. This is the **Great Silt-Plain**, a continental expanse where the ground is not earth, but something older and more deliberate: compacted, layered sedimentary memory.

### The Terrain: A Floor Under Construction

The land is low, barely rising above sea level if such a thing still existed here. But there is no ocean. There is only the **Substrate**.

It looks like dried mud cracked by a thousand years of drought, but touch it, and it yields slightly, warm and plastic. This is not natural geology. It is *emergent*. The world is being built from the bottom up, molecule by molecule, in real-time. The surface ripples with slow, tectonic breaths. Cracks open—not from drying, but from growth—revealing fresh, pale layers beneath that smell of wet clay and ozone. These fissures heal within hours, sealing over with a glossy, ceramic skin before reopening elsewhere. The landscape is a living puzzle, constantly rearranging its tiles.

Erosion is negligible because nothing has been here long enough to wear down. Instead, there is *accretion*. Dust settles, binds, hardens, and becomes part of the floor. The ground is always new, always raw, yet ancient in its repetition.

### Water: The Condensation Cycle

Water exists here as a ghost. It does not rain in storms, but in **micro-events**. 

At dawn, when the temperature dips just below the scorching peak, humidity condenses out of the air in sudden, localized bursts. One moment, the plain is bone-dry; the next, a patch the size of a city block is slick with dew, pooling in shallow depressions. These pools are clear, heavy, and silent. They last for an hour, maybe two, before the heat evaporates them back into the thick, hazy air. 

There are no rivers, only **veins**: narrow, winding channels carved by centuries of these ephemeral flows. They are dry most of the time, filled with fine, white silt that shifts underfoot like powder. When water comes, it runs through them with surprising speed, gurgling softly, before vanishing into the porous substrate. To find water, you must watch the ground’s mood, not the sky.

### Vegetation: Sparse Sentinels

Life is minimal, almost reluctant. The vegetation consists of **Lithophytes**—stone-plants. They are not green, but shades of rust, slate, and cream. 

They grow in clusters spaced miles apart, looking less like trees and more like geological formations that decided to breathe. Tall, segmented stalks rise thirty feet into the air, covered in a waxy, heat-resistant cuticle. At their tops, fan-like leaves unfold at dusk to catch moisture, folding tight during the day to conserve every drop. Their roots do not dig; they secrete acids that dissolve the Substrate, allowing them to anchor directly into the growing floor. 

Between these giants, there is only dust and occasional mats of lichen-like crust that bloom bright orange after a condensation event, then bleach white and brittle within days.

### Atmosphere & Light

The air is hot, thick, and still. Temperature hovers near 40°C (104°F), pressing against the skin like a warm blanket. Humidity is high, creating a shimmering haze that distorts distance. The light is normal sunlight, but filtered through this humid lens, it feels diffuse, golden, and heavy. Shadows are sharp-edged in the morning, softening into blurred smudges by noon.

There is no wind. Not really. Just thermal currents rising from the heated ground, causing the air to waver like a mirage. Breathing feels like drinking soup. Sweat does not evaporate quickly; it beads and rolls, cooling the body inefficiently.

### Sound: The Silence of Growth

The dominant sound is **silence**. Not empty silence, but *active* quiet. 

If you stand very still, you hear:
- The faint *crack* of the Substrate shifting, like ice forming on a lake, but slower.
- The *hiss* of water evaporating from stone.
- The distant *thrum* of condensation falling, a soft patter that echoes across the vastness.
- The creak of Lithophyte stalks expanding in the heat.

No birds. No insects. No animal calls. The fauna parameter is zero; this place is too harsh, too sterile, too *new* for complex life. Only microbes thrive in the damp crevices, unseen and unheard.

### Ecology & Rules of the World

This is a **closed-loop system**. Nothing decays because nothing dies. The Lithophytes shed leaves, which turn to dust, which becomes part of the Substrate, which feeds the next generation. Water cycles endlessly between air and ground. Heat drives the process.

**Rules:**
1. **Do not trust the ground.** What is solid now may be soft tomorrow. Walk on established veins or hardened ridges. Avoid areas that look "too new"—they may sink.
2. **Follow the damp.** Life clings to moisture. Where condensation pools, Lithophytes cluster. These are the only places with shade and slight coolness.
3. **Time is fluid.** Without animals or decay, there is no sense of urgency. Days blend into weeks. The landscape changes slowly, imperceptibly, until one day you realize the path you took yesterday is gone, sealed over by fresh silt.
4. **You are alone.** Not metaphorically. Literally. No other creatures exist here. If you hear a voice, it is your own echo bouncing off the curved horizon.

### Scale & Vastness

The scale is disorienting. Because the terrain is flat and uniform, depth perception fails. A Lithophyte three miles away looks identical to one three hundred yards away. The horizon is a perfect line, unbroken by mountains or hills. Weather systems form internally: clouds gather over heated patches, drift slowly, release their brief rain, and dissipate. You can see a storm forming fifty miles away, a dark bruise on the golden sky, and watch it crawl toward you over days.

### Emergent Details

As you walk, you notice patterns in the Substrate. Swirls, concentric circles, radial lines. They are not random. They resemble **growth rings** or **cellular structures**. Some scholars say this place was once a giant organism, frozen mid-metamorphosis. Others say it is a machine made of biology, building itself according to a code written in chemistry.

Occasionally, you find **pockets**: hollow spaces beneath the surface where condensation has pooled underground. Break through the thin crust, and you find cool, dark caves lined with glowing fungi—the only bioluminescence in this sun-drenched hell. They pulse slowly, syncing with the thermal cycles of the surface. Here, the air is cooler, sweeter. Here, you might rest.

But the surface calls you back. The heat rises. The cracks open. The world continues to build itself, indifferent to your presence, vast and silent and endlessly new.
