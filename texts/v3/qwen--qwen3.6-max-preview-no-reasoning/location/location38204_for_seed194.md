The world is not a planet, nor a room, but a **cavity**.

It exists within the hollowed-out core of a colossal, fossilized root system that once anchored a continent-sized tree, long since turned to stone and dust. This cavity is small—no larger than a cathedral nave—but it feels infinite because of the darkness and the way sound behaves here. It is a pocket universe of absolute aridity and suffocating greenery, suspended in a cool, stagnant air that has not moved in millennia.

### The Substrate: The Dry Green
There is no water. Not a drop. The humidity is zero. Yet, the space is choked with vegetation. This is the first contradiction, the first strangeness.

The plants here do not drink; they *listen*. They are composed of desiccated, woody fibers, translucent membranes, and crystalline silica structures that mimic leaves. They are pale, ghostly things—shades of bone-white, ash-grey, and bruised violet. Without water to turgidly hold them up, they rely on tension and interlocking geometry. Every vine, every fern-like frond, every bulbous pod is rigid, sharp-edged, and brittle as glass.

They fill the volume completely. You cannot see the "floor" or the "ceiling" because there is no distinction. The plant matter is a continuous, tangled lattice from one boundary of the cavity to the other. It is a three-dimensional web of life that has stopped growing outward and instead grown *inward*, filling every cubic centimeter of empty space with its own dried husk. To move through this place would be to shatter yourself against the architecture of thirst.

### The Atmosphere: Cool and Still
The temperature is consistently cool, hovering just above freezing. There is no wind, for there is no outside to draw air from. The air is thick with the scent of old paper, crushed mint, and ozone. It tastes metallic on the tongue, a residual charge from the static electricity that builds up between the dry, rubbing surfaces of the plant-fibers.

Because there is no moisture, the air does not carry scent well, yet the smell is overwhelming. It is the smell of preservation. Nothing rots here. Nothing decays. The ecosystem is locked in a state of permanent mummification. The leaves do not fall; they simply become part of the wall.

### The Light: Absolute Darkness
There is no light source. No sun penetrates the miles of rock above. No bioluminescence flickers in the deep, for these plants have evolved beyond the need for photosynthesis. They are chemosynthetic, drawing energy from the slow, radioactive decay of minerals embedded in their roots, which dive into the bedrock below the cavity.

In this total blackness, vision is useless. The landscape is defined entirely by touch and sound. If you were to stand here (if you could find a space to stand), you would feel the press of a thousand dry stems against your skin. You would feel the vibration of the city before you saw it.

### The Sound: The Loud Silence
This is the most defining feature of the place. It is **loud**.

Without water to dampen vibrations, and with the entire cavity lined with hard, dry, resonant plant-materials, every movement creates a symphony of clicks, creaks, and snaps. But there are no animals. There is no wind. So where does the noise come from?

It comes from the **city**.

Embedded within the dense tangle of dry vegetation is a settlement. It is not built *on* the plants; it is woven *into* them. The inhabitants—tall, slender beings with skin like polished obsidian and joints that click like beetle wings—have constructed their homes by binding the rigid vines together with cords of spun spider-silk and resin. Their buildings are spheres and tubes of woven fiber, suspended in the dark void.

The noise is the sound of their industry. It is primitive, manual, and relentless.
*   The *scritch-scratch* of bone tools carving new channels into the woody stems.
*   The *thrum-thrum-thrum* of looms weaving new walls from shed bark.
*   The *clack-clack-clack* of signal-drums made from hollowed seed-pods, communicating across the dark.

Because the space is enclosed and the materials are highly resonant, these sounds do not fade. They bounce. They amplify. A single hammer strike echoes for minutes, layering over itself until the air is a solid wall of percussion. The inhabitants have adapted to this; they speak in whispers so low they are almost subsonic, feeling the words in their chests rather than hearing them. But their work is loud. The city is a machine of noise, grinding slowly in the dark.

### The Ecology: The Static Web
There are no animals. No insects. No birds. The food chain is circular and closed. The inhabitants harvest the oldest, driest parts of the plant-lattice, grinding them into a powder that serves as both food and building material. In return, their waste products—rich in nitrates and phosphates—are carefully injected back into the living tips of the vines, stimulating minimal, slow growth.

It is a delicate balance. If they take too much, the structure collapses. If they add too much, the vines grow too fast and crush the city. So they move slowly. Deliberately. A single construction project might take a generation. A single meal might be harvested over a week.

The plants themselves are strange. Some have leaves that act as parabolic dishes, focusing the faint thermal radiation from the earth’s core onto sensitive nodes. Others have roots that pulse with a faint, rhythmic glow—not visible light, but infrared heat, felt as a warm spot in the cool dark. These "heat-flowers" are the only sources of warmth in the cavity, and the inhabitants cluster around them like moths.

### The Rules of the World
1.  **Silence is Death:** To stop making noise is to disappear. The inhabitants believe that the darkness is a predator that listens for stillness. Therefore, the city must always hum. Always click. Always thrum. If the noise stops, the dark will eat them.
2.  **Water is Myth:** They have no concept of liquid water. Their language has no word for "wet." They describe emotions in terms of dryness: "I am parched with grief," "Her joy is crisp," "His anger is brittle."
3.  **Time is Measured in Echoes:** Since there is no day/night cycle, time is measured by the decay of sound. A "moment" is how long it takes for a drumbeat to fade into silence. A "day" is the time it takes for the great central resonance-chamber to complete one full cycle of harmonic buildup and release.
4.  **The Boundary is Sacred:** The edges of the cavity, where the plant-lattice meets the raw rock, are forbidden. It is said that beyond the rock lies the "Wet Place," a hell of chaos and drowning. No one goes there. No one returns.

### The Experience
Imagine standing in the center of this cavity. You are blind. The air is cool and dry, scratching at your throat. You cannot move without breaking something—a twig, a leaf, a thread. And every breakage sends a shockwave of sound rippling outwards, answered by a thousand distant clicks from the unseen city.

You are surrounded by life, but it is dead life. Hard, sharp, unyielding. You are in a city, but you cannot see its streets. You are alone, but you are never silent.

The place is a tomb that refuses to die. It is a garden that refuses to bloom. It is a song that has been singing itself for ten thousand years, waiting for an audience that will never arrive.

And in the dark, the vines tighten. Just slightly. A fraction of a millimeter. A sound like a cracking knuckle echoes through the void. The city answers with a chorus of drums. The cycle continues. Slow. Patient. Eternal.
