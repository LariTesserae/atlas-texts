The world here is not found; it is poured.

You stand in the throat of the **Sump-City**, a vast, subterranean industrial basin that stretches out into a dim, echoing horizon. There is no bedrock beneath your feet, only layers of compressed slag, vitrified waste, and reinforced ferro-concrete, stacked like the pages of a black book that has been left out in the rain for a thousand years. The elevation is negative, deep below the surface crust, yet the space feels aggressively open—a cavernous cathedral of manufacturing where the ceiling is lost in a haze of steam and suspended particulate matter.

### The Atmosphere: A Cold Sweat
The temperature hovers just above freezing (0.3), a biting, damp cold that settles into the marrow. It is not the dry chill of a winter night, but the wet, heavy cold of a cellar that has never seen the sun. The air is thick with condensation (Water 1.6). Every vertical surface weeps. Water does not flow in rivers here; it manifests as a constant, oily sweat on the walls of massive cooling towers and the flanks of dormant engines. It drips from overhead gantries in rhythmic, metallic plinks, pooling in shallow depressions in the concrete floor before being sucked away by grated drains that gurgle with a low, digestive hunger.

The light is dim (1.1), filtered through layers of smog and high-altitude fog that hangs in the upper reaches of the cavern. What illumination exists comes from sodium-vapor lamps, flickering amber orbs spaced far apart, casting long, distorted shadows that seem to detach themselves from the objects that cast them. Occasionally, the strobe-light flash of a distant welding arc or the blue-white spark of a failing transformer cuts through the gloom, revealing glimpses of the sheer scale of the place.

### The Terrain: Fresh Scars on Old Bones
The landscape is young in its erosion (1.3), which creates a jarring dissonance. This is an ancient, total built environment (2.7), yet the surfaces are sharp, jagged, and unweathered. Why? Because the ground is constantly being resurfaced. New layers of asphalt and polymer-sealant are sprayed over the old every few cycles, creating a topography of fresh, sticky slickness atop hardened, cracked history. You walk on skin that is still curing.

Vegetation is sparse (1.3) and strange. There are no trees, no grass. Instead, life clings to the margins in the form of **rust-moss** and **oil-ferns**. These are pale, translucent growths that feed on hydrocarbons and mineral runoff. They cluster around leaking pipes and fractured vents, glowing faintly bioluminescent green in the dark. They are fragile, easily crushed underfoot, leaving behind stains that look like bruised blood. They are the only soft things in a world of hard angles.

### The Sound: The Hum of the Machine
Silence is extinct here. The soundscape is loud (1.8) and relentless. It is a symphony of industrial resonance:
*   The deep, sub-bass thrum of ventilation fans the size of city blocks, spinning somewhere in the darkness above.
*   The high-pitched whine of hydraulic pistons cycling in distant factories.
*   The hiss of steam escaping from relief valves, sounding like angry snakes.
*   The occasional, deafening *clang* of metal striking metal, echoing for minutes across the open expanse.

This noise is not chaotic; it is structured. It has a rhythm, a pulse. If you listen closely, you can hear the "heartbeat" of the Sump-City—a regular, thudding vibration that travels up through the soles of your boots. It is the sound of production. Of consumption. Of existence maintained against entropy.

### Ecology and Fauna: The Rare Survivors
Fauna is rare (1.0), but not absent. The creatures here are adaptations to the artificial extreme. You might see a **Gear-Rat**, a small, hairless rodent with teeth reinforced by ceramic shards, scurrying along a pipe bundle. Or a **Condensation-Moth**, with wings like wet cellophane, drawn to the warmth of exhaust vents. They are shy, quick, and silent, moving through the spaces between the loud machines. They do not hunt each other often; there is little energy to spare. They survive on the scraps of the machine—leaked lubricants, fallen insulation, the occasional dead moth caught in a fan blade.

Predator-prey dynamics are minimal. The primary threat is not another animal, but the environment itself. A slip on the wet, oily concrete can mean a fall into a grinding gear or a plunge into a sump of acidic coolant. The ecosystem is one of avoidance, not aggression.

### The Rules of the World
1.  **Everything is Made:** There is no "nature" here. Even the water is recycled condensate. Even the air is scrubbed and recirculated. To find something "natural" would be to find a flaw in the system, a crack in the perfection of the construct. And such cracks are quickly sealed.
2.  **The Cycle of Renewal:** The ground is constantly being repaved. Paths shift. Walls are torn down and rebuilt overnight. Memory is physical; if you mark a spot today, it may be covered by fresh tar tomorrow. Navigation relies on landmarks that are too large to be replaced: the Great Vent, the Central Spire, the Echo Chamber.
3.  **Light is Currency:** In the dimness, sources of bright light are valuable. Workers carry personal lamps, shielding them with their bodies. To lose your light is to become invisible, to cease to exist in the social fabric of the Sump. Darkness is not empty; it is full of unseen movement and sound.
4.  **Weirdness in the Margins:** The strangeness (1.5) lies in the glitches. Sometimes, a screen will display static that forms faces. Sometimes, the hum of the machines will synchronize into a chord that induces nausea or euphoria. Sometimes, you will find a room that was walled off decades ago, filled with obsolete technology that still works, humming with a purpose no one remembers. These are the ghosts of previous iterations of the City.

### The Dynamic: A Living Factory
The place is active (1.9). It is never static. Cranes move slowly across the sky-cavern, carrying loads of unknown material. Steam clouds drift and reform. Lights flicker on and off in sequences that suggest communication. The entire basin breathes. Inhale: the intake of raw materials from shafts leading to the surface. Exhale: the expulsion of heat, waste, and finished goods into the lower depths.

You are a speck in this machinery. Your presence is noted only by the sensors that track movement for safety protocols. A red laser grid sweeps across the floor ahead of you, warning you back from a zone of high voltage. You step aside. The grid passes. The hum continues. The water drips. The cold bites.

And somewhere, deep in the distance, a new section of floor is being poured, hot and steaming, ready to cool into the next layer of this endless, constructed, wet, and loud underworld.
