The light here is not a source, but a state of being. It does not stream from a sun or a lamp; it emanates from the walls themselves, a cool, pervasive luminescence that renders shadows obsolete. This brightness is absolute, washing out color until the world exists in gradients of white, pale gray, and translucent blue.

You are standing in the **Subterranean Lattice**, a vast, urban-scale cavity beneath the crust, where the earth has been hollowed out not by water, but by time itself. The erosion parameter is high—ancient, settled into its final form—and yet the dynamic is turbulent. This is the paradox of the place: the architecture is old enough to be fossilized, but the activity within it is frantic, chaotic, and ceaseless.

### The Terrain and Materials

The floor is a polished expanse of vitrified rock, smooth as glass but textured with microscopic pits where moisture gathers. There is no soil. There is no dirt. The ground is hard, unyielding, and cold to the touch (temperature 1.3/3). It slopes gently downward into the distance, disappearing into a haze of condensation.

Because vegetation is nearly absent (0.3/3), the landscape is mineral and geometric. But it is not empty. The "built" environment (2.2/3) is everywhere, though it defies easy categorization. These are not buildings in the traditional sense. They are structures of woven stone-fiber and compacted dust, rising like coral reefs or termite mounds, but on the scale of cathedrals. They are organic in shape but inorganic in composition. They look grown, not constructed, yet they bear the marks of tool-work—chisel strikes, smoothed edges, fitted joints.

The materials are primitive (tech 0.7/3): no metal, no plastic, no electricity. Everything is made of local stone, clay, and a strange, semi-translucent resin secreted by the fauna. The construction is layered, dense, and intricate, suggesting a civilization that has had millennia to perfect the art of stacking and binding without mortar.

### The Atmosphere and Water

The air is damp (water 0.8/3). Not wet, but heavy with humidity. Condensation beads on every surface, sliding down the smooth walls in slow, deliberate trails. Occasionally, a fine mist rolls through the streets, born from the thermal differential between the cool stone and the slightly warmer bodies of the inhabitants.

There are no rivers, but there are *channels*. Shallow grooves carved into the floor guide the condensation toward central collection points—large, bowl-like depressions filled with clear, still water. These pools are the only sources of drinking water, and they are guarded fiercely. The dampness clings to your skin, making the cool temperature feel colder than it is. You can smell the ozone of static charge and the earthy scent of wet limestone.

### The Sound and Silence

Despite the turbulence (dynamic 2.8/3), the soundscape is quiet (sound 0.2/3). This is because the inhabitants move with a hushed urgency. Their feet are soft-padded; their tools are made of wood and bone, striking stone with muted thuds rather than clangs. The silence is not empty—it is thick, pressurized. You hear the drip of water from stalactites, the rustle of dry husks, the low-frequency hum of thousands of bodies moving in close proximity. It is the silence of a library during an earthquake.

### The Fauna and Ecology

This is where the weirdness (1.1/3) becomes apparent. The fauna is abundant (1.5/3), but it is not animal life as you know it. With vegetation at 0.3/3, there are no plants to eat. So what do they consume?

The primary inhabitants are **Lithovores**—creatures that feed on mineral deposits, specifically the calcium carbonate and silica that make up the city’s structure. They are small, insect-like, but large enough to be seen clearly: six-legged, armored in chitin that matches the color of the surrounding stone. They scuttle across the floors, chewing on the edges of buildings, grinding the rock into a fine paste that they mix with their own secretions to build new structures.

They are the builders and the destroyers. Their constant consumption creates the "turbulent" dynamic. Walls crumble slowly under their gnawing, only to be repaired by other members of the colony who apply fresh resin-stone mixtures. The city is in a state of perpetual renovation. A street corner might be gone tomorrow, replaced by a new spire. The layout shifts daily, forcing the inhabitants to adapt constantly.

There are also larger creatures: **Glass-Walkers**. Tall, slender, bipedal beings with skin like polished obsidian. They do not eat the stone; they tend to the Lithovores. They carry simple tools—brushes made of fibrous root-analogs, scrapers of flint—to guide the construction. They are silent, communicating through subtle shifts in posture and the tapping of their claws against the ground. They are the architects of this chaotic order.

### The Rules of the World

1.  **No Plant Life:** Nothing green grows here. Energy comes from chemical reactions in the rock and the metabolic processes of the Lithovores. The ecosystem is closed-loop, mineral-based.
2.  **Light is Eternal:** The bioluminescent fungi embedded in the ceiling (the only hint of vegetation, at 0.3/3) provide constant, bright illumination. There is no day or night. Time is measured by the cycles of construction and decay.
3.  **Silence is Survival:** Loud noises attract predators from the deeper, darker tunnels below the city. The inhabitants have evolved to move quietly. To shout is to invite death.
4.  **The City Breathes:** Because the Lithovores are constantly eating and rebuilding, the city expands and contracts. Maps are useless after a week. Navigation is based on landmarks that are temporarily stable—a large pool, a unique statue, a pattern in the ceiling glow.
5.  **Primitive Tech, High Complexity:** The tools are simple, but the social organization is complex. The Glass-Walkers coordinate the Lithovores through pheromones and tactile signals, creating a hive-mind efficiency that rivals advanced technology.

### The Experience of Being There

You walk through a wide avenue, the floor slick with condensation. To your left, a wall is crumbling, chunks of white stone falling silently into a pile of dust. Three Lithovores are already at work, their mandibles clicking softly as they grind the fallen debris. A Glass-Walker stands nearby, watching, its obsidian face reflecting the ambient light. It does not look at you. It looks at the wall.

Above you, the ceiling arches high, lost in the brightness. You cannot see the top, only the infinite spread of glowing patches that resemble stars, but closer, brighter, more uniform. The air is cool, carrying the scent of wet chalk and ancient dust.

You feel small. Not just physically, but temporally. This place has existed for eons, changing slowly, relentlessly. It is a machine made of flesh and stone, running on a fuel of patience and hunger. It is beautiful, in a sterile, terrifying way. It is a city that eats itself to stay alive.

As you move deeper, the density of structures increases. The open spaces narrow into alleys, then into tunnels. The silence deepens. You realize that the "turbulence" is not chaos, but a rhythm. A pulse. The city is breathing. And you are inside its lungs.
