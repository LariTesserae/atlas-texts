The place is a **Sunken Ward**, a bowl of earth no larger than a city block, yet compressed by gravity and geology into a space that feels suffocatingly full. It sits at a low elevation, perhaps only twenty feet below the surrounding plateau, but the drop is sharp—a sheer, jagged cliff face of raw, unweathered stone that marks the boundary between the "outside" world and this interior pocket.

### The Terrain: Fresh Wounds in Stone
The landscape is young. The erosion parameter (1.2) suggests this is not an ancient valley worn smooth by millennia, but a recent collapse or subsidence. The rock faces are angular, fractured, and sharp-edged, like broken teeth. There is no soil here in the traditional sense; instead, there is a slurry of gravel, silt, and clay that has pooled at the bottom.

Because the water level is high (2.0), the floor of the ward is a permanent marsh. But it is not a wild swamp. It is a *built* marsh. The ground is saturated, weeping constantly from the porous limestone walls. Condensation drips from the overhanging cliffs above, creating a perpetual mist that clings to the ankles. The air is cool (0.6), carrying the chill of damp stone and evaporating moisture.

### The Built Environment: Packed Primitive Urbanism
Here lies the central tension: **Density 2.7** meets **Built 2.0** and **Tech 0.8**.

Imagine a settlement constructed entirely of materials scavenged from the immediate vicinity—mud-brick, woven reeds, rough-hewn timber, and stacked slate. Because the space is small (Scale 0.9) and the density is packed, there is no concept of "street." There are only **thresholds**.

Houses are stacked vertically against the cliff walls, clinging to the rock like barnacles. They are connected by narrow rope bridges, cantilevered wooden walkways, and ladders carved directly into the stone. Every inch of vertical surface is occupied. A roof is someone else’s floor. A window looks out onto another family’s drying rack. The architecture is primitive—no metal nails, only joinery, lashings, and mud mortar—but it is complex, layered, and incredibly dense.

The buildings lean inward, supporting each other. To remove one would risk collapsing the whole. This creates a sense of communal fragility and intense interdependence. The structures are dark-stained by algae and moss, blending into the wet rock until you can barely tell where nature ends and habitation begins.

### Atmosphere and Light
Light (1.5) filters down from the rim of the bowl, diffuse and grayish due to the constant condensation. Direct sunlight rarely touches the bottom of the ward. Instead, illumination comes from small, shuttered windows, oil lamps made from animal fat, and the occasional glow of bioluminescent fungi cultivated in cracks.

The atmosphere is thick with humidity. Breathing feels heavy, not because of heat, but because of the moisture-laden air. Clothes never fully dry. Wood swells. Metal rusts within days if exposed. The smell is distinct: wet wool, cured fish, woodsmoke, and the metallic tang of fresh stone dust.

### Ecology: Sparse Vegetation, Rare Fauna
Vegetation (0.7) is sparse but strategic. No trees grow in the ward itself—the space is too cramped and shaded. However, every available crevice hosts something: ferns unfurling from mortar joints, patches of edible moss on north-facing walls, and hanging baskets of hardy herbs suspended from balconies. The plants are small, tough, and utilitarian.

Fauna (1.2) is rare but present. You might see a single rat scurrying along a beam, or a pale, blind cave-cricket hopping across a wet stone. Birds do not nest here; the enclosure is too tight for flight. Instead, the dominant life forms are human and microbial. Mold patterns bloom on ceilings like maps of forgotten countries. Slime molds creep along drain channels, feeding on organic waste.

### Sound and Dynamics
Sound (1.1) is mostly quiet, but not silent. The acoustics of the stone bowl amplify small noises. A cough echoes. A footstep on a wet plank creaks loudly. But because the community is so packed, people have learned to move softly. Voices are hushed. The dominant sounds are:
- The drip-drip-drip of condensation from the cliffs.
- The squelch of boots in mud.
- The low hum of weaving looms or grinding stones.
- The occasional splash as someone draws water from the central pool.

Dynamics (1.7) are active but internal. Weather cycles are muted—the wind cannot reach the bottom. Instead, the dynamics are social and mechanical. Water must be managed: pumps made of leather and wood lift wastewater up to filtration beds on the upper tiers. Heat must be conserved: fires are kept small and contained. Waste must be processed: composting toilets feed the fungal gardens. Everything is in motion, but slowly, carefully, deliberately.

### Rules of the World
1. **Verticality is Status**: Those who live higher up, closer to the light and fresh air, are wealthier. Those at the bottom, in the perpetual damp, are laborers or outcasts.
2. **Silence is Survival**: Loud noises disrupt the delicate balance of the structure. Shouting is forbidden. Arguments are conducted in whispers.
3. **Water is Currency**: Fresh water is collected from condensation traps on the upper rims. It is rationed. Access to clean water determines power.
4. **No Expansion**: The ward is bounded by sheer cliffs. You cannot build outward. You can only build upward or inward. Space is finite. Conflict over square inches is common.
5. **The Rock Remembers**: The stone is young and unstable. Cracks appear overnight. Settlers must constantly monitor their homes for shifts. A new fracture means evacuation. Trust in the ground is low; trust in neighbors is high.

### Weirdness (1.4): The Weeping Walls
The most unusual feature is the **"Weeping"**. Certain sections of the cliff face exude a viscous, clear fluid that is neither water nor sap. It smells faintly of ozone and old blood. Locals collect it in clay jars. When applied to skin, it numbs pain. When drunk, it induces vivid dreams of places outside the ward—places none of them have ever seen. Some believe the ward is alive, and the fluid is its sweat. Others say it is runoff from some unknown source deep underground.

This fluid is also used in construction. Mixed with mud, it creates a mortar that hardens like steel but remains slightly flexible, allowing the buildings to sway gently during minor tremors without cracking. Thus, the very substance that makes the place strange also keeps it standing.

### A Moment in the Sunken Ward
You stand on a narrow walkway, ten feet above the muddy floor. Below, a child skips stones across a puddle, the ripples spreading slowly in the thick air. Above, a woman hangs laundry from a line strung between two chimney stacks. The cloth is heavy with moisture, dripping onto the roof below. To your left, a man carves a notch into a wooden beam, his knife scraping softly. The sound echoes off the curved wall, returning to you three seconds later, distorted and distant.

A drop of condensation falls from the ceiling, landing on your shoulder. It is cold. You look up and see hundreds of such drops forming on the underside of the bridge above you, trembling, waiting to fall. The entire ward is breathing. Inhale: the mist rises. Exhale: the water falls.

There is no horizon here. Only layers. Layer upon layer of life, pressed together, held up by faith, rope, and the strange, weeping stone.
