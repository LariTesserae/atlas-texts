Rain is rare here, intercepted by the upper layers before it can fall deep. Instead, water exists as condensation—beads of sweat forming on cold pipes, dripping steadily into moss-lined troughs. Pools of stagnant, warm water sit in the hollows of massive gear-teeth, reflecting the bright, diffuse light that filters down from the open sky above.

The scale is regional but intimate. You can see the curve of the structure extending for miles, a vast, curved bowl of metal and greenery, but every few meters, the density increases. 1.  **Everything is Anchored:** Nothing falls. Gravity is strong, but the structure is solid. If you drop something, it will land on another terrace, another pipe, another leaf. There is no "down" into emptiness, only deeper into the labyrinth.
2.  **Water Flows Upward:** Due to capillary action in the dense vegetation and the heat-driven convection currents, moisture often moves upward from the lower, damper levels to the higher, warmer ones. Dew forms on the undersides of leaves at midday.
3.  **Time is Slow:** Changes happen gradually. A vine might take a year to cover a wall. A rust spot might take a decade to eat through a plate. There is no urgency. The inhabitants move slowly, deliberately, respecting the pace of the structure.
4.  **The Machine Breathes:** The hum of the engines changes pitch slightly with the seasons, though seasons are barely noticeable. The structure expands and contracts with temperature, causing creaks and groans that signal the passage of days.
