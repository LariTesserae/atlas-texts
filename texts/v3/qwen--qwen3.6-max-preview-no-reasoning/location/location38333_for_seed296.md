You are standing on the **Silt-Plateau of Oth**, a vast, elevated tableland that defies the usual logic of geology. At an elevation where one might expect jagged peaks and thinning oxygen, there is instead a profound, crushing flatness. This is a place that has finished its story; the mountains have been ground down to their roots, not by wind or rain alone, but by time itself, leaving behind a surface that feels less like rock and more like hardened memory.

The terrain is a mosaic of ancient erosion. The ground is composed of compressed strata—layers of shale, limestone, and something darker, metallic, and unidentifiable—that have fused together under eons of pressure. It is cool here, perpetually hovering just above freezing, a damp chill that seeps into the bones rather than biting at the skin. Predation is slow and quiet; life here conserves energy, moving only when necessary.

This is a place of endings that have become beginnings. It is cool, damp, and densely packed with life that feeds on death. The urban remnants serve as trellises for the new green world, and the extreme elevation isolates it from the rest of the planet, preserving it in a bubble of primordial stability. To walk here is to witness the final chapter of a civilization being written over by the first chapter of a new, slower, wetter world. It is beautiful in its melancholy, terrifying in its silence, and alive in its decay.
