The place is not a planet, nor a continent in the traditional sense. It is a **Hollow Sphere of Green**, suspended in a void that has forgotten how to be dark because there is no sky to hold it.

Imagine a shell the size of a moon, but constructed entirely from woven root-mass, petrified sap, and living bark. This is the **Built** parameter taken to its absolute extreme: there is no stone here, no dirt, no mineral substrate. The ground beneath your feet is not earth; it is the calloused, hardened skin of an ancient, continental-scale tree that grew inward rather than outward. The "elevation" is high because you are standing on the inner curve of this sphere, looking out into the vast, empty center where gravity is a suggestion rather than a law.

### The Architecture of Roots

The terrain is a labyrinth of **plant-as-architecture**. Massive buttress roots, each thicker than skyscrapers, arch across the interior horizon, forming natural bridges and cavernous halls. These structures are not dead wood; they pulse with a slow, rhythmic warmth (Temperature 2.4). The air is thick with humidity (Water 1.7), condensing on every surface. Water does not flow in rivers here; it weeps. It beads on the undersides of leaves the size of ship sails, drips from hanging vines that span miles, and pools in the concave bowls of giant fungi.

Because the density is so low (0.5), the space between these colossal vegetative structures is immense. You can stand on a platform of moss-covered bark and look across a chasm of pure, silent air for ten miles before seeing another cluster of growth. This emptiness creates a vertigo that is both terrifying and serene. The scale is vast (2.8); weather systems form within the hollow sphere itself. Warm updrafts rise from the humid lower curves, creating localized clouds that drift lazily through the central void, casting long, dim shadows over the green world below.

### The Light and the Silence

Light is scarce (0.9). There is no sun. Instead, illumination comes from bioluminescence. The entire ecosystem glows with a soft, ambient teal and amber hue. Patches of lichen emit a steady, cold light; certain flowers bloom only in the dark, unfolding petals that radiate warmth and faint photons. This dimness gives the landscape a dreamlike quality, blurring the edges of reality. Shadows do not behave correctly—they stretch toward the heat sources rather than away from light, a subtle hint of the **weirdness** (2.7) that permeates this place.

Sound is nearly absent (0.5). The vegetation absorbs vibration. Footsteps on the spongy moss are muted. The drip of water is the loudest noise, echoing strangely in the vast spaces. But if you listen closely, past the silence, you hear the **dynamic** hum of the ecosystem (1.7): the creak of growing wood, the rustle of unseen fauna moving through the canopy layers above, the low-frequency thrum of sap moving through veins as wide as highways.

### Ecology and Fauna

Life is abundant (Fauna 2.0) but elusive. Because the environment is total plant-life, animals have evolved to be part of the architecture. Birds do not build nests; they *are* the fruit, camouflaged until they take flight. Insects are translucent, their bodies mimicking dewdrops on leaves. Larger predators—cat-like creatures with fur like velvet moss—move silently along the root-bridges, hunting herbivores that graze on the tender shoots of the giant ferns.

The ecology is self-contained and established (Erosion 2.1). This system has existed for millennia in a state of perfect balance. Nothing decays in the traditional sense; everything is reabsorbed. A fallen leaf becomes a nursery for spores, which become mold, which feeds the insects, which feed the birds, whose droppings fertilize the next generation of leaves. It is a closed loop, a breathing entity.

### The Primitive Craft

Despite the alien nature of the place, there are signs of intelligence, but it is **primitive** (Tech 0.6). No metal, no plastic, no electricity. Small communities live in the hollows of the great trees, building homes from woven vines and hardened resin. Their tools are carved bone and sharpened flint-like seeds. They understand the rhythm of the sphere—the way the wind shifts when the central cloud mass moves, the way the glow of the lichen changes with the "seasons" of the sphere’s internal cycle.

These people do not speak loudly. They communicate through gesture and touch, respecting the silence. They believe the sphere is alive, a god that breathes them in and out. And in a way, they are right. The weirdness of the place lies in the fact that the boundary between the observer and the observed is thin. If you stay too long, you feel your own heartbeat syncing with the pulse of the roots beneath your feet. Your thoughts begin to feel less like your own and more like whispers from the surrounding foliage.

### The Reality Breakdown

Here is where the **weirdness** (2.7) truly emerges. The laws of physics are suggestions. Gravity is weakest at the center of the sphere, allowing some plants to grow in spirals that defy orientation. Water sometimes flows upward, drawn by capillary action in vines so large they resemble pillars. Time feels different here; days are measured not by the rising sun, but by the blooming of specific flowers. A "day" might last hours or weeks, depending on the species.

Occasionally, travelers report seeing glimpses of other worlds reflected in the dewdrops—not reflections of the present, but memories of places that never existed. The atmosphere is thick with psychic residue, the accumulated dreams of the millions of beings who have lived and died within the green shell. To breathe deeply is to inhale history, emotion, and possibility.

### Summary of Experience

To walk this landscape is to move through a cathedral of life. It is warm, wet, and overwhelmingly green. The air smells of ozone, damp soil, and sweet nectar. You are small in the face of the vast, empty spaces between the giants, yet you are intimately connected to every fiber of the world around you. It is a place of profound quiet, where the only true sound is the beating of your own heart, slowly synchronizing with the slow, ancient pulse of the world-tree. It is beautiful, terrifying, and utterly alive.
