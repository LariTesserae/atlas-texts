### Vegetation: The Green Mold

It grows over valves, gauges, and walkways, swallowing the machinery whole. In the corners, where condensation drips steadily from weeping pipes, fern-like structures made of translucent plastic waste and fungal hyphae have taken root. ### Atmosphere and Sound: The Deafening Silence

The most striking feature of this place is the sound (2.7/3). It is deafening, yet it is also static.

There is a massive turbine housing in the center of the chamber, suspended above the sludge pool by chains that have rusted into solid pillars. The turbine itself does not spin—it is frozen in place, seized by decades of neglect. However, the *echo* of its former motion remains trapped in the acoustics of the room. Or perhaps, it is the hum of the high-voltage lines running along the ceiling, vibrating at a frequency just below human hearing, causing the teeth to ache and the bones to resonate.

It is a white noise roar, a continuous, unchanging drone that fills the empty space (0.3/3 density) between objects. Because the room is largely hollow—vast stretches of air between the cluttered edges—the sound has nowhere to go. It bounces back and forth, amplifying itself until it becomes a physical pressure. You do not hear the sound; you feel it in your chest cavity. It is the sound of equilibrium, of a system locked in a permanent state of idle tension.

There are no other noises. No dripping water (the drops fall silently into the thick sludge), no wind, no birds. Just the Roar.

Fauna is rare (0.6/3), but present. Life here is adapted to the toxic, warm, loud environment.

These creatures are silent, moving with deliberate slowness, unaffected by the deafening roar because they have evolved without ears.

Your thoughts will slow, syncing with the deafening, unchanging hum. You will begin to forget why you came, or if you ever left.
