The place is a throat. Not metaphorically, but literally—a cylindrical, vertical shaft of polished white stone, no wider than three men standing shoulder-to-shoulder, plunging down into a warm, bright abyss.

It is entirely constructed. There is no dirt here, no bedrock, no organic soil. The walls are made of seamless, vitrified ceramic tiles, glazed in shades of pale cream and bone-white, fitted together with such precision that the mortar lines are invisible to the naked eye. This is not a ruin; it is fresh. The edges where the wall meets the waterline are sharp, unchipped, smelling faintly of wet clay and ozone. It feels as though the builders left only moments ago, or perhaps have been building for an eternity, adding ring upon ring to this infinite tube.

You are suspended in the middle of it.

**The Water**
The space is filled with water up to your waist, then your chest, then over your head if you dive. But it is not ocean water. It is clear, distilled, and unnaturally warm—like bathwater heated by a sun that doesn’t exist. The temperature is a constant 30°C (86°F), a humid, clinging heat that permeates the skin. The water has weight and presence; it is thick with dissolved minerals, giving it a slight viscosity, a silkiness that makes movement feel slow and deliberate.

Because the scale is pocket-sized, the water does not behave like an ocean. It behaves like a contained fluid dynamic system. There are no tides, but there are *pulses*. Every few minutes, a low-frequency thrum vibrates through the liquid, causing ripples to race up the walls in perfect concentric circles. The water level rises and falls by inches, breathing.

**The Light**
It is blindingly bright. The light does not come from above, nor from below. It emanates from the walls themselves. The ceramic tiles are embedded with millions of microscopic, bioluminescent algae—or perhaps synthetic phosphors—that glow with a harsh, white daylight intensity. There are no shadows. Every surface is illuminated evenly, eliminating depth perception unless you focus on the texture of the glaze. The brightness is aggressive; it forces the eyes to squint, creating halos around every object. In this light, dust motes dance like galaxies, suspended in the warm air above the waterline.

**The Sound**
The silence is loud. Or rather, the absence of animal life is replaced by the roar of mechanics and hydrodynamics. Because the space is narrow and enclosed, sound reflects off the hard, smooth surfaces with violent efficiency.
*   **The Drip:** From somewhere high above, unseen, water condenses on the cool upper reaches of the shaft and falls. Each drop hits the surface with a sound like a pistol crack—*ping!*—echoing for seconds, multiplying into a cacophony of rhythmic percussion.
*   **The Hum:** A deep, sub-bass vibration runs through the structure. It is the sound of pumps, or perhaps the earth itself shifting beneath this artificial construct. It rattles the teeth.
*   **The Rush:** As the water pulses, it creates a sucking sound against the walls, a wet, slapping noise that sounds like breath being drawn in.

**The Vegetation**
Sparse, struggling, and stubborn. Despite the total construction, life finds a way in the cracks. Here and there, where two tiles meet imperfectly, a single frond of fern pushes out, its roots drinking the mineral-rich moisture from the grout. These plants are pale, etiolated by the lack of true sunlight spectrum, their leaves translucent and veined with purple. They do not grow in clusters. One fern per ten feet. One moss patch per thirty. They are lonely survivors, clinging to the artificial substrate like barnacles on a ship’s hull. Their presence highlights the sterility of everything else.

**The Atmosphere & Ecology**
There is no fauna. No fish swim in these waters. No insects buzz in the humid air. The ecosystem is purely botanical and mechanical. The "food chain" is nonexistent. The ferns feed on the mineral leachate from the tiles. The algae in the walls feed on the ambient light they produce. It is a closed loop, sterile and efficient.

The air is heavy with humidity, near 100%. It tastes metallic and sweet. Breathing feels like inhaling steam. Sweat does not evaporate; it beads on the skin and rolls off, joining the pool. The warmth is oppressive, not tropical, but industrial—like the inside of a greenhouse built inside a sauna.

**The Rules of the World**
1.  **Reflection is Truth:** Because the walls are so smooth and the light so even, reflections are nearly perfect. If you look at the wall, you see yourself distorted by the curvature, but clearly. There is no hiding here.
2.  **Verticality is Absolute:** You cannot go sideways. The shaft is circular and uniform. Up is toward the source of the dripping water (which recedes infinitely). Down is toward the deeper pressure, where the water becomes darker not because of lack of light, but because of density.
3.  **Time is Measured in Pulses:** There is no day or night. Time is marked by the rhythmic rising and falling of the water level, and the intermittent *ping* of condensation drops. A "hour" might be fifty pulses. A "day" might be when the hum changes pitch.
4.  **Construction is Ongoing:** Though the place seems finished, if you press your ear against the tile, you can hear the faint scratch of tools far above or far below. Someone, or something, is still building this shaft. It is never done.

**The Experience**
To stand here is to feel both exposed and confined. The brightness strips away mystery; everything is visible, yet nothing is understood. Why was this built? Who maintains it? Where does the water come from? Where does it go?

The warmth soothes the muscles but agitates the mind. The loudness of the environment prevents thought from settling. You are forced into the present moment, anchored by the physical sensation of the warm water against your legs, the glare in your eyes, the echo in your ears.

It is a place of purification without redemption. A washroom for gods who have forgotten how to sin. Or perhaps a test chamber for materials that must withstand eternal dampness and light.

As you float, suspended in the buoyant, warm liquid, you notice a new detail: a single, perfect sphere of air trapped under a lip of tile, trembling with the vibration of the shaft. It holds a tiny rainbow, refracted from the white light. It is the only color in the world. It pops silently, merging with the larger body of water, and the cycle begins again.
