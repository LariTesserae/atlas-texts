The air is not air. It is a heavy, warm broth of suspended vapor and microscopic algae, thick enough to feel like a physical weight against the skin, yet thin enough to breathe with effort. You are standing on a ledge of polished brass, but there is no ground beneath you, only the infinite, vertical drop into the Green Deep.

This is the **Sump-Cathedral**, a singular, enclosed ecosystem contained within a massive, spherical glass-and-steel vessel that hangs suspended in a void we cannot see. The scale is intimate—no larger than a grand library or a small hamlet—but the density of existence here is suffocatingly absolute. Every cubic inch is occupied. There is no empty space. If you reach out your hand, your fingers will brush against a leaf, a pipe, a rivet, or a droplet of condensation before they can extend fully.

### The Terrain: A Vertical Garden of Machinery

The world is built entirely of industrial artifacts repurposed by time and biology. The "ground" you stand on is a mesh of grated iron walkways, suspended over chasms filled with swirling, emerald fog. These walkways do not lead to cities; they lead to other walkways, spiraling inward toward the center of the sphere, which is hidden behind curtains of hanging vines.

The terrain is sharp and young. The erosion parameter is low because nothing has had time to wear down. The brass pipes are still gleaming with factory polish, their edges crisp and dangerous. The steel girders are rust-free, coated in a protective layer of bio-luminescent moss that glows with a soft, teal pulse. This is a place recently emerged from the assembly line, or perhaps recently flooded, where the machinery has not yet surrendered to decay, but has instead been colonized.

There is no soil. Everything grows from metal. Roots of fern-like plants drill directly into copper conduits, drinking the warmth and minerals leaking from the joints. Moss carpets every surface, turning gears and pistons into soft, green sculptures. The vegetation is lush, aggressive, and silent. It does not rustle in the wind because there is no wind—only the slow, thermal convection currents of the warm water-vapor atmosphere.

### The Atmosphere: Warm, Wet, and Heavy

The temperature is comfortably warm, around 28°C (82°F), maintained by the constant hum of the buried engines below. But it is the humidity that defines the experience. The air is at 100% saturation. Water beads on every surface, rolling slowly down leaves and pipes, collecting in pools on the grates. It feels like being inside a mouth, or a womb.

Light filters through the upper hemisphere of the sphere, diffused by layers of cloud-condensate and dense foliage. It is normal daylight, but softened, golden-green, casting long, distorted shadows. There are no harsh contrasts. Everything is bathed in a uniform, aqueous glow.

Because the environment is fully constructed, there are no natural rock formations. The "mountains" are stacks of discarded turbine casings. The "valleys" are gaps between boiler rooms. The "rivers" are leaks from cooling systems, cascading in slow, viscous sheets down the sides of towering smokestacks that have been overtaken by ivy.

### The Ecology: Silent Growth

There is no fauna. No birds sing. No insects buzz. No fish swim in the mist. The silence is profound, broken only by the occasional *drip* of water falling from a leaf tip onto a metal plate, echoing like a bell in the enclosed space. This absence of animal life makes the vegetation seem predatory, watchful. The plants grow in tight clusters, competing for the limited light and the nutrient-rich runoff from the machines.

Ferns unfurl like fists opening. Orchids bloom from the exhaust ports of dormant generators, their petals vibrant purple and orange, contrasting with the grey steel. Algae coats the interior of the glass sphere, creating a living wallpaper that shifts color slightly as it photosynthesizes. The ecology is one of pure consumption and conversion: heat becomes growth, moisture becomes mass, metal becomes substrate.

### The Rules of the World

1.  **Everything is Connected:** Because the density is so high, touching one thing inevitably touches another. To move is to displace. To breathe is to inhale the spores of the garden. There is no privacy, no isolation. The environment presses in from all sides.
2.  **Slow Time:** Change is glacial. A gear might turn once an hour. A vine might grow an inch a day. The dynamic is patient. This is not a place of action, but of observation. You could stand still for hours and notice only the gradual shift of light, the slow accumulation of dew on your eyelashes.
3.  **The Machine Breathes:** Though there are no animals, the place feels alive because the infrastructure functions. Pumps thrum deep below, vibrating through the soles of your feet. Steam vents release plumes of warm mist in rhythmic cycles. The sphere itself expands and contracts slightly with temperature changes, creaking softly—a metallic groan that sounds like a sleeping giant turning over.
4.  **No Exit, Only Depth:** The boundaries are clear—the curved glass wall of the sphere—but what lies beyond is obscured by the dense fog and vegetation. The sense of enclosure is total. You are in a bottle, and the world outside is irrelevant.

### Sensory Details

*   **Smell:** Ozone, wet earth (though there is no earth), crushed mint, hot oil, and the sweet, rotting scent of decaying orchid petals. It is a complex, layered perfume that clings to your clothes.
*   **Touch:** Everything is slick. Metal is smooth and cool under the moss. Leaves are waxy and firm. The air is thick, resisting movement. When you walk, your boots make no sound on the rubberized grating, but you feel the vibration of the pumps beneath you.
*   **Sight:** A kaleidoscope of green and grey. The sharp lines of industrial design are softened by the organic curves of plant life. Rivets become nodes for buds. Pipes become branches. Glass reflects the interior, creating an illusion of infinite depth, though you know the space is small.
*   **Sound:** Silence, punctuated by the *tick-tick-tick* of cooling metal, the *hiss* of steam, and the *plink* of water drops. It is a quiet so complete it rings in your ears.

### The Weirdness

The strangest aspect is the **intent**. The machinery appears to be designed for production, but it produces nothing visible. The factories are idle, yet the power flows. The lights stay on. The heat persists. It is as if the system was built to sustain the garden, not the other way around. Or perhaps the garden is the engine, and the machines are merely its bones.

You find a console near the center, covered in ferns. The buttons are worn smooth by human hands, long gone. A screen flickers with static, then displays a single word: **GROW**. Then it fades. The implication is unsettling. This place is not abandoned. It is waiting. It is cultivating something. And you are part of the substrate.

As you stand there, breathing the warm, wet air, you realize that your own presence is altering the balance. Your breath adds carbon dioxide. Your body heat warms the air around you. In this packed, sensitive environment, you are not an observer. You are an ingredient. The vines nearest to you seem to lean closer, not with malice, but with curiosity. They are measuring you. Calculating your volume. Integrating you into the dense, silent, beautiful machine.
