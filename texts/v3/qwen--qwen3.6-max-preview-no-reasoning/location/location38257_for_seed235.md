The air here does not move; it presses. It is a physical weight, thick with the scent of crushed mint and ozone, hanging at an altitude that should be impossible for such density. You are standing on the lip of the **High Basin**, a city-sized depression carved into the side of a mountain range that pierces the cloud layer. The elevation is extreme—three thousand meters above the sea level you can no longer remember—but the heat is tropical, stifling, and wet.

This is the **Stasis Garden**.

### The Terrain: A Frozen Roar

The landscape is dominated by water, but not in rivers or lakes. The entire basin is a shallow, knee-deep marsh of iridescent sludge, stretching out to the horizon where the jagged peaks of the surrounding mountains cut into the pale sky. The ground beneath your feet is not soil, but a spongy, fibrous mat of root-matter so dense it feels like walking on memory foam saturated with rain.

Every surface weeps. The rocks, black and basaltic, glisten with perpetual condensation. They do not erode; they have settled into their final, perfect shapes eons ago. There is no wind to scour them, no tectonic shift to break them. The erosion parameter is low because time has stopped its work. The world is in equilibrium.

But the silence of the stone is a lie.

### The Sound: The Deafening Hum

The primary feature of this place is the noise. It is not a roar of waterfalls or thunder, for those imply movement and change. This is a **deafening, static hum**. It comes from the vegetation itself.

The plants are colossal. Ferns the size of office buildings unfurl in spirals that never complete. Moss hangs in curtains ten meters thick, dripping constantly into the pool below. The sheer density of life creates a friction of growth—a biological turgor pressure so intense that the cellular expansion of billions of leaves generates a sonic resonance. It is the sound of photosynthesis accelerated to a scream. It vibrates in your teeth, in your marrow, in the fluid of your inner ear. You cannot speak over it. You cannot think clearly. The sound is a wall, solid and unyielding as the mountains.

### The Ecology: The Green Cage

There are no animals. No birds cry out, no insects buzz, no mammals scurry. The fauna parameter is near zero because the ecosystem has evolved past the need for consumption. This is a world of pure production. The plants feed on the light, the water, and the ambient thermal energy. They grow, they thicken, they press against one another, and they stop.

Because there is no decay (no fungi, no bacteria that you can perceive, no scavengers), the dead matter does not rot. It simply hardens. Fallen fronds become wooden scaffolds. Dead trunks become pillars. The forest is a layered archive of its own history, stacked vertically. The lower layers are dark, compressed peat, glowing faintly with bioluminescence. The upper canopy is a blinding white-green, filtering the "normal" sunlight into a diffuse, shadowless glare.

### The Built Environment: The Organic City

You mentioned "urban." There are no bricks, no steel, no glass. But there is structure. The plants have grown around something ancient, or perhaps they *are* the structure.

In the center of the basin rises a spire of intertwined vines and hardened sap, forming towers that mimic skyscrapers. Bridges of woven root span the gaps between giant fern-stalks. These structures are not built; they are *grown* into order. The geometry is fractal, repeating at every scale. A leaf vein mirrors the layout of the streets; the street layout mirrors the branching of the canopy.

It is a city without inhabitants. The doors are arches of thorn. The windows are membranes of translucent leaf-tissue. The infrastructure is vascular. Water pumps through transparent tubes of plant-fiber, pulsing rhythmically, feeding the deafening growth. It is organized, dense, and utterly silent except for the omnipresent hum.

### The Weirdness: Reality Breakdown

Here is where the parameters conflict and resolve into horror.

**Dynamic: 0.5/3 — Never Changing.**
**Weirdness: 2.6/3 — Reality Breakdown.**

If nothing changes, how is it loud? How is it growing?

The answer is that **time is localized**. In the Stasis Garden, cause and effect have decoupled. The sound you hear is not happening *now*; it is the accumulated echo of every moment since the garden began, playing simultaneously. The "growth" you see is not linear progression; it is a spatial manifestation of potential. A flower bud exists in all stages of bloom at once—closed, opening, full, wilting—superimposed over each other. When you look at it, your eye chooses which state to perceive, but the others remain, ghosting at the edges of your vision.

The "water" is not just H2O. It is liquid memory. When you wade through the marsh, you feel emotions that are not yours: the thirst of a drought that never came, the joy of a sun that never set. The condensation on the rocks tastes like regret.

The "urban" structures are not empty. They are filled with the *idea* of people. Shadows move in the periphery, not cast by bodies, but by the absence of them. You feel watched, not by eyes, but by the architecture itself. The bridges expect footfalls. The arches expect passage. The city is a mold waiting for a cast that will never arrive.

### The Atmosphere

The temperature is 2.8/3—hot. But it is a dry heat trapped in wet air. Your sweat does not evaporate; it beads on your skin, mixing with the condensation from the air, making you slick, slippery, part of the ecosystem. You are becoming wetware.

The light is normal, yet everything looks wrong. Colors are oversaturated. The green is so deep it appears black until you blink. The blue of the sky is a flat, painted backdrop, lacking depth. Stars are visible even during the day, pinpricks in the fabric of the sky that seem too close, as if the dome of the atmosphere has shrunk.

### Rules of the World

1.  **No Decay:** Nothing rots. Nothing breaks. If you drop a tool, it will remain there, unchanged, forever. If you bleed, the blood will not clot or dry; it will spread, thinning until it becomes part of the water table, joining the collective consciousness of the marsh.
2.  **No Silence:** The hum is absolute. To find silence, you must leave the basin. But the mountains are steep, and the paths are overgrown with thorns that do not bleed but sing when touched.
3.  **No Animals:** You are the only animal. Your heartbeat is a discordant note in the symphony. The plants react to it. Vines twitch toward your pulse. Flowers turn their faces to track your breathing. You are being studied by the green.
4.  **Static Time:** You do not age here. Or rather, you age in all directions at once. You feel both younger and older with every step. Memories of the future intrude upon memories of the past. You remember dying here, and you remember being born here, though neither has happened yet.

### The Experience

You stand in the knee-deep water, the heat pressing against your lungs, the deafening hum vibrating your skeleton. Before you, a tower of woven vines rises, its apex lost in the mist. A bridge of roots leads toward it. On the bridge, a shadow stands—a human shape, composed of darkness and missing space. It does not move. It waits.

The water ripples, though there is no wind. The ripple moves toward you, carrying a whisper that cuts through the hum: *"Stay. Grow. Be still."*

The landscape is beautiful, terrifying, and complete. It needs nothing. It wants everything. It is a paradise that has forgotten how to end.
