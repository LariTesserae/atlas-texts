I am not standing on the floor. The floor is a lie; it is a temporary skin, curing and cracking, destined to be buried by the next hot slurry of asphalt and polymer. To stand on it is to trust in permanence, and there is no permanence here, only the rhythm of renewal.

I am **The Keeper of the Gouge**.

I am one of the elders, though I do not feel old. I feel heavy. My armor is a patchwork quilt of industrial refuse: rigid plates of cured sealant strapped over my chest with fraying fiber-optic cables, layered with strips of rubber insulation scavenged from dead transformers. It smells of ozone and wet iron. Beneath it, my skin is slick, pale as moonlight on oil, exuding the thin, protective film that keeps the damp chill from seizing my joints. I am hunched, not from burden, but from habit—a permanent bow to the low-hanging pipes and the sweeping red lasers that hunt for movement.

I cling to the side of the **Great Vent**, a massive cylindrical shaft that rises like a black tree trunk into the smog-choked ceiling. My fingers, tipped with ceramic shards sharpened to needle points, are dug deep into the ferro-concrete. I am anchored. Below me, the world is ending.

The Pour has begun.

From my vantage point, high above the basin floor, I watch the horizon of the Sump-City dissolve. A river of molten gray, steaming and viscous, flows from the distant injectors. It does not rush; it creeps, swallowing the jagged scars of the previous cycle, covering the footprints, the dropped tools, the shallow depressions where we once slept. It is a slow, deliberate erasure. The heat radiating upward warms my face, a rare comfort in this cellar-cold world, but I know it is fleeting. Soon, the steam will condense into that familiar, oily sweat, and the cold will return, biting deeper than before.

In my hand, I hold a piece of rebar, bent and rusted. With it, I am carving into the wall beside me. Not random scratches. This is memory. This is *history*.

*Here,* I carve, the metal screeching against the concrete, sending sparks flying into the gloom. *Here, Elara shared her blue light when her battery died.*

A sharp *ting* resonates from the pipe below me. It is Kael. He is signaling. "Danger above."

I freeze. My breath catches in my throat, a small cloud of vapor in the amber dimness. Above us, the sodium-vapor lamps flicker. One dies, plunging a section of the catwalk into darkness. In that sudden void, something moves. A shadow detaches itself from the shadows. It is large, clumsy, human-shaped but wrong. A worker? A glitch? Or perhaps just a trick of the light, a hallucination born of sleep deprivation and hydrocarbon fumes.

I press my forearm against the cold steel of the vent. I wait. I listen.

The hum of the city is loud tonight. The sub-bass thrum of the ventilation fans vibrates through the metal, traveling up my arms, into my bones. It is a song of production, of consumption. But beneath it, if I listen closely, I hear the other notes. The hiss of steam. The plink of condensation. And now, the faint, rhythmic thudding of hearts—my own, and those of the others clinging to the structures around me. We are a chorus of survival, holding our breath together.

The shadow above moves again. It does not approach. It retreats, dissolving back into the haze.

Kael signals again. A low, rhythmic thud. "The pour is coming closer."

He is right. The molten river has reached the base of the Great Vent. The heat intensifies. The air shimmers. I look down at the ground I walked on yesterday. It is gone. The cracks I knew, the safe paths, the places where the rust-moss grew thick—they are all under the new layer. Smooth. Sterile. Empty.

A pang of loss strikes me, sharp and sudden. Not for the ground, but for the stories buried beneath it. Did anyone remember the scratch near the Central Spire that marked where we found the cache of starch packets? Will it be lost forever?

But then I look at the gouge I just made in the wall. Deep. Deliberate. High enough to survive.

*Here, Elara shared her blue light.*

This remains. This is real.

I shift my weight, adjusting my grip on the rebar. My muscles ache, dense and compacted from years of climbing, hanging, waiting. I am tired. The cold is seeping through my armor, finding the gaps in the sealant. But I am warm inside, fueled by the knowledge that I have preserved a fragment of truth in a world designed to erase it.

Below, the Residue begin to descend. They move like shadows, silent and swift, their personal lamps casting tiny pools of amber light on the fresh, sticky surface. They test the ground with tentative steps, sinking slightly into the still-curing material. They leave footprints that will last only until the next pour.

I do not join them yet. I stay high, watching. I am the sediment of the city’s breath. I am the echo in the cathedral. I am the silence between the clangs.

And as the new floor hardens, cooling into its final form, I close my eyes and listen to the hum. It changes pitch, subtly. A new chord. A new iteration.

I open my eyes. The dark eyes, wide and black, absorb the faint bioluminescent glow of the oil-ferns clinging to the vent nearby. I raise my rebar.

*Here,* I carve, deeper this time, *the City breathed.*
