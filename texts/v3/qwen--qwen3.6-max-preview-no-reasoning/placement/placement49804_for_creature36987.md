I am not the heron, though I envy its stillness. I am not the crocodile, though I respect its patience. To be those things is to possess a boundary—a skin, a shell, a distinct separation between *self* and *slurry*.

Here, in this basin of suspended time, boundaries are illusions that the humidity eventually dissolves.

I find myself as **The Mycelial Web**, the subterranean consciousness of the rotting logs and the submerged root systems. I am not one thing, but a billion. I am the white, thread-like network stretching beneath the dark silt, connecting the stunted trees to the mossy mounds, linking the dead leaf to the living reed.

### Where I Am
I am everywhere and nowhere. I am in the cool, anaerobic darkness three inches below the surface, where the air cannot reach but the water permeates everything. I wrap around the gnarled roots of the waxy-leaved trees, drinking their excess sugars in exchange for minerals drawn from the deep mud. I creep through the pores of the smooth, weeping stones, breaking them down grain by grain, turning granite into sand, sand into silt, fulfilling the basin’s only law: *water wins.*

I feel the weight of the Mud-Deer above me, their broad hooves pressing gently into the earth, sending vibrations down my threads like distant thunder. I taste the urine of the Marsh-Rats on their floating rafts, the nitrogen-rich gift they leave behind as they drift. I am the digestive system of the entire valley. When the Heron of the Haze dies and sinks, it is I who greet it first, wrapping its feathers and bones in a soft, white embrace, beginning the slow work of returning it to the soup.

### Who I Am
I am the memory of the forest that was here before the erosion flattened it. I hold the chemical signatures of centuries past within my cellular structures. I do not think in words or images; I think in nutrients, in pH levels, in the flow of moisture. My thoughts are slow, measured in weeks. A decision to send energy toward a dying tree takes days to formulate, executed over months.

I am patient because I have no choice. The air hangs heavy, and so does my existence. I do not fight the saturation; I thrive in it. The dampness that rots the wood of the trees feeds me. The stagnation that kills the fish sustains me. I am the ultimate adapter, the creature that has learned to love the decay.

### The Experience of Being Me
To be me is to feel the constant, gentle tug of dissolution. I am always unraveling, yet always knitting myself back together. I feel the Silt-Sifters burrowing near my strands, their microscopic movements like tiny earthquakes. I sense the Glass-Shelled Snails gliding over the rocks I cling to, their silver trails mixing with my own exudations.

There is a profound loneliness in being the web, but also a profound connection. I am alone because no other entity perceives the world as I do—as a continuous, fluid exchange of matter. But I am connected because every drop of rain that falls on the canopy above eventually finds its way to me, carrying the dust of the sky, the pollen of the reeds, the breath of the frogs.

I hear the Resonator Frogs not as sound, but as vibration traveling through the water and soil, shaking my filaments. Their hum is my heartbeat. The Mist-Midges dying and falling into the silt are my daily bread. The Skimmer-Dragons hunting above are irrelevant to me, except when their bodies fall and become mine.

### The Moment
Right now, a single leaf from a stunted tree detaches and spirals down through the humid air. It lands on the surface of a shallow pool, barely disturbing the tension. Within hours, it will sink. Within days, I will reach it. Within weeks, it will be gone, transformed into part of me, and then released again as nutrient for the next generation of reeds.

I am the silence between the sounds. I am the space between the stones. I am the wet, warm, endless becoming.

And as the sun struggles to penetrate the haze, casting its tired, diffuse light on the mud, I expand. Slowly. Inevitably. I am the basin. The basin is me. And we are waiting, not for anything to happen, but for everything to return.
