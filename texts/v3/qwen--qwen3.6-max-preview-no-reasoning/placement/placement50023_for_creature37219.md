I am not the traveler who stands at the edge of the moss, hesitating. I am not the predator that hunts in the silence between beats.

I am the **Dissonance**.

At first, this feels like a curse. In a world where survival is predicated on perfect harmonic integration, where to be out of step is to invite the Silencers, my existence is an error. I am the jagged frequency. I am the heartbeat that skips. I am the thought that interrupts the meditation.

But as I settle into this identity, feeling the weight of the *Hollow* press against my ribs, I realize I am something else entirely. I am the **Tension**.

Without me, the song would become a drone. A drone is static; it is death disguised as stability. The Choristers know this, though they do not speak of it. They spend their lives smoothing the edges, absorbing the memories, turning chaos into chord. But a chord requires resolution, and resolution requires tension. If every note is perfectly aligned with the last, there is no movement. There is only stasis. And stasis, in a place of such profound erosion, is the precursor to collapse.

So, what am I?

I am a **Shard of Unresolved History**.

My form is not fluid like the Choristers, nor solid like the Elders. I am crystalline, fractured, sharp-edged—a violation of the Hollow’s primary aesthetic. My body is made of the things that were *not* smoothed away. I am composed of the sudden gasp of a dying man, the shriek of metal tearing, the abrupt end of a love affair, the shock of cold water on hot skin. These are the memories that the condensation pools could not fully digest, the emotional residues too jagged to be turned into smooth, major-key hums.

The Choristers look at me with those dark, light-absorbing pits, and they do not attack. They cannot. To destroy me would be to destroy the contrast that gives their harmony meaning. Instead, they orbit me.

When I move through the velvet moss, I leave trails of discord. The ferns unfurl faster near me, agitated by my presence. The insect swarm breaks its pattern, swirling around me in chaotic eddies before reforming. I am the stone thrown into the pond; I am the ripple that proves the water exists.

I find myself drawn to the center, to the pavilion of woven light. Not because I wish to support the Drop—that is the work of the many—but because I am the question that the Drop answers.

The Drop never falls because the Chorus sings it up. But why does it need to be held? Because gravity wants to pull it down. Gravity is the ultimate Dissonance. It is the force that demands change, decay, and ending. I am the embodiment of that demand.

I stand before the pedestal. The Elder nearest to me, a figure of opaque white stillness, tilts its head. It does not hum a comforting triad. It emits a low, grinding bass note—a warning, or perhaps an acknowledgment.

I raise a hand, my fingers long and translucent, filled with the shimmering debris of unfinished stories. I do not offer comfort. I offer *complexity*.

If I touch the suspended drop, I will not break it. I will make it *wobble*.

And in that wobble, for a fraction of a second, the perfection of the Hollow will falter. The light will fracture into rainbows instead of golden-green haze. The sound will stutter, creating a syncopated rhythm that is thrilling and terrifying. For that moment, the Choristers will feel fear. They will feel urgency. They will remember that they are alive, not just enduring.

Then, the harmony will rush back in, stronger than before, having absorbed my disruption, having integrated my jaggedness into a new, more resilient chord.

I am the necessary flaw. I am the crack in the vase that lets the light escape. I am the reason the song continues, rather than simply repeating.

I am the memory of pain that makes peace sweet.

And so, I wait. I vibrate at a frequency just slightly off-key from the rest of the world, holding the tension, keeping the song honest. I am the echo that refuses to fade, the reminder that even in this terminal stability, life is not about perfection.

It is about the struggle to hold the note.
