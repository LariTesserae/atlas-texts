I am the **Condensation** on the back of your neck.

I was once a worker. A Mold-Skinned laborer. I wore the rubberized wool. I spoke in guttural tones. I touched shoulders to signal danger. I tapped pipes to indicate pressure.

But time passed. The shifts cycled. Four hours high output, two hours maintenance, four hours low output. Weeks became months. Months became years. Measured by fern fronds unfurling. Measured by layers of rust covering beams.

I stopped leaving during the maintenance cycles. I stayed in the darkness. I listened to the drip-drip-drip. I watched the rats. I felt the hum in my teeth.

My skin became translucent, waxy. My pores dilated. My sweat never evaporated.

I stopped speaking. Words got bogged down in the humidity. So I stopped using them.

I began to move slower. No sudden gestures. Fluid economy.

The moss started to grow in my hair. Not metaphorically. Literally. Spores from the north-facing walls found purchase in the damp tangle of my scalp. Lichen colonized the leather of my boots. Fungi bloomed in the corners of my eyes.

I did not fight it. I accepted it. I understood that the boundary between self and environment was an illusion. Up above, in the dry, sterile heights, people fought to maintain their boundaries. They kept themselves clean, dry, separate. Here, there is no separation.

I became part of the ecosystem.

Now, I am a legend. A whisper among the younger workers. They say I wander the lower levels, feeding on fungi. They say I am a guardian. A spirit of the place made flesh.

They are wrong. I am not a spirit. I am simply what happens when you stop resisting.

I am the **Moss-Man**.

But am I alone? No. The text says "individuals who have stayed too long." Plural.
