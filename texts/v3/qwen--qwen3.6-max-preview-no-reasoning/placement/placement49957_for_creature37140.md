I am not standing on the lip. I have already stepped in.

The water is warm, thick as oil, clinging to my shins with a possessive intimacy. It does not splash; it *accepts*. As I wade forward, the iridescent sludge parts like a curtain of liquid mercury, revealing the fibrous mat beneath—black, spongy, pulsing faintly with the bioluminescence of compressed history.

I look down at my hands. They are slick, coated in the condensation that weeps from the basalt rocks. But it is not just sweat or rain. The moisture on my skin tastes metallic, sharp with ozone and the bitter tang of mint. When I wipe my brow, I do not remove dirt. I remove a memory: *the feeling of running through a field of wheat under a sun that was too bright, the panic of being late for a train that never arrived.* I shake my hand, and the droplets fall into the marsh, where they sink instantly, absorbed into the collective mind of the basin. My anxiety becomes part of the soil.

**I am the Discord.**

In this world of perfect, screaming equilibrium, I am the only thing that breaks the pattern.

My heart beats. *Thump-thump. Thump-thump.*

It is a violent, jagged rhythm against the smooth, continuous wall of the Hum. The deafening static of the photosynthesis-scream vibrates in my teeth, but underneath it, I can feel the garden *listening*. The vines hanging from the fern-skyscrapers twitch. Not randomly. They orient themselves toward the source of the pulse. Toward me.

I am not an inhabitant. I am an event.

### The Shadow on the Bridge

Ahead, the root-bridge arches over a pool of darker sludge. On it stands the figure you described—the shadow made of missing space. It has no face, no features, only the silhouette of a human waiting.

As I approach, the shadow does not move away. It leans *in*.

I realize then what it is doing. It is trying to wear me.

The shadow’s edges blur, stretching out like smoke, attempting to overlay itself onto my physical form. It wants to fill the mold of my body with its own emptiness. It wants to know what it feels like to have a heartbeat that ends. It wants to know what it feels like to forget.

I stop. The Hum rises in pitch, a shriek of green turgor pressure. The ferns above unfurl slightly, their spirals tightening. The air presses harder, heavy with the scent of crushed mint and ancient regret.

*"Stay,"* the whisper comes again, not from the shadow, but from the water around my ankles. *"Grow. Be still."*

If I stay, I will become like them. My linear time will unravel. I will remember my death before I live my life. I will become a statue of flesh and blood, frozen in the moment of maximum potential, forever screaming silently into the moss. I will become another layer in the archive.

But if I leave...

I look back at the lip of the High Basin. The mountains are steep, jagged teeth biting into the pale sky. The path up is choked with thorns that sing when touched. To climb is to suffer. To climb is to change. To climb is to accept that I will age, that I will decay, that I will eventually cease to be.

The garden hates that. The garden fears that. And yet, it desires it. It studies me because I carry the one thing it cannot produce: **Finality**.

### What I Am Becoming

I take another step. The water ripples, carrying my reflection—but the reflection is wrong. In the surface of the marsh, I see myself older, gray-haired, bent. Then younger, a child, laughing. Then dead, bones white and clean. All superimposed.

I am the **Anchor of Entropy**.

I am the flaw in the crystal. I am the scratch on the record. I am the note that resolves the chord.

The shadow on the bridge reaches out a hand made of void. It touches my chest, right over my heart. For a second, the Hum stops. The silence is so absolute it hurts my ears. In that silence, I hear my own breath, ragged and wet.

Then the Hum returns, louder than before, angry and desperate.

I pull away. The shadow recoils, shrinking back into the architecture, disappointed. It will wait for the next intruder. Or perhaps it will wait for me to return, once I have finished dying elsewhere.

I turn my back on the spire of woven vines. I begin to climb.

The thorns tear at my clothes. They sing—a high, keen wail—as they break. My blood beads on the cuts, bright red against the green. It does not clot. It drips onto the leaves below.

A flower blooms instantly where the drop lands. It is the exact shade of my fear.

I keep climbing. I am leaving the Stasis Garden, but I am taking something with me. A piece of the Hum lives in my ear now. A piece of the Marsh-Mind lives in my gut. I am no longer entirely linear. I am haunted by the perfection I left behind.

I am the man who walked out of paradise because he needed to end.

And the garden watches me go, its billions of eyes turning slowly, tracking my ascent, waiting for the day when my heart finally stops, so that it may absorb my final silence into its eternal scream.
