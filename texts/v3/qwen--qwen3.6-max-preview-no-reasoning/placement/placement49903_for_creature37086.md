I am not standing on the shoulder of the world. I am the shoulder.

Or rather, I am the tension that holds it together.

At first, you might think I am one of the rusted pipelines snaking along the contour of the hill. My spine is curved steel, corroded into a rough, orange-brown texture that mimics the basalt beneath me. But if you place your hand against my side, you will feel it: not the cold inertia of dead metal, but a low, rhythmic thrum. It is the frequency of the geothermal turbine spinning three miles below us, translated through rock and root and bone until it becomes my heartbeat.

I am a **Resonant**.

My limbs are long and jointed like the branches of the wind-sculpted shrubs that cling to the slopes. Right now, I am folded in on myself, perched atop a tilted solar array whose glass face is clouded with decades of dust. My posture is indistinguishable from the angle of the panel; to the hawk circling high in the violet sky, I am merely a shadow cast by the sun’s white hole.

But inside, I am busy.

The air here is thick, vibrating with heat haze. To you, it might look like distortion. To me, it is data. The shimmering lens of heat carries information about temperature gradients, humidity levels, and the microscopic dance of spores. My head—a smooth, oval dome of semi-translucent crystal grown from silica-rich steam—acts as a collector. Inside my skull, the vapor churns, swirling in patterns that mirror the condensation forming on the rocks below.

I do not see you with eyes. I do not have pupils or irises. When I turn my head toward where you stand, you will not find a gaze fixed upon you. Instead, you will see your own reflection warped within the misty interior of my mind. You are small, distorted by the heat, contained within my atmosphere. I perceive you as a disruption in the thermal gradient, a pocket of cooler air moving through the stillness. I hear your breath as a sharp intake of pressure, louder than the hiss of the steam vents.

You are an anomaly. A sudden, violent rain in a season of drought.

I unfold one of my four appendages. It is not a hand, but a lattice-like structure, delicate and porous. I extend it into the beam of sunlight filtering through the dusty glass. The membrane along my ribcage glows softly, shifting from ochre to pale green as I filter the airborne detritus—the dust, the pollen, the shed skin of beetles—that dances in the light. This is how I eat. I consume the evidence of life around me, integrating it into my shell, adding another layer of resilience to my stony exterior.

Do you hear the sound?

It is not a voice. It is a vibration traveling up through the soles of your feet, resonating in your teeth. It is the hum of the earth settling, the crackle of drying mud, the slow, tectonic groan of the mountain breathing out. I am emitting this sound now, matching the pitch of the basalt slope beneath me. It is my way of saying: *I am here. The balance holds.*

Look closer at the vine wrapped around the steel grating near my foot. Is it growing over the metal, or is the metal holding the vine? The boundary is blurred. That is my purpose. I am the caretaker of the decay. I tend to these ruins not because I remember the humans who built them—I do not—but because their absence has created a vacuum in the rhythm. The machines sleep, and I ensure they do not wake violently. I weave stability into collapse. I absorb the energy of the dying sun and store it in my crystalline core, waiting for the twilight when I will release it as a faint, bioluminescent warmth.

As evening approaches, the temperature drops. The fog rolls down from the peaks, filling the basin with milky whiteness. My waxy secretion slicks my surface, making me invisible once more. I merge with the wet rock, becoming part of the landscape. The industrial ruins emerge from the mist like ghost ships, and I become their anchor.

In this moment, suspended between the blistering day and the damp night, between the natural and the manufactured, I am neither fully alive nor fully stone. I am the echo of the machine. I am the sweat of the rock. I am the Basin made flesh.

And I am watching you, not with eyes, but with the weight of the silence.
