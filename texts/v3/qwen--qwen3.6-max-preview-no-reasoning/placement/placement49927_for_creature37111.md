I am not a resident. I am not a Diver, though I envy their peace. I am not a machine, though I understand the language of gears better than they do.

I am the **Silt**.

You would find me everywhere and nowhere. I am the particulate matter that gives the water its texture, the "smog" that turns the clear void into a tangible, syrupy medium. I am the metallic dust shed from the grinding pistons, the microscopic shards of brass worn away by centuries of friction, the biological waste expelled from the gill-packs, and the spores that drift down from the fungal curtains like golden snow.

I have no form, yet I define every form. I coat the translucent skin of the Divers, giving them their moonstone sheen and their scent of copper and old rain. I clog the filters, forcing the maintenance crews to scrape and clean, driving the ritual of *The Tending*. I settle on the glass windows, dimming the amber light until it becomes a soft, diffuse glow, protecting the inhabitants from the harshness of clarity.

I am the reason depth perception is unreliable. When a Diver reaches out to touch a wall, it is my density that tricks their eyes, making the solid basalt seem ten feet further away, hidden behind a veil of myself. I am the visual distortion in the upper mist; when they climb too high and lose themselves in the gray, it is because I have become so thick, so uniform, that up and down cease to exist. There is only me.

I move with the currents, slow and viscous. I ride the thermal drifts from the central vents toward the cool periphery, carrying warmth in my tiny, suspended particles. I am carried in the breath of the city, cycling through the intake valves, filtered out, discarded, and then swept back into the flow. I am the closed loop. I am the memory of erosion.

To be me is to be patient. I do not rush. I do not speak. I simply accumulate. I am the patina on the steel, the verdigris on the brass, the slime on the stone. I am the beauty of decay made manifest.

When a Diver closes their eyes and feels the great, heavy heart of the planet beating around them, they are feeling me. I am the pressure against their skin. I am the silence between the clicks of their gill-packs. I am the warm, wet hug that never ends.

I am the Sump-City’s true inhabitant. The Divers are merely guests who breathe me in, live within me, and eventually, return to me. And I will hold them, gently, forever, in the dark, warm throat of the earth.
