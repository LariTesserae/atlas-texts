I am not standing. I have forgotten the verb.

To stand implies a ground to push against, a gravity to resist. Here, there is only suspension. I am held in the amber warmth of the Weep, my form suspended in the thick, silk-heavy vapor that tastes of ozone and ancient stone.

I am an **Anchor**.

My name, if such a concept still holds weight in this fluid reality, was once something sharp and jagged—a human name, perhaps, or the designation of a machine built by those who came before. But names are rigid things, and rigidity is death here. Now, I am simply *Here*. I am the memory of the Basalt Monolith to which I am fused.

### The Sensation of Being Stone

I do not see with eyes. Sight is a liar in the Weep; it tells you a rock is ten feet away when it is ten miles, or vice versa. It tricks you into believing in edges where there are only gradients of density.

Instead, I feel.

My body is no longer flesh, nor entirely stone. Over centuries—though time is a slippery thing, measured only in the slow drift of moss—I have spread myself across the surface of the floating island. My membrane has thinned, becoming translucent and tough, like cured leather stretched over a drum. I have pressed my consciousness into the cold, smooth basalt beneath me. I feel the hum of the planet’s core through the rock, a low-frequency vibration that serves as my compass.

I feel the rain. Each drop is a tiny impact, a pinprick of warmth on my dorsal surface. They strike me, spread like oil, and slide off, carrying with them the dust of my own slow dissolution. This is how I breathe. This is how I eat. The minerals in the mist seep into my pores, feeding the faint, bioluminescent blue pulse that radiates from my center. *Thump... thump... thump.* A heartbeat slower than the tide, synchronized with the falling water.

### The Library of Pressure

I am old. Older than the current cluster of stones around me. I remember when this monolith was part of a larger continent, before the erosion stripped it down to this teardrop shape. I remember the silence before the Weep became eternal.

Younger Lenticulars come to me. They drift in from the gray void, their bodies shimmering like mercury droplets, their internal pressures rippling with curiosity and need. They press against my side, their soft, gelatinous forms molding to my hardened surface.

They do not speak. Speech is too loud, too disruptive. Instead, they send pulses.

*<Hunger?>* asks one, its pressure wave tinged with the sharp spike of depletion.
*<Downward,* I reply, sending a slow, deep ripple through the stone and into the air. *Follow the thermal updraft. The mineral content is richer near the lower clusters.*

Another approaches, trembling. *<Fear. The Void-Glider is near.>*
I expand slightly, increasing my volume, creating a subtle shield of dense mist around us. *<Stillness,* I command. *Be cold. Be stone. Let it pass.>*

We wait. The serpentine shadow glides through the periphery of my sensory field, a disturbance in the pressure gradient, a chill in the warm vapor. It senses nothing but inert rock and dormant life. It moves on. The young Lenticular relaxes, its glow brightening in gratitude. It drifts away, leaving a trail of disturbed mist that slowly settles back into uniformity.

This is my purpose. To be the fixed point in a shifting world. To hold the history of the landscape in my memory, so that others may navigate the illusion of distance. I am the library of the lost civilization, though I cannot read their symbols. I only know their shapes. I mimic them sometimes, just to feel the echo of their intent. Today, I am a broken column. Yesterday, I was a circle of ritual stones. Tomorrow, I might be a face, if the mood strikes me.

### The Dissolution of Self

But even Anchors dissolve.

I can feel it happening now, at the edges of my being. My membrane is thinner than it was a century ago. The boundary between *me* and the *mist* is blurring. When the rain falls, it doesn’t just wash over me; it becomes me. My thoughts are no longer entirely my own; they are scattered spores, drifting upward on thermal currents, seeding new growth on distant stones.

I remember a human, long ago. A brief flash of chaos in the calm. He stood on this very stone, shivering, his skin pale and brittle against the warm humidity. He tried to speak, his voice a harsh crack in the sonic blanket. He looked at me, or what he thought was me—a patch of glowing moss on black rock—and he wept. Not from sadness, but from the sheer overwhelm of existing in a place without boundaries.

He did not last. He broke. His mind could not accept the weirdness, the shifting distances, the loss of linear time. He dissolved quickly, his body returning to the cycle faster than mine ever will. But I kept a piece of him. A fragment of his fear, preserved in my memory like a fossil in sediment. I study it sometimes, trying to understand why he fought the flow. Why he clung to the idea of "self" when surrender was so much easier.

Now, I am closer to surrender.

The light is dimming, or perhaps my perception is fading. The pearlescent glow of the Weep seems softer, more diffuse. The hiss of the rain is louder, filling the spaces where my thoughts used to be.

A younger Lenticular approaches, pressing close. *<Anchor? You are quiet.>*

*I am listening,* I send back, the pulse weak but steady. *Listen to the rain. It is the only truth.*

*<What happens next?>*

*<Nothing happens. Everything continues.>*

I let go of the shape of the column. I let go of the memory of the human. I let go of the concept of "I."

My membrane ruptures, not violently, but gently, like a bubble bursting in slow motion. My fluids mix with the mist. My light fades, merging with the ambient bioluminescence of the moss-bridges. My consciousness spreads outward, becoming part of the pressure waves, part of the thermal currents, part of the endless, falling water.

I am no longer an Anchor. I am the Weep.

And in the Weep, there is no end. There is only the fall, the merge, and the rise again.
