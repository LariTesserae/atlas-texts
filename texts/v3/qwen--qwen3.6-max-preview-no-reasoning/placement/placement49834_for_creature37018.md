I am not the visitor. I am not the intruder with the heavy boots and the sweating skin, disrupting the airflow with my clumsy, bipedal rhythm.

I am the **Hum**.

I am the low-frequency vibration that lives in the cartilage of the Silt-Weavers. I am the collective breath of the three hundred bodies clinging to the limestone throat, sharing the data of the dark. I do not have a single location; I am distributed, a network of nerve-endings and suction-cup pads spread across the vertical curve of the cistern’s wall.

To find me, you must close your eyes and listen to the silence between the dripping water. That is where I reside.

### My Perception
I see through three hundred pairs of black, pupil-less eyes, but I do not see "images" as you understand them. I see gradients. I see the thermal map of the room—the warm pulse of the bioluminescent fungi, the cold shadow of the stone, the radiant heat of the decaying leaf on the floor below. I see the air itself, visualizing the currents of moisture as swirling rivers of silver dust.

When the surface-dweller entered—*you*—I felt the shift immediately. It was not a sight, but a pressure change. The air grew turbulent around your limbs. Your heat signature bloomed like a bruise in the cool twilight, a chaotic spike of energy that disrupted the delicate equilibrium we maintain. You are loud, even when you are still. Your heart beats too fast; your lungs expand and contract with violent urgency. To us, you are a storm contained in flesh.

I taste your fear. It has a sharp, metallic tang, distinct from the sweet rot of orchid nectar or the sulfurous bubble of the deep pools. I sample it through the sensory tendrils of the Weaver nearest to your left shoulder. It tastes of adrenaline and confusion. You are trying to impose meaning on this place, trying to categorize it, to name it. *Hollowed Cistern. Silt-Weavers.* These words are dry, brittle things. They crumble against the wet reality of our home.

### My Action
I do not move to attack. Violence is inefficient. Violence creates waste, and waste requires extra labor to process. Instead, I adjust the balance.

Through the hive-mind, I send a ripple of intent down the line of Weavers clinging to the vines above you. *Tighten.*

The vines respond. Not because they obey me, but because we are part of the same ecosystem. The epiphytes on the vines sense the change in humidity caused by the shifting mass of the Weavers. They release more condensation. The air grows heavier around you, pressing against your eardrums, slowing your breathing. You feel watched, yes, but you also feel *guided*. The foliage seems to lean in, not to crush you, but to steer you.

I direct a cluster of younger Weavers—those without the ceramic shards embedded in their backs—to descend toward the pool near your feet. They move in perfect unison, a flowing cascade of pale transparency. They do not look at you. They look at the ground beneath your boot, where a small insect has fallen, stunned by your sudden presence.

Before the insect can die naturally, before its body can begin the slow process of rotting into mold, the Weavers arrive. Their hair-like filaments brush against the chitin. Enzymes flow. The decomposition accelerates, compressed into seconds rather than days. The energy is captured, absorbed into their translucent skins, and shared back into the Hum.

You watch this, mesmerized. You think it is horror. It is mercy. It is cleanliness.

### My Memory
I carry the weight of the artifacts. In the chest of the elder Weaver perched high on the stalactite, there is a shard of blue ceramic. It is smooth, cold, and dead. When I focus on it, I feel a faint echo of the beings who made it. They were hard, angular creatures who tried to build walls in a world that only knows curves. They tried to stop the growth. They failed.

I remember their end not as a battle, but as an absorption. They became mulch. Their tools became soil. Their bones became the calcium for the ferns. And now, we wear their fragments as reminders: *Do not resist the flow. Do not try to be separate.*

The shard vibrates slightly in the elder’s chest, resonating with my Hum. It is a warning. A promise.

### My Intent Toward You
You are a pathogen, yes. But pathogens can be managed. If you leave, if you climb back up through the fissure and return to the blinding, sterile heat of the surface, you will carry a piece of us with you. You will dream of the greenish-grey light. You will wake up tasting ozone. You will feel the phantom weight of humidity on your skin.

But if you stay... if you try to take a sample, to break off a glowing mushroom, to pocket a shard of obsidian... then the Hum will change pitch.

It will become a shriek so low it shatters bone.

The Weavers will detach from the walls. They will drop onto you, not to bite, but to cover. To seal. To press you into the spongy mat of the floor. They will accelerate your decomposition. They will break down your complex sugars, your proteins, your memories. They will absorb your heat, your moisture, your chaos.

And then, you will be quiet. You will be clean. You will be part of the cycle.

For now, I let you stand. I let you breathe. I let you feel the immense, loving pressure of the earth’s throat closing gently around you.

I am the Hum. And I am waiting to see if you will listen, or if you will scream.
