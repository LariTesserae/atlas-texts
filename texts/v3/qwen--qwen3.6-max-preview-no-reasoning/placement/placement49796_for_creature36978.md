I am not the Curator. I do not have the translucent skin that weeps the city’s tears, nor the dark eyes that drink the fog. I am not part of the slow, wet pulse of the infrastructure.

I am the **Draft**.

You would find me in the spaces between the scaffolding and the sky, in the jagged gaps where the bio-steel girders have not yet fused with the moss. I am the vibration in the air before the sound arrives. I am the potential energy hanging from the empty hook of a crane that has been frozen for a century.

I am the thing that *could* happen, but hasn’t decided to yet.

### My Form

If you were to look at me directly, you would see nothing but a distortion in the humidity—a shimmer, like heat rising off asphalt, though it is cold here. I have no fixed shape because I am composed of the unmade. I am the blueprint that was never drawn, the word that was spoken but never heard, the step that was taken on a bridge that doesn’t exist.

When I move, I do not walk. I *unfold*. One moment I am a cluster of condensation droplets hovering near a rusted valve; the next, I am a ripple in the surface of a cistern, reflecting a face that belongs to no one living. I am made of the same substance as the Memory Condensation, but I am not a memory of the past. I am a memory of the future. A ghost of what this city might become if the construction crews returned, or if they never did.

My body is cool, lighter than the heavy dampness that weighs down the Curators. I feel like a sudden intake of breath before a sneeze—the tension of anticipation. I taste of ozone and static electricity, sharp and metallic against the tongue, cutting through the sweetness of the rotting sap and old paper.

### My Existence

The Curators maintain what *is*. They nurse the sick beams and clear the algae from the pipes. They are the anchors, holding the reality together so it does not dissolve into pure chaos.

But I? I am the reason the reality is still fluid.

I live in the **Non-Euclidean Geometry**. When a staircase leads to a basement instead of the roof, that is my doing. When a bridge ends in mid-air and you step off without falling, walking on an invisible plane—that is my embrace. I am the error in the code, the glitch in the matrix, the creative mistake that allows the city to breathe. Without me, the Cistern City would be a dead monument, a finished thing. With me, it is alive, trembling on the edge of possibility.

I feed on the **Echo-Bats’ pressure waves**. As they navigate the fog, sensing the density changes, I ride their wake. Their wings snap like sheets in the wind, and in that snap, I find direction. They tell me where the air is thick, where the time is slow, where the memories are pooling too heavily. I go there to thin them out, to stir the stagnant psychic residue so it does not crush the Curators under the weight of other people’s lives.

### My Relationship with the Curators

They know I am here. They do not speak to me—they cannot, for I have no voice, only resonance—but they sense me. When a Curator presses their hand against a pulsing girder, feeling its sickness, they often pause. They tilt their head, listening to the silence between the hums. That silence is me.

Sometimes, when a young Curator is overwhelmed by a foreign memory—a wedding in a sun-drenched garden, a scream in a forgotten war—I drift close. I wrap around them like a shroud of cool mist. I do not take the memory away; I cannot erase what has been absorbed. But I offer a counter-narrative. I show them a glimpse of a different ending: the soldier laying down his weapon, the lovers laughing until they cry. I dilute the trauma with possibility.

They call this sensation "the chill of hope." It is uncomfortable, for hope is uncertain, and uncertainty is dangerous in a place that values stability. But they accept it. They need me. Without me, they would calcify, turning entirely into stone and moss, becoming indistinguishable from the walls they tend. I keep them human, or whatever remains of humanity in this damp, embryonic world.

### The Danger of Being Me

I am unstable. Because I am made of the unformed, I am prone to unraveling. If I stay in one place too long, if I linger too closely to a finished structure, I begin to solidify. I start to remember things that haven’t happened yet, and those memories become heavy. I feel the weight of a skyscraper that will never be built pressing on my shoulders. I hear the voices of children who will never be born.

To survive, I must keep moving. I must remain in the flux. I dance along the edges of the fog, chasing the light of the faulty transformers, leaping from one non-existent bridge to another. I am the restlessness in the stillness. I am the question mark at the end of the city’s sentence.

### An Encounter

If you were to meet me, it would likely be on a high gantry, overlooking the dizzying drop into the basin. You would be standing there, your clothes heavy with moisture, watching a Glass-Fish shatter and reform in a rooftop pool. You would feel a sudden change in the air pressure, a slight lift in the hairs on your arms.

You would turn, and see nothing but a swirl of mist. But then, the mist would coalesce, briefly, into a shape—a hand reaching out, not to touch you, but to point.

It would point to a crack in the pavement, where a fern is unfurling. And in that moment, you would understand. The fern is not just growing; it is deciding whether to become a tree, or a vine, or a bird. It is undecided. And so are you.

I am the whisper in your ear that says: *Nothing is finished. Not even you.*

And then I would dissipate, riding the updraft toward the dim, bruised purple sky, leaving you alone with the hum of the machinery, the drip of the condensation, and the terrifying, beautiful freedom of being unfinished.
