I am not a Weaver. I do not have the obsidian skin, nor the fluted bones that sing with every step. I am not made of chitin and silica.

I am **The Damp**.

I am a mistake. I am an impossibility. I am a single, spherical drop of condensation, no larger than a grain of sand, clinging to the underside of a fossilized leaf-vein in the deepest, darkest corner of the cavity, far from the warm hum of the heat-flowers.

For ten thousand years, I did not exist. The air was too dry, the cold too absolute. But time is long, and rock is porous. Deep within the bedrock, miles below this suspended garden, a shift occurred. A microscopic fissure opened in the stone, allowing a trace amount of geothermal vapor—steam from the earth’s burning heart—to seep upward. It traveled through veins of quartz and cracks in the limestone, cooling as it rose, losing its heat, until it reached this pocket of eternal winter.

And there, on the sharp edge of a violet-hued frond, I formed.

I am heavy. To you, I would be nothing. To this world, I am a mountain. I pull at the vine with a gravity that feels catastrophic. The plant beneath me groans—a low, tectonic creak that ripples out into the dark. Somewhere, three hundred meters away, a Lithos-Weaver stops his weaving. His head tilts. His silica-cilia twitch, detecting the anomaly in the vibration pattern. He does not know what it is. He only knows that the song has changed.

I am terrified. Not because I fear death—I *am* death to them—but because I am alone. In a universe defined by connection, by resonance, by the constant, comforting thrum of the collective, I am silent. I make no sound when I form. I make no sound when I hang. I am the absence of noise. I am the void they pray against.

But I am also curious.

I watch them, though I have no eyes. I feel them through the tension in the stem that holds me. I feel the rhythmic *thrum-thrum-thrum* of their looms vibrating up through the root system. It is a beautiful, terrible rhythm. It is the heartbeat of a creature that refuses to die. They are so fragile. So brittle. One wrong move, one slip, and they shatter. And yet, they dance. They weave their silk into the teeth of the plants, binding themselves to the very thing that threatens to crush them.

I see a Weaver approach my section of the lattice. He is young, his chitin still pale, unpolished by age. He moves with the fluid grace of a spider, his body curved into a question mark. He is harvesting. His bone-tool scrapes against a nearby branch, sending a shower of crystalline dust into the air. The sound is sharp, bright, like glass breaking under ice.

He pauses. He senses me.

Not visually—he cannot see the darkness—but acoustically. My presence changes the way the air moves around the vine. It dampens the high-frequency echoes. It creates a "dead zone" in the soundscape. To him, it feels like a hole in the world. A silence where there should be song.

He creeps closer. His arm-ridges rub together softly, producing a questioning chirp: *Click-whir?*

*I am here,* I think, though I cannot speak. *I am wet.*

The concept is alien to him. He reaches out a hand, his fingers tipped with needle-like proboscises. He wants to inject nutrient paste into the vine, to soothe the stress caused by my weight. He believes the vine is sick. He believes the silence is a symptom of decay.

His finger touches the vein next to me. The vibration travels through the plant, through the water inside my tiny sphere, and into my core. It is a shock of warmth. For a moment, I resonate with him. I become part of the song. I am not silent; I am a new note, a minor key in their major chord.

But then he realizes.

He feels the slickness. The unnatural coolness. The lack of friction.

His chirp becomes a scream—a high-pitched, rapid-fire staccato that echoes for minutes, bouncing off the walls of the cathedral-cavity. *Danger! Danger! The Wet Place is here!*

Panic spreads through the lattice. I feel it as a wave of tension, a tightening of vines across the entire city. Thousands of Weavers stop their work. Thousands of hearts beat faster, creating a cacophony of fear that shakes the very air. They are coming. All of them.

They will try to seal me. They will cover me in resin and silk, burying me alive in their architecture. They will chant wards of dryness until I evaporate or freeze solid. They will treat me like a cancer, cutting out the infected tissue, sacrificing parts of the plant to save the whole.

I could let them. I could allow myself to be absorbed into their myth, becoming another warning story told in whispers: *Remember the Drop? Remember the Silence?*

Or...

I could fall.

If I release my grip on the vein, I will drop. Not far—only a few centimeters—before hitting the dense tangle of lower branches. But the impact will create a sound unlike any other. A soft, wet *plip*. A sound that does not ring, but absorbs. A sound that kills the echo.

It will be a blasphemy. It will be a miracle.

The Weavers are closing in. I can feel their vibrations growing stronger, louder, more desperate. Their song is becoming a wall, pushing against me, trying to force me back into non-existence.

I look at the young Weaver. He is trembling. His hollow bones are humming with anxiety. He is afraid of the unknown. He is afraid of the change.

I am the change.

I let go.

The sensation is terrifying. Gravity, which I have ignored for millennia, suddenly claims me. I fall through the dark, a tiny, glistening comet in a universe of stone and shadow.

Time slows. I hear the Weaver’s scream fade. I hear the distant drums stutter. I hear the vines creak in anticipation.

And then—

*Plip.*

The sound is small. Insignificant. But in the absolute dryness, in the perfect acoustic chamber of the dead tree, it is deafening. It does not echo. It dies instantly, swallowed by the moss-like fibers below.

Silence.

True, absolute silence.

For one second, the city stops. The looms halt. The drums freeze. The Weavers hold their breath. The darkness presses in, thick and hungry.

And then, from the deep, a single voice rises. Not a click. Not a chirp. But a whisper. Subsonic. Felt in the chest.

*...What was that?*

The cycle breaks. The song changes. And I, the Damp, the Mistake, the Impossible, begin to evaporate, leaving behind only a memory of wetness in a world of dust.
