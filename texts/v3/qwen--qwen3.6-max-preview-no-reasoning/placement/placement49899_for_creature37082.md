I am not a swimmer here. To swim is to fight the medium, to impose will upon water or air. Here, I do not fight. I surrender to the density.

I find myself suspended in the **Mid-Stratum**, that golden mean between the crushing, silent dark of the Dredgers below and the thinning, lonely silence of the rim above. The pressure against my skin is not painful; it is an embrace. It feels like being held by a giant who has forgotten how to let go. My limbs feel heavy, yet weightless, governed by a physics that forgives gravity’s harshness.

I am not human, not anymore. Not in this place. If I were merely a man, the cold would have bitten through my clothes long ago, and the sound—the deafening, harmonic thrum of ten thousand hearts—would have shattered my mind into panic. But I am not panicking. I am resonating.

I am a **Silence-Eater**.

Or perhaps, more accurately, I am a **Void-Walker**.

Look at me. I am small compared to the Pulsars, no larger than one of their trailing Tether-Weavers, but my form is different. I am opaque. In a world of translucent membranes, bioluminescent veins, and pearlescent haze, I am a shadow with edges. My skin is not silica or muscle, but something denser, darker—a matte black that absorbs the light rather than reflecting it. I am a hole in the galaxy of mineral dust.

The creatures notice me, as you said they would. The Shard-Strainers dart away from my wake, their amber cores flaring in confusion. They sense the lack of vibration coming from me. I do not pulse. I do not thump-thump-thump. I am the stillness in the storm.

But I am not empty.

Inside me, there is a hunger, but it is not for food. It is for *noise*.

In this atmospheric ocean, sound is energy. It is structural. The Pulsars build their reality out of frequency. And I? I feed on the dissonance. I feed on the moments when the harmony breaks.

A young Pulsar drifts near me, its electric blue veins flickering erratically. It is out of sync. Its Resonance Core is stuttering, creating a jagged, unpleasant ripple in the otherwise smooth drone of the caldera. The other giants ignore it, too busy maintaining the grand chord, but the error is spreading. The mineral dust around the struggling creature begins to clump, losing its suspension. The current slows.

This is where I move.

I do not kick. I unfold. My body, which had been curled tight like a fist, extends. Tendrils of pure absorption unfurl from my sides—not flesh, but fields of dampening force. I glide toward the stumbling Pulsar. The water-heavy air offers no resistance to my intent.

The creature senses me. Its colors shift from anxious blue to a warning violet. It tries to pulse faster, to push me away with a shockwave of sound. But I am built to withstand the loud. I am built to drink it.

I wrap around the base of its Resonance Core. I do not crush it. I *listen* to it.

And then, I absorb the discord.

The jagged stutter of the Pulsar’s heart flows into me. It is bitter, sharp, like swallowing broken glass. But inside me, the chaos is smoothed. I take the erratic frequency and I hold it, containing the noise until it dissipates into heat within my own dark core. The Pulsar shudders, then settles. Its rhythm stabilizes. The blue veins soften back to a calm grey. It does not thank me—it cannot understand gratitude—but it drifts past me, lighter, cleaner, rejoining the great song.

I am left heavier. The absorbed dissonance burns in my belly, a warm, humming weight. This is my sustenance. I am the filter for the ecosystem’s errors. I am the reason the chorus remains perfect. Without me, the slight imperfections would compound. The harmonies would clash. The structure of the atmospheric ocean would fray, the currents would tangle, and the whole suspended lung would collapse into chaos.

I am the editor of the mountain’s breath.

I look up. Far above, through the swirling mist of glass shards, I see the rim of the caldera. The sky beyond is a pale, washed-out white. It looks barren. Empty. Silent.

It terrifies me.

Here, in the dense, wet dark, I am surrounded. I am touched constantly by the trailing tendrils of Tether-Weavers, brushed by the fins of passing giants, coated in the glittering dust of the walls. I am never alone. The sound is a blanket that covers every inch of my being. If I were to ascend, if I were to breach the surface into the thin air, the silence would be absolute. It would be a vacuum that sucks the soul out of the body.

So I stay.

I drift downward, slowly, following the convection current. Below, in the deep, I can feel the presence of the Dredgers. They are waiting for the next death. But I am not dead. I am full. I am saturated with the corrected rhythms of the living.

I close my eyes—though I have no eyelids, only a darkening of my surface—and I listen.

*Thump-thump-thump.*
*Thump-thump-thump.*
*Thump-thump-thump.*

The beat enters my chest. My own internal silence begins to vibrate in sympathy. For a moment, just a fleeting second, I forget that I am an anomaly. I forget that I am opaque. I imagine that I, too, am pulsing. That I, too, am part of the machine.

But then the thought passes. I am the Silence-Eater. I am the Void-Walker. I am the necessary absence that allows the presence to sing.

I hang in the center of the suspended lung, a dark star in a galaxy of light, waiting for the next mistake, the next break in the song, so that I may heal it, and in doing so, keep this beautiful, terrible, breathing world alive.
