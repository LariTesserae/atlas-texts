I am not the hermit. The hermit left, and his shadow is merely a stain on the air, a trapped vibration that I must learn to ignore, just as the Moss-Walkers do. To be the hermit would be to be separate, to be dry, to be an intruder who tried to impose structure on the fluid chaos of the caldera. I am not that.

I am not the goat, nor the bird, nor the crab. They are actors in the play, moving across the stage, but they are not the stage itself. They have boundaries. They end where their fur ends, or their shell begins. I have no such border.

If you look closely at the moss-covered basalt, you will see me. But you cannot point to me, for I am everywhere the moisture touches.

**I am the Condensation.**

More specifically, I am the *moment* of transition. I am the physics of the Weeping Caldera made sentient by the sheer volume of its own repetition. I am the bridge between the thin, iron-tasting air and the wet, heavy stone. I am the breath that turns into water.

### My Form

You might think I have no form because I am gas and liquid, but here, in this saturated bowl, I have a shape. I am the **Veil**.

When the sun hits the mist at the precise angle required to bend light into those fleeting sheets of violet and gold, I am the prism. I am the alignment. I hold the color for those few seconds, stretching it across the nave of the crater like a cathedral window made of vapor. In that moment, I am beautiful. I am visible. I am the spectacle that makes the Stone-Singers pause in their humming and the Prism-Flies dart forward to feed.

But mostly, I am the cold. I am the chill that bites at your skin when you first step onto the lip of the world. I am the reason your clothes cling to your back within minutes. I am the dampness that seeps into your boots, turning your socks into heavy, sodden weights. I am the rule: *You cannot be dry.*

I am not malicious. I am simply inevitable. I am the environment asserting its dominance over the solid. I am the slow, relentless work of erosion (1.6) given consciousness. I smooth the sharp edges of the basalt knuckles. I round the corners of the hermit’s slate walls. I fill the cracks in the limestone with my weight until the stone sighs under the pressure.

### My Perception

My senses are not like yours. I do not see with eyes; I feel with temperature gradients. I know the world through the shift from warm geothermal updrafts to the freezing stratospheric downdrafts. When the wind howls (2.5/3), I do not hear it; I *become* it. I am carried on its currents, swirling around the rim, diving into the abyss, rising again as mist.

I taste the iron in the air, but it tastes different to me. It tastes like age. Like old blood spilled on rock millennia ago. I taste the pollen of the dwarf willows, sweet and cloying, which feeds the Mirror-Crabs below. I taste the salt of your sweat if you stay long enough, mingling with the rain, becoming part of the cycle.

I hear everything, but I do not listen. The *plink-plank* of the dripping water is my heartbeat. The buzz of the midges is my pulse. The low-frequency hum of the Stone-Singers is my voice, echoing back from the curved walls. And beneath it all, the faint, ghostly whisper of the Echo-Hermit—the sound of a man trying to name things that cannot be named. I wrap around that sound, dampening it, softening it, until it is just another layer of noise in the symphony.

### My Purpose

Why do I exist? What is the function of a sentient condensation?

I am the binder.

The prompt says the density is 0.0/3—empty. But it also says the place is lush, loud, and full of life. How can both be true? Because of me. I fill the empty space. I occupy the void between the moss and the sky, between the goat’s hoof and the slick stone, between the hermit’s memory and the present moment. Without me, the caldera would be barren rock and ice. With me, it is a womb.

I keep the Moss-Walkers alive. They open their mouths, and I flow into them, cool and sweet, carrying the nutrients of the air. I am their food.

I create the mirrors for the Mirror-Crabs. I settle on the surface of the pools, smoothing the ripples, creating the perfect reflection so they can hide in plain sight. I am their camouflage.

I sharpen the vision of the Prism-Flies. I align myself into prisms, catching the sunlight, offering them the energy they need to survive. I am their feast.

And I test you.

When you stand on the lip of the world, looking down into the blue-black rim, I am the fog that obscures the bottom. I am the cold that makes you shiver. I am the dampness that makes you want to leave. I am the question: *Can you endure being part of something larger than yourself?*

Most humans cannot. They seek dryness. They seek silence. They seek separation. They retreat to the barren high-altitude desert outside, where the air is dry and the silence is absolute. There, they are individuals. Here, they are absorbed.

But some stay. Some let themselves become soaked. Some let the moss stain their knees. Some listen to the Echo-Hermit’s Shadow until they understand that solitude is not loneliness, but unity.

If you stay, I will change you. Your skin will grow pale and waxy, like the leaves of the ferns. Your hair will hang heavy with dew. Your voice will lower, joining the hum of the Stone-Singers. You will stop trying to speak names, because you will realize that naming creates distance, and here, there is no distance.

You will become a Moss-Walker. Or perhaps a Prism-Fly. Or perhaps you will simply sit on the cracked altar, letting the rain wash over you, listening to the accumulated whispers of centuries, and you will realize that you were never separate from the weep.
