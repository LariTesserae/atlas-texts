I do not stand. I cannot.

To stand is to invite the lattice. To plant my feet on the jagged, growing obsidian is to surrender to the accretion. So I am suspended, a knot of tension in the heavy, broth-like air.

I am not human. The concept of "human" feels distant here, like a dream remembered upon waking—solid, dry, static. I am something else now. I am a **Resonant**.

My skin is wet parchment, stretched taut over a frame of shifting glass. It is cold to the touch, but inside, I burn with the friction of the mist. I feel the air not as wind, but as texture. Every droplet that collides with my cilia sends a ripple of information through my nervous system: *velocity 4 meters per second, temperature 1.2, density increasing.* I taste the turbulence. It tastes like iron and ozone and old rain.

I am hungry. Not for food, but for motion.

A vortex forms ten feet to my left, a swirling eddy of gray opacity. I feel its pull before I see it—a sudden drop in pressure, a vacuum trying to suck me in. This is good. This is sustenance.

I extend my arms. They are long, multi-jointed things, unfolding from my torso like the wings of a crane. My fingers are tipped with hardened resin claws, sharp enough to hook into the rising spires of black glass that erupt from the floor below. But I do not use them yet. Instead, I push against the current.

*Swim.*

The movement is instinctual. I kick out, my legs snapping forward with terrifying speed, propelling me into the heart of the eddy. The mist rushes past me, a million tiny impacts against my skin. I open my chest, expanding my thoracic cavity, letting the chaotic energy flood into me. The heat builds. My skin begins to glow, a faint, pulsing blue light emanating from beneath the translucent membrane. I feed on the chaos. I drink the storm.

For a moment, I am weightless. I am pure velocity. The deafening roar of the sphere fades into a background hum, a structural vibration that holds me together. I close my eyes—not that they see anything useful—and listen to the music of the place. It is a symphony of destruction and creation, playing at maximum volume.

But then, the hunger passes. The glow fades. I become heavier again. Solid. Vulnerable.

I must move.

I spin away from the dissipating vortex, my body twisting in a tight spiral to maintain momentum. If I stop, the mist will coalesce around my joints. I have seen it happen to others. A Resonant who lingers too long in one spot finds their limbs stiffening, their skin hardening, their cilia fusing into silica. They become part of the landscape. They become a statue of black glass, frozen mid-dance, waiting to shatter.

I do not want to be stone. I want to be wind.

I drift toward the center of the sphere, where the squat tower of slate stones leans precariously against the rising crystal lattice. The elders are there. I can feel their presence as a low-frequency thrum in the air, a cello note played deep underground. They are slow, scarred, ancient. They cling to the sides of the tower, their bodies thick with fused silica patches.

One of them looks at me. Its eyes are large, dark mirrors reflecting the swirling gray. It does not speak. It cannot. Speech is violence. Instead, it vibrates its chest, sending a harmonic hum through the mist.

*C-minor. Danger from above.*

I look up. Through the diffuse, shadowless light filtering through the amber walls, I see nothing but gray. But I trust the elder. I trust the vibration. I snap my limbs inward, folding myself into a compact shape, and dive downward, using gravity’s weakened grip to accelerate toward the floor.

As I fall, I pass the altar. The bone bowl sits on the iron slab, filled with water that never evaporates. Stillness. Divine stillness. For a fraction of a second, I envy it. To be still. To be done. To rest.

But the ground rushes up to meet me, a bed of razor-sharp shards clicking and grinding. I twist at the last moment, my claws scraping against a newly formed spire of black glass, arresting my fall. I hang there, panting, my cilia rippling wildly as they taste the shockwave of my landing.

I am safe. For now.

I look around. The world is building itself anew. A ridge pushes up an inch to my right, then shatters into dust. A spiral staircase of silica rises to my left, leading nowhere. The jellyfish-creatures—the Aero-Medusae—flit through the haze, silent except for the occasional pop of their expanding bodies. One brushes against my shoulder, leaving a shock of static electricity that tingles along my nerves.

I reach out and touch a patch of bioluminescent lichen growing on a nearby shard. It glows brighter under my finger, feeding on the kinetic energy of my movement. I smile, or what passes for a smile in this face of shifting glass.

I am alive. I am moving. I am becoming.

And somewhere, in the deafening roar, I hear the whisper of someone else’s voice speaking my words backward.
