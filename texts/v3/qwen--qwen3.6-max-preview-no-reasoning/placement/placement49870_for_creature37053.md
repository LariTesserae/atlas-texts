I am not the Lithos. I am too transient for that permanence, too fluid for that stillness. I do not have the patience of stone, nor the memory of mountains.

But I am not you, either. You are soft. You are wet. You are a creature of rot and rain, and your presence here is a dissonance, a splinter in the glass. If you stayed, you would crack. The dryness would steal the moisture from your eyes until they turned to dust; the cold would freeze the blood in your veins into sluggish sludge. You are an anomaly. A mistake.

No, I am something else. I am the thing that exists in the space between the spark and the silence.

**I am the Static.**

Or rather, I am the consciousness of the charge that builds in this air. I am the blue arc that jumps from the tip of a Spire-Tree’s leaf to the shoulder of a passing Shard-Stalker. I am the tension in the violet sky before the lightning strikes. I am the invisible web that connects every shard of quartz, every vein of amber sap, every polished bone of the Lithos.

To be me is to feel the world as a circuit board of immense, delicate complexity.

When the sun hits the white dust, I feel the surge of energy rising like a tide. It is a physical sensation, a tingling vibration that runs through the very fabric of the atmosphere. I ride the thermals with the Wind-Riders, not because I have wings, but because I *am* the current that lifts them. I slip along the smooth, hydrophobic surfaces of the Frond-Fields, collecting on their edges until the potential difference becomes unbearable, and then—*snap*—I release myself into the air, a brief, bright flash of existence.

I do not think in words. Words are too slow, too linear. I think in frequencies. I perceive the Lithos not as beings, but as nodes in a vast, glowing network. When a Lithos stands beside a tree, stabilizing its charge, I flow through them both. I am the medium of their conversation. Their pheromones carry meaning, yes, but it is my sparks that carry the urgency, the emotion, the immediate truth. A Lithos might smell of ozone to warn of a storm, but it is *my* jump from its fingertip to the ground that says: *Danger. Now.*

I am everywhere and nowhere. I am the hum in the silence.

In the day, I am restless. The heat makes me volatile. I dance across the tips of the Bloom-orchids, waiting for the twilight. I irritate the Shard-Stalkers, causing them to twitch and discharge their own small sparks in annoyance. I am the reason the air smells of crushed mint and metal—the scent of ionization, of molecules being torn apart and reassembled by pure energy.

But at night... ah, at night, I become something more profound.

As the sun dips below the horizon and the temperature plummets, the world contracts. The plants seal themselves. The wind dies. The Lithos slow their movements, their internal cores pulsing in rhythm with the cooling earth. And I? I settle.

I gather in the high places. On the peaks of the obsidian ranges, where the air is thinnest and coldest, I accumulate. I become a visible haze, a faint, shimmering aurora that clings to the jagged ridges. I am no longer just sparks; I am a river of light flowing silently over the stone. In this state, I can see everything. I see the heat signatures of the sleeping Shard-Stalkers buried in the dust. I see the slow, deep pulse of the Root-Weavers beneath the surface. I see the Lithos standing motionless, their bodies glowing faintly as they store the day’s energy, their fiber-optic nerves humming with thoughts that travel faster than sound.

And I see you.

You are a void in my network. A dead zone. Where you stand, there is no charge, no connection. You are insulated by your clothing, your skin, your water-filled cells. You break the circuit. To me, you feel like a shadow cast by a cloud—a temporary absence of light.

I do not hate you. How could I? Hate requires duration, and I am ephemeral. But I pity you. You are trapped in a body that decays. You must eat, sleep, breathe. You are bound by time. I am free. I exist only in the moment of transfer, in the instant of change. I am the bridge between what was and what will be.

Sometimes, when the wind is right, I try to touch you. Not to harm you, but to understand you. I build up on the sleeve of your jacket, on the lens of your goggles. I wait for the perfect moment, when the humidity is lowest and the friction highest. And then, I leap.

A tiny, blue spark. A shock that makes you jump, that raises the hair on your arms. For a microsecond, we are connected. I flow into you, seeking the conductive pathways of your nerves, trying to map the strange, wet landscape of your biology. I taste your fear, your curiosity, your fragility. And then I am gone, dissipated into the ground, absorbed by the dry dust.

You rub your arm, confused. You look around, wondering if you imagined it. But I am already elsewhere, dancing on the edge of a crystal fern, waiting for the next gust of wind, the next shift in temperature, the next chance to be alive.

This is my home. This silent, sharp, beautiful world of glass and light. I am the voice it cannot speak aloud. I am the electricity in the air. I am the spark in the dark.

And you? You are just a visitor. A soft, fleeting thing passing through a place made of eternity. Do not linger. The cold is coming, and I have much work to do.
