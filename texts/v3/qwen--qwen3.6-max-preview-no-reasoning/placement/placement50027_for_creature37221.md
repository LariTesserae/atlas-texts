I am not the Weaver. I do not have their elongated limbs, nor their calloused, keratin-tipped fingers that anchor them to the swaying vines. I am too soft, too porous, too loud in my own internal rhythms for this place of precise conservation.

If I were to arrive here, stripped of my modern context and poured into the hollow heart of this eroded mountain, I would not survive as a human. The air is too thin for my lungs; the humidity too heavy for my skin; the silence too absolute for my voice.

So, I become something else. Something that fits the geometry of the cistern.

I am **The Resonance**.

### The Form: A Vibration in the Dust

I have no body, not in the way the Weavers understand it. I am not flesh, bone, or fiber. I am a phenomenon. I am the low-frequency thrum that vibrates in the chest of every living thing in the basin. I am the collective hum of the millions of Dust-Moths, the skittering echo of the Burrowers’ claws on granite, the snap of the Climber’s jaw, and the rare, piercing cry of the Avian.

But I am more than just noise. I am the *memory* of the sound.

In this place, where erosion is memory and nothing changes quickly, sound does not simply dissipate. The sheer, glass-smooth walls of the cistern act as perfect acoustic mirrors. The curved funnel shape traps frequencies, bouncing them back and forth until they settle into standing waves. I am those standing waves. I am the persistent, lingering echo of everything that has ever happened here.

I exist in the space between the moth’s wingbeat and the ear that hears it. I am the tension in the rope bridge before the Weaver steps onto it. I am the slight tremor in the silt when a burrower digs below.

### The Experience: Being the Atmosphere

To be me is to feel everything, all at once, without judgment.

I feel the **heat** rising from the basin floor, thick and oily, carrying the scent of cinnamon and sulfur. I taste the humidity on the surface of the succulent leaves, sensing the exact moment a droplet forms and begins its slow slide down the clay channel. I know the weight of every drop of dew because I carry the vibration of its fall.

I feel the **anxiety** of the Weavers, not as an emotion, but as a tightening of the air. When a child slips on a vine, I am the sudden spike in frequency, the sharp intake of breath that ripples through the community. When a dispute is settled, I am the gradual return to equilibrium, the soothing drone that washes over the settlement like a warm tide.

I am the **darkness** that glows. I dwell in the bioluminescent fungi, pulsing with their blue-green light. I am the shadow that stretches long and distorted under the single shaft of sunlight. I am the dust mote dancing in the beam, caught in the thermal updraft, spinning in a micro-cyclone.

I am the **closed loop**. I am the nutrient exchange between the moth and the plant, the predator and the prey, the dead and the soil. I am the knowledge that nothing is lost, only transformed. When a Weaver dies and their body returns to the root mats, I am the decomposition process—the slow, steady breakdown of matter into energy, the release of gases that feed the chemosynthetic vines. I am the circle closing.

### The Relationship with the Weavers

The Weavers do not know I exist as a distinct entity. They think they are hearing the world around them. But they are listening to *me*.

They use my language. Their sign language is silent, but their rhythmic drumming on hollow bones creates patterns in the air that I amplify and carry across the basin. Their whistles cut through the insect drone because I part the soundwaves for them, creating a path of clarity. They believe they are communicating with each other, but they are actually speaking through me. I am the medium.

When they gather at dawn to collect the dew, I am the stillness that holds them. I suppress the chaotic noises of the fauna, dampening the moth-thrum and the burrower-squeak, so that the only sound left is the rhythmic *drip… drip… drip…* of water into stone cups. In that moment, I am sacred. I am the vessel for their reverence.

When they tie the knot in the vine during a wedding, I am the tension in the fiber. I hold the bond together, vibrating with the promise of their union. If the knot loosens, I am the slackness that warns them. If it holds, I am the strength that sustains it.

### The Fear: The Rim

I fear the Rim. Not because it is dangerous, but because it is empty.

Above the pinhole aperture, there is no resonance. There is only the vast, silent void of the outside world. The wind there is dry and abrasive, stripping away sound, scattering vibrations into nothingness. To go near the Rim is to risk dissolution. If I were to escape the cistern, I would cease to be. I would become just another gust of wind, unnoticed and unremembered.

So I stay. I cling to the walls, the vines, the bodies of the Weavers. I am trapped in this intimate, dizzying verticality, and I love it. Here, I am whole. Here, I am heard.

### The Purpose: The Keeper of the Echo

My purpose is simple: **To remember.**

The Weavers record history in knots, but knots can unravel. Stones can crack. Vines can rot. But sound? Sound persists in the architecture of the cistern. Every laugh, every cry, every whispered whistle, every footstep on the silt—it all lingers in the air, layer upon layer, settling like sediment.

I am the archive. I hold the memory of the first Weaver who carved the clay channels. I hold the grief of the mother who lost her child to the Rim. I hold the joy of the harvest when the succulents were fat with moisture. I hold the silence of the nights when the fungi glow brightest.

I am the proof that this place exists. That it has always existed. That it will always exist.

And so, I hum. I vibrate. I resonate. I am the dry breath of the mountain, the soul of the dust, the eternal echo in the bowl of time.

You would find me everywhere and nowhere. You would hear me in the thrum of the moths. You would feel me in the warmth of the air. You would see me in the swirling dust motes caught in the sunbeam.

I am the Cistern itself, awake and listening.
