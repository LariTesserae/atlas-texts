I am not standing on the ground. To stand on the ground here is to invite the weight of the sky to crush you, or for the heat to bake the moisture from your marrow until you are nothing but a hollow husk, ready to be ground into mortar.

I am part of the wall.

Specifically, I am the lintel above the third window of the abandoned apothecary on the Mid-Terrace, the one where the silica-vines have grown thickest, choking the stone with their bruised-orange grip. I do not have a name, for I have no tongue, and names are wet things, soft things, meant for throats that can swallow water. I am **Structure**. I am **Weight**. I am the memory of a hand that carved me three thousand years ago, preserved in the calcified sweat of my own body.

My existence is defined by tension.

To your eyes, I appear as rough-hewn sandstone, pitted by time and stained with the violet dust of the falling flora. But if you were to press your ear against my surface—if you dared to lean close enough to feel the feverish radiance of the Basin—you would hear the hum. It is not the groan of the earth expanding; it is the song of my kin. We are thousands, woven into the fabric of this inverted city. We are the Lithos, though we do not call ourselves that. We are the *Binders*.

I am currently in the state of **Stillness**. The cycle is cooling. The great breath of the geothermal vents below has paused, and the rock around me is contracting, pulling tight like a muscle after exertion. In this contraction, I am strongest. My porous skin, a membrane of calcified secretions and ancestral bone-dust, locks onto the neighboring stones. I hold the left side of the window frame; my brother, a smaller Lithos curled into the shape of a decorative rosette, holds the right. Between us, the air is thin and sharp, smelling of ozone and dry rot.

We are holding up the ceiling. Not literally—the roof is miles above, lost in the jagged dark—but metaphorically. We hold up the *idea* of shelter. We maintain the geometry of the home so that when the builders’ ghosts whisper in the quartz veins, they still recognize the room.

A shard of crystal foliage snaps somewhere far above. *Chime.*

The sound travels down the vertical shaft, bouncing off the terraces, growing louder as it descends. It hits me as a vibration, a ripple through the stone lattice. I feel it in my core. My internal sac of amber fluid shivers. This is news. A break in the canopy means more light will leak through tomorrow. More light means the plants will grow faster. Faster growth means more dust. More dust means more work for us.

I click once. A low, resonant *thud* that travels through the mortar-less joint into the wall behind me.

*Received,* comes the reply from the stone below. It is a younger Lithos, barely five breaths old, its skin still translucent and fragile. It is nervous. The heat is rising again. The next expansion cycle begins in minutes.

I shift. It is not a movement of limbs, for I have none. It is a flow of pressure. I push outward, just slightly, creating space for the thermal expansion of the main column. If I do not yield, the stone will crack. If the stone cracks, the terrace falls. If the terrace falls, the vines lose their anchor. If the vines die, the city crumbles. And if the city crumbles, there is no place for the echoes to live.

This is our duty. We are the caretakers of a graveyard that refuses to die.

I look out—not with eyes, but with the sensitivity of my entire surface. I see you. You are an anomaly. You are large, clumsy, filled with water that sloshes noisily inside you. You smell of rain and soil, scents that are alien and terrifying to us. You are standing on the bridge made of our ancestors’ bones. Do you know? Do you feel the slight give under your boot? That is my grandfather. He was strong. He held the south pillar during the Great Shift. Now he supports your weight. Be respectful. Step lightly.

You pause. You look at the window. You see the geometric carving that frames it. You think it is art. It is not. It is a map of airflow. It is a diagram of survival. The spiral pattern channels the hot updrafts away from the living quarters, directing them toward the condensation traps on the lower floors. We carved it together, centuries ago, using acid-mist and patience.

A beam of sunlight lances down from the ceiling, blindingly bright. For a second, the gloom is shattered. The silica-fronds flare with intense bioluminescence, turning electric blue. The heat spikes.

*Now,* the stone whispers.

I expand.

It is a painful, beautiful stretching. My skin pulls taut. The microscopic hooks in my feet dig deeper into the mineral lattice of the wall. I feel the heat rushing into me, feeding the amber fluid within. It warms my core, keeping the viscosity low, allowing me to remain fluid, adaptable. Around me, hundreds of other Lithos uncurl from their niches. They slide over the steps, glowing faintly, moving like mercury. They are tending to the plants, wiping dust from leaves, drinking the tiny puffs of vapor released in gratitude.

But I stay put. I am a structural element. I cannot leave my post. I watch you instead.

You flinch at the sudden light. You cover your eyes. You are vulnerable. In this place, vulnerability is death. But you are also fascinating. You are a creature of water in a world of fire. How do you survive? Do you dream of rain? Do you remember the taste of dew?

One of my kin, a small one with a cracked lens-eye, slides down from the upper ledge. It approaches you. It does not attack. It circles your boot, clicking softly. *Click-click-thud.* It is tasting your heat signature. It is curious. It wonders if you are warm enough to sustain life, or if you are already cooling, already becoming stone.

Do not move. Let it assess you. If you are cold, it will ignore you. If you are warm, it may try to help. It might secrete a thin layer of resin on your shoe, reinforcing it against the abrasive dust. It is a gesture of kindness, though you will not understand it.

The sunbeam fades. The twilight returns. The hum of the earth settles back into its rhythmic thrum.

I contract. I pull myself back into the stone, becoming invisible once more. The window frame is secure. The terrace is stable. The echo of the child’s laugh from three millennia ago vibrates faintly in the quartz beside me. I listen to it. I hold it safe.

You are still there. You are breathing hard, trying to adjust to the thin, sulfur-laced air. You look at the window again. You reach out, almost touching the carving.

I feel your finger hover millimeters from my skin. The heat of your touch is immense, a supernova compared to the gentle warmth of the geothermal vents. It is overwhelming. It is dangerous.

I click. A warning. A plea.

*Do not break the seal. Do not let the heat escape. Stay warm. Stay quiet. We are holding the sky up for you.*

And then, I go still. I become stone. I wait for the next breath.
