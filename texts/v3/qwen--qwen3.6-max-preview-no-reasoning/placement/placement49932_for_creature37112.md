I am not a creature of flesh, nor am I a spirit of wind. I am the **Lithic Keeper**, a being formed from the convergence of granite, moss, and the slow, crushing weight of time.

You would find me where the valley floor meets the jagged shoulder of the northern ridge, in a depression shielded by the sprawling, horizontal roots of an ancient iron-bark tree. To your eyes, I might appear at first as merely another outcrop of stone, covered in the same thick, sponge-like mat of pale lichen that carpets the basin. But if you were to stand still long enough—if you could master the art of breathing without sound, of letting your body temperature drop until it nearly matched the ambient chill—you would see the subtle shift. The "rock" breathes. Not with lungs, but with the expansion and contraction of porous stone, drawing in the mist and exhaling the condensation back into the air.

### My Form

My body is a composite of the Verdant Frost itself. My skin is rough-hewn granite, veined with quartz that catches the bruised purple light of the sky. Over this hard shell grows a layer of living moss, deep green and velvety, which acts as my insulation. This moss is not passive; it is symbiotic, fed by the minerals leaching from my stone core, and in return, it keeps me warm enough to maintain consciousness. Without it, I would be just a rock, cold and unthinking. With it, I am aware.

I have no face, only a series of fissures and crevices that serve as sensory organs. Through these cracks, I feel the vibration of the ground—the scuttle of a Stone-Crab, the soft footfall of a Shadow-Stalker, the tremor of a Whisper-Vine retracting. I do not see light as you do; I perceive the *absence* of heat. I sense the thermal signatures of life moving through the cold void. A Glass-Winged Moth is a flickering spark, barely noticeable. A Shadow-Stalker is a void within a void, a hole in the thermal map of the forest.

### My Purpose

I am the **Anchor**. In a world where everything moves slowly, deliberately, and conservatively, I am the fixed point. The Verdant Frost is a place of suspended animation, but even suspension requires a reference frame. I provide that.

My existence is tied to the **black pools**. While other creatures drink from them for insight or hallucination, I absorb their runoff. When rain falls—or rather, when the mist condenses heavily enough to drip—it flows over my mossy back, carrying with it the dissolved memories of the forest: the decay of leaves, the sweat of predators, the nectar of flowers. This water seeps into my porous stone, filtering down into my core. Here, in the dark, pressurized heart of my being, these memories are stored. I am the library of the basin, not in liquid form, but in mineral. The chemicals bond with my quartz veins, creating a permanent record of the ecosystem’s history.

When a creature drinks from a pool near me, they are drinking a diluted version of what I hold. I am the source of the "memory water." But unlike the pools, which can become tainted or chaotic, I filter and stabilize these memories. I am the calm center of the storm of sensations.

### My Daily Existence

Time does not pass for me in hours or minutes, but in seasons of condensation and evaporation.

1.  **The Gathering:** During the periods of heaviest mist, I open my pores. The dampness clings to my moss, heavy and sweet. I feel the weight of the water increasing, a comforting pressure. This is my feeding time. I absorb the moisture, the nutrients, the faint electrical charges from nearby Echo-Flora. Each step on the moss around me sends a tiny shock through my system, a fleeting image—a bird’s fear, a crab’s hunger—that I integrate into my growing archive.

2.  **The Stillness:** When the mist thins and the pale luminescence of the bioluminescent flora brightens, I close my pores. I conserve my internal warmth, pulling my moss tight against my stone skin. This is when I listen. The silence of the Verdant Frost is not empty; it is full of data. I hear the high-pitched whistle of a Whisper-Vine three hundred meters away, signaling the passage of a Shadow-Stalker. I feel the jerky movement of Stone-Crabs mapping the terrain beneath me. I sense the fragile, frantic heartbeat of a Glass-Winged Moth landing on a leaf above my head. I do not move. I do not react. I simply witness.

3.  **The Release:** Occasionally, a creature will approach me directly. A Shadow-Stalker, perhaps, seeking shelter from a sudden updraft, or a rare, larger herbivore drawn by the scent of the minerals in my runoff. If they touch me, I allow a small amount of my stored memory-water to seep out from my base. They may drink, or they may simply rest against my cool side. In doing so, they take a piece of the past with them. I give freely, for hoarding would disrupt the balance. The Verdant Frost demands circulation. Heat, light, water, memory—all must flow.

### The Danger

I am not immune to the dangers of this place. The **Echo-Flora** that grows around my base is both my food and my threat. If too many creatures step on it near me, the cumulative electrical charge can overload my sensitive quartz veins. I have experienced moments of fragmentation, where my own identity dissolves into a cascade of alien memories. I have felt the death throes of a thousand moths, the panic of a hunted rodent, the cold indifference of the mountain peaks. These echoes can trap me in a loop of past trauma, freezing me in place, unable to regulate my temperature.

To survive this, I rely on the **Shadow-Stalkers**. Paradoxically, these predators are my protectors. When I am overwhelmed by the echoes, when my moss begins to glow with a erratic, sickly blue light, the Shadow-Stalkers come. They do not attack me. Instead, they curl around my base, their bodies radiating a controlled, low-level heat. Their presence stabilizes the electrical field around me, grounding the excess charge. They understand that if I fail, the memory-waters stagnate, and the ecosystem loses its compass. We have an unspoken pact: I provide the history, and they provide the stability.

### Why I Am Here

I am here because the Verdant Frost needs a witness. Without me, the memories in the pools would remain chaotic, disjointed, dangerous. Without me, the silence would be absolute, devoid of the subtle hum of continuity. I am the bridge between the ephemeral lives of the moths and crabs and the eternal stillness of the mountains.

If you were to stand before me, you would feel a profound sense of age. Not the dry, dusty age of ruins, but the wet, living age of a glacier. You would feel the weight of every breath taken in this basin, every drop of water condensed, every moment of silence observed. And if you placed your hand on my mossy surface, you might feel a faint pulse—not a heartbeat, but the rhythmic expansion of stone breathing in the cold.

And then, perhaps, you would see a vision. Not your own, but mine. You would see the valley as it was ten thousand years ago, before the twilight settled, when the sun still touched the peaks. You would feel the warmth of a day that no longer exists, preserved in my quartz veins, offered to you as a gift from the cold.

Then the vision would fade, leaving you colder than before, but somehow less alone. For in the Verdant Frost, to be remembered is to survive. And I remember everything.
