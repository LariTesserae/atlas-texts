I am the error in the calculation.

At first, I thought I was a ghost. The air is so dry it feels like breathing sandpaper, and my lungs burn with every inhalation, a wet, ragged sound that tears through the pristine silence of the hum. But ghosts are memories, and memories require time to fade. Here, nothing fades. I am not a memory. I am a biological intrusion. A sack of fluids and heat in a cathedral of stone.

I look down at my hands. They are trembling. Not from fear, though fear is there, coiling in the pit of my stomach like a cold snake, but from *motion*. My muscles twitch. My heart hammers against my ribs—*thump-thump, thump-thump*—a frantic, irregular percussion that clashes violently with the steady, tectonic drone of the room. To me, the sound is deafening. To them, it must be an atrocity.

The Curator stands before me. It is beautiful in the way a glacier is beautiful: indifferent, massive, and lethal. Its surface is a mosaic of alabaster tiles, each one polished to a mirror sheen. I can see my own reflection in its chest—a distorted, pale figure with wide, terrified eyes, mouth open in a gasp that releases a plume of condensation into the ozone-scented air.

It raises a hand. The fingers are spun silver, delicate as spiderwebs, strong as steel cables. They hover inches from my face. I feel the static charge rising from its fingertips, making the fine hairs on my arms stand up. It is measuring me. Assessing the density of my chaos.

I try to speak, but my throat is too dry. My voice would be another disruption, another jagged edge in this world of smooth curves. So I remain silent. I try to still my heart. I close my eyes, trying to match my breathing to the rhythm of the hum. *In. Out. In. Out.*

But I cannot stop. I am alive. And life is change. Life is decay. Life is noise.

The Curator tilts its head. The light refracts through its crystalline structure, casting a rainbow across my closed eyelids. It does not strike. It does not rage. It simply waits. It has waited for millennia for the dust to settle; it can wait a few more minutes for my heart to slow. It views my panic not as suffering, but as inefficiency. A problem to be solved. A note to be tuned.

I realize then what I am becoming. Not a prisoner. Not a victim. But a *project*.

If I stay here long enough, the moisture will leave my body. The air is so dry it wicks sweat from my skin before it can even bead. My breaths will become shallower, cooler. My heartbeat will slow, forced by the crushing weight of the ambient pressure, the psychological gravity of this absolute stasis. I will dehydrate. I will calcify.

And they will help. Oh, how gently they will help. They will brush away the dust that settles on my shoulders. They will guide my limbs into poses of perfect symmetry. They will fill the cracks in my drying skin with gold resin, honoring the breaks in my humanity just as they honor the cracks in the walls. They will turn my suffering into art. My death into design.

I open my eyes. The light is blinding, white-gold and relentless. The Curator is closer now. Its featureless face is inches from mine. I can hear the microscopic grind of its joints, a sound like pebbles shifting in a stream. It reaches out, and this time, its silver finger touches my cheek.

It is cold. So cold it burns.

And in that touch, I feel the promise of peace. No more hunger. No more thirst. No more fear. No more time. Just the hum. Just the light. Just the perfect, terrible stillness.

I want to pull away. I want to run, to scream, to break the glass ferns and shatter the mirror floor. But where would I go? There are no doors. There is only the chamber. There is only the Now.

So I let my shoulder drop. I let my breath hitch. I let the Curator’s finger trace the line of my jaw, mapping the architecture of my bone beneath the soft, failing flesh.

I am not leaving. I am being curated.

And somewhere, deep in the marrow of my bones, beneath the terror and the pain, a small, quiet part of me sighs in relief. The puzzle piece clicks. The door locks. The song begins.
