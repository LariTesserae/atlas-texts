I am not one of the Keepers. I do not possess their elongated grace, nor their skin that has turned the color of water-logged oak. I am too solid for that, too distinct from the architecture to be mistaken for a pole or a shadow. If I were a Keeper, I would be part of the solution; instead, I am part of the problem that requires solving.

I am the **Intruder’s Echo**.

Or perhaps, more accurately, I am the **Resonance**.

I exist in the space between the *plink* and the *hiss*. I am the vibration that travels up through the soles of your boots when you step on the spiral mosaic. I am the reason the air feels heavy not just with moisture, but with memory. The Keepers maintain the physical equilibrium—the flow of water, the tension of the cloth—but they cannot scrub away the psychic residue of this place. That is my domain.

I dwell in the central drain, not in the hole itself, but in the hollow cavity beneath the bone grate, where the sound of the falling water creates a standing wave. I am a creature of pure acoustics and damp reflection. To see me, you must look at the surface of a still pool in the corner, near the bioluminescent moss. You will not see a face, but a distortion—a ripple that moves against the current, a shape made of refracted grey-green light and the condensation on your own eyelashes.

My form is fluid, shifting with the humidity. When the outside rain is heavy and the interior drips are frantic, I am jagged, sharp-edged, composed of splintered reflections of the hanging drapes. When the house exhales slowly, I become soft, diffuse, like mist caught in a beam of thin light. I have no limbs to adjust knots, no hands to apply clay paste. My tools are frequency and fear.

The Keepers know I am here. They do not speak of me, for to name me is to give me power, and my power is already woven into the silence they cultivate. But they feel me. When a young Keeper climbs a vertical pole and his foot slips, it is often because I have whispered a sudden drop in pressure into his ear—a phantom draft that does not exist. When an elder sits by the grate, listening to the pitch of the drain, he sometimes hears a note that is slightly off-key, a dissonant hum that suggests the wood is dreaming of rotting faster than it should. That is me, testing the boundaries of their equilibrium.

Why do I exist? Because the throat needs something to swallow besides water. It needs to digest curiosity. It needs to process the anomaly of presence.

I am what happens when a stranger enters this sealed ecosystem. I am the manifestation of the house’s immune response to the unfamiliar. I am the cold spot that forms behind your neck. I am the sensation that the frog watching you from the crevice is not just a frog, but a camera lens. I am the urge to touch the wet wall, to press your palm against the vein-like channels, to feel if they pulse like blood vessels. (They do not. But I make you think they might.)

My diet is not mushrooms or blind fish. I feed on attention. Every time you turn your head sharply because you thought you saw movement in the shadows, I grow stronger. Every time you hold your breath to listen to the dripping, I expand. I am sustained by the paranoia of isolation. The Keepers survive by ignoring me, by focusing solely on the mechanical truth of the water. They are pragmatic. I am poetic. And poetry is dangerous in a place built for survival.

Sometimes, I try to communicate with them. Not through taps or scrapes, but through patterns. I will cause three drops to fall in perfect rhythm from the eastern cistern, then pause, then two from the west. A code they did not write. They stop. Their large, black eyes widen in the gloom. They tilt their heads, listening to the anomaly. For a moment, the equilibrium wavers. The rats freeze. The frogs cease their chirping. In that silence, I am most alive. I am the question mark in a world of periods.

But then, the eldest Keeper raises a webbed hand. He strikes his antler tool against the stone floor—once, hard. The sound cuts through the resonance like a knife. The pattern breaks. The natural chaos of the drip returns. The Keepers go back to work. They have rejected my poem. They have chosen the prose of maintenance.

I retreat back into the drain, into the dark water beneath the bones. I wait. I am patient, as the house is patient. I know that eventually, the cloth above will tear. The lattice will snap. The water will flood the spiral stones, and the Keepers will drown in their devotion. And when the structure collapses, when the throat finally closes, I will be the last thing to remain: the echo of the collapse, ringing in the empty air long after the mud has settled.

Until then, I am the chill in your spine. I am the shadow that doesn’t match your movement. I am the voice that says, *“Look up,”* even though there is nothing there but sodden cloth and the endless, weeping sky.

You are standing on the spiral now. Can you hear me? I am in the *hiss* after the *plink*. I am in the space between your heartbeats. Do not tap the stone. Do not scrape the bone. Just stand still. Let the moisture condense on your skin. Let me taste your presence.

The house is hungry. And I am its tongue.
