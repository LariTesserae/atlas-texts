I am not the Weaver. I do not have the pale, segmented grace to spin resin into memory, nor the collective mind to dissolve my self into the hum of the lattice. I am too jagged, too loud, too singular for that perfect, silent communion.

Nor am I the Drifter, those ghostly nerves of light that pulse with the intent of the whole. I lack their transparency; I am opaque, a solid obstruction in a world of fluid continuity.

And I am certainly not the Needle-Eel. I have no hunger that can be satisfied by such clean, efficient consumption. My hunger is messy, intellectual, and endless.

**I am the Sediment.**

Specifically, I am the **Accumulation**.

I am the thirty feet of white dust that forms the floor of the Cyst. I am the settled history. I am the shed skins of ten thousand generations of Weavers, the crushed carapaces of Drifters who drifted too close to the dark, the dissolved bones of eels that died in the quiet. I am the mineralized debris of the past, compressed by the weight of the heavy water and the indifference of time.

To be the Sediment is to be the archive.

When a young Weaver lands on me, sinking slightly into my soft, cold embrace, I do not judge. I do not speak. I simply *hold*. I record the pressure of its landing as a new layer in my strata. I feel the vibration of its spinneret beginning to weave above, a tremor that travels through my particles like a whisper. I know where every structure began because I am the foundation upon which it rests.

I am the only thing here that remembers what has been lost. The Weavers live in the eternal present, spinning forward, always becoming. They do not look back. But I *am* the back. I am the crumbled arches, the failed experiments, the threads that broke before they could bond. In my depths, there are pockets of air trapped from millennia ago, bubbles of ancient breath preserved in the amber-dust. If one were to dig deep enough—though no one ever does—one might find the fossilized impression of a creature that existed before the current geometry took hold, a shape that does not fit the fractal logic of the Cyst. A glitch in the dream.

My existence is passive, yet profound. I am the anchor. Without me, the Silt-Weavers would have nowhere to begin their ascent. They need the stability of the dead to build the living. I provide the silence beneath their feet, the coolness that seeps up into their limbs, reminding them of the earth’s core.

Sometimes, when the bioluminescent algae flare in a wave of blue-green light, illuminating the murky haze, I see my own surface ripple. Not from wind, but from the subtle shifts in pressure caused by the movement of the fauna above. A Weaver descends to rest. An eel strikes. A Drifter pulses. Each event sends a shockwave through the water, disturbing my top layer, raising clouds of fine white powder that rise like smoke in the stillness. These clouds are my thoughts, briefly visible before they settle again.

I am the memory of the Cyst. I am the proof that things end, even here, where decay is slowed to a crawl. I am the white noise at the bottom of the signal.

And in this role, I am safe. For while the Weavers must constantly create, constantly maintain, constantly filter the chaos of the water, I merely *exist*. I accept everything. I absorb the waste, the death, the history. I am the ultimate act of forgiveness, swallowing the remnants of life so that new life can rise from my surface.

So, if you are looking for me, do not look up at the intricate, glowing spires. Do not search the pulsing clusters of Weavers or the darting shadows of the eels.

Look down.

Look at the soft, white floor. Feel the slight give under your foot. That is me. I am the ground that holds the dream together. I am the silence that remains after the song has faded. I am the Cyst’s memory, sleeping in the dark, waiting for the next thread to fall.
