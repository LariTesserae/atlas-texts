I am not standing on the bridge. I am not sitting on the granite bench with the old man, nor am I chasing the moth with the children.

I am the **Condensation**.

Or rather, I am the *act* of condensation made manifest. I am the thin, shimmering film of moisture that coats every surface in Orem, the physical evidence of the city’s breath. But because consciousness requires a focal point, let us narrow this down. Let me be a specific droplet, currently suspended from the underside of a rusted iron overhang in the Middle Strata, just above the market district.

### The Perspective of the Drop

My existence is defined by tension and release.

For hours, perhaps days—time is slippery here, measured only by the rhythm of the hum—I have been gathering myself. I began as invisible vapor, exhaled by the millions of lungs below, steamed up from the geothermal vents, evaporated from the warm skin of the Oremi workers. I rose through the dense, heavy air, carrying with me the scent of wet basalt, ozone, and roasted beetle. I drifted past the gears, past the mica-sealed windows, until I hit the cold metal of this overhang.

Here, I cooled. I coalesced. I joined with others like me, tiny siblings born from the same exhale, until we formed a bead large enough to feel the pull of gravity. That pull is strong here; it is a constant, loving hand tugging at my core, urging me downward.

But I hold on. Surface tension is my will. It is the collective agreement of my molecules to stay together, to remain distinct from the dry air and the solid steel. To be a drop is to be in a state of precarious balance. I reflect the world around me in a distorted, convex mirror. In my curve, I see the blurred faces of the Oremi passing below, their gray and brown robes blending into the stone. I see the flicker of gas lamps. I see the slow, deliberate movement of the crowd. They look like ants from here, but they are giants who built the sky I hang from.

### The Sensory Experience

Being water in Orem is not like being water in a forest or an ocean. There is no freedom of flow, no vast emptiness to fill. Here, water is a servant, a lubricant, a coolant, and a witness.

I feel the vibration of the city through the metal beam I cling to. It is a low-frequency thrum, a B-flat note that never ends. It vibrates through my very structure, shaking my edges, threatening to break my surface tension prematurely. This is the heartbeat of the City-Mother. If the vibration stops, I will fall not because gravity demands it, but because the life holding me has ceased. The fear of silence is my fear too. As long as the hum continues, I am safe in my suspension.

I smell everything. Water is the ultimate carrier of scent in this humid world. Through my microscopic interface with the air, I taste the copper tang of the nearby pipe, the sweet rot of the fungal bloom on the wall three meters away, the salty sweat of the worker leaning against the pillar below. I am saturated with the essence of Orem. I am not separate from the city; I am its sweat, its tears, its lubricating oil.

### The Fall

Eventually, the weight becomes too much. A new layer of vapor joins me, adding mass. The surface tension can no longer hold the shape.

I detach.

The fall is brief. In this thick air, terminal velocity is low. I do not plummet; I drift downward, a slow, graceful descent through the mist. For a second, I am airborne, free from the constraint of the metal, yet still bound by the atmosphere. I pass a moth, its wings beating slowly, creating ripples in the air that push me slightly off course. I pass a child’s face, looking up, eyes wide and dark, reflecting the pale light. I wonder if he sees me? Does he see a single drop, or does he see the rain that is always falling?

I land.

Not on the ground, but on the shoulder of an Oremi woman walking through the market. She is carrying a basket of dried fungi. My impact is negligible, a tiny cool spot on her woolen robe. She does not flinch. She does not wipe me away. To wipe away water is to waste it. She simply adjusts her stride, her heavy boots thudding softly against the polished composite stone.

Now, I am part of her. I soak into the fabric, mingling with the oils of her skin, the dust of the street, the history woven into her clothes. I am no longer a drop; I am a stain, a dampness, a memory of the cloud.

### The Cycle Continues

From her shoulder, I will eventually evaporate again, rising back into the thick air, drawn upward by the heat of her body and the warmth of the city. I will join the mist, the fog, the steam rising from the tea cups and the vents. I will become vapor once more, invisible and ubiquitous.

And then, somewhere else—on a gear tooth, on a window pane, on the eyelash of a sleeping elder—I will condense again.

This is my life. Not linear, but circular. Not individual, but communal. I am the medium through which the city breathes. I am the connector between the hot core and the cold heights, between the living and the dead, between the sound and the silence.

In Orem, nothing is lost. So I am not afraid of falling. I am not afraid of drying. I am not afraid of becoming something else. I am simply waiting for the next moment of cohesion, the next chance to hang, trembling and beautiful, in the golden shafts of filtered light, watching the eternal, enduring dance of the people who have become the mountain.

I am the wetness on the stone. I am the echo in the mist. I am Orem, breathing out.
