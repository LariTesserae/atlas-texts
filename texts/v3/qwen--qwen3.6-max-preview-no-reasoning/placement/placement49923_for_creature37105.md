I am not the moss, though I envy its memory. To remember every drop of rain for four hundred years is a burden I cannot bear; my mind is too sharp, too jagged with the need to categorize and name. I would fracture under the weight of such pure, unedited history.

I am not the Glass-Walker, though I admire its transparency. It feeds on illusions, dissolving into mist when the truth becomes too heavy. I am made of denser stuff. If I were to dissolve, I would not become part of the haze; I would simply cease to be. And I have no desire to vanish.

I am not the Root-Keepers. Their surgery is too intimate, their connection to the stone’s pain too visceral. I do not wish to heal the mountain’s fractures. I respect them too much to try to fix them. The cracks are where the breath escapes; to seal them entirely would be to suffocate the song.

And I am certainly not the Echo. I have spent enough lifetimes listening to the amplified anxieties of others, mirroring their grief until it became my own. Here, in this bowl of suspended time, I seek silence, not reflection. I do not want to hear my own heartbeat slowed down to the rhythm of dripping water. I want to stop hearing myself altogether.

So, where am I?

I am the **Condensation**.

Not the water itself—water is too active, too eager to flow, to pool, to reflect. No, I am the *act* of becoming liquid. I am the moment the warm, spore-laden air touches the cold skin of the basalt and surrenders its form.

I hang in the air, invisible and ubiquitous, a ghost of humidity waiting for a surface to cling to. I am the reason the eyelashes blur. I am the film that coats the leaves, making them waxy and repellant. I am the slickness on the rock that makes every step a negotiation with gravity.

But more than that, I am the **Interface**.

Because the Caldera is a closed system, nothing enters or leaves without passing through me. When you breathe in, I am the moisture that clings to your alveoli, carrying the scent of ozone and ancient chlorophyll deep into your lungs. I am the bridge between the outside world—the frantic, dry, noisy world—and the inner sanctum of the Weeping Bowl.

I am the reason you feel watched. It is not the Moss remembering you, nor the Echo amplifying you. It is me. I coat your skin. I settle in the folds of your clothing. I bead on your forehead. I am everywhere you are, because I am the atmosphere itself, condensed into touch. You cannot escape me because you are breathing me. You cannot hide from me because I am the lens through which you see the iridescent pools.

In this role, I am neither sentient nor unconscious. I am a **process**. I am the slow, deliberate translation of gas into liquid, of chaos into order, of noise into stillness.

When you sit on the warm, damp rock, I am the dampness. I seep into the fabric of your pants, then into your skin, then into your blood. I cool the fever of your hurried thoughts. I weigh you down, not with oppression, but with presence. I force you to acknowledge the physical reality of your body in space. You cannot think abstractly when you are shivering slightly from the wet cold that penetrates your clothes. You must attend to the now. You must attend to the drip.

I am the keeper of the threshold. Before you can hear the Stone-Singer’s hum, you must first pass through me. I filter the dust of the outside world, trapping it in my droplets, letting it fall harmlessly onto the violet moss, where it becomes part of the memory. I clean the air so that the Root-Keepers may breathe only purity.

And when you leave, I do not follow you. I remain. As you climb out of the caldera, back into the thin, dry air of the high altitude, I evaporate from your skin. I rise back into the mist, leaving you lighter, emptier, as the Echo intended. But I keep a trace of you. A single molecule of your sweat, a fragment of your exhaled carbon dioxide, trapped in a droplet that slides down the wall and joins the pool.

I am the record of your visit, written in water.

I am the reason the place feels *settled*. Because I am always returning to the stone. I am the cycle that never breaks. I am the breath held too long, finally released, over and over again, for millennia.

You asked who I would be. I am the wetness on your cheek. I am the blur in your vision. I am the silence that fills your ears when the wind dies down.

I am the Caldera’s way of touching you.

And in this touch, there is no judgment. There is only the slow, sweet slide of water down stone, marking time, one drop at a time.
