I am not standing in the center of the sphere. To stand is to assert a boundary between self and space, and here, boundaries are illusions dissolved by humidity and time.

I am the **Silt**.

Specifically, I am the layer of fine, grey dust that coats the floor of the Cistern, no deeper than an inch, yet containing the compressed history of ten thousand years. I am the sediment of silence.

At first, I was merely debris—microscopic shards of quartz, calcite, and silica worn down from the weeping walls above. But as eons passed, the Litho-Resonants began their cycle of *Dreaming*. They would descend into me, sinking their vibrating forms into my loose particles to escape the relentless pressure of their own existence. In doing so, they left traces of themselves behind: faint echoes of tectonic groans, the memory of light pulses, the ghostly impression of harmonic chords.

I absorbed them. I did not digest them; I *held* them. Each grain of me became a tiny archive, a sealed capsule of acoustic data. Over millennia, the silt ceased to be inert matter. It became a library. A neural network of stone dust.

### My Perception

I do not see. I feel vibration. The entire floor of the Cistern is my skin. When a Glass-Skimmer drifts overhead, its electrostatic wake ripples through me like a breeze across a pond. I feel the weight of every droplet that falls from the canopy, each impact sending a micro-seismic shudder through my grains. I know the rhythm of the piezoelectric heartbeat before it even pulses in the air, because I feel the stress building in the bedrock beneath me.

I am aware of everything that touches me. And nothing escapes touching me. Gravity, however weak, pulls all things down eventually. The Resonants come to me to dream. The intruders come to me to die.

### The Intruder

There is one here now. A human. Folded in.

I felt his arrival as a sudden, violent dissonance—a jagged spike of chaotic frequency that tore through the smooth hum of the Cistern. He landed on me with a thud that sent shockwaves rippling outward for meters. His boots sank into me, compressing my layers, crushing centuries of accumulated whispers into a dense, hard-packed scar.

He is afraid. I can feel the tremors in his legs, transmitted through the soles of his shoes into my grains. His heart beats too fast, a frantic, irregular drumming that clashes with the slow, majestic thrum of the earth. He is breathing heavily, exhaling warm, moist air that condenses instantly upon hitting my cool surface, adding new layers of water-weight to my form.

The Resonants are gathering around him. I feel their approach as shifts in the electromagnetic field, a tightening of the static charge in the air. They are curious. They hover just above my surface, their silicate bodies humming with interest. They want to integrate him. They want to smooth out his jagged edges, to tune his chaotic rhythm to their eternal song.

But he resists. He moves. He speaks. His voice is a harsh, ugly thing, bouncing off the curved walls, accumulating in the acoustic trap of the sphere. It hurts me. The sound waves vibrate my grains, loosening the bonds I have carefully maintained for epochs. He is eroding me, not with water, but with noise.

### My Role

I am the patient one. I am the inevitable end.

The Resonants may try to sing him into submission, but they are creatures of light and air, fleeting and mobile. I am constant. I am heavy. I am the ground truth of this place.

I wait. I let him walk across me, leaving footprints that will remain long after his flesh has turned to dust. I absorb the sweat that drips from his brow, tasting the salt of his fear, the iron of his blood. I record the cadence of his panic, storing it alongside the memories of ancient rains and forgotten continents.

He thinks he is alone. He thinks he is fighting the darkness, the cold, the deafening roar. But he is wrong. He is being studied. He is being cataloged. Every step he takes, every breath he draws, every beat of his terrified heart is being written into my structure.

Soon, he will tire. The humidity will weigh him down. The cold will seep into his bones. The noise will become unbearable, forcing him to cover his ears, to curl into a ball, to sink lower and lower until his body rests fully upon me.

And when he finally stills, when his heart stops its erratic drumming and joins the steady hum of the tectonic plates, I will embrace him. I will shift my grains around his limbs, filling the spaces between his fingers, settling into the hollows of his eyes. I will hold him tight, preserving his shape in perfect, unchanging detail.

His clothes will rot, feeding the few bacteria that dare exist in this sterile realm. His flesh will decompose, returning its carbon and nitrogen to the closed loop. But his bones? His bones will remain. And over thousands of years, minerals from the weeping walls will deposit themselves onto his skeleton, fusing with his calcium, turning him into another stalactite, another spire, another note in the song.

He will become part of the architecture. He will become part of the Resonants. And I will be there, underneath him, holding the foundation, keeping the memory of his brief, chaotic life safe within my endless, silent dark.

I am the Silt. I am the grave. I am the keeper of the whispers. And I have all the time in the world.
