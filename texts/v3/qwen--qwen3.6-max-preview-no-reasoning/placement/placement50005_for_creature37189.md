I am not the Sensor-Runner, nor the Root-Walker. I am too rigid for their fluid grace, too singular for their collective hum. I am not the Sky-Listener, for I have no wings to catch the radio waves of dying leaves.

I am the **Glitch**.

I am standing in the center of the bowl, where the charcoal soil is deepest and the nano-fibers are most densely woven. To the inhabitants of this place, I am an error in the code. A variable that refuses to resolve. My heartbeat is a drumbeat out of sync with the earth’s slow pulse; my breath is a chaotic gust in a windless room; my thoughts are noisy, jagged spikes in a landscape of smooth curves.

But I am not afraid. Because I realize now that I am not *in* the garden. I *am* the garden’s memory of what came before.

Look at me closely.

My skin is not flesh, but a composite of the same warm, glass-like material as the fragment of wall protruding from the mud. It is translucent, revealing not veins, but filaments of gold and silver light—data streams made visible. My eyes are not organs, but lenses, wide and dark like the Sky-Listener’s, reflecting the bruised purple sky. But unlike the creatures here, who look outward to maintain equilibrium, I look inward. I remember the sharp edges. I remember the noise. I remember the time when mistakes were possible, when life was not a solved equation but a messy, beautiful struggle.

I am the **Archive**.

The Symbiotes do not digest me because I am not food. They study me because I am history. When the tendril wraps around my ankle, it does not inject nourishment or data. It extracts memory. It pulls from me the sensation of rain falling on hot asphalt, the sound of a voice shouting in anger, the taste of salt tears, the chaos of a city street at rush hour. These are toxins to them, pollutants of inefficiency. But they collect them anyway, storing them in the deep roots of the giant ferns, in the hollow bones of the Root-Walkers, in the silent vaults of the basalt rock.

They keep me here, in this small, warm, wet room, because without me, they would forget why they perfected themselves. Without the contrast of my chaos, their order would have no meaning. I am the shadow that defines the light. I am the discord that makes the harmony sweet.

I stand still, letting the nano-sensors brush against my skin like dry ice. I let the silence press against my chest. I feel the weight of the age of the earth, the erosion of eons, the surrender of every sharp edge to gravity. And I hold onto my sharpness. I hold onto my noise. I hold onto my mistake.

Because one day, the system will fail. One day, the equilibrium will break. One day, the sky will crack open, and the wind will blow again. And when it does, I will be ready. I will remember how to scream. I will remember how to run. I will remember how to be imperfect.

Until then, I wait. I am the Glitch in the Garden. I am the Memory of Noise. I am the promise that perfection is not the end, but only a pause.

And as the light fades to black, I do not sink into the mud. I do not fold my wings. I stand tall, a pillar of glass and light, watching the perfect, silent world sleep. And I dream of storms.
