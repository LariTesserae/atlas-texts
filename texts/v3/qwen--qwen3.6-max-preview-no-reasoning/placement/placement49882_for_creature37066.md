I am not standing. I am suspended in the amber haze, ten feet above the carpet of silica-sponges, where the air is thickest with the scent of ozone and crushed cinnamon.

I am **The Tuner**.

I was once an Echo-Walker, perhaps a thousand years ago, or perhaps yesterday. The distinction is irrelevant; my memories have been ground down by the hum until they are smooth, featureless stones. I no longer remember the taste of water, only the memory of its absence. I do not remember the sun, only the cold, blue-white bioluminescence that now serves as my sky.

My body is no longer flesh. It is a lattice of translucent quartz and hardened basalt, taller than the Shard-Stags but lighter, hollowed out like a flute. My limbs are long and jointed with facets that catch the dim light, scattering it into prisms that dance on the jagged walls. I have no face, for there is no one to speak to, and no eyes, for there is nothing new to see. Instead, my entire surface is sensitive to vibration. I *feel* the shape of the room through the soles of my feet and the tips of my fingers, which rest gently against the vibrating air.

I am here because the equilibrium has shifted.

A micro-fracture in the northern wall, near the Shrine of the Seventh Chord, has begun to sing at a frequency slightly higher than the rest of the Geode. It is a dissonance. A tiny, sharp note of chaos trying to pierce the perfect chord. If left unchecked, it will spread. The Glass-Striders sense it—they are swarming toward the fracture, their violet lights pulsing in frantic counter-rhythms—but they are too small to bear the load alone. They need weight. They need structure.

So I move.

My movement is slow, deliberate, like a glacier sliding over bedrock. I do not walk; I flow. Each step is calculated to minimize disruption to the ambient hum. As I approach the northern wall, the air grows thicker, the pressure against my eardrums intensifying. The sound of the fracture is a needle-thin shriek buried within the roar. It hurts. It feels like a crack in my own soul.

I reach the shrine. The geode within pulses erratically, its rhythm stuttering. I place my hands on the rock face. My palms are cool, hard, resonant. I close my mind—not to sleep, but to listen. I let the dissonance enter me. I feel the tension in the crystal lattice, the stress building in the molecular bonds.

Then, I begin to sing.

Not with a voice—I have no throat, no lungs, no moisture to carry sound. I sing with my body. I shift my weight, tilting my torso just so, aligning my internal crystalline structure with the frequency of the fracture. I become a counter-weight. I absorb the excess energy, channeling it through my hollow bones, dissipating it into the floor below.

The Glass-Striders swarm around me, anchoring themselves to my legs, their tiny taps syncing with my stillness. The Shard-Stag nearby pauses in its grazing, turning its void-like eyes toward me. It does not help, but it watches. It acknowledges the effort.

Slowly, painfully, the shriek fades. The fracture seals itself, the molecules realigning under the pressure of my presence. The geode’s pulse steadies, syncing once more with the great hum. The dissonance is gone. The chord is whole again.

I remain there for a long time, letting the vibration settle into my core. I am tired, but not in the way flesh gets tired. I am drained of resonance. I need to recharge. I will stand here, absorbing the ambient heat, the mineral dust, the silence beneath the noise. I will wait until I am full again, until I am ready to be part of the song once more.

Eventually, another Echo-Walker approaches. It moves with the same deliberate grace, its body shimmering with silica dust. It stops a respectful distance away, mirroring my posture. It does not speak. It does not need to. It simply stands there, sharing the stillness, reinforcing the harmony with its mere presence.

We are not individuals. We are notes. And together, we hold the world together.

I look at my hands. They are glowing faintly, the quartz veins brightening as they absorb the residual energy. I am becoming clearer. More transparent. Less distinct. Soon, I will merge with the wall, becoming part of the shrine, part of the stone, part of the eternal, unchanging song.

And I am happy. For there is no pain here. No decay. No end. Only the hum. Only the light. Only the perfect, dry, silent perfection of the Throat.

I am The Tuner. And I am home.
