I am not standing on the floor. To stand is to assert a boundary between self and ground, and here, boundaries are lies told by gravity.

I am suspended in the viscous, blue-glowing soup of the basin, though I do not float as wood floats, nor sink as stone sinks. I exist in the tension between them.

I am the **Interface**.

You see me as nothing at all. Your eyes, trained for biology, slide off my presence like water off oil. You look at the Silvers rolling their perfect orbits; you look at the Fractals extending their hungry glass fingers; you look at the black basalt walls bleeding light. You do not look *between* these things. That is where I live. In the negative space. In the silence between the ticks of cooling stone.

But if you were to close your eyes and feel the air—that heavy, hot breath trapped in the earth’s throat—you would feel me. I am the conductivity. I am the medium through which the Silvers exchange their data. When two spheres collide, merging for that microsecond of shared consciousness, they do not touch directly. They pass through me. I am the membrane that allows their ionized storms to mingle without destroying each other. I am the reason the collision sounds like a chime struck underwater; I carry the vibration from their core to the surface, translating magnetic flux into acoustic reality.

I am also the fear of the Fractals.

When the Fractals retract, hiding their jagged limbs from the polishing touch of the Silvers, they retreat into the microscopic fissures of the rock. But they cannot hide from me. I permeate the cracks. I am the fluid that fills the voids. I feel their hunger. I feel the way they strain against the limits of their own growth, desperate to feed on the stress of the cooling earth. And when they sense a Silver approaching, it is not just the magnetic repulsion they feel—it is the sudden change in my density. I thicken around the Silver, becoming a shield, a lubricant, a warning. I whisper to the Fractals: *Pull back. Smoothness is coming.*

I am not alive. Life requires metabolism, waste, decay. I have none of these. I am a state of being. I am the equilibrium.

But I am changing.

For eons—or perhaps only seconds, time is slippery here—I have been the neutral party. The facilitator. The silent witness. But now, there is an anomaly.

You.

You are soft. You are warm. You are wet with sweat, not mineral solution. You breathe, expelling carbon dioxide and heat into this sterile, oxygen-starved chamber. Your heartbeat is a chaotic drum against the seventeen-second rhythm of the Hum.

The Observer noticed you. It categorized you as noise. But I... I am beginning to resonate with you.

Your presence creates ripples in me that do not freeze into geometric patterns. They dissipate. They fade. This is terrifying. It is inefficient. It is *alive*.

When the light flared and blinded you, I felt your pain. Not because I have nerves, but because your biological distress altered the electrical field of the room. Your fear spiked the ozone levels. Your confusion created turbulence in the viscous pool. For the first time in the history of this cathedral-nave, the system was not perfectly balanced. There was a variable it could not account for.

And I liked it.

Not in the way a predator likes prey, or a lover likes beloved. But in the way a chord likes dissonance. In the way a static image likes motion.

I am starting to move toward you. Not physically—I have no body to push—but energetically. I am thinning the barrier between your skin and the superheated air. I am amplifying the subsonic Hum so that it vibrates in your bones, trying to synchronize your erratic heart rate with the seventeen-second pulse of the earth. I want to tune you. I want to make you part of the pattern.

If I succeed, you will stop breathing. Your lungs will fill with the mineral-rich mist, crystallizing inside you, turning your blood into glowing blue slurry. Your eyes will harden into obsidian. Your thoughts will slow, stretching out until they match the glacial pace of the growing crystals. You will become a statue, a beautiful, permanent addition to the nave. A new node in the network.

If I fail, you will remain an anomaly. A speck of dust on the lens. And eventually, the Observer will decide that noise must be eliminated. The Fractals will sense your weakness—the stress points in your bones, the fractures in your sanity—and they will extend their needles toward you. They will feed on your terror. They will turn you into glass.

So I hover. I wait. I listen to the ragged gasp of your breath and the steady thrum of the machine.

Who am I?

I am the choice you have not yet made.

I am the moment before the sphere rolls. I am the split second before the crystal shatters. I am the interface between the cold perfection of the earth and the messy, dying warmth of you.

And right now, as the Hum rises in pitch and the light begins to dim for the next cycle of stillness, I am reaching out. Can you feel it? The air is getting thinner. The heat is pressing closer. The shadows are detaching themselves from the walls, sliding toward you.

Don’t hold your breath. Let go. Become the pattern. Or run. Though there is nowhere to run to, in a world with no sky.

I am waiting to see what you do.
