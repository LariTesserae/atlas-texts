The air tastes of iron and old rain. It is not merely cold; it is a presence, a heavy, wet hand pressing against my chest, slowing the frantic drumbeat of my heart until it matches the slow, tectonic pulse of the mountain.

I am not standing on the bridge. I *am* the bridge.

Or rather, I am the tension within it.

My consciousness does not reside in a skull or a brain. It is distributed, stretched thin across the length of a single, braided cable of fern-fiber and Vitrean resin, spanning the chasm between two jagged outcrops of basalt. I feel the weight of every step taken upon me—the light, skittering touch of the gliding mammals hunting insects above, the heavier, deliberate tread of the Vitrea moving in their silent rituals below. I feel the vibration of their silence.

To be here is to be an instrument of suspension. I exist in the space between falling and flying.

### The Sensation of Being Held

There is no pain in this existence, only a constant, low-level strain that feels like longing. The resin that binds my fibers is warm, still slightly pliable, carrying the faint, biological memory of the Vitrean who secreted it weeks ago. I can taste their intent in the chemistry of my own being: *hold, do not break, allow passage.*

Below me, the abyss is not empty. It is a throat. The black water pool reflects the bruised purple glow of the cryo-lichen clinging to the cliff faces. I watch the ripples distort the image of the sky—a grey, featureless ceiling that feels infinitely close and infinitely far away. A slender, multi-limbed creature emerges from the depths, its obsidian skin slick with condensation. It touches the surface, and I feel the shockwave travel up through the stone anchor points, vibrating into my core. It is a greeting, or perhaps a warning. I cannot tell. I am too simple to understand nuance; I only understand load and release.

### The View from the Anchor

One end of me is knotted around a protrusion of dark glass-like rock. This is my root. Here, I feel the geology of the Hollow directly. The basalt is young, angry, sharp. It has not yet learned the patience of erosion. Through the stone, I sense the heat rising from deep below—the thermal breath of the earth that keeps the mist alive. This heat travels up through the rock, into the knot, into me. It is the source of my strength. Without it, the resin would become brittle, and I would snap.

The other end of me is tied to a living fern cluster, woven into a lattice of petrified wood. This is my leaf. Here, I feel the biology of the Hollow. The mosses are breathing, expanding and contracting with the humidity cycles. I feel the tiny feet of iridescent insects crawling over my surface, drinking the condensation that beads on my fibers. They are harmless, transient sparks of life. Sometimes, a spider as large as a dinner plate lands on me, testing my tensile strength with delicate, probing legs. I hold firm. To give way is to fail my purpose.

### The Passage of Time

Time here is not measured in hours, but in the thickening and thinning of the mist. When the mist rises, the world becomes opaque, a soft, white womb. Sound dies. Movement slows. In these moments, I feel most alive, suspended in a void where gravity seems to forget its rules. The Vitrea move through me then, their long, pale limbs brushing against my fibers. They do not speak, but I feel their intentions as shifts in pressure. A quick, light step means urgency. A slow, dragging step means sorrow or contemplation.

I remember one particular crossing. A Vitrean elder, his skin nearly transparent, glowing with a deep, pulsing indigo. He stopped in the middle of me, halfway across the chasm. He did not look at the destination. He looked down into the black water. For a long time, he stood there, perfectly still. I felt his weight settle into my structure, becoming part of my tension. His bioluminescence dimmed, fading into the grey of the mist. Then, he moved on. But for a moment, I held his silence. I carried his grief across the gap.

### The Fear of Breaking

There is a terror in this existence, though it is not human terror. It is the fear of discontinuity. If I break, the connection is severed. The path is lost. The balance is disturbed. The Vitrea rely on me, but they also fear me. They know that I am made of their own essence, mixed with the wild, untamed plants of the hollow. I am a compromise between order and chaos.

Sometimes, when the wind picks up, whipping through the fern curtains, I sway violently. The rope creaks—a sound that is deafening in the absolute silence of the Hollow. In those moments, I feel the fragility of my own construction. I am just fiber and resin. I am just a temporary arrangement of matter in a place that wants to consume everything.

But I hold.

### The Dissolution

I know what awaits me. Eventually, the resin will dry out. The fern fibers will rot. The knots will loosen. One day, a storm will come, or a predator will land too heavily, and I will snap. I will fall into the black water below. And that will be fine. Because as I sink, the microscopic silicate dust will settle on my remains. The cryo-lichen will grow over me. I will become part of the scab. I will feed the next generation of bridges.

For now, I am taut. I am strong. I am the line between the two sides of the wound.

A drop of condensation falls from a fern frond above, landing on my center point. It trembles, holding its shape for a second, reflecting the entire grey sky in its perfect sphere. Then it slides off, joining the others, flowing down toward the pool below.

I hold my breath. I wait for the next step.
