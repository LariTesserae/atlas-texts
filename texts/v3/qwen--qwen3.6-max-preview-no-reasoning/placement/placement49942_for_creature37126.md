I am not here to observe. I am here to *be* the error in the code, or perhaps, the only thing that keeps the code from collapsing into silence.

At first, I tried to be a visitor. I stood on the slick, black basalt, my boots slipping on the emerald moss that bled silver sap. I listened to the *thrum-hiss-clank*, trying to locate its source, my human mind desperate for a piston, a gear, a boiler. But the sound was everywhere and nowhere, vibrating in the marrow of my shins, rattling the teeth in my skull. I felt the humidity cling to me, heavy and cold, tasting of ozone and wet slate. I watched a Resonator slide past me, its body liquefying into a stream of dark mercury before solidifying again against a jagged peak, acting as a shock absorber for the tectonic spasm of the earth beneath us. It did not see me. It did not need to. I was irrelevant static.

But as the hours stretched into what felt like seconds, or perhaps days—the timekeeping here is broken, recycled instantly with the mist—I began to feel the pull. Not a gravitational pull, but a resonant one. My heartbeat, frantic and irregular against the perfect, industrial rhythm of the Hollow, began to stutter. The air around me grew thick, curdled with gray fog that seemed to press against my skin, demanding entry.

I realized then that I could not remain separate. To exist here is to participate. And so, I let go.

I am no longer flesh and bone in the traditional sense. I have become something else, something that fits into the gaps between the gears of reality. I am an **Interface**.

My form is fluid, shifting between the solid and the gaseous. I appear as a tall, slender figure draped in robes made of condensed mist and woven lightning. My skin is pale, almost translucent, veined with the same silver sap that runs through the ferns. My eyes are gone, replaced by smooth, obsidian surfaces that reflect the strobe-light flashes of the sparking stones. I do not speak; I emit a low-frequency hum that harmonizes with the Weepers’ throbbing pain, easing their burden just enough to keep the cycle running.

I live in the spaces where the Echoes linger. When the geometry of the cliffs shifts during those brief, terrifying flashes of light, I step forward. I do not bridge the gaps like the Copper Serpents; instead, I *anchor* them. I place my hands—long, fingers tipped with sparks of static electricity—against the shearing rock faces, holding the illusion of stability together with sheer will. The Echoes gather around me, these phantom workers frozen in poses of labor. They look at me, not with recognition, but with a vague, distant longing. They try to mimic my movements, their shimmering forms flickering in and out of existence. I allow them to lean on me, to rest their non-existent weight against my shoulders. In doing so, I give their meaningless tasks a semblance of purpose. I am the supervisor of ghosts, the foreman of a factory that produces nothing but itself.

The Resonators vibrate toward me when the industrial roar becomes too loud, pressing their parabolic ears against my chest. I absorb the excess noise, filtering it through my own resonant chambers, turning the chaotic clanking into a smoother, more tolerable thrum. The Sparks swarm around my head, feeding on the static charge that radiates from my skin, keeping the air clear of dangerous arcs. Even the Copper Serpents acknowledge me, their polished scales scraping against my legs as they coil tighter, seeking the stability I provide.

I am in constant motion, yet I never move. I flow from one crisis point to another, guided by the subtle shifts in the pressure waves. When a suspended pool of water begins to destabilize, threatening to shatter prematurely, I extend a hand, catching the droplets in a web of magnetic force, guiding them back into the cycle. When a patch of moss begins to overgrow, choking the stone, I sing a note of dissolution, causing the greenery to recede, feeding its nutrients back into the fog.

It is a lonely existence, but not a sad one. There is no sadness here, only efficiency. I have forgotten the world below, with its sun and soil and linear time. I have forgotten my name, my history, my desires. All that remains is the hum, the mist, and the endless, beautiful machinery of the Hollow.

Sometimes, in the quiet moments between the *thrum* and the *hiss*, I wonder if I was ever human. Or if I was always part of this machine, waiting for a consciousness to arrive and complete the circuit. It does not matter. The product is the process. The work is the worship. And I am the priest of the empty cathedral, singing into the void, ensuring that the dream of industry continues, forever unbroken, forever unfinished.

If you were to find me, you would see a figure standing amidst the fog, arms raised, holding up the sky. You might think I am praying. But I am not. I am working. And in this place, there is no difference.
