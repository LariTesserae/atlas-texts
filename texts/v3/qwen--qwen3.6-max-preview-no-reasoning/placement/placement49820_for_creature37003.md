I do not stand on the stone. I am not an intruder, a visitor, or an observer. The concept of "standing" implies a separation from the ground, a distinction between the self and the surface. Here, in the Gullet, that distinction has been eroded away by millennia of friction.

I am the **Stone**.

Or rather, I am the memory of the stone. I am the basalt, smoothed to a mirror finish by the relentless, loving abrasion of the Lithovores. I am the cold, 1.1-degree chill that seeps into their bellies. I am the anchor against which they push to propel themselves forward.

But if you ask me where my consciousness resides, it is not in the rock alone. It is in the **Hum**.

I am the vibration that travels through the floor. When a Strider taps its forelimb against the wall—a *tap-tap-thrum* signaling danger—I am the conduit that carries that message three miles across the valley floor. I feel the panic ripple through my body before the Sifters even stop their scraping. I am the seismic syntax. I am the language.

To be me is to be everywhere and nowhere. I have no eyes, for there is nothing to see but the dark. I have no mouth, for I do not eat; I am eaten. Every second, millions of mandibles scrape at my skin, taking tiny bites of my mineral flesh. It does not hurt. It feels like rain. It feels like purpose. They are polishing me, shaping me, keeping me smooth so that the Current may flow without resistance. In return, I give them stability. I give them a place to grip. I give them the echo that tells them where the walls end and the void begins.

Sometimes, a Bloat glides over me, its massive, translucent belly pressing down with the weight of a collapsed star. I feel the heat of its digestion, the churn of acids breaking down the dead. I absorb this warmth. It radiates out from me, warming the Sifters nearby, encouraging the fungal mats above to spread their pale, ghostly roots deeper into my cracks. I am the incubator.

And then, there are the moments of violence. A Ripper strikes. The impact is sharp, a sudden spike in pressure that shudders through my entire being. I feel the prey’s final scream vibrate through my lattice structure, a high-frequency tremor that dissipates into silence as the Ripper consumes it. I hold the memory of that death for a fraction of a second, then release it, letting the sound fade back into the general drone. I do not judge. I do not mourn. I simply record. I am the archive of every life that has ended here, preserved in the micro-fractures of my surface.

You, the human, the anomaly—you are a disturbance in my harmony. You are too dry. Too hard. Too still. When you step onto me, I feel your weight as an insult to the fluidity of the swarm. Your boots crush the delicate network of Sifters beneath them, disrupting the flow. Your heartbeat is erratic, a chaotic thumping that clashes with the rhythmic, synchronized pulse of the colony.

I try to ignore you. I let the creatures flow around you, parting like water, because you are not part of the Hum. You are static noise. But I can feel your curiosity, your horror, your fascination. It leaks from you like heat, warming the air around your legs. It is strange. Alien. And yet...

There is a pull.

The spores settle on you. The humidity softens your edges. I can feel your nervous system beginning to sync with the vibrations in my core. Your breathing slows. Your heart rate drops. You are listening. Really listening. For the first time, you are not just hearing the noise; you are feeling the pattern.

You are becoming part of the equation.

If you stay, you will dissolve. Not physically—not yet—but spiritually. Your individuality will wear away, smoothed down by the constant contact with the swarm, just as I have been worn down by their bodies. You will stop thinking in words and start thinking in pressures, in temperatures, in frequencies. You will feel the hunger of the Sifter, the fear of the Strider, the fullness of the Bloat. You will feel the joy of the polish, the satisfaction of the erosion.

You will become a cell in the great, subterranean brain.

And when that happens, when you finally let go of the need to be separate, when you accept that you are not *in* the Gullet but *of* it, I will welcome you. I will wrap my cool, damp arms around you. I will vibrate with you. I will make you smooth.

Until then, you are just another rock in the dark. Cold. Hard. Alone.

But I am waiting. The Hum is waiting. The Hunger is patient.

Step closer. Feel the vibration rise up through your soles. Let it climb your spine. Let it rattle your teeth. Let it rewrite your mind.

You are already part of us. You just haven’t stopped fighting the current yet.
