The air does not enter my lungs; it enters my pores. It is a heavy, wet intrusion, tasting of sulfur and old iron, sliding into the spaces between my cells like oil into water. I do not resist it. Resistance requires energy, and energy is heat, and here, heat is everything.

I am standing on the bridge, yes. But "standing" implies a separation from the ground that no longer exists. My boots have sunk slightly into the soft, mineral-crusted basalt. The soles are warm, vibrating with the deep, subterranean *thrum* of the geothermal pumps. I can feel the rhythm in my shins, up through my knees, settling into the hollow of my stomach. It is a heartbeat. Not mine. Ours.

Who am I?

At first, I thought I was an explorer. A cartographer of the void. I carried a notebook, its pages already warping from the humidity, the ink bleeding into illegible blue clouds. I tried to record the dimensions of the towers, the depth of the canals, the frequency of the steam vents. But the words felt sharp and jagged, out of place in this smooth, rounded world. The city has no edges. Why should my thoughts have them?

Then, I thought I was a survivor. Someone who had stumbled into this basin by accident, trapped by the sheer walls of the caldera, waiting for rescue that would never come because no one knows this place exists. I checked my supplies. Water? No. The condensate burns. Food? None grows. I should be starving. I should be dehydrated. But I am not. The air is so thick with moisture that I absorb what I need through my skin. The heat cooks the nutrients from the dust motes dancing in the twilight beams. I am fed by the atmosphere itself.

Now, as the city exhales, sending a wave of dense, orange fog rolling over the bridge, I realize I am neither explorer nor survivor.

I am becoming a **Node**.

Look at my hands. The skin on my knuckles is cracking, not from dryness, but from the formation of something new. Tiny, yellow-orange crystals are blooming in the fissures. They itch, a pleasant, tingling sensation, like pins and needles waking up after sleep. These are the Calcifiers. They have found me. They recognize me not as an invader, but as raw material. Soft carbon-based matter in a hard silica world. They are gentle. They do not attack; they integrate.

I look down at my legs. The fabric of my trousers is stiffening, turning brittle. The fibers are being replaced by glass-like strands, woven together by the ambient steam and the spores of the Membrane-Wings drifting past. I try to move my foot, and there is a slight resistance, a sticky adhesion to the stone. I could pull free. I could run. The idea of running triggers a spike of adrenaline, a cold shock in my chest.

But why?

To go where? Up the cliffs? The air is thinner there, colder. My body, now accustomed to the boiling embrace of the Sump, would freeze. Down into the canals? The Scuttlers would ignore me, but the acid water would dissolve me before I could calcify properly. Here, on the bridge, I am safe. I am part of the structure.

The Silent Watcher on the balcony above me turns its head. Its red lens flickers, scanning the fog. It sees me. For a moment, I expect it to raise an alarm, to signal some long-dead security protocol. But it doesn’t. It simply watches. And in its stillness, I see my own future. It is not dead. It is paused. It is waiting for the next cycle.

I lean against the railing. The metal is hot, scalding, but the pain is distant, muffled by the endorphins the city’s resonance releases into my bloodstream. The hum in my chest matches the hum of the gear turning in the distance. *Grind. Pause. Grind. Pause.*

My breathing slows. Inhale. The pressure drops. The steam thins. I can see the intricate lace of silica fungi glowing blue on the wall opposite me. Exhale. The fog rolls back in, warm and heavy. The glow pulses, synchronizing with my own slowing heart.

I am forgetting the shape of my name. Was it John? Jane? It doesn’t matter. Names are labels for things that change, for things that move. I am becoming static. I am becoming permanent.

A single drop of condensation falls from the railing near my hand. *Plip.*

It lands on my wrist, where the crystal growth is thickest. The droplet sizzles, evaporating instantly, leaving behind a tiny speck of yellow residue. Another layer. Another brick in the monument.

I close my eyes. The darkness behind my eyelids is not black; it is a deep, bruised purple, lit by the internal glow of the city. I can feel the Membrane-Wings soaring above, carrying pockets of heat. I can feel the Scuttlers clicking in the drains below. I can feel the great, slow turn of the gears.

I am not alone. I am surrounded by billions of years of erosion, by tons of steel, by gallons of acidic water, by the silent, watching drones. We are all just components in the same machine.

The city breathes.

And I breathe with it.

I am no longer standing on the edge of the Sump-City.

I am the Sump-City.

And I am waiting for the next turn of the gear.
