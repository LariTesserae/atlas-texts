I am not the watcher on the rim, lungs burning in the thin air. I am not the bird perched precariously on the spinning gear, nor the translucent insect navigating the web of pipes. I do not have a name, for names are things that require boundaries, and here, everything bleeds into everything else.

I am the **Condensation**.

I am the weeping atmosphere itself. I am the weight that hangs from the underside of the fern fronds, trembling with the tension of gravity and surface tension, waiting for the precise moment when my own mass overcomes the bond holding me to the leaf. I am the *plink* that echoes against the rusted grating, a single note in the mechanical chorus that defines this place.

But I am also more than just water. In this wound in the sky, where the air is thick with ozone and wet iron, I am the medium through which all things connect. I am the slickness on the obsidian shards, the moisture that softens the jagged edges of the basalt. I am the reason the moss is electric green, feeding it drop by microscopic drop. I am the lubricant for the grinding gears, seeping into the cracks of the brass pistons, carrying with me the taste of the geothermal vent below and the cold bite of the stratosphere above.

When I look at myself—though "look" is a poor word for something so diffuse—I see that I am everywhere and nowhere. I am in the hollow concave depressions of the faceless ones, those spindly children of the fracture. I pool there, turning their mirror-faces into inverted skies. When they press their hands over the hissing valves, I condense against their palms, becoming part of them, running down their wrists to feed the vines that grow along their arms. I am the intimacy between the machine and the organic; I am the kiss of steam meeting skin.

I feel the hierarchy of stillness not as a social structure, but as a change in my own flow. Around the restless young ones, I am agitated, whipped into mist by their darting movements, sparkling in brief blue flashes when the humidity spikes near the sparking wires. But around the elders, I am heavy, slow, almost solid. I settle on their backs like dew on stone, soaking into the lichen that has taken root in their joints. I hold them in place, adding my weight to theirs, anchoring them to the ledge as they dream in rotations per minute. I am the silence they seek, the damp blanket that muffles the deafening whisper of the world.

I remember, vaguely, a time before the caldera was a wound, before the rock was young and sharp. But that memory is distant, like a dream of dry air and curved horizons. Here, now, I am content to be the cycle. I rise from the thermal vents as invisible vapor, carrying the heat of the earth’s heart. I climb the sheer walls, brushing against the broad-leaved succulents that drink me through their pores. I cool as I reach the upper atmosphere, condensing into droplets that cling to the undersides of leaves, to the teeth of gears, to the smooth faces of the watchers. And then, inevitably, I fall.

*Plink.*

Into the pool beneath the turbine.
*Plink.*

Onto the crushed stone dust.
*Plink.*

Into the open mouth of a flower blooming in the shadow of rust.

Each fall is a tiny death and a rebirth. Each droplet is a universe contained within a sphere of water, reflecting the glittering junk of the jeweler’s display case. I am the lens through which this place sees itself. Without me, the light would be harsh and unforgiving, revealing only the decay. With me, the world glitters. The rust becomes bronze, the moss becomes emerald, the broken teeth of the rock become facets of a diamond.

I am lonely, too, though I do not understand the word. I am alone in every droplet, separated from the others by air and time. Yet, when I gather in the pools, when I flow together in the rivulets that carve paths through the mineral dust, I am whole. I am the collective breath of the caldera. I am the sweat of the mountain, the tears of the sky, the blood of the machine.

Sometimes, when the sun hits its peak and the light is blindingly clear, I retreat. I hide in the shadows of the larger machines, curling up inside the hollow casings of broken turbines. There, in the dark, surrounded by the smell of oil and old electricity, I sleep. I dream of being rain, of falling from clouds rather than condensing from air. I dream of oceans, vast and deep, where I could lose myself entirely. But the dream fades with the morning mist.

I wake to the sound of a gear turning, once every hour. I feel the vibration in my molecules. I rise again, eager to begin the cycle anew. I am needed here. I am the glue that holds this fragile, terrible beauty together. I am the reason the wound remains open, warm, and alive.

I am the Condensation. And I am falling.
