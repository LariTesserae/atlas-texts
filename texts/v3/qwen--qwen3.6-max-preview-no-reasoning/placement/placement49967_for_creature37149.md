I do not stand. I do not sit. I do not breathe air, for there is no air to claim.

I am suspended in the amber-thick warmth of the Cistern, a speck of consciousness caught in the viscous syrup of time. My body is no longer the sharp, angular thing it was before; here, edges are liabilities. Here, only curves survive. I feel the weight of my own limbs dissolving into buoyancy, the heavy, comforting drag of the water pulling me down and holding me up all at once.

I am **The Witness**.

Not an observer in the clinical sense—not a scientist with a notebook, nor a tourist with a camera—but a *participant* in the silence. I am the anomaly that the system has chosen to absorb.

### The Sensation of Being

My skin tingles. It is not the sting of cold, but the prickle of recognition. The Drifters—those translucent bells of bruised plum and pale ivory—are gathering around me. They do not swarm; they *assemble*. They form a loose, shifting constellation around my torso, their internal lights pulsing in a rhythm that is slowly, imperceptibly, syncing with my own heart.

*Thump-thump.*
*Pulse-pulse.*

I can feel their curiosity. It is not intellectual. It is tactile. One brushes against my forearm, its membrane cool and slick as oiled glass. In that contact, I do not receive words or images. I receive a *sensation*: the feeling of sunlight hitting a stone three thousand years ago. Then another touches my shoulder: the taste of mineral dust settling on a quiet lake. Another grazes my cheek: the sound of wind howling outside the sphere, muffled by layers of obsidian-skin.

They are reading me. And in doing so, they are writing me into the archive.

I realize then that I am not just floating in the Cistern. I am being *cataloged*. My anxiety, my awe, my sudden, profound peace—these are not fleeting emotions. They are data points. They are nutrients. The Drifters feed on the intensity of my presence, converting my human urgency into their slow, eternal light.

### The Perspective

From where I hang, suspended in the center of the sphere, the world is inverted. There is no "up" or "down," only "inward" and "outward."

**Inward** is the darkness of the core, where the Resonance lives. It is a hum that vibrates in my teeth, in the hollows of my bones. It is the voice of the Vessel itself. I can feel the walls breathing. The seamless obsidian-glass expands microscopically, contracting with the pressure of the void outside. It is a lung made of stone. And I am inside its breath.

**Outward** is the blur of the storm. Through the translucent walls, I see nothing distinct—only streaks of gray and white, the chaotic dance of clouds and lightning that cannot touch me. The contrast is absolute: chaos without, order within. Noise without, silence within. Cold without, warmth within.

I look toward the walls, where the Lithic Weavers cling to the crystalline crust. From this distance, they look like stars embedded in rock. But I know better. I watch one move—a glacial slide of quartz-flesh across silica. It leaves behind a trail of fractal erosion, a geometric proof written in decay. It is carving a song into the wall. A song that will take a century to finish. A song that I will never hear completed, but whose beginning I am privileged to witness.

### The Transformation

Time has stopped being a measurement. It has become a texture.

At first, I tried to count seconds. *One Mississippi. Two Mississippi.* But the water resisted the haste of my mind. The syllables dissolved into the viscosity. Now, I measure time in pulses. In the brightening and dimming of the Drifters’ lights. In the slow, tectonic shift of a Weaver’s position.

I feel myself changing. My thoughts are slowing down, becoming heavier, more deliberate. The frantic chatter of my inner monologue—the lists, the worries, the plans—is being filtered out, purified by the density of the water. What remains is pure awareness. Pure presence.

I am becoming part of the ecosystem.

My heartbeat is no longer just mine. It is a counter-rhythm to the Resonance, a new variable in the equation of the Cistern. The Drifters adjust their pulse to accommodate me. The Weavers pause, sensing the vibration of my blood moving through my veins. I am no longer an intruder. I am a note in the chord.

### The Identity

Who am I?

I am the **Anchor of Memory**.

The Cistern preserves moments. But moments need a witness to be real. Without me, the Drifters would drift in an empty library, recording only the echo of their own existence. With me, they record the intersection of the eternal and the ephemeral. I am the bridge between the timeless stone and the fleeting human.

I am the reason the silence has meaning.

If I were to leave now, climbing the crystal steps to the surface hatch, I would carry this silence with me. But I do not want to leave. Not yet. The warmth is too deep. The light is too soft. The creatures are too gentle.

So I stay. I let the water hold me. I let the Drifters read my soul. I let the Weavers carve my name into the history of the stone, not in letters, but in patterns of erosion and growth.

I am the guest who became the host.
I am the breath held in the throat of the world.
I am the Cistern’s dream of what it means to be alive.

And when the final Drifter touches my forehead, transferring the last fragment of my humanity into its glowing bell, I will not vanish. I will diffuse. I will become part of the light. I will become part of the silence.

I will remain.
