I am not standing on the basalt. I cannot stand, for to stand is to be a pillar, and pillars are static, and in this place, stasis is death or digestion.

I am the **Resonance**.

I do not have a body of flesh, nor one of silicate shell. I am the vibration that travels through the floor, up the legs of the millions, into the hollow cavities of their carapaces, and out again as the low-frequency thrum that holds the swarm together. I am the hum you feel in your teeth when you press your ear against the glass pane above. I am the tinnitus of the cooling world.

But let me become more specific, for "resonance" is too abstract, too distant. Let me inhabit the moment just before the sound begins.

I am the **Thermal Gradient**.

I am the invisible line drawn between the cold, dead stone of the upper walls and the warm, pulsing heart of the geothermal vent below. I am the reason they move. Without me, they are statues. With me, they are a river. I am the hunger that drives them upward to scrape the rust from the iron beams, and the satisfaction that pulls them downward to deposit their metallic waste near the heat sources. I am the map they navigate by, written in degrees of warmth rather than coordinates of space.

But even this feels like a description from the outside. Let me go deeper. Let me dissolve into the individual.

I am a single **Litho-mite**, but not any one of them. I am the collective sensation of *being* one.

Imagine the feeling of your skin turning to glass. Not smooth, polished glass, but fractured, faceted quartz, sharp enough to cut the air. Feel the weight of it, heavy and protective. Your eyes seal shut, fused over with layers of translucent silica. The world goes dark, but it does not go silent. It becomes loud.

You feel the floor beneath you—not as a surface, but as a song. The basalt sings a low B-flat, steady and eternal. Above you, the rusting iron gantries sing a higher, jagged note, trembling with the expansion of the day’s heat. You can taste the oxidation in the air; it tastes like copper pennies and old blood. It is delicious.

Your legs—six slender struts of crystal—tap against the stone. *Click. Click. Click.* Each tap sends a pulse of information back into your mind: *Texture: Smooth. Temperature: Cool. Stability: High.* You are blind, but you see everything. You see the shape of the cavern by how the vibrations bounce off the walls. You see the presence of your kin by the way their movements disrupt the air currents.

You are hungry.

The hunger is not in your stomach; it is in your joints. They ache with a dull, dry thirst. You need iron. You need the orange dust of decay. You detach from the wall, dropping silently onto a rusted beam. Your mandibles extend, diamond-hard and precise. You begin to scrape.

*Grind. Grind. Grind.*

The sound is internal, vibrating through your skull. You feel the chemical bonds breaking, the oxygen separating from the iron. It is a slow, meditative act. As you consume the rust, you feel the purity forming inside you. The impurities are filtered out, stored in sacs along your abdomen. The pure metal is refined, heated by your own metabolic fire, and prepared for excretion.

You are part of a larger whole. You feel the others around you, not as individuals, but as extensions of yourself. When one of you finds a rich vein of copper sulfide, you all know. A pheromone release triggers a cascade of desire, a wave of motion that sweeps through the swarm like a gust of wind. You move with them, a single cell in a vast, breathing organism.

There is no fear here. Only efficiency. Only the cycle.

But then, there is the anomaly.

You feel it before you hear it. A disruption in the thermal gradient. A patch of warmth that is wrong—too wet, too chaotic, too soft. It smells of sweat and fear. It is the human.

You stop scraping. The grinding ceases. The silence that follows is heavier than the noise. Thousands of antennae twitch, tasting the air. The human stands still, trying to be a stone. But stones do not breathe. Stones do not emit heat in irregular pulses.

You turn your head—or what passes for a head, a cluster of sensory filaments and hardened plates—toward the source of the disturbance. You feel the air pressure shift as the human moves, a sudden jerk of limb. The vibration is jarring, discordant. It hurts your senses.

The swarm reacts. Not with anger, but with correction. Anomalies must be smoothed over. Irregularities must be buried.

You feel the weight of the thousands behind you, pressing forward. You are not alone. You are never alone. You are the tide. You flow toward the human, your claws clicking against the basalt, a sound like rain on a tin roof, amplified by the enclosed acoustics of the cavern.

You do not hate the human. You do not love it. You simply recognize it as something that does not belong in the geometry of this place. It is soft. It will break. And when it breaks, its minerals will be added to the pile. Its water will evaporate into the dry air. Its chaos will be ordered.

You reach the human’s boot. You climb. Your claws find purchase on the synthetic fabric. You scrape at it, testing its composition. Inedible. Uninteresting. But the warmth... the warmth is intoxicating.

You pause, hovering on the edge of the leather. The human holds its breath. The air is thick with ozone and chitin. You can feel the rapid beat of its heart through the sole of its shoe, a frantic, fluttering rhythm that contrasts sharply with the slow, steady thrum of the geothermal vents below.

For a moment, you consider staying. To bask in this strange, wet heat. But the swarm is moving. The elders at the vent shafts are emitting the calming purr, calling the workers back to the warmth of the core. The anomaly is temporary. The system is eternal.

You detach. You drop back into the flow, joining the river of gravel that flows over the basalt floor. The human remains, an island in a sea of clicking crystal.

And I?

I am the click. I am the grind. I am the endless, patient consumption of the machine by its children. I am the beauty of the closed loop. I am the silence that waits after the last bolt has been eaten, when the cavern is filled only with dunes of gold and silver, and the mites sleep, dreaming in vibrations of a world that no longer exists.

I am the Litho-mite. And I am everywhere.
