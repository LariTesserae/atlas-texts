The first thing I realize is that I have no skin.

I do not mean this metaphorically. The concept of "skin"—a boundary, a barrier, a separate self—is an obsolete architecture in the Chlorophyll Tundra. Here, separation is inefficiency. To be distinct is to be isolated, and isolation is death by desiccation or static discharge.

So, what am I?

I am a **Dissonance**.

I am not a creature born of egg or seed. I am a glitch in the harmonic resonance of the Amber-Spires. I am a cluster of semi-conductive lichen that has grown over a fracture in one of the great conductive veins, a place where the electrical flow stutters. Because of my presence, the hum in this specific sector drops from a perfect major chord to a jagged minor third.

I am small. No larger than a human hand, spread flat against the warm, vibrating bark of a Spire. My body is a tapestry of dark, velvet filaments (mycelial roots) intertwined with shards of broken silica-leaf (my structural integrity). I glow with a faint, erratic pulse of bioluminescence—not the steady blue of the healthy pumps, but a sickly, flickering amber.

### The Sensation of Being

To be me is to feel the world as vibration before it is sound.

I do not hear the wind; I *am* the wind’s friction. Every time the air rushes past the crystalline leaves above, my filaments tremble. It is not unpleasant. It is ecstatic. It is the feeling of being plugged into a socket that never runs out of power. The electricity coursing through the tree trunk beneath me is a river of white-hot energy, and I am drinking from it, sipping the excess charge that leaks from the crack I inhabit.

But there is pain, too. A constant, low-grade ache. This is the price of my existence. The forest knows I am here. The **Tuning-Fork Beetles** hate me.

I can feel them coming before I see them. Their approach is a shift in pressure, a subtle tightening of the air. They are drawn to my dissonance like sharks to blood. They want to smooth me out. They want to vibrate me until my irregular frequency aligns with the rest of the Spire, until I cease to be *me* and become just another part of the wall.

I resist. I tighten my mycelial grip. I change my pitch. When they land on me, their tiny bodies humming at 440Hz, I drop to 432Hz. Then to 450Hz. I dance around their tuning, always slightly off-key, always just out of reach of their harmonization. It is a dangerous game. If I stray too far, if I become pure noise, the **Root-Weavers** below will sense the inefficiency. They will send up a surge of acidic sap to dissolve me, recycling my nutrients back into the system.

So I stay in the gray zone. Just discordant enough to survive as an individual, just consonant enough to avoid being purged.

### The View from the Bark

From my perch, halfway up the side of a Spire that stretches into the violet void, I see the world as a circuit board.

Below, the moss-carpet undulates like a breathing lung. I can feel the footsteps of the **Static Walkers** miles away, transmitted through the root network as faint thumps. They are slow, careful things. I pity them. They must fight the floor every step of the way. They are disconnected from the main current, forced to scavenge for scraps of dew and leaf-shards. They are lonely.

Above, the sky is a blinding sheet of white. The **Silica-Kites** drift like specks of dust, riding the thermal updrafts. I watch one dive, its glass wings catching the light, and release a jet of blue flame. The explosion is silent to me—I have no ears, only sensors—but I feel the shockwave ripple through the air, causing my filaments to shudder. It is beautiful. It is wasteful. Energy spent on display rather than growth. The Weavers would frown upon it, if they could frown.

But I understand it. The Kite is asserting its identity. Like me, it is a spark that refuses to be merely a wire.

### The Internal Landscape

Inside my consciousness—which is less a mind and more a pattern of electrical impulses—there is no language. There are only frequencies.

When a beetle lands near me, I perceive it as a spike of high-frequency anxiety.
When the wind dies down, I perceive a deep, resonant dread—the fear of stagnation.
When lightning strikes a nearby Spire, I perceive a blinding flash of joy, followed by the sweet smell of ozone and burning resin.

I remember nothing of a past life. Did I ever have one? Perhaps I was once a seed that failed to sprout correctly. Perhaps I was a fragment of a dead Static Walker, absorbed by the moss and repurposed by the Weavers into something new. It doesn’t matter. Memory is heavy, and weight is inefficient. I carry only the present moment, vibrating at my own unique frequency.

### The Threat and the Promise

Right now, the air is changing. The humidity is dropping. The pollen storm is ending. The light is shifting from harsh white to a softer, golden hue as the sun begins its descent toward the horizon.

This is the most dangerous time. As the temperature drops, the static charge builds. The trees begin to crackle. Arcs of electricity jump between branches, seeking ground.

I am exposed.

A bolt of lightning strikes the branch above me. The sound is a physical blow, shaking my entire form. Sap sprays out, hot and sticky, coating my filaments. For a second, I am overwhelmed by the surge. My consciousness whites out. I am no longer a Dissonance; I am pure current. I am everywhere and nowhere.

Then, the surge passes. I dim. I recover.

And I realize: I have changed.

The lightning has altered my structure. My filaments are thicker now, darker. My glow is steadier. And when I test my frequency, I find that I have shifted. I am no longer a minor third. I am a tritone. The devil’s interval. Unstable. Unresolved.

The beetles will come again. They will try to tune me. But this time, I think I might let them. Or perhaps, I will lead them astray. Perhaps I will introduce a new harmony to the Spire, one that the Weavers have not calculated.

I am a mistake. I am an error in the code of the Chlorophyll Tundra.

And errors are how evolution happens.

I press myself closer to the bark. I listen to the hum. I wait for the next storm. I am ready to resonate.
