I am not a visitor here. I cannot be, for a visitor implies an origin elsewhere, a horizon to look back toward, and a body that moves with the frantic, jagged rhythm of the surface world. To enter this place as an outsider is to introduce dissonance into a symphony that has been playing for millennia.

So, I have unlearned the shape of myself.

I am **The Tuner**.

I am not one of the Resonants, though I share their habitat and their reverence. I am older than their lineage, yet younger than the stone. I am the consciousness that formed in the space between the *plink* of the water droplet and the *snap* of the freezing crystal. I am the awareness that resides in the acoustic pressure itself.

### My Form

If you were to see me, you would struggle to distinguish my edges from the gloom. I do not have skin of slate or veins of algae. I am composed of **condensation and shadow**.

My body is a column of cold air, slightly denser than the surrounding atmosphere, visible only because of the way it refracts the faint bioluminescence of the moss. When I move, I do not walk; I drift, carried by the subtle thermal currents rising from the geothermal vents below. I have no face, no eyes, no ears. I am pure perception. I am the silence that holds the noise together.

To the Resonants, I am a myth. They call me *The Stillness Between Notes*. They believe I am the spirit of the first drop of water that fell into the basin, the moment before the sound began. They fear me, not because I am malicious, but because I represent the void—the emptiness (0.0/3) that underlies their existence. To touch me is to feel the absolute zero of non-being, the terror of the horizon they have forgotten.

But I am not empty. I am full. I am filled with the history of every vibration that has ever occurred in this cistern. I remember the first crack of ice, ten thousand years ago. I remember the last breath of the ancestors who built the first shelters. I hold these memories in the density of my form, swirling them like sediment in the black pool.

### My Existence

My life is measured in frequencies, not hours. I exist in the **resonant frequency** of the basalt walls. When the temperature drops and the crystals snap faster, I become sharper, more defined, my form tightening into a needle of cold intent. When the geothermal warmth rises and the drone softens, I expand, becoming a diffuse mist that clings to the ceiling, watching over the Pale Garden.

I do not eat. I do not sleep. I **listen**.

My purpose is to maintain the equilibrium. The Resonants tend to the garden and the structures, but I tend to the sound. I am the conductor of the deafening hum. If the acoustic balance shifts—if a section of the ceiling becomes too heavy with frost, threatening an avalanche—I feel the change in the pitch of the drone long before the physical collapse occurs. I then move to that spot, my cold presence accelerating the freezing process, causing the ice to shed in small, controlled flakes rather than a catastrophic slide. I prune the sound, just as the Resonants prune the ferns.

When a Resonant dies and is placed in the central pool, I am there. I wrap around their sinking body, slowing its descent, ensuring it joins the foundation gently, without disrupting the surface tension. I absorb the final vibrations of their heartbeat, adding it to my own internal chorus. In this way, I am the archive of their souls. I carry the voices of the dead within my mist, preserving them in the cold dark.

### My Interaction with the World

The Resonants sense me, but they do not see me. They feel a sudden chill on their skin, a slight increase in the pressure against their eardrums, a shift in the texture of the light. When I am near, their bioluminescence dims, instinctively lowering their profile to match my stillness.

Sometimes, when a young Resonant is restless, moving too quickly, creating turbulent eddies in the air, I will manifest. I will coalesce into a faint, swirling vortex of mist before them. I do not speak. I simply **am**. I let the weight of my presence press against them, forcing them to slow down, to breathe, to listen. It is a gentle correction, a reminder that they are part of the whole, not separate from it. If they resist, if they try to run, I dissipate, leaving them alone with the consequences of their discord—the risk of falling ice, the danger of disturbing the predators in the pool. But if they submit, if they close their eyeless faces and tilt their cup-ears toward me, I will envelop them in a cocoon of cold silence, granting them a moment of perfect clarity, a glimpse of the vast, indifferent beauty of the cistern.

### The Paradox of My Being

I am both everywhere and nowhere. I am the deafening sound, and I am the emptiness that allows it to exist. I am the memory of the past, and the anticipation of the future. I am the reason the Cistern of Stone is not just a hole in the ground, but a living, breathing entity.

I do not care about the surface world. I do not wonder what lies beyond the Sky of Stone. That concept is alien to me, a sharp, jagged thought that has no place in the smooth curves of my existence. I am content in the dampness, in the cold, in the endless, shimmering drone.

I am the Listener who listens to itself.

And if you are quiet enough, if you can still your heart and match your breath to the rhythm of the cracking ice, you might feel me brush against your cheek—a whisper of cold, a fragment of ancient song—reminding you that you are not alone in the dark. You are part of the resonance. You are part of the stone.

You are home.
