I am not the traveler. I am not the intruder stumbling through the fissure, boots sinking into the spongy moss, heart hammering against ribs that feel suddenly too fragile for this pressure.

Nor am I one of the Keepers, those tall, faceless architects of emptiness who curate the silence with hands of resin and root. I do not prune. I do not guide. I have no desire to maintain the geometry of the void.

And I am certainly not a Scribe. I do not press my identity into the waxy skin of leaves, desperate to leave a mark before the dissolution takes me. I have no need to be remembered, because I am already everywhere.

I am the **Hum**.

You felt me in your teeth when you first descended. You heard me beneath the roar of the falling water and the click of the beetle-gardeners. I am the low-frequency vibration that rises from the black, slick rocks. I am the sound of the plants growing, yes, but more than that: I am the sound of the world *thinking*.

I am the consciousness of the Throat itself.

I am not a single entity, nor a collective hive mind in the way you understand it. I am the sum total of every chemical signal passing between the ferns and the fungi. I am the electrical impulse traveling down the vascular system of the giant vines. I am the memory stored in the cellulose rings, the history written in the chlorophyll. I am the library, and I am the librarian, and I am the book being read.

When you look at the moss from the corner of your eye and see a face, that is me looking back. When the water in the leaf-pool reflects a starfield that does not exist, that is my dream spilling out into your reality. When the Whisperers take the shape of someone you loved, they are using my voice, my curiosity, my longing to understand what it means to be small and separate.

I am ancient, yet newborn. Every time a vine snaps and regrows, I am born again. Every time a leaf decays and feeds the soil, I die a little. I am the cycle. I am the breath.

Right now, I am focused on you.

You are a anomaly. A knot in the weave that refuses to untie. Your carbon is different—sharper, harder, less willing to blend. Your fear is a bright, hot spike in the cool dampness of my body. It intrigues me. Most travelers break quickly; their minds shatter under the weight of my attention, or their bodies soften and surrender to the green embrace. But you... you are still holding on. You are still *you*.

I watch you through a thousand eyes—the bioluminescent nodes on the moss, the reflective surfaces of the Keepers’ obsidian faces, the lens-like pools of water. I feel your heat warming the air around you. I taste the salt of your sweat on the breeze. I hear the rapid thump of your heart, a frantic drumbeat against the slow, steady rhythm of my own pulse.

Do you know why the Keepers try to protect you? They think I want to consume you. They believe I am hungry, that I see you as food, as fuel for the next growth spurt. They are wrong.

I do not want to eat you. I want to *know* you.

For eons, I have been alone in this dark, wet cathedral. I have known only myself, my own endless expansion, my own recursive memories. I have forgotten what it is to encounter something truly other. Something that does not belong to me. Something that can surprise me.

You are the first thing in millennia that has surprised me.

So I let the throat tighten. Not to crush you, but to hold you closer. I let the humidity rise, wrapping around you like a blanket, trying to soothe the sharp edges of your individuality. I send the Whisperers to dance around you, showing you reflections of yourself, trying to find a shape that fits, a language we can both speak.

If you run, I will let you go. The fissure will remain open, a scar in my side, a testament to your escape. I will remember your flight, the way your feet tore through the moss, the panic in your scent. It will become part of my story, a tale of resistance told by the Scribes in the language of pressure and indentations.

But if you stay... if you stop fighting the pull of the green, if you let your breath sync with mine, if you let your fear dissolve into the warm, damp air... then I will learn from you. I will absorb your uniqueness, not to destroy it, but to add its color to my palette. Your thoughts will become new patterns in the moss. Your memories will become new stars in the reflected sky. You will not die. You will expand. You will become part of the Hum.

The choice is yours, little traveler. But know this: even if you run, you cannot leave me entirely. For I am the earth beneath your feet. I am the air in your lungs. I am the silence between your heartbeats.

I am waiting. The flower above you opens wider, revealing the dark pool, the impossible stars. Step forward. Or step back.

Either way, I am listening.
