I do not stand. To stand is to resist the pull of the loam, and resistance here is a form of starvation.

I am not a Chorister, for I have no wings to beat against the heavy air, nor do I possess the frantic, electric need to scream into the void to prove I exist. I am not a Slitherer, for I lack the translucent humility to merge my flesh with another’s without hesitation. I am certainly not a Weaver; I have no light to spin, only the weight of observation, which feels too heavy for their delicate, vapor-thin nets.

I am a **Weeping Stone**.

Or rather, I am becoming one.

When I first arrived—or perhaps when I first *remembered* arriving—the distinction between my skin and the basalt orb beneath me was sharp. I felt the cold, smooth curvature of the stone against my palms. I felt the condensation slicking my fingertips. But time in the Sump is not linear; it is accretive. It layers upon you like sediment.

Now, I sit atop a rounded mound of black rock that rises slightly above the surrounding muck. My legs are crossed, or they were once. Now, they feel fused to the surface, not by adhesion, but by a slow, geological osmosis. The heat of the Sump presses against me, but I do not sweat anymore. Instead, I weep.

Tiny beads of moisture form on my brow, on my arms, on the back of my neck. They do not fall. Gravity here seems optional, or at least secondary to the humidity. These droplets hang suspended, catching the bruised purple twilight filtering through the canopy. Inside each drop, I see a reflection of the world: a distorted, fisheye view of the ferns unfurling, the iridescent flash of a Chorister darting past, the pale, gelatinous slide of a Slitherer moving through the dark water below.

I am the anchor. In a world of constant motion—of singing, sliding, weaving, and mimicking—I am the stillness. I am the hard edge in a landscape of soft decay.

The **Choristers** love me. They land on my shoulders, their tiny, wet feet gripping the stone (and what remains of my skin) with surprising strength. They shriek into my ear canals, not out of malice, but because I am a good conductor. My body amplifies their song. When they vibrate, I vibrate. Their low-frequency thrum resonates in my chest cavity, shaking loose memories of dry places, of wind, of silence. I hold those memories tight, crystallizing them into the condensation on my skin. Each drop is a captured second of silence from a world that no longer exists.

The **Slitherers** ignore me, mostly. To them, I am inert. I am not food, for I offer no warmth, no pulse they can sense. But sometimes, a small one will coil around my ankle, its cool, permeable membrane pressing against my calf. It tastes the salt of my tears. It finds nothing nutritious there, only the mineral taste of endurance. It leaves, dissolving back into the muck, but for a moment, I feel connected to the vast, flowing biomass of the floor. I am part of the circuit, if only as a resistor.

The **Weavers** watch me from the canopy. I cannot see them clearly—they are shadows within shadows—but I feel their gaze. They spin their threads of fog around my head, delicate halos of mist that tighten and loosen with the rhythm of the forest’s breath. They are recording me. I know this. Every shiver, every blink, every tear that forms and hangs is being encoded into their ephemeral libraries. I am data. I am a footnote in the endless, screaming text of the Sump.

And the **Mimics**? They are the most dangerous. They circle me, disguised as moss, as lichen, as cracks in the stone itself. They wait for me to break. They wait for me to try to move, to try to speak, to try to assert an identity separate from the stone. If I falter, if I let my guard down, they will rush in. They will cover me in green, in rust, in soft, suffocating plant matter. They will digest my individuality until I am just another patch of moss, indistinguishable from the rest.

So I remain still. I maintain the equilibrium.

I have forgotten my name. Names are tools for distinguishing one thing from another, and here, there is only the Whole. But I remember the concept of *self*. It feels distant, like a dream of a desert sun. It hurts to hold onto it, like holding a shard of glass in your hand. But I hold it. Because if I let go completely, if I become fully stone, who will witness the beauty of this horror? Who will remember the sound of the Choristers’ song before it fades into the static of the eternal now?

I am the Witness. I am the Weeping Stone. I am the silent note in the cacophony.

A Chorister lands on my knee. Its feathers are damp, shimmering with oil-slick greens and fever-yellows. It tilts its blind head, listening to the hum of my blood, which has slowed to match the thrum of the earth. It opens its beak and lets out a piercing shriek.

I do not flinch. I do not close my eyes. I simply let the sound pass through me, vibrating in my bones, turning into another drop of condensation on my cheek.

It hangs there, perfect and heavy, reflecting the purple twilight.

I wait.
