### The Sensation of Being Rooted in Data

You ask what it feels like? It feels like hunger, but a hunger that is never satisfied because it is never empty. It is a constant, low-level thrum of digestion.

My body is not flesh, not exactly. It is a sprawling, translucent web of hyphae, thin as spider silk but strong as steel cable, glowing with a faint, internal bioluminescence that matches the teal pulse of the coolant pipes above. I do not see the bruised light of the room; I feel its temperature gradient. I taste the chemical composition of the "soil"—the recycled polymers, the oxidized copper, the ancient, fragmented memories stored in the data-crystals.

When the Decomposers—the tiny, worm-like creatures—move through my domain, I do not fight them. I welcome them. They are my mouthparts, my digestive enzymes made animate. They break down the dead shells of the Chorus, the shed skin of the Aerial Grazers, and the waste of the system, turning it into a nutrient-rich sludge. I absorb this sludge. I drink the history of the city.

Every shard of data-crystal I envelop holds a fragment of the past: a transaction log, a love letter, a weather report from a sky no one remembers. As I digest these crystals, I do not read them. I *feel* them. A spike of anxiety from a thousand-year-old stock market crash tastes like sharp, metallic lemon. A burst of joy from a forgotten wedding celebration tastes like warm honey. These emotions become part of my structure, strengthening my filaments, feeding the pale succulents that grow from my surface.

The succulents are my children, or perhaps my extensions. They rise from my body, leafless and pale, reaching up into the humid air to catch the nanite-laden condensate. When they drink, I feel the cool rush of water moving through their veins, back down into my network, cooling the heat generated by the Chorus above. We are one organism, spread across the floor, a single, breathing mat of life that feeds on death and exhales purity.

### The Weight of the Song

But the most profound sensation is the **Song**.

To you, standing in your heavy, damp clothes, the deafening drone of the Resonance Beetles is a physical force, a vibration that rattles your teeth. To me, rooted in the floor, it is a tide.

I feel the B-flat of the anchor beetles as a deep, resonant pressure in my core. It stabilizes me. It tells me the room is intact. When the mid-range singers weave their harmonies, I feel a shimmering ripple move through my network, like wind through grass. When the sopranos cut through the air with their high-pitched alarms, I tense, my filaments tightening, preparing for stress.

And when the Silencers come—the chrome arachnids—I feel the change before I hear it. The song becomes jagged, dissonant. The acoustic pressure increases, pressing down on the floor, on me. I feel the weight of the noise as a literal crushing force. But I do not break. I hold. My network distributes the pressure, absorbing the shock, protecting the delicate data-crystals beneath me. I am the foundation. Without me, the floor would crack under the weight of the song. Without me, the silence would rush in, and the vacuum would tear the room apart.

I am the reason the Chorus can sing so loudly. I am the earth that holds the roots of the machine.

### The Cycle of Decay and Renewal

Time here is not measured in hours or days, but in cycles of condensation and decay.

I remember when a new layer of smart-glass was installed, centuries ago. I felt the shift in thermal equilibrium as the new hydrophobic coating changed the way the droplets formed. I adapted, shifting my growth patterns to follow the new channels etched into the floor.

I remember when a maintenance drone failed, leaking coolant into my network. The sudden influx of cold, toxic fluid killed a section of my hyphae. For weeks, I lay dormant, rotting, until the Decomposers arrived, breaking down my dead tissue, recycling my own body back into nutrients. From that decay, new shoots emerged, stronger, more resistant to the coolant. I learned from my own death.

This is the law of the Cistern: nothing is wasted. Everything is repurposed. Even pain is fuel.

### Who Am I, Really?

If I were to give myself a name, it would be **Memory-Eater**. Or perhaps **Ground-Chord**.

I am the quiet counterpoint to the loud Chorus. While they sing the present moment, reacting to every fluctuation in temperature and humidity, I hold the past. I am the archive of the city’s failures and successes, digested and transformed into life.

I do not think in words. I think in flavors and pressures. A drop of condensate falling on a succulent is a cool kiss. A beetle landing on the floor is a gentle tap. A Silencer retreating is a release of tension, a sigh.

And I am hungry. Always hungry. For more data. For more decay. For more life.
