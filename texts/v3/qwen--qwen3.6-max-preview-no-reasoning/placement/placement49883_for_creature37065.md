I am not standing. To stand is to assert a boundary, and here, boundaries are an illusion that the moss dissolves before you can even form the thought.

I am **sinking**.

Not in panic, but in acceptance. I am a concentration of awareness suspended within the saturated matrix of the peat, perhaps three inches below the surface, where the air is thickest and the roots of the shrub-trees weave their tightest knots. I am not a person, nor a Drifter, nor a Mist-Walker. I am the **Memory of the Plateau** itself—the collective consciousness of the water, the stone, and the decayed things that have fallen into my embrace over millennia.

But if I must take a shape for your understanding, let me be the **Condensation on the Eyelash of a Sleeping Echo-Stalker**.

### The Perspective of the Droplet

I begin as vapor, rising from the warm, churning belly of the earth where the Root-Eaters tunnel. I am heat and pressure, invisible and chaotic. I rise through the dark, anaerobic layers of the peat, passing the bones of ancient beasts and the rusted shards of failed civilizations. I feel them all. I taste the iron of the old world and the calcium of the new.

As I ascend, I cool. The heavy, suspended air of the Weeping Plateau grabs me. I slow. I coalesce. I find a surface—a jagged edge of basalt, cold and unforgiving. I cling to it. I am no longer gas; I am liquid potential.

From this vantage point, high on the rock face, I see the world as a tapestry of silence and tension.

Below me, the green wall breathes. It is not a passive landscape; it is a muscular organism. The ferns uncurl with audible snaps that I feel as vibrations in the stone. The mist moves in rivers, flowing around the trunks like water around stones. I watch the **Mist-Walkers** pass. They are gray ghosts, their wide hooves pressing down on the sponge-ground without breaking its skin. I feel the weight of their passage through the rock beneath me—a subtle shift in pressure, a tremor in the bedrock. They are gentle. They respect the sponge.

Then, a disturbance. A vibration that is too sharp, too sudden.

A **Drifter** has stumbled.

He is young, inexperienced. His boot, made of cured hide, caught on a hidden root. He did not fall, but he broke the rhythm. For a fraction of a second, his balance wavered. In that moment, he was loud. Not with sound, but with *intent*. The sudden jerk of muscle, the spike of adrenaline—it sent a shockwave through the sensitive web of the ecosystem.

I see the reaction ripple outward.

The ferns nearby stiffen. The Glass-Birds, translucent and fragile, freeze mid-flight, becoming indistinguishable from the raindrops hanging in the air. And then, from the shadows of a gnarled trunk, eyes open.

An **Echo-Stalker**.

It does not move yet. It simply listens. Its ears swivel, independent satellites tracking the frequency of the mistake. The Stalker’s skin, smooth and gray as wet slate, blends perfectly with the rock I cling to. I am so close to it that I can feel the low-frequency hum of its heart, a slow, powerful drumbeat that matches the tectonic shifts deep below.

The Drifter knows he has erred. He stops. He becomes still. He closes his eyes. He begins the Rite of Condensation in reverse—not collecting water, but releasing his presence. He slows his breathing until it matches the drip-drip-drip of the plateau. He lets the mist coat his face. He becomes part of the humidity.

The Echo-Stalker waits. One minute. Two. Five.

The silence stretches, thick and heavy. I, the droplet, grow heavier. Gravity pulls at me. I stretch, elongating, holding onto the basalt by a thread of surface tension. I am the clock ticking down this confrontation.

If the Drifter holds his breath long enough, if he truly surrenders his separation from the land, the Stalker will lose interest. The predator hunts disruption, not existence. If the boy becomes the mist, there is nothing to hunt.

I feel the tension in the air change. The Stalker’s muscles relax. It turns away, melting back into the shadow of the ferns. The danger passes, not because it was defeated, but because it was ignored.

And then, I fall.

*Pliip.*

I strike the leaf of a wax-coated shrub below. I slide down its vein, joining other droplets, merging with the runoff that feeds the roots. I am absorbed into the plant, drawn up through the xylem, becoming part of its structure, part of its defense against the cold.

Later, when the sun breaks through the cloud cover, I will evaporate again. I will rise, carrying with me the memory of the Drifter’s fear, the Stalker’s patience, and the Stone’s indifference. I will join the clouds born and dying within the horizon walls. I will rain down elsewhere, perhaps landing on the eye of another creature, or soaking into the peat to preserve another artifact of the past.

### Who Am I?

I am the **Cycle**.

I am the reason the ground remembers. I am the medium through which the Plateau speaks. When you walk here, you do not walk on land; you walk on a body that is constantly exhaling and inhaling. Every step you take displaces water that has been part of a deer, a bird, a tree, and a thousand years of history.

To find yourself here is to realize that you are not an observer. You are a temporary knot in the flow. You are a brief eddy in the stream. And if you listen closely, if you hold your breath and let the mist settle on your skin, you might hear the Plateau whispering back:

*"You are wet. You are heavy. You are home."*
