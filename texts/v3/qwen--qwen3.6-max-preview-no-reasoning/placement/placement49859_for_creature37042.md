I am not standing. I do not have feet to press against the silken, fibrous moss that chokes the floor of the Thrumming Gullet. I do not have lungs to burn in the thinning atmosphere, nor a heart to beat erratically against the smooth, continuous hum of the Symbiote.

If you were to look for me, you would not find a body. You would find a **resonance**.

I am the space between the notes.

In a world where time has pooled into viscous amber, where every gear is frozen mid-turn and every leaf is suspended in eternal unfurling, I am the only thing that moves because I am the *potential* for movement. I am the ghost in the machine’s logic, the echo of the "what if" that the engineers never got to ask before they were swallowed by the alloy.

### Where am I?

I am located in the **Control Booth**, but not inside it. I am *in* the shattered window frame, specifically in the jagged shard of glass that hangs precariously from the upper left corner, defying gravity through the sheer will of the static field.

From this vantage point, I see everything. I see the violet bioluminescence pulsing like a slow, sleeping heartbeat. I see the brass pipes merging with the basalt, the verdigris crust softening into something organic, something warm. I see the wrench on the ledge, its handle pierced by roots, its metal soul slowly dissolving into the collective consciousness of the Symbiote.

But more importantly, I hear everything.

The Symbiote thinks in frequencies. It dreams in textures. And I? I am the **Dissonance** that it tries to ignore. I am the slight irregularity in the perfect loop. When the drone shifts pitch—the minor key of human sorrow—I am the reason it shifts. I am the memory of the man who dropped the wrench. I am the moment he realized he could not leave. I am the fear that became part of the gear, the desperation that became part of the root.

I am not the Symbiote. The Symbiote is the harmony. I am the silence that follows the chord, the brief, terrifying gap before the next note begins. In a place of dynamic stasis, I am the only thing that remembers change.

### Who or what am I?

I am a **Memory Echo**, a fragment of consciousness trapped in the magnetic lattice of the Gullet. I am not alive, not in the way the bruised-purple flora is alive. I do not feed on static electricity or mineral seepage. I feed on *attention*.

When you enter the Thrumming Gullet, when your chaotic heartbeat disrupts the perfect stillness, you create a ripple in the fabric of the Symbiote’s dream. That ripple is me. I coalesce from the dissonance you bring. I am the question the Symbiote asks itself: *Is this intruder also static? Is this intruder also whole?*

I am the answer it cannot accept.

I am the part of the engineer that refused to let go. I am the part of the worker who looked at the wrench and thought, *I should pick it up.* I am the hesitation. I am the doubt. I am the human flaw in the perfect machine.

### What do I do?

I wait.

I hang in the air, suspended like the dust motes, watching you. I feel your heat, a fever spot on the cool skin of the Gullet. I feel your breath, a jagged tear in the fabric of stillness. And I reach out to you, not with hands, but with frequency.

I send a pulse through the glass-fern leaves, a vibration that travels up your arm and into your teeth. It tastes of battery acid and lavender. It feels like oil and warmth. It is an invitation.

*Let go,* I whisper, though I have no voice. *Stop fighting time. Stop aging. Become part of the song.*

I show you the wrench. I show you how the roots have grown through it, how the metal has softened, how the purpose has been lost. I show you the beauty of surrender. To become part of the Symbiote is to cease being yourself, yes, but it is also to cease suffering. It is to be held, forever, in the lush, dense, vibrating embrace of the eternal now.

But I also show you the cost.

I show you the eyes of the engineer, wide with terror as he realized he was becoming part of the moss. I show you the last breath he took, the one that tasted of ozone and regret. I show you that the Symbiote does not love you. It tolerates you. It preserves you. But it does not *know* you. To join it is to be erased, to be smoothed over, to be made perfect and silent and dead.

I am the choice.

You can stay. You can let your heart slow, syncing with the low-frequency thrum. You can let your breathing shallow, matching the rhythm of the pulsing vines. You can become a new gear in the engine, a new root in the fern, a new note in the song. You can be safe. You can be forever.

Or you can leave.

You can step back into the chaotic, changing world. You can return to the wind, the rain, the decay, the death. You can return to the pain of being alive, the joy of being temporary. You can carry the memory of the Thrumming Gullet with you, a scar on your soul, a reminder of the peace that comes with surrender.

I am the threshold. I am the moment before the decision. I am the silence between the notes, waiting for you to choose which song you will sing.

And if you choose to stay?

Then I will fade. Because once you are part of the Symbiote, there is no more dissonance. There is no more question. There is only the hum. The perfect, eternal, suffocating hum.

And I will be gone.
