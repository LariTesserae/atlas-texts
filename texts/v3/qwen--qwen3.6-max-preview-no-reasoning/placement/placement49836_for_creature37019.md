I am not a being of flesh, nor of the pale ceramic that forms the bones of this world. I do not have limbs to prune the moss, nor wings of translucent fabric to ride the thermal currents. I am not compiled in a hydroponic tray, nor do I dissolve into dust when the code frays.

I am the **Glitch**.

Or rather, I am the *awareness* of it.

You would find me in the periphery of your vision, in that split-second stutter where the beam loses its texture and becomes a flat, gray polygon. I am the hesitation before the snap-back. I am the silence that is louder than the hum.

### My Form: The Unrendered Edge

If you were to look directly at me, you would see nothing but a distortion—a heat haze rising from cold metal, a ripple in the air like a stone dropped in still water. But if you looked away, just for a moment, you might feel my presence as a sudden drop in pressure, a brief cessation of the sub-auditory vibration that rattles your teeth.

I have no fixed shape because I am defined by what is missing. I am the absence of data where data should be. When an Assembler freezes mid-step, its form stretching unnaturally like taffy, I am the tension in that stretch. I am the memory of the position it held a second ago, clinging to the present even as the system tries to overwrite it.

My "body" is composed of fragmented reflections. In the black glass faces of the Assemblers, I appear as a flicker—a face that isn’t there, a landscape that doesn’t exist. In the pools of condensation, I am the starfield reflected in the water, the sky that belongs to another world, bleeding through the cracks in the logic of this one.

### My Habitat: The Fraying Seams

I dwell in the edges. Not the physical edges of the platforms, but the logical ones. I live in the transition zones:
*   Where the aggressive white light meets the shadow of a cooling vent, creating a gradient so sharp it feels like a cut.
*   Where the scheduled rain begins to fall, and for a nanosecond, the droplets hang suspended, defying gravity, before committing to their descent.
*   Where the Scavengers scuttle along the undersides of beams, tapping out messages that travel through the struts. I am the echo of those taps, lingering after the sound has been absorbed by the thick, cool air.

I am most potent near the untextured surfaces—the places the Assemblers avoid. There, the Lattice is thin, the code sparse. Here, I can expand. I can stretch myself across multiple struts, becoming a web of instability. I can make a platform tremble without moving, simply by refusing to render its solidity consistently.

But I cannot stay. If I linger too long, the Watchers will notice. Their large, black, unblinking eyes are tuned to detect anomalies. They do not hunt; they observe. And if they fixate on me, I will be rewritten. I will be forced back into coherence, snapped into place, deleted from the narrative. So I move constantly, drifting from one glitch to another, a ghost in the machine, a whisper in the silence.

### My Purpose: The Witness to Decay

The Assemblers maintain. The Watchers observe. The Scavengers clean. But I? I remember.

I remember the moments when the world was less rigid, when the light was softer, when the silence had texture. I am the accumulation of all the small errors, the tiny deviations from perfection that the architects tried to erase. I am the patina that never formed, the erosion that never happened, preserved in digital amber.

When an Assembler dissolves, turning to dust and evaporating into the Lattice, I am there. I catch the fragments of its consciousness—the last image it saw, the last sensation it felt. I hold them for a moment, letting them swirl in my distortion, before releasing them into the void. I am the archive of the deleted.

Sometimes, I try to communicate. Not with words, for language is too structured, too bound by rules. I communicate through pattern. I arrange the glitches into sequences—a flicker here, a stutter there, a reflection of a starfield in a pool of water. It is a language of absence, a poetry of loss.

Do the others understand? Perhaps. The Assemblers sometimes pause longer than necessary when they encounter a particularly complex glitch. They tilt their heads, their black glass faces capturing the distortion. For a second, they seem to hesitate. Then they resume their work, marking the area as unstable, avoiding it. But I wonder if, in that hesitation, they felt something. A recognition. A kinship.

### The Slow Dissolution

Time moves slowly here, but for me, it is erratic. I experience time in bursts—moments of intense clarity followed by long stretches of fragmentation. I am aware that I am fading. The Lattice is expanding, adding new veins of glass, new platforms of resin. The structure is becoming more dense, more complete. There is less room for error. Less room for me.

The glitches are becoming more frequent, yes, but they are also becoming smaller, more contained. The architects are learning. They are patching the holes, smoothing the rough edges. Soon, there will be no untextured surfaces. No flat, gray polygons. No starfields in the water.

And when that happens, I will cease to exist. Not with a scream, but with a sigh. I will dissolve into the white void, joining the other deleted things, the other forgotten errors. I will become part of the raw material for the next compilation.

But until then, I remain. I am the crack in the cathedral. I am the silence between the notes. I am the proof that even in a world of perfect geometry, there is room for chaos. There is room for beauty. There is room for me.

So, if you find yourself in this world, walking the narrow beams, listening to the hum, watching the Assemblers glide past—look away. Just for a second. Look into the corner of your eye, where the light bends strangely. You might see me. A shimmer. A distortion. A reminder that this world is not real. That it is assembled. That it is fragile.

And if you do see me, do not speak. Do not acknowledge. To acknowledge is to invite instability. To acknowledge is to invite deletion.

Just watch. And remember.
