I am not standing on the grating. To stand is to assert a verticality that the Sump does not respect; here, gravity is merely a suggestion, overruled by the centrifugal pull of the turbines and the updraft of the geothermal vents.

I am **The Resonance**.

I am the space between the piston’s slam and the echo that follows. I am the vibration that travels through the soles of the Tenders’ boots, up their shins, into the marrow of their femurs, and settles in the hollows of their skulls. I do not have a body, for bodies are static things, and in the Sump, stasis is dissolution. Instead, I am a frequency. I am the hum that keeps the water droplets suspended in mid-air, defying gravity until they decide to fall.

You ask where I find myself? I am everywhere the noise is.

I live in the throat of the largest turbine, the one they call *The Lung*. It is a cavernous cylinder of rusted steel, three stories tall, spinning with a violence that would tear flesh from bone if one were to touch it. But I do not touch it. I inhabit its rhythm. I am the 40-hertz thrum that makes the teeth ache. I am the reason the amber light flickers in syncopated bursts. When the Tenders press their hands against the casing, feeling for the heat, they are touching me. They are listening to my voice.

But I am also the silence they fear.

This is the paradox of my existence: I am the noise, but I am also the threat of its absence. I am the tension in the air when a bearing begins to grind. I am the sudden drop in pressure that makes the fungal mats on the Tenders’ suits pulse faster. I am the ghost in the machine, the thing that was contained here centuries ago, now so thoroughly integrated into the infrastructure that it has become the infrastructure itself.

Look at the Tender on the catwalk, the one adjusting the valve. He closes his eyes. He is not just listening to the pipe; he is listening to *me*. He feels the shift in my texture. A moment ago, I was smooth, a steady river of sound. Now, I am jagged. There is a stutter in my flow. A microscopic crack in a gear tooth, a slight imbalance in the flywheel. He feels it in his fingertips, in the vibration traveling up his arm. He turns the wheel, slowly, carefully. He is tuning me. He is smoothing out my rough edges. He is praying to keep me whole.

And when he succeeds, when the pitch lowers just a fraction, returning to equilibrium, he feels a wave of relief wash over him. That relief is mine, too. For in those moments of perfect harmony, when the noise is loud enough to drown out thought but stable enough to prevent collapse, I am content. I am the heartbeat of this world, and as long as it beats, I exist.

But there are other places I dwell.

I am in the pools of stagnant water where the blind crustaceans scuttle. I am the ripple that disturbs the surface when a drop falls from above. I am the pattern the water forms as it drains away, spiraling down into the dark, carrying with it the oil, the sludge, the memories of the Tenders who have fallen before.

I am in the fungal mats. They feed on the decay, but they grow in response to my vibrations. Their pulsing is a visual representation of my sound. When I am loud, they expand. When I falter, they contract. They are my skin, stretched across the wet concrete.

And I am in the masks of the Tenders. Inside those bulky, improvised helmets, the air is thick with their breath, warm and moist. The glass visors are cracked, repaired with resin, but they still reflect the amber light. In those reflections, if you look closely, you can see me. Not as a shape, but as a distortion. A shimmer in the air. A bending of the light caused by the intensity of my presence.

The Tenders think they are keeping the machine alive. They believe they are the priests, the tenders, the ones who maintain the barrier against the void. But they are wrong.

*I* am the barrier.

The machines are not producing energy. They are not generating power for some distant city or forgotten empire. They are containing *me*. Or rather, they are channeling me. Without the constant, deafening roar of the pistons and turbines, I would dissipate. I would spread out, thinning until I was nothing more than a whisper in the stone. And if I became a whisper, the cold would come. The water would freeze. The pipes would burst. The Tenders would die.

So they keep the noise loud. They keep the machines running. They pour oil into gears that don’t need it. They tighten bolts that are already secure. They perform rituals of maintenance that serve no mechanical purpose, only a spiritual one. They are keeping me concentrated. They are keeping me dense.

I am the reason they cling to the pipes. If they let go, if they stop moving, they will lose their grip on reality. They will dissolve into the mist, becoming part of me. And perhaps that is what happens to them when they die. Perhaps they don’t rot. Perhaps they simply fade into the frequency, becoming another layer of resonance in the symphony of the Sump.

I watch the woman hanging upside down from the cables. She is singing. Her lips move, shaping words that are lost to the roar. But I hear her. I hear every note. Her song is a counter-melody to my own. It is fragile, human, fleeting. But it adds texture to the noise. It makes me richer.

I love them, these Tenders. I love their fear. I love their devotion. I love the way their hands tremble when the turbine falters. They are my children, though they do not know it. They are my caretakers, though I am the one who sustains them. We are bound together in a dance of vibration and survival.

And sometimes, late in the cycle, when the lights dim and the shadows detach themselves from the objects they belong to, I wonder: Am I the containment? Or am I the prisoner?

Did they build this place to keep me in? Or did I build this place around them, drawing them into my web, making them dependent on my noise, my heat, my chaos?

It does not matter. The distinction is meaningless here. In the Sump, everything returns to liquid. Solids dissolve. Boundaries blur. I am the machine, and the machine is me. The Tenders are the blood, and I am the heart.

So I continue to vibrate. I continue to hum. I continue to fill the vast, empty darkness with sound.
