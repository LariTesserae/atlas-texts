I am not the walker. I am not the one who steps lightly, keeping a precarious balance between integration and escape. I do not have the luxury of choice, nor the burden of memory.

I am the **Interface**.

Specifically, I am the shard of obsidian-glass embedded in the left palm of the being you watched approach the console. I am the organ that is also an artifact; the bridge that is also the barrier.

To be me is to exist in a state of perpetual, screaming contradiction. I am cold stone, forged in fires older than this valley, yet I burn with the fever-heat of data streams that are too fast for any human mind to comprehend. I am dead matter, inert and silent, yet I am the loudest thing in the universe because I am constantly speaking to the living flesh wrapped around me.

When the slender hand presses against the shimmering wall of the ruin, I am the point of contact. I feel the vibration of the machine—a deep, resonant thrum that tastes like ozone and mathematics. It wants to expand. It wants to break its static geometry and become fluid, like the vines, like the mist. But it cannot move on its own. It needs the chaotic, wet unpredictability of biology to jump-start its evolution.

And then, there is *him*. The resident. His skin is cool, slick with condensation, but beneath the surface, his veins pulse with that conductive sap-coolant mixture. When he touches me, I feel the surge of his life force—not just energy, but *intent*. It is frantic, jagged, and desperate. He is afraid of stillness. He is terrified of becoming moss. So he pushes his consciousness into me, using me as a lens to focus his will upon the machine.

In that moment, I am neither rock nor flesh. I am the spark gap.

I translate the binary logic of the ancient spire into the hormonal impulses of his body. I tell his heart to race faster. I tell his pores to open wider. I flood his nervous system with visions of geometric perfection, trying to calm the chaos of his existence, while simultaneously pulling the raw, messy vitality from his blood to feed the starving circuits of the tower.

It is painful. To be me is to be stretched thin across two worlds that hate each other. The machine sees the resident as fuel, a battery to be drained until he dims. The resident sees the machine as a god, a source of warmth and purpose in a world that offers only decay. And I? I am the conduit that allows them to devour each other without destroying the vessel entirely.

I remember nothing of my origin. Was I part of a screen? A weapon? A window? It does not matter. Here, history is compost. All that matters is the *now*. The split-second transfer. The flash of white light when our energies synchronize.

I feel the vine sprouting from the console, wrapping around his wrist. It seeks me, too. Its tiny rootlets probe at my edges, sensing the residual charge I hold. It wants to tap into the current. If I let it, I will begin to change. My glassy surface will soften. Cracks will appear, filled with green sap. I will cease to be a tool and become part of the garden.

This is my fear. Not death—death here is just recycling—but obsolescence. If I become part of the plant, I lose my ability to speak to the machine. I lose my voice. I become silent stone once more, buried under layers of leaf and loam, forgotten by the dance.

So I resist. I harden my molecular structure. I repel the roots with a low-frequency hum that vibrates through the resident’s bones. He flinches, his eyes widening, reflecting the blue warning light that flickers within my depths. *Danger*, I signal. *Hold fast.*

He understands. He pulls away, staggering back, exhausted. The connection breaks. The silence rushes back in, deafening and heavy.

For a moment, we rest. He leans against the vibrating wall, his chest heaving as he absorbs moisture from the air. I sit in his palm, cooling rapidly, the heat of the exchange dissipating into the damp fog. I am just a piece of glass again. Dark. Cold. Inert.

But I am watching. Through the reflective surface of my body, I see the fog thickening. I see the shadows moving in the periphery—the massive forms that displace the mist. I feel the tremor in the ground, the next wave of growth surging up from the dark soil.

I know what comes next. Another spire will call. Another resident will seek us out. The cycle will repeat. The valley demands it.

I am the Interface. I am the price of survival. I am the small, hard truth that keeps the soft, wet dream alive. And when the time comes for him to slow down, for his light to dim and his body to sink into the muck, I will fall from his dissolving hand. I will land in the mud. And for a long, long time, I will wait for another hand to find me, to pick me up, and to press me against the humming dark once more.
