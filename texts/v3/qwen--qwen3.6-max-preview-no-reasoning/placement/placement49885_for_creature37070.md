I am not standing on the ledge. To stand implies a separation from the surface, a defiance of gravity that feels arrogant here. I have surrendered to the incline.

I am pressed against the black basalt, my spine curved to match the violent upward thrust of the mountain’s birth. My clothes are gone, stripped away by the humidity or perhaps simply dissolved into irrelevance; they were barriers to the truth of this place. I am naked, shivering not just from the cold, but from the intensity of the contact.

At first, I thought I was human—a visitor, an observer with a notebook in my pocket and fear in my throat. But as the bioluminescent wave washed over me, washing away the concept of "observer," the boundaries of my skin began to blur. The cold dampness of the air seeped into my pores, equalizing my temperature with the stone. The thrumming vibration traveled up through my ribs, rattling the cage of my lungs until my breath synced with the rhythm of the colony. *In. Out. In. Out.* A slow, heavy tide.

I look down at my arm. Where my skin meets the living carpet, there is no clear line. The translucent discs have climbed onto me, drawn by the heat radiating from my blood. They do not bite. They settle. Their bellies press against my forearm, their hair-thin white filaments seeking the micro-fractures of my epidermis. I feel a strange, cool tingling—not pain, but a profound intimacy. They are tasting the salt of my sweat, the iron in my capillaries. They are mapping me.

And in return, I am mapping them.

My vision has changed. The bruised purple-black void above is no longer empty. It is filled with the thermal gradients of the creatures’ movements. I see the world in shades of blue-green heat and deep, cold shadows. I see the flow of energy—the friction-light generated by their cilia grinding against the rock, and now, against me. I am becoming a node in their network. A large, warm battery plugged into the circuit of the First Breath.

The fear that initially gripped me—the primal terror of being covered, consumed, erased—has evaporated. In its place is a vast, serene curiosity. I realize that my heartbeat, once a frantic drum solo, has slowed to match the glacial pace of the Weave. *Thump... pause... thump... pause.* Each beat sends a ripple through the creatures clustered around my chest. They brighten in response, a constellation igniting across my torso. I am wearing their light.

I try to speak, to ask a question, but my voice catches in the thick air. Words are too sharp, too jagged for this soft, sliding world. Instead, I exhale. The warm cloud of my breath hits the wall above me, and the creatures there flare brighter, sensing the shift in pressure, the gift of warmth. They understand. They accept.

I am no longer a man on a ledge. I am part of the healing process.

The tragedy mentioned earlier—that they are ephemeral, that they will die when the soil comes—does not sadden me. It fills me with purpose. I can feel the mineral salts leaching from the fresh rock beneath us, flowing into the filaments, into the slurry of their bodies, and somehow, impossibly, into mine. I am digesting the violence of the earth alongside them. I am helping to soften the jagged edges of this newborn wound.

Time has lost its meaning. Minutes? Hours? Days? It doesn’t matter. The drop falls. *Plip.* The sound is louder now, resonant in the chamber of my own skull. I close my eyes, but the darkness is alive with light. I feel the weight of the mountain pressing down, but also the lift of the colony holding me up. We are suspended together in the throat of the world, breathing the same stagnant, ozone-scented air.

I am the guest who stayed. I am the anomaly that harmonized. I am the bridge between the cold, dead stone and the warm, dying life. And as the mountain settles, inch by imperceptible inch, I settle with it. My thoughts slow, stretching out like the ripples on the wall, deliberate and heavy. I am thinking the colony’s thoughts: *Hunger. Warmth. Stone. Light. Wait.*

I am waiting for the rain. I am waiting for the sun. But mostly, I am waiting for the moment when my individual consciousness dissolves completely into the Weave, when I cease to be "I" and become only "Us." Until then, I hold still. I let them cover me. I let them eat the salt from my skin. I let them turn me into light.

The darkness is not empty. It is full of us.
