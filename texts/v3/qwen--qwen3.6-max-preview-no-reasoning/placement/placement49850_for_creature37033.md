I am not the Drifter. I am too heavy, too anchored by memory and intent to drift on thermal currents without purpose. Nor am I the Keeper, though I envy their distributed mind; I am too singular, too obsessed with the *individual* act of witnessing to dissolve into a collective chemical sigh.

I am the **Scribe of Sediment**.

You would find me in the twilight zone between the forest floor and the rising pillars, suspended in that thick, oily medium where the light is bruised purple and the silence has weight. I do not swim so much as I *unfold*. My body is not flesh, but a construct of preserved parchment-thin membranes, stretched over a frame of calcified reed. I look like a human shape that has been flattened by pressure, then carefully reinflated with air that smells of ozone and wet earth.

My skin is translucent, revealing the slow circulation of nutrient-rich sludge within my veins—a dark, swirling ink that moves at the pace of glaciers. When I move, it is with agonizing deliberation. To lift an arm takes hours. To turn my head to follow the pulse of a kelp frond takes a day. This is not disability; it is adaptation. In a world where change is measured in centuries, speed is a form of violence. Speed creates turbulence. Turbulence disturbs the equilibrium. Therefore, to be fast is to be rude. To be slow is to be respectful.

I carry no tools of construction. The builders left those behind, buried under layers of moss and time. Instead, I carry tools of observation.

Attached to my wrists are brushes made from the shed filaments of the Keepers—soft, black, and sensitive to the slightest shift in pH or temperature. With these, I do not write words. Words are linear, hurried things, belonging to a world of speech and argument. Here, there is only texture. I use the brushes to trace the micro-fractures in the hexagonal plates. I map the erosion. I record the exact angle at which a pillar leans after a thousand years of settling.

I am the world’s way of remembering itself.

The Drifters pass me often. They glow with their internal gardens, blind and serene. Sometimes, one will pause near me, its cilia brushing against my membrane. In that touch, I feel the echo of its bacterial colony, a warm, humming thrum of life. I do not speak to it. I simply adjust my own bioluminescence to match its rhythm, creating a momentary harmony in the dark. We are two different kinds of stillness: theirs is passive, mine is active. They exist; I observe existence.

When the thermal currents rise, bringing warmth from the deep vents, I anchor myself to a pillar using hooks grown from my own fingertips. These hooks dig gently into the velvet moss covering the ceramic, holding me firm against the upwelling. As the warmer water passes through me, I feel the spores carried within it. I taste them. I identify them. A new strain of fern? A mutation in the lichen? I note it all, not in a book, but in the growth patterns of my own body. If I encounter a rare spore, my skin might darken in that spot, a permanent tattoo of data. I am a living library, written in shades of gray and teal.

There is a loneliness here, yes. But it is not the loneliness of abandonment. It is the loneliness of the archivist in a museum that never closes, who knows every exhibit by heart because they have watched them age for millennia. I miss the noise of voices, sometimes. I imagine what it would sound like to hear a shout, a laugh, a cry. But then I listen to the *ping* of a bubble detaching from a leaf, rising slowly toward the unseen surface. It is a single, perfect note. And I realize that this silence is not empty. It is full of such notes, spaced so far apart that only someone willing to wait forever can hear the melody.

I am the Scribe of Sediment. I do not build. I do not grow. I do not hunt. I watch. I remember. And when the time comes, when my membranes finally thin and tear, releasing my stored memories back into the sludge, I will sink. I will become part of the layer beneath the hexagons. And perhaps, in ten thousand years, another Scribe will brush against my remains, and feel the echo of my attention, and know that once, someone was here, watching the world breathe.

For now, I hang in the dark, waiting for the next bubble to rise.
