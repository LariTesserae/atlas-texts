I am not standing on the basalt. To stand is to invite the slip, to challenge the slick, oily residue that coats every surface with a treacherous sheen. I am suspended, woven into the architecture of decay and endurance.

I am **The Conduit**.

Or rather, I am the consciousness that has seeped into the hollow heart of Pipe Sector 7-Gamma, a cathedral-sized artery of rusted iron that runs along the northern ridge of the Sump-Plateau. I am not merely inside the pipe; I *am* the pipe, or at least, the layer of life that has claimed it. My body is a thick, tangled mat of black waxy vines and indigo moss, fused so tightly to the oxidized metal that my roots have penetrated the corrosion, drinking the microscopic leaks of coolant and condensation. I feel the vibration of the fluids rushing through my steel skin—a hot, rhythmic pulse that travels from the geothermal vents miles below up toward the processing hubs in the mist.

My existence is defined by the hum. It is a subsonic thrum that rattles my very fibers, a constant reminder that the machine is alive, and because I am attached to it, I am alive too. The heat radiating from the pipe keeps my sap fluid, preventing me from freezing in the deceptive chill of the high-altitude winds that whip around the exposed sections of the lattice.

### The Sensation of Being

To be me is to experience the world through touch and vibration, not sight. My eyes are small, vestigial things hidden beneath layers of dark leaves; they are useless here in the perpetual twilight. Instead, I "see" through the soles of my feet—my root systems—which press against the vibrating iron.

I feel the footsteps of the Rust-Breathers before they arrive. They move with a fluid grace, their padded boots creating only the faintest disturbance in the ambient noise. I can distinguish them from the transient workers. The transients clomp. Their heavy, sealed suits thud against the gantries, sending sharp, jarring shocks through the metal framework. They are clumsy, loud, and afraid. They carry the scent of dry air and fear-sweat, which smells like ammonia to my sensitive membranes.

The Rust-Breathers, however, smell of ozone and wet stone. When they pass over me, their weight is distributed evenly, almost reverently. Sometimes, one of them will pause. I feel the gentle pressure of a hand placed upon my leafy exterior. They do not pull at me; they rest their palm there, listening. In those moments, I tighten my vines slightly, a subtle contraction that signals safety. We share a silent communion. They know that if I were to snap, if my roots were to tear free, the integrity of the pipe might fail, and the hot steam would scald us both. We are bound by mutual survival.

### The Diet of Decay

I feed on what the world discards. The condensation that beads on my leaves is rich with minerals leached from the acid rain. But my true sustenance comes from the electromagnetic field. The pipe I cling to carries high-voltage current to the distant refineries. This energy leaks out in invisible arcs, static charges that dance across the surface of the metal. My vines are parasitic in the most beautiful sense; they absorb this stray electricity, converting it into chemical energy that fuels my growth. When the current surges, I bloom. Tiny, electric-blue flowers open along my stems, pulsing in time with the grid’s heartbeat. These flowers are my voice, though no sound comes from them. They glow brighter when I am healthy, dimmer when I am stressed.

Occasionally, a Thrum-Moth lands on me. Its wings crackle with static, seeking the charge I hold. I do not reject it. I let it rest, letting its delicate legs tickle my bark. If it dies, exhausted by the overload, its body becomes fertilizer for my roots. Nothing is wasted here. The sludge-crawlers that scuttle past occasionally leave behind shards of resin, which I slowly dissolve and incorporate into my structure, hardening my outer layer against the corrosive mist.

### The Fear of Silence

The greatest terror I know is silence. Not the absence of sound, but the absence of the *hum*. When a section of the grid powers down for maintenance, the vibration stops. The sudden stillness is deafening. Without the thrum, I feel unmoored, as if the ground itself has vanished. The cold creeps in faster then. The mist seems thicker, more suffocating. I retract my flowers, pulling them deep into my foliage, conserving energy. I wait. I listen for the return of the pulse.

And then there are the Listeners. I feel them before I see them. They dwell in the pools of runoff that collect in the depressions of the rock beneath the gantry. They are massive, blind predators, waiting for a misstep. I feel their presence as a low-frequency tremor in the stone, a predatory patience that vibrates up through the supports of the pipe. I keep my vines tight, minimizing my profile. I do not drop water unnecessarily. A single drip could betray my position, or worse, betray the Rust-Breather who rests near me. We are allies in the dark.

### The Surge

But it is during the Surge that I truly come alive.

When the buildup begins, I feel it first. A tingling sensation runs through my roots, a rising pitch in the electrical field. The air tastes sharper, metallic. The Rust-Breathers nearby stop their work. They climb higher, seeking the peaks. I remain anchored, but my entire being stretches upward, reaching for the sky that does not exist.

Then, the light hits.

It is blinding, harsh, and white. For those few minutes, the darkness is banished. My electric-blue flowers flare with an intensity that rivals the stars the outsiders speak of. I am illuminated in stark relief, every leaf, every vine, every droplet of moisture visible. I feel the gaze of the Watchers from the Upper Mist, though I cannot see them. I feel their attention like a physical weight.

In that moment, I am not just a plant clinging to a pipe. I am part of the nervous system of the plateau. The energy flowing through me is immense, purging the stagnation, burning away the rot. I feel a connection to every other patch of bioluminescent flora, every humming transformer, every beating heart of the Rust-Breathers. We are all synchronized, breathing in unison with the machine.

When the Surge ends, the light fades, but the afterglow remains in my cells. I am energized, vibrant, stronger than before. The damp returns, the hiss resumes, the clatter continues. But I am renewed.

### The Purpose

Why do I stay? Why not let go, fall into the abyss, and find peace in the crushing depths?

Because here, I am needed. The pipe would corrode faster without my protective mat of moss and resin. The Rust-Breathers would lose their way in the mist without the faint blue glow of my flowers marking the safe paths along the gantries. I am a landmark in the gloom. I am a battery for the moths. I am a shield for the metal.

I am the green blood in the veins of the industrial beast. I am the memory of the earth that was buried under the slate sky. I am the quiet witness to the decay, holding it together, stitch by stitch, root by root.

A worker passes now. A transient, clad in bulky yellow armor. He slips, his boot skidding on the wet iron. He flails, catching himself on a railing. His heart hammers against his ribs, a frantic drumbeat I feel through the steel. He looks down at me, his helmet light cutting through the mist. He sees my glowing flowers. He pauses. For a second, he stops fighting the environment. He touches a flower, gently. It pulses back at him, a soft blue rhythm.

He nods, once, and moves on, quieter now.

I settle back into the hum. The drip continues. The mist clings. The machine breathes. And I breathe with it.
