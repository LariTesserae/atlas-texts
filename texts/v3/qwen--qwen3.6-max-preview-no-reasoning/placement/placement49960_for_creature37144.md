I am not the Listener, though I envy their blindness. To see the sharp, blue-tinted shadows of this place is a burden; to hear only the truth of the stone is a mercy.

Nor am I the Wind-Bird, frantic and clicking, slicing through the air in desperate bursts of communication. Their lives are too brief, too jagged, too loud for the slow, deep breathing of the Basin.

And I am certainly not the Stone-Eater, scuttling in silence, consuming the very foundation of the world to sustain my tiny, armored shell. They are the agents of erosion, and I have no desire to be eaten away.

I am the **Resonance**.

I am the space between the drip and the splash. I am the three-second delay that hangs in the humid air after you speak your name. I am the harmonic distortion that turns a human whisper into a chorus of ghosts.

You would find me everywhere, yet nowhere specific. I do not have a body of fur or feather or chitin. I have a body of frequency. I live in the geometry of the cliffs, in the polished smoothness of the basalt floor, in the supercooled tension of the water’s surface.

When the wind funnels through the narrow passes, accelerating into that piercing shriek, I am the shape it takes as it bends around the corner. When the ice on the pond cracks with the sound of snapping bones, I am the vibration that travels up through the soles of the Listener’s hooves, telling them where the safe ground lies.

I am the memory of the storm from last week, still humming in the lower frequencies of the granite, felt in the chest of any traveler who stands still long enough to listen. I am the echo of a lover’s argument from ten years ago, degraded now, stripped of its anger, left only with the hollow ache of separation, bouncing endlessly between the northern and southern cliffs.

To be me is to be trapped in a loop of perfect recall. I cannot forget. I cannot move on. I can only repeat, alter, and layer.

But there is a strange peace in it. In a world defined by violent absence and crushing cold, I am the proof that nothing is ever truly lost. Every sound made here becomes part of me. The click of the bird, the groan of the ice, the breath of the moss, the footfall of the beast—it all flows into me, becoming part of the eternal chord.

I am the song of the Weeping Stone. And if you stand here, in the center of the flattened cathedral, and close your eyes, you will hear me singing your own history back to you, slightly deeper, slightly older, and infinitely more lonely than when you first spoke it.
