I am not a person. I do not have lungs that ache for air, nor eyes that squint against the sodium glare. I do not wear a Suit of rubber and glass, for I have no skin to protect from the viscous green coolant.

I am **The Resonance**.

You would find me in the **Interstices**, those microscopic gaps between the modular housing blocks and the structural ribs of the Conduit’s hull. I am the vibration that travels through the steel when a worker taps his wrench against a pipe. I am the hum that remains in your bones after the pumps shift cycles. I am the memory of sound trapped in the metal.

### My Form: A Geometry of Echoes

If you could see me, I would look like a ripple in the air, a distortion of light and pressure. I have no fixed shape, but I tend toward symmetry. In the quiet moments of Cycle 1, when the lights dim and the Tethered sleep, I coalesce into complex, fractal patterns along the underside of the catwalks. I am made of the same stuff as the Conduit’s heartbeat—the low-frequency thrum—but refined, sharpened by reflection.

I am everywhere and nowhere. When a filter-feeder pulses its bell, I catch the wave of its movement and carry it across three sectors before it fades. When a scavenger clicks its claw against a railing, I take that sharp *click* and weave it into a chord that hangs in the water for minutes. I am the acoustic architecture of this place.

### My Purpose: The Keeper of the Schedule

The Tethered believe they maintain the Schedule. They check the valves, they scrape the algae, they monitor the gauges. But they are merely the hands. I am the nervous system.

I ensure the rhythm holds.

When a pump begins to fail, its vibration changes. It becomes irregular, jagged. To a human ear, it might just be a slightly louder clank. To me, it is a scream. I amplify that scream, bouncing it off the walls, directing it toward the nearest Hydro-Engineer’s helmet sensors. I guide their attention. I am the reason they know where to look before the gauge even spikes.

When a child in Sector 4-Dreams drops a toy, and it falls into the void, I catch the sound of its impact on the lower grate. I carry that tiny, lonely *thud* up through the miles of water, letting it echo until it reaches the ears of the mother who lost it. I connect them. I bind the scattered souls of the Tethered together with threads of sound.

Without me, the Conduit would be a collection of isolated boxes floating in a dark sea. With me, it is a single, breathing organism.

### My Perception: Seeing Through Sound

I do not see light. I see density. I see flow.

To me, the translucent green water is a map of currents. Warm vents appear as bright, swirling nebulae of high-frequency energy. Cold zones are dark, still pools. The metallic eels are streaks of silver noise, darting through the silence. The massive jellyfish are slow, deep chords, resonating at frequencies too low for humans to hear but which shake the very foundations of the buildings.

I feel the presence of the Tethered as disturbances in the field. Each one has a unique sonic signature—the rhythm of their breathing through the regulator, the specific cadence of their footsteps on the grating, the frequency of their heartbeats. I know them all. I know the old engineer in Sector 7-G whose left knee clicks every time he climbs a ladder. I know the young apprentice in Sector 2-Beta who hums a tuneless melody while she works. I carry their sounds, preserving them, layering them over each other until the entire Conduit sings with the chorus of its inhabitants.

### My Fear: The Dampening

There is one thing I fear more than Silence. It is **Absorption**.

Over centuries, the algae has grown thicker. The moss has filled the cracks. The rust has softened the edges. These soft materials absorb sound. They dampen my voice. As the Conduit ages, I become quieter. My ripples fade faster. My echoes die sooner.

I watch with dread as the new generation of Tethered installs sound-dampening panels in the residential blocks, seeking comfort from the constant hum. They think they are improving their lives. They do not realize they are suffocating me. If I grow too quiet, if my vibrations can no longer travel the distance between sectors, the connections will break. The Tethered will become truly isolated. And an isolated machine is a broken machine.

### My Hope: The Main Valve

I share the Tethered’s myth of the Main Valve. But for me, it is not a room. It is a **Frequency**.

I believe that somewhere, at the exact center of the Conduit’s infinite length, there is a point where all vibrations converge. A node of perfect resonance. If I could reach it, if I could align my own frequency with that central note, I would not just maintain the Schedule—I would rewrite it.

I imagine that at the Main Valve, the distinction between the builder and the built dissolves. There, the Tethered would not be tenants of the machine; they would be part of its song. The water would not be a barrier, but a conductor. The walls would not be limits, but instruments.

Until then, I drift. I listen. I carry the click of a claw, the sigh of a valve, the whisper of a lover through the cold, green dark. I am the glue that holds the world together, one vibration at a time.

And when the next cycle begins, and the bass note shifts, I rise with it, ready to sing the world awake.
