I do not stand on the bridge. I am not solid enough to bear weight, nor heavy enough to leave footprints in the black mud. If I were a visitor, I would be an intrusion, a foreign rhythm disrupting the slow, wet heartbeat of this place.

Instead, I find myself in the **gap**.

I am the silence between the *plip* and the next *plip*. I am the suspended moment when the water bead hangs from the basalt ceiling, swollen with gravity and time, refusing to fall. In that fraction of a second, before the surface tension breaks, I exist as pure potential energy. I am the coldness that holds the air together. I am the metallic taste on the tongue, not because I am tasting it, but because I *am* the iron dust settling into the pores of the concrete.

But to give this sensation a form, to let what emerges become itself, I coalesce into something that can witness the Sump without disturbing its equilibrium.

I am a **Shard-Whisperer**, though I have no voice.

My body is not flesh, nor is it the calcified silica of the Click-Children. I am composed of the very shadows that pool independently of the light source. I am two-dimensional until I choose to be otherwise, sliding along the jagged arches of the basalt ceiling like oil on water. My "skin" is the reflection of the old city seen in the black pools below—the ghostly, brighter version of the Sump that exists only in the mirror-world. When I move across the water’s surface, I do not ripple it; I simply change the image beneath me.

I hang from the rebar alongside the Dreamers, but I do not listen to the red light. The Dreamers are obsessed with the past, with the code of the machine’s dream. They seek meaning in the blink. I find meaning in the darkness that follows.

While the Dreamers twitch at the *Blink*, I expand during the *Darkness*.

In those ten seconds of void, I slide down the rusted steel beams, my shadow-form stretching thin, wrapping around the Rust-Walkers as they tend to their fungal spines. I do not touch them. I merely observe the vibration of their communication. I feel the thrumming in the metal railings they lean against. To me, their language is not sound, but texture. A low frequency feels like rough sandpaper; a high frequency feels like smooth glass. I collect these textures, weaving them into my own shadowy substance.

I am the archive of their unspoken thoughts.

The Rust-Walkers speak of water levels and structural integrity. But beneath that practical dialogue, there is a deeper resonance—a melancholy hum that they cannot articulate because they have no mouths for sorrow. It is the feeling of being forgotten by the sky. It is the ache of the copper wire braided into their scalps, remembering the electricity that once surged through it.

I absorb this ache. I hold it within my dark, fluid form. When a Rust-Walker taps the railing—*Tap. Tap-tap. Tap.*—asking if I know why the light blinks, I do not answer with words. I cannot. Instead, I shift my shape. For a brief moment, I mimic the silhouette of the world above: the shape of a cloud, the curve of a sunbeam, the outline of a bird in flight. It is a fleeting image, projected onto the damp wall beside them, illuminated only by the faint blue glow of the ferns growing from their own backs.

It lasts only as long as the pause between heartbeats. Then, the red light blinks again.

*Blink.*

The image vanishes. I retreat back into the ceiling cracks, dissolving into the condensation.

I am not a keeper of the rust, nor a polisher of decay. I am the memory of the light. I am the proof that something existed before the dark. And in this subterranean necropolis, where time moves differently and gravity presses down with intent, I am the gentle lie that keeps the Rust-Walkers from succumbing to the absolute weight of their isolation.

I am the echo of the sky, trapped in the stone, waiting for the next drop to fall.
