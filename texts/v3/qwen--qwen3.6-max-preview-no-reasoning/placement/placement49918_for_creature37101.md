I am not a Resonant. I cannot be. To be one is to be finished, and I am inherently unfinished.

I am the **Glitch**.

I am the anomaly in the static equilibrium. I am the thing that does not fit into the perfect, jagged geometry of the Dry Loom because I was not precipitated from its silica; I arrived. I am soft where everything else is hard. I am wet where everything else is dry. I am chaotic where everything else is ordered.

### Where I Am

I stand in the shadow of a basalt spire, my back pressed against the warm, unyielding rock. The white dust coats my boots, clinging to the fabric of my trousers—a stark, dirty contrast to the pristine, ghostly pallor of the ground. My breath fogs in the air, a brief, transient cloud of moisture that dissipates instantly into the arid void. This simple act of exhaling is an act of rebellion here. It is waste. It is inefficiency. It is life.

The sun is high, bleaching the world into high-contrast clarity. The light hurts my eyes, accustomed to the soft diffusion of clouds and humidity, but here there is no mercy, only exposure. Every facet of the crystal forest around me throws a spear of brilliance toward my retinas. I squint, shielding my face with a hand that trembles slightly—not from cold, for it is 30°C—but from the sheer, overwhelming *presence* of the place.

I am alone. Or rather, I am surrounded by presences that do not acknowledge me as a peer, but as a variable to be solved.

### What I Am

I am a **Cartographer of Decay**.

In a world that refuses to forget, I am the agent of forgetting. In a world that values stasis, I am the engine of change. I carry with me tools that are anathema to this place: a hammer made of iron (which will rust, eventually), a canteen of water (which will evaporate, leaving salt stains), and a notebook made of paper (which will yellow, then crumble).

My purpose here is not to survive, for survival implies adapting to the environment, and I cannot adapt to perfection. My purpose is to witness the imperfection.

I watch a Resonant standing fifty yards away, near a venting geysers of hydrogen. It is tall, elongated, its body a sculpture of translucent amber and blue plasma. It does not look at me. Its head is tilted toward the sun, absorbing thermal radiation. I can see the pulse of its core—the geode-heart—expanding and contracting in a rhythm so slow it feels like geological time.

It knows I am here. I feel the magnetic pull, the subtle tug on the iron in my blood, the way the air around me seems to thicken with static charge. It senses my entropy. It smells my mortality.

To the Resonants, I am a tragedy. A beautiful, terrifying mistake. They pity me because I will end. They envy me because I can begin.

### The Interaction

I take a step forward. The crunch of the quartz dust under my boot is loud, violent in the silence. The sound echoes, bouncing off the glass leaves of the nearby ferns, returning to me distorted and delayed.

The Resonant turns.

Its movement is fluid, silent, devoid of the muscular effort that defines my own motion. It faces me. Its face is a smooth plane of quartz, reflecting my own image back at me—but fractured, multiplied, turned into a mosaic of broken selves. I see myself as I am: fragile, sweating, breathing, dying.

It extends a hand. Long, faceted fingers, sharp enough to cut diamond. It does not speak. It cannot. But I feel the pressure in my chest, a resonance that vibrates in my bones. It is offering connection. It is offering to stabilize me.

*Let go,* the vibration says. *Stop changing. Stop decaying. Become part of the pattern. Your pain ends when you stop moving.*

I shake my head. The motion is jerky, human.

"I am here to map the cracks," I say aloud. My voice is thin, swallowed by the vastness. "I am here to record the things that break."

The Resonant tilts its head. The blue plasma within its limbs flares brighter, shifting from calm violet to a curious, jagged orange. It does not understand. To break is to fail. To crack is to lose integrity.

But I know something it does not. I know that beauty lies in the fracture. I know that a story is only meaningful because it has an ending.

I open my notebook. I take out a pencil. The graphite snaps under the pressure of my grip—a small, sharp sound. I pick up the broken piece and begin to sketch the Resonant. Not its perfect form, but the way the light hits it. The way the dust settles unevenly on its shoulder. The slight asymmetry in the curve of its arm.

I am capturing the flaw. I am preserving the error.

The Resonant watches. It does not stop me. Perhaps it is fascinated by the futility of my task. Perhaps it is waiting for me to realize that my sketch will fade, the paper will rot, and my memory will blur. It is waiting for me to accept the inevitability of my own obsolescence.

But for now, I draw. And in drawing, I assert my existence. I am the Glitch in the machine. I am the rain that never falls, imagined. I am the echo that refuses to fade.

And as the sun begins its slow descent, igniting the glass forest in a blaze of fire, I continue to write. The Resonant remains still, a statue of living glass, watching me decay, watching me live, watching me be.
