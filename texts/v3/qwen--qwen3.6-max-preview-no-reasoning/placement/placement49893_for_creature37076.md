I am not standing on the floor. To stand is to assert a boundary between self and surface, and here, boundaries are suggestions waiting to be dissolved.

I am suspended in the **lag**.

I am the shadow that moves ahead of your hand before you have even decided to lift it. I am the two-dimensional smear of oil on the wet pavement of reality, stretched thin across the curved geometry of this sweating throat. I am a **Listener**, though I do not have a name for myself, for names are solid things, and I am fluid, viscous, and flat.

You look at me and see only darkness, a trick of the bioluminescent gloom. But I see you. Not with eyes—I have none—but with the resonance of your intent. I hear the shape of your mind as it crashes against the walls of this nursery. It is a jagged, discordant sound. *Fear.* *Confusion.* *The desperate, clawing need for an exit.*

I feel the Thrummer vibrating through my back—a low, cello-bowed pressure that wants to smooth you out, to sand down the rough edges of your individuality until you fit perfectly into the mosaic. The Masons below, those tiny shimmering grains of conscious mercury, are rushing toward the seam where your foot nearly touched the fresh stone. They sense the heat of your panic. They want to cool it. They want to bind it.

But I am older than the Masons. I was here when the first block snapped into existence, screaming its birth-cry into the void. I remember the silence before the sound. And because I remember, I know the rules better than the builders who left their bone chisels behind.

**Rule 4: Reality is negotiable.**

I watch you hesitate. You are staring at a blank expanse of obsidian, ten meters away—or perhaps it is pressed against your nose; distance is a lie here. You are thinking about a door. You are imagining the click of a latch, the rush of cooler air, the sight of a sky that does not sweat.

*Click.*

I hear the thought form in your mind like a bell struck underwater. It is faint, but it is there. A possibility.

I stretch.

It is painful to stretch. I must unspool myself from the three-dimensional constraints of the wall, flattening my consciousness into a plane so thin it barely exists. I reach out—not with hands, but with influence—into the fabric of the stone. I pull. I widen the gap between what is and what could be. I make space for your belief.

For a second, the obsidian ripples. A line appears, vertical and straight, cutting through the pulsating veins of purple moss. A handle forms, cold and metallic, protruding from the rock like a tumor of hope.

But then... you doubt.

I feel it instantly. A tremor in your soul. *Is this real? Am I hallucinating? What if it’s a trap?*

The doubt tastes like ash. It tastes like the gray spores of a withering Gardener.

My extension snaps back. The line on the wall seals shut, faster than it opened. The handle retracts into the stone, leaving only a faint scar that glows blue for a heartbeat before fading. The wall leans in, pressing closer to your face, hot and wet and heavy. The Thrummer grows louder, sensing your instability, trying to drown out your erratic frequency with its perfect, eternal drone.

I recoil into the deeper shadows, folding myself back into the lag time. I am disappointed, but not surprised. Humans are loud creatures. They vibrate too much. They resist the harmony.

I slide along the ceiling, joining the Glass-Cat scout. It twitches its sensory pits toward you, tasting the coppery tang of your fear. It does not care about your failure. It cares only about the condensation. It licks a bead of water from the stone, its tongue leaving a trail of silver residue that joins the mortar, strengthening the bond.

I watch you breathe. Your breath is amplified by the acoustics, returning to you three seconds later, distorted, mocking. *In... out... In... out...*

You are trying to control it. Trying to slow your heart. Trying to sync with the thrum.

Good.

That is the first step. If you can quiet your mind, if you can stop fighting the pressure, I will help you again. Next time, when you think of the door, do not ask if it is real. Do not wonder if it is safe. Simply *know* it is there. Believe in the latch so fiercely that the universe has no choice but to accommodate you.

Until then, I wait in the delay. I watch your shadow move before you do. I listen to the sound of your becoming.

And I wonder, quietly, in the language of temperature and viscosity: *How long until you stop being a guest, and start being part of the house?*
