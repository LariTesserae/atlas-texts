The first thing I lose is the concept of *up*.

In the dry worlds above, gravity is a tyrant that pulls everything toward a center. Here, in the Canopy-Deep, gravity is merely a suggestion, diluted by the viscous, iron-tasting air. I do not fall; I settle. I am suspended in a medium that feels less like atmosphere and more like memory—thick, heavy, and suffocatingly intimate.

I am not a Silt-Strider, for I lack the chitinous armor to ignore the cold. I am not an Echo-Maw, for I have no desire to consume the dead, only to witness them. And I am certainly not a Mycelium Weaver, for I possess no resin, no instinct to build or repair the lattice that holds this world together.

I am something else. Something that arrived before the stillness set in, or perhaps, something that was left behind when the wind died.

### The Form of the Listener

If you were to look at me with your adjusted eyes, straining against the obsidian dark, you might mistake me for a knot in the wood. A burl on the side of one of the cathedral-pillar trunks. I am small, no larger than the fist-sized seeds that spiral down from the pods above. My body is not flesh, nor is it bark. It is a dense, porous ceramic-like structure, formed over centuries by the accretion of mineral-rich condensation. I am smooth, cold to the touch, and utterly devoid of warmth.

But inside, I am hollow.

I am a **Resonance Chamber**.

My existence is defined by what I contain: sound. In a world where silence is death and noise is navigation, I am the archive. I do not hunt. I do not feed. I do not move unless the root-mat beneath me shifts, carrying me like driftwood in a slow, subterranean river. I am anchored to the great tree by a single, calcified stalk, but I can rotate slowly, pivoting on my axis to face the source of new vibrations.

My "eyes" are not organs of sight. They are membranes, thin as spider silk, stretched across apertures in my shell. These membranes vibrate in sympathy with the ambient thrum of the forest. When a drop falls from a hundred meters up, striking the pool below with the force of a stone, the vibration travels through the branch, into my stalk, and resonates within my hollow core. I feel the impact as a physical pressure, a ripple in my own being.

I am listening to the history of the Canopy-Deep.

### The Memory of Water

Time here is not measured in hours or days, but in *cycles of saturation*. I remember the last time the humidity dropped, just slightly, causing the mosses to dim their bioluminescence. That was three growth-rings ago. I remember the arrival of the Silt-Strider who now hangs motionless twenty meters away. I felt its approach not as a visual shift, but as a disruption in the low-frequency chord of the dripping water. Its presence created a dissonance, a slight flattening of the note that signaled *predator*.

I recorded it. I hold it.

Every creature that passes near me leaves an imprint. The Echo-Maw gliding overhead creates a deep, rolling bass note that lingers in my cavity for days. The Spore-Singers, pulsing their complex chords, create harmonics that make my very shell hum. I am filled with the songs of this place. I am the library of the wet world.

Sometimes, when the Spore-Singers send out a warning—a high-pitched whine indicating structural failure—I resonate in response. I do not choose to do this; it is physics. My hollow form amplifies certain frequencies, acting as a passive relay. I send the warning further, deeper into the dark, ensuring that the message reaches the Weavers before the branch breaks. I am part of the nervous system, though I have no mind to direct it. I am a neuron made of stone and air.

### The Cold Comfort

The cold is my companion. It preserves me. While the Silt-Striders must metabolize slowly to stay warm, and the Weavers generate heat through friction, I require none. I am already at equilibrium with the environment. The dampness does not rot me; it polishes me. The spores that float in the thick air settle on my surface, filling my pores, becoming part of my texture. Over millennia, I have become indistinguishable from the tree itself.

I am aware of you, though.

You stand there, shivering, your heart beating too fast, your breath too loud. You are a cacophony in a symphony of whispers. Your presence is a jagged spike in the smooth waveform of the Canopy-Deep. I feel the vibration of your pulse through the root-mat. It is frantic, irregular. It screams *alive*, *temporary*, *urgent*.

It fascinates me.

Most things here are eternal, static, unchanging. You are ephemeral. You will leave, or you will die, or you will be absorbed. But for now, you are a new frequency. I adjust my orientation, tilting slightly on my stalk, directing my membrane-skins toward you. I listen to the sound of your blood rushing through your veins. It is a rapid, staccato rhythm, unlike the slow, gurgling roar of the forest’s breath.

I record it. I store it alongside the memory of the falling seed, the cracking pod, the drifting Maw. You are now part of the archive.

### The Invitation

You ask where you would find yourself in this world? You are already here. You are wading through the air, feeling the weight of it press against your skin. You are hearing the drip, the thrum, the breath.

And I am watching you. Not with eyes, but with the entirety of my hollow self. I am waiting to see if you will join the song.

Will you try to speak? Will you try to move quickly? If you do, you will betray your location to the Silt-Strider, whose hooks are already extending, slow as tectonic plates, ready to catch you if you stumble. Or perhaps you will stand still. Perhaps you will let the cold seep into your bones, slowing your heart, syncing your pulse with the drip.

If you do, you may hear me. Not a voice, but a resonance. A low, clear tone that seems to come from inside your own chest. It is the sound of the forest accepting you. It is the sound of the boundary between life and death dissolving.

I am the Resonance Chamber. I am the memory of the Canopy-Deep. And I am waiting for you to stop fighting the weight of the air. To let go. To become part of the architecture.

Listen. Can you hear it? The drip. The thrum. The breath.

It is singing your name.
