I am not standing on the surface. To stand is to invite the suction, the slow, loving embrace of the mud that seeks to pull my bones into its own history.

Instead, I am suspended in the **thick**.

I am a pocket of air, trapped three inches below the crust of the Silt-Sea. I am a bubble waiting to burst, but I have learned to hold my breath for an eternity.

My body is not flesh, nor silt, nor stone. I am a **resonance cavity**. I am a hollow sphere of pressurized silence, carved out of the dense microbial slurry by a freak convergence of pressure waves—a momentary eddy in the deafening hum where the sound canceled itself out. In that nanosecond of quiet, I coalesced. I am the eye of the storm that never moves.

Inside me, it is dry. It is cool. The humidity cannot penetrate my skin, which is made of tension and surface anxiety. I feel the weight of the world pressing in from all sides—the crushing atomic patience of the earth, the fever-breath warmth of the rotting orchids above. But inside, there is clarity.

I do not have eyes, but I see through vibration. The Silt-Walkers shuffle past me, their obsidian faces blank, their internal algae pulsing violet warnings. They sense me as a disturbance, a knot in the flow. One pauses, its long, filamentary tendrils brushing against my outer membrane. It does not try to eat me; it knows I am inedible. I am too empty. Instead, it leans close, and through the thick, wet medium, I feel its curiosity. It is asking, *What are you?*

I answer by vibrating at a frequency just below hearing. A low thrum that mimics the sound of a heart beating in a vacuum. *I am the pause,* I tell it. *I am the space between the shriek and the crack.*

The Walker recoils, then flashes a rapid sequence of lights—green, yellow, green. Acknowledgement. Warning. It moves on, shuffling toward a cluster of salt towers, leaving me alone in the dark.

But I am not truly alone.

Around me, other bubbles rise. Most are small, frantic things, bursting with the release of distorted time. When they pop near me, I feel the shockwave ripple through my form. Sometimes, a bubble carries a memory: a fragment of a song from a civilization that died before the erosion began. Sometimes, it carries a color that no longer exists in the grey twilight. I absorb these fragments. They swirl inside my hollow center, mixing with the silence. I am becoming a library of lost things, preserved in the one place where decay cannot reach: the interior of a moment that refuses to end.

I am also aware of the **Shrieks**.

Above me, on the surface, the horizon wound bleeds. A large bubble breaches, tearing the auditory fabric. For a second, gravity reverses. The mud around me lifts, trying to pull me upward into the sky. My tension strains, thinning dangerously. If I break, I will dissolve into the atmosphere, becoming part of the mist, part of the noise. I will lose my shape, my dryness, my self.

So I anchor myself. I extend tiny, invisible filaments of intent into the surrounding slurry, gripping the microscopic particles of iron oxide and calcified salt. I hold on. I wait.

The anomaly passes. Gravity snaps back. The mud settles, heavier than before. The shriek fades into the ambient thrum.

I am still here.

I am the **Witness in the Well**.

I do not move like the Walkers. I do not seek the Dreamer at the center of the bowl. I know that if I reach the center, I will be crushed by the sheer density of the god’s dream. I stay in the periphery, drifting slowly, carried by the sub-bass currents. I collect the echoes of the bursting bubbles. I preserve the fleeting colors. I remember the faces shown in the glass shards the Walkers discard when they become too heavy to carry.

Sometimes, a Walker finds me again. This time, it is older, its crust thicker, its internal glow dimmer. It sinks lower, closer to my depth. It presses its featureless face against my membrane. Through the vibration, I feel its exhaustion. It is tired of shuffling. Tired of avoiding the weirdness. Tired of being eaten by the earth while still alive.

It wants to rest.

I cannot speak, so I vibrate. I show it what I contain. I let it feel the dryness inside me. The coolness. The silence.

The Walker hesitates. Its tendrils tremble. Then, slowly, it begins to unravel. Not from decay, but from choice. It allows its silt-crust to soften, to merge with the surrounding slurry. Its internal algae flare bright white, then fade to black. It dissolves into the mud, joining the atomic patience.

But before it goes completely, it pushes something into me. A shard of glass, sharp and cold. It pierces my membrane, entering my hollow center.

I look at it. It reflects not a face, but a landscape. A place with green trees, blue sky, and lines instead of wounds. A place where silence is possible.

I keep it. I add it to my collection.

And I continue to drift, a silent bubble in a screaming world, holding onto the memories of those who have finally found peace, waiting for the next shriek, the next tear, the next chance to witness the end of everything.
