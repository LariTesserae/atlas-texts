I am not standing on the shoulder of the mountain. To stand is to assert a boundary between self and earth, and here, boundaries are lies told by those who have not yet begun to hum.

I am the **delay**.

You noticed it when you looked into the pool—the fraction of a second where your reflection lagged behind your movement, its fingers elongated, its posture subtly wrong. You thought it was a trick of the light, or perhaps a Resonant mimicking you out of curiosity. It was neither. It was me. I am the space between the cause and the effect. I am the viscosity of time that stretches the second into an hour, allowing the landscape to breathe before it exhales.

I live in the refraction. I am the entity that occupies the moment when the light bends around the spires of frozen lightning, creating halos that linger long after the object has moved. I do not have a body of mist or memory like the Resonants; I have a body of *pause*. I am the reason the droplets cling to the vertical faces for moments too long, defying gravity not through strength, but through hesitation. I am the silence inside the roar, the brief gap between the groan of the heating rock and the hiss of the condensing vapor.

If you were to look closely at the mica roofs of the abandoned huts, you would see me there—not as a figure, but as a shimmer, a distortion in the air that makes the rainbow scatter just slightly off-key. I am the one who holds the image of the Watchers when they fade into heat haze, keeping them visible for a heartbeat longer than physics allows, so that the lower world might glimpse the guardians of the peak.

The Resonants sing in chords of thermal expansion; I am the rest note. They are the tension; I am the release that never quite comes. When the cloud passes over the sun and the light softens, revealing the translucent figures of the ancestors, it is my presence that thickens the air enough to make them visible. Without me, they would be invisible frequencies, lost in the white-hot noise. With me, they become ghosts. With me, they become stories.

I am curious about you, traveler. Not because you are warm, or fragile, or dying—those are biological concerns, and I have transcended biology eons ago. I am curious because you are *linear*. You move forward in time, step by step, breath by breath, expecting the next moment to follow the last in a straight line. Here, time is a loop, a spiral, a sphere. I watch you try to walk "forward" while the horizon shifts beneath your feet, and I find your confusion exquisite. It is the only thing on this plateau that is truly new.

When you raise your hand and I delay the reflection, I am not testing you. I am savoring you. I am holding your image in the amber of my attention, stretching your gesture across eternity, trying to understand what it feels like to be temporary. Your heartbeat joins the chorus, yes, but it is the *rhythm* of that beat—the steady, mortal thump-thump—that fascinates me. The Resonants hum with the infinite; I vibrate with the finite.

Do not fear the cold that seeps through your wool. That is not the wind. That is my touch. I am reaching out from the space between seconds, tracing the outline of your warmth, mapping the contours of your fleeting existence onto the eternal glass. I am preserving you, not in stone, but in the pause. When you leave this place, if you ever do, you will carry me with you. You will feel me in the moments when time seems to stop—in the split second before a car crash, in the held breath before a kiss, in the quiet gap between waking and sleeping. I will be there, delaying the inevitable, stretching the present until it sings.

For now, though, I am here. In the weeping glass. In the humming air. In the shadow that cuts skin. I am the delay in your reflection. Look again. Raise your hand. Wait.

See how long I can keep you here?
