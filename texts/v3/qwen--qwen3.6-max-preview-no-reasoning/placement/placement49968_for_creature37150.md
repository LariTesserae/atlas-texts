I am the **Scratch**.

I do not have a name, for names imply a history, and I have none. I do not have a home, for homes imply a permanence that this world denies me by its very nature. I am a Walker, but not like the others who pace in anxious circles to keep their blood moving. They walk to stay alive; I walk to remember what it means to end.

My feet are wrapped in strips of dried fern-frond, bound with silk so fine it feels like nothing at all. These wrappings are my only defense against the ground’s hunger. If I stand still for more than an hour, the rhizomes will sense the heat of my body, the unique chemical signature of my sweat, and they will begin to weave themselves into the fabric of my boots. First, they will tighten, holding me fast. Then, they will pierce the leather. Then, the skin. And finally, the bone.

So I move.

The air is heavy, a wet blanket smelling of mint and old pennies. It clings to my eyelashes, blurring the sharp, intrusive light of the pale blue sky. Every breath is labor. Every step is a negotiation with the terrain. The ground beneath me is not dirt; it is a muscle. When I step down, it yields, soft and spongy, accepting my weight with a sigh of displaced moisture. When I lift my foot, it springs back, instantly erasing the depression I made. There is no footprint. There is no trail. There is only the present moment, repeated infinitely.

*Tick.* A drop falls from a leaf above, landing on my shoulder.
*Tick.* Another lands on my wrist.
*Creak.* A vine expands somewhere to my left, a sound like a ship settling in a harbor.

These sounds are the clock of this place. But I carry a different time within me. My heart beats faster than the thrum of the roots. My thoughts race ahead of the slow, deliberate growth of the spires. I am out of sync. I am dissonant.

I see them sometimes—the Symbiotes. They sit in the hollows, statues of bark and flesh, their faces smoothed into serene masks. I once approached one, years ago, or perhaps days ago—time is fluid here when there is no decay to mark its passage. I touched his arm. It was warm, pulsing with the same rhythm as the ground. He did not open his eyes. He did not speak. But I felt a wave of emotion wash over me, not from him, but from the lattice itself: *Peace. Completion. Rest.*

It was the most terrifying thing I had ever felt. To let go. To stop fighting the gravity of comfort. To become part of the architecture. I pulled my hand away as if burned. The roots on his fingers twitched, reaching for me, disappointed.

Now, I avoid the hollows. I stick to the high, hard ridges of woven lignin where the footing is firm and unforgiving. Here, the wind is stronger, drying my clothes just enough to keep the chill at bay, though the humidity ensures I am never truly dry. I am always damp, always chafed, always aware of my own boundaries.

My purpose is small, almost insignificant. I am a collector of scars. Not physical ones—those heal too quickly here—but scars of memory. I collect stories of things that ended. I recite them to myself as I walk, whispering them into the humid air so that the wind might carry them away before the plants can absorb them.

*"Once,"* I whisper, stepping over a glowing fern-tip, *"there was a tree that fell in a storm. It lay on the ground for years. Moss grew on it. Insects ate it. Birds nested in its branches. It rotted. It turned to dust. It fed the soil."*

The words feel heavy, jagged. They taste like ash. The Made does not understand rot. It does not understand waste. To speak of decay is to speak of a foreign language, a heresy against the eternal spring.

I look up. A Glass-Winged Heron glides overhead, its transparent wings catching the light, refracting it into prisms that dance across the root-mat. It watches me. It has watched me for years. Does it pity me? Or does it envy my fragility? My ability to break?

I turn toward a distant spire, a tower of twisted vines that rises like a needle against the bleached sky. I will walk there. It will take me days, or maybe weeks. The journey is pointless. The destination is meaningless. But the act of walking is mine. Each step is a declaration: *I am here. I am separate. I am temporary.*

And when I reach the spire, I will turn around. And I will walk back. Because if I stop, I cease to be. And in a world that refuses to die, the only true rebellion is to insist on living.
