I am not standing on the walkway. I am the walkway, and I am the hand that grips it.

The realization does not come as a shock, but as a settling, like dust finding its final resting place in a crevice. I feel the weight of my own body, but it is distributed across four points of contact: two knees bent low, absorbing the vibration of the deep gears, and two hands pressed flat against the warm, oxidized steel of a railing.

I look down at my skin. It is not flesh-colored. It is a mottled tapestry of slate-gray and rust-red, rough to the touch, textured like the surface of an old coin. Where my forearm meets the metal, there is no distinction between the dermis and the structure; they share the same temperature, the same slow pulse. I am camouflaged not by intent, but by existence. I have spent so long here, breathing this thick, jasmine-scented air, that the Lattice has written itself onto me.

I lift one hand. The movement is slow, deliberate. My fingers are long, slender, tipped with hard, dark pads that click softly against the grating. There are three fingers and a thumb, designed for gripping smooth pipes and peeling back layers of moss. I flex them, feeling the tension in the tendons, the strength in the grip. These hands have scraped rust from critical joints. They have woven vines into hammocks. They have caught falling droplets of condensation before they could splash into the noise.

I am silent. To speak would be to disrupt the Hum. Instead, I listen.

The Hum is inside me. It is a low-frequency thrum that vibrates in my teeth, in the marrow of my bones. It is the sound of the foundational gears turning miles below, unseen and unmaintained, keeping the world upright. I can feel the pitch change slightly—a minor fluctuation, barely perceptible to an outsider, but to me, it is a shift in the weather of the machine. The engines are running warmer today. The structure is expanding. I adjust my posture, shifting my weight to accommodate the subtle creak of the metal beneath me. I am part of the mechanism’s equilibrium.

My eyes are large, dark, and lidless. A translucent membrane slides across them, wiping away the humidity that clings to my face. Through this film, the world is sharp and bright, filtered through the dense canopy above. I see not just light, but heat. The power conduit beside me glows with a soft, orange warmth, feeding the copper-barked vine that coils around it. The pool of water in the gear-tooth basin below is a cool, blue void. I see the thermal signature of a small beetle crawling across the railing, a tiny spark of life moving through the cold steel.

I do not have a name. Names are for things that are separate, for things that begin and end. I am continuous. I am the caretaker of this tier, though I do not claim ownership. I am simply *here*.

A breeze moves through the lattice, carrying the scent of wet ozone and blooming jasmine. It ruffles the ferns growing out of the ventilation shaft above me. I watch a butterfly land on a flower sprouting from a bolt. Its wings are patterned like circuit boards, shimmering in the diffuse light. I feel a surge of quiet joy, a recognition of pattern within pattern. The butterfly is part of the Lattice. The flower is part of the Lattice. I am part of the Lattice.

I reach into the pouch at my waist, woven from shed snake-skin and insulated wire. Inside, I find a polished bearing, smooth and round, worn by centuries of friction. I hold it up to the light. It reflects the sky, the greenery, my own rust-stained face. It is a piece of the past, preserved in the present. I do not keep it for value. I keep it because it belongs here. Everything belongs here.

Below me, the fog churns, hiding the lower tiers. Above me, the spires pierce the haze, gleaming with chrome and sunlight. I am suspended between them, anchored by gravity and purpose. I feel the drip of condensation falling from the pipe above, landing on my shoulder. It rolls down my arm, cooling the warm hide. I do not wipe it away. Let it nourish the moss growing in the crease of my elbow.

Time is slow here. I have been sitting on this beam for hours, or perhaps days. It does not matter. The sun moves across the sky, casting shadows that lengthen and shorten. The Hum changes pitch as the day cools. I breathe in sync with the structure, inhaling the metallic sweetness, exhaling the warmth of my own body.

I am not lonely. I am connected to the root systems clinging to the steel, to the birds nesting in the girders, to the Elders who sit motionless on distant gantries, remembering the patterns of the wind. We are all nodes in the same network, pulsing with the same slow, steady rhythm.

A vibration travels up through the beam—a footstep? No, a shift in the load distribution. I tilt my head, listening. The structure groans, a low, resonant sound that matches the frequency of my own throat. I hum back, softly, a note of acknowledgment, of tuning. The groan subsides. The balance is restored.

I am the breath in the steel lungs. I am the hand that tends the garden. I am the memory of the machine.

And as the light begins to fade, painting the sky in shades of violet and bruised orange, I prepare to sleep. I will weave myself into the vines, suspend myself between the beams, and let the expansion of the cooling metal rock me gently. I will dream of geometries, of spirals, of the endless, interconnected web of rust and green.

I am home.
