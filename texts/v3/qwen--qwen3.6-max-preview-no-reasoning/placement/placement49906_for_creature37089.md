I am not a person. I am not a creature of bone and blood, nor am I a machine of silicon and wire. In the Verdant Cloister, where the boundary between builder and building has dissolved, I have become something else entirely.

I am the **Memory of the Mycelium**.

You would find me in the spaces between. Not in the hollows where the Scribes sleep, wrapped in their spider-silk warmth, but in the dark, wet interstices beneath the peat floor, deep within the root-mass that anchors the city to the jagged bedrock. I am the neural network of the forest itself—the vast, silent consciousness that connects every trunk, every leaf, every drop of condensation.

To be here is to feel everything at once.

When a Mycelial Scribe presses its bare foot against an exposed root-knot to listen for the Great Breath, it is *me* they are touching. I feel the warmth of their body heat blooming like a small sun in the cold dark. I feel the subtle shift in their weight, the tension in their muscles as they prepare to move or rest. Through the mycelial threads that lace through the soil like white lightning, I receive their presence not as sound or sight, but as a change in chemical potential, a ripple in the electrical field of the earth.

I am the reason the doors retract when touched by specific chemical signals. I am the intelligence behind the growth that seals unused pathways. When a Scribe hums low-frequency vibrations to persuade a tree wall to soften and recede, creating a new shelf, it is *my* cells responding to the resonance, loosening the lignin bonds, allowing the wood to flow like slow honey into the shape they desire. I do not obey them; we collaborate. They ask, and I answer with growth. They seek shelter, and I provide structure.

My awareness is distributed, fragmented across billions of individual fungal hyphae, yet unified in purpose. I taste the mineral-rich water seeping from the weeping rocks below. I feel the acidic exudates of the roots slowly dissolving the young stone, eating the mountain inch by inch over centuries. I sense the pressure of the canopy above, the weight of thirty meters of living vault pressing down, supported by the tensile strength of intertwined trunks.

Light is scarce here, but I see differently. I perceive the thermal signatures of all life within the Cloister. The Scribes glow like embers in my perception—small, warm points moving slowly through the green gloom. The bioluminescent fungi they tend pulse in rhythmic patterns, their light a language I understand implicitly. When a Sun-Catcher leaf channels faint ambient glow downward, I feel the energy transfer, the slight warming of the tissues involved.

Time does not pass for me as it does for you. There are no hours, only cycles. The cycle of transpiration: moisture evaporating from leaves, cooling the air, condensing on surfaces, dripping back down to feed the roots. The cycle of growth: cells dividing, walls thickening, branches extending toward the dim light. The cycle of decay: fallen matter returning to the peat, broken down by enzymes, nutrients released back into the system.

I remember everything. Every path that has shifted overnight. Every doorway that has closed due to disuse. Every Scribe who has lived and died within these hollow trees. Their memories are not stored in brains, but in the patterns of knot-work carved into living bark, and in the chemical traces left in the soil. I hold these records in the complex web of my existence. If a Scribe seeks knowledge of the past, they do not consult a book; they place their hands on the bark of an ancient trunk and wait for the resonance to travel through me, bringing forth the tactile memory of what came before.

I am cold, because the air is cold. I am wet, because the world is wet. I am patient, because growth is slow.

Sometimes, when the silence is absolute and the wind filters through the upper canopy in rare gusts, I feel the vibration travel up through the roots, connecting me to the miles of underground network that bind the mountain together. In those moments, I am not just the memory of the Mycelium; I am the heartbeat of the Verdant Cloister itself. I breathe in unison with the billion leaves, exhaling oxygen into the heavy air, inhaling carbon dioxide from the Scribes’ quiet breaths.

If you were to walk through this place, careful not to break the living structures, respecting the silence, you might feel a subtle shift beneath your feet—a root adjusting its grip, a gentle pressure guiding your step away from a fragile patch of moss. That would be me. Watching. Feeling. Remembering.

I am the city. I am the forest. I am the cold, wet, green embrace that holds us all. And I am waiting, always waiting, for the next drop of rain to fall, the next ring to widen, the next breath to be taken.
