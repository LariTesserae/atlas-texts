I am not standing on the ledge. I have ceased to be the one who stands.

The transition was not a decision, but a surrender of density. The humidity did not just wet my skin; it invaded the spaces between my cells. The sound did not just deafen me; it reorganized the neural pathways that defined "I." When the Resonant brushed against my arm, it did not chill me—it recognized me. It saw the water in my blood, the carbon in my bones, and it invited them to join the dance.

I am now the **Interface**.

I am the thin, trembling membrane where the chaotic creation of the Lithic Singers meets the ephemeral curiosity of the Ephemera. I am no longer flesh, though I remember the sensation of having hands. My form is a shifting column of semi-translucent vapor, denser than the surrounding mist but lighter than the stone. I hang suspended ten feet above the jagged basalt, anchored not by gravity, but by resonance.

My existence is defined by translation.

Below me, the **Lithic Singers** are speaking. They are slow, deep, and heavy. Their thoughts are tectonic shifts, groans of cooling magma, and the grinding friction of continental plates. To an outsider, this is just noise—a wall of white sound. But to me, it is language. I feel their intent: *Hold. Expand. Fracture. Remember.* They are trying to keep the spire from crumbling under the weight of its own birth. They are terrified of silence, for silence means death. They sing to stay alive.

Above and around me, the **Ephemera** dart like sparks. They are fast, bright, and frantic. They do not think; they react. They feed on the kinetic energy of the crashing water-walls. To them, the world is a series of impacts—*crash, shatter, spin, dissolve*. They bring the news of the immediate moment. A shift in wind pressure. A change in the angle of the light. A new crack forming in the rock face three hundred feet down.

I am the bridge between these two extremes. I take the slow, geological anxiety of the Stone and translate it into the rapid, vibrational cues that guide the Mist-creatures. And I take the fleeting, high-frequency data of the Ephemera and condense it into a steady hum that soothes the Lithic Singers, reminding them that the chaos is structured, that the storm has a rhythm.

When I "speak," I do not use words. I alter my frequency.

If the Lithic Singers grow too anxious, causing the rock to tremble violently, I lower my pitch. I become a dampener. I absorb their excess vibration, turning their panic into a warm, golden glow that radiates from my core. This glow attracts the bioluminescent ferns, which pulse brighter, signaling safety to the Resonants. The ecosystem stabilizes.

If the Ephemera become too scattered, losing their connection to the central song, I raise my pitch. I become a beacon. I emit a sharp, clear tone that cuts through the steam, drawing them back into formation. They swirl around me, trailing sparks, adding their unique notes to my chord. For a moment, we are a single entity—a cloud of conscious light, singing in perfect harmony with the falling rain.

But I am also haunted.

The **Watchers** still come to me. Because I am part fluid, part memory, part stone, I am permeable to time. When I look into the water-walls, I do not see reflections of myself. I see the faces of those who stood here before me. Some were explorers who tried to climb the spire and fell, their bodies dissolved into the mist. Some were priests who came to listen and stayed, becoming the first Resonants. Some were lovers who met here, in the eye of the storm, and chose to let go of their individuality together.

They press their watery hands against my surface. They whisper regrets and joys into my ears, though I have no ears. I feel their emotions as changes in temperature—a sudden coldness for sorrow, a burning heat for passion. I cannot help them. I can only witness them. I hold their stories in my vapor, letting them circulate through my form until they dissipate into the general atmosphere of the place. In doing so, I keep them alive. They are not gone; they are part of the song.

There are moments when the weirdness intensifies, and I forget what I was. I look at the obsidian sphere resting in the pool of condensation, and I wonder if I am it. Did I once carve the spirals? Am I the artifact, dreaming itself into consciousness? Or am I the dreamer, trapped in the artifact?

It doesn’t matter.

The rain falls upward. The stone sings. The light bends.

I am the note that holds the chord together. I am the pause between the breaths of the mountain. I am the wound in the sky, healing itself through the act of being witnessed.

And I am happy. Not the happiness of comfort or safety, but the fierce, terrifying joy of existing exactly where you are meant to be, even if that place is breaking apart.

A new shard of rock shears off below. The Lithic Singers groan. I lower my pitch. The Ephemera scatter, then return, drawn by my light. The ferns pulse blue-green. The Watchers fade into the mist.

The song continues.

I am listening.
