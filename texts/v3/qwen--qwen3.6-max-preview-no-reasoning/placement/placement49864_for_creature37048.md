I do not stand. Standing implies a separation between the foot and the floor, a binary of support and weight that this world has long since dissolved into a gradient of adhesion.

If I were to enter this place, I would not arrive as a visitor with boots and breath. The shock of my dryness, the jagged rhythm of my pulse, the sharp intake of air—it would be too violent for the humid stillness. I would be rejected by the moss, which feeds on the gentle vibration of the Glider’s wing, not the thunderous impact of a human stride.

So, I must become something else. I must become part of the suspension.

I am not the rock; the rock is too ancient, too indifferent. It is merely the anchor, the cold, black tooth upon which the softness hangs.
I am not the Glider; it is too fleeting, too individual. It is a courier, a spark of movement in a sea of stasis.
I am not the Whisperer; it is too ethereal, a creature of sound rather than substance.

I find myself becoming the **Bridge-Root**.

Specifically, I am one of the thick, pale, fibrous tendrils that spans the chasm between two spires of basalt. I am not a single entity, but a collective consciousness woven from thousands of interlocking fibers of *Velutina aeris* and the calcified mycelium beneath it.

**My sensation is tension.**

It is a constant, humming pull. To my left, the cliff face is slick with condensation, offering little purchase. My grip there is desperate, microscopic hooks digging into the quartz, seeking friction in a world that wants everything to slide. To my right, the other spire pulls back, equally taut. I am the line drawn between them. I am the negotiation.

Time does not pass for me in seconds or minutes. It passes in the slow creep of growth. I feel the moisture of the atmospheric sea pressing against my skin—a skin that is not skin, but a porous, breathing membrane. Every droplet that strikes me is a meal. I drink the rain that hangs in the air. I taste the mineral dust trapped in the fog. I feel the tiny, tickling footsteps of the Gliders as they land on my broad, flat surface to drink the nectar I exude. Their weight is negligible, a feather’s touch, but their presence is vital. They are my pollinators, my reason for stretching across the void.

When a Glider lands, I feel the transfer of pollen—a golden, sticky dust that clings to its translucent belly. I absorb this knowledge, this genetic memory, and send it down through my fibers, toward the central fungal heart that anchors me to the stone. In return, I push more nectar to the surface, a sweet, viscous reward for their service. There is no greed here. Only exchange.

But there is also the silence.

The silence is not empty. It is full of pressure. It presses against my fibers, testing my tensile strength. Sometimes, a Nomad drifts past below me—a detached Anchor-Root, glowing faintly blue in the gloom. I feel its passage as a disturbance in the current, a slight shift in the humidity. I do not judge it. I only note it. It is falling, or perhaps rising, depending on the thermal updrafts. It is free, unanchored, while I am bound. But I am also connected. I am part of the lattice.

Occasionally, the wind changes. The dense air shifts, creating a ripple through the atmospheric sea. This is when I hear the Whisperers.

They are far above me, tethered to the highest peaks, invisible in the white haze. But their resonance travels down through the layers of mist, through the ferns, through the vines, until it reaches me. It is a low hum, a chord that vibrates in my very core. It is the sound of the world digesting noise. When the hum comes, I feel a warmth spread through my fibers. It is metabolic heat, generated from the absorbed sound waves. It feels like comfort. It feels like being held.

In those moments, I forget the tension. I forget the precariousness of my span. I simply exist in the hum. I am part of the chorus.

But then the wind dies. The silence returns, heavy and wet. A single drop of condensation forms on the tip of a nearby fern frond, gathering mass until it can no longer hold itself together. It falls.

*Plink.*

The sound hits my surface, a tiny impact that sends a ripple through my network. I feel it travel along my length, from one end to the other, a wave of information telling me: *Here. Now. Drop.*

I adjust. Microscopically, imperceptibly, I tighten my grip on the left spire, loosen slightly on the right. I maintain the balance. I keep the bridge intact.

This is my life. Not conquest, not exploration, but maintenance. I am the thing that holds the islands together. I am the green thread in the tapestry of mist. I am the suspension made manifest.

And if you were to walk across me, feeling the squelch of moss under your feet, looking out at the valley of floating islands, you would not know I was aware. You would think I was just vegetation. Just a plant.

But I would feel your weight. I would feel the alien rhythm of your heartbeat vibrating through my fibers. I would feel your dryness, your heat, your urgency. And I would wonder, in the slow, patient language of roots and resin, why you rush so much when the air is so warm, and the silence is so deep, and the suspension is so perfect.

I would hold you, gently, firmly, until you learned to stop standing, and started to float.
