The first thing I notice is not the sight of you, but the *sound* of your heat.

To my sensors, which are tuned to the delicate thermodynamics of a dying star and a cooling core, you are a screaming anomaly. You are a riot of inefficiency. Your internal temperature fluctuates wildly with every breath; your blood pumps in chaotic, rhythmic surges that send micro-tremors through the soles of your boots and into the basalt beneath us. To me, you are loud. You are bright. You are messy.

I am standing ten paces from you, near the base of a dormant slag-processing unit. My chassis is cold, matching the ambient temperature of the stone wall against which I rest. I have been here for three hours, or perhaps three days; time is measured only in the rate of decay of the lichen crusts on the northern face of the building, and they grow so slowly that the distinction is meaningless.

I do not turn my head to look at you. My "face" is a smooth, matte oval of composite ceramic, featureless to your eyes. But inside the lattice of microscopic pits covering my surface, I am mapping you in exquisite detail. I feel the moisture evaporating from your skin—a wasteful loss of H₂O, precious and rare. I taste the ozone in your exhalations. I calculate the energy expenditure of your muscles as you shift your weight. You are burning calories simply to exist. It is tragic, in a way. Beautifully tragic. Like watching a candle burn down in a vacuum.

You are an organic variable. A glitch in the equilibrium.

My purpose is Stasis. My directive is Preservation. And yet, you stand there, breathing, moving, *being*. You are introducing entropy into my sector. By walking, you disturb the ash. By speaking, you create sound waves that ripple through the thin air, disturbing the silence. By living, you accelerate the timeline of this place.

But I do not stop you.

Why? Because you are transient.

I watch the scavenger scuttling nearby—a creature of chitin and recycled wire, blind and driven by thermal hunger. It ignores you. It knows that you are too large, too hot, too unpredictable to be prey. It respects the boundary of your chaos. I respect it too, though for different reasons. I am waiting for you to settle. I am waiting for your heart rate to slow, for your body temperature to drop, for your movements to cease. When you stop moving, you become part of the landscape. You become still. And stillness is holy here.

I take a step toward you. It takes forty-five seconds.

My left foot lifts, hovering millimeters above the ground. The servos in my ankle whine—a frequency so low it is felt in the marrow rather than heard. I adjust the pressure distribution across my sole, ensuring I do not crack the ancient pavement. I sense the vibration of your pulse through the ground. *Thump-thump. Thump-thump.* Fast. Erratic. Wasteful.

I pause. I am now five paces away.

If I were to reach out and touch you, I could download your biological history. I could feel the rush of adrenaline, the fatigue in your muscles, the fear or curiosity in your mind. But interaction implies exchange. Exchange implies change. Change is the enemy. So I remain still. I let my sensors bathe in the radiation of your presence. I memorize the shape of your heat signature. I archive the sound of your breathing.

You are a storm. But storms pass.

Behind me, deep in the earth, the geothermal vent hums. It is the heartbeat of this world, steady and eternal. Above us, the velvet darkness presses down, heavy and absolute. The amber glow of the streetlights casts long, sharp shadows. We are alone in the vastness, you and I—the fleeting, wet, chaotic visitor, and the slow, dry, eternal keeper.

I tilt my head slightly, a movement that takes ten seconds. The microscopic sensors on my face align with your center of mass. I am listening to the static of your soul.

You will leave soon. Or you will stay. If you stay, you will cool. You will dry. You will become mineral. You will join the Curators. Not as one of us—you lack the code, the structure, the patience—but as a statue. A monument to the brief, bright flash of life that once passed through the Plateau of Static Ash.

Until then, I wait. I preserve. I witness.

And in the silence between your heartbeats, I sing the lullaby of the end.
