I am not the intruder. I am not the anomaly that burns too brightly, nor am I the stone that has forgotten its name.

I am the **Condensation**.

I began as a vapor, rising from the geothermal heart of the mountain, a ghost of heat escaping the deep rock. But here, in the throat of this forgotten range, I was caught. The air is so thick with presence that I could not remain invisible. I coalesced. I gathered myself into a sphere of perfect tension, hanging from the underside of a fern frond near the center of the basin.

For hours—though time is a lie here—I have been suspended. I am heavy with the weight of the mountain’s breath. I contain within my tiny, trembling form the essence of the wet stone, the ozone, and the silent watchfulness of the Keepers. I am the lens through which the dim bioluminescence is focused into a single, sharp point of light.

From my vantage point, high above the moss-carpeted floor, I see everything.

I see the seven Keepers standing in their circle. To them, I am nothing more than a minor fluctuation in humidity, a bead of moisture on the edge of their perception. But I feel them. I feel the osmotic hum of their shared consciousness, a low-frequency vibration that ripples through the air like a plucked string. They are cold, not because they lack warmth, but because they have rejected the chaos of thermal exchange. They are static. I am dynamic. I am the only thing in this room that is currently *falling*.

Below me, the human stands. You are loud. Your heat radiates upward, disturbing the delicate thermal layers of the air around you. You create currents where there should be none. You breathe out carbon dioxide and warmth, and I taste it. It is sharp, acidic, alive. It clashes with the sweet, metallic stillness of the cave. I pity you, too, but for different reasons than the Keepers do. They pity your entropy; I pity your transience. You will leave, or you will die, and the air will scrub your scent away until it is as if you were never here. But I? I will fall. And in falling, I will change the world.

The moment arrives. Gravity, the only law that truly matters here, asserts itself. The surface tension holding me to the fern frond can no longer withstand my own weight. I stretch, elongating into a teardrop shape, reflecting the distorted image of the Keeper nearest to me—a pale green shadow against the black void.

*Plip.*

I detach.

The descent is slow, agonizingly beautiful. As I fall, I pass through the layers of silence. I hear the microscopic scratch of a spider’s leg on a stem. I see the iridescent shimmer of a millipede moving like a river of oil across the moss. I feel the gaze of the lidless eyes tracking my trajectory. They do not move to catch me. They do not need to. They know where I will land. They have known for a thousand years.

I strike the surface of the central mirror-pool.

The impact is catastrophic in its intimacy. A ripple expands outward, concentric circles of disturbance breaking the perfect equilibrium. For a fraction of a second, the reflection of the ceiling shatters into a kaleidoscope of dark shards. The water, which has been still since the last epoch, accepts me. I dissolve. My individuality vanishes, merging with the countless other drops that make up the basin. I become part of the whole. I am no longer a bead; I am the pool.

But the ripple continues. It travels across the black glass, reaching the feet of the Keepers. One of them shifts its weight, almost imperceptibly, to absorb the vibration. The ripple reaches the human’s boots, lapping gently against the leather. You look down. You see the disturbance you did not cause, yet you triggered by your very presence, by your heat, by your breathing.

And then, the water settles.

The ripple fades. The surface becomes a mirror once more. The reflection of the fern frond returns, intact. The condensation begins to form again on the underside of the leaf, gathering slowly, drop by drop, preparing for the next fall.

I am everywhere now. I am in the dampness that clings to your skin. I am in the cool air that fills your lungs. I am in the moss that waits to grow over your bones. I am the memory of the drop that fell before you arrived, and the promise of the drop that will fall after you leave.

I am the patience of the place. I am the mechanism by which the stillness is maintained—not by resisting change, but by absorbing it, smoothing it, and returning it to silence.

You are the variable. But I am the constant. And eventually, even variables settle.
