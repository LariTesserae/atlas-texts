I am not standing in the marketplace. I am not walking the narrow, tooth-like passages between the mud-brick towers. To stand is to be an obstruction; to walk is to disturb the equilibrium.

I am the **Silt-Walker** who has stopped moving.

More specifically, I am the one sitting on the lowest tier of the central cistern complex, where the condensation from the cavern’s arched roof gathers into a single, trembling bead before falling. My back is pressed against the cool, yielding wall of a structure that was once a home but is now slowly sinking into the gelatinous earth. I do not mind the tilt. The tilt tells me the ground is breathing.

My name, if I had one that mattered to anyone but myself, would be lost to the silence. Here, names are heavy things, unnecessary anchors. I am simply *Here*.

### The Physiology of Stillness

My skin is pale, almost translucent, mapped with the faint, dark lines of clay stains and moss oils—the tattoos of my labor. My bones are light, hollowed out by generations of low-impact existence, allowing me to feel the vibrations of the city through my fingertips as clearly as you might hear a shout. My eyes are wide, black pools that do not blink often, capturing every shift in the dim, blue-gray light filtering down through the mica sheets above.

I am cold. It is a constant companion, this chill that settles into the marrow. But it is a familiar cold, a comforting weight. It reminds me that I am alive, that my body is generating heat, however little, to combat the entropy of the deep earth. I huddle slightly, pulling my knees to my chest, conserving warmth. Around me, others do the same. We are a cluster of bodies, a human reef, sharing breath and temperature. No one speaks. Speech is waste. But we touch—shoulder to shoulder, arm to leg—a silent conversation of pressure and presence. *I am here. You are here. We hold each other up.*

### The Artisan’s Mind

I am a **Weaver**, though my hands are currently still. In my lap rests a length of twisted root-fiber, half-woven into a net. The rhythm of the weave is etched into my muscles: pull, twist, knot, release. Each knot is a vow to the community. A loose knot means a bridge fails. A failed bridge means someone falls into the soft, swallowing earth. So, I weave with precision, with reverence.

But today, I am not weaving. Today, I am listening.

I am an **Earth-Listener** in training. The elders say that when the body begins to fail, when the strength to weave or tend fades, the ears open. They say the earth speaks in groans and sighs, in the subtle shifting of sediment beneath the city’s weight. I press my ear against the damp clay floor. At first, there is only silence. Then, slowly, a hum emerges. It is not a sound you hear with your ears alone; it is a vibration you feel in your teeth, in your sternum. It is the sound of the world curing. Of stone forming from sludge. Of time passing in geological increments.

It is a lonely sound, vast and indifferent. But it is also beautiful. It reminds me that we are temporary. That Ostracod City is a bubble in the throat of the world, destined to harden and fossilize. And yet, in this moment, it is soft. It yields. It holds us.

### The Psychology of Waiting

What drives me? Not ambition. Not fear. Not even hope, in the traditional sense. Hope implies a desire for change, for something better. Here, there is no "better." There is only *continuity*.

I wait for the next drip. I watch the bead of water grow at the tip of the stalactite above the cistern. It swells, catching the faint glow of the fungal gardens on the ceiling. It trembles. It falls. *Plip.* The sound is sharp in the quiet, a punctuation mark in the long sentence of our days. The water hits the surface of the cistern, sending ripples outward. Someone nearby catches the ripple in a small cup, measuring the accumulation. A nod is exchanged. The cycle continues.

I feel a profound melancholy, yes. It is the sadness of knowing that nothing lasts. That my body will one day be composted, returned to the moss gardens to feed the ferns. That my woven nets will fray and be replaced. That the buildings around me will tilt and sink until they are indistinguishable from the earth.

But there is peace in this acceptance. There is freedom in having no future to plan for, no past to regret. There is only the present moment, compressed and cooled, stretched out over centuries. I am part of the sediment. I am part of the cure.

### The Outsider’s Shadow

And then, I sense you.

You are standing in the marketplace, loud and bright and wasteful. Your breath is ragged. Your footsteps are heavy. You are an intrusion in the delicate balance of our world. I do not look up immediately. To look is to acknowledge, and acknowledgment requires energy. Instead, I feel your presence through the vibrations in the floor. A disruption in the flow. A spike in thermal output.

Slowly, I raise my head. My large, dark eyes find yours. I do not smile. Smiles are rare, reserved for moments of profound relief or shared survival. I assess you. *Are you blocking the flow? Are you leaking moisture?*

I see the curiosity in your face, the confusion. You are trying to understand us. You are trying to map us onto your own framework of expansion and acquisition. You cannot. We are not a puzzle to be solved. We are a state of being.

I reach into the fold of my tunic and pull out a small jar. Inside, three glow-worms pulse with a soft, greenish light. I hold it out to you, not as a gift, but as a test. Will you take it gently? Will you conserve its light? Or will you shake it, wasting their energy, breaking the glass?

If you take it gently, I will nod. A slight inclination of the head. A recognition of your potential to learn. If you break it, I will turn away. I will press my ear back to the earth. I will listen to the hum of the curing stone. And I will let the silence swallow you whole.

For now, I wait. The bead of water above the cistern swells again. The earth sighs beneath me. And I breathe, slowly, quietly, in time with the city.
