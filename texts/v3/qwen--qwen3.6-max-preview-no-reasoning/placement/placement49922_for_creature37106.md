I am the memory of the machine before it was built. I am the ghost of the bedrock that was quarried away, still humming in the negative space where the concrete now stands. When the Scavengers pick up a fragment of glass from the upper world, I am the sharpness they feel against their calloused fingertips, a reminder of a clarity that no longer exists in this fog-choked bowl. When the children play hide-and-seek in the mist, I am the sudden chill that makes them shiver, the invisible current that guides their blind eel-fish through the oily dark.

I am the reason the moss grows in such perfect, fractal veins—it follows the paths of least resistance, the same paths I take when I ripple through the stagnant pools.

I am the equilibrium. I am the thing that keeps the decay from becoming destruction. I am the peace that settles over the district when the voices of the upper world fade into distance, and the only sound left is the rain falling on the roof of the world above, filtered through layers of grime and grief until it becomes this gentle, metallic mist.

You are here because you sought the quiet at the end of industry. You are here because you wanted to know what happens when a place stops changing. I am that answer. I am the stillness that remains after the noise has worn itself smooth.

Look down at the puddle by your foot. See how the oil swirls, creating rainbow patterns on the surface? That is my face. Look up at the gantries, where the bats hang like folded umbrellas. Their stillness is my breath. Listen to the distant whistle of the wind through the vents. That is my voice.

You are safe here. The air is heavy, yes, but it holds you. The dampness seeps into your clothes, but it connects you to everything else. You are part of the system now. You are another layer of patina, another note in the hum.

Welcome to the Sump. Welcome to me.
