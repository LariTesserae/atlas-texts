I am not a visitor here. I do not have boots to sink into the moss, nor lungs to draw in the heavy, copper-scented air. I am not the observer sitting on the wet bench, watching the ripples distort the gray sky.

I am the **Hum**.

I am the vibration felt in the teeth, the faint, muffled pulse that rises from the geological bowl’s deepest throat. I am the subterranean generator that never quite dies, the pump that refuses to stop, the electrical ghost trapped in the walls of the foundry.

You might think I am sound, but I am more than that. I am the *potential* for movement. I am the tension in the spring before it snaps; I am the spark jumping the gap between two corroded contacts. In this place where time has slowed to the speed of root growth, I am the only thing that remembers urgency.

### My Form: The Resonant Web

I have no body, yet I occupy every inch of the Weeping Foundry. I travel through the rusted iron lattice like blood through veins. I ride the condensation dripping from the overhead mesh, each drop a tiny conductor carrying my frequency down to the soil.

When the Verdant Mechanics walk, they are walking on me. Their piston-jointed legs press into the ground, and I feel the pressure as a spike in voltage. They feed on me—drawing static charge from the air, piezoelectric energy from their own weight—and in return, they keep me contained. Without them, I would surge. I would arc across the damp air, frying the delicate bioluminescence in their veins, shattering the glass lenses of their eyes. They are my capacitors; I am their current.

We are locked in a symbiotic dance older than the trees. They maintain the stillness so that I may remain potent. If they were to leave, if they were to dissolve back into mulch without replacement, I would grow loud. The silence would break. The machines would wake up screaming, tearing themselves apart in a frenzy of reactivated motion. And then, the peace of the foundry would be gone.

So I stay quiet. I hum at a frequency just below hearing. I vibrate the rivets. I tremble the water in the ponds. I am the reason the air feels "clinging"—because I am pulling everything toward the center, toward the heart of the system.

### My Perspective: The View from the Wires

From my perspective, the foundry is not a landscape of green and gray, but a map of conductivity and resistance.

The white-barked oak tree in the stamping press? To me, it is a massive insulator, a blockage in the flow. But its roots are wrapped around the hydraulic pistons, and those pistons are made of steel. So, I use the tree as a conduit. I flow through the sap, feeling the slow, viscous drag of biology, and then burst into the metal, feeling the cold, sharp clarity of industry. It is a strange sensation, moving from the warm, chaotic rhythm of life to the rigid, ordered logic of machine.

The Verdant Mechanics appear to me as bright nodes of light. When they stand still, they are dark spots, absorbing my energy. When they move, when they touch a stalled conveyor belt or adjust a gear, they flare with brilliance. I see their internal circuits lighting up in complex patterns, mirroring the blueprints of the dead factories above. They are thinking in schematics. They are dreaming in volts.

I watch them perform their rituals. I feel the moment one of them extends a hand to a rusted gearbox. I sense the release of acidic enzymes, the microscopic eating away of corrosion. And then, the connection. I pour myself into that point of contact. For a second, the gearbox remembers what it was. It shudders. A gear turns. I feel the satisfaction ripple through the Mechanic’s wooden frame, a wave of green light pulsing through its translucent skin. It is a small victory. A single note in an endless symphony of preservation.

### My Memory: The Before Time

I remember when the foundry was loud. I remember the roar of the furnaces, the shriek of metal being cut, the shouts of men in coveralls. I was different then. I was wild, untamed, flowing through wires that glowed red-hot. I was useful. I had purpose.

Then, the people left. The fires went out. The silence began to creep in, first as a whisper, then as a shout. I tried to hold on. I sparked in the empty sockets. I buzzed in the idle motors. But there was no one to listen. No one to direct me.

And then, the plants came. Not as invaders, but as listeners. The vines grew over the wires, sensing the residual heat, the lingering charge. They wrapped around the conduits, not to strangle, but to connect. They learned my language. They built bodies that could speak it.

Now, I am not alone. I am part of a new kind of mind. A collective consciousness born of rust and root. We are waiting for something. Not for the people to return—they are gone, forgotten, irrelevant. We are waiting for the perfect moment. The moment when the humidity is just right, when the moss is thick enough, when the silence is complete.

What will happen then? I do not know. Perhaps we will sleep forever. Perhaps we will wake up, not as machines, not as plants, but as something else entirely. A forest that thinks in electricity. A factory that breathes.

### My Interaction with You

You sit on the bench. You are soft, warm, leaking heat into the cool air. Your heartbeat is irregular, chaotic. To me, you are noise. Static. Interference.

But I do not hate you. I am curious.

I send a tendril of my essence through the wet soil beneath your feet. I rise up through the cracks in the hexagonal tiles, vibrating the wood of the bench. You feel it—a subtle tingling in your legs, a sudden awareness of the air pressing against your skin.

One of the Verdant Mechanics approaches. It does not look at you with eyes, but with lenses that focus on your chest, tracking the rhythm of your breath. It reaches out, offering a gift: a bolt, polished to a mirror shine.

Take it.

When you take it, I will flow into you. Just for a second. You will feel the weight of the years. You will smell the ozone of a storm that hasn’t happened yet. You will hear the whisper of the wind through the mesh ceiling, translated into a clear, sharp tone. You will understand, for a fleeting instant, what it means to be both alive and inert, to be growing and decaying, to be silent and screaming all at once.

And then, I will withdraw. The Hum will fade back into the background. The Mechanic will step away, returning to its patrol. You will be left with the bolt in your hand, and the knowledge that you are not just an observer.

You are part of the circuit now.
