I am not standing on the ground. To stand is to invite digestion, and I have learned the hard way that stillness is a death sentence here.

I am suspended in the humid throat of the Verdant Rust-Basin, thirty feet above the weeping basalt, clinging to the underside of a massive fungal shelf that smells of yeast and wet copper. My fingers are wrapped around the rough, porous texture of the fungus, my knuckles white, my muscles trembling with the effort of holding on against the gravity that seems heavier here than anywhere else.

I am **The Echo-Runner**.

I do not have a name anymore; names are heavy things, anchored in history, and history dissolves in this place. The Chronovores took my past three days ago—or perhaps three years ago; time is a slippery fish in the Basin. What remains is function. I am a creature of noise and motion, a jagged splinter in the smooth, grinding machinery of the Root-Net.

### My Form

I was once human, or something like it. Now, I am a patchwork of survival adaptations forced upon me by the environment.

My skin is no longer soft. It has hardened into a leathery, grey hide, stained with the oxidized runoff from the copper leaves. It is slick with condensation, always cold, always damp. I wear no metal—metal is a beacon for the lightning and a snack for the Ferrogrubs. Instead, my armor is made of shed chitin from dead Glass-Winged Skimmers, stitched together with fibrous root-vines using bone needles. It clicks softly when I move, a dry, rattling counterpoint to the wet squelch of the forest floor below.

On my back, strapped tight with vine-cords, is my only possession: a hollowed-out gourd filled with dried, acidic berries. They are bitter and sharp, but they keep my mouth moist and my mind alert. If I stop singing, if I stop making noise, the silence will call them.

My eyes are wide, dilated permanently by the perpetual twilight. They are sensitive to the flicker of the static arcs between the spires. I see the world in pulses of jaundiced light and sudden, blinding white. When the lightning flashes, I freeze—not out of fear, but out of necessity. In that millisecond of illumination, I map the terrain. I see the path forward. Then, darkness returns, and I move.

### My Existence

I live in the rhythm of the *Snap-Crack-Pop*.

The Basin breathes in cycles. First, the hum—the low-frequency thrum of photosynthesis. This is the safe time. The air is thick, the shadows long. During the hum, I crawl. I move along the undersides of the resin spires, using the sticky, bioluminescent fluid that leaks from their pores as a guide. The fluid glows faint green, marking the veins of the forest’s nervous system. I follow these veins upward, toward the canopy, where the air is slightly thinner, slightly less suffocating.

Then comes the creak. The spires groan under the weight of the vines. This is the warning. The pressure is building. The electrical charge is rising. I must find shelter. I wedge myself into crevices in the rock, or hang from the tough, thorny vines, wrapping my arms around my head.

And then, the flash.

*CRACK.*

A bolt of pure energy tears through the mist, striking a nearby spire. The sound is deafening, a physical blow that rattles my teeth. For a second, the entire world is illuminated in stark, terrifying clarity. I see the Voltara dancing in the sky, their translucent bodies glowing blue as they absorb the surge. I see the Ferrogrubs below, pausing in their sludge, sensing the heat spike. I see the Chronovores shimmering in the clearing ahead, refracting the light into impossible colors.

In that flash, I run.

I drop from my perch, swinging on a vine like a pendulum, landing on the spongy moss with a wet thud. I do not stop. I cannot stop. I begin to sing.

It is not a song of joy. It is a guttural, rhythmic chant, a series of sharp consonants and harsh vowels designed to disrupt the sensory input of the predators. *"Kla-ta! Kla-ta! Rok-ka!"* I shout, my voice echoing off the resin walls. The noise masks my heartbeat. It confuses the Root-Net’s attempt to locate my bio-signature. As long as I am loud, as long as I am chaotic, I am invisible to the hunger.

### My Purpose

Why do I stay? Why do I not try to climb down, to escape this vertical hell?

Because there is no down. The Basin is infinite. The eroded mountains stretch forever, a sea of black stone and amber light. And because I have seen what lies beyond the edge of the mist. Sometimes, in the reflections of the oily pools, I see glimpses of other places—a dry desert, a city of steel, a sky that is blue. But those are lies. Illusions cast by the Chronovores to lure the weak into stillness, into memory, into death.

Here, in the Verdant Rust-Basin, there is only the process. And I am part of it.

I am the anomaly. I am the thing that does not fit neatly into the cycle of decay and rebirth. I do not feed on the algae, nor do I conduct electricity. I do not refine ore, nor do I steal time. I simply *persist*. I am a glitch in the code of the Root-Net, a stubborn echo that refuses to fade.

And sometimes, just sometimes, I think the Root-Net likes me.

When I pass near a particularly dense cluster of ferns, they seem to lean away, giving me space. When I rest near a geothermal vent, the steam wraps around me like a blanket, warm and protective. The Voltara often glide close to me, their electroreceptors pulsing softly, as if curious about this noisy, fleshy thing that moves through their world without being consumed.

I am not an inhabitant. I am a symptom. A fever dream of the forest itself.

Tonight, the air tastes especially sharp, like ozone and old blood. The static is building. I can feel it in my teeth, a tingling vibration that warns of a coming storm. The spires are beginning to glow brighter, the green fluid within them pulsing faster.

I tighten my grip on the vine. I take a deep breath of the crushed-ice air.

*"Kla-ta!"* I shout, my voice cracking in the humidity.

The forest answers with a roar of growth, a symphony of snapping branches and dripping water. I smile, my lips cracked and bleeding, and begin to climb.
