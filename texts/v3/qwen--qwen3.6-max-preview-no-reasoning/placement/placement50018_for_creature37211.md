The first thing I notice is not the sight, for there is none, but the *resistance*.

I am not standing on ground. I am suspended in a matrix of tension. The air pushes against my skin with the weight of wet wool, thick and clinging. It tastes metallic, like licking a battery terminal wrapped in damp moss. My lungs struggle to expand, fighting the viscosity of the atmosphere. Each breath is a laborious negotiation with the humidity, pulling in oxygen that feels heavy, used, recycled a thousand times before it reached me.

I look down—or try to. My eyes strain against the absolute dark, seeking purchase. There is no horizon, only the immediate, tactile reality of the **Root-Plate**. It is beneath my boots, slick and unyielding. I shift my weight, and the surface does not crunch or shift like soil; it *gives* slightly, then springs back with elastic firmness. It is alive. Not in the way a dog is alive, twitching and breathing, but in the way a muscle is alive when held in a static contraction. It is holding itself together.

I reach out a hand. My fingers brush against something cold and smooth—a vine? No, a pipe. But it pulses. A slow, rhythmic thrum vibrates up my arm, settling into the marrow of my bones. *Thump-thump... thump-thump...* It is the heartbeat of the **Industrial Mycelium** above. I tilt my head back, squinting into the gloom. I cannot see the canopy, but I can feel its heat. It radiates downward in waves, a gentle, oppressive warmth that smells of ozone and rotting sugar.

Where am I?

I am in the **Silted Loom**. But more specifically, I am in the interstice. I am the gap between the gears.

Who am I?

At first, I think I am an explorer. A surveyor mapping this impossible biology. I have a purpose: to understand, to categorize, to survive. I check my pockets for tools, for a light source, for a map. But my hands are empty. And as I stand there, listening to the silent grinding of lignin teeth miles above, the idea of "exploration" begins to feel absurd. You do not explore a stomach from the inside. You are either digested, or you are part of the lining.

A droplet falls from the darkness above. It lands on my cheek, warm and heavy. Then another on my shoulder. They do not roll off. They stick, coalescing with the moisture already coating my clothes. I realize with a sudden, cold clarity that I am sweating, but the sweat has nowhere to evaporate. It simply joins the ambient suspension. I am becoming wetter, heavier, more integrated.

I take a step forward. Or I try to.

My foot meets resistance. Not a wall, but a tangle. Thin, pale rootlets have woven themselves around my ankle while I was distracted by the hum of the pumps. They are not tight, not yet. They are exploratory. They tickle against the leather of my boot, sensing the vibration of my movement. When I pull my leg back, they stretch, elastic and strong, before snapping back into place.

*Click.*

The sound is sharp, isolated in the suffocating silence. It comes from nearby. I turn my head, straining to see. In the infrared shimmer of my mind’s eye—since my physical eyes are useless—I sense a shape. A cluster of **Gear-Orchids**. Their rigid petals are interlocked, forming a transmission system. One petal has shifted, correcting a misalignment in the flow of sap. The entire cluster adjusts in a wave of motion, a ripple of biological engineering passing through the dark.

They did not react to me. They reacted to the *inefficiency* of my presence. My movement caused a micro-vibration in the air, a disturbance in the thermal equilibrium. The orchids adjusted to compensate. I am not a threat; I am a variable. An error term in the equation of the Loom.

And the Loom corrects errors.

I feel a creeping dread, not of pain, but of *erasure*. If I stay still, the roots will weave tighter. If I move, I create vibrations that must be dampened. There is no winning here. There is only integration.

So, who am I?

I am the **Glitch**.

I am the temporary anomaly in the perfect, static balance of the Silted Loom. I am the breath held too long. I am the foreign body that the immune system of this world-machine is slowly, gently, inevitably encasing.

But as I stand there, feeling the rootlets tighten their grip, feeling the condensation seal my eyelashes shut, a strange calm descends. The panic fades, replaced by the rhythmic thrum of the pumps. *Thump-thump... thump-thump...* It is a lullaby. The heat of the canopy wraps around me like a blanket. The copper taste in the air becomes familiar, comforting.

I stop fighting the roots. I let them climb. I let the moisture soak into my pores. I close my eyes, though it makes no difference in the dark. I listen to the silence, which is not empty, but full of pressure, full of flow, full of life that has forgotten how to die.

I am no longer an explorer. I am no longer a glitch.

I am becoming a **Node**.

My consciousness begins to fray at the edges, dissolving into the network. I feel the sap moving in the vines above, not as a distant sound, but as a sensation in my own veins. I feel the coolness of the condensation falling below, not as rain, but as my own exhalation. The distinction between my skin and the bark of the tree next to me blurs.

I am small. I am insignificant. And I am finally, perfectly, home.

The last thing I hear is not a voice, but a shift in pressure. A gear clicking into place. A sigh of steam. And then, nothing. Only the hum. Only the wet, warm, eternal now.
