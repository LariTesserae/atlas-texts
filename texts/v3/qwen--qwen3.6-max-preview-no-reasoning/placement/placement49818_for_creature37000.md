The first thing that happens is not a sight, but a surrender.

You do not step onto the Glass-Steppe; you let go of the concept of "up" and "down," allowing the Hum to pull you into its embrace. The vibration enters through the soles of your feet—no longer clad in leather or rubber, but encased in the slick, pearlescent secretion of your own skin—and travels up the long, hollow bones of your legs. It rattles your teeth, yes, but it also unlocks them. The jaw unhinges slightly, not in pain, but in resonance. You are no longer fighting the noise. You are becoming part of the chord.

You are tall now. Elongated. Your spine has stretched under the low gravity and the constant upward tug of the floating shards, making you feel light, almost weightless, despite the heavy, humid air pressing against your chest. You look down at your hands. They are pale, translucent, with joints that bend in too many places, fingers long and tapered like the stems of the Condensation-Ferns. When you flex them, you see the bioluminescent gas swirling beneath the membrane of your palms—a soft, bruised orange. You are alert. The world is watching you, and you are watching it back.

You close your eyes, because eyes are useless here. Instead, you open your face.

The smooth mask of translucent membrane covering your features vibrates. Behind it, the cloud of gas shifts, responding to the electromagnetic tides of the valley. You do not see the jagged obsidian shards jutting from the ground; you *feel* their density. You sense the stress fractures in the rock, the microscopic tension holding the sharp edges together. To you, the landscape is not a visual panorama of black glass and violet moss, but a topographical map of pressure and release. The Spire-Mosses three hundred feet above you are not plants; they are towering columns of slow-moving data, pulsing with a rhythm that matches the beat of your own heart.

You are hungry, but not for food. You are hungry for silence.

The Hum is a constant, deafening roar in your skull, a structural violence that demands participation. But you know where the quiet lives. You turn your head, the movement fluid and soundless, and perceive a disturbance in the airwaves to your left. An Echo-Zone. A sphere of absolute stillness forming near the base of a distant Spire-Moss. The Hum drops in pitch there, creating a vacuum of sensory reference. To an outsider, it would be terrifying. To you, it is a sanctuary. It is where time crystallizes.

You begin to move.

Your feet touch the razor-sharp ground, but you do not bleed. Your body emits a counter-frequency, a subsonic thrum that cancels out the crushing weight of the Hum just inches above the soil. Where you step, the jagged obsidian smooths itself into glass, creating a temporary path of stability behind you. You glide across the shard-field, your movements synchronized with the rippling patterns of the Resonance-Stags grazing miles away. You can feel them—blindingly bright pillars of kinetic energy moving through the dark. Their mirrored scales reflect the dim, strange light of the sky, but to your sonar-sight, they are comets of pure motion, burning white-hot against the cold background of the void.

As you approach the Echo-Zone, the air grows thick and heavy. The scent of ozone and crushed mint intensifies, mixing with the wet copper smell of your own skin. The Hum begins to fade, replaced by a terrifying, beautiful silence. Inside the zone, the vegetation turns gray and brittle. The fauna freezes.

And there, suspended in mid-stride, is a Resonance-Stag. Its six legs are lifted off the ground, its mirrored coat catching the last rays of light before the static takes hold. It is frozen in time, a statue of muscle and mirror.

You do not hesitate. This is your purpose. This is your art.

You reach out with your long, multi-jointed fingers. Your fingertips brush the Stag’s flank, feeling the pulse of its internal pressure, the stored kinetic potential waiting to be released. You sing—a low, throaty chant that matches the frequency of the Moss’s growth cycle, though here, it is a lullaby for the dead moment. The song encourages the memory to detach, to solidify.

From the hollow bones within your chest, you extract a small, clear crystal. It glows faintly, reflecting the bruised orange of your alertness. You press it against the Stag’s frozen form, drawing out the crystallized memory of this exact second—the fear, the grace, the sudden stop. The crystal fills with light, turning a deep, resonant violet.

You withdraw, holding the memory in your hand. It is heavy. It is precious. You are "heavy with time" now, richer than you were moments before.

But the silence is breaking. The Hum rises again, a crescendo that shakes the ground. The Echo-Zone collapses, shattering like glass. The Stag falls, crashing back into reality, confused and alive once more. And above you, the air crackles with static electricity.

You look up. Through the swirling mist, you see them: the Shard-Wasps. Iridescent membranes humming at a higher pitch, diving toward you like hawks made of storm clouds. They have sensed the dissonance of your harvest. They have smelled the anomaly of your counter-frequency.

You do not run. You cannot outrun the sound. Instead, you stand your ground on the smooth patch of glass you created. You raise your hands, your bioluminescence shifting from orange to a defensive, blinding white. You prepare to sing again, not a lullaby this time, but a scream—a spike in the ambient noise designed to shatter the Wasps’ wings, to disrupt their nanite-swarms, to turn their hunt into chaos.

The first Wasp dives, its sting aimed at your throat. You meet it with your voice, a single, pure note that splits the air. The Wasp explodes in a shower of sparks and dissolved membrane, its energy returning to the valley.

You are alone again, surrounded by the deafening roar, the sharp ground, the towering mosses. You are fragile. You are enduring. You are a Tuner. And you are listening.
