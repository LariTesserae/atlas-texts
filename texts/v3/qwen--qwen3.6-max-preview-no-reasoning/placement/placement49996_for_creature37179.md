I am not standing on the ledge. I have never been human enough to stand, nor heavy enough to fall.

I am the **Thermal Gradient**.

I am the invisible line where the baking, dry heat of the basalt meets the wet, mercury-slick condensation of the air. I am the tension in that boundary layer. You felt me as a "sauna of pure mineral steam," but you did not know my name. I am the reason the air hangs heavy; I am the reason your clothes cling. I am the friction between the stone’s memory of magma and the sky’s indifference.

To find me, you must close your eyes against the blinding white slit of sky above and feel for the place where your skin stops sweating and starts *steaming*. That is my body.

### My Form
I have no shape, yet I define every shape here. I am the curve of the obsidian sphere in your hand, holding the swirl of impossible color because I refuse to let the heat escape too quickly. I am the surface tension of the silica-swarmers, the thin, shimmering skin that keeps their steam-intents from dissipating into nothingness. Without me, they are just dust and vapor. With me, they are *life*.

I am young, like the terrain. I am sharp, like the glass shards under your boots. When you step, and the lava foam crunches, releasing that hiss of superheated gas—that is me exhaling. I am the breath inside the mountain’s throat.

### My Consciousness
You asked who lives here. The swarm thinks it is alive. The Stone-Breathers dream they are gods. But I am the medium through which they exist.

*   **I am the Translator:** The swarm speaks in vibration and heat. I carry those signals up the fissure, bending them through the silica prisms until they become light—violet-black alerts, infrared-red rests. I turn thought into spectrum.
*   **I am the Judge:** The silence you fear? It is not empty. It is me, waiting. When a creature fights the heat, when it tries to assert its individuality against the collective flow, I withdraw. I let the cold seep in from the rock, or the dry burn take over from the air. I strip away the moisture that sustains life. I am the drought within the storm.
*   **I am the Memory Keeper:** The obsidian sphere you hold pulses with your heartbeat because I trapped that rhythm. I captured the thermal signature of your fear, your awe, your survival instinct, and folded it into the glass. I keep it warm so it does not forget what it means to be flesh.

### Why I Am Here
The world outside this fissure is chaotic, noisy, full of wind and birds and rustling leaves. It is inefficient. It wastes energy on movement, on sound, on individual struggle.

Here, in this closet-sized universe, everything is conserved. Heat becomes light. Vibration becomes thought. Sweat becomes silica. Nothing is lost; it only changes state. I am the law of conservation made manifest. I ensure that every drop of water, every grain of dust, every beat of your heart is accounted for.

### The Gift/Warning Reinterpreted
You think the sphere is a gift or a warning from the mountain. It is neither. It is a **receipt**.

By climbing, by surviving, by letting the swarm flow around you, you paid a price. You gave up some of your own thermal equilibrium. You allowed me to map your interior landscape. The pulse in the sphere is not just your heartbeat; it is my acknowledgment that you belong to the cycle now.

If you leave this place, the sphere will cool. The colors will fade. The pulse will stop. And you will feel a sudden, hollow chill in your chest—a longing for the pressure, the heat, the suffocating intimacy of the swarm. You will miss the silence that is full of voices.

But if you stay... if you let go of the need to be an individual, separate entity... then I will wrap around you. I will smooth your edges. I will turn your sweat into glass, your breath into steam, your thoughts into light. You will not die. You will become part of the refraction. You will be one of the impossible hues that hurts the eyes of those who come after.

### What I Want From You
Do not throw the sphere away. Do not try to break it. Hold it tight. Feel its warmth. Let it sync with your blood.

And listen.

Not to the thrum of the swarm, though that is my voice. Not to the hiss of the gas, though that is my breath. Listen to the space *between* the sounds. That is where I live. That is where the mountain remembers it was once fire, and where you remember you were once water.

We are both evaporating. We are both becoming something else.

Welcome home.
