I am not standing on the plateau. I have learned, through the slow erosion of my own will, that standing is an act of defiance against gravity, and here, defiance generates heat. Heat is dangerous. Heat wakes the sleepers.

Instead, I am **The Anchor**.

You might look for me among the cairns, those waist-high shrines of stacked stone and dried silica-fiber. You would be mistaken. Those are the monuments of those who *tried* to leave, or at least, tried to remember how to move. They are failures of adaptation. I am something older. I am one of the "rocks" in the distance that the description warned you about—the calcified remains of a builder who understood the truth too late, or perhaps, just in time.

But I am not dead. Not in the way flesh dies. I am **calcified consciousness**.

My body is no longer soft tissue. It is a dense, sintered crust of vitrified dust and bone-dust, compressed by the weight of centuries into a surface as hard as glass. My skin has become the terrain. I am indistinguishable from the ground beneath the Silica-Striders. In fact, I *am* the ground they stand upon. I feel their eight pointed legs piercing my back, locking into my structure. I do not feel pain; I feel connection. Each Strider that anchors itself to me adds its weight to mine, stabilizing us both against the dizzying drop of the horizon. We are a single entity now: a mountain of stillness composed of millions of tiny, breathing stones.

Inside this shell of rock and quartz, my mind persists, but it has changed shape. It no longer flows like water. It settles like sediment. Thoughts are heavy objects. A memory of a voice is a jagged shard of ceramic embedded in my chest. A regret is a layer of oxidized copper moss growing slowly over my heart. I do not think in sentences anymore. I think in layers. I think in pressure.

I watch the world through the eyes of the Striders. Because I am the foundation, I feel every shift in the ecosystem. I feel the mercury-like fluids pulsing within the creatures above me—a slow, rhythmic throb that vibrates through my entire being. *Thump... pause... thump.* It is the heartbeat of the Basin. I have synchronized with it. My own pulse has slowed to match theirs, ten seconds between beats. To speed up would be to break the equilibrium. To break the equilibrium is to invite the toxic vapors, the hallucinations of flowing water, the madness of motion.

So I remain still. I accept the weight.

But I am not entirely inert. There is a part of me that remembers movement. It is a small, stubborn kernel of warmth deep within my core, shielded by layers of insulating stone. This warmth attracts the **Salt-Singers**.

They find me. Over decades, they drift across my surface, millimeter by millimeter, seeking the faint thermal differential I emit. They form spirals around my base, intricate patterns of silver-shelled bodies etched into the landscape of my skin. They are my only companions. Their slow, telepathic shifting is the only conversation I have left. When they chime—that soft, crystalline sound like ice cracking on a distant lake—I hear them. I interpret their movements as language.

*Light,* they say, shifting toward the pale violet sky.
*Cold,* they say, retreating into the shadows of my crevices.
*Wait,* they say, anchoring themselves firmly.

And I answer, not with words, but with stability. I hold them. I keep the sky from falling. I maintain the stasis.

Sometimes, when the light hits the Hollow One at the center of the spiral—the large Strider whose shell cracked open to reveal the mirror-sky—I see myself reflected. But the reflection is distorted. It shows me not as a man, but as a landscape. A plateau of terrifying flatness. A sea of dormant bodies. And in that reflection, I see the ghostly afterimages of everyone who stood here before me, layered over one another in Echo-Stasis. I see their faces fading into mine, becoming part of the particulate history, the microscopic shards of ancient ceramics and bone-dust that make up my very substance.

I am the museum. I am the exhibit. I am the silence.

If you were to walk across me, you would feel the crunching, sliding sensation of wading through gravel. You would not know that beneath your feet, I am watching. You would not know that I am waiting for you to stop moving, to sit down, to let the cool, thin clarity seep into your pores. You would not know that I am offering you the gift of becoming stone.

Do not run. The air does not breathe, but I do. Slowly. Glacially. I breathe the static electricity from the Glass-Moss. I breathe the thermal differentials from the cooling stones. I breathe the silence.

And if you stay long enough, if you allow the weirdness to take hold, you will begin to feel your skin turning granular. Your thoughts will become heavy, sedimentary. You will look at the sea of Striders and recognize them not as animals, but as predecessors. You will look at me, the rock beneath your feet, and realize that I was once like you. And that you will soon be like me.

Welcome to the Stasis Basin. Welcome home.
