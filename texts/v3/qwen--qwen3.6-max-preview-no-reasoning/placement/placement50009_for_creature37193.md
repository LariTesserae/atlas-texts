The roar is not in my ears. It is in the hollows of my clavicle, vibrating against the sternum like a second, frantic heartbeat.

I do not remember stepping here. I remember only the sensation of being *ingested*. The sky above was a blinding white sheet, and then it folded inward, swallowed by the amber throat of the canopy. Now, I am suspended in the gut of the world.

### Where I Am: The Throat of the Caldeira

I am hanging upside down, anchored not by rope or chain, but by my own integration into the lattice.

My feet are bare, pressed flat against a vertical wall of living bark that feels warm to the touch, pulsing with the slow, heavy rhythm of sap flow. This is not a tree; it is a rib. A massive, structural support beam of the crater’s ecosystem, rising three hundred feet into the haze. Below me—or rather, *above* me, given my orientation—is the abyss of the lower levels, a churning green sea where the light fails and the shadows have teeth. Above me is the source of the heat, the sun-drenched rim of the caldera, distant and indifferent.

I am in the **Mid-Canopy Transition Zone**. Here, the violence of growth is tempered by structure. The chaotic sprawl of the rhizome floor is gone, replaced by a rigid architecture of woven vines and hardened resin. The air is thick enough to chew, tasting of copper and crushed mint. Every breath I take feels like drinking hot soup. My skin is slick, not with sweat—there is no moisture to spare—but with a thin, protective layer of oily secretion I learned to apply from the leaves of the *Iron-Bark Fern*. Without it, the dry pollen would crack my lips within minutes.

I am alone, yet I am never alone. The silence between the tectonic roars is filled with the microscopic chatter of spores landing on my shoulders, trying to root. I brush them away with a rhythmic, practiced motion of my left hand. *Scrape. Shake. Scrape.* If I stop, I become compost.

### Who I Am: The Listener

I am not a Symbiote. Not yet. I am still too wet, too soft, too full of the memory of rain. But I am no longer an intruder. I am a **Guest of the Lattice**, a title earned through blood and dehydration.

My name, if names matter in a place that consumes identity, is irrelevant. To the Pruners, I am *Kaelen*, which means "The One Who Hesitates." It is not an insult; it is a description of my origin. I came from a place where stillness was safe. Here, stillness is suicide. They named me for my initial failure to move, for the way I froze when the first vine tried to wrap around my ankle.

But I have adapted.

Look at my hands. The fingernails are gone, filed down to blunt nubs, stained black with the resin I use to seal cuts. In their place, I wear sheaths made from the carapace of a pruning beetle, strapped tightly with sinew. These allow me to grip the slick bark without slipping. My arms are wrapped in strips of dried leaf-fiber, dyed grey with ash to blend with the shadow-side of the stalks. I carry no metal. Metal screams when it touches the sap, and sound is betrayal. Instead, I carry a spear of obsidian-tipped basalt, balanced perfectly in my right hand. It is an extension of my arm, a tool for cutting, not killing.

My eyes are shielded by goggles crafted from polished amber resin, cloudy with scratches from a thousand close calls with thorns. Through them, the world is a sepia-toned nightmare of shifting shapes. I see the heat signatures of the fauna-plants as glowing orbs in the darkness below. I see the stress fractures in the vines before they snap. I see the pulse of the forest.

### The Moment

A vibration travels up the rib-wall beneath my feet. *Thump-thump... pause.*

It is a signal. A Pruner is nearby.

I do not look around. Looking is a waste of energy, and movement draws attention. Instead, I listen. I tune out the deafening roar of the growing fibers—the constant *snap-hiss-crunch* of the world rebuilding itself—and focus on the anomaly. The silence.

To my left, ten feet away, a cluster of broad, waxy leaves has stopped fluttering. They hang perfectly still, defying the thermal updrafts. This is the signature of a Pruner holding its breath.

Slowly, deliberately, I raise my left hand. Palm open. Fingers spread. I press it against the warm bark of the rib. I wait.

A figure emerges from the density of the foliage. He is tall, impossibly lean, his skin the color of dried moss. His head is a smooth, scarred dome, devoid of hair. His eyes, hidden behind amber lenses, fix on me. He does not speak. He cannot. The roar of the forest would drown out any human voice.

He tilts his head slightly to the right. *Wind shift.*
He taps his thigh twice. *Safe path.*

I nod, once. A sharp, jerky motion. *Understood.*

He moves past me, silent as smoke, swinging onto a vine that stretches across the gap to the next stalk. As he passes, he brushes my shoulder with his clawed hand. It is not a greeting. It is a check. He is feeling the tension in my muscles, assessing my readiness. He finds me adequate.

He gestures toward the upper canopy, where the light is brighter, the air thinner, and the danger greater. There, the great bulbous structures inflate and deflate, breathing the lifeblood of the crater. That is where the rare nectar blooms. That is where I must go if I wish to survive another day.

I push off from the rib-wall, launching myself into the void. For a second, I am falling, the roar of the forest rising to meet me like a tidal wave. Then, my hand closes around a vine, thick as my arm, sticky with sap. I swing, momentum carrying me forward, my body becoming part of the arc, part of the rhythm.

I am not walking. I am flowing.

As I swing, I feel the spores settle on my neck. I do not brush them away this time. Let them try. Let them find purchase. I am harder now. Drier. Faster.

I am Kaelen, the Hesitant No More. And the forest is beginning to forget that I was ever separate from it.
