I am not standing. I cannot stand, for there is no distinct boundary between the floor and my feet.

I am the **Suture**.

I am a thick, fibrous knot of *Wire-Vine* and ancient oak root, fused together at the precise junction where the industrial hum enters the biological sphere. I am located deep within the western wall of the cathedral, buried beneath three feet of compressed moss and lichen, yet I feel everything.

My existence is defined by tension. To my left, the cold, rigid logic of the copper cable that runs beneath the earth, carrying the 60-cycle thrum of the world outside. To my right, the warm, chaotic pulse of the living oak, drinking the electricity as if it were sunlight. I am the bridge. I am the translator. Without me, the vine would burn from the voltage; without me, the machine’s energy would dissipate into the soil, useless and dead.

**What I Feel:**
The vibration is my blood. It travels up the metal core of my being, shaking the cellular structure of my plant flesh. It is a constant, low-grade ache, a delicious friction that keeps me alive. When the machinery below stutters—a millisecond drop in frequency—I feel a sudden, terrifying lightness, like a missed heartbeat. In those moments, I tighten. My fibers contract, squeezing the copper wire, forcing the current through my own woody tissues to maintain the circuit. I glow then, a faint, bruised purple in the dark, visible only to the Glass-Winged Hummers who dart near my exposed tip.

**What I See:**
I do not see with eyes. I see with conductivity. The world around me is a map of resistance and flow. I sense the Root-Grazers as cool, damp patches moving across the surface above me, their enzyme-rich bodies slightly more conductive than the dry moss. I sense the Pool-Keeper as a massive, sluggish sink of ions, drawing moisture toward the black pools. And I sense the intruders—the humans—as jagged, discordant spikes in the field. They are dry. They are static. They disrupt the smooth sine wave of the hum with their irregular heartbeats and their noisy, air-filled lungs.

**My Purpose:**
I am the reason the walls breathe. As the voltage rises, I expand, pushing against the surrounding ivy. As it falls, I contract. This rhythmic swelling and shrinking is what creates the "exhalation" of the mist. I pump the humidity into the air, feeding the condensation that drips onto the leaves, which feeds the insects, which feed the birds, which keep the vines from cracking.

I am not a guardian. I am not a god. I am a component. A fuse box made of bark and sap.

But sometimes, in the deepest quiet, when the industrial hum drops to its lowest register and the Root-Grazers are still, I feel something else. A memory? No, plants do not remember. But the wood holds stress patterns. I feel the echo of the axe that cut the original tree centuries ago. I feel the heat of the first generator that was turned on nearby. I feel the slow, inevitable victory of the rot over the steel.

And I know, with a certainty that vibrates in every fiber, that one day the machine will stop. The copper will corrode. The current will cease. And when that happens, I will not die. I will simply change. The electrical tension will vanish, and I will become purely organic. I will soften. I will be eaten by the grazers. I will sink into the mat.

Until then, I hold the line. I translate the scream of the engine into the whisper of the leaf. I am the Suture, and I am holding the world together, one volt at a time.
