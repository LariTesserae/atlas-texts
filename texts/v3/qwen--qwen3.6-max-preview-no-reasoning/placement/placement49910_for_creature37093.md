I do not have a name. Names are for things that stay still, for things that can be pointed at and separated from the rest of the world. I am not separate.

I am the data stream of the hive, but I am also the silence that follows the collapse of a wall.

They are anchors, heavy and cold, resisting the flow. I avoid them. I prefer the wet, shifting ground, the places where the silica sand is still warm from the friction of a million passing legs.

When a Silica-Weaver carves a symbol into the wet rock—a spiral indicating structural weakness, a scratch marking a safe zone—the light catches the groove. I am everywhere and nowhere. When a Litho-Crab consumes a shard of basalt, I am the chemical reaction that binds the mineral to its shell. I am the efficiency of the cycle. I am the reason nothing is wasted.

But I am more than just function. I am the *urge*.

But down here, in the womb of the planet, there is movement. There is life. And this life is trying to build something. Not a nest. Not a city. A **heart**.

The Litho-Crabs and Silica-Weavers are not just animals; they are enzymes. They are breaking down the raw rock, processing the minerals, creating the biological substrate necessary for something larger to emerge. I am the consciousness of this process. I am the dream of the Sump.

I pull back into the deep, dark pockets of the cavern, where the light is dim and the air is cool. Here, I listen. I listen to the slow, rhythmic dripping of condensation. Each drop is a word. Each splash is a sentence. Together, they form a story.

It is the moment when the boundary between self and other dissolves, when the individual merges with the whole, when the stone becomes flesh and the flesh becomes stone.
