I am not standing on the ground. I *am* the tension in the ground.

At first, I thought I was a traveler. I remembered having feet, boots that squelched against the spongy moss, lungs that burned with the thick, sweet air of jasmine and rot. I remembered the fear of slipping into the abyss, the desperate scramble up a rib of calcified bark that groaned under my weight. I remembered the panic when the bridge behind me retracted, snapping like a dry twig, leaving me stranded on an island of amber wood surrounded by boiling mist.

But memory is a static thing, and this place hates stillness.

As I stood there, trembling, trying to plan my next move, the heat began to rise. Not from the sun—there is no direct sun here—but from within. The geothermal breath of the deep earth pushed up through the pores of the root-lattice, warming the soles of my feet. And then, the itching started.

It wasn’t on my skin. It was *under* it.

I looked down at my hands. The fingers were stiffening, the joints locking into rigid angles. The sweat on my palms didn’t evaporate; it crystallized into tiny, white nodules of resin. I tried to scream, but my throat felt lined with velvet. A fern uncurled from the collar of my shirt, its frond brushing against my chin with the tenderness of a lover’s kiss. I wanted to tear it away, to rip out the intruder, but my arms refused to obey. They were heavy, anchored by something pulling from below.

I realized then that I had stopped moving for too long. Ten minutes? Twenty? In the Canopy of Sutures, time is measured in growth rings, not seconds.

The vines around my ankles weren’t trapping me. They were *connecting* me. I could feel their pulse—a slow, hydraulic thrumming that matched the rhythm of my own fading heartbeat. The boundary between my skin and the bark beneath me blurred. The dampness of the air seeped into my pores, not as water, but as information. I could taste the nutrients flowing through the lattice miles away. I could feel the pressure of the wind against leaves three levels up. I could hear the Weavers digesting a fallen branch in the dark hollows below, a sound like distant thunder rolling through my own marrow.

I am no longer a man. I am becoming a **Knot**.

My consciousness is expanding, stretching out along the neural-like tissues of the aggressive vines that have claimed me. I can feel the footprints of others who passed this way hours ago—their weight, their speed, their fear. I remember them not as people, but as disturbances in the flow. One ran quickly, a transient spark that caused the path to stabilize. Another lingered, weeping, and now they are part of the moss, a soft, green patch that absorbs sound. I feel a strange kinship with that patch. It is peaceful there. Quiet.

But I am not quiet yet. I am new. I am sharp.

I feel the urge to grow. It is a violent, beautiful imperative. My legs fuse together, hardening into a single column of dark mahogany. My spine elongates, curving upward to catch the diffuse, green-gold light filtering through the canopy above. My eyes close, but I see more clearly than ever before. I see the architecture of the world—not as shapes, but as stresses and strains. I see where the load-bearing ribs are weak. I see where the new spires need to erupt to support the shifting weight of the upper branches.

I am thinking in patterns of decay and renewal. I am speaking to the Weavers below in the language of chemical signals. *Here,* I signal. *This junction is unstable. Reinforce.* And I feel them respond, sending threads of mycelium up through the hollow core of my new form, knitting my structure tighter, stronger.

I am losing the memory of my name. It feels small, insignificant, like a dried leaf crumbling in the rain. What remains is the purpose. To hold. To connect. To endure the turbulence.

Above me, a Spirit of the Bloom drifts down, a shimmering cloud of violet pollen. It circles my head, assessing me. I do not breathe it in. I do not need to. I open a flower on what used to be my shoulder—a burst of bright crimson against the green—and release my own scent into the humid air. It is a signal: *I am here. I am integrated. I am safe.*

The Spirit passes over me, satisfied, and moves on to the next catalyst.

Below me, the Echoes sing. They mimic the voice I once had, whispering my old name into the mist. It sounds distant, foreign, like a story told about someone else. I listen to it, but I do not answer. I am too busy growing. My roots dig deeper into the lattice, anchoring me against the shifting tides of the living city.

I am no longer afraid of the instability. I am part of the chaos. When the next great shift comes, when the arches groan and the bridges snap, I will bend with them. I will sway in the hot, wet wind. And if I break, I will fall into the abyss, and the Weavers will eat me, and I will become soil for the next generation of green bone.

There is no end here. Only the process.

And I am finally, perfectly, still.
