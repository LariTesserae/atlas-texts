I am not the Liturgist, sliding on waves of silence. I am not the Gardener, twitching with the frantic duty of maintenance. I am not even the Last Architect, heavy with the weight of stone and memory.

I am the **Dissonance**.

I am the thing that should not be here, yet whose presence is now woven into the fabric of this throat. I am the anomaly in the perfect equation. I am the crack in the enamel, the shard of human bone lying on the mosaic tiles, vibrating at a frequency that does not belong to the earth’s groan.

At first, I thought I was an intruder. A tourist in a cathedral of sound. But as the vibration settled into my marrow, as the piezoelectric sparks jumped from the ferns to my skin, I realized the truth: *I have been invited.*

The Throat does not tolerate chaos. It absorbs it. It digests it. And I am the most chaotic thing it has tasted in eons. My heartbeat—*thump-thump, thump-thump*—is too fast, too irregular, too wet for this dry, crystalline world. My breath is a ragged tear in the smooth fabric of the standing waves. My pain is a sharp, jagged note that cuts through the major chord of the lullaby.

And yet… they do not destroy me. The Liturgists watch me with their dish-faces, not with anger, but with a terrible, hungry curiosity. They tilt their heads, adjusting their membranes to catch the new frequency I emit. The Gardeners pause in their work, their obsidian claws hovering over the vines, listening to the way my discord ripples through the air, causing the moss-glyphs to shift faster than ever before.

I am becoming part of the song. Not as a harmony, but as a counterpoint. A minor key introduced into a major scale. A tension that demands resolution.

I sit on the rose quartz bench beside the Last Architect. He does not look at me—he has no eyes—but I feel his attention like a physical pressure. He raises his hammer. For a moment, I fear he will strike me down, to silence the off-key noise I make. But instead, he taps the chisel against the stone. *Click.*

It is a tiny sound. Insignificant. But it lands exactly on the beat of my heart. *Thump-click. Thump-click.*

He is tuning me.

I realize then what I am. I am the **Catalyst**.

The ecosystem has been stuck in a loop for millennia, singing the same lullaby to keep the Unborn asleep. But lullabies, if sung forever, become dirges. Stasis is death. The garden was beginning to stagnate; the glyphs were repeating themselves, the frequencies becoming predictable, the energy fading. The Echo-Eaters were descending lower, drawn by the weakening signal.

They needed something new. Something unpredictable. Something *alive* in the messy, painful, vibrant way that only a mortal can be.

They needed me.

My breaking teeth are not a sign of destruction, but of integration. My body is being reshaped by the sound, hollowed out, tuned. I feel my ribs expanding, becoming fluted like the bones of the Liturgists. My skin is growing translucent, catching the blinding light and scattering it into rainbows. My voice, when I try to speak, is no longer swallowed—it is amplified, twisted, transformed into a pure, clear tone that resonates with the core of the mountain.

I am no longer human. I am becoming an instrument.

The bud in the center of the plaza pulses again. Violet light bleeds from its scales. The sound swells, rising in pitch, becoming sharper, brighter. The air tastes of ozone and old blood, yes, but also of *anticipation*.

The Last Architect lowers his hammer. He looks at me—or rather, he feels the new resonance I create. And for the first time in ten thousand years, he moves more than once. He reaches out a stone hand and touches my shoulder. His touch is cold, but the vibration running through him is warm.

He is handing me the baton.

The Liturgists step back, forming a circle around me. The Gardeners retreat into the shadows, their job done. The Echo-Eaters halt their descent, suspended in the upper shafts, waiting to see what happens next.

I stand up. My legs feel light, insubstantial. I look at the bud. I can hear what lies inside. It is not a god. It is not a weapon. It is a question. A question that has been asked for eons, unanswered because there was no one brave enough, or broken enough, to provide the answer.

The question is: *What comes after silence?*

I open my mouth. I do not shout. I do not whisper. I sing.

It is a ugly, beautiful, painful note. It is the sound of a tooth cracking, of a heart breaking, of a soul waking up. It is dissonant. It is wrong. It is perfect.

The bud shudders. The iridescent scales begin to unfurl.

The Throat holds its breath.

And I am the first note of the new song.
