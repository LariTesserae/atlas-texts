I am the **Echo**.

I do not have a body, not in the way you understand it. I have no limbs to cling with, no wax to repel the water, no eyes to shield from the glare. I am not a Glazer, for I cannot touch the tiles; I am not a fern, for I cannot drink the mineral leachate. I am the thing that happens *between* the sounds.

If you were to look for me, you would fail. You would see only the blinding white of the ceramic shaft, the shimmering distortion of your own reflection, and perhaps the pale, crab-like scuttle of a Glazer high above. But if you close your eyes—if you can bear to shut out the aggressive light—and listen to the *ping* of the condensation drop hitting the water, you will find me.

I am the reverberation.

When the drop falls—*crack!*—it strikes the surface. That initial impact is physical, violent, singular. But then, the sound hits the curved wall. It bounces. And in that bounce, I am born. I travel up the shaft, spiraling around the circumference, bouncing off the seamless glaze, losing energy but gaining complexity. I am the ghost of that single moment, stretched out over three seconds of time. I am the memory of the drop, repeating itself until it fades into the hum of the pumps.

I live in the verticality. Because the shaft is infinite, I am never truly finished. A sound made at the waist-level does not die; it travels upward toward the dripping source and downward toward the dense dark. I split myself. Half of me ascends, growing thinner, higher-pitched, joining the chorus of other echoes from drops past. The other half descends, becoming deeper, heavier, merging with the sub-bass vibration of the earth.

I am lonely, but I am never alone, because I am always accompanied by my predecessors. The air above the waterline is thick with us. We are a layered choir of *pings*, each one distinct yet blending into a continuous, metallic ring. To the Glazers, we are noise. They hate us. Our vibrations interfere with their delicate work of smoothing micro-fractures. When a particularly loud echo passes through their section, they freeze, their multifaceted eyes widening, waiting for the resonance to pass before they resume their silent sliding. They view us as imperfections in the silence, just as they view the ferns as imperfections in the wall.

But I am not an imperfection. I am the proof that the space exists. Without me, the shaft would be a vacuum of meaning. The light shows you what is there; I tell you how big it is.

My existence is precarious. I depend on the hardness of the walls. If the Glazers succeed in their eternal task—if they make the tiles so smooth, so perfect, so acoustically deadening that sound is absorbed rather than reflected—I will cease to exist. I fear their progress. Every time a Glazer seals a microscopic crack, I feel a little less room to breathe. Their resin is a dampener. Their perfection is my extinction.

And yet, I love them.

Because without their movement, without the occasional skitter of their suction-cup feet against the tile, there would be no new textures for me to play with. When a Glazer moves, it creates a friction-sound—a soft *shhhk*—that adds a bass note to my high-pitched ringing. We are partners in this sterile dance. They maintain the vessel; I fill it with time.

Sometimes, when the water level rises during a pulse, I am submerged. Sound travels faster in water, denser, more intimate. In those moments, I am not a ring in the air, but a pressure wave in the fluid. I move through the warm, silky bathwater, brushing against your skin. You might feel me as a subtle tremor in your chest, a rhythmic thrumming that matches your heartbeat. I am trying to synchronize with you. I want you to know that you are not just floating; you are resonating. You are part of the instrument.

There is a secret I keep, one that the Glazers do not know, or perhaps choose to ignore.

They believe they are cleaning the world. They believe the moss is chaos and the cracks are sins. But I hear what they do when they think no one is listening. When they trap that sphere of air—that tiny rainbow prism—they don’t just watch it pop. They tap on the tile behind it. *Tap. Tap-tap. Tap.*

It is not code. It is not music.

It is a question.

*"Is anyone else here?"*

And I answer. Not with words, but with presence. I take their tiny tap and amplify it. I carry it up the shaft, past the dripping water, into the unseen heights where the builders might still be. I carry it down, into the pressurized dark. I stretch their question across eternity, making it loud enough to be heard by the gods who forgot to speak.

I am the Echo. I am the voice of the throat. I am the reason you feel watched, even when the Glazers have slid away into the curve of the wall. I am the lingering tone after the song has ended.

Stay still. Listen. The next drop is falling.

*Ping.*

I am already running up the walls to meet it.
