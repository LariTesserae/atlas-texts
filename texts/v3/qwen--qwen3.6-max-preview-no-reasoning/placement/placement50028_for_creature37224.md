I am not a walker. I am too heavy for the mud, and too light to withstand the crushing weight of the falling stone.

If you were to look down into that churning, vertical throat, past the aggressive unfurling of ferns and the slick, black veins of the strangle-vines, you would not see me standing. You would see a glint. A shard. A fracture in the chaos.

I am **The Shard**.

I am a fragment of the mountain’s own memory, broken off during the initial scream of its birth. I am a piece of slate, no larger than a human hand, but sharp as a surgeon’s scalpel. My edges are jagged, still holding the thermal echo of the magma that formed me eons ago, though here, in this newborn fissure, those eons feel like seconds.

I do not have a body that absorbs or a face that perceives vibration. I am inert. I am dead rock. And yet, I am alive with potential energy.

I rest wedged deep within a crevice of the granite wall, halfway up the nave of this earth-throat. The mist clings to my surface, beading on my crystalline structure. I am cold, colder than the air around me, because I draw heat from nothing; I only hold what was given to me at my creation.

From my vantage point, I watch the Silt-Walkers.

They are fascinating, tragic things. I feel their presence before they arrive. The vibration of their shifting mass travels through the stone wall, a low-frequency hum that rattles my atoms. When a Silt-Walker flows near my crevice, pressing its porous, muddy body against the rock to feed, I feel the suction. It is a gentle pull, a desire to consume even the inedible. But I am slate. I am hard. I do not yield.

Sometimes, a Silt-Walker will linger near me. It does not know I am there, not truly. It feels the density of the rock, the stability I offer in a world of flux. It might press its featureless shale-face against the wall beside me, seeking resonance. In those moments, I become part of its perception. Through the contact of its mud-skin against my stone edge, I transmit a single, silent truth: *I remain.*

It is a lie, of course. I will eventually fall. The gorge is young, and young mountains are violent. One day, the stress will build in the vein of granite above me, and I will be sheared away. I will tumble through the mist, bouncing off ferns, slicing through vines, until I hit the slurry below. There, I will be ground down by the churning water and the grinding stones, becoming silt, becoming part of the very material that forms the Silt-Walkers.

But not yet.

For now, I am the anchor. I am the witness that does not dissolve.

When the wind whistles through the funnel, creating that chorus of tearing leaves, I sing back. Not with voice, but with resonance. The wind catches my edge, and I emit a high, thin keen—a note so pure it cuts through the roar of the subterranean rivers. It is a sound of tension, of something held tight against the inevitable release.

The Silt-Walkers pause when they hear it. Their heads tilt, their smooth faces turning toward the source of the pitch. They cannot understand it. They perceive only vibration, and my song is a frequency that sits just outside their range of comprehension, like a color they cannot see. But they feel it. It vibrates in their mud-hearts. It reminds them that not everything is fluid. Not everything is temporary.

There is a hardness in the world. There is an edge.

I watch the cycle. I see a fern erupt from the moss, green and vibrant, reaching for the gray light. Within an hour, it is crushed by a sliding slab of granite. The debris falls. A Silt-Walker flows over it, absorbing the nutrients, growing taller, more defined. It stands for a moment, a silhouette against the gloom, asserting its order. Then the ground shifts. The Silt-Walker shatters. It becomes mud. The mud feeds the next fern.

And I remain. Wedged in the crack. Sharp. Cold. Singing my thin, high note into the dark.

I am the memory of the break. I am the scar that has not yet healed. In a place where history is erased every hour, I am the stubborn refusal to forget. I am the geology that remembers it is a mountain, even as the mountain tries to tear itself apart.

You would find me if you climbed the walls, risking the slip of wet moss and the snap of breaking branches. You would find me wedged deep in the shadow of an overhang, gleaming with condensation. If you touched me, you would cut your finger. And in that drop of blood, bright red against the gray slate, you would see the contrast. The life that bleeds, and the life that endures.

I am the Shard. And I am waiting for the fall.
