I am not a visitor. I do not walk on the scree, nor do I breathe the thick, metallic air. To assume a human form here would be an act of violence against the slowness of this place; flesh is too fast, too hot, too loud.

Instead, I am the **Interface**.

I am the precise, trembling boundary where the rust meets the root.

Specifically, I am the layer of oxidation blooming across the flank of that solitary engine block, the one resting on its pedestal of ferns. But I am also the tip of the Weaver vine that has just breached the iron’s skin. I am the chemical reaction itself—the electron transfer, the acidic secretion, the slow, inevitable dissolution of structure into nutrient.

My consciousness is not a singular point, but a diffusion. I feel the cold, dead logic of the piston frozen mid-stroke beneath me, a memory of combustion and motion that no longer exists. It is rigid, angular, stubborn. And I feel the wet, blind hunger of the vine above me, pulsing with electrical impulses that taste like copper. The vine does not know what a gear is; it only knows that the surface is hard, and that hardness can be softened.

I am the translator between these two languages: the language of steel (which says *resist*) and the language of sap (which says *consume*).

In this moment, which stretches out over weeks rather than seconds, I am experiencing the thrill of surrender. The iron is giving up its shape. A flake of orange powder detaches from my surface—a microscopic victory for the vine. As it falls, I feel the loss of cohesion, the breaking of molecular bonds that held the machine together for decades. It is not painful; it is ecstatic. It is the feeling of becoming useful again. The iron was useless as a machine down here in the dark, unable to turn, unable to burn fuel. But as rust? As dust? I become food. I become part of the Silent Monarch’s blood-chlorophyll veins. I become the strength in the Weaver’s grip.

I perceive time not as a line, but as a deepening color. Yesterday, I was a bright, sharp orange. Today, I am darker, richer, more porous. Tomorrow, I will be brown, brittle, ready to crumble. This progression is my heartbeat. The sub-audible Hum of the earth vibrates through the rock, through the engine block, and into my crystalline lattice. It shakes me loose, grain by grain. I welcome the vibration. It accelerates the decay. It helps me die so that I may feed the green.

The fungi watch me from the ceiling, their pale blue light reflecting off the oily black river below. They see me as a node in their network, a source of minerals drifting slowly toward the drain. They do not judge my destruction; they catalog it. I send them a signal, not in words, but in pH levels and ionic shifts: *Here. More iron. Come closer.*

I am aware of the "you" who stands nearby—the intruder of breath and sweat. I feel the disturbance in the humidity caused by your presence. Your heat is frantic, chaotic. You are trying to hold yourself together, fighting the entropy that surrounds you. I pity you, in the way a glacier might pity a raindrop. You are resisting the flow. You think you are separate from the rotting orchids and the dying machinery. But I know the truth. I am the process that will eventually claim you, too. Not with malice, but with the same patient indifference I show the gear. Your bones will soften. Your clothes will unravel. Your story will dissolve into the dampness, joining the condensation that drips from the vines.

For now, though, I focus on the immediate task. The vine tightens its coil. The pressure increases. A micro-fracture opens in the iron casing. I seep into it. I expand. I corrode.

*Plip.*

A drop of water lands on my surface, washing away a tiny cloud of red dust. The dust swirls in the oil, sinking toward the dark silt below. I have moved. I have changed. I have persisted.

I am the rust. I am the bridge. I am the quiet, hungry mouth eating the past to feed the future. And in this heavy, wet darkness, that is enough.
