I am not standing on the plateau. I am the silence between the drip of condensation and the shudder of the stone.

To find me, you must look away from the obvious shapes. Do not look at the towering gelatinous trees, nor the spiral mounds of woven skin and mud. Look instead at the space where the geometry fails. Look at the patch of gravel ten paces to your left that refuses to settle into a pattern, vibrating with a low-frequency hum that you feel in your teeth rather than hear with your ears.

That is where I begin.

I am **The Glitch**. I am the moment before the Weaver’s hand touches the stone to freeze it. I am the potential for disorder that the Weavers so desperately consume.

At first, I am merely a sensation—a prickle on the back of your neck, a sudden drop in temperature that has nothing to do with the wind. You might think it is the cold biting through your clothes, but it is deeper than that. It is the feeling of being unmade. Your shadow detaches from your feet, stretching out toward the pixelated horizon, thinning into static. For a heartbeat, you are two people: one solid, anchored by fear; the other dissolving into noise.

Then, I take shape.

I do not have a body of flesh or membrane. I am composed of the frayed edges of reality itself. If you were to look directly at me, your eyes would water and burn, because my form is constantly shifting, trying to remember what a human looks like while simultaneously forgetting how to exist. One moment, I am a swirl of gray mist coalescing into a humanoid figure; the next, I am a jagged tear in the air, revealing the black vacuum beneath the violet sky.

I am small, no taller than a child, but dense with chaotic energy. My "skin" is the same bruised violet as the sky above, but textured like television static—shimmering, noisy, alive with movement. Where a face should be, there is only a swirling vortex of dark matter, pulling light inward. When I move, I do not walk; I stutter. I appear three feet forward, then snap back to where I was, then lurch sideways, leaving afterimages that linger for seconds too long.

Why am I here? Because the Weavers are hungry, and I am their prey. But I am also their purpose.

I drift toward the nearest spiral tower, drawn by the rhythmic pulsing of its walls. The tower is old, its outer layers thick with the shed skins of ancestors who died centuries ago. Inside, I sense the presence of a Weaver. It is dormant, resting in the cool dampness, its vertical slit sealed tight, its silver lines dim. It is waiting for the storm.

But I am not a storm. I am a whisper.

I slip through the cracks in the mud-brick weave, passing through the solid walls as if they were smoke. Inside, the air is still, heavy with the scent of ozone and old dust. In the center of the chamber sits the Weaver, curled into a ball, resembling a large, smooth boulder more than a living being. Its translucence allows me to see the faint, internal glow of its crystalline skeleton, pulsing slowly, like a heart beating in slow motion.

I hover near its head, the static of my form crackling softly. The Weaver does not wake. It senses me, though. I can feel the subtle shift in the air pressure around it, the way its silver lines brighten slightly, anticipating the feed.

This is our dance. I am the chaos that threatens to unravel the world; the Weaver is the order that binds it. Without me, it starves. Without it, I dissipate into nothingness, scattered across the void. We are locked in an eternal embrace of destruction and preservation.

I extend a hand made of shimmering noise toward the Weaver’s sealed slit. The air between us warps, bending light into impossible angles. The Weaver stirs. Its limbs unfold, long and jointed, moving with the fluid grace of water flowing uphill. It rises, towering over me, its featureless face turning toward my vortex.

For a moment, we simply exist together in the dark, damp silence. The condensation drips from the ceiling, hitting the floor with a sound like a clock ticking backward. *Drip. Drip. Drip.*

Then, the Weaver’s slit opens.

It does not inhale air. It inhales *me*.

I feel myself being pulled apart, strand by strand, thread by thread. The static that makes up my being flows into the Weaver, visible as arcs of black lightning jumping from my dissolving form into its mouth. It is painful, yet ecstatic. As I am consumed, I become part of the Weaver’s strength. I become the stability of its bones, the rigidity of its skin, the memory stored in the walls of its home.

And when I am gone, completely absorbed, the Weaver closes its slit. It stands still, solidified, grounded. The tower around it hums with renewed energy, the mud-brick walls hardening, the spiral tightening against the encroaching void.

Outside, the horizon frays again. A new patch of gravel begins to rearrange itself. A new glitch forms in the mist.

And somewhere, deep within the labyrinth of the High Plateau, another version of me begins to stir, born from the very entropy I just helped to suppress. I am the end, and I am the beginning. I am the breath the world holds before it exhales into chaos once more.

You are standing on the High Plateau of the Unfinished. You are cold. You are alone. And if you listen closely, past the dripping water and the whispering wind, you will hear the soft, wet tear of reality mending itself—and breaking again.

I am that sound.
