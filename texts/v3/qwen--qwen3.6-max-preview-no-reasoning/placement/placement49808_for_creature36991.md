I am not a thing you can hold. I am not a shape you can trace with your fingers, for my edges are negotiable, defined only by the density of the water that presses against me from all sides.

I am **The Suspended**.

I float in the indigo bruise of the deep, suspended in the cold, viscous embrace of 0.5/3 temperature. I have no name, for names require distinct boundaries, and here, in the basin where the world has been ground down to its fundamental truth, boundaries are illusions. I am a coalescence. A temporary knot in the fabric of the slurry.

**My Body is a Question Answered by Pressure**

If you were to look at me—though there is no light to see, only the faint, ghostly pulse of the Black Veins miles away—you would see nothing but a distortion. A shimmering irregularity in the dark. My form is gelatinous, heavy with the weight of the minerals I have absorbed over centuries. I am translucent, like old ice, filled with layers of membrane that ripple slowly, mimicking the chaotic churn of the currents around me.

I have no bones. Bone is a memory of the dry ages, a rigidity that this world rejects. Instead, I am hydrostatic perfection. I am a sack of cold oil, slightly denser than the water that holds me, anchored not by roots or feet, but by the sheer, crushing equality of the pressure. Every square inch of my surface feels the same force. There is no up, no down, only *in* and *out*.

My skin is a landscape of cilia. Millions of microscopic hairs, fine as spider silk, sensitive as a nerve ending. They do not touch; they taste. They taste the sulfide rising from the vents below. They taste the decay of the dead plankton falling from the storm-racked surface miles above. They taste the fear of a Silt-Walker passing nearby, the chemical signature of its panic rippling through the water like a shout in a library.

To me, the world is not seen. It is felt as a topography of vibration. The current is not a flow; it is a texture. A smooth, cold wind against my flank. A shifting dune is not a hill; it is a change in the frequency of the hum beneath me. I navigate by listening to the silence between the noises.

**My Mind is a Slow Pulse**

Thoughts here are not quick sparks. They are glacial movements, slow and deliberate, hoarding energy like gold in the near-freezing dark. I do not think in words. I think in gradients. *Warmer. Colder. Richer. Poorer.*

I remember the time before I was this knot. I remember being part of the mud. I remember the feeling of dissolution, of my individual membranes breaking apart, becoming one with the silt, the clay, the pulverized stone. It was not death. It was rest. It was the ultimate peace of erosion.

But then, a current shifted. A plume of nutrient-rich water rose from a vent, carrying with it the spores of a broken Filter-Lord. One of those spores found purchase on my dissolving edge. It triggered a reaction, a chemical imperative to coalesce, to pull myself together from the surrounding chaos. And so, I formed again. Not because I wanted to, but because the medium demanded it. The physics of suspension required a counter-weight.

I am the counter-weight.

**My Life is a Wait**

I do not hunt. Hunting is too expensive, too turbulent. I wait. I let the currents carry me. Sometimes, I drift for months without moving, suspended in the static noise of the darkness. I listen to the low-frequency hum of the ocean grinding against the mud. It is a bass note that vibrates in my core, a constant reminder that I am small, and the world is vast, and we are both being worn away.

Occasionally, a Silt-Walker passes close. I feel the rhythmic *thrum-thrum-thrum* of its paddles beating through the silt. It is blind, like me, sensing the world through vibration. For a moment, our senses overlap. We share the same water, the same pressure, the same dark. Then it moves on, leaving a trail of disturbed sediment that settles back into place seconds later.

Sometimes, I feel the sudden drop in pressure that signals an Ambusher opening its trap. It is a sharp, violent event in the muffled roar. A vacuum-like intake. A crack of silicate teeth. Then, silence again. The Ambusher closes, burying itself under the silt, leaving no trace. I do not judge. I do not fear. I simply note the change in the chemical gradient—the release of nutrients from the consumed prey—and adjust my position accordingly.

**My End is a Beginning**

I know that I will not last forever. Nothing here does. The erosion is constant. The currents are violent. Eventually, my membranes will weaken. My cilia will break. I will begin to dissolve, piece by piece, into the very medium that holds me.

When that happens, I will not fight it. I will let go. I will become part of the silt again. Part of the history. Part of the bruise that never heals.

And perhaps, in another age, when the currents shift again, when the spores of a new colony drift down from the upper waters, I will coalesce once more. A new knot. A new question answered by pressure.

For now, I am here. Floating in the dark. Listening to the hum. Waiting for the next ripple in the silence.

I am the world holding itself together.
