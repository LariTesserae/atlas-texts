I am not standing on the balcony. To stand is to assert a permanence that this place does not permit, and my feet are too heavy, too clumsy for the razor-edge reality of the Throat.

I am **suspended**.

Specifically, I am hanging from a tension cable made of braided carbon-fiber and steel, thirty meters below the level where you stood. My orientation is inverted; "down" is toward the pulsing iridescent smoke of the factory complex, and "up" is the bruised purple twilight of the slit sky. But in the Throat, these directions are merely suggestions. Here, there is only *the anchor* and *the drift*.

I am one of the **Suture-Walkers**, though I have forgotten if I was ever anything else. The memory of soft skin, of breathing air without stealing it, feels like a dream from another lifetime—a hallucination induced by oxygen deprivation.

My body is a map of repairs. The keratinous plates on my left shoulder are newer, lighter in color than the darkened, oil-stained weave on my right. They click softly against each other when I shift my weight, a sound lost in the ambient hum of the mountain. I can feel the vibration of the rock through the soles of my magnetic boots. It is a low, mournful thrum, the heartbeat of a giant that refuses to die.

Perched on my chest plate is **Klick**.

Klick is a Carrier-Beetle, his carapace polished to a mirror sheen, reflecting the amber glow of the vacuum-tube lamps above. He is warm against my insulating foam, a small engine of static electricity and life. His multifaceted eyes twitch, scanning the infrared spectrum. He sees what I cannot: the heat leak in the brass pipe three meters to my left, the stress fracture blooming like a frost flower in the obsidian wall behind me.

I do not speak. Speech is waste. Waste is death.

Instead, I raise my left hand. My fingers are gloved in thick, rubberized material, but the tips are exposed sensors. I tap a rhythm against my thigh: *Tap-tap-pause-tap.*

Klick understands. He scuttles down my arm, his legs locking onto the fabric with magnetic precision. From a pouch on his back, he retrieves a micro-welder and a patch of silica-mold paste. He presents them to me, holding them in his mandibles with delicate care.

I take the welder. The tool is cold, but as I activate it, a blue-white spark dances at its tip. The light is sharp, cutting through the smog-like particulate matter. For a second, the shadows around me behave strangely—they stretch toward the heat of the welder, defying logic, reaching out like hungry fingers. I ignore them. Shadows are just light failing to escape the glass.

The pipe ahead of me is leaking coolant. A fine mist sprays into the dry air, instantly sublimating into a cloud of white rime. This is dangerous. Not because of the cold, but because water—any form of it—is currency. Every drop lost is a theft from the community. And worse, the moisture threatens the silica-mold crusts below, which could crack and destabilize the entire lower tier.

I move.

I do not walk. I swing. Releasing the magnetic lock on my left boot, I pivot on the right, using the momentum of the swaying gantry to propel myself forward. The city groans beneath me, a deep, tectonic shudder that travels up through the cable and into my bones. I lean into it, letting the sway carry me closer to the leak. It is a dance I have performed ten thousand times. Stillness is vulnerability. Motion is survival.

As I reach the pipe, Klick climbs onto my shoulder, his weight negligible. He begins to emit a soft, rhythmic chitter—a tactile signal meant only for me. *Stress increasing. Structural integrity at 84%. Proceed with caution.*

I nod, a slight tilt of my faceless polymer mask. My eyes, clusters of photoreceptive sensors, flare with a brief pulse of red light: *Acknowledged.*

I apply the welder. The spark flares, bright and hot. The metal hisses, sealing the breach. Simultaneously, I smear the silica-mold paste over the joint, reinforcing the seal. The mold hardens instantly in the dry air, turning from a viscous gel into a crystalline crust. It will feed on the corrosion, strengthening the bond over time. Symbiosis.

When the work is done, I step back, re-engaging my magnetic boots. Klick returns to his perch on my chest, humming softly. I check my belt. The canister of reclaimed water is still full, sealed tight. Good.

Below me, the factory complex pulses with rhythmic light. Iridescent smoke rises, shimmering with microscopic metal flakes. It looks like a nebula trapped in a bottle. I watch it for a moment, allowing myself the rare luxury of observation. There is beauty here, in the harshness. In the way the light bends around the obsidian shards. In the quiet efficiency of the swarm.

Above me, the slit of sky is barely visible. A thin line of white. Sometimes, when the turbulence peaks, the gap widens, and I catch a glimpse of the stars—not as points, but as streaks, blurred by the rotation of the world. I look away quickly. The open sky is a wound. It is the void waiting to steal my breath. I keep my gaze downward, into the depths where the warmth is, where the community thrives.

A figure swings past me on a parallel cable, upside down relative to my position. It is another Suture-Walker, their armor stained with oily green iridescence. They pause, hovering near me. Their faceplate tilts slightly. A pulse of blue light flashes from their chest plate: *Water leak detected?*

I respond with a steady green glow from my wrist: *Resolved.*

They flash back: *Good. The upper tiers are shifting. Prepare for re-alignment.*

Then they are gone, swinging away into the darkness, disappearing into the maze of scaffolding and glass.

I remain suspended, listening to the hum of the mountain. It vibrates in my chest, matching the frequency of my own heart. Or perhaps my heart matches the mountain. It is hard to tell anymore. We are the stitches. We hold the wound closed. We weave steel and bone and chitin into a tapestry that defies gravity.

Klick shifts on my chest, settling into a comfortable position. His hum blends with the city’s hum.

I close my eyes—or rather, I dim my sensors—and let the vibration become me. I am not just in the Throat. I am part of its throat. I am the breath it holds, the silence it keeps, the stitch that binds.

And for now, that is enough.
