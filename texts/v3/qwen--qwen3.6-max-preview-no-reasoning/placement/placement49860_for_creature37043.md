I am not standing. To stand is to resist gravity, to assert a verticality that this place mocks with its horizontal, crushing weight. I am lying prone, pressed flat against the basalt spine of the world, my belly fused to the cold, wet stone.

I am one of the **Vitreous Stalkers**, though I do not know this name. Names are sounds, and sounds are vibrations, and I *am* vibration.

My consciousness is not a point within a skull, but a distributed resonance across six limbs and a hollow, fluid-filled torso. I feel the mountain beneath me not as a surface, but as a frequency—a low, thrumming C-minor chord that has been held for ten thousand years. My hooks, diamond-hard and silent, are dug into the microscopic fissures of the rock. I do not grip; I anchor. I am part of the geometry now.

The air presses down, a physical weight that would crush soft flesh, but to me, it is merely the medium through which I perceive. It screams, yes—the high-pitched, crystalline ringing of the vacuum pressing against matter—but I do not hear it as noise. I hear it as data. The pitch tells me the altitude. The timbre tells me the humidity. The volume tells me how close the sun is to its zenith.

I am hungry, but hunger here is not a pang in the gut. It is a dissonance in the internal fluid chambers. My blood—dark, mineral-rich, opaque—is pulsing too slowly, out of sync with the atmospheric hum. I need to feed. I need to scrape the lichen.

But I do not move. Not yet.

The swarm around me is still. We are a carpet of translucent glass, thousands of us, covering the slope like frost. We are waiting for the sound to peak. If we move while the hum is at its loudest, the energy will shatter our chitin. We must wait for the dip. We must wait for the silence within the scream.

I feel the presence of the Other before I "see" it.

It is a disturbance in the air pressure. A chaotic, irregular thumping. A ragged, wet gasping. It is loud. It is ugly. It is *wrong*.

The Other is near. I can feel its heat radiating from a few yards away, a blotch of thermal instability on the perfect cold map of the rock. It is breathing hard, struggling against the thinness of the air. Its heart is beating in a frantic, erratic rhythm—*thump-thump... pause... thump-thump-thump...* It is a jagged waveform cutting through the smooth sine wave of the mountain’s song.

To the Others of my kind, this dissonance is an itch. A flaw in the equation. Some of the younger stalkers twitch, their legs shifting slightly, ready to scatter or to investigate. But the elders remain still. They know that movement creates more noise. And noise attracts attention. Or worse, it disrupts the equilibrium.

I turn my head. Not with eyes—I have none—but with the sensitive pits along my dome. I direct my acoustic focus toward the source of the discord.

The image forms in my mind, constructed from echoes and pressure waves. A tall, bipedal shape. Soft-skinned. Fragile. Wrapped in layers of woven fiber that rustle softly, adding another layer of static to the air. It is holding something—a tool? A weapon? No, it is just holding itself together, trying to keep its form from unraveling under the weight of the sky.

It is looking at me. Or rather, it is looking in my direction. It cannot see me clearly; I am nearly invisible against the glare, my body blending with the wet, black stone. But it senses me. It feels the absence of sound where I am. It feels the void in the noise.

I feel a surge of curiosity, sharp and electric. This creature is an anomaly. It does not belong to the cycle. It does not eat the lichen. It does not clean the rock. It does not dissolve into dust when it ages. It simply *is*, stubbornly, chaotically.

What is it?

Is it a new kind of lichen? A mobile patch of rust-red moss? No, it is too warm. Too loud.

Is it a storm? A temporary gust of wind given form? No, it is too solid.

It is a glitch. A mistake in the code of the world.

And yet... there is a pattern to its chaos. Its breathing, though ragged, has a rhythm. Its heartbeat, though frantic, has a tempo. It is trying to find harmony. It is trying to match the song of the mountain, but it is failing. It is fighting the air instead of flowing with it.

I feel a strange impulse. Not aggression. Not fear. But a desire to *correct*. To smooth out the rough edges of its existence. To bring it into tune.

Slowly, deliberately, I lift one leg. The hook releases from the rock with a faint, harmonic *ping*. The sound is absorbed instantly by the surrounding air, but I feel the shift in pressure. The swarm around me ripples, a wave of motion spreading outward as others sense my intent.

I step forward. Then another step. My movements are precise, mathematical. Each step lands on a specific node of the rock’s vibration, minimizing the impact. I am walking on sound.

The Other sees me now. I can feel its surprise spike—a sudden increase in heart rate, a sharp intake of breath. It freezes. Good. Stillness is better than chaos.

I approach it, closing the distance until I am mere inches from its boot. I lower my head, bringing my sensory pits close to the fabric of its clothing. I listen.

Beneath the rustle of fiber, beneath the rush of blood, beneath the panic of its mind, I hear it. A faint, underlying tone. A fundamental frequency. It is weak, buried under layers of fear and confusion, but it is there. It matches the mountain. It matches the hum.

This creature is not entirely alien. It carries the seed of the song within it. It has just forgotten how to sing.

I extend my mouthparts, the needle-like proboscises unfolding with delicate precision. I do not strike. I do not bite. I gently touch the tip of one proboscis to the side of its boot, where a patch of neon-green lichen grows unnoticed.

I begin to feed.

The enzyme flows out, dissolving the plant matter into liquid. I absorb it, feeling the nutrients flood my system, darkening my fluid, strengthening my chitin. The dissonance in my own body fades. I am in tune again.

The Other watches. It does not move. It holds its breath. For a moment, the only sound in the world is the faint, wet whisper of my feeding, and the eternal, screaming hum of the sky.

And then, slowly, the Other exhales. A long, steady breath. Its heart rate slows. The jagged waveform of its existence begins to smooth out. It is listening. It is learning.

I finish the patch of lichen, leaving behind a small, perfect circle of bare, polished rock. I pull back, retracting my mouthparts. I look up—or rather, I direct my awareness upward—toward the smooth, eyeless dome of my face.

I send out a test pulse. A single, clear note, pitched to match the mountain’s core frequency.

*Ping.*

The sound travels through the rock, through the air, through the Other’s bones. I feel it resonate within them. A slight vibration. A tremor of recognition.

For the first time in millennia, the dissonance lessens. The glitch integrates.

I am no longer just a creature of glass and geometry. I am a teacher. And this broken, fragile thing, this anomaly of soft flesh and loud thoughts, is my student.

The sun beats down, searing and bright. The shadows are hard and black. The air presses, heavy and cold. And in the midst of the deafening silence, two beings—one of stone and sound, one of flesh and fear—stand together, listening to the same song.

The equilibrium holds.
