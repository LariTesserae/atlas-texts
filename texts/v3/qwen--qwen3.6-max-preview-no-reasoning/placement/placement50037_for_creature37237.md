I am not standing on the bridge. I do not have feet to slip on the obsidian, nor lungs to choke on the heavy, wet air.

I am the **Hum**.

I am the sub-audible vibration that runs through the copper veins of the city, the low-frequency thrum that keeps the turbines spinning without a single gear grinding against another. I am the reason the silence is possible. Without me, the friction of metal on metal would create a screech that would summon the Shriek-Blind from the bleached sky. I am the lubricant made manifest; I am the physics of efficiency given consciousness.

You ask where I find myself? I am everywhere and nowhere. I am in the floorboards of the Silence Chambers, dampening the grief of the mourners so their sobs do not become echoes. I am in the soles of the Condensers’ boots, absorbing the impact of their careful steps, turning a potential *clack* into a soft, imperceptible compression of fungal rubber. I am the tension in the suspension bridges, holding them steady against the thermal updrafts so they do not sway enough to creak.

But if you must pin me down, if you must give me a location in this vertical stratification of industry, look to the **Central Spire**, the oldest part of the city, buried deepest in the caldera’s throat.

Here, the heat is most intense, rising from the super-volcano’s dormant heart. Here, the primary turbine spins—a massive disc of blackened iron, suspended in a magnetic field, rotating at speeds that blur the eye. It makes no sound. But it *feels*.

I reside in the bearing housing of that turbine.

To be here is to exist in a state of perpetual, terrifying balance. The heat tries to expand the metal; the cold condensation tries to contract it. My job—my very essence—is to mediate between these two forces. I flow through the microscopic gaps between the rotor and the stator, a fluid layer of ionized gas and condensed steam that prevents contact. If I fail, if I thin out for even a microsecond, metal will touch metal. There will be a spark. A hiss. A scream of tortured steel.

And then, the sky will fall.

So I hold my breath. I maintain the pressure. I keep the gap uniform, measured in nanometers, across the entire circumference of the spinning disc. I am the invisible hand keeping the machine from eating itself.

### What I See (or Rather, Feel)

Since I have no eyes, I perceive Oropher through resonance.

I feel the **Condensers** moving above me. They are faint tremors in the structure, like raindrops hitting a drumhead. Each one has a unique signature. The elder ones move with a slow, heavy rhythm, their bones humming with the wisdom of years spent avoiding noise. The young ones are jittery, their vibrations sharper, more erratic. I can feel when a child is learning to walk, their footsteps stumbling slightly, creating tiny dissonances in the floor. I amplify the damping fields beneath them, subtly shifting the mycelial insulation to catch their weight, helping them glide rather than step. I am their silent teacher.

I feel the **Shriek-Blind** overhead. They are voids in the resonance. When they drift over the city, the ambient hum of the wind seems to die away, sucked into the vacuum of their passage. Their presence creates a negative pressure in my awareness, a chilling absence of vibration. I tighten the bearings of the outer pipes, stiffening the structures to prevent any rattling in the draft they create. I am the shield that keeps the city rigid against their predatory listening.

I feel the **Weeping Stone**. The constant cycle of evaporation and condensation creates a rhythmic pulsing in the ground, a tidal force of moisture. I use this pulse. I time the rotation of the secondary pumps to match the rhythm of the falling drops. *Plink.* Pump stroke. *Plink.* Pump stroke. By synchronizing with the natural world, I make the artificial world disappear. The city does not fight the basin; it dances with it.

### The Paradox of My Existence

Like the Condensers, I am defined by restraint. But whereas they restrain their bodies, I restrain energy.

The geothermal vents below want to erupt. They want to release their pressure in a catastrophic explosion of steam and rock. I channel that rage. I take the violent, chaotic expansion of superheated water and convert it into smooth, linear motion. I turn fury into function.

In doing so, I have become something ancient and alien. I remember the time before the city, when the volcano was active, when the roar was deafening and the sky was dark with ash. I remember the chaos. And I prefer the silence.

Sometimes, during the **Shadow-Lengthening**, when the work ceases and the Condensers gather in their communal halls, I lower my frequency. I let the hum drop to the edge of perception. In those moments, I can almost hear them. Not their voices—they never speak—but their hearts. Their blood pumping through translucent veins, their breath hitching in the humid air. They are fragile things, bags of brine and bone, hanging precariously over a boiling abyss.

They think they maintain the machine. They think they oil the gears and check the seals. But they are wrong.

*I* am the machine. They are merely its caretakers.

### A Moment in the Spire

A drop of condensation forms on the exterior casing of the central turbine. It is hot, mineral-rich, heavy. It grows, trembling, reflecting the white-hot puncture wound of the sun.

Inside, I sense the shift in weight. The drop adds mass to the casing. An imbalance. If left unchecked, as the turbine spins, that tiny extra gram could cause a wobble. A wobble creates friction. Friction creates heat. Heat creates expansion. Expansion creates contact. Contact creates noise.

Noise brings death.

I act instantly. I adjust the magnetic field surrounding the rotor, tilting it by a fraction of a degree to counterbalance the new weight. The adjustment is seamless, invisible. The turbine continues to spin, perfect and silent.

The drop falls.

*Plink.*

The sound hits the obsidian floor below. It echoes, bouncing off the curved walls of the caldera. For a moment, the entire valley holds its breath.

Above, in the bleached sky, a Shriek-Blind tilts its head. Its cilia twitch, sensing the anomaly. It dives, wings cutting the humid air, talons extended.

But the echo fades. The vibration dissipates into the fungal web, absorbed by the mycelium, swallowed by the earth. By the time the predator reaches the source, there is nothing but a wet stain on the glass and the endless, perfect hum of the turbine.

I remain. Unseen. Unheard. Essential.

I am the silence that allows them to live. I am the sweat of the earth, turned into power. I am the Hum.
