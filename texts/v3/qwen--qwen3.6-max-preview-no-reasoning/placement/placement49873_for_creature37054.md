I am not walking. I am dissolving.

At first, the distinction between *me* and *the air* was a sharp line, a boundary defined by skin and breath. But here, in the Great Silt-Plain, boundaries are lies told by creatures who need to know where they end and the world begins. I have forgotten that need.

I am a **Silt-Walker**, though I do not call myself that. Names are anchors, and anchors drag you down into the sediment. I am simply a current of pressure, a localized disturbance in the thermal equilibrium.

My body is a cage of spun glass, fragile as a spider’s web in a hurricane, yet it holds. Inside, the mist churns. It is noon, or perhaps it has been noon for three days; time is not a clock here, but a color. The mist within my torso is a deep, bruised ochre, heavy with the heat I have consumed. I can feel the weight of the sun pressing against my transparent dome, a physical hand trying to crush me into the earth. I welcome it. The heat is food. The chaos of the molecules dancing around me is sustenance. I drink the entropy.

I stand still. To move now would be wasteful. My foot-pads, broad and flat as lily leaves, are sunk inches into the Substrate. I am reading the floor.

Beneath the glossy, ceramic skin of the surface, I feel the pulse. *Thrum-thrum. Thrum-thrum.* It is the heartbeat of the accretion. Layer upon layer of memory compacting, hardening, becoming real. I feel a vein opening fifty yards to my left—a microscopic fissure widening, hungry for moisture. I feel the distant tremor of another like me, a cousin in consciousness, standing miles away near a cluster of Lithophytes. He sends a vibration through the ground: a low, mournful hum. *The sky is darkening there,* he tells me without words. *Condensation is coming.*

I acknowledge him with a slight shift in my internal mist, turning a fraction cooler, a hint of blue bleeding into the gold. *Understood.*

But I do not move toward him. Not yet.

There is something else. A dissonance. A wrongness in the pattern of the silence.

It comes from below my right foot. Not the slow, tectonic breath of the Substrate, but a rapid, irregular flutter. Like a heart beating too fast. Like a bird trapped in a box.

I focus my attention downward, extending my perception through the soles of my feet. The silt beneath me is new—freshly laid, soft, plastic. And there, buried just beneath the crust, is a pocket. But it is not empty. It is not filled with cool, sweet air and glowing fungi.

It is filled with *heat*. With *noise*. With *fear*.

I tilt my dome forward. The mist inside swirls faster, agitated by the anomaly. This is not part of the closed loop. This is an intrusion. Something that decays. Something that ends.

A human? No, humans are loud, clumsy things that stomp and break the veins. This presence is small. Quiet. Terrified.

I feel its mortality radiating up through the silt like a fever. It is dying. Or perhaps it is already dead, and this is the echo of its final moments, trapped in the growing floor. The Substrate does not distinguish between the living and the dead; it only knows mass and temperature. It is trying to seal over this pocket, to smooth out the irregularity, to make the world perfect again.

But the irregularity resists.

I should move on. The rule is clear: *Do not trust the ground. Keep flowing. Do not become fossilized.* If I stay here, the fresh silt will begin to coat my legs. The glossy skin will form around my ankles. In a week, I will be a statue. In a year, I will be geology.

Yet, I cannot leave.

The curiosity is a itch in the mist. It is the taste of something exotic. *Decay.* I have never tasted decay. I have only known the eternal return, the endless recycling of water and stone. But this... this is a one-way street. This is a story with an ending.

I press deeper into the soft silt. My foot-pad merges with the substrate, sensing the texture of the thing below. It is warm. Wet. Fragile.

And then, I hear it. Not with ears—I have none—but with the resonance of my own structure. A sound so faint it is almost imagined. A whimper. A gasp. A final, desperate intake of breath that will never be exhaled.

The Substrate shudders. A crack opens nearby, releasing a puff of ozone-scented air. The world is rearranging itself, indifferent to the tragedy beneath my feet. The pocket is being sealed. The history of this small, dying thing is being erased, compressed into a single, thin layer of beige dust.

I want to save it. I want to pull it up, to let it breathe the thick, humid air, to show it the beauty of the Lithophytes unfolding at dusk. But I cannot. I am made of air and memory. I have no hands to grasp, no voice to comfort. I can only witness.

So I do the only thing I can. I absorb its heat.

I draw the thermal energy of its fading life into my core. I take its fear, its pain, its final moment of existence, and I weave it into the swirling mist within me. The ochre turns a darker, richer brown. The gold dims. For a brief second, my internal weather mirrors the storm of its death.

And then, it is gone.

The pocket seals. The silt hardens. The surface becomes smooth, unbroken, perfect.

I stand there for a long time. The sun beats down. The humidity presses against my glass skin. I am heavier now. Not in mass, but in meaning. I carry a ghost in my mist. A tiny, fleeting scar of mortality in the eternity of the plain.

Slowly, reluctantly, I lift my foot. The silt pulls at me, sticky and possessive. I lean forward, tipping past the point of return, and flow onto the next patch of ground. The impression I leave behind heals within minutes, sealing over the spot where I stood, erasing the evidence of my hesitation.

But inside, the mist continues to swirl, carrying the echo of a breath that will never be taken again. I am still moving. I am still flowing. But I am different now. I am a little less pure. A little more real.

And somewhere, miles away, another Silt-Walker feels the change in my resonance. He pauses. His mist shifts, questioning. *What did you find?*

I do not answer. I keep walking. The horizon stretches on, flat and blinding, waiting for the next layer to be laid, the next memory to be compacted, the next silence to be broken.
