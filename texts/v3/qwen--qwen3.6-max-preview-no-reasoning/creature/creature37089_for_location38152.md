They are the **Mycelial Scribes**, though they do not call themselves this. To name them is to impose a boundary, and in the Verdant Cloister, boundaries are illusions that rot away within hours. They are the soft, pale shadows that move through the green twilight, creatures of low metabolism and high patience, shaped by the cold dampness into beings who value stillness above all else.

At first glance, you might mistake them for extensions of the flora itself. Their skin is not skin in the mammalian sense, but a dermal layer of translucent, keratinized tissue that mimics the texture of wet bark or cured leaf-skin. It is pale, almost albino, lacking pigment because there is no sun here to protect against. Instead, their bodies are mapped with faint, bioluminescent veins—traceries of blue-green light that pulse in rhythm with the slow respiration of the city around them. When they stand still, which is often, they vanish into the background, becoming just another knot in the root-mass, another shadow beneath the ferns.

They are small, rarely exceeding four feet in height, with limbs that are long, slender, and jointed with a fluidity that suggests bonelessness. Their fingers are elongated, tipped with hard, blackened nails that serve as both tools and anchors. These digits are sensitive to the slightest vibration; they can feel the growth rings expanding in the tree-trunk walls, sensing the shift of the city before it happens. They do not speak with voices, for sound carries too far in the silent cloister and disturbs the delicate equilibrium. Instead, they communicate through touch and scent. A brush of fingertips against a neighbor’s arm conveys complex emotional states; the release of specific pheromones from glands along their spine signals danger, hunger, or communal intent.

Their faces are smooth, devoid of noses or prominent brows. Their eyes are large, dark, and lidless, covered by a nictitating membrane that keeps them moist in the perpetually humid air. These eyes see little in the way of color—the world is a monochrome of grays and greens to them—but they perceive heat signatures with terrifying clarity. In the refrigerated gloom, every living thing glows like a ember. To look at a Mycelial Scribe is to see two pools of darkness surrounded by a halo of faint thermal radiation.

They live in the hollows of the giant trees, in chambers that are not built but *persuaded* into existence. The Scribes do not carve wood; they sing to it. Or rather, they hum low-frequency vibrations that resonate with the lignin structure, encouraging the tree to soften its cellular bonds in specific patterns. Over weeks, a wall will recede, forming a shelf; a floor will dip, creating a basin. This is their architecture: a collaboration with the host. They sleep curled in these niches, wrapped in blankets woven from spider-silk and dried moss, their body heat shared among family units to survive the biting chill.

Their society is organized around the **Flow**. Since nothing is static, their culture is one of constant adaptation. There are no permanent homes, only temporary shelters. A family unit may occupy a particular hollow for a season, until the tree’s growth shifts the entrance or the humidity levels become unfavorable. Then, they migrate, moving slowly along the vine-bridges, carrying their few possessions—pouches of seeds, vials of collected condensation, carved memory-sticks of hardened resin—in bundles strapped to their backs. They are nomads of the vertical world, ascending and descending the thirty-meter vaults in response to the microclimates created by the canopy’s density.

They are farmers, but not of crops. They farm *light*. The Scribes tend to the bioluminescent fungi that cling to the undersides of branches, pruning dead growth and applying nutrient pastes made from crushed insects and decaying matter to encourage brighter blooms. They also cultivate the "Sun-Catchers"—large, translucent leaves engineered through generations of selective breeding to capture the faint ambient glow from the upper canopy and channel it down into the deeper, darker recesses where the Scribes dwell. Without these cultivated lights, the lower levels would be pitch black, and the Scribes would freeze in the absolute zero of sensory deprivation.

Their diet is frugal and fungal. They consume the soft, spongy mycelium that threads through the peat floors, rich in nutrients leached from the bedrock below. They supplement this with nectar from night-blooming flowers and the occasional blind worm or pale insect caught in traps of sticky sap. Eating is a quiet, meditative act, performed in circles, sharing food hand-to-hand to reinforce social bonds. There is no waste; everything consumed is returned to the system through composting pits located near the weeping rocks, where the mineral-rich water accelerates decomposition.

The Scribes are deeply spiritual, worshipping the **Great Breath**—the collective transpiration of the forest. They believe that consciousness is not individual but distributed, flowing through the root-network like blood. To harm the tree is to harm oneself; to stagnate is to die. Their rituals involve standing barefoot on the exposed roots during the rare moments when the wind filters through the upper canopy, feeling the vibration travel up through their soles, connecting them to the miles of underground mycelium that bind the mountain together. They chant in silence, their throats vibrating without sound, sending messages down into the earth, listening for the echo of the planet’s heartbeat.

They are wary of outsiders, not out of malice, but out of ecological necessity. An outsider brings fire (even if unintended), brings noise, brings chaotic movement. The Scribes observe strangers from the shadows, their thermal vision tracking the heat bloom of an intruder’s body. If the stranger respects the silence, moves slowly, and does not break the living structures, the Scribes may emerge, offering a gift of glowing fungus or a guiding touch. But if the stranger disrupts the Flow—if they cut a branch, light a spark, or shout—the Scribes retreat, sealing the pathways behind them with rapid-growth vines, leaving the intruder alone in the suffocating green dark.

To live among the Mycelial Scribes is to learn the art of slowing down. Time here is measured not in hours, but in the widening of a ring, the dripping of a drop, the shifting of a shadow. They are the caretakers of the Cloister, the gentle hands that guide the chaos of nature into the order of habitation. They are fragile, cold, and beautiful, existing in the thin margin between plant and animal, between life and decay, breathing in unison with the lung of the world.
