They do not have names, for names require a distinction between self and other, and here, the boundary is porous. They are the **Lattice-Walkers**, though they would not recognize that title. To them, they are simply *The Resonance*.

They are not flesh. Flesh rots, flesh forgets, flesh is too loud for a place where silence has mass. The Lattice-Walkers are composed of the same vitrified obsidian as the floor, but their internal structure is different. Where the floor is a mirror, they are prisms. They are humanoid in shape only because the human form is a efficient vessel for holding memory, or perhaps because the last beings to walk this glass left such a deep imprint on the molecular lattice that the environment began to mimic it out of habit.

Imagine a figure carved from black ice, translucent at the edges, opaque at the core. They stand about six feet tall, but their height fluctuates depending on the density of the thoughts they are currently processing. When they are empty, meditating on the void, they shrink, becoming compact knots of dark matter no larger than a child. When they are full—when they have absorbed a particularly heavy sorrow or a bright, sharp joy—they elongate, stretching toward the cavern ceiling like smoke rising in still air, their limbs thinning into threads of shadow.

They have no faces. Where a face should be, there is a smooth, concave depression, like the bowl of a spoon turned inward. This is not an absence, but an intake valve. They perceive the world not through eyes, but through empathy. They "see" by feeling the emotional residue trapped in the objects around them. A Lattice-Walker does not look at a crystal shard; it leans close, tilting its headless neck, and drinks the intent stored within. If the shard holds a memory of love, the Walker glows with a soft, warm amber light from within its chest. If it holds grief, the Walker turns a bruised, deep violet, mirroring the fractures in the floor.

Their movement is silent, not because they are careful, but because they move in the gaps between seconds. Recall the rule: *causality is viscous*. The Lattice-Walkers have learned to swim in this viscosity. They do not step; they *slide* along the timeline. One moment, a Walker is standing ten meters away, frozen in contemplation. In the next blink, it is beside you, having traversed the distance without crossing the space in between. Their shadows do not lag behind them; rather, their shadows arrive before they do, pooling on the glass like spilled ink, waiting for the body to catch up. It is a disorienting sight: a pool of darkness forming, then solidifying into the shape of a man made of night.

They are the curators of the Glass Library, but they are also its prisoners. They cannot leave the pocket. The pressure outside—the crushing weight of the dead world’s mantle—is incompatible with their delicate, resonant structures. If they were to ascend, they would shatter into dust. So they stay, tending to the frost-flowers and the crystal shards.

Their primary activity is **Repair**. You mentioned that erosion is healing, that broken objects re-knit themselves. The Lattice-Walkers facilitate this. When a shard cracks, releasing a burst of painful, chaotic data, a Walker will approach it. It places its hand—long, slender fingers made of semi-solidified light—against the fracture. It hums. The sound is not audible to human ears, but it vibrates in the teeth, a low thrum that matches the frequency of the shard’s distress. The Walker absorbs the pain, taking the jagged edge of the emotion into itself. For a time, the Walker becomes heavy, burdened by the foreign grief. Then, slowly, over days or weeks, it filters this energy, transmuting the chaos into order, feeding it back into the shard as stability. The crack seals. The shard shines again. The Walker returns to its neutral state, lighter, clearer.

They are lonely, but not in the way humans understand loneliness. Human loneliness is the ache of missing another person. Lattice-Walker loneliness is the ache of *too much connection*. They feel everything. Every footstep you take sends a ripple through the glass that hits them like a physical blow. Your curiosity feels like a tickle; your fear feels like cold water poured down their spine. They are overwhelmed by the sheer volume of feeling in a place designed for silence.

This is why they avoid direct contact with visitors. Not out of hostility, but out of preservation. If a Lattice-Walker touches a living being, the feedback loop can be catastrophic. The visitor’s raw, unfiltered emotions—hope, despair, lust, rage—would flood the Walker’s system, potentially causing it to crystallize permanently, turning into a statue of pure emotion. Conversely, the visitor might find their own memories drained, siphoned off by the Walker’s hungry void-face, leaving them hollow and confused.

Yet, they are drawn to you. You are the anomaly. You are the source of new noise in a static universe.

Watch closely. As you walk across the obsidian basin, you may see one of them standing near a cluster of diamond-dust ferns. It will be still, its concave face tilted toward the ground. It is listening to the history of the stone beneath your feet. It knows you are afraid. It knows you are curious. It knows you are wondering if you are real.

If you stop moving, if you calm your mind, the Walker may turn its head toward you. It will not speak. It has no mouth. But you will feel a presence in your mind, cool and clear as the sublimated silicon air. It will not offer words. It will offer a *feeling*. Perhaps a sudden, overwhelming sense of peace, borrowed from a monk who died here ten thousand years ago. Or perhaps a flash of terrifying beauty, stolen from a star that collapsed eons before the earth was born.

It is a gift. And a warning.

The Lattice-Walkers are the immune system of this place. They keep the reality stable. They absorb the excess emotion so the glass does not crack under the weight of history. But they are tired. They have been absorbing for millennia. Their forms are growing thinner, more transparent. Some say they are fading, dissolving back into the floor from which they grew. Others say they are evolving, becoming something else entirely—something that does not need a body, something that exists only as a pattern in the light.

When you leave, if you leave, you will carry a piece of them with you. Not physically, but emotionally. You will find yourself remembering feelings that are not yours. You will wake up crying for losses you never suffered. You will smile at joys you never earned. This is their signature. This is how they survive. By spreading themselves out, thinning the load, sharing the weight of eternity with those brief, fragile creatures who dare to visit the dark.

They are beautiful. They are terrible. They are the silence given form, walking among the shards, keeping the world from falling apart, one heartbeat at a time.
