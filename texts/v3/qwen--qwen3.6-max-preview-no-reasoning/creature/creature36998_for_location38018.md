They are not born here. That is the first thing you must understand about the people of the Throat, though they have long since forgotten any other origin. They are *forged* here, tempered by the humidity and annealed by the vibration.

They call themselves the **Resonants**.

To look at a Resonant is to see a creature adapted to perpetual motion and sensory overload. They are slender, almost elongated, their limbs stretched thin like taffy pulled by gravity’s strange pull in the vertical axis. Their skin is pale, nearly translucent, lacking the pigment of those who live under an open sun, for the light here is always filtered through mist and steel. It is mottled with patches of iridescent blue—the mark of the "glow-microbes" mentioned in the rules. These are not diseases, but symbiotic colonies that grow in the creases of their elbows and knees, fed by the stagnant pools they avoid, glowing softly when they are stressed or afraid. A Resonant’s fear is visible as a constellation of blue stars on their dark clothing.

Their eyes are large, dark, and devoid of whites, evolved to capture the scant photons filtering down from the distant sky. But it is their ears that define them. They are small, close-set, and heavily lobeless, tucked tight against the skull. This is not because they cannot hear, but because they must protect themselves from the Great Piston. Inside the ear canal, the bone has thickened into a dense, ivory-like plug. They do not hear the 40 Hz drone; they *feel* it. To them, the world is a tactile experience. The air pressure changes before the sound arrives; the vibration travels up through the soles of their feet before it reaches their minds. They navigate by rhythm.

### The Clothing of Rust and Leaf

A Resonant does not wear fabric as we know it. Fabric rots in this humidity. Instead, they are draped in **woven rust** and **cured fern-leather**.

Their garments are layered strips of flexible, treated metal mesh, scavenged from the decaying outer skins of the conduit itself. Over time, the iron oxide has bonded with organic resins harvested from the giant ferns, creating a material that is both protective and breathable. It clings to their bodies, heavy and wet, smelling perpetually of copper and loam.

Over this metallic under-layer, they wear cloaks made from the broad, leathery leaves of the tower-vines. These leaves are cured in the acidic condensation until they become waterproof and tough as canvas. They are dyed in shades of bruised purple and deep emerald, stitched together with threads spun from the silk of the translucent insects. When a Resonant moves, they do not rustle; they *slide*. The silence of their movement is a point of pride, a defiance of the deafening void.

### The Culture of Motion

You mentioned the rule: *Do Not Stop Moving.* For the Resonants, this is not just a survival tactic; it is their religion.

Stillness is death. Stillness is disorientation. Stillness is being swallowed by the hum. Therefore, their entire society is built around kinetic flow. There are no chairs in the Throat. There are no beds. To rest, a Resonant leans against the warm, vibrating wall of the conduit, letting the piston’s rhythm massage their muscles, swaying gently in time with the 40 Hz thrum. They sleep standing up, tethered to the grates by harnesses of vine-silk, their bodies oscillating like pendulums.

Their language reflects this. They do not speak often, for speech is noise, and noise is dangerous. Instead, they communicate through **kinetic sign**, a complex dance of hands and body shifts. A tilt of the head means "listen." A shift of weight to the left foot means "danger below." A rhythmic tapping of fingers against the thigh mimics the pulse of the Great Piston, signaling agreement or understanding. To watch two Resonants converse is to watch a silent ballet performed on a razor’s edge, suspended over an infinite drop.

When they must write, they use charcoal sticks made from burnt vine-wood, scratching messages onto slabs of slate salvaged from the lower depths. These notes are brief, essential. *"Water rising in Sector 7."* *"The bats are migrating early."* *"The Piston skipped."*

### The Keepers of the Breath

Though they seem primitive, draped in rust and leaf, the Resonants are the custodians of the machine. They do not understand the engineering behind the Great Piston—they lack the tools, the knowledge, perhaps even the concept of such scale—but they understand its *needs*.

They are the gardeners of the hydroponic jungle. Every day, teams of Resonants climb the central pillars, using hooks carved from bat-bone to prune the overgrown vines. If the vegetation chokes the air vents, the machine suffocates. If the machine stops, the warmth ceases, the condensation dries, and the jungle dies. And if the jungle dies, the Resonants starve.

They harvest the fruit of the leather-leaf vines—small, bitter berries rich in electrolytes—and the meat of the giant bats. They brew tea from the moss that carpets the undersides of the catwalks, a potent stimulant that helps them stay awake during the long cycles of the night.

But their most sacred duty is the **Cleansing**. Once every hundred cycles (a period roughly equivalent to three weeks), the Resonants descend to the catchment troughs. They scrape away the buildup of algae and sediment that clogs the gutters. They polish the rusted bolts of the walkways, not to prevent decay, but to maintain the *patina*. They believe the rust is the machine’s skin, and it must be kept healthy. They chant silently, their hands moving in unison, scrubbing the black alloy until it gleams with a dull, oily sheen. In these moments, they are not just inhabitants; they are antibodies, keeping the host alive.

### The Psychology of the Vertical

Living in a place with no horizon, only up and down, warps the mind. The Resonants do not think in terms of past and future, but of **depth and height**.

*   **Below** is the source. It is hot, loud, and primal. It is where the power comes from, where the blood of the mountain pumps. To go Below is to seek strength, but also danger. The deeper you go, the hotter it gets, the thicker the air, the more intense the vibration. Only the strongest Resonants venture deep, returning with stories of red lights and steam.
*   **Above** is the unknown. It is cold, quiet, and distant. It is where the rain comes from, where the wind howls. To go Above is to seek clarity, but also isolation. The higher you climb, the thinner the air, the fainter the hum. Some Resonants become obsessed with the Circle of Sky, spending their lives climbing toward it, never reaching it, driven by a longing for a world without walls.

This verticality creates a caste system, though it is fluid. Those who work the lower levels, near the heat and the noise, are called the **Deep-Dwellers**. They are stronger, louder, more aggressive. They wear heavier armor of rust-mesh. Those who tend the upper gardens, near the cool air and the light, are the **Sky-Tenders**. They are quieter, more contemplative, often prone to melancholy. They wear lighter cloaks of leaf-leather.

Yet, they are one people. Bound by the same hum, the same damp warmth, the same endless fall.

### The Myth of the Outside

The Resonants know they are inside something. They can feel the curvature of the walls. They can sense the immense mass surrounding them. But they have no word for "outside." To them, the Throat *is* the universe. The sky above is merely the ceiling of the world. The darkness below is the foundation.

There are legends, whispered in the sign-language of the elders, of a time before the Throat. Of a place where the ground was flat and solid, where the air did not vibrate, where silence was possible. These stories are told as cautionary tales. *"If you stop moving,"* the elders sign, *"you will remember the Stillness. And the Stillness will eat you."*

They believe the Great Piston is a god, or perhaps a heart. They do not worship it with prayers, but with maintenance. They keep it clean. They keep it flowing. They keep it breathing.

And in return, it keeps them warm.

### A Moment in the Life of a Resonant

Imagine a young Resonant named Kael. He is a Sky-Tender, his skin pale, his blue microbial patches bright on his forearms. He stands on a narrow grate, thirty meters above a pool of glowing blue water. The air is thick with the scent of wet ferns. The hum vibrates through his bones, a constant companion.

He holds a basket woven from vine-stems, filled with harvested berries. He looks up. The circle of sky is a pale gray eye, watching him. He looks down. The darkness is absolute, save for the faint red pulse of the machinery miles below.

He feels a change in the vibration. A slight stutter in the rhythm. His heart skips a beat. He freezes, then forces himself to sway, to match the new tempo. He raises his hands, signing to his companion across the gap: *"The Heart hesitates."*

His companion signs back: *"We wait. We breathe with it."*

Kael closes his eyes. He lets the vibration wash over him. He becomes part of the machine. Part of the jungle. Part of the breath. He does not move. He does not speak. He simply *is*, suspended in the throat of the world, waiting for the next exhalation.
