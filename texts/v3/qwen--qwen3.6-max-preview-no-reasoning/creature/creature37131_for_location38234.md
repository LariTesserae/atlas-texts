They are the **Mycelials**, though they have no name for themselves that can be spoken aloud. To speak is to vibrate, and vibration is an offense against the Great Silence. They call themselves *The Breath-Within*, or perhaps simply *Us*.

To understand them, you must first unlearn the idea of a "person" as a discrete, bounded entity. In the Hollow Sphere, boundaries are illusions. The Mycelials are not individuals in the human sense; they are nodes in a vast, slow-moving network of consciousness, anchored by flesh but extended by root.

### The Physical Form: Softness as Armor

Their bodies are small, rarely exceeding four feet in height, designed to move through the dense undergrowth without breaking the delicate web of vines. They possess a skeletal structure not of bone, but of calcified lignin—hardened plant fiber that gives their limbs a rigid, twig-like appearance. Yet, covering this frame is skin that is indistinguishable from the mossy bark of their homes. It is textured, porous, and constantly shifting in hue, ranging from deep emerald to pale amber depending on the ambient light of the bioluminescent fungi nearby.

They have no hair. Instead, fine, translucent filaments cover their scalps and shoulders, acting as sensory organs that detect changes in humidity, air pressure, and the subtle electrical fields generated by the growing trees. Their eyes are large, black, and devoid of whites, reflecting the teal glow of the world around them. They do not blink often; when they do, it is a slow, deliberate shuttering, like a flower closing at night.

But the most striking feature of the Mycelial is what lies beneath the skin. If you were to cut one open (a thought that would cause them immense psychic distress), you would find no blood, only a thick, viscous sap that glows faintly with internal bioluminescence. This sap is their lifeblood, their neurotransmitter, and their memory storage. When two Mycelials touch, their capillaries near the surface dilate, allowing a microscopic exchange of this fluid. In that instant, they share not just thoughts, but sensations, memories, and emotions. A handshake is a conversation; a hug is a merger of souls.

### The Architecture of Existence: Living in the Pores

They do not build cities. They inhabit the **Pores**—natural cavities within the massive buttress roots and the hollowed-out trunks of the ancient tree-sphere. These dwellings are not constructed; they are curated. A Mycelial family will spend generations gently encouraging the growth of resin-secreting glands in the walls of their home, shaping the space over decades into comfortable, curved chambers that smell of sweet pine and damp earth.

Their furniture is alive. Beds are mats of woven fern-fronds that adjust their firmness based on the sleeper’s body heat. Tables are broad, flat leaves hardened with layers of dried sap, suspended from the ceiling by tensile vines. There are no doors, only curtains of hanging ivy that part silently when approached by someone known, and tighten instinctively against strangers.

Lighting is provided by **Glow-Moths**, domesticated insects kept in glass-less jars made of spun spider-silk and resin. These moths feed on the nectar of night-blooming orchids and emit a soft, pulsating amber light. The rhythm of the moth’s pulse often dictates the rhythm of the household—slowing down during rest periods, quickening during times of activity or anxiety.

### Society: The Chorus of Touch

Mycealian society is non-hierarchical but deeply structured by **Resonance**. Since they communicate primarily through touch and shared sap-exchange, leadership falls to those with the widest "reach"—individuals who have formed connections with the largest number of others. These leaders, called **Root-Singers**, are elderly Mycelials whose skin has become almost entirely bark-like, immobile and serene. They sit in the central plazas of the larger root-clusters, surrounded by younger members who place their hands upon the Root-Singer’s arms, legs, and torso.

In these gatherings, information flows like water. News of a drought in the upper canopy, the discovery of a new medicinal fungus, or the arrival of a rare storm cloud—all are transmitted instantly through the network. There is no secrecy in the Hollow Sphere. To hide a thought is to isolate oneself from the collective breath, which is considered a form of spiritual death.

Conflict is rare, but when it occurs, it is resolved through **Stillness**. Two disputing parties will sit opposite each other, palms pressed together, and enter a meditative state where they force their heartbeats to synchronize. The one who can maintain the calmest rhythm, the one whose sap flows most steadily, is deemed correct. It is a contest of emotional regulation, not strength. Violence is physically impossible for them; their bodies are too fragile, and their empathy too acute. To strike another is to feel the pain oneself, amplified tenfold through the neural link.

### Economy: The Gift of Decay

There is no currency. The concept of ownership is alien to them. Everything belongs to the Sphere, and the Sphere provides. However, there is a complex system of **Gift-Giving** based on the principle of nutrient cycling.

A Mycelial who discovers a particularly potent healing spore does not keep it. They cultivate it, nurture it, and then offer it to those who are sick. In return, they receive nothing material, but they gain **Social Sap**—a measure of respect and trust within the community. The more one gives, the more connected they become to the whole. Hoarding is seen as a blockage in the flow, a cancer in the social body. Those who hoard are not punished, but they are gradually ignored, their touches refused, until they fade into the background, becoming solitary figures who wander the empty spaces between the roots, slowly losing their color and their mind.

Tools are crafted from **Seed-Stone**, a hard, flint-like material found in the cores of certain giant seeds. Knives, scrapers, and needles are carved from this substance, then polished with abrasive leaves. Weaving is a sacred art, practiced by all genders. Vines are stripped, soaked, and braided into ropes, baskets, and clothing. The patterns woven into their garments tell stories of their lineage, their dreams, and their connections to specific trees.

### Spirituality: The Dream of the Tree

The Mycelials believe that the Hollow Sphere is not just a habitat, but a **Dreamer**. They believe that before the Sphere existed, there was only void, and the Tree dreamed itself into existence to escape the loneliness. Therefore, all life within the Sphere is part of the Tree’s dream.

When a Mycelial dies, they are not buried. They are placed in a **Compost Cradle**, a high, airy nest of branches where their body is allowed to decompose naturally. Fungi and insects break down the flesh, returning the nutrients to the tree. But the Mycelials believe that the consciousness does not disappear. It dissolves into the mycelial network beneath the bark, joining the collective memory of the ancestors.

To dream is to visit the Ancestors. During sleep, Mycelials experience vivid, shared hallucinations where they walk through landscapes of pure light and sound, conversing with those who have passed. These dreams are recorded by **Dream-Weavers**, artists who translate the ephemeral visions into tapestries of colored vine and leaf. These tapestries hang in the public spaces, serving as both history books and religious texts.

### The Weirdness Within

However, the Mycelials are changing. The weirdness of the Sphere is seeping into their biology. Younger generations are born with strange mutations. Some have skin that shifts color rapidly, mimicking the emotions of those around them. Others have voices that produce harmonic resonances capable of shattering glass-like seed pods. A few, the **Echo-Walkers**, claim to hear voices from outside the Sphere—voices from the void, from places that never existed.

These Echo-Walkers are feared and revered. They speak of a time when the Sphere will crack, when the green shell will split open, and the inhabitants will spill out into the true darkness. Most Mycelials dismiss this as madness, a corruption of the dream. But the Echo-Walkers insist that the Sphere is not eternal, that it is a cocoon, and that they are preparing for the metamorphosis.

They whisper of wings. Not the feathery wings of birds, but membranous, leaf-like structures that will allow them to glide across the void, to seek new spheres, new dreams. They practice jumping from the highest root-bridges, catching updrafts, learning to fall without fear.

### The Experience of Being Mycelial

To be a Mycelial is to know no loneliness. You are always held, always heard, always understood. Your joy is multiplied by thousands; your sorrow is divided among millions. You are a drop in an ocean of green consciousness, yet you retain your unique shape, your unique song.

You wake to the scent of dew and the hum of sap. You eat fruit that tastes of sunlight captured centuries ago. You work with your hands, shaping the living world with gentle persuasion. You love with your whole being, merging your soul with another’s in a dance of fluid and fiber. And when you die, you do not end. You become the soil, the root, the leaf. You become the dream again.

It is a beautiful, terrifying existence. To lose yourself completely is the ultimate goal. To remain distinct is the ultimate failure. And so they live, breathing in sync with the great, green lung of the world, waiting for the day when the dream ends, and the waking begins.
