They do not walk. To walk implies a horizon, a flatness that does not exist here. They **climb**.

They are the **Suture-Walkers**, though they have no name for themselves that can be spoken aloud, for speech is waste, and waste is death in the Throat. They are humanoid in silhouette only because bipedalism is the most efficient way to navigate verticality when hands are needed for more than balance. But look closer, and the human shape dissolves into something harder, sharper, and infinitely more adapted to the razor-edge reality of their home.

Their skin is not flesh. It is a layered composite of **keratinous plates** and **synthetic weave**, stitched together with threads of spun carbon fiber. This armor is necessary. The air is full of microscopic shards of obsidian dust, shed from the vibrating walls. A soft-skinned creature would be flayed alive by the ambient atmosphere within hours. The Suture-Walkers’ faces are smooth, featureless masks of polished black polymer, save for two narrow slits where eyes reside—eyes that are not biological orbs, but clusters of photoreceptive sensors capable of seeing in the infrared spectrum of heat leaks and the ultraviolet spectrum of structural stress fractures.

They wear their environment like a second skin. Every citizen is draped in layers of **insulating foam** and **radiant-heat blankets**, bulky and cumbersome to an outsider, but essential for survival in the cool, dry twilight. These garments are stained with the iridescent smoke of the factories below, shifting colors from bruised purple to oily green as they move. From their belts hang the tools of their existence: vacuum-sealed canisters of reclaimed water (their currency, their lifeblood), wrenches made of lightweight titanium, and patches of silica-mold paste used to seal minor cracks in the city’s skin.

But it is their relationship with the **arthropods** that defines them. You see a man hanging upside down from a tension cable, repairing a brass pipe. He does not work alone. Perched on his shoulder, its chitinous shell gleaming with metallic alloys, is a **Carrier-Beetle**, the size of a terrier. Its legs lock onto the man’s insulated jacket with magnetic clamps. When the man needs a bolt, he does not reach into a pocket; he taps a code on the beetle’s carapace. The beetle scuttles down his arm, retrieves the component from a pouch on its own back, and presents it. This is not slavery; it is symbiosis. The beetles feed on the static electricity generated by the man’s movement and the ozone-rich air exhaled by his respirator. In return, they provide mobility, storage, and protection. To harm a Carrier is to invite the wrath of the swarm; to neglect one is to die of thirst or exposure.

The Suture-Walkers are silent, but they are not mute. Their communication is a language of **touch and light**. Because sound travels too efficiently in the confined fissure, threatening the fragile fungal networks that hold the lower levels together, they have developed a complex system of hand signals and bioluminescent flashes embedded in their clothing. A quick tap on the left elbow means *danger above*. A pulse of blue light from the chest plate means *water leak detected*. A steady red glow from the wrist signifies *structural failure imminent*. They converse in a flickering dialect of LEDs, their conversations looking like a slow, rhythmic dance of stars against the dark glass.

They are masters of **temporary stability**. Watch them as they traverse a swaying gantry. They do not fight the motion of the city; they flow with it. When the mountain groans and the scaffold lurches, the Suture-Walker leans into the sway, using the momentum to propel themselves forward. They are constantly moving, never still, because stillness is vulnerability. If a platform collapses—and they do, regularly—the citizens nearby do not scream. They detach. Magnetic boots release, grappling hooks fire, and they swing through the void, landing on adjacent structures with practiced ease. They rebuild as they go. A broken beam is not just repaired; it is repurposed. A shattered window becomes a mirror for signaling. A dead arthropod’s shell becomes reinforcement for a child’s armor.

There is a deep, ancestral fear in them, not of heights, but of **the open sky**. The slit above is a wound in their world, a place of blinding light and freezing vacuum. They rarely look up. Their gaze is fixed downward, into the depths where the factories pulse and the silica-molds thrive. Down there is warmth, industry, and community. Up there is only the void, waiting to steal the breath from their lungs.

Yet, there is beauty in their harshness. Observe a group of them gathered around a communal heating vent, sharing a ration of condensed nutrient paste. They sit in a circle, backs to the cold glass, facing inward. Their faceplates are tilted slightly, reflecting the amber glow of the streetlamps. The Carrier-Beetles curl around their ankles, humming softly. For a moment, the chaotic vibration of the city seems to still. They are not merely surviving the edge of existence; they are curating it. They are the stitches holding the wound of the mountain closed, weaving steel and bone and chitin into a tapestry that defies gravity, logic, and nature itself.

They are the **Throat’s Keepers**. And if you listen closely, beneath the hum of the generators and the click of insect legs, you might hear the faintest whisper of their song—a low, resonant chant that vibrates in your chest, matching the frequency of the mountain, telling the stone: *We are here. We hold. We remain.*
