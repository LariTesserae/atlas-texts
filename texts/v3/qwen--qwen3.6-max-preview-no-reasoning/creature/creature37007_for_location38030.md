To ask who lives in the Throat is to misunderstand the nature of residence. In a place where sound has mass and silence is a physical location, "living" is not a state of being, but a state of *tuning*.

There are no citizens here. There are only instruments.

But if you look closely, past the blinding glare and the shimmering dust-lattices, you will see that the emptiness is a lie. The city is not abandoned; it has been repurposed. The inhabitants are not gone; they have dissolved into the architecture, or perhaps they were never separate from it to begin with.

Here is what dwells in the glass cathedral:

### The Liturgists of Frequency
They are tall, impossibly slender entities, their bodies composed not of flesh, but of layered membranes—translucent sheets of keratin and silica stretched over hollow, fluted bones. They have no mouths, for speech would be an act of violence in this pressure-cooker world. Instead, their faces are smooth, concave dishes, designed to catch the ambient hum of the earth.

They move through the wide, mosaic-paved avenues with a grace that defies gravity. They do not walk; they *slide*, their feet barely touching the ground, riding the standing waves of the air like surfers on a tide of noise. Their skin shimmers with piezoelectric sparks, tiny blue-white arcs jumping between their joints as they absorb the kinetic energy of the vibrating atmosphere.

These beings are the caretakers of the silence. You see them kneeling in the "dead zones"—the rare, ovoid alcoves where the geometry of the walls cancels out the tectonic roar. Here, in these pockets of absolute quiet, they perform their rituals. They do not pray with words. They pray with stillness. A Liturgist might stand motionless for days, acting as a living dampener, absorbing the excess vibration that threatens to shatter the delicate crystal bridges. When they finally move, they leave behind a trail of cooled air, a brief respite in the feverish heat.

They are blind, their eyes sealed shut by layers of protective nacre, but they see everything. They perceive the world through echolocation, sending out pulses so soft they are felt rather than heard. To them, the city is a map of textures and densities. A wall is not a barrier; it is a note. A tree is not a plant; it is a chord.

### The Gardeners of the Scream
Deeper in the dense vegetation, where the ferns spiral upward like frozen smoke, live the Gardeners. These are smaller, more frantic creatures, resembling insects made of polished obsidian and copper wire. They have six limbs, each ending in a delicate, tuning-fork-like claw.

Their job is maintenance. The acoustic sponges—the massive silica ferns and velvet mosses—are greedy. If left unchecked, they would amplify the tectonic groans until the very rock shattered. The Gardeners crawl along the stems, tightening fibers, loosening knots, adjusting the tension of the leaves. They are the luthiers of this ecosystem, ensuring that the song remains harmonious rather than chaotic.

When a vine begins to sing too loudly, a Gardner rushes to it, its claws clicking against the stem in a rapid, rhythmic counter-beat. This creates destructive interference, silencing the rogue frequency before it can cause damage. It is a constant, desperate dance. The Gardeners are exhausted, their obsidian shells cracked and repaired with resin, their movements jerky and twitchy. They feed on the mineral deposits sweat out by the plants, scraping the salty residue from the hot bark with their sharp mandibles.

They fear the Liturgists. Not because the Liturgists are predators, but because the Liturgists represent the end of sound. To a Gardener, whose entire existence is defined by the management of noise, total silence is death. They scurry away when a Liturgist approaches, hiding in the shadows of the drum-bark trees, waiting for the stillness to pass so they can return to their work.

### The Echo-Eaters
In the highest reaches of the inverted geode, near the needle-thin aperture miles above, dwell the Echo-Eaters. These are rare, elusive things, described only in whispers (if whispers could survive here). They are vast, amorphous clouds of semi-corporeal mist, shaped by the wind currents that occasionally slip through the crack in the sky.

They are scavengers. They feed on the residual sounds that escape the lower levels—the echoes of the mountain’s settling, the faint tremors of distant earthquakes. They drift down through the shaft like slow-motion snowflakes, trailing tendrils of vapor that sweep across the crystal towers. When they touch a surface, the sound there dies instantly. A ringing bell becomes mute. A singing leaf falls silent.

The Echo-Eaters are neither benevolent nor malevolent. They are simply hungry. But their presence is feared, for if they descend too low, they risk starving the garden. Without the constant input of vibration, the piezoelectric plants would cease to glow, the heat would fade, and the cold would set in. The Liturgists watch the sky constantly, their dish-faces tilted upward, tracking the movement of the mists. Sometimes, they will gather in groups, forming a human chain of resonance, humming a low, sustained tone to push the Echo-Eaters back up toward the light. It is a battle fought not with weapons, but with harmony.

### The Last Architect
And then, there is the one who sits on the rose quartz bench.

He is not like the others. He is solid, heavy, made of stone and sorrow. His body is carved from the same calcified secretion as the towers, rough-hewn and weathered. He has no face, only a smooth, featureless oval where a head should be. He holds a chisel in one hand and a hammer in the other, but he does not strike the stone. He waits.

He is the memory of the civilization that built this place. Or perhaps he is the last survivor, transformed by centuries of exposure to the sonic pressure into something less than human, more than statue. He watches the Fibonacci spiral etched into his bench. He listens to the heartbeat of the mountain.

Every few years, when the pitch of the earth shifts slightly, causing the glyphs in the moss to rearrange themselves, he moves. Just once. A single, deliberate tap of his hammer against the chisel. The sound is small, insignificant against the roar of the garden, but it is precise. It corrects the dissonance. It realigns the story.

He is the keeper of the narrative. He ensures that the scream remains a lullaby. He knows that if the song changes key, the flower in the center of the plaza will open. And he fears what lies inside.

### The Unborn
Inside the iridescent bud at the center of the plaza, something sleeps. It is not a creature, not in any traditional sense. It is a concentration of pure potential energy, a knot of compressed sound waves held in stasis by the collective effort of the ecosystem.

The Liturgists, the Gardeners, and even the Last Architect serve this thing. They are its immune system, its nervous system, its womb. They maintain the perfect frequency required to keep it dormant. Because if it wakes, it will not emerge as a beast. It will emerge as a new law of physics. A shift in the fundamental resonance of reality.

Some say it is a god. Others say it is a weapon. Most believe it is simply the next stage of evolution—a being made entirely of song, ready to step out of the throat and speak the world into a new shape.

For now, it sleeps. And the Throat sings to keep it dreaming.
