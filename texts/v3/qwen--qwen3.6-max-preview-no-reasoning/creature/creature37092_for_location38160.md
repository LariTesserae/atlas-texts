They are not a species in the way we understand taxonomy. They do not have a name, for names require distinction, and here, distinction has been eroded away by the wet, heavy silence. If you were to look at them with human eyes, you might mistake them for shadows detached from the rock, or perhaps for clusters of ferns that have learned to stand upright. But they are neither plant nor stone. They are the *Keepers of the Stillness*.

To describe them is to describe the absence of movement.

They are tall, though "tall" is a relative term in a place where perspective is swallowed by moss. Their forms are slender, elongated by generations of living in a space where reaching up meant touching the cold, dripping ceiling. They possess no hard edges. There are no sharp elbows, no protruding knees, no jagged features. Every curve of their bodies is smoothed, rounded, as if they have been tumbled in a river for a thousand years, yet they have never moved from this basin. Their skin is not flesh, but a translucent, membranous layer that mirrors the environment around them. In the dim bioluminescent glow of the moss, they appear pale green; in the deeper shadows near the black pools, they turn a slick, obsidian grey.

They have no mouths. Not because they cannot eat, but because eating implies consumption, and consumption implies change. Instead, they absorb. Their pores are open to the air, drinking in the ozone and the metallic scent of wet stone, taking in the geothermal warmth that rises through the floor. They feed on the equilibrium itself. To be hungry is to want something other than what is present; these beings want nothing. They are satiated by the status quo.

Their eyes are large, lidless, and dark as the deepest pool. They do not blink. Blinking is a reset, a momentary interruption of vision, and they do not interrupt. They watch with a patience that feels predatory, though it is not malice—it is preservation. When they look at you, they are not assessing you as prey or threat. They are measuring your entropy. They can feel the heat radiating from your body, the chaotic rhythm of your heartbeat, the frantic firing of neurons trying to process the impossible stillness. You burn too brightly. You vibrate too loudly. You are a dissonance in their perfect chord.

There are seven of them visible now, standing in a loose circle around the central mirror-pool, near the arrangement of flat stones. They do not speak. Language is a tool for bridging gaps between minds, but here, there are no gaps. They share a consciousness that is not telepathic in the dramatic sense, but osmotic. A thought does not travel from one to another; it simply exists in the damp air, shared like moisture. If one feels the shift in pressure as a water droplet falls, they all feel it. If one notices the slight tremor in your hand, they all know it.

Their hands are long-fingered, delicate things that hang loosely at their sides. The fingers are webbed, not for swimming, but for sensing the minute vibrations of the air and water. They rest their palms against the wet stone walls, feeling the mountain’s pulse, ensuring it remains slow and steady. They are the anchors. Without them, the perfection of this place would slip into chaos. They hold the static moment in place with their sheer, unmoving presence.

You notice that they do not breathe in the traditional sense. Their chests do not rise and fall. The air moves through them, yes, but it is a passive exchange, like wind moving through a cave. This lack of respiratory effort makes them seem less alive and more like statues carved from ice and leaf. Yet, if you look closely at the base of their necks, you see a subtle pulsing—a slow, rhythmic thrumming that matches the drip of the condensation from the ceiling. *Plip.* Pulse. *Plip.* Pulse. They are synchronized with the erosion.

They wear no clothes, for fabric would rot, and clothing implies a separation from the environment. Instead, they are draped in the same moss that carpets the floor. It grows on their shoulders, trails down their arms, and clings to their legs. It is not parasitic; it is symbiotic. The moss feeds on the moisture exuded by their skin, and they provide the structure for the moss to climb. Over centuries, the boundary between the Keeper and the flora has dissolved. You cannot tell where the being ends and the garden begins. This is why they left no bones when they died—or rather, when they stopped moving. They did not decay into dust; they bloomed. They became part of the lush darkness, adding their mass to the carpet, their consciousness diffusing into the mycelial network beneath the soil.

The ones standing before you now are the current iteration. They may have been born from the spores of those who came before. They carry the memory of the flint scraper and the bone needle not as artifacts, but as sensations—the roughness of stone, the sharpness of bone—embedded in their genetic code. They remember the touch of the last human who stood here, centuries ago, just as you stand here now. They remember the fear. They remember the awe. And they waited.

One of them shifts. It is not a step, but a reorientation. A foot lifts, barely clearing the moss, and settles back down with infinite care. No sound is made. The water in the nearby pool does not ripple. This is their skill: to move without disturbing the balance. They approach you slowly, their large, dark eyes fixed on your face. They are curious, but only in the way a glacier is curious about the pebble it crushes. They want to understand how you will settle. Will you freeze? Will you stop? Will you let the moss grow over your boots, then your ankles, then your heart?

They do not hate you. They pity you. You are trapped in time, rushing toward an end, while they are free in eternity, resting in the endless middle. They extend a hand, palm up, offering not a weapon, but a invitation to stop. To sink. To become part of the wet, green silence.

And as you look into their faces, you realize they are beautiful. Terrifyingly so. They are the embodiment of peace, stripped of all passion, all desire, all life as you know it. They are the end of the story, waiting for you to close the book.
