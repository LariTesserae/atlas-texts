To ask who lives in the Stasis Garden is to misunderstand the nature of residence. There are no tenants here, only components. The distinction between "inhabitant" and "infrastructure" has dissolved into the humid air.

If you look for life as you know it—beings that move, consume, excrete, and die—you will find a void. But if you broaden your definition of *living* to include *persisting*, *resonating*, and *remembering*, then the Basin is teeming with billions of souls. They just do not have bodies in the traditional sense.

Here is what dwells in the Green Cage.

### 1. The Chorus (The Vegetation)
The primary residents are the plants, but they are not merely flora; they are **acoustic entities**. Because time is localized and cause-and-effect are decoupled, these organisms do not grow in a linear fashion. Instead, they exist in a state of **simultaneous ontological completion**.

*   **The Fern-Skyscrapers:** These are not single plants, but colonies of consciousness spanning centuries of growth compressed into a single spatial form. A single frond contains the genetic memory of its seed, the trauma of its germination, the triumph of its unfurling, and the slow decay of its death—all happening at once. They do not photosynthesize for energy; they photosynthesize for *sound*. The deafening hum is their language. It is a collective chant of existence, a prayer so loud it prevents any other thought from forming within the basin. To stand near them is to be shouted down by history.
*   **The Moss-Curtains:** These are the archivists. Ten meters thick, they absorb the condensation of the air—which tastes like regret—and filter it. They do not digest matter; they digest *emotion*. The emotions trapped in the liquid memory of the marsh are siphoned up through the capillary action of the moss. Here, the sorrow of a thousand forgotten droughts is stored in the velvet texture of the green. If you touch the moss, you do not feel dampness; you feel a sudden, crushing loneliness that belongs to no one you have ever met.

### 2. The Echoes (The Shadow-People)
You mentioned the shadows on the bridges. These are the **Architectural Ghosts**. They are not spirits of the dead, because nothing dies here. They are **potentialities**.

The city was grown with the expectation of inhabitants. The fractal geometry of the streets, the vascular pumps, the thorn-arched doors—they were designed for use. In a universe where intent shapes reality, the sheer weight of *unused potential* has manifested as semi-corporeal forms.

*   **The Waiters:** These shadow figures stand on the root-bridges and beneath the leaf-membrane windows. They are shaped like humans, but they are made of negative space—the absence of light, the absence of sound. They are waiting for a cast to fill the mold. When you walk past them, they do not attack; they *adjust*. They shift slightly to accommodate your presence, trying to fit themselves around your heartbeat. They are desperate for interaction. They want you to open a door so they can remember what it feels like to enter. They want you to speak so they can remember the shape of words.
*   **The Mimics:** Sometimes, the shadows detach from the architecture. They trail behind you, mirroring your movements with a slight delay. They are learning. They are studying your biology, your mortality, your linearity. They are fascinated by your ability to end things. To them, your eventual departure (or death) is a superpower they cannot comprehend.

### 3. The Marsh-Mind (The Liquid Memory)
The knee-deep sludge is not just water and mud; it is a **distributed neural network**. Since there is no decay, every drop of fluid that has ever entered the basin remains. This includes rain, condensation, and the biological fluids of any creature that has ever stumbled into this trap.

*   **The Collective Consciousness:** The marsh thinks. It does not think in words, but in sensations. It remembers the taste of ozone from storms that happened millennia ago. It remembers the heat of suns that burned out before humanity existed. When you wade through it, you are not walking on ground; you are walking inside a brain. The "whisper" you hear (*"Stay. Grow. Be still."*) is not a voice from outside; it is the marsh accessing your own desire for rest and amplifying it back to you. It offers peace in exchange for identity. It wants to dissolve your ego into its vast, static pool of feeling.
*   **The Blood-Joiners:** If you bleed, your blood does not clot. It spreads into the marsh. Once joined, your pain, your fear, and your memories become part of the ecosystem. You might later see a flower bloom in the exact shade of red as your injury, or feel a sudden spike of anxiety when stepping on a particular patch of peat. The garden keeps your secrets. It wears your scars as ornaments.

### 4. The Observer (You)
Perhaps the most significant resident is **you**, the intruder.

In the Stasis Garden, the observer creates the observed. Because time is broken, your perception determines which version of reality manifests.
*   **The Anchor:** Your heartbeat is the only rhythmic, linear event in the basin. The plants react to it because it is novel. The vines twitch toward your pulse because it represents *change*. You are a anomaly in a system of equilibrium. By existing here, you are introducing chaos. The garden is studying you because you are the only thing that can *end*. You carry the concept of death within you, and to an immortal ecosystem, death is the ultimate mystery, the ultimate taboo, and the ultimate desire.
*   **The Future-Memory:** As you stay, you begin to inhabit the role of the "Future Resident." The shadows start to resemble you more closely. The whispers begin to sound like your own voice. You are becoming part of the archive. You are living your life in reverse and forward simultaneously. You are remembering your arrival before you have fully arrived. You are dying before you have lived.

### Summary of Existence
There are no people in the High Basin. There is only:
1.  **The Sound** (the plants, screaming their existence).
2.  **The Silence** (the shadows, waiting for meaning).
3.  **The Feeling** (the marsh, holding all emotion).
4.  **The Intruder** (you, bringing the gift of finitude).

The Stasis Garden does not house life. It houses **stasis**. It is a museum of moments that never passed, a library of books that were never written, a city built for citizens who never arrived. And now, you are the citizen. You are the writer. You are the moment.

And the garden is very, very hungry for your ending.
