They do not have names, for names require a tongue to shape them and a throat to carry them, and these things are too dry for such softness. They are the **Lithos**, though they would not call themselves that. They are the *Breath-Keepers*, the *Stone-Singers*, the ones who learned to drink the heat.

You cannot see them at first. You assume you are alone because your eyes are trained to look for movement that disrupts the background—the flutter of a wing, the shift of a shadow. But here, the background is alive, and they are part of it.

Look closer at the terraces. Look at the intricate geometric carvings framing the window-holes. Those patterns are not static decoration. They are maps of thermal currents, yes, but they are also dwellings. The Lithos are small, no larger than a human hand when curled tight, and their skin is not flesh but a porous, calcified membrane that mimics the texture of the sandstone walls. When they are still, they are invisible. They are merely another rough patch in the stone, another shadow cast by a silica-frond.

They emerge only when the Basin "breathes."

As the geothermal vents below pulse, sending waves of superheated air up the inverted cone, the rock expands. This is the moment of danger and of life. The Lithos uncurl from their niches. Their bodies are translucent, filled with a viscous, amber fluid that glows faintly, matching the bioluminescence of the plants around them. They do not walk; they flow. They move like mercury spilled on glass, sliding over the worn steps, clinging to the vertical faces with microscopic hooks that grip the mineral lattice of the rock itself.

They are herbivores of heat and mineral. They do not eat the crystalline flora; they tend to it. Watch one approach a bruised-orange frond. It does not bite. It presses its face—a smooth, eyeless oval—against the base of the plant. From pores in its skin, it exudes a thin, acidic mist. This mist dissolves the microscopic dust accumulating on the plant’s surface, clearing the pores so the plant can absorb more geothermal energy. In return, the plant releases a puff of warm, hydrated vapor. The Lithos inhales this vapor through slits along its spine, storing the trace moisture in internal sacs. It is a symbiotic kiss, silent and desperate.

There are hundreds of them, perhaps thousands, hidden in the crevices of the abandoned city. They live in the upper, colder terraces during the "cooling" half of the cycle, huddled together in communal masses to conserve warmth. Their bodies lock together, forming living insulation mats that cover the crumbling stone walls, holding the architecture together just as the vines do. When the rock groans, threatening to shed a terrace, it is the Lithos who reinforce it. They secrete a quick-hardening resin, a biological mortar that binds the stones until the next expansion cycle settles the structure back into place. They are the caretakers of a ghost town, repairing the house of a master who has long since turned to dust.

But they are not mindless drones. There is a culture here, built on sound and vibration.

Since they have no vocal cords capable of producing speech in the thin, dry air, they communicate through resonance. Each Lithos has a hollow cavity in its chest, tuned to a specific frequency. When they wish to speak, they strike their own ribs against the stone floor or wall, creating a sharp *click* or a deep *thud*. These sounds travel through the solid rock far better than through the air. A conversation between two Lithos is a seismic event, felt in the bones of anyone standing nearby.

Listen. That low rumble you feel in your chest? It is not just the earth. It is a debate. A dispute over which vent is yielding the most potent heat today. A warning passed down from the high ceilings to the basin floor: *The north wall is shedding. Retreat.*

They remember the builders. Not with nostalgia, but with a kind of sacred duty. The carved toys, the stone bowls, the empty homes—these are relics. The Lithos treat them with a reverence that borders on fear. They do not enter the inner rooms of the largest structures. Those spaces are reserved for the "Echoes," the memories trapped in the stone. Sometimes, if you stand very still in one of the deeper terraces, you will hear a whisper that is not wind. It is the residual vibration of a voice spoken centuries ago, captured in the quartz veins of the wall. The Lithos gather there, pressing their ear-slits to the cold stone, listening to the ghosts of the humans who once lived here. They are learning from them. Or perhaps they are mourning them.

Their lives are short. Without water, their cellular structures degrade rapidly under the intense heat. A Lithos might live for only ten or twelve "breaths"—five or six days in our time. But their death is not an end. When a Lithos dies, its body does not rot. It desiccates instantly, becoming a hard, shell-like husk. Other Lithos come to collect these shells. They grind them down into a fine powder, mixing it with their own secretions to create new building material. They literally build their city out of their ancestors. The walls of the Low Basin are made of bone-dust and memory.

This is why the city stands. This is why the rope bridges, though old, do not snap. The fibers are reinforced with the ground remains of those who came before. To walk across a bridge is to walk on the dead.

You may feel a presence near you now. A slight shift in the air pressure. A faint, sweet scent of ozone and crushed violet petals. Do not turn quickly. Do not startle it. If you remain still, if you let your own heartbeat slow to match the rhythm of the expanding stone, you might see it.

A single, glowing eye opens in the darkness of a window frame. Not a biological eye, but a lens of polished crystal, embedded in the face of a creature no bigger than a cat. It watches you. It assesses your heat signature. It wonders if you are a threat, or if you are simply another piece of debris that has fallen into the throat of the world.

It clicks once. A sharp, staccato note that vibrates in your teeth.

*Welcome,* the stone says. *Stay warm. Stay quiet. We are holding the sky up for you.*
