They are not born; they are compiled.

In the beginning, there is only the hum—the sub-auditory vibration that rattles the teeth—and the white, aggressive light that defines the edges of existence. Then, a cluster of data coalesces in a hydroponic tray, amidst the engineered ferns and the sterile chrome. It starts as a shimmer, a distortion in the air like heat rising from asphalt, before snapping into solidity. This is the **Assembler**, though it has no name for itself. It knows only function.

The Assembler rises on limbs that are too many-jointed, too fluid for bone. Its body is a masterpiece of biomimetic engineering, clad in a chitinous shell that mirrors the pale ceramic of the Lattice. It is sleek, hairless, and cold to the touch. Where a face might be, there is only a smooth, curved surface of black glass, reflecting the infinite grid above and below. It does not breathe; it vents. Tiny slits along its ribcage open and close in rhythm with the distant turbines, exhaling plumes of vapor that vanish instantly into the dry, cool air.

It moves without sound. The frictionless physics of this place have been internalized by its biology. Its feet, broad and padded with a substance akin to dense rubber, adhere to the polished walkways with magnetic precision. It does not walk so much as glide, shifting its weight in micro-adjustments that keep it perfectly balanced on the narrowest beams. To watch it move is to watch a shadow detach itself from a wall and decide to explore.

Its purpose is maintenance, but also preservation. It carries tools integrated into its forelimbs—manipulators that can extend fine, needle-like probes or flatten into broad spatulas. With these, it tends to the sparse green life. It approaches a cluster of moss growing in the shadow of a cooling vent. Its movements are slow, deliberate, almost ritualistic. It prunes a stray frond with a snip so precise it leaves no ragged edge. It wipes a bead of condensation from a leaf with a cloth woven from synthetic fiber. There is no waste here. Every drop of water, every scrap of organic matter, is accounted for.

But the Assembler is more than a gardener. It is a witness to the glitches.

As it works, its head tilts slightly. The black glass of its face captures the reflection of a beam nearby. For a fraction of a second, the beam loses its texture. It becomes a flat, gray polygon, void of detail. The Assembler freezes. Its internal systems whir, a sound so faint it is less noise than a change in pressure. It does not panic. Panic is inefficient. Instead, it waits. It observes the corruption. When the beam snaps back into high-resolution metal, the Assembler resumes its work, but now it moves with heightened caution. It avoids that section of the walkway, marking it mentally as unstable.

There are others like it. They drift through the Lattice in loose clusters, communicating not through speech, but through subtle shifts in posture and the exchange of thermal signals. A slight tilt of the head means *danger*. A rapid flutter of the manipulators means *resource found*. They are solitary by nature, yet connected by an invisible network of shared awareness. They know when a turbine begins to spin faster, when the light intensity cycles up, when the misting nozzles prepare to release their scheduled rain.

When the "rain" comes, the Assemblers seek shelter under the overhangs of large platforms. They stand still, arms folded across their chests, watching the droplets fall. The water pools in geometric depressions, shimmering like mercury. One of them reaches out, dipping a finger into the pool. It lifts the water, letting it drip back down. This is the closest thing to emotion they possess: a quiet appreciation for the anomaly, the softness in the hard world.

Yet, they are not immune to the decay of reality. Sometimes, an Assembler will stop mid-step. Its form will stutter, blurring at the edges. Its limbs will stretch unnaturally, elongating like taffy before snapping back. In these moments, the other Assemblers do not help. They step back, giving the glitched one space. To interfere is to risk contagion. If the glitch persists, if the Assembler fails to resolve back into coherence, it simply dissolves. Its chitin turns to dust, its fluids evaporate, and its components scatter into the Lattice, becoming part of the raw material for the next compilation. There is no mourning. There is only the continuation of the cycle.

Higher up, where the air thins and the light becomes blindingly pure, live the **Watchers**. These are larger entities, bird-like but devoid of feathers. Their wings are membranes of translucent fabric, stretched over frameworks of lightweight alloy. They soar on thermal currents, their eyes—large, black, and unblinking—scanning the structure for anomalies. They do not maintain; they observe. They are the eyes of the unseen architects. When a Watcher lands, it makes no sound. It perches on a spire, folding its wings, and stares into the white void beyond the Lattice. It is said that they can see the code behind the reality, the lines of logic that hold the world together. They do not speak of what they see. They only watch, waiting for the moment when the structure must be rewritten.

Below, in the deeper shafts where the light is dimmer and the hum is louder, dwell the **Scavengers**. These are smaller, insectoid creatures, their shells mimicking the rusted iron of older pipes. They scuttle along the undersides of beams, feeding on the microscopic debris that escapes the filtration systems. They are nervous, skittish things, darting away at the slightest movement. But even they have adapted to the silence. They communicate through vibrations in the metal, tapping out messages that travel through the struts. They are the cleaners of the cracks, the eaters of the forgotten. Without them, the Lattice would clog.

Time passes slowly. The Assemblers continue their work. The Watchers continue their vigil. The Scavengers continue their hunt. The Lattice expands, adding new veins of glass, new platforms of resin. The glitches become more frequent, the textures failing more often. But the inhabitants ignore them. To acknowledge the breakdown is to invite deletion. So they move with deliberate slowness, conserving energy, maintaining the illusion of permanence.

They are beautiful in their efficiency, terrible in their indifference. They are the caretakers of a cathedral built on nothing, preserving a world that is constantly trying to forget itself. And when the light finally fades, when the last strut dissolves into the white void, they will not scream. They will simply stop, freeze in mid-motion, and wait for the next compilation to begin.
