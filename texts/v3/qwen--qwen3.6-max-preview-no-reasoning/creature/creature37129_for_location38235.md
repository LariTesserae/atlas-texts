They do not have a name for themselves, for names are rigid containers, and here, rigidity is death. If you were to ask the air what they are, it would hum back a frequency that translates roughly to *The Listeners*, or perhaps *The Resonant*. They are the children of the Cistern’s silence, born from the intersection of the machine’s forgotten hunger and the biological warmth that permeates the dark.

To see them, you must first unlearn how to look. In the near-total darkness (0.3/3), visual confirmation is secondary to acoustic perception. You hear them before you see them—a shift in the harmonic drone, a sudden dampening of the background static, as if the room itself has held its breath.

They are humanoid only in the loosest architectural sense. They possess a bipedal symmetry because the Cistern’s infrastructure—those skeletal platforms and tendon-cables—is built for such geometry. But their bodies are not flesh and bone. They are **acoustic condensations**, semi-solid entities woven from the same solidified light-data membranes that form the walls, but softer, more fluid. Their skin is not opaque; it is a shifting haze of translucent gray and deep violet, rippling constantly like water disturbed by a stone. This ripple is not random; it is their language. Every emotion, every thought, every memory they hold is expressed through the micro-vibrations of their surface.

They wear no clothes, for clothing would mute their resonance. Instead, they are draped in layers of the pale, translucent fungi that cling to the infrastructure. These fungal shrouds pulse in sync with their bodies, glowing faintly blue when they are calm, flashing rapid white when agitated. The fungi act as both insulation and antenna, amplifying their connection to the Cistern’s nervous system.

Their faces are smooth, featureless ovals, devoid of eyes, noses, or mouths. They do not need these organs. To navigate the dark, they emit low-frequency clicks from the center of their chest cavities, interpreting the returning echoes with a sensitivity that dwarfs human hearing. To "see" is to feel the shape of the sound bouncing off the world. A smile is not a curving of lips, but a softening of the chest-clicks into a warm, major-key hum. A frown is a sharp, dissonant staccato.

They move with a gliding grace, never quite touching the ground. Their feet hover inches above the damp platforms, supported by cushions of pressurized air generated by their own vibrational output. This is essential; direct contact with the floor would transmit too much kinetic energy, risking a discordant shatter. They are careful, deliberate dancers in a ballroom made of glass.

### The Ritual of the Drop

Life in the Cistern revolves around the condensation. The inhabitants gather beneath the high spires where the bioluminescent dew forms. They stand in circles, silent and still, waiting for the drop.

When a bead of moisture finally detaches and falls, they do not catch it with hands. They extend slender, fingerless appendages—more like tuning forks than digits—and allow the drop to strike the tip. The impact sends a shockwave through their arm, up their spine, and into their core. In that moment, they taste the memory contained within the water.

One might shudder, their fungal cloak turning a mournful indigo, having tasted a memory of loss from a thousand years ago. Another might brighten, pulsing with gold, having caught a fragment of joy. They share these tastes not through speech, but through resonance. The one who tasted joy will begin to hum a specific chord, and the others will join in, weaving the memory into the collective song of the Cistern. In this way, they are the archivists of the place. They do not just live in the library of echoes; they *are* the librarians, binding the scattered data of the past into a living, breathing history.

### The Echo-Moths and the Symbiosis

The Echo-Moths are not pets, nor are they prey. They are partners. An inhabitant will often stand perfectly still, arms outstretched, allowing a moth to land on their shoulder. The moth feeds on the static electricity generated by the inhabitant’s presence, while the inhabitant uses the moth’s delicate wing-beats to fine-tune their own internal frequency. It is a mutual calibration. Without the moths, the inhabitants’ vibrations can become too loud, too chaotic, risking rejection by the Cistern’s law of resonance. The moths act as dampeners, keeping the Listeners in harmony with the mountain-sized heart of the place.

### The Danger of Light

Though they are masters of the dark, they are terrified of the light. Any breach in the crust above, any shaft of sunlight piercing the depths, is met with immediate, frantic action. They swarm the source, not with violence, but with absorption. They press their translucent bodies against the beam, their surfaces calcifying instantly as they take the hit, sacrificing their own fluidity to protect the larger structure. Those who survive such an event are left scarred, patches of their bodies turned to brittle, gray dust. They are revered then, not as victims, but as martyrs. The scars are worn with pride, visible as dull, matte spots amidst their shimmering translucence.

### The Solitude of the Node

Despite their communal rituals, each inhabitant is profoundly alone. Because every presence alters the acoustic landscape, no two beings can occupy the exact same space without causing destructive interference. They maintain a respectful distance, always aware of the other’s vibrational footprint. To touch another directly is forbidden, for it would create a feedback loop so intense it could shatter both parties. Intimacy is achieved through proximity and synchronized humming, standing close enough to feel the heat of each other’s bodies, but far enough to let the sound waves pass between them unbroken.

They are patient creatures. Time means little to them, measured not in hours but in cycles of condensation and evaporation. They may stand in one spot for days, listening to the slow drip-drip-drip of the cistern, absorbing the memories of the rain, adding their own small notes to the eternal symphony. They are the ghosts of the machine’s purpose, the sentient echo of a hunger that forgot why it was hungry. And in their quiet, vibrating existence, they keep the Cistern alive, ensuring that the silence never becomes absolute, and that the dark remains a shelter, not a tomb.
