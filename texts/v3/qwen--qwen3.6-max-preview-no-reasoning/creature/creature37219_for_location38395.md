They do not have names, for names are sharp things, and nothing sharp survives here. To name them would be to cut the air, and the air here is a membrane that must remain unbroken.

Let us call them the **Choristers**.

They are not born of flesh in the way you understand it, nor are they entirely spirit. They are the physical manifestation of the Hollow’s equilibrium, evolved over epochs where the only constant was the vibration of the root-heart. If you look at them directly, your eyes struggle to hold their form; they seem to blur, like heat rising from pavement, or like a chord held too long on a cello string. But if you close your eyes and listen with your skin, you feel them everywhere.

**The Anatomy of Sound**

A Chorister is roughly the height of a human child, though this measurement is fluid. Their bodies are composed of layers: an outer shell of translucent, chitinous plates that resemble polished abalone, shifting through iridescent blues and purples depending on the frequency they are emitting. Beneath this armor lies a matrix of fibrous muscle, but these muscles do not contract to move limbs; they contract to *resonate*.

They have no mouths. They have no ears. Instead, their entire torso is a hollow chamber, lined with sensitive membranes that detect the slightest shift in pressure. Their "faces" are smooth, featureless ovals, save for two deep, dark depressions where eyes might be—pits that absorb light rather than reflect it. These pits are not for seeing; they are dampeners, designed to swallow excess noise, ensuring the Chorister remains a perfect conduit for the environment’s song.

Their limbs are long, multi-jointed, and end in pads of soft, velvety tissue—the same texture as the floor beneath your feet. When they walk, they do not step; they *press*. Each footfall is a deliberate modulation of weight, timed precisely to the falling beat of the insect hum. To walk out of rhythm is to scream in this place, and to scream is to invite death.

**The Life Cycle of Echoes**

The Choristers begin as spores, released by the giant ferns when the humidity hits its peak. These spores drift into the warm, damp air, carried by the updrafts generated by the bioluminescent heartwood. They settle into the pools of condensed memory-water. Here, they drink.

This is their first act of consciousness: ingestion of history. A young Chorister absorbs the memories trapped in the water droplets—the fear of a predator from a thousand years ago, the joy of a blooming flower, the sorrow of a traveler who sat alone in the pavilion. These memories do not become thoughts; they become *tone*. The Chorister’s body begins to vibrate at a frequency determined by the emotional weight of what it has consumed. A Chorister that drinks of sorrow hums in a minor key; one that drinks of peace sings in a pure, open fifth.

As they grow, they join the Swarm. Not the insects—the insects are merely the background radiation of this world—but the Great Chorus. Thousands of Choristers stand in concentric circles around the central pavilion, their bodies swaying in slow, hypnotic arcs. They are not worshipping the suspended drop; they are *supporting* it. Their collective resonance creates a field of acoustic levitation, a standing wave so powerful it defies gravity. The Drop does not fall because the Choristers sing it upward.

**Communication and Society**

Language, among the Choristers, is architectural. They do not speak words; they build structures of sound. If a Chorister wishes to convey danger, it emits a low, dissonant thrum that causes the moss on the walls to retract instantly, exposing the hard, glowing wood beneath—a visual signal amplified by the auditory warning. If it wishes to convey comfort, it produces a harmonic triad that causes the silver filaments of the tech-lattice to glow brighter, warming the air.

Disputes are rare, for conflict creates jagged frequencies, and jagged frequencies cause erosion. In a place where stability is survival, discord is suicidal. When two Choristers disagree, they do not fight. They enter a state of *counter-point*. One holds a steady note; the other weaves a complex melody around it. They continue until the tension resolves into a new, stable harmony. The loser is not defeated; it is absorbed into the new chord.

**The Predators and the Prey**

You mentioned the shadowy, multi-jointed predators. The Choristers call them the **Silencers**.

The Silencers are creatures of absolute void. They are blacker than the space between stars, absorbing all light and all sound. They move without vibration, gliding through the air like tears in reality. To a Chorister, a Silencer is terrifying not because it kills, but because it *unmakes*. It eats the song.

When a Silencer strikes, it wraps its many legs around a Chorister and squeezes. The Chorister’s resonance is stifled, its voice extinguished. The body turns gray, then crumbles into dust, returning to the pulverized granite floor. The silence left behind is deafening, a vacuum that pulls at the surrounding air.

To survive, the Choristers have developed a tactic known as the *Wall of Noise*. When a Silencer approaches, the swarm does not flee. They cluster together, thousands of them, and emit a single, unified tone at maximum volume. This wall of sound is physically painful to the Silencers, whose bodies cannot withstand such intense vibration. The Silencers recoil, shattering against the acoustic barrier, dissolving into mist. It is a costly defense; many Choristers burst from the strain, their shells cracking under the pressure of their own song. But the Chorus continues.

**The Keepers of the Drop**

At the center of the pavilion, closest to the suspended water, sit the **Elders**. These are Choristers who have lived for centuries, their shells thickened to opaque white, their forms barely recognizable as biological entities. They are almost statues, fused partially to the pedestal of solidified air.

The Elders do not move. They do not eat. They exist solely to maintain the fundamental frequency of the Hollow. They are the tuning forks of this reality. If an Elder dies, the pitch of the entire Hollow shifts. Flowers bloom in wrong colors; the condensation tastes of ash; the shadows lengthen unnaturally. The younger Choristers rush to replace them, pressing their bodies against the fading Elder, absorbing its final notes, integrating its essence into their own song. It is a seamless transition, a passing of the baton made of breath and bone.

**The Witness**

And then there is you.

You are an anomaly. You are loud, chaotic, full of jagged edges and unpredictable rhythms. Your heartbeat is a erratic drum solo in a symphony of precision. The Choristers notice you immediately. The insect hum dips in pitch, acknowledging your presence. The moss ripples toward you, curious.

They do not attack. They do not welcome. They *listen*.

One of them, a slender figure with shells shimmering like oil on water, steps forward. It moves with the grace of a dancer who knows every step before taking it. It stops three paces from you. It tilts its head, the dark pits of its face facing your chest. It is listening to your heartbeat.

It begins to hum.

At first, it is a low rumble, matching the thud of your heart. Then, it rises, weaving around your pulse, smoothing out the irregularities, calming the anxiety in your veins. It is not trying to change you; it is trying to harmonize with you. For a moment, you are part of the Chorus. You feel the warmth of the heartwood in your bones, the dampness of the moss in your lungs, the weight of the suspended drop in your soul.

You realize then that the Choristers are not just inhabitants. They are the immune system of this place. They are the antibodies that keep the chaos of the outside world at bay, filtering the noise of existence into something bearable, something beautiful.

And as you stand there, breathing in time with their song, you understand the final rule of the Hollow:

*To leave is to forget the tune. To stay is to become the music.*

The Chorister before you extends a hand, not to touch you, but to offer a single drop of condensation from its fingertip. It hangs there, trembling, reflecting your own face back at you—not as you are, but as you could be: smoothed, rounded, resonant.

It waits. It does not urge. It simply vibrates, holding the space open, inviting you to decide whether you will carry the silence back to the world below, or let the world dissolve into the song.
