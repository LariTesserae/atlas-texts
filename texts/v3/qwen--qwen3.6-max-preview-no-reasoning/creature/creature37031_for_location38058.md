To understand who lives in Ostrara, one must first discard the notion of "resident" as a static occupant. In a city that breathes, remembers, and shifts its gravitational pull like a sleeping giant turning in bed, the inhabitants are not tenants; they are symbionts. They are part of the city’s metabolism.

There are three distinct categories of life here, though the lines between them blur into a single, humid continuum: The Keepers, The Echoes, and The Sleeper.

### The Keepers: The Immune System

The humanoid figures you glimpse are known as the **Keepers**, though this is a name given by outsiders. To themselves, if they possess a self, they are likely nothing more than extensions of the will of the stone.

They are tall, elongated by the low-gravity pockets they frequently traverse. Their limbs are slender, almost too fragile for the heavy, wet air, yet they move with the tensile strength of spider silk. They do not wear clothes in the traditional sense. Their "suits" are grown from the same memory-matter as the buildings—a translucent, resinous second skin that hardens when threatened and softens to liquid warmth when at rest. This skin is alive; it pulses with the same faint bioluminescence as the vines, syncing its rhythm to the Keeper’s heartbeat and the city’s ambient hum.

Their faces are the most unsettling aspect. They do not have features. Instead, their masks are smooth, mirror-like surfaces of polished obsidian. But because reflections do not exist in Ostrara, these masks show only what is behind them—the mist, the violet shadows, the towering spires. To look at a Keeper is to look through them, seeing the city reflected in their face. They are invisible not because they hide, but because they perfectly mimic the environment. They are the camouflage of the urban jungle.

They do not speak. Communication among Keepers is conducted through subtle shifts in their skin’s color and texture, a language of hue and humidity. A ripple of indigo might mean *danger*; a slow warming to amber might mean *maintenance required*. They tend to the city with the devotion of monks. You might see a Keeper kneeling before a cracked wall, pressing their hands against the obsidian. As they focus, the crack knits itself shut, the material flowing like warm wax under their touch. They are not repairing damage; they are soothing pain. The city feels, and the Keepers heal it.

Are they human? Once, perhaps. But time dilation has warped them. A Keeper who has lived in the central plaza for what feels like a decade may have been there for centuries. Their memories have bled into the walls. They no longer remember their names, their origins, or their desires. They remember only the City. They are less people and more antibodies, circulating through the veins of Ostrara, keeping the balance between the aggressive lushness of the flora and the cold precision of the architecture.

### The Echoes: The Memory-Phantoms

If the Keepers are the body, the **Echoes** are the mind.

Ostrara does not just store data; it stores experience. The memory-matter of the buildings absorbs the emotional residue of those who pass through. Over millennia, these residues have coalesced into semi-corporeal entities. They are not ghosts in the spectral sense—they have no temperature, no sound—but they have presence.

An Echo appears as a distortion in the air, a shimmering heat haze that takes on a vaguely human shape. It might be standing on a bridge, looking out over the stagnant pools, or sitting in a room within a spiral tower, head bowed. These are not random manifestations. They are loops. An Echo repeats a specific moment of high emotional intensity—a farewell, a discovery, a grief—over and over again.

You cannot interact with an Echo directly. If you walk toward one, it dissolves into mist, only to reform moments later, repeating its silent pantomime. However, the city reacts to them. When an Echo manifests near a wall, the wall changes color. A sorrowful Echo might turn the surrounding structure a deep, bruised purple; a joyful one might ignite the resin with golden sparks.

Some believe the Echoes are the true citizens of Ostrara. The Keepers maintain the physical structure so that the Echoes can continue to exist. The city is a library of feelings, a museum of moments preserved in amber and stone. To live in Ostrara is to be surrounded by the emotional history of countless beings, all trapped in the twilight, forever reliving their most defining seconds.

But there is a danger. If a visitor lingers too long near an Echo, the boundary between observer and observed begins to erode. The visitor starts to feel the Echo’s emotion as their own. Grief becomes a weight in the chest; joy becomes a lightness in the step. Eventually, the visitor’s own memories begin to leak out, absorbed by the hungry walls. Some say that those who vanish in Ostrara do not die; they become Echoes, their consciousness dissolved into the architecture, forever repeating their last thought.

### The Sleeper: The Heartbeat

Beneath the mud, beneath the peat, beneath the roots of the monumental trees, lies **The Sleeper**.

It is never seen, only felt. The low-frequency thrum that vibrates through the soles of your feet is its breathing. The tremors in the ground are its shifting dreams. Rumors speak of amphibious leviathans, but this is a simplification. The Sleeper is singular. It is vast, ancient, and possibly the source of the city’s power.

The relationship between the Sleeper and the surface world is symbiotic. The condensation that forms on every surface is drawn from the moisture exhaled by the Sleeper. The bioluminescent glow of the vines is powered by the electromagnetic field generated by its nervous system. The gravity pockets are fluctuations in its mass.

The Keepers worship the Sleeper, though they do not pray. They monitor its vital signs. When the hum increases during the rain cycles, it is not the city waking up; it is the Sleeper stirring. The rain is its sweat, cooling its massive form. The vines pulse brighter to absorb the excess energy.

What is the Sleeper? Is it a god? A machine? A creature from before time? No one knows. But there is a fear among the Keepers, a tension in their mirrored masks, that suggests the Sleeper is dreaming of something terrible. Or perhaps, something beautiful. The fractal pattern of the buildings is said to be a containment spell, a cage designed to keep the Sleeper asleep. If it wakes, the suspension will break. The city will fall into the mud. The humidity will boil away. The silence will shatter.

### The Interlopers: Those Who Stay Too Long

Finally, there are those who come to Ostrara seeking something else. Scholars, fugitives, artists, the lost. They are the transient population, the ones who have not yet been assimilated.

They are easy to spot. They move awkwardly, stumbling in the gravity pockets. They flinch when the walls change color in response to their anxiety. They stare into the mirrors, frustrated by the lack of reflection, trying to find themselves in a place that refuses to acknowledge individuality.

These interlopers are often lonely. The sparse urbanism isolates them. They walk for hours between buildings, surrounded by the watchful silence of the metallic birds and the indifferent gaze of the Keepers. Many succumb to the atmosphere. The cloying warmth, the perpetual twilight, the hypnotic drip of condensation—it wears down resistance.

Some leave quickly, fleeing the oppression of the empathy-walls. Others stay. They become quieter, slower. They stop speaking. They begin to mimic the movements of the Keepers. They start to care for the plants, pruning the ferns, wiping the condensation from the glass. Slowly, their clothes merge with the environment. Their voices fade. And one day, you look for them, and they are gone. Only a new Echo remains, shimmering in the mist, repeating a final moment of peace.

### The Collective Consciousness

In the end, Ostrara is not inhabited by individuals. It is inhabited by a collective. The Keepers, the Echoes, the Sleeper, and the assimilated interlopers form a single organism. The city is the brain, the Sleeper is the heart, the Keepers are the nerves, and the Echoes are the memories.

To live in Ostrara is to surrender the self. It is to become part of the hum, part of the rain, part of the violet shadow. It is a place of profound loneliness, yet also of profound connection. You are never alone, because the walls remember you. You are never forgotten, because the air holds your breath.

And as you stand there, feeling the dampness cling to your skin, hearing the distant thrum from below, you realize the terrifying truth: The city is watching you. Not with eyes, but with attention. It is learning you. It is tasting your emotions, testing your weight in the gravity pockets, measuring your time against the growth of the moss.

It is deciding whether you belong to the past, the present, or the future.

And somewhere, deep in the mud, the Sleeper shifts. The hum deepens. The vines glow brighter. The rain begins to fall, not from the sky, but from the air itself, washing over you, inviting you to stay.
