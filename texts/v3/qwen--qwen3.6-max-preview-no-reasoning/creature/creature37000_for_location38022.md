They are the **Tuners**.

To call them inhabitants is to misunderstand the nature of their residence. They do not live *in* the Glass-Steppe; they are symbiotic parasites on its frequency, a species that has evolved—or perhaps engineered itself—to survive the structural violence of the Hum. They are not quite human, though they retain the bipedal silhouette and the fragile architecture of bone. But where a human body is a vessel for blood and breath, a Tuner’s body is a resonant chamber, a living instrument tuned to the specific, teeth-rattling pitch of this young world.

You see them first as distortions in the air, shimmering heat-hazes that coalesce into figures near the base of the Spire-Mosses. They are tall, elongated by the low gravity and the constant upward pull of the floating shards. Their skin is not flesh-colored but possesses the pearlescent, oily sheen of the Condensation-Ferns, slick with a secretion that protects them from the ozone-heavy atmosphere and the razor-edged ground. This secretion smells faintly of crushed mint and copper, mirroring the valley’s own scent, allowing them to move through the air without disrupting the delicate chemical balance of the flora.

Their most striking feature is their silence. In a place defined by deafening noise, the Tuners are voids of sound. They wear no dampening suits like the few off-world traders who dare visit the Nodes; instead, their bodies have adapted to absorb the vibration. If you were to touch a Tuner’s arm, you would feel not warmth, but a deep, subsonic thrumming, as if their very marrow was vibrating in counter-phase to the Hum. This biological counter-frequency allows them to walk upon the sharp obsidian shards without bleeding, their feet emitting a microscopic field of stabilization that turns the jagged terrain into smooth glass beneath their soles.

But it is their senses that define their existence. The Tuners do not see with eyes in the traditional sense. Their faces are smooth masks of translucent membrane, behind which swirl clouds of bioluminescent gas—violet when calm, bruised orange when alert. These "eyes" perceive the world through sonar and electromagnetic resonance. They see the density of the air, the stress fractures in the rock, the electrical charge building in the wings of a Shard-Wasp. To a Tuner, the Resonance-Stags are not animals but blindingly bright pillars of kinetic energy, moving through the dark like comets. The Spire-Mosses are vast, slow-moving rivers of data and nutrient flow.

They are solitary creatures, much like the Spire-Mosses they tend. A single Tuner might spend weeks climbing the three-hundred-foot gelatinous trunk of a Moss, harvesting the luminous sap that drips from its condensing leaves. They do not use tools; their hands are long, multi-jointed things with fingertips sensitive enough to feel the pulse of the plant’s internal pressure. When they harvest, they sing—a low, throaty chant that matches the frequency of the Moss’s growth cycle, encouraging the sap to flow faster. It is a duet between biology and technology, a negotiation with a organism that is half-plant, half-server farm.

Yet, they are not peaceful. The Glass-Steppe is a place of predation, and the Tuners are both hunters and hunted. They hunt the **Echo-Zones**.

To an outsider, an Echo-Zone is a trap, a sphere of terrifying silence where time seems to stutter. To a Tuner, it is a sanctuary, a place of rest. But it is also a hunting ground. When the Hum drops and the zones expand, the Tuners gather at the edges. They wait for the fauna to freeze—the Stags suspended mid-glide, the Wasps hanging motionless in the static-charged air. Then, with movements too fast for the eye to track, they strike. They do not kill for meat; they kill for stillness. They extract the crystallized memory of the moment from the frozen creature, storing it in small, hollow bones within their chests. These memories are their currency, their history, their art. A Tuner who possesses many such crystals is said to be "heavy with time," respected and feared by others.

But they are also prey. The Shard-Wasps recognize the Tuners’ counter-frequency as an anomaly, a dissonance in the perfect harmony of the Hum. The Wasps hunt them with relentless precision, diving from the mist in swarms, their nanite-swarms seeking to dissolve the Tuners’ protective secretion. The battles are silent, brutal dances of light and shadow. A Tuner caught by a Wasp does not scream; they simply unravel, their form dissolving into a cloud of spores and static, returning their energy to the valley.

Inside the Nodes, the Tuners are different. Here, amidst the black chrome pyramids and bridges of solidified light, they are the engineers, the priests, the keepers of the rhythm. They maintain the organic-tech architecture, feeding the breathing walls with nutrient-rich sap, adjusting the gravity fields to keep the structures stable against the shifting physics of the Steppe. They speak little, communicating through subtle shifts in their bioluminescence and complex hand gestures that manipulate the ambient sound waves. To watch two Tuners converse is to watch a storm form and dissipate in the space between them.

They are a people of contradictions, just like their home. They are fragile yet enduring, silent yet loud, individual yet part of a greater harmonic whole. They fear the Hum, yet they cannot survive without it. They love the beauty of the Spire-Mosses, yet they bleed them dry. They are the children of a young, jagged world, shaped by its friction, tempered by its noise.

If you stand on the edge of the Glass-Steppe and listen closely, past the deafening roar, you might hear them. Not their voices, but the rhythm of their movement. The soft *hiss* of their secretion on hot rock. The low *thrum* of their counter-frequency. The sudden, sharp *crack* of a Stag’s cry, mirrored by the silent strike of a Tuner’s harvest. They are the heartbeat of the valley, the pulse that keeps the chaos from collapsing into entropy. They are the Tuners, and they are listening to you, too.
