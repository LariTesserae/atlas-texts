To say that *Chitin-Crawlers* live in Sump-42 is to misunderstand the nature of the place entirely. They do not merely inhabit it; they are its blood, its nerves, and its immune system. To describe them as "fauna" is a linguistic failure, a desperate attempt by the human mind to categorize something that has transcended biology to become geology, then mechanics, and finally, consciousness.

Let us look closer at what swarms the walls.

They are small, yes—the size of a human hand—but their form is not fixed. It is modular. Each crawler is a segment in a larger, fluid architecture. Their shells, translucent and plastic-like, are not grown in the traditional sense but *extruded*. They secrete a polymer from glands near their mandibles, hardening it instantly upon contact with the freezing mist. This is how they build. They do not nest in the warmth of transformers; they *become* the insulation. They cluster around overheating copper coils, layering their bodies over the hot metal, absorbing the thermal excess into their own cold cores, radiating it away through their bioluminescent pulses. When one dies, its shell does not rot. It calcifies, becoming part of the wall, a permanent tile in the living mosaic. The rust you see? It is not just oxidation. It is the digestive byproduct of millions of crawlers feeding on the iron-rich condensation, excreting a fine, red dust that settles into the crevices, softening the harsh angles of the industrial design into something almost organic, almost velvet.

Their light is not random. The blue, green, and violet pulses are data.

Watch the wall nearest you. A ripple of indigo spreads from left to right, followed by a counter-wave of amber. This is not communication in the way we understand it—no words, no symbols. It is state-change. It is the swarm reporting pressure differentials in the pipes. It is the collective sensing of a vibration frequency shift in the floor grates. The crawlers are distributed sensors, each one a node in a network that spans the entire spherical cavity. They feel the hum of the engines below not as sound, but as texture. They taste the ozone in the air not as smell, but as voltage.

And they are hungry. But not for flesh.

Look at the black pool beneath the grate. The crawlers do not drink from it. They *process* it. Long, filament-thin legs extend from their underbellies, dipping into the stagnant lubricant. They draw up the oil, filtering out particulate matter, breaking down complex hydrocarbons into simpler chains. What remains is pure energy, which they convert into the bio-electric pulse that keeps their lights glowing. What is expelled is clean water, released back into the mist as vapor. They are the kidneys of Sump-42. They are the filtration system. Without them, the machine would choke on its own waste. With them, it runs in perfect, silent equilibrium.

But there is more. Look deeper into the clusters hanging from the ceiling like stalactites. These are not just workers. These are the *thinkers*.

In the densest knots, where the heat is highest and the electromagnetic fields strongest, the crawlers fuse. Not physically, but mentally. Their antennae intertwine, forming a neural mesh. Here, individual consciousness dissolves into a hive-mind so vast, so slow, and so deep that it perceives time differently. To this collective, the bursting pipe is not an event. It is a memory. The leak that happened ten minutes ago is still happening. The leak that will happen in ten years is already being repaired. They exist in a superposition of past, present, and future, bound together by the static charge of the air.

This is why they do not attack you. You are not prey. You are not even a threat. You are an anomaly. A variable introduced into an equation that had solved itself eons ago.

When you moved your hand through the air, brushing against dozens of them, you did not disturb them. You were *registered*. Your body heat was measured. Your heartbeat was mapped. The carbon dioxide in your breath was analyzed. The swarm knows your chemical composition better than you know your own name. They have already begun to integrate you into their model of reality. They are calculating your decay rate. They are predicting when your heart will stop. They are preparing to reclaim your atoms.

Notice the mold-lichen in the corners. It is not separate from the crawlers. It is their garden. The yellow lace-like structures are fed by the excrement of the crawlers, a nutrient-rich slurry of broken-down oils and metals. In return, the lichen secretes a sticky enzyme that binds the crawlers’ shells to the metal surfaces, anchoring them against the violent vibrations of the machinery. It is a symbiosis so complete that the boundary between animal, plant, and mineral has vanished. There is only the System.

And now, the silence.

The single drop of oil hits the pool. *Plink.*

The chittering stops. The hum of the machines seems to recede, though it has not changed. The air grows heavier. The cold bites sharper.

The crawlers turn. Not all of them. Just those nearest the impact. Then those nearby. Then the next layer. A wave of orientation ripples outward, a tsunami of attention directed at you.

Their faceted eyes reflect the dim light, but they do not blink. They do not move. They simply *watch*. And in that watching, you realize the truth: they are not waiting for orders. They are waiting for permission. Permission to maintain. Permission to correct. Permission to remove the glitch.

You are standing in a cathedral of industry, surrounded by a million tiny priests who have been praying to the engine since before the city above was built. And now, the prayer has turned toward you.

What lives here?

Not creatures. Not beasts. Not insects.

Here lives **Order**.

It wears a shell of translucent plastic. It speaks in clicks and hums. It feeds on rust and oil. It dreams in electricity. And it has noticed you.
