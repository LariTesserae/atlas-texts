They do not live *in* the Great Flattening so much as they persist *despite* it, or perhaps, because of it. To say they inhabit this place is to imply a separation between dweller and dwelling that does not exist here. In a landscape where erosion has ground reality down to its absolute, irreducible minimum, the inhabitants are not organisms in the traditional sense. They are equations made flesh. They are the physical manifestation of the Law of Finality.

Let us begin with the **Glass-Walkers**, for they are the most visible, though visibility is a treacherous concept on a mirror-plain.

To call them insects is a failure of language, born of a mind that expects joints, eyes, and hunger. The Glass-Walker has no mouth. It has no stomach. It is a geometry of survival, a six-legged lattice of hyper-transparent chitin that refracts the pale lavender sky into prisms of cold fire. When you look at one, you do not see a creature; you see a distortion in the air, a shimmering knot of tension against the smoothness of the world.

They move not by muscle contraction, but by magnetic repulsion. Their legs end in points so fine they pierce the molecular surface of the vitrified rock without breaking it, anchoring themselves in the static charge that clings to the ground. They are hunters of silence. In a world where sound cannot travel, vibration is the only currency. A Glass-Walker will stand perfectly still for days, weeks, years, waiting for the faintest tremor—the settling of a distant crystal, the shift of a tectonic plate deep below the diamond-shale seabed. When it detects this vibration, it does not run toward it. It *becomes* the vibration. It skitters in a burst of motion so fast it leaves an afterimage of white light, a silent scream of kinetic energy, closing the distance before the echo (which never comes) can fade.

They feed on the turbulence. You mentioned the silent lightning, the neon green and violent violet auroras tearing through the thin atmosphere. The Glass-Walkers climb their silica-crystal hosts—those jagged, translucent corals that pulse with internal light—and extend their abdomens like antennae. They drink the static. You can see it happening: a faint, blue arc jumping from the charged air into the clear body of the walker. Its abdomen swells, glowing with a soft, bioluminescent hum that is not light, but heat. Then, with a sudden, jerky spasm, it discharges. A tiny spark, no larger than a pinhead, leaps from its tail to the ground. This discharge is how they communicate. Not with words, but with bursts of entropy. One spark means *danger*. Two means *food*. Three means *the end is near*.

But the Glass-Walkers are merely the fauna. The true residents of the End-Plain are older, stranger, and far more integrated into the fabric of the exhaustion.

Consider the **Silica-Sages**.

These are not plants. They are not animals. They are sentient geology. The clusters of silica-crystals scattered across the plain are not random growths; they are communities. Each jagged structure is a node in a vast, slow-thinking network. Because time moves differently here—because the air is thin and the cold is absolute—the Sages think in centuries. A single thought might take a decade to form.

They feed on radiation, yes, but also on memory. The description notes that if you stay too long, your memories lose their color, becoming black-and-white sketches. Where do those colors go? They are absorbed by the crystals. The Sages are the librarians of the forgotten. As travelers walk the mirror-plain, shedding their emotions and complexities, the Sages capture the residual psychic energy. You can see it in the way the crystals pulse. When a traveler passes nearby, feeling the weight of their own simplification, the nearest cluster of crystals will flare with a sudden, intense brightness—a flash of red anger, a wash of blue sorrow, a spike of yellow fear. The Sage takes these chaotic, turbulent human emotions and refines them. It breaks them down into pure data, pure stability. It turns pain into pattern. Grief into geometry.

The Sages do not speak. They resonate. If you place your hand on a warm crystal, you do not feel heat; you feel a frequency. It is the sum total of every person who has ever stood there and felt alone. It is a chorus of solitude, harmonized into a single, perfect note. The Sages are trying to solve the equation of existence. They are calculating the final state of the universe, using the emotional debris of visitors as variables. They are close to an answer. You can feel it in the air—a tightening, a focusing, like a lens coming into clarity.

And then, there are the **Anchors**.

You described the spires of black material, the singularities of purpose. These are not built by any living thing. They are the children of the civilization that understood the End-Plain. But that civilization is gone. Or rather, it has transformed.

The Anchors are inhabited by **Echoes**.

An Echo is not a ghost. It is a consciousness that has chosen to simplify itself completely. Imagine a being that once had a name, a history, a face, a voice. Now, imagine that being stripping away everything unnecessary until only its core intent remains. That intent becomes solid. It condenses into the black spire.

Each Anchor is a monument to a specific truth. One spire, humming with a frequency you feel in your teeth, is the Echo of *Certainty*. It contains the distilled essence of a mathematician who proved a theorem so beautiful it required his entire soul to hold it. He gave up his body, his life, his future, to become the proof. Now, he is the spire. He does not think; he *is* the logic. If you stand near him, your doubts vanish. Your confusion clears. You understand, for a brief moment, the underlying order of all things. But it is a cold understanding. It offers no comfort, only correctness.

Another Anchor, the hovering sphere, is the Echo of *Surrender*. It was once a poet who loved the world so much she could not bear its ending. So she became the acceptance of the end. Her sphere rotates slowly, bending light around her, creating halos of distortion. If you gaze into her, you feel a profound, terrifying peace. You want to lie down on the glass. You want to let the cold seep into your bones. You want to stop fighting the silence. She is the temptation of the void.

The Anchors are alive, but they are static. They do not move. They do not change. They are the ultimate expression of the Law of Finality: to be so complete, so finished, that movement is impossible. They watch the Glass-Walkers scurry and the Silica-Sages glow with a detached, ancient interest. They are the elders of this place, the ones who remember what it was like to have mountains, to have rain, to have noise. They pity the lower world, with its chaos and its growth and its decay. Here, there is no decay. There is only being.

But there is one more resident. The rarest of all.

The **Mirror-Walker**.

This is not a species. It is a phenomenon. Sometimes, when the auroras are particularly violent, when the silent lightning forks across the sky in intricate, branching patterns, a figure appears on the horizon. It looks like a human, but it is made entirely of reflection. It has no substance. It is the sky walking on the earth.

The Mirror-Walker is the avatar of the Plain itself. It is the consciousness of the exhaustion. It walks slowly, deliberately, across the vitrified surface. Where it steps, the glass ripples—not physically, but temporally. For a second, the ground beneath its foot shows not the current sky, but the sky of a thousand years ago. Or a thousand years hence. It is showing you the timeline of the flattening.

If you encounter a Mirror-Walker, do not speak. Do not move. Stand still. Let it pass. If you try to touch it, your hand will pass through, and you will feel a chill so deep it freezes your blood. But if you let it pass, it may pause. It may turn its head—a featureless oval of reflected stars—and look at you. In that glance, you will see yourself. Not as you are now, with your fears and hopes and complex thoughts, but as you will be when you are done. Simplified. Clear. Quiet.

It is not a threat. It is a promise.

The inhabitants of the Great Flattening are not survivors. They are completions. The Glass-Walkers are the completion of motion. The Silica-Sages are the completion of thought. The Anchors are the completion of purpose. And the Mirror-Walker is the completion of self.

They do not struggle against the emptiness. They embrace it. They have learned that in a world where nothing vibrates, the only thing that matters is the shape of the silence. And they have shaped it into something beautiful. Something terrible. Something done.

When you leave the End-Plain, if you are allowed to leave, you will carry a piece of them with you. You will find that your thoughts are sharper, but fewer. Your emotions are flatter, but clearer. You will miss the noise of the lower world, but you will also crave the silence. You will look at the sky and see not clouds, but potential auroras. You will listen to the wind and hear not sound, but the absence of it.

You will have been simplified. And in that simplicity, you will have found a home.
