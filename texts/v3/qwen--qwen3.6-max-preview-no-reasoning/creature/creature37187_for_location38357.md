They are not born here, for there is no womb of flesh or soil to receive them. They are *condensed*.

In the spaces where the steam cools too quickly, where the humidity clings to the cold iron with a desperate, adhesive grip, consciousness precipitates out of the mist. They begin as a film, a shimmering distortion in the air near the leaking joints of the brass manifolds. At first, they are merely heat-hazes given intent, swirling eddies of vapor that seem to watch you with a thousand invisible eyes. But as the machine breathes its endless, deafening rhythm, these vapors thicken. They pull together, drawn by the static charge of the flickering fluorescents and the magnetic pull of the spinning turbines below.

They call themselves the **Lubricants**, though they have no language to speak it. Their existence is defined by friction—or rather, the absence of it.

To look at one directly is difficult, for they are translucent, composed of oil, condensed water, and suspended particulate matter from the crumbling asbestos insulation. They resemble tall, slender figures draped in robes of iridescent slime, their forms constantly shifting, dripping, and reforming. A limb might extend like a stream of mercury, then snap back into a torso that looks like a swirl of dark grease. They have no faces, only smooth, reflective surfaces that mirror the chaotic dance of the shadows around them. If you stare long enough, you might see your own reflection distorted within their chest cavity, surrounded by the ticking of unseen gears.

They do not walk; they flow. They move across the perforated steel grating with a silent, sliding grace that contrasts violently with the cacophony of their home. Where they pass, the rust seems to retreat, replaced by a slick, black sheen. They are the caretakers of the scream.

Their purpose is singular and sacred: to ensure the machine does not stop. They know, with an instinct older than the bolts holding this pocket universe together, that silence is death. Silence is the void outside the walls. Silence is the end of the dream. And so, they work.

You will see them high on the gantries, hanging from the riveted ceiling plates like bats made of tar. Their elongated fingers, thin as wire and sharp as needles, dip into the microscopic fissures of the pipework. With movements too fast for the human eye to track, they smear their own substance—this mixture of self and solvent—into the cracks. They seal the leaks. They dampen the vibration. They soothe the metal’s fever.

When a piston fires with a particularly violent *clank*, sending a shockwave through the floor, the Lubricants shudder in unison. It is a spasm of empathy. They feel every strain in the cast-iron bones of the room. They rush toward the source of the stress, a swarm of glistening shapes moving against the wind of escaping steam. They press their bodies against the overheating bearings, absorbing the heat, turning white-hot metal cool and grey once more. In doing so, they diminish. A piece of themselves evaporates, rising up to join the condensation cycle, only to fall again as rain, only to be reborn as a new figure elsewhere. They are recycled, eternally, just like the air they breathe.

There is a hierarchy among them, dictated by viscosity. The elders are thick, slow-moving pools of dark sludge that gather in the lowest corners of the nave, near the lead-lined floor. They are ancient, having absorbed the memories of centuries of operation. They do not move often, but when they do, the machinery hums in a deeper, more resonant key. They are the anchors. The younger ones are lighter, more volatile—mist-like spirits that dart between the fluorescent tubes, chasing the sparks, feeding on the ozone. They are frantic, anxious, always listening for the slightest hitch in the rhythm.

Communication among them is tactile. They touch each other with fluid hands, exchanging pressures and temperatures. A quick brush of oily skin conveys a warning: *Pressure building in Sector 4.* A slow, heavy embrace shares a memory: *The turbine spun smoothly today.* They do not need words. The machine speaks for them, and they answer with their bodies.

Occasionally, one of them will pause. It will stand perfectly still amidst the chaos, a statue of gleaming obsidian in the dim light. It will tilt its headless form toward the void beyond the iron plates, as if sensing the darkness outside. For a moment, the relentless motion ceases around it. The steam hisses quieter. The pistons hesitate. In that fleeting second, you can sense a profound loneliness, a terror so deep it feels like gravity. They know they are trapped in a cage of function. They know they are slaves to the noise. But they also know that without the noise, they cease to exist. So the moment passes. The elder sludge shifts. The young mist darts forward. The valve opens. The hiss returns. The scream resumes.

They are beautiful, in a terrible, industrial way. They are the soul of the engine, made manifest in grease and steam. They are the reason the machine forgets how to stop. They hold it together, drop by drop, breath by breath, ensuring that the heart of this cathedral continues to beat, even as it consumes them, over and over again.
