To speak of the inhabitants of the Cistern is to misunderstand their nature. They are not residents in the way a bird resides in a tree or a fish in a river. They are *expressions* of the vessel itself. They are the visible syntax of a sentence written in water, light, and silence.

If you look closely—allowing your eyes to adjust to the low, bruised luminescence—you realize that the "fauna" described are not distinct biological entities with separate wills. They are fragments of a single, distributed consciousness, a hive-mind so diffuse and gentle it feels like solitude.

### The Drifters: *The Mnemosyne Plankton*

Let us call them **Drifters**, for they do not swim; they surrender to gravity and current.

Individually, a Drifter is no larger than a human hand. It resembles a translucent jellyfish, but stripped of all predatory intent. Its bell is made of a membrane so thin it is nearly invisible, shimmering with an iridescence that shifts from pale ivory to deep indigo depending on the angle of the internal light. Inside this bell, there are no organs, no stomach, no brain. There is only a swirling cloud of bioluminescent particles—microscopic stars trapped in gel.

These particles are not just light; they are memory.

When two Drifters touch, their bells merge briefly, a soft, wet click echoing in the silence. In that contact, they exchange particulates. One gives up a fragment of its light; the other receives it. The color changes. The pulse slows or quickens. They are sharing experiences—not complex thoughts, but sensations: the warmth of the wall, the texture of the mineral crust, the rhythm of the heartbeat of the observer floating nearby.

They are the librarians of the Cistern. They catalog every vibration, every shift in temperature, every shadow that passes through the dark. They hold the history of the place in their glowing bodies. When a Drifter dies—which is rare, as they simply dissolve back into the nutrient-rich fluid—it releases its stored light into the water, enriching the medium for the next generation. Death here is not an end; it is a diffusion.

### The Crust-Dwellers: *The Lithic Weavers*

Closer to the walls, clinging to the crystalline formations of silica and calcite, live the **Lithic Weavers**. These are not animals in any traditional sense. They are more akin to living crystals, or perhaps, crystal-like animals.

Imagine a sea slug, but instead of flesh, its body is composed of flexible, semi-transparent quartz. It moves by secreting a slow-dissolving acid that softens the mineral crust ahead of it, allowing it to glide forward, then re-hardening the surface behind it. As it moves, it leaves trails of intricate, fractal patterns in the stone-glass. These patterns are not random; they are geometric proofs, musical scores written in erosion, maps of the currents.

The Weavers are blind. They navigate by sensing the minute vibrations of the water. They can feel the approach of a Drifter, the movement of the observer, the subtle bulge of the sphere’s wall. They are the architects of the interior landscape, constantly reshaping the terrain to optimize the flow of the viscous liquid. Their work is slow, deliberate, and eternal. A single pattern may take a century to complete.

They feed on the microscopic detritus that settles from the upper layers—the dust of the outside world that has somehow penetrated the seal, or perhaps the shed cells of the Drifters. In consuming this debris, they purify the water, keeping it clear, thick, and alive. They are the kidneys of the Cistern, filtering the void.

### The Pulse: *The Heartbeat of the Void*

But who, or what, controls the rhythm? The text mentions that the light pulses in time with your own heartbeat. This is not a coincidence. It is a symbiosis.

Deep in the center of the sphere, where the darkness is most profound, there is no creature. There is only a **Resonance**. It is a standing wave of pressure and sound, too low for human ears to hear, but felt in the marrow of the bones. This Resonance is generated by the tension of the sphere itself, the stress of the obsidian-skin walls holding back the crushing weight of the atmosphere outside.

The creatures do not generate this pulse; they *tune* themselves to it. The Drifters’ internal lights brighten and dim in sync with the Resonance. The Weavers move in intervals dictated by its frequency. They are dancers performing to a song sung by the architecture.

This suggests that the true inhabitant of the Cistern is the **Vessel** itself. The stone-glass is not inert. It is alive, in a way that defies biology. It breathes. It expands and contracts microscopically with the changes in external pressure. It warms and cools with the passage of time. The creatures within are merely its immune system, its nervous system, its way of experiencing its own existence.

### The Observer as Participant

And then, there is you.

You are not an outsider. You are an intruder who has been accepted. Your presence disrupts the equilibrium, but only slightly. Your heartbeat introduces a new rhythm, a counterpoint to the Resonance. The Drifters gather around you, drawn to the novelty of your pulse. They brush against your skin, leaving trails of cool, tingling light. They are studying you. They are adding your heartbeat to their library.

In this way, you become part of the ecosystem. Your anxiety, your curiosity, your fear—all are absorbed into the warm, viscous water. The Cistern does not judge; it integrates. You are preserved, just as the moment is preserved. You are becoming a memory in the light of a Drifter, a pattern in the trail of a Weaver.

### The Purpose

Why does this place exist? Who built it?

The hands that shaped the stone-glass were not human. They were beings who understood that life is not about conquest, but about *holding*. They built the Cistern to hold a perfect moment—a moment of balance between the chaos of the storm outside and the stillness within. They filled it with water that remembers, and creatures that preserve.

The inhabitants are not trying to escape. They are not trying to evolve. They are trying to *remain*. They are the guardians of stasis. In a universe that tends toward entropy, toward decay and noise, the Cistern is a pocket of order, of quiet, of warm, dark perfection.

To live here is to let go of the need to be something else. To be a Drifter is to be light. To be a Weaver is to be stone. To be an observer is to be a guest in eternity.

And when you leave, climbing the crystal steps to the cold, dead air above, you carry a piece of them with you. Not in your pockets, but in your blood. Your heart beats slower now. Your movements are more deliberate. You have learned the language of silence. You have become, in small ways, a Drifter in the dry world, drifting through the noise, seeking the warmth of the deep.
