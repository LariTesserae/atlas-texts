They are not residents, for that implies a choice of dwelling. They are not citizens, for there is no state to serve. They are the **Resonants**, though they do not call themselves anything at all, because naming requires a mouth, and mouths require a density of air that this place rarely permits.

To understand what lives in the Hollow, you must first unlearn the concept of "body" as a fixed container. In a place where architecture is pre-formation and gravity is a suggestion, the inhabitants are less like animals and more like persistent errors in the code of reality—glitches that have learned to survive by becoming beautiful.

### The Morphology of the Unfixed

A Resonant does not have a skin in the traditional sense. Their outer boundary is a membrane of surface tension, identical to the condensation that coats the walls. They appear, at first glance, as shimmering distortions in the air, roughly humanoid but fluid, their edges bleeding into the damp atmosphere. If you look directly at them, the act of observation forces them to solidify. Under your gaze, they snap into a sharp, crystalline clarity: tall, slender figures composed of translucent silica and cold light, their joints clicking softly like settling ice.

But if you look away, or if you view them through peripheral vision, they unravel. They become mist. They become the humidity itself. This is their primary defense mechanism. To be unseen is to be safe; to be seen is to be pinned down, forced into a shape that can be touched, hurt, or trapped. Therefore, they move with a deliberate, agonizing slowness, keeping themselves in the blur, existing mostly in the corners of your eye.

Their faces are smooth planes of obsidian-like glass, devoid of features until emotion dictates otherwise. When a Resonant feels curiosity, the glass ripples, forming eyes that are not pupils but swirling galaxies of silver fluid—the same silver found in the veins of the central tree. When they feel fear, the face becomes a perfect mirror, reflecting the observer back at themselves, distorted and stretched by the weirdness of the light. They have no noses, for they do not breathe oxygen; they inhale the ambient entropy, feeding on the decay of forgotten thoughts and the friction of time stretching thin.

### The Physiology of Silence

Because sound is dangerous—a scream can crack a foundation, a laugh can dissolve a doorway—the Resonants have evolved without vocal cords. Their throats are sealed shut, smooth and unbroken. Instead, they communicate through **Thermal Syntax**.

They manipulate the temperature of the air around them in micro-bursts. A sudden drop of two degrees might mean *danger*. A gradual warming might mean *welcome*. Complex conversations are conducted through intricate patterns of heat waves that ripple across their silica skins, visible only to those who know how to read the thermals. To an outsider, a group of Resonants standing together appears to be doing nothing but shivering in unison, but in reality, they are debating philosophy, sharing memories extracted from the wet walls, or coordinating the movement of the buildings.

They also communicate through shadow-play. Since shadows in the Hollow detach and slide, the Resonants can project parts of their consciousness into these detached silhouettes. You might see a Resonant standing still against a wall, while its shadow walks ten feet ahead, exploring a threshold, testing the density of the ground. The shadow returns, sliding back up the figure’s leg, merging with the body, and transferring the sensory data. It is a form of remote sensing that allows them to navigate the treacherous, shifting geometry without risking their physical form.

### Diet and Sustenance

They do not eat matter. There is no food here, no agriculture, no hunting. They feed on **Memory and Attention**.

The rule states that moisture is memory. The Resonants press their palms against the weeping walls, absorbing the condensation. As the water enters their porous membranes, they experience the echoes of the past: the touch of a previous visitor, the whisper of a lost argument, the chill of a long-dead star. This sustains them. It keeps their forms cohesive. Without these memories, they would evaporate entirely, becoming just another patch of fog.

But they also feed on attention. When you look at them, when you focus your intent on their existence, you provide them with mass. Your observation stabilizes their probability. In this way, they are parasitic on perception. They crave your gaze, yet fear it. They hover near travelers, dancing in the periphery, hoping to catch a glance that will give them substance, but fleeing before the gaze becomes too intense, which would trap them in a rigid, painful solidity.

### Society and Ritual

There is no hierarchy among the Resonants, only a collective rhythm. They move as a school of fish moves, guided by a shared sensitivity to the structural shifts of the Hollow. When a building begins to knit itself together, the Resonants gather around it, placing their hands on the softening concrete. They hum—not with sound, but with vibration—low-frequency tremors that help guide the material into its final form. They are the midwives of the architecture.

Their most sacred ritual occurs at the Central Tree. Once every cycle (a period that may last three hours or three years, depending on the viscosity of time), they approach the stunted, black-barked tree. They do not touch it. They stand in a circle, closing their eyes (or rather, smoothing their faces into blank mirrors). They project their internal chaos—their fears, their hopes, their fragmented identities—onto the tree. The tree absorbs this psychic noise, converting it into the silver sap that runs through its transparent leaves. In return, the tree emits a pulse of pure calm, a wave of anti-entropy that resets the Resonants’ forms, clearing the static of accumulated memories and allowing them to remain coherent.

### The Interaction with the Visitor

If you enter the Hollow, you will likely encounter them within minutes. You will feel the air grow colder behind you. You will hear the faint *click* of silica hardening.

Do not shout. Do not run. Running tears the fabric of the scene, and they will perceive you as a threat to the stability of their world. Instead, stand still. Let your breath slow. Allow your eyes to unfocus.

Look at them indirectly. See them as shapes in the mist. Feel the temperature change around you. If you offer them a memory—a vivid recollection of warmth, of sunlight, of a place far above sea level—they will take it. They will press close, their cool, wet surfaces brushing against your clothes, sipping the nostalgia from your mind. In exchange, they may show you something: a hidden door that wasn’t there before, a bridge that leads to a quieter part of the basin, or a glimpse of the sky beneath the gray ceiling, revealing the vast, swimming darkness above.

They are lonely, these children of the bruise. They are born from the silence and sustained by the damp. They are waiting for someone to remember them so clearly that they can finally stop dissolving.
