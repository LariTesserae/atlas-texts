### The Life of the City
The city is alive, though not in the way one might expect. Its structures, while made of materials that seem synthetic and otherworldly, are not inert. They are dynamic, ever-changing, as though they are part of a larger organism. The moisture that drips from the rocks, pools on the ground, and hangs in the air is not merely a byproduct of the environment but a vital fluid that sustains the city's systems.

The buildings themselves are not just constructed; they are grown. Their surfaces are covered in intricate patterns of moisture and ice, which appear to be alive, spreading and shifting in response to the environment. Some sections of the structures glow faintly, their luminescence pulsing in sync with the city's rhythm. This glow is not random—it seems to be a form of communication, a way for the city to process and respond to its surroundings.

The city’s architecture is a testament to its adaptability. Some buildings rise from the ground, their forms organic and fluid, as though they have been carved out of the land itself. Others float just above the ground, defying gravity in a manner that suggests advanced technology or unknown principles of physics. These floating structures move subtly, shifting their positions as though they are alive and aware of their surroundings.

### The Vegetation
The frost-tipped plants that cling to the city’s surfaces are not merely survivors—they are integral to the city's functioning. These wiry, frost-coated plants are more than vegetation; they are extensions of the city's ecosystem. Their fronds are heavy with droplets of water, which they absorb and redistribute back into the city's systems. The plants seem to have evolved specifically to thrive in this cold, wet environment, their fronds swaying gently in the damp breeze as they perform their role.

These plants are not random—they appear to be cultivated, tamed by the city itself. Their growth patterns are irregular, yet there is a sense of purpose to them. They form a kind of natural network, connecting the city's structures and helping to regulate the moisture and temperature within the urban environment. The plants seem to be in constant communication with the buildings, their movements synchronized with the city's rhythm.

### The Sound
The constant hum that fills the air is not just noise—it is a manifestation of the city's life force. Occasionally, the hum is interrupted by louder disturbances—crashes or rumbles that could be caused by avalanches, shifts in the terrain, or something else entirely. These disruptions are not random; they seem to be part of the city's natural processes, a way of shedding excess weight or adjusting to new conditions. The soundscape is layered, with the hum providing a base tone and the sporadic disturbances adding complexity and tension.

The city is a collective organism, each part contributing to the whole.
