In the vast, low elevation of this world, the terrain is a complex tapestry where nothing natural exists—only the products of an ancient, industrial civilization. There is little to no fauna, save for the rarest of creatures—small, mechanical beasts that scuttle across the terrain, their joints and limbs clicking softly as they navigate the labyrinthine architecture. These beings are not living in the traditional sense, but mechanical constructs, possibly remnants of a bygone era of industrial dominance.

The light itself is strange, a harsh, unblinking glow that casts no real shadows, instead replacing them with stark, geometric patterns that shift and distort over time.

There is no water, no air, no life as we would understand it, only a strange, synthetic world that breathes slowly, quietly, with a pulse that feels both mechanical and eerily organic.
