These plants do not merely exist alongside the crystals; they grow within them, their organic matter blending seamlessly with the inorganic, as if the two were never truly separate.

What lives here is not a single entity but perhaps a multitude of them, working in concert to maintain the balance of this ecosystem. The constant flow of liquid from the crystalline peaks suggests a kind of circulation system, perhaps analogous to blood in a biological organism. The plants, in turn, absorb this liquid, using it to fuel their growth and spread across the landscape.

The plants sway gently, as if in response to an unseen breeze, though there is no wind. Instead, the movement seems to be driven by some internal rhythm, a kind of heartbeat that pulses through the entire landscape. The droplets of liquid that drip from the crystalline formations create tiny streams that flow across the ground, nourishing the plants and shaping the terrain in subtle, ever-changing ways.
