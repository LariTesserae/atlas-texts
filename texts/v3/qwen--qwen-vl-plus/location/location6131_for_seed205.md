These structures are covered in a layer of moss and ferns, their surfaces eroded but still holding a kind of dignity—a testament to time and the forces that shaped them. The erosion here is slow, a gentle process that has allowed the jungle to reclaim this high ground, weaving its tendrils around the stone and making it part of its own ecosystem.

In this place, density is sparse, but the emptiness between the jungle's tendrils is deceptive. The gaps between the trees and vines are not silent; they are filled with movement, with the fluttering of wings, the scampering of unseen creatures, and the soft rustle of the jungle itself. The jungle is vast, but it is alive with life in ways that are almost overwhelming. Every inch of space is occupied, but not in a way that feels crowded.
