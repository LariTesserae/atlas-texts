The soundscape is layered, with the hum providing a base tone and the sporadic disruptions creating a sense of tension.

Others look like ancient ruins, their surfaces weathered and pitted by the relentless cold. The architecture is layered, with different sections appearing to be built on top of one another, creating a labyrinthine effect. The city is vast, sprawling across the landscape in a seemingly endless expanse, with no clear boundary between structure and environment.

The scale of the place is overwhelming, a reminder that this is not simply a town or city but something larger, something that feels planetary in scope. The horizon is never straight—it curves upward, suggesting that the ground itself has been shaped by forces beyond human understanding. The light is normal, but it feels muted, as though the atmosphere is filtering it, giving everything a cold, bluish tint. Even the brightest moments of daylight feel somber, as if the world is in mourning.

The moisture on the rocks and buildings suggests a kind of slow, steady growth, as if the place is slowly consuming itself. The sound of water is constant, but it’s not random—there’s a rhythm to it, a pulse that seems almost deliberate. Some of the structures seem to defy gravity, floating just above the ground, while others appear to be shifting or moving subtly, as though they’re alive. The materials used in construction are unfamiliar, a mix of metals and synthetic compounds that seem to react to the environment in unpredictable ways. The erosion is minimal, the structures intact, the landscape seemingly frozen in time. It’s as though the city and the land have reached a kind of balance, a stillness that feels both unnerving and awe-inspiring.
