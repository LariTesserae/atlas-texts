The air is thick as felt and twice as warm, pressing against the skin like a second body. It hums.

The chamber—if chamber is the right word, and no word is quite right—sits below everything. Not simply underground; this is a place that was never intended to see the sky. The ceiling, some twelve feet overhead, is not rock exactly but a vault of interleaved crystalline plates the colour of burnt honey, their edges sharp as freshly broken glass. They have not been cut. They have grown this way, meeting in angles that drift just slightly away from Euclidean, as though the geometry of the place was decided by a committee that almost agreed. Nothing drips. Nothing dissolves. Nothing settles. This ceiling has looked exactly this way for longer than the surface above has had soil.

Below it, in the permanent amber dusk, the veg-etation riots.

Lush, yes, but lush in a dry way. The plants—though plants is an assumption—have never met water. Their leaves are fans of metallic-looking foil, copper and rose-gold and a green so deep it reads as black, each one thin as a moth’s wing and dry as paper ash. They do not photosynthesize in any spectrum the human eye knows; the dim light here is not sunlight but a low amber phosphorescence emanating from nodules in the crystal ceiling, a light that carries heat but no ultraviolet, a light that tastes of tin if you open your mouth too long. The flora drinks that light directly into its vascular tissues, and it has drunk deeply for uncounted millennia.

The foliage is abundant but not dense. That is the sparse-ness: every frond and fan-leaf and spiraling stalk stands with deliberate room around it, as though each occupies an invisible territory it will not breach. Three feet between one plant and the next, always three feet, give or take an inch, a spacing so regular it suggests a rule, a law, a treaty. The ground between them is not soil. It is a pale, fine-grained mineral floor, something between pumice and compacted salt, and it is warm. Not sun-warmed. Body-warm. 98.6 degrees, approximately, and absolutely stable. Place your hand on it and it returns your heat to you exactly, neither taking nor giving, a perfect thermal mirror.

And the sounds.

Loud does not capture it. The place is a continuous roar of small noises, a dry chorus that never rises and never falls and never, ever pauses. The primary source is the fauna, and the fauna is everywhere in the gaps between the plants, filling the airspace the vegetation disdains.

They are the Clatterwings.

Imagine a creature built from twigs and parchment and abandoned intentions. Their bodies are the size of two fists held together, articulated from chitinous segments that interlock with audible clicks. They have too many legs—seven, always seven, an odd number that makes their gait a syncopated limp—and their wings are not membrane but thin slats of the same copper-coloured material as the ceiling plates, which they beat against the air with a sound like a thousand tiny looms working in unison. Tock-tick-tock-tick-tock. They do not sing. They percuss. The air fills with the click of their leg joints, the rattle of their wings, the scrape of their mandibles worrying at the crisp edges of the foil-leaves, which they consume in slow, deliberate mouthfuls, digesting trace minerals and excreting fine pale dust that settles back onto the floor. Everything is recycled. Nothing leaves.

A Clatterwing lands on a frond near you—there is a you here, for scale's sake, a human figure in this pocket of impossible ecology—and it regards you with eyes that are not eyes but dimpled sensor patches the colour of tarnished silver. It has no fear. Nothing here has ever had cause to develop fear. The ecosystem has no gradients, no seasons, no day, no night, no predators. The Clatterwings eat the foil-leaves; the foil-leaves drink the amber light; the amber light emanates from the ceiling at a rate that has not varied by a single photon in the duration of the place's existence. A perfect closed loop. A frozen equilibrium. The population of Clatterwings is exactly the number required to consume the foliage at exactly the rate it grows. They reproduce by parthenogenesis, one offspring per adult per eon, because the math requires it, and they die by simply stopping, mid-click, falling to the warm floor where their copper wings slowly delaminate and integrate into the ceiling's crystal lattice over periods too long to count.

Built things exist here, scattered and strange. They are the only sign that anything with intent ever passed through, but they are clearly not native to the chamber's logic. Small cairns of stacked stones that do not match the mineral floor—river rock, water-smoothed, somehow transported from a wet world that cannot possibly be near. A single clay bowl, hand-formed, unglazed, sitting perfectly centered in a three-foot gap between two copper ferns, holding nothing but the same warm air. Carved marks on one of the ceiling plates, primitive scratchings that might be a tally, or a star map, or a child's drawing of a Clatterwing, six crude legs and an accidental seventh scratched in later by a different hand. These artifacts do not decay. Nothing decays. Nothing changes. The bowl will sit forever, or until someone moves it, and no one has moved it in a very long time.

The warmth is the real mind of the place. 2.5 on a scale of 3, yes, but it is more than a number. It is the body heat of a sleeper who will never wake. It is thermal stasis, the temperature at which all reactions here have been optimized, the Goldilocks point for chitin-flex and crystal-growth and foil-leaf metabolism. You feel it in your lungs when you breathe—the air is dry, utterly dry, no water anywhere, not in the air, not in the walls, not in the sap of the plants (which flows as a suspension of mineral salts in some clear, inert medium that evaporates without cooling). Your own sweat rises from your skin and seems to vanish before it can bead, wicked away by the thirsty atmosphere, and yet you do not feel dehydrated. The place somehow balances you, too. You are part of the equilibrium now. You have been added to the math.

Above, or around, or beyond—directions are slippery here—the ceiling plates shift. Not physically; the shift is in the light. The amber glow pulses once, a slow blink that takes a full twenty seconds from dim to bright to dim again, and every Clatterwing in the chamber, thousands of them, responds with a synchronized beat of their copper wings, one massive dry CHCK that rolls through the space like a wave. Then silence for a heartbeat. Then the individual tock-tick-tocking resumes, and the moment passes, and everything is as it was.

This is the strange-ness. Not that the place is alien, but that it *sees* you. The pulse was not random. It never is. It has been happening at intervals since the ceiling grew, and the intervals are precisely timed—no, that's not it either. They are precisely *placed*. They occur when a condition is met. The condition was your arrival. The condition will be your departure. And then, for another stretch of centuries or seconds (time has the same quality as the light: thick, amber, indivisible), the chamber will hold its breath and not-pulse and simply be, a pocket of dry warm loud life beneath a world that has never suspected it exists.

The elevation parameter said below surface, but that's true in a way that transcends mere topology. This place is beneath the surface of reality's attention. It has been forgotten by geology, by weather, by erosion—the erosion score is low because nothing here erodes, the crystals are eternally sharp, the mineral floor eternally smooth, no water to carve canyons, no wind to wear down edges. The only forces are the slow growth of foil-leaves and the steady consumption by Clatterwings and the amber light that feeds it all, a trinity in perfect thermodynamic stasis.

You stoop to touch the clay bowl. It is warm. Of course it is warm. Everything is warm. And inside the bowl, now, there is something that was not there a moment ago: a single copper wing-slat, shed by a Clatterwing or placed by something else, something that stacks stones and scratches tallies and leaves offerings in bowls that wait for millennia to receive them. The wing catches the light and glints, and the light glint is a message you can almost read, and the message says: *You are here now. The math has adjusted. Stay as long as you like. Nothing will change.*

The roar of small clicks continues. The foil-leaves rustle in no breeze. The ceiling plates hold their amber vigil.

Nothing changes.

Everything is exactly as it must be, and exactly as it has been, and exactly as it will be, forever, until the single variable that is you decides to carry its water-smoothed stones and its carbon-dioxide exhalations and its disruptive, temporary heartbeat back to the surface world where things erode and grow and die.

The chamber will remember you, though. It remembers everything, in its static, frozen, eternal way.

Tock-tick-tock.
