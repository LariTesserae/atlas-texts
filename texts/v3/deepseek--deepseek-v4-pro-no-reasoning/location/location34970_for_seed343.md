The first thing that hits you is the sound. It’s not a single noise but a wall of vibration, a constant, grinding thrum that seems to come from inside your own skull. It’s the sound of a trillion tiny mouths scraping, of chitin on living wood, of high-pitched chittering calls layered so thick they become a solid drone. Conversation is impossible; you feel your own vocal cords buzz, but you can't hear a thing.

You are standing on a bridge, or what was once a bridge. It’s a braided cable of a fibrous, woody material, as thick as a man is tall, suspended across a gulf of open air. The scale of the place is disorienting. The bridge, one of dozens visible from this single point, spans a gap between two colossal pillars of vegetation. The pillars are not trees in any normal sense; they are the trunks of the great Stalk-Coral, a single organism that has become the entire vertical world. The bridge beneath your feet is a root, an exploratory tendril it sent out centuries ago and then thickened into a transit-way.

Look down. The view is vertiginous. The pillars descend into a warm, hazy abyss, their pale, smooth bark disappearing into a gloom lit by faint, drifting motes of bioluminescence. There is no ground, not in any memory of this place. The world is an open, three-dimensional lattice of these pale trunks, some a few feet wide, others vast enough to hold their own internal weather. Between them, for miles in every direction, is empty, humid air—a near-void filled only with drifting spores and the ceaseless traffic of life.

The vegetation is total. Plant-as-architecture. Plant-as-world. The Stalk-Coral’s surface is not a passive bark but a living skin, slick with condensation from the perpetual warm fog. Water doesn't fall as rain here; it simply exists as a supersaturated mist that beads on every surface, dripping slowly from the edges of fungal shelves and the tips of fern-like fronds. This is the only water, a constant, intermittent seepage collected in the cup-shaped hollows of symbiotic bromeliads. The sound of dripping would be a welcome peace, but it is utterly lost in the drone.

The fauna is overwhelming, a new definition of the word. The air itself feels thick with bodies. The primary architects of the deafening noise are the Skirls—segmented, larval creatures the size of a finger, with translucent bodies and frantic legs. They swarm the Stalk-Coral in plumes, constantly scraping the living skin for microbial film. Their movement is the ecosystem’s heartbeat. They part around you like a river of living silk, their individual high-pitched stridulations merging into the cosmic hum. They are the base of everything, the bottomless food source that supports the horrors above.

Against the pale pillars, you see scattered remnants of a prior intelligence. Here, a platform of woven sticks lashed with sinew clings to a sheer vertical face. There, a pattern of hand-prints is etched into the living bark, now distorted and stretched by the trunk’s slow growth over centuries. These are the marks of the Weavers, a people who seem to have been entirely absorbed or erased. Their structures are primitive, scattered, and few—a rope ladder hanging into mist, the collapsed frame of a domed hut, the unused, rusting blade of a simple iron knife, a technology that arrived and died. They are not here now. There are no voices to cut through the insect drone.

One of the great pillars nearby bears a massive, vertical scar, a cleft in its side where the flesh has been hollowed into a cave. The edges of the wound are healed over with a thick, calloused growth, marking it as ancient, a settled-into-final-form feature of the terrain. Inside, a viscous, amber-hued sap still weeps, an eternal, primordial scab. It is a process of decay and renewal so old it has become a feature, not an event. The ecosystem is active, a churning wheel of predation, but the landscape itself is exhausted, ancient.

Your eye catches a flicker of different movement. A Shroud-Wing drifts up from the abyss, its broad, membranous wings painted with false eyespots. It is silent, a predator on the wing. Its hunting method isn't speed but confusion; as it floats through a plume of Skirls, the eyespots flash in a cascading sequence, disorienting the swarm. It lazily scoops a hundred of them into its wide, toothless maw. This is a normal light occurrence, happening a dozen times a minute, but it is undeniably unusual, a spectacle of alien logic. The Skirl swarm’s hum pitches erratically for a second, then returns to its crushing, oscillating frequency.

The warmth is constant, a blanket you cannot escape. It rises from the decomposition happening deep in the abyss below and is trapped by the dense, interwoven canopy of the highest fronds above, a roof of vegetation a thousand feet over your head. The world is a warm, open, hollow cathedral, its pillars built of pure plant, its congregation a deafening, overwhelming tide of insects, its god a silent, ancient process of growth and slow, weeping erosion. The Weavers’ scattered bridges are the only suggestion that this place was ever meant to be a home, rather than simply to exist.
