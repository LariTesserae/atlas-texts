Split the sky.

That is the first instruction — not from any voice, but from the place itself. The sky here does not sit above; it impales. A vastness of pale ochre, almost white at its zenith, darkening to burnt sienna at the horizons, and across it, at all angles, are the seams. They are not clouds. They are fissures in the light, hairline cracks through which a deeper, non-optical blackness presses, a darkness that seems to be looking back. The normal light — a flat, shadowless radiance — pours from the sky's unbroken membrane, but at the seams, light leaks *upward*, drawn out of the world in slow, soundless draughts.

You are high. The elevation is not a peak but a plateau so ancient and so vast that the concept of a "summit" has been erased. You stand on a plain of fused, vitrified mineral, a single continuous sheet of something between obsidian and frosted glass, coloured a deep, intestinal red. It is called the Clinker-Flat. It stretches in every direction to a horizon that is perceptibly, unnervingly curved — not with the curve of a planet, but with the sag of a membrane under tension. The Flat rings faintly underfoot, a frequency just below hearing, a vibration that makes the bones in the inner ear hum in sympathy. This is the only sound that belongs to the ground itself. The rest is fauna.

The fauna is overwhelming. Not in number, but in presence. The density of the place is so low, the air so thin and dry, that the space between living things is a chasm. But each one fills that chasm completely. They are the Engines.

The nearest is a quarter-mile off, yet its sound presses against you like a hand. A low, rhythmic chuffing — a colossal, idling piston the size of a grain silo, but organic. Its body is a column of nested, chitinous rings, each one rotating against the next with a friction that throws off spirals of copper-green sparks. It is boring into the Clinker-Flat with a slow, geological patience. A cloud of finely ground ruby-glass dust plumes from its base, hanging in the thin air for an hour or more before settling. This one is a Steadfast-Drill. It will take a thousand years to breach the crust, and it will not stop until it does.

Beyond it, striding across the plain, is a Mantle-Loom. A tripod of spindly, articulated legs supports a torso of woven, metallic sinew. From its underside, it pays out a glistening, monofilament thread, laying it onto the vitrified plain in a precise, mathematically pure spiral that extends for miles. The thread is a conductor. It flashes with intermittent pulses of dull orange energy, carrying some charge from the seams in the sky to the deep place the Drill seeks. The Loom's motion is a slow, deliberate ballet. Each step takes a visible fraction of a minute. The sound of its leg-joints is a series of clean, metallic reports — *crack, crack, crack* — that echo across the empty distance.

And there are others. In the far middle-ground, a cloud of airborne forms — Shard-Motes. They are like scraps of torn mirror, tumbling in a loose, spiraling vortex. Their sound is a constant, high-pitched glassy chiming, a shattered wind-chime choir. They are feeding on the light-leaks at a low seam, clustering around the upwelling darkness like moths at a wound, their mirrored bodies flashing with captured, redistributed photons. Their motion is the fastest thing here, a rapid, hungry swirling that contrasts with the majestic slowness of the giants on the ground.

Between these presences, the land is almost completely empty. No rubble, no sand, no dust but the freshly bored plumes. The erosion here is not by water or wind in any recognizable sense. It is an erosion of *state*. The Clinker-Flat has been scraped and polished not by particle abrasion, but by the presence of the fauna, by the backwash of their non-biological metabolisms. The Loom's thread scores microscopic grooves. The Drill's spark-showers pit the surface with tiny craters, an industrial-acid rain without the rain. The Flat has been in a state of balanced, patient weathering for millions of years. It is a finished surface, a mature landscape. It has settled into its form.

There is no water. The chemical basis here is non-aqueous, a silicon-alkali cycle. The red of the glass-plain is the colour of potassium leached and re-fused in a cycle that bypasses liquid entirely, sublimating and condensing as a metallic fog too heavy to rise, that rolls down-slope to the drills to be cracked apart again. The atmosphere is a thin exhalation of this process, a shimmer of distortion rather than a breathable gas. It tastes of ozone and hot ceramics.

There is no vegetation, no built structures, no recognizable biology. The weirdness is complete. The Engines are not machines built from a blueprint. They are the endemic life of a reality where the laws of thermodynamics have a different, more structured personality. The line between object and process is gone. The Drill is not a creature; it is the act of drilling, made manifest and given continuity. The Loom is the principle of connection. The Shard-Motes are the event of reflection. They are ontologies in motion.

A deep, sub-sonic groan rolls across the plateau. On the far horizon, where the sky's seams converge on a point of leprous darkness, one of the Steadfast-Drills has finished. Its chuffing stops. The final ring rotates, locks into place, and the whole column sinks, magnificently slow, into the hole it has made. There is no collapse, just a silent, dignified descent into the deep. A geyser of red light, cold and clear, erupts from the borehole in its wake, a perfect laser-line straight into the splintered sky. A new seam forms, instantly, like an afterimage. The cycle is complete. Another will begin, patient and absolute.

The sound of this place is the aggregate of all these processes. It is loud, but it is a loudness of specific, isolated points. A vast, stereophonic field of industrial-precise punctuations: the chuff, the crack, the chime. In the silence between them, you hear the true baseline: the ringing of the Clinker-Flat, the low hum of a reality with its idle set slightly too high. It is a landscape not of matter, but of forces, working out a slow, noisy, and utterly alien logic. It is a factory for the end of the world, and it is running perfectly.
