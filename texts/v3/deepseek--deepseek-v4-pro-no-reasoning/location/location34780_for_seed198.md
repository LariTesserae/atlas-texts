The first thing that strikes you is the sheer, unrelenting *presence* of it all. This is not a landscape you enter; it’s a landscape that closes around you, a fully saturated volume. The air itself is a substance, dense with a cool mist that never quite resolves into rain but is far too heavy to be called fog. It’s a perpetual state of post-rain, where every surface is beaded, weeping, or trickling. You breathe it in, and it coats your lungs with the taste of wet metal and humus.

You are high up, not on a peak, but on a sprawling, elevated plateau that has been entirely, industrially terraformed. The ground underfoot isn’t ground. The “total” built parameter is literal here: there is no exposed earth, no naked rock. The substrate is a continuous, riveted mosaic of steel grates, corrugated iron, and worn-smooth ferroconcrete. It’s a single, unbroken floor that extends for miles, a regional-scale factory floor. The elevation is felt in the thin, cool edge to the air and the way distant structures are caught in banks of low cloud.

Because the density is absolute, there is no empty space. The plateau is a packed forest of verticality. Soaring distillation columns, wrapped in a circulatory system of condensing pipes, stand shoulder-to-shoulder with skeletal winding towers. Each tower and column is connected to its neighbor by a mesh of catwalks, cable trays, and ductwork, creating a secondary, tangled canopy overhead. This is the source of the “lush” vegetation score: it’s a lushness of the industrial, a mechanical jungle. Pipes as thick as ancient sequoias are wrapped in a verdant pelt of moss and liverwort, thriving on the constant condensation. Ferns, huge and prehistoric, erupt from the joints of steam manifolds, their fronds unfurling in the dim, mist-filtered light.

The light itself is a permanent gloaming. High above, a lattice of rusting gantries supports the real ceiling of this world—an array of enormous, age-blackened copper hoods and flues that capture and re-condense every wisp of vapor. Only a dim, diffuse, grey-green luminescence filters down, thrown by caged bulkhead lamps on the widest processional catwalks. Their glow reflects in a million beads of water on every pipe, rail, and rivet, making the entire world seem to glisten from within. Shadows are soft and pervasive, a place where your eyes never fully adjust.

And it is turbulent. The air trembles with a constant, frenetic energy. Great machines, diesel or perhaps steam-driven, pound rhythmically beneath the floor grates, a felt vibration in the bones more than a heard sound. Every few minutes, a new process begins or ends with no discernible logic: a section of pipework will shudder, hiss, and jet a plume of steam, only to have it sucked instantly into a vent. A conveyor belt on a high catwalk will clatter to life, transport nothing for a few seconds, then fall silent. Flocks of pressure-relief valves chatter hysterically in the distance. It is a Solaris-like chaos, a hyperactive, aimless industry, an anthill energy constructing and destroying its own internal logic in real time. This instability, however, is happening on an “established” framework. The rivets are worn smooth by decades of this vibration, the iron is deeply pitted with a stable, settled rust, and the moss is thick, indicating a long-standing balance between creation and decay.

The sound is mostly the quiet hum of this contained chaos. The sheer density of the machinery absorbs and deadens noise. The roar of a furnace is felt as a pressure in the ears but heard only as a muffled throb. The dominant sound is the water: the drip, the trickle, the weeping of a million metallic forms, a liquid, percussive susurrus that unifies the space. There are no animal calls, no bird song. Fauna is rare. The only movement you might spy, from the corner of your eye, is the slow, gelatinous progress of a thumb-sized, translucent slug dragging itself across a steam-warmed dial face, leaving a glistening trail. Or perhaps a single, fat, pigment-less moth, battered and tattered, orbiting a light cage in an endless, dazed flight.

It is a landscape of total artifice that has, over a vast stretch of time, been recolonized by a simpler, wetter nature. It is a paradoxical ecosystem, a cool, high-altitude mechanical swamp, packed to saturation, bathed in perpetual twilight, and trembling with a purposeless, beautiful, and perfectly balanced internal storm.
