The air is warm and still, and it presses against you like a held breath. It is not the heat of a sun—there is no sun here, and there never has been—but a deep geothermal warmth that rises from the floor and radiates from the walls, a heat that seems to belong to the stone itself. The darkness is nearly complete, the light parameter barely a whisper, but it is not the darkness of a closed room. It is a vast darkness, a continental darkness, the kind of darkness that has its own geography. You sense the space around you not with your eyes but with the way sound behaves—or fails to behave—and with the faint stirrings of the air against your skin.

You are below the surface of something, though “surface” here is a tricky concept. The world above, if there is one, is not a place anyone goes. This is the interior of a world that never finished becoming a planet, or perhaps one that took a different evolutionary path entirely. The terrain underfoot is mineral, but not cold mineral. It is warm to the touch, slightly yielding in places, like compressed ash or sintered dust, and everywhere it is sharp. The features are young, geologically speaking—razor-edged ridges, brittle fins of crystalline growth, fragile terraces that crumble if you look at them too long. This place was freshly made, or freshly exposed, and erosion has not yet had time to round its edges. There is no water to speak of, not even humidity. What little moisture might once have existed has long since been baked out or bound up in the chemistry of the rock. The dryness is absolute, a desiccation that you can taste on your tongue, a faint metallic tang that might be the air itself losing patience with the concept of moisture.

And yet the place is not empty.

The fauna here is abundant, and it moves in the dark. You cannot see them, but you can sense them—not as individuals, at first, but as a kind of background texture, a constant low rustle and click and whisper that fills the enormous space. The sounds are mostly quiet, but they are everywhere, a tapestry of tiny noises that never quite resolves into a pattern. Clicking. Scraping. A faint, high-frequency hum that might be communication or might be digestion. The creatures of this world are not like animals you know. They are dense in number, layered in the space, occupying the sharp terrain in ways that seem to defy ordinary biology. Some cling to the crystalline fins. Some burrow into the warm, ashy floor. Some drift, or seem to drift, in the still air, suspended by mechanisms you cannot guess at.

They are strange. Profoundly strange.

The weirdness here is not subtle. It is a wrongness that sits just behind your sternum, a constant low-grade dissonance. The fauna moves in ways that seem to ignore certain physical constraints. A shape will be in one place, and then it will be in another, without crossing the space between—or perhaps it does cross, but so quickly or so smoothly that your senses refuse to track it. The creatures are jointed, or not jointed. They are segmented, or maybe that's just how they choose to present themselves. They have too many limbs, or the wrong number of limbs, or limbs that are also sensory organs, or sensory organs that are also mouths. They are built, somehow, from the same mineral sharpness as the landscape, as if they crystallized out of the warm stone and decided to start moving. Their bodies click against the terrain with a sound like glass tapped against glass.

There are structures here, too. Scattered. Sparse. Some are clearly built, though “built” might be too strong a word. They are arranged—piles of sharp stones, lean-tos of crystalline blades, small enclosures that might be nests or might be something more deliberate. The technology is primitive, but it is technology: shaped tools, scrapers, small hammer-stones, objects that have been chosen and modified for a purpose. The fauna makes these things. The fauna uses them. The fauna is, in some sense, intelligent—or at least tool-using—and it has been here for a long time. Long enough to leave marks on the landscape. Long enough to build a culture, of sorts, in the permanent dark.

The place has a scale that is difficult to hold in the mind. It is vast enough to have its own weather, though “weather” here is not clouds and rain. It is a slow convection of warm air, rising from the deeper places, cycling back down in great, invisible currents. You can feel it on your skin—a faint, periodic drift, a breath of air that is imperceptibly warmer or cooler, carrying with it the clicking sounds of distant fauna. The space is not a single cavern, but a network, a world of interconnected voids and passages and chambers, some of them large enough that their walls are lost in darkness, some of them so small that the fauna must compress their strange bodies to pass through. The density of life is high, but it is not evenly distributed. There are places where the fauna clusters thickly, where the sounds layer into a kind of white noise, and places where the terrain is bare and silent, waiting.

And yet, for all this activity, the landscape never changes. The dynamic parameter is nearly zero. This is a world in equilibrium, a frozen moment that has persisted for geological ages. The sharp edges never erode. The warm stone never cools. The fauna moves and builds and communicates, but it does not evolve. The structures are rebuilt in the same places, in the same configurations, generation after generation. The tools are made and used and discarded, and the new tools are identical to the old ones. There is no gradient, no direction, no unfolding story. The place simply is, and has always been, and will always be, exactly as it is now. The creatures do not seem to mind. Perhaps they cannot conceive of change. Perhaps change is a concept that simply does not apply here, a foreign import from a universe with different rules.

The atmosphere is thick, but not humid. The gases are not the ones you are used to—there is oxygen, of a sort, but it is bound up in compounds that your lungs were not designed for. The air has a weight to it, a density, as if the entire space is gently pressurized. It carries smells: hot stone, a faint sulfurous tang, something organic but not rotting, a sharp mineral scent like crushed feldspar. The smells layer on top of each other, and they never dissipate, because there is nowhere for them to go.

The terrain itself is a patchwork. In some places, the floor is smooth and pocked, like the surface of a lava flow that cooled in minutes rather than years. In others, it is a forest of thin, vertical spines, some of them needle-sharp, some of them broad and blade-like, all of them warm to the touch. The spines ring faintly if you brush against them, a sound that propagates through the still air and is answered, sometimes, by a flurry of clicks from the darkness. There are fields of small, spherical nodules, each about the size of a fist, that are not attached to the floor but simply rest there, perfectly round, perfectly identical. The fauna uses these for something—you see them carried, stacked, arranged in patterns that might be decorative or might be functional or might be both.

The walls, where they are close enough to touch, are not vertical but slanted, fractured, faceted. They glitter in the almost-dark, a faint phosphorescence that might be mineral or might be biological. The light is so dim that it barely qualifies as light at all—more a suggestion of visibility, a hint that the darkness is not absolute. It comes from everywhere and nowhere, a ambient glow that has no source, as if the air itself is faintly luminescent. It does not illuminate so much as it deepens the shadows.

The fauna is aware of you. They do not approach, but they are watching—or sensing, or whatever verb applies to creatures that may not have eyes. You can feel their attention, a prickling on your skin, a sense of being assessed by minds that operate on entirely different principles. They are not hostile. They are not friendly. They are simply present, and abundant, and busy, and they have incorporated you into their frozen equilibrium, a new variable that the system has already accounted for and neutralized.

The sounds continue, endless, quiet, unchanging. Click. Scrape. Hum. The warm air shifts. The sharp terrain glints. The vast dark space holds everything in place, a world without water or plants or sun or change, a world that built itself differently from the start and never saw any reason to alter course. Somewhere in the distance, a structure is being built that is exactly like the structure that was built before it, and the one before that, stretching back into a past that has no beginning. The fauna moves. The stone breathes heat. The darkness waits.

And nothing ends, because nothing ever started. It just is.
