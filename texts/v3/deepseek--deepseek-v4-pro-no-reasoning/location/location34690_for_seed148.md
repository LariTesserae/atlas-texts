Where you stand, the ground gives slightly. Not soil. A woven density of pale, finger-thick roots and rhizomes that form a continuous mat, a single interlocking surface that stretches away in all directions. No bare earth anywhere. No rock visible. You are inside something. That's the cathedral scale: not vast in the way of deserts or oceans, but enclosed, chambered. And the fauna. They are everywhere, once you learn to perceive them. Not in the open—the empty quality, the 0.1 density, means that between the great pillars and the root floor and the ceiling, there is almost nothing. The predator-prey cycle here is not always one of consumption. Sometimes it's assimilation. Sometimes the prey enters the predator and they become a third thing, a temporary composite that will later divide into two new forms, neither the same as before. You watch this happen three times before you understand what you're seeing, and then you stop trusting your categories.

The built element: you keep finding evidence of intention. Not current, not recent, but layered into the very structure of the plant architecture. The braided trunks forming pillars—some of them were guided. You can see the scars of old pruning, the way certain limbs were trained horizontal to form beams, the sockets where bioluminescent nodes were once—or are still—cultivated in regular geometric arrays. This is a city that grew itself, or was grown, or grew its growers into itself until the distinction stopped mattering. There are hollows in the root walls that are unmistakably doorways, arching openings leading to smaller chambers, some still holding shapes that might have been furniture, masses of softer mosslike growth contoured to the form of resting bodies. Everything is overgrown but not abandoned—maintained by the ecosystem itself, the way a body maintains its organs without conscious direction.

Somewhere in the middle distance, a rhythmic sound: metal on something, or something on metal. A slow, regular clanking, industrial in its regularity, muffled by the vegetal mass but persistent. You follow it through the dark, wading the shallow root-braided river where it forks, ducking under a low arch of fused lianas that pulse with their own slow bioluminescence, and you find a machine.

It's embedded in the living wall, half swallowed by roots that have grown around and through its iron housing. A pump. Or a heart. Pipes—copper, corroded green—emerge from its base and burrow into the root floor, pulsing with liquid you can feel through the metal. Scale shifts. You realize the cathedral grove is itself one chamber in a larger structure, connected by those root-walled corridors to other chambers, other hollows, a hamlet of such spaces, each with its own microclimate, its own variant fauna, its own embedded machines performing functions you can only guess at.
