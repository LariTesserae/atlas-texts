The place has no name. It was not made for naming.

You enter it slowly, though "enter" is the wrong verb — there is no threshold, no boundary where the world changes from one thing to another. Instead, there is a gradual thickening. The darkness is not the absence of light but a substance in its own right, warm and faintly humid, pressing against the skin like a second atmosphere. It carries weight. It has texture. It breathes.

The ground is not ground. It is a continuous, living mattress of vegetation, spongy and resilient underfoot, woven from countless interpenetrating forms — stems as thick as thighs running horizontal for miles before humping upward into what might be trunks or might be something else, rhizomes that surface and dive and surface again, leaves the size of houses folded and refolded into geometries that feel architectural even though nothing here was built. The word "plant" fails. This is plant-as-world, plant-as-terrain, plant-as-medium. There is no soil visible. There is no rock. There is only this body, this single contiguous organism (or is it many? the boundary problem is unresolved and possibly unresolvable) that fills the space from whatever lies beneath to whatever arches above, and above is very far.

You cannot see the canopy. The darkness swallows vertical distance. But you feel it — not with your skin but with some deeper proprioception, a sense of scale that registers in the bones. The air moves in vast, slow exhalations, currents of warmth and moisture that rise and fall on rhythms longer than breath, longer than seasons. The place is big enough to have its own weather, and it does: a constant, barely-perceptible precipitation, not rain but a condensing film that beads on every surface, that drips from every edge, that keeps the air saturated and the plant-body glistening. Water is everywhere, not as a feature but as a condition. The atmosphere itself is half liquid. The leaves (if they are leaves) weep continuously, not from sorrow but from physics, from the simple inability of the air to hold any more moisture against the warmth that rises from below.

Below. That is the strangeness.

Because this place is young. The forms are sharp, unweathered, crisp in their biological geometry. There are no fallen trunks, no rotting masses, no detritus. Everything is fresh, as if the whole system emerged yesterday from some immense seed and has not yet begun to decay. The edges of the fronds (if they are fronds) are still curled from unfurling. The surfaces are glossy with new cuticle. And yet the scale is planetary. A contradiction: a thing both newborn and continental. It makes no sense in geological time. It makes no sense in biological time. But here it is.

And here, threaded through the darkness, is the other impossibility.

The machines.

They are not built structures — the parameter was clear, and it holds: there are no buildings, no artifacts, no constructed things in the sense of human making. But there is technology. It is woven into the plant-body like a vascular system made of metal and current. You glimpse it only when you are close: conduits of something that is not sap running alongside the true veins, surfaces that are too smooth, too reflective, too patterned. There are sounds, barely audible — a hum at the lower threshold of hearing, a click like a relay switching somewhere deep in the tissue, a whine that rises and falls with the slow breath of the air. Once, your hand brushes a surface and you feel vibration: a steady, industrial oscillation, the heartbeat of a factory that is also a forest.

It is all one thing. The plants do not host the machines; the machines do not parasitize the plants. They are a single integrated system, purpose-built, purpose-grown. Photosynthesis and photovoltaics have merged into some third process. The leaves are collectors, the stems are conduits, the roots are processors, and somewhere in the darkness something is being manufactured — though what, and for whom, and why, are questions that dissolve in the face of the place itself.

It never changes. That is the part that settles into you after a time, the part that begins to unnerve. The warm wet air does not shift in temperature. The weeping does not quicken or slow. The machines do not cycle, do not start up or wind down. The vast breathing is perfectly regular, a sinusoid without variation. There is no day, no night, no season. No gradient. No direction. The density is uniform — everywhere, the same interpenetration of leaf and stem and conduit, the same near-touching layering, the same saturated presence. You could walk for days (there are no days) and find nothing different. The strangeness is not that the place is alien. The strangeness is that it is so perfectly, so completely, itself.

No animals. No birdsong, no insect whine, no distant call. The only sounds are the machines and the breath and the drip of water condensing on water. Silence, except when it isn't. And when it isn't, the sounds are so quiet, so continuous, that they become a kind of silence themselves.

You stop walking. The plant-body holds you, slightly buoyant, slightly giving. In the darkness, your own boundaries begin to feel uncertain. The warmth is your warmth. The moisture is your moisture. The slow breath moves through you as much as around you. You are not sure if you are standing in the landscape or if the landscape is standing in you.

You understand, finally, what the place is.

It is a factory that is also a world. It is a newborn thing the size of a continent. It is a plant that has become industry, an industry that has become a plant. It has no name, no history, no future. It simply is, and it has always just begun to be, and it will never be anything other than it is right now, in this moment that does not move.

The darkness does not lift. The breath does not quicken. The machines hum their sub-audible hum.

Somewhere far above, a leaf the size of a city adjusts its angle, and a single drop of warm water falls for a very long time before it joins the general wetness.

Nothing happens. Nothing will happen. The place is complete.

You are still there.
