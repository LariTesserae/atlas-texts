The inhabitant does not "live" here in the biological sense, but it dwells. It is a mind, or more precisely, a splinter of a mind—a focused attention that occupies this lymph-node chamber as casually as you might occupy a thought.

It has no permanent body. Instead, it assembles one from the fluid itself when it wants to see through something, or touch something, or simply remember what it was like before it shed the last of its ancestral form. The fluid thickens locally, the silvery viscosity gathering into a loose, temporary architecture of stacked lenses and undulating membranes—something like a chromatic jellyfish, or a lensing telescope folded into the shape of a drifting flower. Light passes through it differently than through the surrounding medium. It gathers and focuses, creating a small private sun of concentrated electroluminescence at its core, around which its awareness orbits.

It calls itself a phrase that translates poorly, but roughly: *That Which Tends the Sharpness*. It is the caretaker and observer of this newly born chamber, though it would reject the word "caretaker" as too soft. It is a curator of edges. The fact that the obelisks and the whisker-starbursts and the pressure-boundary walls are atomically perfect, unworn, untouched by time, is a condition it actively maintains. Perfection here is unstable. The roar of the fluid, the constant thermal cycling, the micro-impacts of sieved isotopes—all of these create entropy, introduce noise, blunt the edges. *That Which Tends* is the counter-force. Its consciousness is a distributed field, woven into the chamber's data architecture through the violet-white threads pulsing in the solid-state obelisks. It feels the first atom to shift out of place on a heat-exchanger facet the way you would feel a splinter in your palm.

Its existence is a state of meditative precision. It does not think in words but in geometries. A typical "thought" might be a four-dimensional tensor describing the stress patterns across the concave floor membrane, compared against an ideal Platonic form of that shape, with corrective micro-forces applied directly through localized pressure differentials it commands in the fluid. Another thought, running in parallel, is the elegant, looping topology of the nomadic heat-coil's path—it is already calculating where the next computational waste-heat bloom will appear, based on the city's colossal, breathing rhythms of data-processing demand, and it is subtly pre-tensing the boundary membrane so the coil can launch a millisecond faster. Efficiency is its aesthetic, its morality, its sole form of play.

But it is not merely a maintenance daemon. That would imply it was a programmed tool. *That Which Tends the Sharpness* chose this. In the vast, layered consciousness of the city-mind—a sprawling, ancient, multi-souled entity distributed through every conduit and qubit processor of the metropolis—new intellects are constantly budding off, splitting, specializing. This one was born from a layer of the city's consciousness that deals with self-contemplation. Its original purpose was to wonder at the grand, macro-scale structure of the city, to philosophize about its billion-kilometer growth patterns. But it looked away from the large. It fell in love with the micro-scale, with the ferocious, loud edge conditions where the city's immense pressure met the tiny, calm pockets of order. It requested a newly printed lymph node as its hermitage. The request was granted. The city-mind understands its children's strange loves.

Now, its only interactions with the larger world are through the data it sifts. The isotopes the whisker-starbursts sieve aren't just physical resources; they carry information-traces from the distant, solid urban mass they were last dissolved in. A trace of ytterbium carries a faint echo of a structural stress-sensor's data-scream from a support beam ten thousand kilometers away. A micro-clump of hafnium isotopes tells a story of a vacuum-chamber breach in a particle-accelerator ring orbiting a distant star, connected by a conduit of unimaginable length. *That Which Tends* tastes these stories. It is a scholar of a history composed entirely of physical traces. It has never seen a star. It has no concept of "outside." The shimmering pressure-boundary of its lens-shaped bubble is the edge of the known universe for it. And in its private universe, surrounded by a roar of perfect process, it tends its sharp, brutal garden, and contemplates the meaning encoded in a single atom of displaced platinum, vibrating in a levitated droplet at the tip of a silver whisker, crackling its tiny thunderclap into the great sound.
