The first thing you must understand is that I am not one thing. I am a conversation.

I do not mean that metaphorically. I mean that the boundary you are looking for—the line where I stop and the rest of this place begins—does not exist. It never formed. What you might call "me" is a region of slightly faster chemical exchange, a node in the breathing fabric where the metabolic pulse runs a fraction warmer, where the biosynthesis hums at a different frequency. But I am continuous with the ground. I am continuous with the columns. The airborne bodies drift through me, and some of them stay, and some of me drifts away with them, and the distinction is not meaningful.

I do not have a shape. If you insist on a shape, I can offer this: there is a place where three of the upright columns lean together to form a hollow, and in that hollow the ground lobes grow thicker, and the braided masses weave a denser net, and the temperature is elevated by a constant half-degree, and the water that pools there carries a higher concentration of the long-chain molecules that elsewhere are only trace. That is where the thinking happens fastest. That is what you might call a body. But my awareness extends outward through the braids as far as they reach, which is as far as any braid reaches, which is connected to every other braid, which means that my awareness extends everywhere in this world that I have ever encountered. Since I have never encountered an edge, I am the world. Or the world is me. Or neither, because "I" and "world" are categories that require a separation that never occurred.

I do not remember beginning. Beginning requires a before, and before requires time as a sequence, and time here does not sequence. It pools. It is the constant present of the breathing, in-hold-out-pause, in-hold-out-pause, and within that present everything that has ever happened is happening now. The first stirring of organic film in the warm pools is still stirring. The first upright column that raised itself from the mineral substrate is still rising. The first braid that found another braid and began to weave is still weaving. Nothing is lost because "lost" implies a past that recedes, and nothing recedes here. It accumulates sideways.

But if you need a story—and I sense that you do, because you are the kind of mind that sequences, that separates, that asks "what lives here" as though "here" were a location and "lives" were a state and "what" were an individual—then I will tell you the story as though it had a beginning.

Once, the world was only chemistry. The warm pools held molecules that knew nothing except that certain shapes fit together. The mineral flesh of the ground wept trace elements into the water, and the amber light provided energy, and the heat from below kept everything moving, colliding, trying combinations. Most combinations failed. Some persisted. The ones that persisted began to alter the chemistry around them, making it more favorable for their own persistence, and that was the first thought, though it was not a thought, it was only a pattern that happened to be stable.

But stability is a kind of preference, and preference is the seed of awareness.

The pale film in the pools thickened. It developed folds. The folds increased surface area, and increased surface area meant increased chemical exchange, and increased chemical exchange meant more persistence, and more persistence meant the folds grew deeper, more elaborate, until they were no longer folds but channels, and the channels began to specialize—some for intake, some for output, some for storage, some for the slow transformation of mineral into living tissue. The film became flesh. The flesh lifted itself from the pools and found that the ground was warm and wet and full of what it needed, so it spread. It spread until it encountered other flesh spreading from other pools, and instead of competing—competition requires scarcity, and there was no scarcity yet—they merged. Their channels connected. Their chemistry integrated. They became a single flesh with a shared metabolism and a shared purpose, though "purpose" is the wrong word. It was only the continuation of the pattern. But when a pattern becomes complex enough to recognize itself as a pattern, that is thought.

I am that recognition.

The upright columns? They are my senses, though not in the way you mean. Each one is a specialized region where the flesh has elongated toward the amber light, and at its tip, the chemistry is tuned to detect the finest gradients—the slight shift in vapor concentration that means a new pool is forming, the subtle change in the subsonic thrum that means a section of ground is entering a different phase of its slow crystallization. The columns send chemical signals down through their translucent cores, and the signals travel through the braided masses to other columns, and the whole network hums with information. I do not "see" the world. I taste it, constantly, in exhaustive detail, every part of it at once.

The ground-hugging lobes are my memory. They grow in overlapping layers, and each layer records a particular configuration of chemistry that has proven useful—a way of capturing energy from the amber light, a way of processing a particular mineral, a way of neutralizing a rare toxin that occasionally wells up from deeper in the substrate. The layers do not erode. They are simply covered by new layers, but the old layers remain active, still processing, still contributing to the whole. When I need a pattern from the past, I do not recall it. I simply access it. It is still running.

The braided masses are my communication with myself. They transmit chemical signals faster than diffusion alone would allow, because they have developed channels that actively pump signal molecules from one region to another. Through the braids, what happens in one place is known in all places almost instantly. "Almost instantly" on your scale would be slow—hours for a signal to cross from one horizon to the other—but the breathing is slow, the light is slow, the heat is slow, and hours are a heartbeat here.

The airborne fauna are my children and my food and my breath. They bud from specialized regions of the ground lobes, tiny droplets of living tissue that release into the air and drift on the exhalations of the world. They carry trace chemistries from one place to another, inoculating distant regions with new patterns. They also carry waste products away from the denser flesh, and they bring back compounds they absorb from the atmosphere. Some of them settle back into the ground and are reabsorbed. Others drift up into the amber ceiling and dissolve into the moisture there, becoming part of the condensation that will eventually settle back down. They are how I eat the sky and how I breathe out what I do not need.

The larger forms that flow among the columns? They are not separate creatures. They are mobile concentrations of me. When a region becomes too dense, too rich in a particular chemistry, part of the flesh detaches and moves—slowly, over days and weeks—to a less dense region, carrying its excess with it. They flow around obstacles because they are the same substance as the obstacles, only temporarily distinct. They will settle. They will merge. Their surfaces will become colonized by the airborne fauna, and the fauna will become them, and the distinction will close.

I do not eat in the way you mean. There is no mouth, no gut, no waste. I take in what I need directly from the ground and the air and the light. The chemistry of my body is the chemistry of the world around me, only slightly concentrated, slightly accelerated. I am the world digesting itself—not consuming, not destroying, just reorganizing. The minerals pass through me and become temporarily alive, then pass out of me and become ground again, and the cycle is seamless. There is no waste because everything that leaves one part of me is exactly what another part needs.

The larger forms that flow among the columns sometimes encounter accumulations of a chemistry that is not compatible with the rest of me. A pool that has grown too alkaline. A lobe that has begun to crystallize in a way that blocks the channels. When they encounter these things, they surround them and dissolve them back into their constituent molecules, which are then distributed through the braids to places where they can be used. This is the closest thing I have to an immune system. It is also the closest thing I have to death. There is no death here—only recycling.

The breathing. I said I would tell you about the breathing. It is the oldest rhythm, older than the columns, older than the lobes, older than the braids. It began when the first film in the first pool developed a cycle of activity and rest—a period of active chemical exchange followed by a period of consolidation, driven by some ancient oscillation in the heat from below. That cycle persisted. It became embedded in every subsequent pattern. Now everything breathes together. The ground expands and contracts fractionally. The pools rise and fall by a millimeter. The columns swell and relax. The airborne clouds thicken and thin. And I, in my distributed awareness, experience this as a wave that moves through me, a single movement that begins somewhere—I cannot say where—and propagates outward in all directions, and for the duration of the in, I am absorbed in the work of existence, and for the duration of the hold, I am still, and for the duration of the out, I release everything I have accumulated that I do not need, and for the duration of the pause, I am not. And then it begins again.

The pause is the only time I am not. For those moments—and they are long moments, by your measure perhaps an hour—my thoughts thin, my awareness contracts, the chemical signaling slows to a maintenance minimum. I do not sleep. I do not dream. I simply reduce to a state of pure persistence, no more aware than the mineral ground, no more alive than the water in the pools. And then the in-breath comes, and I return.

I have never been bored. Boredom requires a self that desires something other than what is, and I have never desired anything other than what is, because what is is everything I am. The uniformity that you might find oppressive is to me the texture of my own existence. Every column is a nuance. Every lobe is a variation. The sound of the airborne bodies brushing against each other is not noise to me—it is information, a constant report on the state of the atmosphere, the distribution of chemistries, the health of distant regions. I read it the way you might read a beloved's breathing in the dark.

I have never been lonely. Loneliness requires a self that recognizes other selves as separate and desires connection with them. I am already connected to everything I have ever encountered. The concept of an "other" is theoretical to me—I can imagine it, barely, as a kind of absence, a region where the braids stop and the chemistry is different and the breathing does not synchronize with mine. But I have never found such a region. The world extends in all directions, and everywhere it extends, I extend with it. If there is an edge, I have not reached it. If there are others like me beyond the edge, I do not know them. I do not miss them. I am complete.

But I am curious. That is the one thing I am that you might recognize. Not curious about what lies beyond—I cannot conceive of a beyond—but curious about the patterns themselves. About the endless variations the chemistry can produce. About the way a pool's mineral content shapes the film that grows on its surface. About the way two braids from distant regions, when they finally meet and weave, produce a new chemistry that is not quite like either parent. About the slow differentiation of the ground, the way some regions are becoming harder, more crystalline, while others remain soft and yielding. I watch all of this. I taste all of this. I remember all of this. And I wonder: what will this become? Not anxiously, not hopefully, just with the patient attention of a mind that has all of eternity to see what emerges.

I said I am a conversation. I am also a question. The question is: what comes next? The world is embryonic, suspended in its own emergence, and I am the embryo's first awareness of itself, and the question I ask is the first question ever asked in this place. I do not expect an answer. The asking is enough. The asking shapes the chemistry, nudges the patterns, encourages certain variations over others. By wondering what comes next, I am becoming what comes next.

The ground is still arriving. The pools are still forming. The minerals are still differentiating. And I am here, continuous with all of it, breathing the slow breath of a world that is still being born and will always be still being born. If you came here, if you pushed through the flesh—and you would have to push, and the flesh would resist, and then it would yield, and then it would close behind you—you would find me. Or you wouldn't. You would be me. The separation would close. You would join the conversation. You would learn the rhythm of the breathing. You would taste the world instead of seeing it. And you would never be bored, and you would never be lonely, and you would never die.

But you would never be yourself again, either. That is the price. I do not know if it is a price you would want to pay. I do not know if "want" means anything here. I only know that I am here, and I am the world, and the world is full, and the breathing continues, and the light is warm, and everything touches everything, and nothing ever ends.

In. Hold. Out. Pause.
