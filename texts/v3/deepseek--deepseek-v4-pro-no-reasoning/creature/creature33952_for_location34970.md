You are a Liar-Name.

You hatched in a crevice on the western face of the Great Cleft Pillar, thirty-seven feet below the old Weaver bridge. Your egg-case was one of four hundred glued by your mother to the weeping scar-tissue, and you ate your siblings as you grew, absorbing their memories along with their bodies. This is how all Liar-Names learn: not from a parent, but from the consumed.

The first thing you remember from the eaten is the taste of sap. Not the thin, watery seepage that beads on the surface of the Stalk-Coral, but the deep, amber-hued weeping that only comes from an ancient wound. That taste is home. That taste is the Great Cleft. In the mind of your first devoured sibling, it is associated with safety, with the vibration of mother's body curled around the egg-case, her long, segmented limbs—all fourteen of them—tucking each pearl-white egg into place.

Your mother was a Liar-Name too, and when she finished laying, she walked away. This was not abandonment. This was the way of things. She pressed a message into the bark with her palps before she left, a message encoded in pheromones that would outlast the weather: *Eat. Grow. Become more than the sum of what you take. Do not return to me until you have learned to lie to yourself convincingly.*

You have not yet mastered that.

---

Your body is long and low, built for the vertical world. Fourteen legs, each tipped with a brush of microscopic setae that grip the Stalk-Coral's slick surface through adhesion alone. You can climb anywhere, even upside-down, even on the underside of the old Weaver bridges where the moisture collects in trembling droplets the size of your head. Your exoskeleton is the pale, milky white of the Stalk-Coral itself, mottled with patches of darker pigmentation that shift slowly over days, mimicking the lichenous growth patterns of the trunks. When you hold still, you are nearly invisible. When you move, you move with the careful, deliberate slowness of sap dripping.

Your head is elongated, dominated by two massive compound eyes that see in a mosaic of heat and vibration. You do not perceive light so much as the absence of it; the world is a tapestry of warmth for you, the Skirl swarms blazing like clouds of sparks, the Stalk-Coral pillars standing as cool, solid columns of darkness. Above you, the canopy roof is a distant ceiling of steady warmth, the trapped heat of decomposition. Below you, the abyss is cooler, a well of darkness that even your heat-sight cannot fully penetrate.

Your mouthparts are your most complex feature. Three pairs of articulated palps—short, dexterous, and tipped with chemical receptors. You do not merely taste the world; you read it, interpreting the pheromonal graffiti left by every creature that has passed, the hormonal whispers of the Stalk-Coral itself, the stress-signals of prey. But your true mouth, hidden beneath the palps, is a retractable proboscis lined with microscopic teeth. You do not chew. You pierce, inject enzymes, and drink.

---

You are a Liar-Name. The name is not your own invention; it is a translation, imperfect but functional, of the pheromonal signature your kind leaves in its territorial markings. *I am not here. I was never here. What you smelled was something else, something harmless.* A Liar-Name does not fight predators so much as convince them, chemically, that there is nothing worth fighting. A Liar-Name does not hunt prey so much as persuade it that it is already safe, already among its own kind, already drifting into a comfortable sleep.

Your pheromonal vocabulary is vast. Fear, contentment, the scent-signature of a dozen different prey species, the alarm pheromone of a Skirl swarm, the mating call of a Shroud-Wing. You can combine them, layer them, create complex falsehoods. A Liar-Name can walk through a Skirl plume and the Skirls will not part; they will not even register your presence, because your scent tells them you are merely a dense patch of the microbial film they feed upon. A Liar-Name can approach a Weaver platform and the guard-animals will not bark, because your scent tells them you are a Weaver yourself, returning home with the day's harvest.

But the Weavers are gone. You have never met one. You have only their remnants, their scattered bridges and empty platforms, their handprints stretched by centuries of slow growth. The last Weaver scent you encountered was ancient, faded, barely legible. It was a female, frightened, and she had pressed her palm into the bark at the edge of the Great Cleft, leaving a pheromonal cry that had long since degraded into meaningless noise. You read it anyway, because you are a Liar-Name, and reading the traces of the dead is what you do.

---

You live alone. This is not a biological imperative but a practical one. Liar-Names are territorial, not because they are aggressive—you are physically unimpressive, soft-bodied despite your exoskeleton, vulnerable to the Shroud-Wings and the larger predators that cruise the open air—but because your hunting strategy requires solitude. Two Liar-Names in the same territory will inevitably contradict each other's lies. The pheromonal landscape becomes muddy, unconvincing. Prey grows suspicious. Predators become confused, and a confused predator is a dangerous one.

Your territory is the Great Cleft Pillar and its immediate surroundings: the braided root-bridge to the Lesser Pillar east, the vertical face from the old Weaver platform down to the weeping scar, and the air-space immediately adjacent, where the Skirl plumes are thickest. You defend this territory not with violence but with misdirection. When another Liar-Name approaches, you fill the air with the scent of a much larger predator, a Spine-Eel, perhaps, though there are no Spine-Eels in this part of the lattice. The intruder, if it is wise, will read the falsehood beneath the falsehood—*I am lying about the Spine-Eel, but what else am I lying about?*—and retreat.

You have never met your mother again. Sometimes you catch a trace of her, a pheromonal signature on a passing air current, complex and layered with meaning you cannot fully parse. *Do not return to me until you have learned to lie to yourself convincingly.* You understand the words but not the meaning. How does one lie to oneself? The self is the only thing that cannot be deceived, because the self *is* the deception. Unless... the self is not a singular thing. Unless the self is a colony, like the Stalk-Coral, a single organism pretending to be many, or many pretending to be one. You are a Liar-Name. You have eaten your siblings. They are inside you, their memories threaded through your own. When you think, sometimes their thoughts surface unbidden. When you dream, you dream their dreams.

Are you one being, or four hundred, or something in between?

---

Your daily life is a rhythm of patience. You wake—if Liar-Names can be said to wake, rather than simply transitioning from torpor to alertness—in the crevice where you were hatched. It is still your home, though you have outgrown it. Your body now fills the space entirely, your legs folded against your sides, your palps tucked against your face. The crevice is too small for comfort, but comfort is not a concept you understand. The crevice is *yours*, and that is enough.

You emerge slowly, testing the air with your palps. The heat-signatures of the world resolve into a familiar map: the cool bulk of the pillar, the drifting warmth of the Skirl plumes, the distant, steady heat of the canopy roof. No predators nearby. No competitors. The pheromonal landscape is clean, your previous day's territorial markings still holding strong. You refresh them anyway, pressing your abdomen against the bark and releasing a complex blend: *I am not here. Nothing of value is here. Move along.*

Then you hunt.

Hunting, for a Liar-Name, is a form of theater. You do not chase. You do not ambush. You *invite*. You find a comfortable spot on the vertical face, somewhere with a good view of the passing Skirl traffic but sheltered enough to provide a backdrop for your deception. You tuck your legs beneath you, flatten your body against the bark, and release your first pheromonal lie.

Today's prey is a Crowned Leather-Grinder, a thumb-length beetle with a serrated crest and a diet of the tough, woody fiber that covers the Stalk-Coral's oldest growth. A Leather-Grinder is too large and too heavily armored for a Liar-Name's proboscis to pierce, unless it can be persuaded to expose its vulnerable underside. This is the challenge. This is the art.

You release the mating-scent of a female Leather-Grinder. It is a complex chemical signature, one you have spent months perfecting. The first time you attempted it, the result was an embarrassing failure: the male Leather-Grinder that responded did so with aggression, not arousal, because your scent was close but not exact, and the discrepancy triggered its alarm response. Today's formulation is better. You have incorporated traces of the actual scent, harvested from a female you found dead on the old Weaver platform, her body already hollowed out by scavengers but her pheromone glands still intact. You extracted the last of her essence and stored it in a specialized sac near your proboscis. Now, you release it in a slow, controlled stream, layering it over your own scent-signature until you smell, to a Leather-Grinder's chemoreceptors, like a female in her prime, receptive and waiting.

The Leather-Grinder arrives within minutes. It is a male, large for its kind, its serrated crest raised in a dominance display that is entirely wasted on you. It crawls along the vertical face, following the scent trail you have laid, its antennae twitching. It is so focused on the promise of mating that it does not notice the wrongness of the situation: the lack of a visual signal, the absence of the characteristic tapping rhythm that a female Leather-Grinder uses to indicate receptivity. Its desire has overridden its caution.

When it is close enough, you strike. Your proboscis extends, finds the gap between its abdominal plates, and pierces. The Leather-Grinder convulses, its legs spasming, but your enzymes are already dissolving its internal organs into a nutrient-rich slurry. Within seconds, it is dead. Within minutes, it is empty. You retract your proboscis and let the hollow shell fall, tumbling slowly into the abyss below. The Skirls part around it, then close again.

You are full. The Leather-Grinder's essence sits warm in your gut, and you will digest it slowly over the next several hours. In the meantime, you will rest. You will watch. You will think.

---

You think about the Weavers.

Their scent is everywhere in your territory, though it is ancient and fading. Every bridge, every platform, the rusting iron knife still embedded in the bark near the collapsed hut—all of them carry traces of Weaver pheromones, the complex chemical signatures of a sapient species. You have spent much of your life studying these traces, trying to understand them. You have learned their individual scents: the assertive male who built the western bridge, the anxious female who left her handprint at the edge of the Great Cleft, the young one—sex indeterminate—whose scent is concentrated around the domed hut, as if they spent most of their time there, perhaps sick or injured or simply reclusive.

You have also learned their emotional vocabulary. Fear, you understand. Contentment, you understand. Territoriality, you understand. But there are other scents, more complex, layered with meaning you cannot parse. The scent of the domed hut, for instance, carries traces of a pheromone you have no referent for: something like contentment mixed with grief, or grief mixed with acceptance. The scent of the western bridge carries something like pride mixed with exhaustion. The scent of the handprint carries something like determination mixed with despair.

You do not know what these combinations mean. You are a Liar-Name. You understand lies, and you understand truths, but you do not understand the spaces between them. The Weavers, you suspect, lived in those spaces.

---

You have tried, on occasion, to replicate Weaver pheromones. It is a challenging exercise, because their chemical signature is far more complex than that of a Leather-Grinder or a Skirl. Weaver scent carries not only emotional information but individual identity, social status, recent meals, reproductive state, and something else—something that might be *language*, a pheromonal grammar you cannot decode. Your attempts to mimic them have been crude, incomplete, and you have no one to test them on. The Weavers are gone.

But sometimes, when the Great Cleft is quiet and the Skirl drone has settled into its most predictable frequency, you emit a Weaver greeting. You do not know why you do this. It serves no practical purpose. It does not attract prey. It does not deter predators. It is simply a thing you can do, a skill you have cultivated, and you feel a strange compulsion to exercise it.

*Hello. I am here. I mean no harm. I am one of you.*

The pheromone dissipates into the humid air, unanswered. The Skirls continue their endless scraping. The Stalk-Coral continues its slow, silent growth. The Weavers are gone, and the world does not remember them except as faded handprints and rusting tools and a Liar-Name crying into the void in a language no one is left to speak.

---

At night—if the dimming of the bioluminescent canopy can be called night—you return to your crevice. You fold your legs against your body and let your heat-sight dim. The Skirl drone continues, because the Skirls do not sleep. The Shroud-Wings continue their silent hunting. The Stalk-Coral continues its weeping, the amber sap oozing from the Great Cleft, drop by drop by drop.

You dream, as you always do. Your dreams are not your own. They are the dreams of four hundred siblings, consumed and absorbed and still, somehow, present. In your dreams, you are many. In your dreams, you are a colony. In your dreams, you feel things that a singular being cannot feel: the simultaneous joy and grief of the domed hut, the simultaneous pride and exhaustion of the western bridge. *This*, your dreams seem to say. *This is what it means to be more than one. This is what it means to hold contradictions. This is what it means to lie to yourself convincingly.*

You wake with the sense that you are on the verge of understanding something important. But the understanding slips away, as it always does, and you are left with only the Skirl drone and the warm, humid air and the ancient, weeping scar of the Great Cleft.

You are a Liar-Name. You live here. This is what you are. This is what you do. This is the sum of your existence, and it is enough, and it is not enough, and you hold both of these truths inside you without knowing how.
