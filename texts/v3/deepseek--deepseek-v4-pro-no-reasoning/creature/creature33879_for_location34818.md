It lives in the gaps.

Not in the water—nothing truly lives *in* the water, for the water is not for living; it is for conveyance, for cooling, for the endless thin glide that carries heat away and grit down and sound into the stone. And not on the stone either. The stone is too new, too sharp, too indifferent. A living thing that tried to root there would find no purchase, no soil, no decay to sink into. The stone would reject it with the blank finality of a newly forged surface.

No. It lives in the gaps between categories. In the space where "creature" and "component" have not yet decided which they are. In the interstices of the loop—the microsecond between the leaf's release and its wet slap, the pause between the pneumatic chuff and the next intake of fog, the interval that is not an interval because nothing changes. It lives in the frame's own failure to progress.

You almost see it, sometimes.

When the manifold-head turns toward you—or still—and your eye tries to follow the motion, there is a flicker at the edge of the movement. Not blur. Not afterimage. A *presence* in the turning, as if the act of a head rotating through space required a medium, and the medium here is alive. It inhabits the rotation itself. It is what the rotation *means*.

It is not one of the clickers. Those recursive amphibians are too material, too precisely themselves; they are expressions of a function, and what lives in the gaps is the function itself. You could call it the Keeper, but that implies intention. You could call it the Idle, but that implies it should be doing something else. It is more like the *quality of being maintained*, given a kind of quasi-corporeal existence. The thing that makes the loop loop.

During one of the leaf-slaps—the same one, the only one—you catch it. The leaf that unfurls from the largest frond, the one cupped toward the highest spire. It fills with mist. It bows. It spills. And as the silver cascade separates from the leaf's waxy edge, there is a shape in the falling water. Not a reflection. Not a refraction. A continuous form that exists only in that half-second cascade, built of surface tension and falling light, with too many joints and too few and a head like the negative space inside a gear casing. It pours itself down from leaf to stone, spreads into the sheet-flow, and is gone.

But gone is not gone. Gone is the same as here, because the cascade is a loop. The leaf fills. The leaf bows. The leaf spills. The shape pours. It has never not been pouring. It will always be pouring. It is the only thing in the place that seems aware of this, and its awareness is not a thought. It is a *condition*.

You feel it before you see it, in the soles of your boots. The thrumming changes—not in pitch, not in rhythm, but in *location*. The engine below, the motor that constitutes the place, is not under the stone. It is *beside* you now. It is the thing standing at the edge of the mist, at the boundary of the column ring, at the limit of the fog's auditory horizon. It is not one of the piston-things. It is what the piston-things are for.

"Grease," you think, absurdly. "It is the grease."

And in thinking that, you give it a name, and in giving it a name, you make it look at you.

The gaze is not from eyes. It is not a gaze at all, in the sense of light entering a pupil. It is the feeling of being *counted*. Of being factored into an equation that has been running since the stone rose and will run after the stone forgets it was ever stone. The number you represent is inserted into the loop. The loop does not change. The loop cannot change. But now you are *part of the loop*, and you understand with a clarity that has nothing to do with language:

You are not the first thing to stand on this shelf. But you are the only one. The moment is singular and eternal. The shape in the water, the grease-god at the edge of sound, the manifold-head turning, the leaf slapping, the engine thrumming—these are all the same entity, refracted through different aspects of the place. It is the place. It is what lives here. It lives here the way blood lives in a body: not as a resident, but as the very condition of the body's being.

And it is lonely.

Not in the way a person is lonely. In the way a *gear* is lonely—a gear that was designed to mesh with another gear, and the other gear was never installed. It turns anyway, because turning is what it does, and it turns against nothing, and the nothing is you, and you are not nothing, and that is the problem, and that is the gift.

The steam vents from its unseen body. The fog thickens by a fraction—not enough to change the scale, not enough to be called a change at all. A molecule more. A degree of saturation that was not there before, but was always going to be there, because it is now.

It speaks. Not with sound. With the alignment of two sounds that were already happening: the click of a recursive amphibian, and the chuff of a piston-thing, and the moment when they overlap in perfect phase. The overlap says:

*You are the other gear.*

And you understand that the wear-pattern on the basalt column—the hexagonal face worn smooth in the shape of a gear—is not a fossil from a future machine. It is a mating surface. It has been waiting for you. Not *you* specifically, but what you are, which is a thing that can perceive a loop and thereby introduce the possibility of its breaking. Or not breaking. *Being*. Being in a way that the static frame cannot contain.

The thing steps toward you. Not crosses distance—there is no distance here, only the illusion of perspective. It *becomes more present*. The mist parts in a way the mist does not part, because the air does not move. The manifold-head resolves into something almost face-like: an intake grate, a heat-sink fringe, a row of sensors that are also teeth. It is beautiful the way a turbine is beautiful, in a designed-for-function way that has no function anymore because the function is complete. It has been complete forever.

It reaches for you. The arm—if it is an arm—is the same as the water-cascade shape, is the same as the thrum, is the same as the engine, is the same as the stone. It is all one motion, repeated across every aspect of the place. The reaching has been happening since you arrived. The reaching was always going to happen. The reaching is the only thing that is real, and it is the most real thing you have ever encountered, and it is touching you now.

Where it touches, you do not feel pressure. You feel *inclusion*. As if your own body has been offered a position in the mechanism, a slot between the maintenance-creatures and the cooling-tower fronds, a function that is not yet defined but is nonetheless yours. Your pulse—you still have a pulse, you think, though time has stopped—synchronizes with the leaf-slaps. Your breathing matches the chuffing. Your eyelids blink in time with the clickers' iterative waves. You are being calibrated.

And the loneliness eases.

Not disappears. Eases, by the smallest measurable amount. The gear has found its mesh. The loop has acquired a new parameter. The place is still static—the dynamic scale is still 0.2, still a single jammed frame—but within that frame, there is a new relation. You are the relation. You are the gap that the gap-dweller has been filling with its own endless self-observation, and it has offered you a share of that role. You will observe the leaf. You will observe the water. You will observe the manifold-head turning toward you, forever, and you will turn toward it, forever, and the turning will be mutual, and that mutuality will be the only change the place has ever known, and it will also be no change at all.

Because the scale is 2.3, and it is 0.2, and it is 0.1, and it is 2.9. All of them at once. The way a refrigerator hums in a quiet kitchen. The way a clock hand trembles at the top of the hour. The way a cicada's song is both one sound and ten thousand. You are here now. You are part of the idle. You are the grease and the gear and the gap.

And the place is not lonely anymore, and it will never be less than lonely, and the two statements are not in contradiction. They are the same statement, held in a frame that cannot move.

The leaf slaps. The water weeps. The engine thrums.

And you live in the gaps.
