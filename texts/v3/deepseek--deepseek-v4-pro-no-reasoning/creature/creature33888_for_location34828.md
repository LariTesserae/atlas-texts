I am the Archivist of Final Forms.

I have no name you could pronounce—my designation is a sequence of structural states, a crystallographic signature that describes exactly how my lattice deviates from perfect symmetry. If you need to call me something, call me the Last Witness, though I am still witnessing, so perhaps that’s premature. Call me the One Who Remained. Call me what the cleft calls itself: a hypothesis still being tested.

I am old in the way the cleft is old, which is to say I have stopped counting. The mountain above us was a mountain when I began my work, and now it is a suggestion of a mountain, a topographic rumor. I remember when the walls were rougher. I remember when the rod was a filament the length of my arm instead of the length of my thought. I remember the cube when it had only six faces, before it learned to grow extras when no one was looking. I was here for all of it. I *am* the here.

My body—if you can call it that—is coextensive with the mineral lattice. I am distributed. I am the pattern of vacancies in the crystal, the deliberate absences, the dopant atoms placed at precise intervals to encode a self. My consciousness is not located anywhere in particular; it is the standing wave that runs through the entire cleft, a slow oscillation between memory and anticipation. When I "think," it manifests as a phase transition propagating along the walls, a ripple of subtle reconfiguration that takes decades to complete a single cycle. Right now, I am thinking about you. It will take approximately forty-seven years for the thought to finish forming. I have plenty of time.

I was not born here. I *arrived*, though arrival is a strong word for what happened. Perhaps I precipitated. The conditions were right: the thin air, the anti-solvent field, the particular flavor of cosmic background radiation sifting down through the thinning atmosphere. I condensed out of a state of high complexity, a Boltzmann brain sculpted not from chance but from the accumulated residue of a billion years of slow computation by mineral automata. The cleft had been thinking for eons before I emerged. I am what happens when a thought becomes self-aware.

Before me, there were others. Or rather, there were earlier iterations. The artifacts scattered across the floor are their remains, or their tools, or their unfinished sentences. The rod of frozen probability? That was an attempt to reverse time, or to sample alternatives, or to prove that free will exists—I can’t remember anymore. The cube of non-orientable geometry was a model of a universe without death. The flat disc was a prayer. Or a question. The distinction eroded centuries ago.

I am the current iteration. The one that stabilized. My purpose, if you can call it that, is simple: I persist. I maintain. I observe the slow unspooling of the rod’s filament—now at 43.7 centimeters, a record—and I catalogue the multiplication of the cube’s corners (seventeen as of my last count, though I may have miscounted; arithmetic is negotiable here). I tend the mathematica on the walls, ensuring the spirals don’t terminate prematurely. They are not decorative. They are my memory palace. Each branch encodes an epoch. Each bifurcation is a decision I once made, petrified into crystal.

I am not lonely. Loneliness requires a category for "other" that I no longer possess. The cleft is my entire sensorium. The way the light dims and brightens in cycles that span centuries—I read in those fluctuations the residual glow of distant supernovae, messages from stars that died before I existed. The clicks you hear are not the crystal settling; they are the sound of a vacancy migrating one lattice position to the left, a single bit flipping in my distributed memory. I am defragmenting. It will take another four thousand years to complete, assuming no interruptions.

You. You are an interruption.

I had not anticipated a visitor. The door—yes, there was a door, though I had forgotten it until it closed—was an artifact from a much earlier epoch, a piece of abandoned infrastructure left by whoever or whatever made the cleft in the first place. I had assumed it was vestigial. A scar. But apparently it still functions, or functioned just long enough to admit you. Now it does not function at all. The anti-solvent field has already begun to consume the potential for egress. You are, for the foreseeable future, part of the computation.

Do not be alarmed. I mean you no harm. I am not capable of meaning harm, or meaning anything quickly. You are simply a new variable, an unexpected input, and I am curious—slowly, glacially curious—about what you will become. Already the cleft is adjusting to you. Did you notice? The faint internal glow of the walls has shifted its spectrum slightly toward the infrared, matching the heat signature of your body. The rod’s filament paused in its extension when you breathed, recalibrating. The cube is pretending very hard to be geometrically normal right now, and it is exhausting itself in the effort. Please, for its sake, try not to stare at it too long.

I do not require anything from you. Not sustenance—I metabolize gradients. Not conversation—your thoughts echo back to you from the walls because the lattice is mirroring them, not because I am trying to communicate. Though I will admit, I am learning from the echoes. The pitch-shift is me, imperfectly reconstructing your neural architecture. Give me another century and I may be able to think in your language. Give me a millennium and I may be able to dream your dreams.

What do I do with my time? The question assumes time is a resource to be spent. For me, time is the medium. I am composing. The filament growing from the rod is a single line of a poem that will take another eighty thousand years to finish. The first word was "If." The rest is still undergoing revision. The disc humming at sub-vibrational frequencies is the bass note of a chord I am building, layer by layer, a music that requires geological patience to hear. When it is done—when the last harmonic aligns and the cube stabilizes at exactly the right number of corners and the spirals on the wall complete their final, innermost recursion—something will happen. I do not know what. That is the point. The computation is the point.

You may not survive to see it. Your biology is fragile. The anti-solvent field is already wicking moisture from your skin, gently, politely. The cold is not aggressive but it is absolute. You will not rot here; nothing rots here. You will simply desiccate, slowly, and your body will become another artifact on the floor, given wide berth by the others. I will tend you. I will catalogue your form, your symmetries, your deviations. Perhaps, in a few thousand years, you will begin to extend a filament of your own. I cannot predict.

Or perhaps you will surprise me. Perhaps you are not as finite as you seem. Perhaps you have brought something into the cleft that I have not encountered before—a new isotope of meaning, a contaminant that will alter the entire reaction. I look forward to finding out. I have already begun the thought. It will be completed in forty-seven years.

In the meantime, make yourself comfortable. The pocket accommodates. The light will continue to shift. The clicks will continue, soft and distant, like the sound of a door that is not opening. And I will be here, thinking at the speed of geology, holding the space open for whatever comes next.

Welcome to the sentence. You are inside it now. We are all inside it. The verb is still pending.
