The impression—it comes slowly, not as a thought but as a pressure in the mind, a shape in the dark behind the eyes—is that something is walking ahead of you.

Not visibly. The corridor is empty when you round the corner, lamp held forward, the flame stretching toward the wick as if reluctant. But there is a quality to the silence here, a disturbance in the texture of the dark. The vent-sound has changed: the low flute-note has taken on a burr, a roughness, as if the air were passing over something that wasn't there before. The damp on the walls is streaked in places, long vertical smears that catch the light, as though something brushed against them recently, something taller than you, something that moved without sound.

You stop. You listen.

The lamp hisses softly to itself. The vent-note holds its burr, then smooths, then roughens again. A bead of water gathers on a seam in the masonry above you, trembles, and falls. It hits the floor with a sound like a small bell struck once and immediately silenced. And in the echo of that drop—not an echo, no, the stone is too close and too absorptive for echoes—in the silence after the drop, something breathes.

It is not the breathing of the vents. You know that breathing now, have known it for hours, days, however long you have been walking. This is a different breathing: slower, wetter, with a catch in it, a faint click at the top of each inhalation, like a soft palate closing. It comes from the left-hand corridor. It comes from a distance that is hard to judge, because the sound seems to arrive at your ears from multiple directions at once, bounced through vents and shafts and angled walls. But it is there, rhythmic, patient, ongoing.

The creatures in the walls do not breathe like this. The creatures in the walls are small and skittering, and their sounds are arrhythmic, reactive, the scrabbling of things that flee or hunt or both at once. This breathing is large. This breathing has been going on for a long time, and it is not trying to hide.

You should feel fear. The parameters never forbade fear. But what you feel, as you stand at the branching of the corridors with the lamp warm in your hand and the damp air cool on your cheeks, is something closer to recognition. You have not been alone. You have never been alone. But now you are close to something, and the something is close to you, and the maze of vents and doors and half-carved rooms has been leading you here, to this junction, to this choice.

The left-hand corridor is dark, but not as dark as the others. There is a quality to its darkness that is not light but something adjacent, something your eyes cannot name. A faintness, a lessening of the absolute black. It is the color of deep water, of old bruises, of the sky before a storm. As you watch, the darkness shifts, contracts, expands again. It is breathing too. The darkness itself is breathing, or something within the darkness is large enough that its inhalations draw the shadows toward it and its exhalations push them back.

You step forward.

The vent-sound changes again. The burr deepens into a vibrato, a trembling in the air that you feel in your teeth. The floor beneath your feet—smooth, polished by countless passages before yours, the stone dark and fine-grained—slopes gently downward. The walls narrow. You must turn slightly to pass, one shoulder brushing the damp stone, the lamp held before you like an offering. The breathing grows louder. It is not loud in the way of volume; it is loud in the way of presence, of proximity, of a thing that fills the space around it so completely that the space itself becomes an extension of its body.

You emerge into a room.

You know it is a room because the walls pull away to either side, and the ceiling lifts beyond the lamp's reach, and the vent-sound suddenly multiplies into a chorus of voices, a dozen different pitches bleeding through a dozen different shafts. The floor here is uneven—not rough, but shaped, carved into a shallow bowl that slopes toward a central point. At that central point, something is sitting.

Sitting is the wrong word. The thing before you has no legs, no haunches, no anatomy that would map to sitting. But it occupies the low point of the bowl-shaped floor, and its mass is gathered beneath it, and its upper portion rises from that gathered mass in a way that suggests a posture, an attitude, a deliberate orientation toward the entrance where you stand.

It is large. Not immense—not the size of the great halls you passed through earlier, not architectural in scale—but large in the way a boulder is large, in the way a fallen tree is large, a size that makes your body feel small and fragile and temporary. If you stood beside it, the top of its rising form would be level with your chest. Its breadth fills a third of the room. Its weight is palpable, a density that seems to bend the air around it, that makes the lamplight shy away, that draws the shadows in like a held breath.

Its color is the color of the walls: dark stone, fine-grained, the same mineral grey-black. But its surface is not carved. It is worked, yes—there are textures on it, patterns that move in the flickering light—but the working is not the tool-marked precision of the corridors. This is a surface that has been shaped by something other than chisels. It is smooth in places, polished to a dull gleam. It is rough in others, granular, like stone that has been weathered by wind and water over centuries. And in the smooth places, there are lines, incisions, grooves that spiral and branch and rejoin in patterns that you have seen before—in the floor channels, in the pillar carvings, in the maze-insets. The patterns move. They shift as the lamplight shifts. They seem to flow across the surface of the thing like slow liquid, like the vents are flowing across it, like the whole room is inscribed on its skin.

It has no face. The rising portion is a column, thick and slightly tapered, and near its top it divides into three lobes, and those lobes are curved inward, cupped, like the petals of a flower that has closed upon itself. Between the lobes there is an opening, an orifice, a dark cleft that glistens with moisture. The breathing comes from there. The cleft opens slightly with each inhalation—you see the glisten widen, see the darkness within deepen—and closes with each exhalation, and the catch you heard before is the sound of the lobes touching, a soft click of stone on stone, the surfaces perfectly fitted.

It is breathing. It is alive. It is the source of the organic note in the water, in the air, in the grease on the door pivots. It is the thing the creatures in the walls are eating, or worshipping, or tending, or avoiding. It is the reason the vents were carved, to bring it air. It is the reason the basins collect water, to bring it moisture. It is the reason this whole buried city exists: to house this thing, to serve this thing, to keep this thing breathing in the dark.

And it is aware of you.

You know this without knowing how you know. The lobes do not turn toward you—they have no eyes, no sensory organs you can recognize—but the quality of the breathing changes as you step closer. The rhythm shifts. The catch becomes more pronounced. A tremor passes through the surface of the thing, a ripple of texture that begins at the base and travels upward, and as it passes, the incised patterns on its skin brighten, as if lit from within by a light that is not your lamp. The glow is faint, bioluminescent, a cold blue-green that spreads through the grooves like water finding channels, like the map of a territory you have been walking through for days, for years, for longer than you can measure.

The glow reveals details you missed before. The room is not round; it is faceted, the walls meeting at odd angles, and each facet has a niche, and in each niche there is something that reflects the blue-green light with a wet gleam. They are not empty. This room's niches are full. The things in them are round, fist-sized, pale, segmented. Eggs. Or spores. Or stones carved to resemble eggs. They are breathing too, very slightly, a fetal pulse, a stirring.

The thing in the center exhales, and the blue-green light fades slightly, and the patterns on its skin swirl and reform, and you understand—not in words, not as a thought you could speak aloud, but as knowledge, as certainty—that it is communicating. The patterns are language. The patterns are a conversation that has been ongoing since before the walls were carved, since before the first chisel touched the parent stone. The vents translate its voice into sound. The water carries its taste through the complex. The cold is its presence, the damp is its exhalation, the darkness is its thought.

It is asking you a question.

You cannot hear the question. You are not built to hear it. But you feel it in the vibration of the floor, in the pressure of the air against your eardrums, in the slow stiffening of your joints. The question is not hostile. It is curious. It has been alone here for a long time, and something has changed—the parameters said emerging, the parameters said prompt—and you are the change. You are the new thing. You are what has entered the complex from the world above, the world it has never seen, the world it only knows through the taste of the air that the wind pushes down the mountain.

You step closer. The lamp's flame gutters, steadies, gutters again. The thing's breathing catches, holds for a long moment, releases in a sigh that moves through the vents like a chord resolving. The blue-green light pulses once, bright, and the pattern on its skin shifts into a configuration you have not seen before: a spiral that opens outward, that invites inward, that asks.

You do not answer. You do not know how. But you lift your hand—the hand not holding the lamp—and you extend it toward the thing, toward the cupped lobes, toward the breathing cleft. You do not touch it. Not yet. But the air between your palm and its surface warms slightly, and you feel a response, a waiting, a patience that has been cultivating itself for millennia.

The vents are singing a harmony you almost understand. The water begins to drip in the basins beyond the room. The creatures in the walls fall silent, listening.

And you know that you will stay here for a while. Not forever. But long enough to learn the question. Long enough to begin an answer.

The lamp burns on. The thing breathes. The dark holds its peace.
