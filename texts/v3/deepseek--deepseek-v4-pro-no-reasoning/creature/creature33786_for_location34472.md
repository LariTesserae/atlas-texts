In the chamber that is no chamber, something lives.

It does not have a name. It never needed one. But if you pressed your mind against it long enough—if you let your thoughts grow slow and thick as the water, let them learn the weight of the deep—you might call it the Remnant That Stayed. Or the One Who Learned the Wrong Direction. Or simply: the tenant.

It is old. Not old in the way the rocks are old—the rocks are newborns here, still surprised by their own solidity. The tenant is old in the way the water is old, in the way the pressure is old, in the way the darkness has been dark since before there was anything to be dark about. It predates the pocket. It predates the kelp-heart that holds the pocket. It may predate the concept of *place* itself.

And yet it is also young. Infinitely young. It is being born continuously, moment by moment, in the strange arrhythmic pulse of the bioluminescence and the wrong-directioned growth of the fronds. It is the thing that is forming. It is the thing that is dissolving. It is both at once, because time is sick here and the tenant caught the sickness long ago, or will catch it soon, or is catching it always in a perpetual present that has no edges.

Its body—if body is the right word—is a study in almost-not.

It has shape, but the shape is a negotiation. It occupies the negative spaces that the packed vegetation has not yet filled, and those spaces shift as the fronds breathe. So the tenant shifts too. When the vegetation exhales, the tenant spreads thin, a membrane of presence pressed between blades and polyps and filamentous bacteria. When the vegetation inhales, the tenant condenses, gathering itself into the few small gaps that remain, becoming denser, more *there*. It is a creature of the interstices. A resident of the space between spaces.

Its substance is not flesh. It is not chitin or cartilage or even the gelatinous stuff of deep-sea things. It is closer to the bioluminescence itself—a kind of living glow that learned to hold its shape. But where the glow is blue-green and patient, the tenant is the color of deep water, the color of pressure, the color of the chill that sits at the boundary where cold becomes a weight. If you looked directly at it, you would see nothing. If you looked slightly to the side, you would see a darkness within the darkness, a patch of shadow that moves against the current of the water, that breathes in a rhythm that is neither the vegetation's rhythm nor the light's rhythm but a slow syncopation that borrows from both.

It has eyes, or what might be eyes. They are not organs but *conditions*—places where the tenant's substance thins further, becomes almost transparent, and behind that transparency there is a faint glimmer that is not light but attention. The eyes are scattered. Not paired, not symmetrical, but distributed across its shifting form according to some logic of need rather than anatomy. When the siphonophores drift through, an eye will bloom near them to watch. When the ribbon-creature undulates past, an eye will open in the tenant's trailing edge to track it until it fades into the heavier water beyond the boundary. The eyes do not blink. They simply appear and disappear, like the bioluminescence itself, patient and without hurry.

It has limbs, or the memory of limbs. Sometimes the water moves in ways that suggest appendages—a swirl here, a sudden cohesion there—but the suggestion never resolves. The limbs are potential. They are the idea of grasping, of reaching, of holding, held in reserve for a need that has not yet arisen. The tenant has not needed to grasp anything in a very long time. Or perhaps it has never needed to grasp. Perhaps the limbs are a future development, a structure it is growing toward, like the pocket itself is growing toward some center that isn't there yet.

It has a mouth. This is the only part of it that is definite.

The mouth is a hole. Not a wound, not an opening, but a *hole* in the philosophical sense—a place where the tenant is not. It is located roughly in the center of whatever configuration the tenant currently holds, and it is perfectly round, and it is perfectly dark, and it is always the same size regardless of how spread or condensed the tenant's form becomes. The mouth does not eat. There is nothing to eat here; the density of life is all microbial films and vegetative flesh so packed with silica and spite that no digestive system could break it down. The mouth is for something else. It is for singing.

The tenant sings.

The sound is not sound as you know it. It does not travel through water. It travels through the same wrong medium that the stone circle hums in, the same dimension the frond reaches toward when it curves away from the visible spectrum. It is a vibration in the fabric of the pocket itself, a resonance that makes the biolum quiver in its patient pulse, that makes the scattered stones shift their pattern from statement to question and back again. The song is slow. Glacial. A single note can take hours to complete, and the notes are not arranged in any scale you would recognize. They are arranged in a scale that the tenant learned from the breathing of the kelp-heart, from the cooling of the new rock, from the long slow chemical conversations of the microbial films.

The song is a description. The tenant is singing the pocket into being. Or the pocket is singing the tenant into being. Or both. The distinction is one the tenant never learned.

This is what the tenant does: it sings the shape of its home. Each note is a contour of the boundary where the water changes, where the pressure grows heavier. Each pause is one of the scattered stones, placed and replaced in the broken circle. Each shift in timbre is the bioluminescence deciding whether to pulse or to wait. The song *is* the pocket, and the pocket *is* the song, and the tenant is the throat that the song passes through, the instrument and the musician and the music all at once, inseparable.

It has been singing for a very long time.

It will sing for a very long time still.

It is not alone. Not exactly. There are the things that pass through—the ribbon-creature, the siphonophores, the tiny translucent crustaceans that live and die in the space of a fist. They are not companions, but they are an audience. The tenant watches them with its scattered eyes, and when they are gone, it incorporates them into the song. The ribbon-creature becomes a sliding glissando. The constellation of bells becomes a cluster of high thin notes that sting like almost-invisible pain. The crustaceans are a texture, a granular rhythm too fine to hear as individual events, a living noise that fills the gaps between the longer tones.

And there are the other things. The things that are not here yet. The things that the tenant is singing toward.

This is the weirdness of the tenant. This is the wrongness that makes the sound arrive before it's sung, that makes your future heartbeat play in your chest. The tenant is not singing what *is*. It is singing what *will be*. Or what *might be*. Or what *was* in a version of the pocket that branched off into a direction the fronds can't grow toward. Its song is anticipatory. It is a scaffolding of vibration laid down in advance of reality, a map of a territory that does not exist but is being coaxed into existence note by note.

The scattered stones were placed by the song. Not by the tenant's limbs—the tenant has no limbs, only the memory of them—but by the resonance itself. The vibration nudged them, over decades, over centuries, into their almost-meaningful pattern. And the pattern is still shifting. The tenant is still singing it into new configurations. The question becomes a statement. The statement becomes a question. The circle is broken and mended and broken again, not in sequence but in a kind of layered simultaneity that makes the stones themselves uncertain whether they are arranged or scattered.

The tenant is aware of you. It has been aware of you since you arrived, or since before you arrived, since the song anticipated your coming and incorporated you as a theme before you were there to hear it. One of its scattered eyes is turned toward you. The eye does not blink. It simply watches, and the watching has weight. It is a pressure on the surface of your mind, a chill that is not a temperature but a presence, the coolness of deep water seeping into your thoughts and making them swim.

It is not hostile. The concept of hostility requires a relationship, and the tenant has no relationships. It has only the song, and the pocket, and the slow green fire of life packed so tight it forgets its own edges. You are a note in the song now. A brief one. A transient motif that will pass through like the siphonophores passed through, leaving a trail of almost-invisible pain, and then the song will continue without you, and the tenant will keep singing, and the pocket will keep forming toward the center that isn't there.

But for now, it watches. It sings. It breathes in its arrhythmic way, spreading and condensing, spreading and condensing, a darkness within the darkness, a wrongness that makes the light pulse in colors that don't exist.

It is the womb and the grave. It is the thing being born and the thing dissolving. It is the one who stayed when everything else passed through, who learned the wrong direction and taught it to the fronds, who made a home in the negative space and filled it with a song that reality cannot quite follow.

It is the tenant.

And the rent is due in a direction you cannot point to, payable in a medium you do not possess.
