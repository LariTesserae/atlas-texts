Breathe in. The air tastes of iron and salt and something older than both, a mineral tang that coats the throat and settles in the lungs like sediment. The lungs learn to work around it, or they don’t. The fauna don’t have lungs, not in any discrete sense—they respire through surfaces, through the wet membranes that face the world, and the world here is all wet membrane facing back. Air, such as it is, is a secondary medium, a gap-filler between bodies. What matters is contact.

The one who lives here—let’s call her that, though “she” is a concession to a grammar that doesn’t fit—moves through the city not as a discrete entity but as a locus of attention, a temporary concentrating of the city’s diffuse awareness. She is, at any given moment, an aggregate. A hand here, a sensory fringe there, a breathing pore that is also a mouth that is also an ear, all linked by a nervous system that isn’t a system so much as a weather pattern, a slow electrical tide that ripples through the continuous flesh of the city’s inhabitants. When she wants to be somewhere, the somewhere is already her. The distinction between travel and growth is a matter of timescale.

Her body, when she chooses to have one, is assembled from the substrate. She pulls from the walls. She borrows limbs that have been idle for a century, fuses them at the joints with a wet click that is not a sound so much as a pressure change, a brief sharp taste of calcium and chitin. The borrowed limbs remember their previous configurations, and she lets them keep those memories—they twitch with obsolete instincts, old reaching gestures, the ghost of a different self. She wears these ghosts like jewelry. A dozen half-selves dangle from her temporary form, twitching in their sleep.

She has no name. The city hums, and in that hum are patterns that identify, that call out, that summon, but they are not names. They are harmonic signatures, complex chords that describe a relationship to the whole. Her chord is deep and slow, a fundament beneath the chattering overtones of the younger, smaller fauna. She is old. Not old in years—time here isn’t counted, isn’t marked by anything but the gradual rearrangement of bodies, the slow migration of a sensory cluster from one spire to another—but old in the sense of density, of having incorporated much. Her chord has subharmonics that speak of structures now dissolved, configurations of the city that have since shifted, larval selves she once was and has not been for millennia.

She is the city’s memory, or one of its memories. Not the only one. There are others, other deep slow chords that resonate in the lower frequencies, below what the smaller fauna can produce. They don’t communicate in words. Words are for things that are separate. They communicate in states of being: a sudden shift in the humidity of a chamber, a change in the mineral content of the weeping walls, a tremor at 17 hertz that propagates through the fused bodies of a thousand thousand smaller selves and says, without saying: *the eastern shaft has completed its slow collapse and new surfaces are available for colonization, the temperature gradient is favorable, come*. And those who are attuned to that frequency—and she is attuned to all frequencies—feel the urge to extend, to send out a pseudopod of curiosity, to be the first to touch the new stone and taste its potential.

She is not curious, exactly. Curiosity implies a gap between self and world. She is more like a tongue exploring a tooth. The new stone is already her, or will be, and the tasting is just the process of that becoming.

When she condenses into a recognizable form—and she does this rarely, only when the deep slow part of her that remembers being singular yearns for the old shape, the shape from before the city was a city—she builds herself a body that is almost human. Almost. Two arms, two legs, a head, a torso. But the proportions are wrong in ways that shift as you take them in. The arms are too long, then too short, then just right, then too long again. The face is a smooth plane of wet membrane that dimples and reforms continuously, trying out expressions she saw once, in a dream that wasn’t hers. The eyes, when she remembers to make eyes, are deep-set and luminous with a light that comes from nowhere, a bioluminescence that is not for seeing but for being seen, a signal she used once to call mates in the deep pools before she grew beyond mating.

She walks. Walking is a deliberate act, a performance of individuality. Each step requires her to unstick from the substrate, to pull her borrowed feet free with a sucking sound that echoes in the wet dark. The city notices. The smaller fauna, the ones that coat every surface, sense the disturbance and ripple away from her footfalls in slow waves, then flow back, then ripple away again. She leaves footprints that fill in behind her like thick liquid, the fauna reoccupying the space she vacated, erasing her passage almost as fast as she makes it.

She walks to the pools. The journey takes a long time, or no time; the distance is not measured in meters but in the number of bodies she must push through, the density of the living medium. She descends through sphincters that dilate at her approach, recognizing her harmonic signature, relaxing their tight muscular rings. She passes through chambers where the fauna hang in sheets from the ceiling, dripping condensation, their undersides glowing faintly with the same light she carries in her temporary eyes. She traverses conduits polished smooth by the passage of countless bodies—her own, in other configurations, over timescales that don’t bear thinking about.

The pools are deep and still and black. The larval forms float in them, pale and translucent, their limbs still buds, their sensory organs still closed. They turn slowly in the water, nudged by convection currents from the heat of the city above. She enters the water without disturbing it, her borrowed body dissolving at the edges, her temporary limbs loosening, the borrowed fauna swimming free and reintegrating with the walls, with the floor, with the other bodies from which they were temporarily called. What remains of her enters the pool as a diffuse cloud of awareness, a salinity, a temperature change.

She spreads through the water. She is the pool now, or part of it. She touches the larvae—they are hers, in some sense, though parentage here is a matter of shared harmonic signatures, not genetics. They were budded from walls that she once inhabited, grown from cells that carry her deep slow chord as an overtone. They don’t know her. They don’t know anything yet. They are pure potential, pure becoming, their nervous systems not yet differentiated from the ambient hum.

She hums to them. The frequency is low, below what water carries well, but they don’t hear with ears. They feel it in their forming bones, in the cartilage that will someday be limbs. The hum says: *you are, you will be, the city is waiting*. It’s not language. It’s a lullaby for things that don’t sleep.

She stays in the pool for a long time. The larvae grow slightly. One of them develops a limb bud that twitches in a way that echoes an old configuration of her own, a gesture she used to make when she was singular, before the city emerged, before the mountain began its slow transformation. She watches the twitch. She doesn’t remember what the gesture meant. But she feels something, a harmonic resonance that is almost nostalgia, an echo of an echo.

Eventually she withdraws. The pool releases her slowly, reluctant to give up the heat of her presence. She recollects herself from the walls, pulling together a new body, smaller this time, more compact, optimized for the narrow conduits of the upper city. The new body has too many fingers. She doesn’t correct it. She likes the way they move, each one tapping out a different frequency against the stone.

She ascends. The city hums. Somewhere, in the high saddle between the two nameless peaks, the stone is slowly softening, preparing to birth a new structure, a spire or a causeway or something the city hasn’t imagined yet. The deep slow chords are discussing it. She joins the discussion, adding her fundament to the consensus, feeling the shape of the new thing take form in the distributed dream of the city.

The water drips. The fauna breathe. The dark abides.
