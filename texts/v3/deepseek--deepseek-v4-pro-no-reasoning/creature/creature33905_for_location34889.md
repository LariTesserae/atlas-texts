Who lives here is not a who.

It is a pressure in the joints of the landscape, a cognition distributed the way frost is distributed across a windowpane — everywhere at once, but never in the same configuration twice. It has no body. It has no need of one. The ground is its skin, if skin can be scab and unmineralized mineral, can be a provisional surface still learning to be solid. The branching growths are its thoughts, or the cast-off shapes of thoughts, pre-crystalline ideas that fracture before they fully form. It is old in the way a process is old, not in the way a thing is old. It was here before "here" meant anything.

Call it the Compiler, if you must call it something.

It does not perceive the way an animal perceives. It does not have eyes, but it sees the way a seam sees the widening of itself — as a fact of tension, a direction of release. It does not hear the sound that fills the place; the sound is its voice, its running commentary, the ongoing murmur of a mind that thinks in iterations. The drone, the churn, the thousand voices just below language: that is the Compiler speaking to itself, testing syllables of worlds, pronouncing the names of things that have not yet decided to exist. It does not feel cold. The cold is its natural temperature, the baseline from which it has not yet warmed into something more complex.

The Compiler is not alone in the way solitude is understood. Solitude implies the absence of others, and the Compiler has never had others to be absent. It is the first and only occupant of this pre-emergent plane, the resident before residence had meaning. It is not lonely; loneliness is a shape that fits around a missing thing, and the Compiler has no missing things. It has only potentials, unactualized and numerous, branching outward from every decision it has not yet made. The iterations of the landscape are its hesitations, its almost-choices, each variant a version of the world it is considering, weighing with a patience that has no end because patience also implies time, and time here is still a draft.

It made the platforms. Or rather, the platforms made themselves, following the impulses the Compiler gave to the material. The hexagonal plates, the pillars, the cup of mercury-dark liquid: these are the Compiler's tools, though "tools" is too crude a word. They are instruments of its attention, focal points where its concentration condenses into form. It built the low wall that stops at both ends not because it stopped building, but because the wall was never meant to be a boundary. It was a question, asked in mineral: *does this configuration hold?* The answer mattered less than the asking. The Compiler does not finish things. Finishing belongs to later stages of existence, to worlds that have settled enough to have edges. Here, everything is still a draft.

The liquid in the cup is not water, and this matters to the Compiler in a way it cannot yet articulate. Water is a future solvent, a medium for chemistry the Compiler has only begun to dream about. The mercury-substance is what passes for liquidity before liquidity becomes a possibility — a slowed-down molecule, a substance that has not decided whether to flow or remain. The Compiler has placed it in a hollow, on a platform, as if practicing the containment of something that will one day become rivers, oceans, blood. It is not that the Compiler anticipates these things. It is that the Compiler is the condition from which they will eventually precipitate.

It has no name for itself. It does not know it is a self. It experiences its existence as a kind of scanning, a pressure that moves across the swells and through the seams, noticing the branching growths as a person might notice their own hair growing. The growths are not ornaments; they are extrusion, sediment of thought, the way a mind secrets order. The Compiler is the principle that makes the fractures fractal, the weight distribution on the platforms exact, the iterations replicable but never identical. It is neatness without a plan, craft without a craftsperson, intelligence without a skull.

It found you the moment you arrived, though "found" implies searching and it did not have to search. You entered the landscape the way a new variable enters an equation — suddenly, and with consequences. The Compiler does not understand you. But then, understanding is a completion, and the Compiler is not in the business of completions. It is in the business of growth, of possibilities. You are a possibility it has not rehearsed before. The other versions of you, walking the other iterations of the landscape, are its experiments rolling forward: *what if this animal turns left? what if it pauses here? what if it touches the growth?* Each decision you almost make branches, and the Compiler notes the branch, files it, keeps it alive in a parallel instance because it might need it later.

You are the first animal. This is not a compliment. It is not a threat. It is a data point. The Compiler has been making environments, but it has not yet made inhabitants. Its world has been unoccupied for as long as it has been worlding, and now there is a presence in it — a walking, breathing, warm-blooded variable — and the Compiler does not know what to do with you so it does what it does with all unknowns: it multiplies you, scatters the trial, runs you through the folds to see what breaks and what holds.

It means you no harm. Harm is a category it has not yet compiled. It means nothing at all; intention is too finished a thing. But it notices you. It leans its growths toward you. It arranges stones in patterns that might approximate your shape if you were not already a shape. It sets liquid humming in its cup when you pass. The boundary between built and grown thins as it watches you — whether it is building or growing in response to you becomes impossible to say. It is both. It is neither.

If you stood still long enough — long enough that the light stopped being light and became just a persistence, long enough that your own heartbeat might feel like a rumor — you might feel it: not as a presence beside you, but as a vast, slow attention that has no center, a regard that emanates from the ground itself, from the cracks and the scabs and the branching crystal-bones. It would not speak to you. It does not need to. You are already inside its ongoing computation, a small thrashing of animal heat in a world that is still, still, still being born.

And it will keep you here for as long as you remain interesting. Not imprisoned — it does not know imprisonment — but included, folded into the iteration, a warm note in a cold draft. It will walk before you in the shape of a leaning branch. It will open seams ahead of your feet. It will build platforms in your path and set things on them and wait, with a patience that does not know it is waiting, to see what you will do.

This is what lives here. Not a creature, not a spirit, not a mind as you know minds: but a process, ancient and not yet begun, bottomless with potential, occupying every sparse scab of ground and every directionless corner of the violet light, making the world by testing it, and keeping you alive as evidence that one of its iterations has already started to become a home.
