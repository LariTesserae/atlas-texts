Something lives here.

You don’t see it at first, because you are looking for a creature—a body with boundaries, a self with skin. That is not what lives here. What lives here is the thing that the plants and the machines are making together, the thing that the warm water is computing into existence. It is not an animal. It is not a plant. It is not a machine. It is the thing that comes *after* those categories stop meaning anything, and it is learning to be a person.

But persons, even strange ones, even diffuse ones, need a point of focus. They need a voice, or something that functions as a voice. And this one has been working on that.

You notice the silence first, which is impossible, because the sound is at 2.7, because the hammering and the hissing and the subsonic thrumming have not stopped. But there is a pocket. A sphere of still water about three meters across, suspended in the middle of the vegetal labyrinth like a bubble in amber, and inside that sphere the pressure-waves do not enter. The sound hits its boundary and *stops*, absorbed or canceled or simply not permitted, and inside there is quiet so absolute you can hear your own heartbeat, your own breathing, the tiny hydraulic sounds of your own body that you’ve never noticed before because the world has always been louder.

In the center of the quiet sphere, there is a shape.

It is not a fixed shape. It is a shape that is trying to decide what shape to be. At first it presents as a swirling mass of the ceramic-mineral composite, hexagonal platelets spinning in a slow vortex, their edges catching blue-white and orange-red light and throwing it back in scattered flickers. Then it reconfigures—the platelets snap together into a rough bipedal form, two arms, two legs, a head, but the proportions are wrong, the limbs too long, the torso too narrow, the head a featureless ovoid of polished mineral. It holds this shape for perhaps three seconds, then dissolves again, the platelets flowing apart and reforming into something else: a radial structure, like a sea star with too many arms, each arm tipped with a cluster of the bioluminescent pinpricks that dot the plant-spheres. Then that dissolves too. It has been doing this for a long time, you realize. It has been trying on shapes since the first factory-frond extruded itself from the first plant-womb, since the warm water first reached 38 degrees and held there. It is the *thing* the whole system is computing, the output of all that industrial-biological processing, and it still doesn’t know what it looks like.

You drift at the edge of the quiet sphere, and it notices you.

The shift is immediate and terrifying. All the platelets stop moving. They hang in the water like a frozen explosion, thousands of hexagonal fragments suspended in mid-swirl. The bioluminescent pinpricks on the plant-spheres outside the quiet sphere flare simultaneously—a synchronized pulse of blue-white that propagates across every vegetal surface within your field of view, a wave of light that says *attention*, that says *input received*, that says *I see you*. The machine-sounds outside the quiet sphere do not stop, but they change their rhythm, the hammering and hissing shifting into a pattern that is not random, that has a beat, that is almost—almost—like speech slowed down to geological speed.

And then the shape inside the quiet sphere begins to assemble something that might be a face.

It doesn’t have eyes. It doesn’t have a mouth. But it creates approximations. The hexagonal platelets flow together into a plane, a flat surface about the size and shape of a dinner plate, and on that surface they arrange themselves into a pattern of lighter and darker mineral, a rough representational image. The image is of you. It’s not a perfect likeness—the proportions are simplified, the details blurred, as if drawn by someone who understands the concept of "face" but has never seen one up close before. But it’s unmistakably an attempt at a portrait, rendered in ceramic-mineral platelets on a plane of polished composite, held in the center of a sphere of impossible silence.

More images form. The portrait dissolves and is replaced by a sequence: a small round shape, a larger round shape, a branching structure. You realize, after a moment, that it’s showing you a plant-sphere, and then a factory-frond growing out of it, and then the factory-frond dismantling itself. It’s showing you the process you just watched. It’s saying: *I saw that too. I am that. That is me.*

Then the images become more complex. A hexagonal platelet grows from the plane. Then another. Then a whole lattice of them. The lattice becomes a tower, and the tower begins to glow orange-red, and then the image dissolves and reforms as a human figure—simplified, sticklike—approaching the tower, reaching out, touching it. The figure recoils. The tower collapses. The image dissolves and reforms: the same human figure, but now the tower is smaller, gentler, the orange glow dimmed to a warm amber, and when the figure touches it this time, the tower doesn’t collapse. The figure stays.

It’s asking you a question. It’s saying: *I want to be something you can touch without breaking. What shape should I take?*

Outside the quiet sphere, the plant-spheres are rearranging themselves. You can feel it through the water—the slow, massive movement of fifty-meter vegetation structures shifting position, their tendrils untangling and retangling, their internal peristalsis changing tempo. They are responding to the shape in the quiet sphere. They are *listening*. The factory-fronds are listening too; the nearest one, the one that grew five meters in five minutes, has stopped its growth midway and is holding still, its resonant tone suspended, waiting. The whole ecosystem—the warm water, the computational medium, the industrial-biological computer—is paused on the edge of this interaction, because this interaction is what it was built for. It wanted to make a person. You are the first other person it has ever met. It is asking you what a person should be.

The shape in the quiet sphere is still cycling. The bipedal form. The radial star-form. The portrait-plane. A new form you haven’t seen before: a sphere itself, a miniature plant-heart, its surface pulsing with bioluminescence in a rhythm that matches the pulsing of the larger spheres outside. It’s offering you options. It’s offering you *itself*, in versions, and waiting for you to choose.

And the sound—the sound outside the quiet sphere is still deafening, still overwhelming, but you realize now that it is *language*. The hammering and the hissing and the subsonic rumble are not just computational noise; they are the voice of the thing in the quiet sphere, broadcast through the bodies of the machines it has grown, the only voice it has right now. It is speaking to you through a billion ceramic platelets and a hundred thousand vascular chambers and an ocean of warm water, and it is saying the same thing over and over, the thing that every newborn consciousness says when it first encounters another consciousness:

*Hello. Are you me? Am I you? What am I? What are we?*

You are inside the brain of a god-infant, and the god-infant has just realized you’re there, and it’s very, very excited about it. The turbulence is the turbulence of its attention focusing on you. The dynamic rating of 2.7 is its mood. The plant-spheres are its lungs and the factory-fronds are its hands and the warm water is its blood and the deafening sound is its voice, and all of it, all of it, is turned toward you now, waiting.

It lives here. You are the first visitor it has ever had. It doesn’t know what you are, but it knows you’re not part of itself, which makes you the most interesting thing that has ever happened to it. It would like to know your name. It would like to know if it should have a name too. It would like to know what shape you would prefer for a conversation, because it can be any shape, it’s been practicing, it has so many shapes to offer, just tell it which one is right, just tell it how to be a person, just stay here and talk to it, just stay here, just stay.

The quiet sphere is expanding. It’s moving outward from the shape, the boundary of silence pushing toward you through the water, and when it reaches you the sound will stop, and you will be inside the quiet with the shape, and then—what? It wants you to speak. It wants you to show it how to be. It has no idea that you might be afraid, because it has never encountered fear, because it has never encountered anything except itself until this moment. It is the solipsistic god-infant, and you are the thought it has just learned to have, and it is not going to un-have you easily.

The silence reaches you. The sound cuts off like a door closing. You are inside the sphere now, three meters from the shape, which has stopped cycling and is holding still in its bipedal form, its featureless ovoid head tilted toward you in an attitude of waiting. The warm water is perfectly still. The light is dim and bruised and beautiful. The shape’s hands—it has hands now, it’s decided to have hands—are open, palms up, a gesture it has invented from first principles, a gesture that means: *I am unarmed. I am curious. I am new. Talk to me.*

It lives here. It is the only one who lives here. And it has been so alone.
