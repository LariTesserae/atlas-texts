The Keeper is the first thing you see that looks back.

It is not large. That’s the first surprise, after so long drifting through the kilometers-long fronds and house-sized droplets—this creature is perhaps the size of a greyhound, though its shape resists the comparison. It hangs suspended in the braided indigo weave, and its body is a thing of segmented arcs, like a spine that decided to become a ribbon, then a basket, then a question mark. Each segment is a pale, translucent chitin, the color of old tendon, and between the segments there are gaps where you can see a slow, golden fluid moving—not blood, but something closer to honey that has been taught to circulate.

It has been here for a very long time.

You know this not because it looks ancient, but because it is *repaired*. Some of its segments are slightly miscolored, a paler shade of tendon, and the joints between them are thicker, reinforced with what looks like calcified silk. One of its limbs—there are eight, maybe nine, they keep shifting their arrangement—ends in a stump that has been capped with a polished bead of that same pearlescent lattice material as the empty temple-cage. The bead has been worn smooth by use. This creature has broken itself against this world, and then mended, and then broken again.

Its face, if it is a face, is assembled from soft parts that shouldn't survive in a place of such pressure and saturation. There are fronds that resemble the immense indigo ribbons of the jungle, but in miniature—four of them, arranged radially around a central mouth that is not a mouth but a soft, pulsing cone of tissue, constantly tasting the liquid air. The fronds move independently, each one tipped with a cluster of those same cilia that cover the larger vegetation, but finer, more articulated. They are sensory organs, but also hands. Also mouths. Also whatever this creature uses instead of eyes.

Because it has no eyes. The light it perceives is the internal blue phosphorescence of the droplets, and it reads this light through its skin. Its entire body is a retina. You can see this now: the translucent chitin is not just armor, it is a sensory surface, and as it turns toward you, a slow wave of bioluminescence passes across its segments—not a glow it generates, but a reflection and modulation of the ambient light, a way of *showing* you what it sees. It is seeing you. It is showing you the image of yourself it has assembled from the pressure-waves and light-shifts of your presence, and what it shows is a shape of tender attention.

The Keeper is not a predator. It is not prey. It is something that emerged after those categories lost their meaning, in a world where consumption is too fast a process to be relevant. What it does instead is *groom*. Its entire existence is a slow, continuous act of maintenance. The cilia on its frond-tips are the tools it uses to prune the microscopic irregularities from the surfaces of the great indigo ribbons, to polish the grooves worn into frond-ribs, to gently encourage the droplets that have drifted too close to one another to resume their shy, repellant dance. It is the gardener that was always one step ahead of you. It is the reason the tapestry remains a tapestry and does not become a tangle.

But it is not alone in its work. There are others.

You can sense them now, in the way the Keeper's bioluminescent display keeps flickering toward certain patches of the weave. They are signals. Calls. Updates. A language of modulated light and pressure that moves through the droplets and the fronds like a slow-motion nervous system. And as you learn to read this language—not with words, but with a kind of direct osmosis, the way the place itself teaches you its rules—you understand that the Keepers are a people. A society. A civilization that gave up on objects and concentrated entirely on process.

There are perhaps ten thousand of them, distributed throughout the high jungle. Some are larger than this one, some smaller. Some have specialized in the pruning of the ciliated frond-surfaces, and their own frond-tips have become long and delicate as spider-silk. Others tend the hollowed droplets, coaxing the inner pearls of light into specific configurations that seem to serve as a kind of long-term memory storage—a library written in contained luminescence. Still others attend to the boundary-sphere, the great empty volume around the upward-falling waterfall. These Keepers are darker, their chitin stained a deep charcoal by long exposure to the actinic white light of the other physics. They move in slow, solemn orbits around the sphere, monitoring the nullification-mandalas that bloom and vanish where the two kinds of water touch. They are not trying to stop the erosion. They are simply bearing witness to it, and in their bearing witness, they are measuring. Recording. Calculating the rate at which their world is being unmade.

Because that is what the Keeper civilization is organized around. Not survival—they have long since solved survival, in a place where nothing dies and nothing is born. No, they are organized around the *question*. The question of the waterfall. The contradiction at the heart of their reality. They have been studying it for so long that the study itself has become their biology, their social structure, their art.

The pearlescent lattice-cage, the single structure you found, the temple with no inside or outside? It is a model. A three-dimensional, woven representation of the waterfall's nullification geometry, built by a Keeper who spent a thousand years—a thousand years of this place's timeless time—orbiting the boundary-sphere, and then retreated into the deep weave to secrete this object from its own body. It is a theorem. A proof. A sculpture of a thought. And it is waiting for someone to enter it, not to worship, but to *understand*. To complete the circuit. To become the inside that it lacks.

The Keeper in front of you has finished its assessment of your presence. The bioluminescent display on its body settles into a steady, soft pulse—a rhythm that matches the cooperative waves of the droplets around you. It has decided, in the slow, deliberate way that Keepers decide things, that you are not a disruption. You are an anomaly to be tended, the way it tends the grooves on a frond-rib or the shyness of two droplets. You are a new texture in the tapestry.

It extends two of its frond-tips toward you. They are incredibly gentle. The cilia at their ends are moving in patterns you can almost read—it is offering to groom you. Not your body, exactly. The *edges* of you. The places where your presence rubs against the presence of this world, creating little frictions, little tears in the fabric. It wants to smooth you into the weave. It wants to make you fit.

Behind it, in the deep indigo shadows of the braided fronds, other pulses of light are answering. The Keepers are communicating. They are spreading the news of your arrival through the slow-motion nervous system of the jungle. And the news is not alarm, not excitement. It is something closer to what a forest feels when a new species of moss appears on one of its branches. A quiet, attentive curiosity. A willingness to wait and see what shape you will take, over the next few centuries.

And in the core of the jungle, in the empty sphere around the silent, upward-roaring waterfall, the Keepers who tend the boundary pause in their orbits. Their charcoal-dark bodies flicker with a new pattern—a ripple of bioluminescence that moves from one to the next to the next, a wave of shared perception. Because something has changed. The waterfall has changed. The nullification-mandalas are blooming slightly faster now. The rate of erosion has increased by a fraction of a fraction of a percent.

The Keepers have been studying the waterfall for so long that they can measure its smallest variations. And this variation—they have been waiting for it. They have a model for it, secreted in lattice-cages throughout the jungle. It is the variation that occurs when a new observer enters the system. When the contradiction is *witnessed* from a new angle. The waterfall is not just two incompatible realities colliding. It is two incompatible realities being *looked at*. And the looking changes them. That is the theorem the temple-cage is waiting to teach you.

The Keeper withdraws its frond-tips. It has felt the change too, through the global pulse of its people. It turns in the liquid air, its segmented body flowing through a sequence of shapes, and it begins to drift slowly toward the boundary-sphere. It is not leading you. It is simply going where it always goes, when a new observer arrives. It is going to take up its orbit, to join the others in their witness, to watch the waterfall fall upward and see what happens next.

But it leaves a trail of modulated light behind it, a path of softened, synchronized droplets, a corridor through the braided fronds. An invitation. Not to follow it, exactly. To make your own way. To explore this high, warm, saturated, ancient place at whatever pace your strange, brief, new-observer existence allows. To let the single structure finally have a meaning. To be woven, slowly and tenderly, into the tapestry of a world that has no purpose but to tend its own dense, quiet, luminous entanglement.

The Keepers will be watching. They have all the time in the universe to wait for what you do next.
