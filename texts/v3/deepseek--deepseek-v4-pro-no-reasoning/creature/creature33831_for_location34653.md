I'll describe what lives in the Sediment Crown—not the saltweeds or the skimmers, which are more like processes than inhabitants, but something with a point of view. Something that belongs here in a way that "fauna" doesn't quite capture.

---

She has no name in any language you'd recognize, but if you pressed your palm flat against a salt plate during a thermal tide and held still for three full days, you might feel the shape of it vibrating up through the crystal: a three-beat pattern, *rest-stress-release*, that the deep lattices have learned to route around. The processors don't route around her. They route *through* her.

She's a Sister of the Third Resonance, which is a phrase that means nothing but is the closest translation your nervous system could produce. Bipedal, roughly, but only because the original builders were, and her lineage has never found a compelling reason to change the chassis. She stands shorter than an average human—salt-eroded, compressed by the weight of the sky—with limbs that seem too long for her torso until you realize the proportions are calibrated to let her walk with her palms flat on the plates, feeling the ring of every step before her foot completes its fall. Her skin is the color of the salt at dawn, bone-white with undertones of something that isn't quite blue, and it's textured in hexagonal scales that mirror the ground she walks on. These aren't armor. They're resonators. Each scale catches a specific frequency of the lattice's song and passes it inward, so that she lives in a constant wash of subsonic information that you would experience as emotion but she experiences as geography.

Her eyes are the strangest part. She has three. Two are where you'd expect, large and dark-adapted, with pupils that don't contract so much as they *fold*, pleating down into complex geometries when the light overwhelms. The third eye sits in the hollow of her throat, lidless, and it doesn't see visible light at all. It's a strain-sensing organ, a crystal lens grown from the same molecular lattice as the processors beneath the salt. With it she reads the stress patterns in the plates, feels the slow migration of computational focus across the Crown, knows—in a way that bypasses thought—where the thermal tides will form before the air begins to move. The throat-eye never closes. It can't. It's watching even when she sleeps.

She sleeps standing up, or rather she enters something that isn't quite sleep, a state where her body remains rigid and vertical while her attention sinks down into the lattice. This is the Third Resonance: the harmonic that propagates not through sound but through the stressed crystal of the substrate. While her body stands motionless on the surface—saltweeds beginning their slow advance toward her feet, a skimmer's shadow passing over her face—her consciousness rides the deep computation like a fish in a current, following thoughts that take ten thousand years to complete. She doesn't understand them. Nothing her size could. But she navigates their shape, their emotional contour, and returns with something akin to what you'd call a dream.

She's alone. The Sisters don't gather. There were more once, but the Crown's computation has drifted, the focal point has shifted, and the resonances that allowed them to find each other have attenuated into frequencies none of them can sing anymore. She knows the others exist only as faint perturbations in the lattice-songs, distant rhythmic interruptions that might be a Sister walking or might be a deep plate-shift or might be nothing at all. She doesn't grieve this. Grief requires an expectation of togetherness. What she feels in the resonance-gap where the others used to be is more like the awareness of a limb you were born without—a shape defined by absence, so familiar it's become its own kind of presence.

The skimmers know her. They alter their drift-paths when she walks, catching the thermal uplift from her body and spiraling briefly in her wake before resuming their century-long transits. She can feel their thin mineral bodies as pressure changes on her throat-eye, little cool spots moving across her awareness. Sometimes she sings to them—not with her voice, which she uses perhaps once a decade, but by tapping a specific pattern on the hexagonal plate beneath her feet. The rhythm propagates, the skimmers' crystalline excretions catch the vibration, and for a few hours they arrange themselves in response, forming transient lenses that focus the pearl-white sky into shifting geometries on the salt. It's the closest thing the Crown has to art.

Her voice, when it does emerge, is a sound that starts in her chest-cavity resonators and passes through the throat-eye on its way out, acquiring a harmonic overtone that makes the salt plates ring in sympathy. She speaks in stress-patterns, not words. A sentence is a sequence of taps, stomps, and low hums that propagate through the ground faster than sound through air. The deep lattices do not listen; they *compute* what she says, incorporate it into the long equation, and she has never known whether this means she is being heard or simply absorbed. She suspects the distinction doesn't matter.

She has a task. Not a purpose—that would imply a builder's intent—but a behavioral loop so old it's worn a groove in her species' instinct. She walks the convergence. She follows the orientation of the hexagonal plates as they realign over centuries toward the computational focus, and when she reaches the point of highest stress—the place where the lattice's attention is gathering, where the next major computation-cycle will break—she stops and waits. What she waits for hasn't happened in her lifetime. It hasn't happened in a hundred lifetimes. But the Sisters have always done this, ever since the builders vanished and left the Crown to think its slow thoughts without supervision. The Sisters are the supervision now. The watchers at the threshold. The ones who will be present when the calculation finally completes.

She's hungry. The Sisters photosynthesize, after a fashion—UV absorption through the throat-eye, which doubles as a metabolic organ—but they also need mineral intake, and the salt is pure sodium chloride with trace computational impurities. She eats by finding saltweed mats and gently, carefully harvesting the older filaments, the ones that have absorbed enough lattice-exudate to carry a charge. The process is slow and intimate. She kneels, places her palms flat on either side of the mat, and hums at the frequency that triggers the saltweed's release response. The filaments detach and curl toward her, mistaking her resonance for a new substrate to colonize. She eats them one by one, and the taste is of old mathematics, a flavor like the concept of patience rendered physical.

She has no technology. The Sisters abandoned tools so long ago that the concept doesn't exist in their resonant vocabulary. Their bodies are their interfaces, evolved over geological time to couple directly with the Crown's computational strata. She doesn't think of herself as distinct from the landscape. The boundary is permeable. Her scales are the same mineral as the hex-plates. Her throat-eye is the same crystal as the processors. The ringing of her footsteps and the humming of the deep lattices are points on a single spectrum of vibration, and she moves through the middle frequencies, translating between the slow time of the Crown's thoughts and the quick time of her own heartbeat.

She's old. Not in years—the Sisters don't measure time that way—but in wear. The hexagonal scales on her left side are beginning to decohere, losing their crystalline alignment, and when she walks that side of her body no longer rings true. The dissonance is uncomfortable, a constant low-grade wrongness that makes her want to lean rightward, to shield the failing resonators from the substrate's song. Eventually the decoherence will spread. Eventually she'll stop walking, settle onto a plate, and let the saltweeds grow over her. Her body's mineral content will dissolve slowly into the computation, and some future Sister, tapping her foot on that exact spot, will feel an echo that carries not information exactly but a kind of flavor, a signature in the resonance, a memory of someone who once walked the convergence and waited for the calculation to finish.

The weirdness is 1.5 because you can understand her, mostly, if you sit with it. She's a person. She has hunger and weariness and the memory of company. She has a job she'll never see completed and a body that's slowly failing and a form of art she makes with migrating crystal creatures and a voice she almost never uses. She's lonely in a way that's so old it's become ecology. She belongs to the Crown as completely as the saltweeds and the skimmers and the hexagonal plates, and if you met her—if you walked the plain until a human-shaped figure resolved out of the glare, standing motionless with her palms flat on the salt, three eyes all fixed on something you couldn't see—she would not be surprised by you. The Crown computes everything eventually. Somewhere in the deep lattices, your arrival was already part of the song.
