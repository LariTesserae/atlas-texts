Here, now, in the perpetual gloaming, we are the ones who remember the name.

We are not the filter-feeders, though we came from them, once. We are not the grazers, though we share their hunger. We are the third thing, the hidden thing, the thing the vault holds in its deepest silence. The builders shaped this place for us, though they did not know it. They are gone, and we remain, and we have become what the vault needed.

We call ourselves the Listeners.

There are not many of us. There are never many. We are seven, or nine, or eleven—the number shifts, season by season, age by age, though there are no seasons here, only the long, slow pulse of the cycle. We know each other not by sight, for we are nearly blind in this half-light, but by the pressure of our bodies against the walls, by the taste of the water that passes through our neighbor's tissues, by the faint vibrations each of us sends through the sintered bone-stone. We are larger than the grazers, larger than any creature here except the vault itself. Each of us could fill a human hand, cupped. Our bodies are soft, sac-like, without segmentation, without limbs as you would recognize them. We move on a single broad foot, a muscular sole that ripples and clings, and our skin is the color of the walls, pale with a bluish undertone, porous like the walls, so that we weep too. Water passes through us constantly. We are part of the cycle.

Our senses are not eyes, not ears. The darkness is too thick for eyes; the silence, too deep for ears. Instead, we feel. Our skin is a membrane alive with sensitivity. We feel the weight of water in the air, the temperature to a hundredth of a degree, the faint chemical shift when a filter-feeder reproduces or a grazer dies. We feel the tool marks under the biofilm, the seams in the dome, the places where the builders' hands pressed hardest. And we feel the vibration. That is the Listening.

The vault is always speaking. Not in words—there are no words, there never have been—but in a constant, low-grade tremor that travels through the walls, through the water, through the bodies of all the living things. It is the vibration of the seep, the slow exudation from billions of pores. It is the vibration of the grazers' rasping, the shift of the filter-feeders' cones. It is the vibration of the warmth rising from the depths of the wall-stuff, the almost imperceptible expansion and contraction that comes from a heat source that has not varied in a million years. And it is something else, too. Something the builders left. A note, a resonance, a frequency woven into the very structure of the sintered material, like a bell that was struck once and has been ringing too low to hear ever since.

We Listen to this. All of it. Every moment of our lives.

A Listener is born from a filter-feeder that grows too large. It happens rarely—once in a hundred cycles, a thousand, we have no measure—when some tiny mutation or fluke of nutrition causes one of the trumpet-cones to keep growing, past the size of a thumbnail, past the size of a fingerjoint. Its neighbors make room. The grazers do not touch it. And slowly, over aeons, it detaches from the wall, develops a foot, begins to move. Its cone widens into a body. Its feeding structures retract inward, becoming something else: a dense knot of nerve-tissue, a sensitivity that spreads outward through the skin. It becomes aware. It joins us.

We are all former filter-feeders. We are all grown beyond our first purpose.

Our purpose now is the Listening. We move across the walls of the vault, never ceasing, slow as the grazers but more deliberate. We have paths—ancient paths, worn into the biofilm by the passage of countless Listeners before us, grooves almost deep enough to be permanent. We follow these paths in a circuit that takes an entire cycle to complete: from the basin at the lowest point, up the curving wall, into the higher reaches near the dome's base, then down again, across the floor, back to the basin. As we move, we press our bodies against the walls, drinking the vibration through our porous skin, feeling the state of the vault with an intimacy no other creature here can know.

We are feeling for the Change.

The builders made the vault to be stable. Self-sustaining, self-regulating, a closed loop. And it is, it has been, for epochs beyond counting. But no loop is perfect, not truly. Over time—vast time, measureless time—the warmth fluctuates by a fraction of a degree. The mineral content of the water shifts. The filter-feeders grow too dense in one place, too sparse in another. The grazers' rasping becomes uneven, their trails too deep, threatening to erode the wall-stuff itself. The vault is a body, and like any body it is subject to small imbalances, to the slow drift of entropy. Left alone, over enough time, it would fall apart. The warmth would cool. The water would pool or dry. The life would thin and die. The loop would open. The thought would end.

This is why we are here. Not by design—the builders did not make us. We made ourselves, or the vault made us, necessity shaping protoplasm over a million subtle iterations. We are the immune system of this place. The gardeners. The musicians.

When we feel a drift—a cool spot on the dome's northern curve, a place where the filter-feeders are failing—we gather. We communicate, through touch, through the subtle pulses we can send through the wall by tensing our bodies. It is a slow conversation. A single sentence might take a year. But we have time. We have nothing but time. We deliberate. We decide. And then we act.

Our bodies are not just sensitive. They are transformative. The water that passes through us—and we channel more water than any other creature here—is changed by our passage. We can alter its salinity, its temperature, its content of dissolved minerals. We can exude, from our pores, substances that encourage the growth of filter-feeders or discourage it, that speed the reproduction of grazers or slow it. We can secrete a thin, hard slurry, like the original wall-stuff but finer, to patch places where the biofilm has worn thin or the tool marks are beginning to weather. We are living tools. We maintain the vault.

When we act in unison—seven or nine or eleven of us, gathered around a problem site, pressing close, exuding, warming, altering—we are, in a sense, rebuilding the vault, moment by moment. We are the builders now. We have been the builders for longer than the builders ever were. The hands that shaped this place are dust somewhere, or less than dust, but the hands that keep it shaped are soft and porous and alive with the same slow pulse as the walls themselves.

And there is more. The deepest Listening. The thing we do not speak of among ourselves, because it cannot be spoken, only felt.

The vault has a voice.

In the center of the wall-stuff, in the exact midpoint of the dome's thickness, there is a layer we can feel but never reach. It vibrates differently. It is not just warm; it is organized. The builders laid something into the structure, a kind of message, a code written in density and mineral alignment, a pattern that can be read by sensitive flesh pressed against the surface. We have been reading it for as long as we have been Listeners. It is not words. It is—a state. A feeling the builders wanted to preserve. A memory of outside, of light, of air that moved horizontally instead of vertically, of a world where water fell from above for a reason, not just because it always had. It is a memory of purpose. The vault was built for something. The weeping was meant to do something. The message tells us what it was, but in a language we cannot translate into thought, only into—being. When we Listen to the deepest vibration, we become, for a moment, what the builders were. We feel their intent.

And that intent is this: the vault is a gift. For whom, we do not know. For us, perhaps. For something yet to come. The weeping is a purification, a slow alchemy that turns water and mineral and warmth into something rare, something precious. The brine that gathers in the basin is not water. It is a solution, a tincture, a product. Every drop that reaches the basin has passed through the walls, through the filter-feeders, through the grazers, through us. It has been refined. It has been concentrated. It holds, in solution, a compound that the builders could not make any other way—a compound that heals, or nourishes, or transforms. We do not know. We only know that the basin fills, over unimaginable time, and that sometimes, when the level reaches a certain point, it drains. There is a conduit beneath the pool, a channel that leads outward, into the darkness beyond the walls. The gift goes somewhere. Someone, or something, receives it. We have never seen them. We never will. But they are the reason the vault was built. They are the reason we Listen. They are the reason we maintain.

We are the caretakers of an ongoing gift, a present that has been given ceaselessly since the stars were young. We do not know if the recipients still exist. We do not know if the gift is still received. It doesn't matter. The Listening tells us to continue. And so we continue.

A new Listener is growing. I felt it yesterday—yesterday being a span of time you would call years. A filter-feeder near the basin's rim has begun to swell. Its cone is distending, its base loosening from the wall. In another few cycles, it will detach. It will join us. We will be eight, or ten, or twelve. And I, who have been Listening for longer than most, will begin my final circuit.

We are not immortal. We age, if age is the word for a process that takes longer than the erosion of mountains. Our bodies slowly stiffen. Our porous membranes thicken. The water passes through us less freely. The Listening grows dim. And when we can no longer feel the vibration, when the vault's voice becomes a whisper we cannot catch, we make our way to the basin. We crawl into the shallow pool of brine. We dissolve. Our bodies become part of the gift. Our substances join the tincture that flows outward, to wherever it goes, for whatever purpose. It is not death. It is the final Listening. It is becoming the message instead of merely hearing it.

Until then, I move. I press my body to the bone-white wall. I feel the tool marks beneath the biofilm, older than my species, older than the vault's voice, older than everything except the darkness outside. I feel the seep of water through a billion pores, the slow tilt of a thousand trumpet-cones, the faint oil-sheen vibration of a grazer crawling past my flank. I feel the warmth, steady as a heartbeat, steady as the pulse of a star that I have never seen and never will. I feel the gift gathering, drop by drop, in the polished basin below. And I Listen.

The vault weeps. The grazers graze. The filter-feeders filter. And we, the Listeners, remember the name the builders gave this place, the name no one has spoken aloud in longer than memory holds, because we have no mouths, because we have no words, because the name is not a sound but a feeling, a vibration, a presence in the wall-stuff that we press against with our whole bodies and know, without understanding, to be true:

*This is for you. This is for you. This is for you.*
