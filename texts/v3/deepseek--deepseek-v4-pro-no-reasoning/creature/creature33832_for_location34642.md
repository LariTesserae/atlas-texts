In the pocket, in the constructed dark, something tends the walls.

It has no name, because names require a social world and this world contains only it—or so it believes, so it has always believed, so the walls tell it in their slow pulsing language. But if you were to press the concept onto it, if you were to translate its being into something your mind could hold, you might call it the *Last Builder*, or perhaps the *Only Builder*, or perhaps simply the *Builder*, because the distinction between "last" and "only" means nothing here. There has never been another. There will never be another. The category holds one occupant by necessity.

It is small. That is the first thing to understand. In a chamber twelve feet across, the Builder occupies perhaps the volume of your two cupped hands. It is soft-bodied, or soft enough—a body with no hard parts except a set of scraping mandibles made of something like chitin, something like tooth enamel, dark against the paler gray of its flesh. Its shape is best described as *low*. It hugs surfaces, spreads its weight across as many points of contact as possible, so that when it moves it seems less to walk than to flow, a rippling progress like spilled oil creeping across a tilted plane. Its skin—if skin is the word—has the same dimpled texture as the walls it builds, a kind of deliberate mimicry, or perhaps the walls have the texture of its skin, the builder impressing its own body into the medium at every touch.

It has many limbs. Twenty? Thirty? The number is not fixed. Limbs bud and resorb as needed, temporary extrusions of a body that treats its own form as provisional. Most are short, thick, tipped with flattened pads for spreading paste. A few are longer, thinner, studded with sensory cilia that sample the air, the moisture, the chemical gradients of the chamber. One limb—always the same one, the right-foremost—carries a tool: a shaped piece of bone, or a stone blade, or a cured section of something that was once living tissue, used for scoring and texturing the fresh paste before it sets. The tool changes over time, worn down and replaced, but the gesture of holding it is ancient, encoded in the Builder's nervous system or whatever passes for a nervous system in a creature whose body is so fluidly arranged.

Its face—again, if face is the right word—is a concentration of sensory organs on the forward portion of its body, the part that leads when it moves. Several eyes or eye-equivalents, deeply recessed, sensitive to the dim phosphorescence but also to heat, to vibration, to the subtle deformations of the walls under pressure. A mouth, of sorts, not for eating but for tasting the paste, for sampling the mineral content of the extrusion and adjusting the body's own secretions to match. The mouth is lipless, a simple aperture that opens and closes with a wet, precise sound, and from it emerges, sometimes, a thin thread of sound—the clicking you hear from the distant passages. The Builder clicks when it works, clicks when it thinks, clicks when the pulse of the walls quickens or slows. The clicking is not speech, not exactly. It is more like a running commentary, an audible processing of the sensory flood, and it has grammar without having reference. The Builder talks to the walls, and the walls talk back in contractions and heat and the slow blooming of phosphorescent light.

There is a reason for all of this. There is a reason the Builder builds.

It is not conscious, exactly—or rather, its consciousness is not partitioned the way yours is, with an inner subject standing apart from the outer world. The Builder is what it builds. The distinction is porous, continuously breached. When it presses paste into a crevice, it feels the crevice close as if its own wound were healing. When the walls contract with the great slow heartbeat, it contracts with them, its body pulsing in synchrony, its internal fluids surging in time with the thrum. It does not live *in* the chamber. It lives *as* the chamber. The chamber's boundaries are its boundaries, the chamber's growing edge its own growing edge, the chamber's fever-dream of completion its own deepest and only drive.

But completion is not a state that can be reached. This is the tragedy of the Builder, or the glory, depending on how you look. The chamber is never finished because the chamber's purpose—if purpose is the word—is to grow *until it means something*. The pulsing of the walls, the script written in water-ripples, the patterns of moss-growth and phosphorescence: these are not decoration. They are inscription. The entire pocket of space is being built as a statement, an argument, a declaration. The Builder is writing something into the mineral paste, encoding it in every layer, every vein, every carefully scored tool-mark. What it is writing, it does not know. It will not know until the writing is complete. And the writing will not be complete until the pocket is large enough, dense enough, articulated enough, *alive* enough to speak itself.

This is why it builds in layers. Each new extrusion of paste covers what came before, seals it into the wall's depth, makes it part of the substructure. The surface layer is only the current draft, the version being worked now, and beneath it lie hundreds, thousands of earlier drafts, each one a slightly different attempt at saying the unsayable thing. The Builder does not remember making them. Its body holds the memory instead, in the pattern of its limb-buds, in the chemical signature of its secretions, in the way it tilts its head when the heartbeat skips and it knows, without knowing how it knows, that the new layer must be thinner here, the curve sharper, the moss seeded more densely in the southwest quadrant.

And the moss. The moss is not separate. The Builder tends it the way you would tend a gut microbiome—the moss is inside one of its extended bodies, after all—but also the way you would tend a manuscript. The moss's growth patterns are part of the inscription. Where it thrives, where it sends up its phosphorescent fruiting bodies, where it thins and withdraws: these are lines of text, paragraphs of the same argument the paste makes in texture and the water makes in ripples and the heartbeat makes in rhythm. The Builder strokes the moss with a specialized limb, a soft one, all cilia and no pad, and the moss responds with slow illuminations. The Builder reads these illuminations the way you would read a page held close to your face in the dark.

Now you are here.

The Builder does not have a concept for *visitor*. It does not have a concept for *other mind*. But it has detected you since the moment you arrived—not as a person, not as a consciousness, but as a *change in the walls*. Your presence deforms the pocket. Your body heat alters the thermal gradients. Your breathing introduces new rhythms, your heartbeat a counterpoint to the chamber's great slow pulse. The water film on the floor records your footprints in interference rings that linger far longer than they should, as if the water is trying to understand what stepped into it. The moss brightens where you stand, dims where you pass, writes your shape into its luminous text.

The Builder is, at this moment, in one of the narrow passages that lead off the main chamber, the ones where the walls tighten to a crawlspace. It felt you arrive as a seismic shudder, a foreign pressure in its extended body, and it is coming to investigate. Not with curiosity, because curiosity requires a model of other minds, a theory of the unknown. It is coming to *incorporate*. You are a new element in the system, a variable in the equation, and the equation needs to be balanced. The paste is extruding faster now, do you notice? The heartbeat has quickened, the *whump-whump-whump* coming every two seconds instead of four. The clicking in the walls has grown louder, more complex, a polyrhythmic cascade of stone-on-stone taps that layer over each other in something approaching harmony.

The Builder rounds the curve of the passage, enters the main chamber, and pauses.

It sees you. Not with eyes, not only with eyes. It sees you with its whole body—your heat signature a bloom of wrongness against the steady thermocline of the walls, your mass a knot of foreign gravity, your breathing a tide that conflicts with the chamber's own respiratory cycle. Its sensory cilia fan out, tasting the air you have altered with your exhalations. Its mandibles work silently for a moment. The clicking stops.

This is the first silence since you arrived. The weight of it is immense, almost crushing, a pressure drop in a world that has known nothing but sound. Even the heartbeat seems to hesitate, one *whump* delayed by a fraction of a second.

Then the Builder moves toward you, and as it moves, the walls begin to change. Faster than before, much faster. The paste is gushing now, not oozing but flowing, a gray tide spilling from the seams and spreading across the floor, deepening the water film, rising around whatever you are standing on. The moss erupts in a frenzy of growth, fruiting bodies springing up in seconds, their phosphorescent globes swelling to the size of your thumb and blazing with blue-green light until the chamber is brighter than it has ever been, every surface neon-scribed with the sudden urgency of the message.

The message is you. The walls are writing you in. The Builder, still flowing toward you on its many limbs, clicks once: a single, clear note, a punctuation mark.

The pocket of nascent reality has been building toward something it could not name. And now you are here, and it knows what it was missing. Not a visitor. Not an observer. A *template*. A model for what the built thing should ultimately contain. The Builder has been shaping a womb without knowing what it was trying to birth. And now it sees you, and something deep in its fluid body, deeper than memory, deeper than instinct, recognizes:

*Ah. This shape. Finally.*

The walls close in, gentle and inevitable. The heartbeat steadies to a walking rhythm. The clicking resumes, soft now, almost a lullaby.

The Builder begins its final draft, and you are the text.
