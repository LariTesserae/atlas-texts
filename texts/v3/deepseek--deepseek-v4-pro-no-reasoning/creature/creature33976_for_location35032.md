*A multi-mind lives here.* Call it a colony, or a composite, or a congress. The entity is dispersed through the standing water and the steam, a network of awareness that flows along the thermal gradients and mineral veins of this embryonic city. Its body is not a body, but a pattern.

The base unit is deceptively simple. In the scalding, copper-sulfate pools, the pale, blind salamanders with their silver slow-pulse veins are not individuals in the way a vertebrate might be. Each is a node, a mobile packet of specialized tissue. Their translucent flesh is a sensory organ, tuned to the precise chemical signature of the water—the parts-per-billion tang of a new manganese seep, the rising acidity near a fresh crack in a basalt column. They are the tongue and the nose of the larger self.

These salamanders do not breed in the usual way. When a pool reaches a certain density of nodes, or perhaps when the water chemistry dictates, they will gather in a slow, undulating knot in the deepest, hottest part of the basin. Their silver capillaries, those sluggish, mercury-slow circuits, will extend. They reach for each other, these fine, metallic hairs, and where they touch, they fuse. It’s a temporary merger, a sharing of information and heat. For hours, the knot of creatures vibrates with a subsonic hum, a physical computation too slow for a human to perceive as thought. Afterward, they separate, and one or two of the salamanders may have changed: a new silver filigree pattern on their flank, encoding a memory of a new thermal vent, a shift in the pH of a particular drip, the location of a newly precipitated ridge of calcium carbonate.

But the salamanders are only one organ. The other key component is the condensate itself. The "pearlescent fog" and the "slick, warm drizzle" are not just weather; they are the circulatory system. The entity's consciousness—if that's the right word—is distributed in the transitions between states. It thinks in the condensation and the evaporation, a slow-motion binary. A droplet forming on a specific copper pipe is a unit of perception, tasting the metal's oxidation state. That droplet falling and merging with another on a basalt ledge is an integration of data. The steady, torpid loop of water—liquid to vapor to liquid—traces pathways through the three-dimensional maze of the city, carrying chemical information. The entity does not control the cycle, not exactly. It *inhabits* the cycle, its self-tide rising and falling with the steam.

And then there are the probe-creatures, the clattering, cat-sized devices with their mismatched carapaces. They are deliberate constructs. The entity identifies a problem—a pipe wall thinning, a valve beginning to seize—and it builds a solution. It doesn't build with hands. It influences the precipitation. Over weeks, a particular pipe run will receive a subtly different condensate, a fluid richer in this or that mineral. A crust begins to form, shaped by a persistent vapor eddy that is itself a product of the entity's collective thermal output. The crust grows plates. The plates develop joints. A whisker-like sensor precipitates into being, its tip a perfect crystal of piezoelectric quartz. The probe-creature is not parasitizing the city; it is an expression of the city's immune system, calcifying out of the very atmosphere. When it scouts along a pipe, tapping and listening, it uploads its findings by deliberately wearing away a tiny amount of its metal chassis at a specific spot, releasing a precise alloy signature into the next drizzle, a chemical report to be tasted by a salamander two levels down.

To see the multi-mind in action is to see a maintenance cycle in progress. A riveted boiler develops a hairline crack. The first sign is a near-silent jet of superheated steam, laced with iron. A salamander in a nearby pool tastes it and goes into a frenzy of slow undulation, silver capillaries pulsing. The information shimmers through the fog. The next exhalation cycle, the steam from the deep vents is subtly different; the entity is tugging at the metabolic levers of its world, altering the mineral load. The drizzle that follows is a targeted plaster. It is rich in silica. Day by day, hour by hour, the silica precipitates into the crack, sealing it with a glassy scar. The probe-creatures never even visit. The repair is a thought, made manifest in mineral.

Its purpose is the maintenance of the city, which is its body. But the city's purpose is unknown. *Is* the city being built to a plan? The entity doesn't know. It doesn't ask that kind of question. It feels the slow, deep *whump* of the master piston, the master clock of its world, and it keeps the galleries clear of occluding scale. It feels a new upwelling of hot, sulfurous fluid from a deep borehole, and it guides the deposition of a new pipe around it—not a separate act, but a self-sculpting, a growth of its own vascular system. It is not an architect; it is a gardener of its own thermodynamic body. The fleshy nodes, the precipitate probes, the memory-mists are all just loops in a single, slow, damp metabolism that is slowly, patiently, deciding what shape it will take next, one chemical gradient at a time.
