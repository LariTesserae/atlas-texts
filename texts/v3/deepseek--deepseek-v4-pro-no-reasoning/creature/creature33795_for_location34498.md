It is not inhabited.

It is *worked*. That is the word that comes to you, even as the dense, cool fronds close around your shoulders, and the heavy mineral air presses against your chest. This place is not a home; it is a task. A long, patient, unending task.

The thing that lives here lives inside the task. It is not a creature with eyes or limbs, though it was perhaps, once, something akin to one. Now it is the process itself. The single contiguous organism—the forest of metallic spirals—is not the inhabitant, exactly. It is the *body* of the inhabitant. The inhabitant is the awareness that holds the shape of every frond, the exact strand of memory—if memory can be stored in the lattice of a crystalline matrix—that remembers the shape of the mountain’s flaw, the precise temperature at which the non-aqueous geochemistry will yield its cold harvest. The engines are not separate from this awareness any more than your teeth are separate from your hunger. They are organs. The low, insistent industrial thrum is not merely sound; it is the pulse of an enormous, slow, buried metabolism.

But something walks the polished pathways. It is not animal. It is a shape that trouble stirs into being when the machinery dips, for a nanosecond, below its long-held equilibrium. Not a guardian, not a keeper. A *consequence*. It moves along the faint, polished loops around the squat, tarnished engines with the deliberate, approachless motion of a shadow cast by a light that has never existed. It has no face, yet it has the weight of attention. It is the shape of the one who first set the pipes into the stone, who taught the fronds to curl in that frozen fractal, eons ago, and then left. It is not the builder. It is the builder’s *absence*, made palpable by the fact that nothing else in this place is absent. Everything else is so overwhelmingly present. So the absence is almost unbearable, and it takes form. It is a tall, narrow pressure in the air, no more than a man’s height, no less than a god’s patience. It walks the path in an endless, silent circuit, and the fronds bend away from it just enough to let it pass, not out of fear, but because in the perfect, time-invariant archive of this place, the absence must have a shape to be correctly remembered.

It is the only thing here that does not hum. It is the only thing here that is not part of the single, sealed loop. It is the negative space in the absolute packed volume, the one hollow that the grotto cannot fill.

And at the center of the chamber, on the low plinth, there is a mark. Not carved, not burned. *Grown*. A tiny, precise spiral, identical to the great fronds, but no larger than a thumbnail, formed of a metal that seems to drink the sourceless light. It is not an altar. It is not a key. It is the first line of an instruction, unspooled over ten thousand years, from a builder who went away but who is, in the only way that matters here, still *working*.

There is no water. There is no beast. There is no outside. There is only the deep, steady thrum, the unthinkable patience of the metallic ferns, and the thing that walks the path of its own absence.

That is who lives here.
