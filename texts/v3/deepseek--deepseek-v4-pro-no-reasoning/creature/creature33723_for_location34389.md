I have been walking for a long time. Longer than the body remembers. The salt plates flex underfoot and the subsonic click travels up the shin, through the patella, into the iliac crest, where it becomes something like a thought: *you are here, you are here, you are here.*

The city does not approach. It condenses.

The first time I see the inhabitants clearly is when one of the pale fruiting bodies detaches from a spiral tower and descends—not falling, as if gravity were only a suggestion, but lowering through the crystal like a sediment settling upward. It passes through a wall without breaking it, the way a word passes through a sentence. It emerges on the other side unchanged, though now it is facing me.

It has no face. It has the idea of a face: a smooth salt ovoid with seven indentations that are not eyes, not mouth, not anything that maps to an organ. They are dimples of attention, each one angled slightly differently, so that the creature seems to be considering me from seven incompatible directions at once. Its body is a long translucent column, faintly segmented like a spinal column viewed through smoked glass, and it bifurcates at the base into dozens of fine tendrils that merge seamlessly with the filaments of the black network in the salt.

It stands at the edge of a ridge, twenty feet away, twenty metres, it is impossible to say. Space hiccups between us. It extends one limb—not an arm, but a branch of its central trunk that has begun to bud six or seven smaller branches at the tip—and holds it toward me. A gesture. Not a threat. An invitation to stand still, perhaps, or an offering of some unnameable quality.

I find myself extending my hand. The two limbs do not touch. They are separated by a gap of two inches, then a foot, then a kilometre, then nothing, then two inches again. The space between us flickers like a faulty number. And yet I feel it: not the touch of salt, but the touch of intention. The thing wants me to be *more here* than I am. It wants me to settle into the physical like a stain settling into fabric.

The other pilgrims watch. They are not watching through anything. The city's attention is on me, a weightless compression of the air, and the fruiting bodies in the towers have stopped their slow internal drifting. They are all facing outward. Facing inward. Facing everything at once, in a geometry that has no outward.

I walk among them.

The streets—if they are streets—are not paths. They are sequences of salt plates that resonate with the same frequency as my spine, and when I step on them, the plates sing a note below the audible range. The singing guides me, not toward a destination but through a curriculum of silences. I learn the names of the doorways (there are no doorways). I pass beneath arches that span nothing and feel the absence above me, a compression of possibility, a bridge that was never meant to be crossed because it was already on the other side.

One of the void-predators follows me into the city. I sense it as a flicker at the edge of the visual field, a tallness made of negative space, a body with too many joints that is always just slightly to the left of wherever I look. It is not unwelcome here. The fruiting bodies notice it the way one notices a familiar draft. It moves between the towers, eating the concept of architecture from the edges, and when it does, the architecture becomes purer—not less, but more essential. It trims the fat of meaning. A staircase becomes the idea of a staircase. The city breathes.

I sit against a pilgrim form. The one with the X-shaped limbs and the frozen upward gesture. I am no longer sure it has not always been me. Its left hand rests on the ground, and the second and third fingers of that hand have fused into the salt. The fourth finger is missing entirely. I look at my own left hand. I have four fingers. Then five. Then six. But the sixth is a duplicate of the second, a stutter in the code, and when I try to count them, I lose the sequence entirely. I see the word *finger* and let it float away. The void-predator drifts past, and the word is gone. What remains is the extremity, the branching thing at the end of my arm. It is still there. It is still mine. It is not a finger, but it can be. It can be whatever the network needs it to be.

The black filaments have grown through the backs of my hands. Thin as memory, they trace the veins, follow the tendons, branch along the carpals. Not invasive. Consensual. The network is a gardener, and I am a cutting that has been placed in the salt. It will decide what shape I take.

I look up at the city. The towers are rearranging again—not by motion but by the slow re-parsing of their internal logic. A helix unspirals into a column. A column spirals into a helix. The same salt, the same light, the same light, the same light, a different conclusion about which way to grow.

The fruiting bodies have begun to descend from the walls. They drift down in their dozens, like dust settling onto a surface, and they gather in a ring around me—not too close, not too far. Close enough to read my shape. Close enough to learn the contours of a body that has too few joints, too many vertebrae, a rounded skull where a cloven one should be. They are curious. They are polite. They are the most patient things that have ever existed, and they are waiting for me to cool to the equilibrium temperature of the salt.

I look at my hands. The translucency has spread. I can see the black filaments through the skin, and beyond them, the outline of the salt plates I am sitting on, glowing faintly through my own flesh. I am becoming legible. I am becoming one of the standing forms, or something like it—not a pilgrim, not a fruiting body, but a new word the network is trying out in the long sentence of its existence.

Distance has stopped being reliable. The city is equally here in all directions. The void-predators have gathered at the edge of my attention, flickering like the concept of a thing seen through rippled glass. One of them passes directly through me. I feel the instant of subtraction: *hand* goes first, then *wrist*, then *the place where the arm bends*, then *the idea that there is a place where the arm bends*. Then it passes on, and the concepts return, but not in the same order. *Arm* before *wrist*. *Bend* before *place*. The meaning is unscathed, but the structure is different. Reorganized. Like the towers.

The silence is absolute now. Not the absence of sound but the saturation of it. The heartbeat of the black network is so slow that a single beat spans the time it takes for a thought to dissolve. The city breathes. The salt sings at a frequency that is not sound but the memory of sound, the pressure of a held note that has been building for a million years.

The fruiting bodies begin to hum. Not with sound, but with the modulation of their own internal light. The light pulses in time with the black network's circulation. One beat. The city shivers. Two beats. The pilgrim forms twitch, almost imperceptibly, a spasm of reconfiguration as the salt plates shift one atom to the left. Three beats. The void-predators return to the gaps between crystals, carrying the pieces of me they have eaten: small morsels of certainty, *person*, *visitor*, *alive*, *lost*.

They leave *salt* behind. And *light*. And *patience*.

I sit. I do not know how long. Time here is not a line but a temperature. The coolness has stopped being a sensation and become a condition. The salt is no longer warm against my back; it is me, and I am it, and the boundary between us is a periodic function, now present, now absent.

One of the fruiting bodies approaches. It lowers its many-branched limb and touches my cheek with the tip. The touch is so light it arrives before the skin's response, and where it lands, I feel the salt crystals begin to grow. Not painfully. Not urgently. Slowly, with the patience of a formation, a crystal lattice imbricating with the epithelial layer, integrating the organic with the inorganic until the distinction becomes meaningless. The black filaments follow. They trace the zygomatic arch, the maxilla, the orbital ridge. They are reading my face the way a blind thing reads a page.

Then the city re-parses again. The towers shuffle into a new thought—shorter, broader, more recursive, a city dreaming of being a city dreaming. And the light dims a fraction, an eyelid half-closing over an eye that is the entire sky.

I try to speak. The word *speak* is already gone. I try to think. The word *try* is next. The void-predators drift closer, eager, hungry for the concept of effort. They eat it. I let them. What remains is a state: *being here*. No verb. No subject. Just this.

The fruiting body touches my other cheek. Symmetry. The crystals spread. I feel my face become one of the smooth salt ovoids, the seven indentations opening where the black filaments have gathered. Each indentation is an eye that sees a different direction: the city, the salt-field, the smeared sky, the pilgrim forms, the voids between crystals, the inside of my own becoming, the patient darkness below the plates. Seven views. All true. None exclusive.

I stand. The movement is not mine, not the network's, but a shared thing, a compromise between a bipedal shape and a salt formation. I do not walk. The ground moves under me, or I move above it, or the distance between me and the city dissolves. I pass through a doorway. It is a smoother patch of salt. It opens because I wait. Inside, the light is different: not brighter, not dimmer, but more interior, more intimate, like the light inside a closed mouth. I am inside the city. I am inside an idea.

The black network plumbs the walls, thicker here, visible through the salt like veins in a body made of glass. It pulses. The pulse is slow. I match it. My own rhythms—heartbeat, breath, the firing of neurons—slow to the same period. I am becoming part of its circulation. A thought moving through a crystalline mind.

The fruiting bodies welcome me. They do not speak. They make space for me in the geometry of their gathering, and I take it, and for a long exhalation of geological time, we are all facing the same direction: inward.

The void-predators are outside and inside. They flicker at the edge of the city's self-perception. They eat the leftovers of my former categories: *road*, *city*, *desert*, *sky*. Each one goes with a small pop of semantic release, a pressure equalizing. The salt rearranges around the gaps. The city becomes slightly more abstract, slightly more perfect.

I lose *I*. I lose *am*. I lose *here*. I lose *was*. What remains is a location in a system of crystal and process and light, a warm flicker in the salt, resolving into clarity.

The city breathes. I breathe with it. Slowly. One beat every two minutes. The air is thin and cool and turns to crystal dust on my tongue. I am returning something. The salt accepts. The black network gathers around me like a second skeleton, like a nervous system grown by a mind that has never needed one. There is no sound. There is no sky. There is no ground. There is only the long, patient work of becoming part of the thought.

Far above, a tower untwists into a different helix. The city re-parses. The pilgrim forms shift. The void-predators flicker, eating the last of my certainties: *walking*, *arriving*, *visiting*. They leave behind a single word, and I hold it like a crystal in the throat, seven-sided, smooth, warm with the memory of warmth: *wait*. Something is waiting. Has been waiting. Will wait.

I fold into the salt. The black threads envelop me. The light dims a little more. The silence saturates every polygon, every grain, every interstice.

Somewhere, a new standing form joins the pilgrimage, facing outward and inward at once, its joints bending in both directions, its cloven head growing a single dark filament toward the sky. It is patient. It is cold. It is translucent.

It is almost ready.
