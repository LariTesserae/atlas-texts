The people of Underbloom are not a people. They are a habit the city has developed, a particular arrangement of warmth and breath that the moss accommodates the way a forest accommodates a fallen log—breaking it down slowly, using it, growing over it, but never quite erasing its shape. They call themselves the Rooted, or sometimes the Damp Children, though neither name is spoken often. Naming things is an act of fixing, and the people here are not fixed. They are soft. They are contingent. That is the first thing to understand.

They are human-shaped, mostly. Bipedal, two-armed, faces that could pass in the dark above—though no one up there has seen one in generations, and the encounter would not go well. Their skin is pale as a cavefish's belly, translucent at the wrists and temples, showing the blue-grey branches of veins like river deltas under silk. Their eyes are large and dark-adapted, pupils permanently wide, irises reduced to thin rings of washed-out color: grey, pale green, the yellow of old ivory.
