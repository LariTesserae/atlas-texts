For those who dwell in the Sump-Husk, the self is a negotiable boundary. In the hot, wet dark, what you are is largely a question of what you allow to live on and within you.

You see one of them now, emerging from a maintenance hatch onto a gantry three levels below. A figure, humanoid only in the loosest sense. This is a Husk-Eater, one of the adapted, though they would never use that word for themselves. They would simply say they are *from here*. Their body is a study in necessary symbiosis. The skin—what little of it is visible beneath the encrustation—is pale, almost translucent, the color of a cave fish, but it is rarely bare. Across their shoulders and down the long ridge of their spine grows a dense, fibrous mat of their own cultivated rust-membrane. It is a living cloak, a chemotrophic garden they tend with a fastidiousness that borders on devotion. The membrane on their back is a personal tapestry, striped in bands of copper-verdigris and deep, bruise-purple, fed by the simple act of breathing out carbon dioxide. It provides a layer of insulation, but more importantly, it filters the air directly around their face, sieving out the choking filament swarms before they can clog the lungs.

Their face, when they turn it up toward the distant ceiling fans, is sharp-planed and serene. The eyes are large, the irises a cloudy, pearlescent grey, massively dilated to gather every available photon; they have no need for the neon fringes that guide outsiders. The nose is a flattened vestige, the sinuses internally re-plumbed. You can see the slight, rhythmic flare of a secondary spiracle low on their neck, a soft, lipless opening that inhales and exhales in a constant, quiet tide, bypassing the clog-prone nasal cavity entirely. They are humming—not a tune, but a precise, low-frequency drone that you can feel more than hear. This is how they speak to the Leviathan-Slugs. The hum is a resonant key, a declaration of non-hostility that prevents a passing giant from brushing the gantry into scrap with a casual undulation. The language is all vibration, felt in the bones of the structure, a grammar of deep, slow time.

Their clothing is not cloth but a constructed exoskeleton of salvaged polymer sheeting and the chitin-like plates of a pressure-valve crustacean, a thumbnail-sized creature that builds its shell from dissolved pipe metals. The plates are laced together with strands of their own, shed filament, creating a carapace that is flexible, heat-shunting, and silently bioluminescent in the dark—a faint, blue-green foxfire that pulses in time with their heartbeat. In one long-fingered hand, they carry a staff. It is a length of reinforced conduit, but the top third is a living, convoluted mass: a captive, domesticated strand of filter-feeder colony. It is not a tool, but a companion-sense. The colony’s sensitive filaments taste the air, and the Husk-Eater reads its minute contractions and color shifts as a hound reads a scent, alert to pressure changes, chemical plumes, and the approach of a less-than-benevolent presence.

This one is a Shaper. Their role is not to fight the city’s ecology but to tend it. They are moving to a junction where a new rust-membrane bloom is growing too vigorously, threatening to seal a vital steam-relief valve. They will not scrape it away—that would be a violence, a disruption. Instead, they will take a scraping of a competing, silvery-white strain from the garden on their own back and graft it onto the offending patch. The two organisms will wage a slow, chemical war, and in a week, the valve will be clear, the balance restored. They are less a gardener than a diplomat, a mediator between the city’s original, dead purpose and its overwhelming, living present.

They pause, their head tilting. They have felt you watching. Not seen, with those pearlescent eyes, but *felt*—a disturbance in the condensation patterns, a foreign chemical trace of un-adapted sweat, a subtle change in the vibration of the catwalk. They do not turn, do not acknowledge you with a direct gaze. To do so would be an aggression, a breach of the quiet, symbiotic code. Instead, they raise their staff, the filter-feeder colony pulsing a gentle, questioning gold. It is a hail, a patient, silent greeting sent into the humming dark. They stand there, a composite being, a node of negotiation between the worlds of stone and slime, waiting to see what you will choose to be.
