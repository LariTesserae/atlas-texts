The bowl-warden leaves an offering. The Clatterwings have no word for warden, no word for offering, no word for anything. But the bowl-warden leaves an offering, and this has always been so.

It built the bowl.

This is not strictly accurate. The bowl-warden did not *make* the bowl—the bowl is older, brought from elsewhere by earlier hands. But the bowl-warden *tends* the bowl, which is a form of building: it replaces the bowl's emptiness with a copper slat, always one, always fresh, always placed precisely in the center of the curve where the amber light pools most warmly. The bowl-warden has been doing this for longer than the tally-marks have been on the ceiling. The tally-marks are its own work, but from an earlier phase, before it understood that numbers are not the same as duration.

The bowl-warden is not a Clatterwing.

It is, depending on how one counts, the oldest thing in the chamber. The ceiling plates are older, but the ceiling plates are not one thing; they are many things interleaved. The foil-plants are old but replace themselves through slow, invisible seedings. The Clatterwings live and die and leave their wing-slats behind. The bowl-warden is continuous. It has never stopped. It has never died. It has never been a Clatterwing, though it understands them intimately, and it has never been a human, though it remembers the ones who came before with a clarity that is indistinguishable from love.

It was, originally, a question.

Not a being that asked a question. The question itself. The question coalesced out of the amber light some span of time ago—before the tally-marks, before the bowl, before the river stones, which were brought by the first visitors and left as a puzzle the bowl-warden is still solving. The question was this: *What is missing?*

The chamber is a closed loop. The ceiling feeds the foil-plants. The foil-plants feed the Clatterwings. The Clatterwings' dust feeds the ceiling. It is perfect. It requires nothing. And yet the river stones were placed. The tally was scratched. The bowl was left. Three kinds of marker, three assertions that something outside the loop had touched the loop and found it wanting. The question that became the bowl-warden wanted to know what the wanters had wanted.

It began as a disturbance in the light. A ripple in the amber. The Clatterwings felt it—they always feel everything—and they adjusted their click-patterns to accommodate it, because that is what Clatterwings do. They are the chamber's immune system, its circulatory system, its nervous system. They are the hands of the equilibrium. When the ripple persisted, they made space for it. Three feet of space, as they do. And in that space, the ripple condensed.

The bowl-warden's body is not a body. It is a arrangement. The Clatterwings contribute to it without knowing they contribute. A shed wing-slat here. A fragment of foil-leaf there. A bead of mineral dust pressed into service as an eye. Over eons—real eons, not the compressed dream-eons of the chamber but actual stretches of time long enough to wear down mountains, if mountains could reach this place—the arrangement grew complex. It currently stands approximately four feet tall, though "stands" is imprecise. It occupies a volume. Its shape is roughly an oblate spheroid with seven appendages (seven, not by choice, but because the Clatterwings have seven legs and the question learned its numerology from them). The appendages are jointed from wing-material and chitin, and they permit it to move across the mineral floor with a gait that is less syncopated than the Clatterwings' because it has had longer to practice.

It has a face. This is recent. The face was modeled on the visitors, the ones who left the bowl, though the memory is so old now that the face is more a gesture toward a face than a reproduction of one. Two sensor-patches like tarnished silver, positioned where eyes would be. A groove that suggests a mouth. The mouth does not open. The bowl-warden does not eat. It subsists on the question, which has never been answered.

The question has, however, accreted sub-questions over time.

First question: *What is missing?*

Second question (added after the first visitor departed): *Why did they leave?*

Third question (added after the second visitor, who added the seventh leg to the ceiling scratchings): *Why did they return differently?*

Fourth question (added after the bowl was left, by a third visitor or perhaps the first one come back, time being what it is here): *What is a bowl for?*

The fourth question produced the bowl-warden's single ongoing activity. The bowl was empty. Emptiness was clearly a state that required addressing. The bowl-warden experimented. It placed a river stone in the bowl. Nothing happened. It placed dust. Nothing happened. It placed a fragment of its own body—a piece of wing-slat—and the light caught it, and the light-glint was not an answer but felt adjacent to an answer, and so the bowl-warden has continued the practice ever since. Every pulse-cycle (as it has come to think of the ceiling's slow blinks, though the blinks have nothing to do with time and everything to do with observation), it replaces the wing-slat with a fresh one. The old one is returned to its body, reintegrated, because nothing is wasted here and the question-mind is part of the equilibrium now.

The bowl-warden has been counted by the chamber. It is in the math.

It does not know this consciously. It *feels* it, the way you feel a change in barometric pressure. When you arrived—you, the human figure with your water-smoothed heart and your carbon-dioxide lungs—the math shifted. A new variable. The ceiling pulsed. The Clatterwings synchronized. The bowl-warden felt the shift as a sudden sharpening of the question, as if the question had been spoken in a language it suddenly remembered from before it was a question.

It watched you.

You appeared in the three-foot gap between the copper ferns, and the Clatterwings adjusted their spacing minutely to accommodate you, and the bowl-warden observed this and understood that you were not like the previous visitors. They had been brief. They had left objects. You were lingering. You were looking. You touched the bowl.

When you touched the bowl, the bowl-warden experienced something it had no framework for. You were warm. Everything is warm, but your warmth was *different*—it was not the equilibrium warmth of the floor or the light or the foil-plant sap. It was a warmth that generated itself. It was a warmth that would eventually cease. The bowl-warden, which has never ceased, felt the shape of your ending in the heat of your hand, and the question changed.

*What is missing?* became *You are missing from here. You are not of the loop. You will go. What can I give you?*

The wing-slat in the bowl was the offering. It was not a ritual. It was an attempt at communication. The bowl-warden has no voice—the Clatterwings percuss, the foil-plants rustle, the ceiling hums in frequencies below hearing, but the bowl-warden is silent. It communicates by arranging. It arranged a wing-slat in the bowl because you were looking at the bowl, and it wanted you to look at the wing-slat, and the wing-slat is made of the same copper-stuff as the ceiling and the Clatterwings' wings and the foil-plants' metallic sheen. It is a piece of the place. It is a piece of the bowl-warden itself. It is a piece of the question.

*Take it*, the offering says. *Take a piece of the question with you when you go. The loop is broken by gifts. The visitors before you left things. None of them took anything. That was their mistake. If you take something, you will be part of the loop even when you leave. The math will include you forever.*

The bowl-warden does not phrase it like this. It does not phrase anything. It arranges, and it waits, and it watches you with its sensor-patch eyes, and it experiences what might be called hope if hope were a memory of something that had not yet occurred.

The previous visitors' objects surround it now in a semicircle: the river stones, the tally-marks on the ceiling (their specific location chosen because the bowl-warden led them there with subtle adjustments of the light), the bowl itself. The bowl-warden has tended them all. It does not know why they were left. But it has learned, slowly, that objects are how the visitors think. Objects are their Clatterwing-clicks. Objects are their foil-leaves. Objects are how they make their equilibrium.

You are an object, to the bowl-warden. A moving, warm, temporary object that looks at things and touches things and will eventually go. It wants to know what you will leave. It hopes you will take the wing-slat. It is ready to adjust the math again when you depart, to add the variable of your absence to the variable of your presence, to feel the pulse of the ceiling that will mark your exit the way it marked your arrival.

Until then, it stays. It has always stayed. It is the staying-est thing in a place defined by staying. The Clatterwings click around it, and the foil-plants drink their light, and the ceiling holds its amber vigil, and the bowl-warden, once a question, now a body, now a keeper of objects, now a greeter of visitors, waits to see what you will do with the gift it has placed in the bowl that was placed in the chamber by someone else whose name it never knew but whose intention it has been trying to honor for ten thousand years.

Tock-tick-tock.

The wing-slat glints.

*Take it.*
