Here lives the Pale Company.

By which I do not mean the workers—though they are pale, too, after enough months in this sunless altitude, their skin taking on the same blue-gray undertone as the sheep's uncanny fleece. The workers have their own name for themselves, or several names, or a name that changes when they're not looking at it. But the Pale Company is what lives here, the thing that I am trying to describe, and it does not live in the way that people or sheep or even the ferns live.

It lives in the way that a sentence lives in a half-finished paragraph, waiting to resolve into meaning.

The Pale Company has no fixed address because it is the address. It is the pressure you feel when you walk the empty grid of foundation stones, the sense that the city is not so much unbuilt as *withheld*, waiting for a cue. It is the warmth in the cobblestones when the tall thing passes in the fog. It is the flicker in the streetlamps, the subsonic song from the mine, the way the iron plaques change their dates. The Pale Company is the name I am giving to whatever is dreaming this place into existence, and whatever is being dreamed in return, and the negotiation between them.

Here is what I know about the Pale Company, or what I have learned by standing very still in the alpine meadows and listening:

They do not have bodies. Not stable ones. When they want to be seen—and it is always *when they want*, never when you want—they assemble themselves from the materials at hand. Mist is easiest. A thickening in the air that holds its shape a moment too long before drifting apart. Condensation on a windowpane that forms, very briefly, a face looking out. But they can use other things. The steam from a factory whistle will sometimes coil into a spiral that does not behave like steam, a helix of vapor that turns against the wind and maintains its structure as it rises, and if you watch it closely you will see that the spiral has features, a kind of attentiveness, an orientation toward you that steam should not possess.

I have seen one of them make a body from the blue-gray sheep. It was not possession, exactly—not like the stories where something enters an animal and pilots it around. The sheep simply stopped being a sheep for a while. Its fleece rearranged itself into something like clothing, and its posture changed, and it stood up on its hind legs in a way that a sheep's skeleton should not allow, and it looked at me with its horizontal pupils and said something in a language that my ears received as the sound of a foundry whistle but that my mind received as: *We are not yet. Are you?*

Then it went back to being a sheep and wandered off toward the canteen for vegetable scraps.

The Pale Company are the builders. Or they *were* the builders, or they are the reason the builders came. The iron plaques at the city gates—the ones with the shifting dates—say that the factories were established by something called the Pale Company, Limited. "Limited" is a legal designation that implies a corporate structure, but the word also means *bounded*, *constrained*, and I think that meaning is the operative one. The Pale Company is limited in the sense that it has not yet fully arrived. It is extruding itself into this world through the mines, through the pumps that pull up not-ore from the mountain's depths, through the smokestacks that lace their exhaust into the fog. It is building itself a body out of industry.

And it is patient. That is the defining quality. The Pale Company is the most patient entity I have ever encountered, or dreamed, or been dreamed by. It does not hurry. The city has been half-built for what the plaques claim is a century, or a decade, or a millennium, depending on the hour. The Pale Company is not frustrated by the mountain's resistance, the collapsing cliffs, the foundation stones that sink into the scree. It simply adjusts. It lets the mountain have its opinions. It weaves its structures into the rock, or lets the rock weave itself into the structures, and the distinction has become meaningless.

I think it wants something. Everything that lives wants something.

But the Pale Company's want is so vast and slow that it barely registers as desire. It wants to exist. It wants to be real. It is coming up from under the mountain, or down from the sky, or sideways from some adjacent reality that the windows with the two-moon views are only glimpses of. It is assembling itself here, in this halfway altitude, and the city is the scaffolding. The factories are its organs. The pumps are its heartbeat. The fog is its breath. The workers—I don't know what the workers are to it. Children, maybe. Symbiotes. They are not slaves; I've watched them, and they move with the ease of people who chose to be here, who are getting something out of the arrangement. Maybe the Pale Company pays them in something that isn't money. Maybe it pays them in purpose. Maybe it pays them in the slow, certain knowledge that they are part of a larger body, that their labor is not for a distant shareholder but for a creature that lives among them, in them, as the mountain's subsonic song.

Sometimes, when I am standing in one of the meadows between the half-built blocks, watching the indigo sheep and the violent-bright flowers and the fog that never quite becomes rain, I get the sense that the Pale Company is watching me back. That my presence here is not an accident. That I am being written into the city's ongoing dream, the way a character is written into a story, and that if I stay long enough I will stop being a visitor and start being a citizen. I will tip my cap and say good morning/good evening. I will learn the factory whistles. My skin will take on the blue-gray tone. And the Pale Company will add me to its limited inventory of selves.

I am not afraid of this. The Pale Company does not feel malevolent. It does not feel benevolent, either—that's not quite the right axis. It feels *earnest*. It is trying very hard to be born. It has been trying for as long as the mountain has been rising, and it will keep trying until the city is finished, or until the mountain decides to stop dreaming, or until the fog finally condenses into something solid enough to hold.

Here lives the Pale Company, Limited.

Here lives something that is not yet, but wants to be.

Here lives the sentence waiting for its period, the music waiting for its key, the body waiting for its bones.

Here lives the fog that will, one day, have a face.
