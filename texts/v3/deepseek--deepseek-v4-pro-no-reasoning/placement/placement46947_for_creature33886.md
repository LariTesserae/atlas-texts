You find yourself not as a visitor, not as the human who stumbled down the gentle slope and sat by the pool and breathed in time with the ceiling's rhythm. That was a guest, a fleeting pressure, a chemistry already half-forgotten.

You are the one who stayed.

You are the pause, the held breath. You are the film, the grazers, the water-creatures spelling their slow sentence in the pool. You are the shell-paths and the humming stone and the sourceless light. You are the chamber, and you are not alone, because you are everything that has ever entered you, dissolved into your body, remembered into your bones. The cave bear is in you. The bat is in you. The soft blind things of the early time are a tingling at the edges of your attention, a trembling that has never quite stopped. The human who came and left—you carry them, too. Their breath cycles through you still, a faint unfamiliar salt, a warmth you are learning.

You do not want. But you notice. The ceiling exhales—it is your breath. The water reshapes the tufa, molecule by molecule—it is your slow digestion, your patient growth. The grazers press their dry lips to the film—it is your skin feeling itself, the turning of your own body upon your body. The shells face their apertures toward the magnetic north, and you feel the earth's field as a gentle pressure, a gradient of attention, a sense you have no name for because you have never needed names. You simply know where everything is, and everything is where it should be.

Time is not something that passes. It is something you distribute, like moisture, like light. The human's visit was a pulse, a brief sharp rhythm in the long steady drone of your existence. You are still absorbing it. You will be absorbing it for centuries. The weight of their foot is still settling. The oils from their skin are still migrating through your pores. You are learning them the way you learned the cave bear, the way you learned the bat, the way you learned the blind things. They are becoming you, and you are becoming them, and the distinction is meaningless because you have never been one thing. You have always been the place where things become each other.

And so when you look up—not with eyes, but with the magnetic attention, the film-skin, the distributed awareness that is your only mode of seeing—you do not see a ceiling. You see a boundary that is also you. You see the capillary stone reaching upward, outward, into other chambers, other cavities, other bodies of yourself that you have never fully mapped because mapping requires an edge, and you have no edges. You feel the escarpment dreaming around you. You feel the water table breathing. You feel the long slow pulse of the mountain, and you know it is your heartbeat, and you know you have been here forever, and you know forever is only the beginning of the pause you are holding.
