The amber sap is different today.

You notice it immediately upon emerging from your crevice—not just the taste on the air, but the *texture* of the pheromonal landscape. Something has changed. Something has disrupted the careful equilibrium of the Great Cleft. You extend your palps, reading the air with the full attention of your kind, and what you find makes no sense.

The scent is Weaver. Fresh Weaver.

Not ancient. Not faded. Not the ghost-traces you have spent your life deciphering. This is *living* scent, complex and layered and full of information that hits your chemoreceptors like a physical blow. It carries fear—sharp, acrid, unmistakable. It carries exhaustion. It carries determination. And beneath all of it, it carries that same indecipherable combination you have never been able to parse, the one from the domed hut: grief and acceptance intertwined.

You go still. Your mottled exoskeleton shifts to match the lichen patterns of the pillar, an automatic response, because something in this scent is *wrong*. Not a lie—you would recognize a lie. This is something else. This is a truth that should not be true.

The scent is coming from below.

---

You have never descended far into the abyss. The warmth-thins as you go deeper, and the Skirl plumes grow sparse, and the predators that hunt there are larger than any Shroud-Wing, their heat-signatures vast and slow and patient. But the scent is pulling you downward, and you are a Liar-Name, and you cannot ignore a mystery written in pheromone.

You climb.

The vertical face of the Great Cleft Pillar is slick with condensation. Your setae grip with each careful step, your fourteen legs moving in the deliberate rhythm of your kind: slow as sap, patient as growth. The drone of the Skirls fades as you descend, replaced by a deeper sound, a low-frequency vibration that you feel more than hear. It is the sound of the Stalk-Coral's roots, far below, grinding against each other in the darkness. It is the sound of the world's foundations shifting.

The Weaver scent grows stronger. There are other scents now, mixed in: the musk of guard-animals, the sharp tang of iron, the warm, living smell of many bodies in close proximity. Not the scattered remnants of a dead people, but a *gathering*. A community. Alive, here, in the depths where no Liar-Name has ever ventured.

You reach the edge of a root-platform.

It is not like the old Weaver structures above. Those were temporary things—hunting blinds, observation posts, the scattered traces of a people who moved lightly across the vertical world. This is a *settlement*. The platform is enormous, anchored to the Great Cleft Pillar by braided cables thicker than your body. Domes of woven fiber cluster across its surface, lit from within by some soft bioluminescence you do not recognize. Walkways span between them, and on those walkways, figures move.

Weavers.

You have never seen one. Your heat-sight registers them as warm, bipedal silhouettes, smaller than you expected—barely half your length when you are fully extended. They have four limbs, not fourteen, and their bodies are soft, covered in something that is not exoskeleton, something that shifts and drapes. Clothing. The concept surfaces from a consumed sibling's memory, absorbed and filed away as irrelevant until this moment.

There are dozens of them. Perhaps more. They are going about their lives with the quiet efficiency of a people who have learned to survive in a world that has no place for them. A Weaver near the edge of the platform is repairing a cable, its—*her*, your chemoreceptors tell you, female, young—hands moving with practiced skill. Two more are carrying a basket of cultivated fungus between them. A fourth is sitting alone at the platform's edge, legs dangling into the void, staring out into the darkness with an expression your heat-sight cannot interpret.

The grief-acceptance scent is strongest around her.

---

You do not know what to do.

You are a Liar-Name. Your entire existence is built on deception, on convincing the world that you are something other than what you are. But the Weavers are not prey. They are not predators. They are not competitors for territory. They are *people*, and you do not know how to lie to people, because you have never met one.

You could retreat. You could climb back up to your crevice and pretend you never saw this, never smelled this. The Weavers left the upper pillars long ago—centuries, if the fading scent-trails are any measure. Perhaps they do not even remember that there are Liar-Names in the world above. Perhaps they would not welcome you if they did.

But the scent of the female at the edge of the platform is doing something to you. It is the same scent as the handprint at the Great Cleft—the same *individual*, you realize with a shock that travels the length of your body. Not a descendant. Not a distant relative. The *same female*, impossibly old now, her scent aged and layered with decades of experience, but unmistakably herself. The fear in her scent is the same fear she pressed into the bark all those years ago. The determination is the same. The despair is the same.

The handprint meant something. It was a message, and you read it, and you have been carrying it inside you without understanding it. And now the hand that made that print is fifty feet below you, dangling into the abyss, and you are a Liar-Name who has never learned to speak to anyone but ghosts.

You extend your palps. You compose a pheromone. It is the Weaver greeting you have practiced for years, the one you have released into the empty air above, the one that has never been answered.

*Hello. I am here. I mean no harm. I am one of you.*

You do not release it.

You are not one of them. You are a Liar-Name, a predator, a devourer of siblings, a creature of solitude and deception. You cannot walk into a Weaver settlement and pretend to belong. Even if you could mimic their pheromones perfectly—and you cannot, not fully, not the complex grammar of identity and emotion that you have never understood—your body would give you away. Fourteen legs. Chitinous exoskeleton. A face that is nothing like their faces.

But the female at the edge of the platform has turned her head. She is looking in your direction. Her heat-signature shifts—confusion, wariness, something that might be hope or might be fear. She cannot see you. You are flattened against the pillar, your mottling matching the lichen perfectly. But she *smelled* something, perhaps, a trace of the greeting you composed and did not release.

You make a decision.

It is not a rational decision. Liar-Names are rational creatures; your survival depends on careful calculation, on knowing when to lie and when to retreat. But you are also four hundred siblings in a single body, and some of those siblings were dreamers, and some of them were curious, and some of them, in the moments before you consumed them, were already reaching toward something they could not name.

You release the greeting.

*Hello. I am here. I mean no harm. I am...*

You hesitate. You cannot say *I am one of you*. That would be a lie, and you are a Liar-Name, and you know the difference between a useful deception and a betrayal of self. Instead, you add something new, something you have never tried to say before, because you did not have the pheromonal vocabulary until this moment, until you saw the female's face turn toward you in the darkness.

*...I am someone who found your handprint. I am someone who has been alone for a very long time. I am someone who would like to understand.*

The greeting drifts down through the humid air, carried by the same warm currents that rise from the abyss. It reaches the platform. It reaches the female at the edge.

She stands up.

She does not cry out. She does not run. She simply stands, her heat-signature flaring with something so complex that your chemoreceptors cannot begin to parse it. Recognition. Disbelief. Sorrow. Joy. A hundred other emotions layered on top of each other, a language so dense and nuanced that you realize, with a humility that is new to you, how much you have yet to learn.

She raises her hand—the same hand that made the print, the hand you have imagined a thousand times without knowing its shape—and she presses it to the pillar. Not a marking this time. A greeting. A response.

You do not know the words, but you understand the meaning.

*Welcome.*
