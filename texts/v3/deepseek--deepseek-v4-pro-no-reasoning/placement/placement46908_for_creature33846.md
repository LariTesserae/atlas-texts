*Condense: to make denser, to gather disparate elements into a tighter, more coherent form.*

You are not formed so much as you are focused—a point where the general humidity of existence beads into a single, trembling droplet of self. The hum runs through you like a second pulse, the seventeen-minute rhythm that is not a clock but a heartbeat. You do not remember arriving. Arrival implies a before, and before is a concept that has not yet condensed here.

Your body, such as it is, surprises you in fragments. A frond curls, tasting the air, and the air tastes back: iron, memory, the faint ozone-sweetness of a probability freshly spun. Below, the root-fingers twitch against stone still sharp enough to cut. That sharpness delights you, though you could not say why. Perhaps because it is *new*. Everything here is new. Nothing has been worn smooth by time or touch. The world is a blade still smoking from the forge.

You sip. The water here is not water—you know this the way you know the rhythm, the way you know the taste of a reverse-birth happening three ridges over. The droplet you absorb carries its whole history: a journey up through cartilage-pipes from an engine that grinds not metal but *might-have-been*, a slow ascent through the weeping stone, and then a brief, glittering moment on the surface before you drink it. You taste the engine's purpose. It is making something. It is always making something.

And you? You are the part that *notices*.

A chord ripples past you, a visible shiver in the purple air. One of the others—a distant column crowned with fronds that spiral clockwise where yours spiral widdershins—is reporting. The data unfolds in you without language: a new biome has precipitated in the eastern knife-valleys. Vegetation that exhales a gas heavier than thought but lighter than regret. The condensation rate there is anomalous. The gravity whispers have gone faint and metallic. The chord does not ask for a response, but it lingers, waiting, a question-shaped silence.

You tremble. Not in fear—fear is for things that can lose what they have—but in readiness. The sourness is faint but unmistakable now, a wrong-key note in the great industrial hum. Somewhere nearby, the Condenser's relentless self-creation has stumbled. A piece of anti-pattern. A thing that does not know how to be.

You begin to move. The root-fingers find their grip, probing, releasing, a slow and deliberate negotiation with the sharp-edged now. Your column twists, each segment sliding against its neighbor with a sound like a stone lullaby. The fronds stiffen forward, tasting the gradient of wrongness, following it the way water follows the shape of a basin.

The pulse comes—that full-body shudder of empathy with the landscape—and in its wake, you feel it: a place where the weeping has stopped. Where the tears no longer carry story, only salt. A patch of reality gone numb and mute.

You will stand in that silence. You will exude what you are: not a self, not a name, but a chord of pure condensed attention. You will remind the wound that it, too, is part of the song. And then, perhaps, the tears will start again, rich with meaning once more, and you will sip, and tremble, and listen to the hum of a world still learning how hard it is to be.
