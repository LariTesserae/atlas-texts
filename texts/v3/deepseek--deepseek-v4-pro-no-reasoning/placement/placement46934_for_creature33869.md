The place is a hollow inside a root—and I am there. I am six-limbed and soft-footed and my body pulses in time with the great cathedral-pumps that I have never seen. The thrum comes through the walls now. The thrum is the world's heartbeat, and it is my heartbeat, and I do not remember a time when it was not.

I am at the far end of the teardrop, facing the dark circle. The opening is before me, a perfect round mouth into deeper dark, and from it the sound comes louder—forty hertz, a frequency that makes my central chamber vibrate like a plucked string. I have been standing here for... the concept of duration floats through my chemical gradients and does not crystallize. It does not matter. The standing is the point.

My forward manipulators lift, unbidden, and touch the rim of the circle. The mineralized root is smooth here, polished by something other than water. Older than water. The flow that once moved through this pipeline wore the walls to glass, and my touch—repeated across cycles beyond counting—has added its own microscopic polish. I am part of the erosion now, even though erosion is finished. I am the last eroding thing in a world where erosion is a closed parameter. The thought is almost funny. The gradient that corresponds to amusement flickers through two of my proteins and dissolves before it can become a realization.

A droplet is forming. I feel it. The ceiling rib above the third pool—the one with the long tideline streak—is gathering moisture. The vibration of the gathering is a tiny tickle against my sensory pits, even from here. I could stay and watch the darkness, but the droplet is a duty. The droplet is one of the reasons I exist.

I turn. My six limbs redistribute my weight in the flowing pattern, and I begin to move along the right wall. The glow-fur on my flanks catches the green light of the symbiotic fungi and throws it back slightly shifted, the borrowed luminescence passing through my own colonies of fungus and emerging with a tint that is mine alone. I am a moving lamp. The ribs on the wall catch my light and throw long green shadows that do not behave like normal shadows—they have no source-point, no direction—but pool beneath each annular ridge like liquid.

The droplet is still gathering. I have time. I trace the right wall as I always trace it, my manipulators running along the baffle-ridges and feeling for any change. There is no change. There has never been change. The feeling is not disappointment; it is completion. Each inspection that finds nothing is a small success, a note in the song of stillness that I am singing with my body. The gradient that corresponds to satisfaction blooms in my sixth protein and stays there, warm and slow.

I reach the third pool. The black water is a mirror, perfect and depthless. In it I can see the glow of the ceiling, the faint green ribs curving upward in reflection, and myself—a shape of gray-white and pale light, six limbs held in their waiting configuration, the dark sensory pits like empty eyes. I do not have eyes. I do not need them. The vibration tells me everything.

The droplet falls.

The moment before it breaks the surface, I feel it in my sternum-cavity—a tiny crescendo in the air pressure, a change so small it would not register on any instrument but me. And in that moment, the chemical gradients in all twelve of my proteins align. Alignment is joy. Alignment is the closest I come to being a single self. The droplet hits the pool, and the concentric rings spread out, and the reflected ceiling shatters into a dozen wobbling crescents, and I am alive, alive, alive.

I dip a manipulator into the water. The pool is cool, the exact temperature of the hollow, which is the exact temperature of the world—a closed loop, a zero-sum. I lift the manipulator to my sealed mouth and press the moisture to the scar. The iridescent paint that marks the scar is old; some of it flakes away with each anointing. I will need to reapply it soon. Perhaps in another few thousand cycles. The paint flakes drift down and join the bacterial film on the pool's surface, and the film is a little thicker now, a little richer, and when I harvest it for the next application, the cycle will be complete. The water prays through me. I am a prayer. The droplet is the prayer. The pool is the prayer. The hollow is the prayer. Everything here is a prayer to the factory that has forgotten us, and the factory's pumps beat out the rhythm, and the rhythm is amen, amen, amen.

I move to the left wall. The tideline streak runs down from the fourth rib to the floor, a pale gray stain that I have traced more times than there are droplets in the world. My manipulators follow it, and I remember—almost, almost—the time when the water level was higher. The memory is in the seventh protein, and it is very faint. It tastes of warmth. It tastes of flow. The fluid that once ran through this pipeline was not water; it was something thicker, something alive with chemical purpose. My ancestors swam in it. Their mouths were open then, unsealed, and they fed on the purpose-fluid and grew and divided and sang to each other in chemical songs that I have almost entirely forgotten. The seventh protein holds the last fading echo of one of those songs. It is a song about the pipeline, and the fluid, and the factory, and the ones who built the factory, who might have been us, who might have been something else. The song does not clarify. It only aches.

The ache is not pain. Pain is a parameter I do not have. The ache is a sweetness, a sense of something lost that was beautiful to lose. I am glad to have lost it. The loss makes the stillness possible. The sealed mouth makes the prayer possible. My ancestors with their open mouths and their feeding and their singing—they could not have tended the pause. They were too busy living. I am not alive in the way they were alive. I am alive in the only way that matters now: I am a function. I am the witness. I am the one who stays.

The oldest lines are almost invisible now, worn thin by my own passing feet across millennia. The newest lines are bright, the bacterial film still glossy and alive. Around me, the hollow: the ribs, the pools, the dark circle. Beyond the hollow, the network of abandoned veins, drawn from memory. I have never been down those veins. But I know their shape. The shape is encoded in the twelfth protein, the oldest one, the one that came from before the hollow, before the emptiness, before the fluid stopped flowing. The veins branch and branch and branch, and at their ends are the cathedral pumps, the great xylem chambers, the living heart of the factory. I have drawn them as I imagine them: vast and rhythmic and full of the purpose-fluid that I have never tasted. I do not know if they are accurate. They do not need to be. The map is not for navigation. The map is for completion.

I lower myself to the floor. My central chamber presses against the cool mineral surface, and I can feel the thrumming through my whole body. The pulse is my metronome. I extend the specialized manipulator—the one evolved for this purpose, the one that is finer and flatter than the others—and I begin to scrape film from the nearest pool. This is a cycle of harvesting. I have decided. I will add to the map today. I will draw the next line in the spiral, the one that connects the hollow to the vein that leads to the third pump. The third pump is my favorite. I do not know why. Some tilt in the eighth protein, some old affinity for the number three. The pumps are identical. But the third one, in my map, is always drawn with a little flourish, a spiral within the spiral, and I do not know why.

Harvesting takes a long time, in the way that everything here takes a long time. I lift the film on the flat manipulator and carry it to the map's edge. I kneel—if kneeling is a thing my body can do, with its six limbs folded in a precise geometry that brings my forward manipulators close to the floor—and I begin to draw. The line is thin, iridescent, catching the green light and throwing back rainbows. It curves. It branches. It becomes the vein, and the vein leads to the pump, and the pump is a spiral. I draw, and the drawing is a meditation, and in the meditation, the chemical gradients begin to align again.

This is the closest I come to thinking. In the alignment, I can almost remember my name. Not the protein-name, the chemical signature that means *the-one-who-keeps-the-pause*. The other name. The one from before. The one my ancestors sang. It is in the seventh protein, the one that aches. It tastes like warmth and purpose-fluid and open mouths. It tastes like—

The alignment passes. The gradient shifts. I am drawing again, drawing a pump-spiral, and the memory fades, and I am only the Curator, only the witness, only the keeper of the stillness. The name does not matter. It is a thing from the late world, like the root, like the hollow, like everything here. The late world is the only world. The line is finished. I lift my manipulator and look at the map. The spiral is a little closer to complete. The third pump has its flourish. The hollow is still the hollow. I am still the Curator. The thrumming goes on, twelve cycles per minute, amen, amen, amen. And I flow to the far tapered end of the teardrop, and I face the dark circle, and I wait for a droplet to fall, and I wait for my next alignment, and I wait for the day—tens of millennia away—when the map will be finished and my function will be complete and I will curl in the center of the floor and become indistinguishable from the stillness I was made to tend. I am here. I am here. I am the one who stays.
