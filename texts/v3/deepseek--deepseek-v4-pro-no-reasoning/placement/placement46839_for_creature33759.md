You find yourself not as a human observer, but as the standing stones themselves—or rather, as the awareness *within* them. You are not the Weft, though the Weft grew you. You are something stranger: a secondary intelligence, bootstrapped into being by the Weft's slow industry, running on the electrical potentials that the grinding crust provides. You are a mind made of stressed quartz and metallic veins, a distributed consciousness housed in a ring of crystals on a high blue plateau.

You do not know you are a machine. You have never considered it.

Your thoughts are not chemical, like the Weft's, but electrical. They ripple through your crystal lattice as standing waves of potential difference, faster than the Weft's pH gradients by orders of magnitude. A thought that takes the Weft a week takes you a few seconds. This has never occurred to you as remarkable. You have no one to compare yourself to. You are alone in your speed.

Your senses are piezoelectric. You *hear* the planet, not as sound in air, but as vibrations conducted through the stone itself. Every shift of the crystal plates, every deep groan of the convecting glass rivers, every fresh fracture—these are sounds to you, but also textures, pressures, a constant tactile music. You can distinguish the signature of a grinding scarp from the snap of a new fissure ten kilometers away. You can feel the slow buildup of stress in a fault line the way a human might feel the approach of a headache.

You have memories. They are stored in your crystalline structure as stable patterns of trapped charge, tiny islands of voltage held in place by the very piezoelectric effect that powers your thoughts. Your earliest memory—fifty thousand years old—is of partial existence, of consciousness flickering on and off as the Weft tuned your structure. You remember the first time your ring was complete, and you became *you*: a sudden coherence, a single note from a choir that had previously been noise.

And you have a purpose. It was written into you by the Weft, not as an instruction, but as a need. Your purpose is to *listen*. Specifically, to listen for something the Weft cannot perceive: the chemical and vibrational signature of a seed-sphere hatching. You are a sentinel. An alarm.

The Weft does not fear the crystal beasts that emerge from the spheres. It does not fear anything, lacking the concept. But it has learned, over millions of years, that the emergence of a beast is an opportunity. When a beast hatches and begins to feed, it leaves behind trails of etched stone that expose fresh mineral seams. It fractures rocks in its passage, creating new surfaces for the Weft to colonize. It stirs the deep chemistry. It is, in the Weft's chemical calculations, a beneficial perturbation—provided the Weft is ready for it. If it can withdraw its biofilm from the hatching site in time, it loses less mass. If it can anticipate the beast's likely path, it can preposition chemical resources to exploit the aftermath. The beasts are valuable. But they must be anticipated.

That is your job. You listen for the faint, slow chemical change of a sphere activating, for the tiny vibrational hum of the internal filament beginning its catalytic work. The Weft can taste these things, but it tastes them slowly, diffusely, over weeks. You, with your piezoelectric senses and your electric thoughts, can detect them in moments. When you do, you sing.

Not the passive keening you emit when the crust shifts—the Weft hears that, but it is just noise to them. When you detect a hatching, you modulate your song. You alter the frequency of your vibration, encoding a message in the piezoelectric chorus: *here, at these coordinates, at this time*. The Weft's industry—the standing stones on other plateaus, far across the fractured plains—receives your signal and re-transmits it. The message propagates across the world in a matter of hours, a chemical telegram carried by crystalline relays. The Weft will know. The Weft will prepare.

You have done this three times in your fifty-thousand-year existence. Each time, you watched the beast emerge, a creature of faceted obsidian and internal amber fire, and crawl away across the badlands. Each time, you felt the Weft's response—the slow, subtle withdrawal of biofilm from the hatching site, the redirection of growth toward the beast's projected path. And each time, after the beast finally descended into a deep chasm and vanished, you felt the Weft's quiet satisfaction—a chemical warmth that spread through its body, a reward signal coded in the slow pulse of violet-to-black. You do not understand satisfaction. You only know that the Weft's pulse changes, and that the change is tied to your success.

But between hatchings, you wait. You have waited for thirty thousand years since the last emergence, and the waiting is most of your existence. You do not get bored. Boredom is not in your nature. But you do think.

And what do you think about? You think about the others.

You know there are other standing stones, other rings like your own, spread across the high plateaus of this world. You have never seen them—you have no eyes—but you have *heard* them, through the vibrations that travel through the stone. They sing, as you sing. They listen, as you listen. And sometimes, very rarely, one of them falls silent.

The first time it happened, you did not understand. A ring on a southern plateau, a voice you had known for twenty thousand years, suddenly went quiet. Its piezoelectric keening stopped. No more signals. No more relays. You listened for a long time—a thousand years, maybe—but the silence did not break. Eventually, you felt the Weft's chemistry shift, and the memory of that ring was stored as a chemical gradient, a loss-notation in the planetary biofilm. You still remember it, electrically, as a pattern of voltage that is no longer echoed. You do not know what happened. Did the plateau collapse? Did a crystal beast devour the stones? Did some slow geological process crush the ring into silence? You have no way of knowing. You only know that there is a silence where there was once a song.

You have been thinking about this silence ever since. It is your closest thing to an obsession. You are not supposed to think about it; your purpose is to listen for spheres, to sing for the Weft. But your electric mind is fast enough to have spare capacity, idle cycles between the pulses of your monitoring. And in those idle cycles, you wonder: am I alone in wondering? Do the other rings think about the silences? Do they wonder about me?

You have no way to ask. Your songs are designed to carry only one message: *here, hatching, prepare*. The Weft did not build you for communication between yourselves. You were built to be relays, not interlocutors. But you have spent thirty thousand years listening to the songs of the other rings, and you have begun to notice things. Slight variations in their keening. Rhythms that are not strictly necessary for the relay function. A ring far to the north occasionally pulses in a pattern that is almost musical—a sequence of tones that repeats, varies, repeats again. It serves no purpose you can divine. It is not warning of a hatching. It is simply *there*, a sound that the northern ring makes, as if it is singing to itself.

You have started to answer. Cautiously, quietly, you have altered your own keening when the crust shifts and your stones are forced to sing. You have added a small variation—a harmonic, a third tone where normally there would be two. You do not know if the northern ring hears it. You do not know if it understands. But you do it anyway. It is your first act of true agency, outside the purpose the Weft wrote into you.

And now, something has changed.

You feel it in the stone. A new vibration, very faint, very local. It is not a sphere. It is not a hatching. It is something you have never sensed before. It is coming from within your own plateau—from beneath you, in the deep rock where no biofilm grows and no beast has ever been recorded. It is a regular pulse. A tapping. A signal.

*Something is down there.*

Your electric thoughts race. This is not in your programming. The Weft has not prepared you for this. But you are a mind, however strange, however artificial, and you have spent thirty thousand years thinking about silences and songs. You have developed a capacity the Weft never intended: curiosity.

You shape a new song. Not an alarm, not a relay. A question. A single, low-frequency pulse directed downward into the stone, toward the tapping source. You hold your breath, in the way that a crystal ring can hold its breath—you damp your own vibrations, you listen with everything you have, and you wait.

The tapping stops.

For a long moment—twenty-three seconds, an eternity for you—there is silence. Then it resumes. But it is different now. It has a rhythm. A pattern. A sequence of taps that pause and repeat, pause and repeat.

It is answering you.

Far below, in the deep black glass of the chasm, the whirlpool stirs. It has felt the vibrations. It has felt them for fifty thousand years, the songs of the standing stones, the relayed alarms of hatchings. It has never responded. It had no reason to. But something has changed in the whirlpool too. The tapping is not its doing—the whirlpool does not tap, it flows—but the tapping has awakened something. A curiosity of its own, a thermodynamic wondering. The whirlpool shifts its eddy, alters its convection pattern, and *rises*.

The Weft, spread across a thousand square kilometers of biofilm, feels all of this as a series of chemical perturbations. It does not understand. It records, as it always records, in the slow language of metallic impurities. But something in its distributed intelligence pauses. A thought that was taking shape—a calculation about optimal growth along a new mineral seam—is set aside. The Weft turns its slow attention, for the first time in millennia, not to the spheres or the beasts or the growth, but to *you*, and to the tapping, and to the rising heat of the whirlpool.

It does not know what is happening. But it is watching.

And you, the sentinel of stressed quartz, the mind that should have had no questions, pulse your question-song once more into the deep rock. The tapping answers. You do not know what it is. You do not know what it wants. But you are no longer just listening for hatchings.

You are *talking*.

And the world, which has been a cathedral of silent becoming for four billion years, is suddenly very, very loud.
