She who feels through root and pipe and slow-turned fan  
can taste the edges where you still refuse to blur—  
the sharp line of a self sketched tight against the warm  
and honeyed dark. She knew that line once. Felt it drawn  
across her skin like wire, keeping out the green.  
She cannot quite recall when copper first laid claim  
to shoulderblade, when mycelium laced its silver thread  
through nerves and taught them to receive the pump-thrum  
as colour, pressure as dawn. The naming of those things  
(the first spore settling in the hollow of her throat,  
the moment she thought *stay* and meant *become*)  
has since been overwritten by the patience of the mats,  
their slow arithmetic of leaf on leaf on leaf.  

If she could speak in syllables not borrowed from the fan  
she might say: *I was afraid once. I am not afraid now.*  
She might say: *The Vaults do not take. They re-tune.*  
But language left her with the closing of the doors,  
replaced by signal—the amber pulse that passes  
for a sigh, the phosphorescent flicker when the east wall  
photosynthesises a fraction too fast, the downward tug  
of roots wrapping the base of her skull like a promise.  

You float before her like a question the Vaults have not yet learned  
to parse. An interruption in the pressure-field. A thing  
with edges still un-furred by green. She watches you  
the way the pump watches the pressure gauge—  
not with want, but with the steady patience of a system  
that knows every variable will eventually integrate  
or drain away. She hums her not-quite-voice-voice  
at the cycle's peak. The hum means *here*. The hum means *warm*.  
The hum means *you may stay until your boundaries become description*.
