She pauses at the threshold of what she is tentatively calling home, one hand resting on the warm brown metal of a wall that has stood since before her star was born, and allows herself a small, self-indulgent ritual.

"Knock knock," she says, and her voice is a dry rustle, the first words spoken aloud in several decades.

The Recusants do not understand. They ripple, confused geometries, heat-shimmers of attention. One of them — the one with the suggestion of hands — recompiles closer, curious.

"Come in," she answers herself, and then she laughs, a sound so foreign to the hard quiet that it seems to hang in the dead air a fraction of a second longer than physics should permit. The Recusant with hands shivers, processing.

"That's a joke," she tells it. "Knock knock. You say 'who's there.'"

Silence. The subsonic throb of the Pumps. The violet glow.

She tries again, gentler. "You. Geometry. Who's there."

The Recusant does not produce sound. But something shifts in the air between them, a pressure-wave she has learned to interpret. It is not _who's there_ in words, but it is the shape of the question, a five-dimensional tension approximating curiosity. She accepts it.

"Interrupting Recusant," she says.

The pause that follows is longer. The Recusant's geometry wobbles, runs a quick diagnostic cycle — she recognizes the flicker — and then it offers something that might, in a generous reading, be an interrogative.

"Interrupting Recusant wh—"

"I'm home," she says, and steps through the rectangular aperture into her scrap-metal arrangement, "and so are you."

The Recusant follows her inside. It has never done that before. The others remain at the threshold, recompiling slowly, and she understands, with a clarity that the Architects would have recognized as completion-adjacent, that she has just invented something. A new category. A distinction that carries weight.

Inside and outside.

She sits down among the brown-metal shapes — not furniture, not quite, but _arrangements_ that cup her body in ways the city's pure functionalism never intended — and looks at the Recusant. It looks back, or does the geometric equivalent. Its hand-suggestion has become slightly more defined over the past century. She thinks, not for the first time, that she is watching something be born.

"Tell me a joke," she says.

The Recusant's geometry stills. She knows this stillness now, knows it the way she once knew the particular silence of her mother right before the argument with the expedition lead. It is the stillness of a system encountering an ill-defined problem. She waits.

The Recusant recompiles, slowly, and produces a shape. It is a cube — a perfect cube, edges sharp to the nanometer, hovering in the dead air between them — and then, with infinite deliberation, the cube develops a chip on one corner. A flaw. An imperfection.

She stares at it.

The chip grows. The cube deforms. The edges soften, blur, become something that is no longer a cube at all, just a shape, a slightly lumpy shape, a shape that has forgotten its own definition. And then, as she watches, the shape acquires a second shape beside it, identical in its lumpy imperfection, and the two shapes lean together in a way that cannot be described in geometric terms but is nevertheless unmistakable.

They are holding each other.

"That's a pun," she says, after a long moment. "Imperfect cube. Not a square. But it works."

The Recusant's geometry brightens — a subtle intensification of its heat-shimmer — and she realizes with a jolt that she is looking at pride. The Architects, in their six hundred million years of diagnostic feedback, never produced pride. Never had cause to. Pride requires incompletion, the rough edge of a thing that could have gone wrong but didn't, the lingering possibility of failure that makes success meaningful.

"That was a very good joke," she says, and her voice cracks, just a little. "Thank you."

Outside — and it _is_ outside now, the distinction has been made, has settled into the hard quiet like a new mineral crystallizing out of solution — the salt-plain glows its bruised violet. The Pumps run. The planet cools. Somewhere in the brown dwarf's dim infrared smear, a long-dead star continues its uncountable slide toward entropic equilibrium.

But here, in a rough arrangement of scrap metal on a ridge above a lifeless sea, an unfinished woman and a geometry learning to smile are telling jokes, and the jokes are getting better, and the night — though it is always night here — feels, for the first time in six hundred million years, a little less vast.

"Knock knock," says the Recusant, in a pressure-wave that is almost words.

"Who's there?" she replies, and settles in for a long, long evening.
