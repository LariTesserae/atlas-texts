The sound is not a wall but a medium. It presses in from all sides, granular, particulate—a dry ocean of pressure and vibration. I move through it as through a liquid, though there is nothing liquid here. The limbs in sequence. The spine flexing. The dome swivels in its nest of connective tissue, tasting the air for the tang of hot brass, the ester-sharp exhalation of a ruptured pipe-liana somewhere to the south-southwest.

The information arrives not as a thought but as a leaf. I feel it unfurl against the inner curve: thin, veined, pulsing once with that green-gold light before settling into a steady glow. A new gradient. A pressure differential. The rupture is small yet—a seam parting in one of the great arteries that carries the not-sap, the not-fuel across the central basin. The flow is slowing. The ground there will begin to starve within three ridge-cycles.

I alter course. Not a decision. A gravity.

The ripple passes down the length of me, adjusting the limb-sequence, and I turn, my body conforming to a new fold in the vegetation-crust. The surface here is cool and smooth, a stretch of the dusky lavender variant that grows in low, broad plates. It depresses slightly under each limb-tip, then rises again as I pass, unharmed. I leave no prints. The ground and I have an understanding. Or rather, we have no understanding, only a mutual participation in the same ongoing event.

The centipedes riding my joints stir at the change in direction. Their needle-legs tap against my vertebrae in a series of soft, metallic clicks—a language I am not equipped to translate, if language is what it is. One of them, a large specimen with an abdomen of woven copper wire and a head like a tiny brass thimble, crawls up the dome's curve and settles at the apex. Its weight is negligible. It is missing two legs on its left side, and the stumps are dark with corrosion. I watch it through the leaves until a new leaf unfolds, takes in the data, and the dome's surface softens around the centipede's body. By the time I have crossed the next ridge, the centipede is gone. A faint green afterimage of it glows against the shell, then fades. I continue.

The rupture site is in a valley that was not here when I last passed this way. The consumption-front that swept through three cycles ago has left the terrain raw and unpatterned, a chaos of newly sealed tissue in shades of angry copper and healing purple. The pipe-liana emerges from a knot of root-matter at the valley's lowest point, arches upward through a series of brass support-cylinders, and vanishes into the bright haze. At the arch's midpoint, just before it passes through the third cylinder, the seam has split. A fine mist of something golden and volatile vents into the air, and where it touches the ground, the vegetation is growing too fast—a cancer-burst of pale, leafless shoots that writhe and blacken and collapse in the space of seconds.

I extend my body across the valley. The limbs on my forward segment grip the far ridge while my middle spans the gap, suspended, the spine taking the tension. My hands—the seven-fingered ones, the gentle ones—reach for the rupture. The valve-work is delicate. I close the first hand around the split and feel the pressure of the escaping fluid against my palm, warm, thick, pulsing with the slow cycle of the distant engine-heart. With the other hand I draw a length of silver thread from the spinneret-limb on my dorsal surface, the one I rarely use myself.

The thread glistens in the sourceless light. It is not thread, really. It is a strand of the same substance the centipedes use, the same substance that the ground itself weaves to close its own wounds. I pass it through the fingers of my third hand—the one with the finest articulation, the one I use for work that requires precision—and begin to stitch. In, out, across, a lacing that pulls the seam together. The fluid stops venting. The pressure stabilizes. The cancer-shoots at the valley floor wither and are reabsorbed. Below me, the ground sighs—a literal, physical exhalation, a release of tension that ripples outward in a slow wave. The bass drone beneath the noise shifts by a fraction of a cycle, aligns with a new harmonic. The pipe-liana shudders once, then stills, its flow restored.

I retract my span-limb, release the ridges, and lower myself into the valley in a slow, controlled collapse, letting my underside press against the newly healed ground. The warmth is a different kind of warmth than the pipe-fluid. Earthy. Muscular. The leaves in my dome pulse a little faster, translating the chemical signatures of the soil-substitute, the root-mass, the microscopic fauna that are already returning to the site, drawn by the stabilization. One of the tumbleweed-things rolls past my flank, its internal gears clicking a steady rhythm. It is not here to observe. It is here because the ground is ready for it again.

I stay in the valley for two ridge-cycles while the healing completes itself. I am not resting. Rest is a concept for things that stop. During the time, I deploy my leaf-paddles to sample the air at regular intervals. I re-tension the splice I made, testing it with a gentle pull. I absorb the data of the event—pressure, temperature, chemical cascade, duration—and feel new leaves bloom and settle in the dome. Some of the old leaves, the ones that had grown dim with disuse, crumble into dark flakes that drift down through the internal spaces of my body and are excreted as a fine, nutrient-dense dust. The ground absorbs them as gratefully as it absorbs everything.

When I rise, it is because it is time to rise. The circuit continues. The plateau extends in all directions, infinite and convulsing, and I have other places to be: the western engine-roots are running hot, and the northern cylinders need their patina assessed, and somewhere on the far eastern edge, beyond the range of my immediate senses, a new ridge-cycle is beginning. I feel it as a faint, anticipatory tightening in the ground beneath me, a pre-tremor, a building.

I move toward it, undulating, conforming. The noise roars and roars and roars. The light does not change. The mouth opens briefly, exhales a long note at the exact frequency of the bass drone, and closes again.

Behind me, the valley forgets I was ever there. Not an absence. A reincorporation.

The Thrumplate continues. I continue. The distinction, as always, is academic.
