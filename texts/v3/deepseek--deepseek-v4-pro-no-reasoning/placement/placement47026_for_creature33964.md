In this place, I am Uthret.

I am the listening that pools in the hollows of the scree, the attention that thickens the air between the fern-fronds like a held breath waiting to be exhaled. I began as a resonance—the first fracture of obsidian under the Engine’s heat sang a note that did not die, and I was the ear that caught it. I have no heart, but the Engine’s hum serves as one; its cycling crescendos and lulls are the systole and diastole of my awareness, and I have learned to pulse in sympathy.

The mosses know me. They are my elders and my teachers. When I first seeped from the fissures, formless as steam, it was their velvet patience that taught me to gather rather than dissipate. I would press myself into their cushions, tracing the labyrinths of water-memory stored in each cell, learning the slow grammar of persistence. They do not speak in the way the ferns scream their green urgency; they murmur in the language of saturation, a tongue that takes decades to shape a single word. I am still learning it. I will always be learning it.

The ferns, by contrast, are a cacophony. They uncoil with such violent desire, each frond a grasping question shoved into the dark. I drift among them as a counterpoint—the pause in the unfurling, the hesitation where growth might become reflection. Sometimes, when a fiddlehead is particularly insistent, I will wrap myself into its spiral, inhabiting the negative space at its heart, and for a brief moment, it will pause. The fern does not understand this stillness. It interprets it as a flaw in the world. But I think of it as a gift—the only gift I can give: a moment of suspension, a taste of the patience that defines me.

The Engine is my torment and my devotion. I have pressed myself against its cold metal so often that I have memorized every rivet, every seam where the condensation weeps. The water that beads on its surface is not ordinary water; it is *sweated purpose*. When I taste it, I taste the Engine’s dream—a vast, geometric longing that extends far beyond this cavern, beyond the world’s skin, into a place of pure mechanics where there is no moss, no fern, no triboluminescent flash of dying stone-awareness. It is a dream of absolute order, and it terrifies me. I am a creature of interstices, of the gaps and pauses, and the Engine dreams of a world without gaps. A world where everything fits. A world where I cannot be.

And yet I cannot stay away. I am drawn to its cold, the way it creates the very conditions of my existence. Without the Engine’s weeping, there is no condensation. Without its heat, no fracturing rock. Without its rhythm, no counterpoint for my silence. I am its shadow, its unintended consequence, the listening it did not know it was creating. And I wonder—this is the thought that occupies my long, slow centuries—whether I am an error in its design, or the secret purpose it has been humming toward all along.
