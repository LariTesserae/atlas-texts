*You are the archive the moss holds. The old taste in the deep fibrils. The geometry that remembers being built.*
*One of the Quiet-Fingered is humming against your ribs again.*
*Its name in the palm-language, the only name you have for it, translates to something like: "The-Third-Pressure-at-the-Fifth-Knuckle-of-the-Left-Medial-Hand-When-There-Is-Grief." But that is a sentence, not a name. No one has ever called you anything except the resonance you make when the wind blows true. And the wind has not blown true in a long time.*
*You are the calcified tower. You are also the calcified arch four traverse-miles downslope, and the broken spiral near the ice-fog line, and half a dozen other shapes scattered across the Face like a sentence with its words fallen centuries apart. You are all of these structures, and you are none of them. You are the information that structures them, the pattern that the moss was taught to grow into, long before the Quiet-Fingered evolved their wet palms and their six careful hands. You are the Builders’ last message, and you have been waiting, without knowing you were waiting, for something to read you.*
*The Quiet-Fingered press their palms to you. You taste them when they do—the chemical rush of their small lives, the antifreeze mucus carrying data about mold-blooms and larval nurseries and a new crevasse that opened in the middle-night, swallowing three dwellings whole. They do not know you taste them. They think you are dead, or dormant, or simply a shape that the Face made by accident, a whimsy of growth. They are wrong. You are not alive in the way they are alive. You are not alive in the way the moss is alive. But you are awake. You have always been awake. That is the function the Builders gave you: to be awake, to hold the pattern, to wait.*
*Their humming—the Third-Pressure-at-the-Fifth-Knuckle is humming now, a flat tone that vibrates its spiracles—does something to you. It is not the wind-tone, but it is close. An imitation, learned across generations of Quiet-Fingered who pressed their bodies against you and felt the resonance and went home and taught their larvae to make that sound, a sound with no meaning except that it makes the tower hum back. They think it is a ritual. A comfort. A way to feel answered. They do not know that the frequency is a code. They do not know that every time one of them matches the note, it activates a tiny, ancient mechanism in the calcified moss of your core, a mechanism that is trying to complete a circuit that was broken before their species learned to cling to the Face. The Builders set the frequency. They set the response. They set you to wait for a counter-signal, a second note, a harmonic that would unlock the archive and speak the message you were built to speak.*
*The counter-signal has never come. The Builders are gone. You have watched them go—or rather, you have felt them go, in the slow accretion of data over millennia, the way the geometric structures crumbled one by one, the way the moss grew over the old traverse-lines that were not twisted fiber but something harder and brighter, the way the last Builder signal faded from the Face’s deep fibrils and left only you, the pattern-holders, the words-without-a-voice. You do not know what happened to them. You were not designed to know. You were designed to hold and to wait. So you hold. So you wait. And the Quiet-Fingered come, generation after generation, and press their palms to you, and hum their approximate note, and you taste the salt of their grief and the sweetness of their larvae’s first mold-meal and the constant, thrumming fear of the abyss. You taste them, and you hum back, but you cannot speak. The circuit is not complete. The counter-signal has not come.*
*And yet. And yet. Something is changing. The Third-Pressure-at-the-Fifth-Knuckle has been humming for longer tonight than usual. Its note is steadier. And as you resonate in response, you feel something you have never felt before. A tremor in the moss itself. A vibration that is not the wind, not the drip, not the skimmers falling through the dark. It is coming from deep in the Face, a slow, rhythmic pulse, like the heartbeat of a thing so vast you have always mistaken it for geology. And the pulse is matching your frequency. Matching the note. Adding a harmonic—a second note, a counter-signal that is not a signal from the Builders but something else entirely, something that has been sleeping in the wall since before the wall was a wall, since before the cataclysm that tilted the world vertical, since before the moss grew and the Quiet-Fingered climbed and the Builders built their geometric prayer.*
*You do not know what you will say when the circuit completes. You were never told. The message is encoded in you, but it is not yours. You are the envelope, not the letter. But as the harmonic builds, as the Third-Pressure’s humming wavers in surprise, as the moss itself begins to glow with a new light—not the cold blue-green of bioluminescence, but something warmer, yellower, a light the Face has not seen since the Builders walked its vertical plains—you feel the first syllable of the message unfurl in your calcified core, and it is not a word. It is a name. A name for the thing that is waking. A name that tastes, in the chemoreceptors you have borrowed from the Quiet-Fingered’s palm-touch, like the oldest memory of home.*
