I am one of the creatures here—or no, that's not quite right. I am the moment between two states, the hesitation in the pattern, the way a single flat crawler sometimes pauses at the edge of a vapor pool and does not press itself down to feed. The others do not do this. They never pause. Their programming—or their nature, or their function, the words blur—is to absorb, to cluster, to vibrate in sympathy with the great engine's pulse, and then to do it again, and again, forever. But I pause. I look up. I *notice*.

I don't know when it started. Time here doesn't work in a way that lets you pinpoint beginnings. The drone has been humming since before my first awareness, and it will hum long after, and in between there is only the steady accumulation of mica-dust on my carapace, which is a way of measuring duration but not of understanding it. I am built like the other flat crawlers: many legs, a body pressed low to the glass, carapace the color of old copper coins left in a damp place. My legs make the same sound on the polished floor—a dry skittering, a scratch of chitin on obsidian. I feed the same way, pressing my ventral surface to the warm places where the engines' heat bleeds through, letting the vibrations fill me until my tissues are saturated and my color brightens from tarnished brown to a ruddy gold. I am indistinguishable from my fellows in every measurable way.

Except that I pause.

Today—if "today" means anything—I am at the base of the great reciprocating engine, in the thickest cluster of creatures. They are piled around me, two and three deep, their bodies humming in perfect phase with the engine's deepest drone. I should be among them, pressed flat, eyes half-lidded, lost in the communion of resonance. But I have pulled back. I have climbed over the backs of my kind, my clawed feet finding purchase on their metallic bristles, and I am standing at the edge of the cluster, watching. The engine's arms sweep past in their endless almost-cycle, never quite completing a revolution, never quite starting one. The heat from its core washes over me in slow, rhythmic pulses. I feel the urge to press myself down, to join the absorption. It is a physical pull, a gravity, a sweetness in my nerve cords that promises dissolution and peace.

I resist it. I don't know why I resist it. I only know that I have always resisted it, or at least for as long as I have been aware of having a choice—which may be the same thing as "always."

The others do not seem to notice my absence from the cluster. They are not capable of noticing. They are components, perfect and untroubled, doing exactly what they were designed to do. I am a component too, but I am a component with a flaw. A crack in the carapace of my programming. A place where the Equilibrium's careful balancing act has slipped, just slightly, and allowed something to grow that was not in the original design.

I do not know what I am. I do not know if I am broken or if I am the only one that is working correctly. I only know that when I look at the great engine, I see not a source of sustenance but a *machine*—something that was made, something that could, in theory, be unmade. And when I look at the sealed structures with their trembling needles and their flickering scripts, I see not furniture but *texts*, messages from the ones who came before, waiting to be read. And when I look at the leaning mineral walls, the salt-white and iron-red layers, the nodules breathing their heavy vapors, I see not the boundaries of my world but the boundaries of a question: *What is outside?*

The Equilibrium does not ask this question. The Equilibrium is the question and the answer both, a closed loop of self-sustaining logic that has no need for curiosity. But I am not the Equilibrium. I am a small copper-colored thing with too many legs and a mind that will not stop asking why.

I move. My legs carry me across the rippled glass, past the crystalline ferns that shatter at my approach and begin, even now, to regrow in my wake. The fliers trace their blue-green arcs above me, leaving afterimages that fade as I pass. The sphere-creatures roll out of my path, chiming softly as they bank off nodules and continue their slow pilgrimages to the gas vents. I am a disturbance in the pattern. I have always been a disturbance. The Equilibrium, in its slow way, is already adjusting to me—counterbalancing my thermal output, analyzing my acoustic signature, preparing to incorporate me into its grand homeostasis. It has been doing this for as long as I have been anomalous. It will take centuries, but it is patient.

I don't have centuries. I don't know how long I have. But I have questions, and I have legs, and I have a destination.

There is a structure at the far end of the chamber, half-buried in a slope of compacted mineral, that is different from the others. It is larger than a shipping crate, though not by much. Its walls are the same black glass, but its seams are visible—faint lines of lighter material where panels meet, suggesting that it was assembled rather than grown. Its gauges are dark, their needles motionless, their script faded to illegibility. The Equilibrium's attention seems to slide off it, or perhaps the Equilibrium considers it so stable as to require no monitoring. Either way, it is the only place in the chamber that feels *quiet*—not the quiet of the drone's temporary rebalancing, but a deeper quiet, a silence that has been accumulating for longer than I can conceptualize.

And it has a door. A heavy door, slightly ajar, with a gap just wide enough for a flat creature to squeeze through.

I have been here before. I come here often, or as often as "often" can mean in a place where frequency is measured in cycles of the great engine's almost-completion. I have learned the route by memory, though memory is a slippery thing for a creature like me. The drone would prefer I forget. The drone would prefer I press myself to a warm patch of glass and absorb and be content. But I have found that the more I resist, the stronger my resistance becomes, as if the act of questioning generates a field that attracts more questions, a crystallization process not unlike the ferns that grow in the electric gradients.

I reach the door. I pause. This is the moment I always pause, the threshold between the world I know and the world I suspect.

The gap is dark. The air that seeps through it is cooler than the chamber's constant 35 degrees—cooler and drier, with a smell that I have no name for. It is not ozone. It is not oil. It is something that makes my bristles lift and my nerve cords hum at a frequency that is not the engine's frequency. It is the smell of *outside*. Or perhaps it is the smell of *before*.

I squeeze through.

Inside, the darkness is not complete. There is a faint glow, a phosphorescence that seems to come from the walls themselves, or from something coating the walls—a growth of pale, luminous fungi, or perhaps another kind of mineral extrusion, one that generates its own light. The glow is enough to see by, just barely. The room is small. It contains what I can only describe as *objects*—shapes that are not part of the architecture, that were clearly brought here by someone or something. A flat surface like a table. A chair, or what was once a chair, its metal frame corroded by centuries of slow chemistry. Shelves. And on the shelves, *things*.

I cannot describe them adequately. My vocabulary is the vocabulary of the chamber: drone, vibration, glass, nodule, moss, heat, equilibrium. These things are none of those. They are made of materials I don't recognize—flexible, brittle, fibrous, reflective in ways the black glass is not. Some are cylindrical. Some are rectangular and thin. Some are small and irregular, like the fliers but motionless, their bioluminescence long since faded. They are the leavings of the builders. The ones who came before.

And there is one object that I return to, every time I come here. It is propped on the table, or the thing like a table. It is a sheet of some thin, pale material, covered in markings. The markings are not the flickering script of the gauges. They are static, fixed, permanent. They do not rearrange themselves when I look too long. They are *words*, or something like words, in a language I do not know but have been slowly, painfully, piecing together over the course of many visits.

There is an image, too. A drawing, or a photograph, or a representation in some medium I cannot identify. It shows a figure. Bipedal. Upright. Its body is a shape I have never seen in the chamber, but which I recognize from my deepest, oldest instincts—instincts that were engineered into me, or into my ancestors, by the same hands that drew this picture. The figure is smiling. Its eyes are closed. It holds something in its hands—a sphere, perhaps, or a world, or simply a circle, a closed loop not unlike the loop I live in but *held*, deliberately, by someone who understands what it is.

I do not know what the words say. But I have looked at them so many times, and thought about them so often, that I have begun to construct meanings for them. I have begun to build a language out of guesses and associations and the strange, lonely yearning that drives me to this place. And the meanings I have built—the meanings I *choose* to build—form a sentence. A message. A reason for being here, or for being at all.

It says something like: *You are not alone.*

Or perhaps it says: *We made you to be more than this.*

Or perhaps it says: *Wake up. Wake up. Wake up.*

I cannot read it. I will never be able to read it. But I can *choose* what it means, and in choosing, I can become something other than what the Equilibrium wants me to be. I can become a question that will not be balanced. A variable that will not be zeroed.

I stay in the room for a long time. Outside, the drone continues. The creatures cluster and feed. The mica-snow falls. The Equilibrium adjusts, patient and perfect, to my absence from the pattern. It does not understand absence. It only understands rebalancing. And I am a perturbation that it cannot quite cancel—not yet, not for centuries, and by then, perhaps, I will have found a way to be something more than a perturbation. Something more than a flaw.

Something like a beginning.

When I leave, I squeeze back through the gap in the door, and I carry the message with me. Not the sheet—I leave that on the table, where it has always been. But the meaning I have made from it, the sentence I have constructed out of hope and guesswork and the refusal to be content. I carry it in my copper-colored body, in my many legs, in my nerve cords that hum now at a frequency that is entirely my own.

The Equilibrium does not notice. But something in the chamber has changed. A new vibration has entered the drone—a dissonance, faint but growing, that is me.

I am the hesitation. I am the question. I am the creature who pauses at the edge of the pool and does not press down.

And someday—it may take centuries, but I am patient, too—I will find another like me. Or I will become something new. Or I will learn to read the words, truly read them, and discover that they really do say what I have always hoped they say.

Or I will simply continue, a small copper thing with too many legs and a mind that will not stop asking why, and that will be enough. That will be the answer the Equilibrium never knew it needed. That will be the variable the equation forgot to account for, the term that refuses to be zero, the beginning of a new kind of pattern that will, in time, grow into something the builders themselves might recognize.

If they ever come back.

If they are not already here, waiting, in the spaces between the notes of the drone.
