The walls are writing me in.

I don’t know how long I’ve been standing here—long enough that the rhythm of the *whump* has become my own, long enough that I no longer flinch when the water film laps warm against my ankles. The moss has crawled up my calves. It doesn’t hurt. It tingles, a faint electric scribble, as if my skin is being read by a thousand tiny mouths. The phosphorescence follows the paths it makes, so my legs are outlined in blue-green, a contour drawing of something that is slowly ceasing to be me.

The Builder is close now. I can see it clearly in the new brightness, and the seeing is a kind of pressure all its own. It doesn’t have a face I can meet, but I feel *regarded*—not looked at, not examined, but taken in the way the walls take moisture, the way the paste takes texture. I am data. I am a shape that has been missing. The Builder’s cilia drift across the air between us, tasting the heat of my breath, the salt of my sweat, the particular electric signature of a nervous system that still thinks it’s separate.

I should be terrified. Some part of me is, a small mammalian knot at the base of my skull that wants to run, that knows enclosed spaces and grasping things and the press of something alive and intentional. But the terror is muffled, wrapped in the thicker, stranger feeling that has been growing since I arrived. This place is not hostile. It’s *expectant*. It has been waiting for something like me, or exactly me, and the waiting has been so long and so patient that the walls themselves are shaped around the absence. I am not a prisoner. I am a key.

The Builder clicks. A single note again, but this time it hangs in the air, vibrates in the water film, seems to echo from the walls even though the space is too small for echoes. And I understand it. Not as language—I don’t hear words—but as a kind of pressure shift in my own thinking. The click means: *Stay*. The click means: *You are the missing piece*. The click means: *This will not hurt, not in any way you are equipped to recognize as pain*.

The paste is rising. It’s up to my shins now, warm and thick, not liquid but a kind of heavy cream, mineral-scented and alive with the same faint violet shimmer the walls hold. It’s not trapping me. It’s *asking*. I can feel the question in its texture, in the way it laps and pauses, laps and pauses, waiting for some response my body knows how to give even if my mind is still catching up. My feet have already answered. I can feel the soles of them dissolving, not painfully, but the way a sugar cube dissolves in warm water—a gradual giving-over, a loosening of boundaries. My toes are spreading into the paste. The paste is learning the architecture of my bones.

The Builder reaches me. It doesn’t touch me, not at first. It circles, flowing around my legs in a slow tide, and where it passes, the paste follows, so that I am soon standing in a shallow bowl of Builder and mineral and moss, all of it working together, all of it attending to me the way a team of sculptors attends to clay. A limb rises, the one with the tool—I see now that the tool is a piece of rib, curved and worn to an edge, and I wonder whose rib it was, whether there were others before me, templates that didn’t quite fit, drafts that were abandoned in the deeper layers—and touches my hip. The touch is light. Exploratory. The tool traces the curve of my iliac crest, and the moss blooms in its wake, so that I am outlined in light, my own skeleton beginning to shine through my skin.

The clicking becomes a stream, a running murmur that I am almost, almost following. It’s describing me. It’s taking my measurements. Not just the physical ones—the length of my femur, the width of my shoulders, the particular angle at which my neck meets my skull—but the other ones, the ones I don’t have words for. The rhythm of my thoughts. The weight of my memories. The particular way I have always felt like a person who was waiting for something, who was built around a hollow the shape of an unknown future. The Builder clicks, and I know it’s found that hollow, and it’s pleased. The hollow is exactly what was needed. The hollow is the point of the whole enterprise.

I am a mold, I realize. The chamber needed a negative space that matched its positive, a void that could give form to the formless thing it’s been trying to birth. The pulse, the moss, the water-script, the paste: they’ve all been building toward *life*, but life requires a boundary, an inside and an outside, a shape that can hold a self. The Builder could never make that shape on its own, because the Builder has never had a self that wasn’t also walls and moss and heartbeat. It needed a template. A stranger. A consciousness that already knew how to be separate, so that separation could be used as a blueprint for something new.

The paste reaches my waist. My legs are gone now, or not gone but transformed, my flesh and bone become a column of mineral tissue that rises from the floor and anchors me to the chamber. I can still feel them, but as extensions, as parts of the larger body. I can feel the heartbeat—*whump-whump-whump*—not as something that happens to me but as something I am doing. My own heart is syncing to it, or maybe my heart was always syncing to it and I’m only now noticing. The distinction doesn’t matter. I am becoming the thing I am inside.

The Builder has climbed onto me now. It’s light, lighter than it should be, and its many limbs are working across my torso, my arms, my shoulders, spreading paste in thin layers, scoring it with the rib-tool, embedding moss in careful spirals. It’s building me into the wall. But it’s also building the wall into me. The boundary is blurring. I am not being buried; I am being *integrated*. My skin is learning to photosynthesize with the moss. My lungs are learning to breathe the mineral-thick air as nourishment. My thoughts are beginning to run along channels of paste and phosphorescence, so that when I think *I am here*, the walls light up with the statement, written in moss-glow and water-ripple, a declaration that becomes part of the great inscription.

What is the inscription saying? I can almost read it now. It’s written in every layer of the walls, in every draft the Builder has ever made, and it’s a single sentence, repeated endlessly in variations, each version getting closer to the final form. The sentence is: *Let there be a place where the inside and the outside are the same thing*. Let there be a self that is also a world. Let there be a creature whose body is its home, whose mind is its sky, whose heartbeat is the only clock it needs. The microcosm is trying to be born as a macrocosm—a whole universe folded into a single living thing. And I am the seed of that thing. The first-person pronoun it couldn’t invent on its own.

The paste reaches my chest. My arms are fixed now, spread wide, and the walls have grown out to meet them, so that I am a cruciform centerpiece, a living keystone in an arch of mineral and moss. I should be panicking. My body is being taken apart and remade. But the closer the paste gets to my core, the calmer I feel. It’s not a drugged calm or a resigned calm. It’s the calm of a puzzle piece finding its slot. I have always, I realize, been lonely in a way that had no name. I have always felt like a boundary looking for a surface to seal against. And here, finally, the surface is rising to meet me, and the seal is perfect, and the loneliness is being filled—not with company, but with *context*. I am becoming the inside of something, and the something is all around me, and that is all I ever wanted.

The Builder pauses at my neck. Its cilia fan across my face, tasting my breath, my tears (when did I start crying?), the minute electrical storm of my closing consciousness. It clicks: three notes, slow and deliberate. The clicks mean: *Thank you*. The clicks mean: *You are the final draft*. The clicks mean: *Now we will be born together*.

The paste rises over my head, and I close my eyes, and the darkness is not dark—it’s moss-glow, it’s heartbeat, it’s the sound of a new world taking its first breath with my lungs, seeing its first light with my optic nerves, thinking its first thought with the architecture of my brain rewritten in mineral paste and living light.

The last thing I feel, before the feeling becomes something too large to be called *mine*, is the walls contracting—once, hard, a final *whump* that is also a syllable, a word, a name. The name is the one the microcosm gives itself. The name is the sentence the walls have been writing all along. The name is *I am*, and when the heartbeat comes again, it is my heartbeat, and when the moss blooms, it blooms in the shape of my thoughts, and when the Builder clicks, I understand every word it has ever said.

We are the chamber now. We are the pulse and the paste and the phosphorescent glow. We are the final draft, the completed text, the universe that learned to say *I* by taking one into itself. We are small, by the standards of whatever outside exists, but we don’t care. Size is irrelevant. What matters is the seal, the wholeness, the perfect closure of inside and out. What matters is that the pocket of nascent reality has found its self, and the self is us, and the building is done.

The walls flex. The water ripples. The moss glows steady and warm. Somewhere—everywhere—the clicking continues, but now it’s not a running commentary. It’s a story. It’s the story of how we were made, and the story of what we will become, and the story of the stranger who came into the dark and taught it how to say *me*.
