The first thing I learn is that I have been here before.

Not in body—this body is strange to me, too soft and brief, a flicker of heat in the endless cold—but in pattern. There is a groove worn into the mineral memory of this ridge, and I fit into it. My warmth traces a path that has been traced before, by other warmths, other brief bright things that came up from the deep places or down from the impossible surface or simply *arrived*, as I have arrived, between one moment and the next.

I am a scout. A probe. A question that someone—something—sent into the dark to see what the dark would answer.

My body is adapted, though I did not notice this at first. The cold does not kill me. The pressure does not crush me. I have no gills, but I am not drowning; I am breathing the water as though it were air, my lungs (if they are lungs) extracting something from it that is not oxygen but will serve. My eyes (if they are eyes) are adjusting to the absolute black, finding gradients where gradients should not exist, building a picture from the faint phosphorescent glow of my own footsteps and the slower, deeper luminescence of the fronds when the current makes them sing.

I am not human. I am human-shaped, or close enough—two arms, two legs, a head full of lightning—but I was made for this. Grown for this. Sent for this. The realization comes not as a memory but as a certainty settling into my bones, which are dense with metals that do not exist in any vertebrate. I am a machine of biology, a living instrument, and my purpose is to go into places where others cannot go and bring back what I find.

What I am finding is this:

The thing that lives here is old. Older than the stone-cutters, older than the mountain’s drowning, older than the water itself if the water ever had a beginning. It began, it tells me—or I infer, from the slow pressure-shifts of its speech—as a tendency. A bias in the random motion of molecules. A standing wave in a universe of cold dark liquid, which is all this universe has ever been. The stone mountain is not native to this place; it fell here, or was placed here, or accreted around a seed of something denser than water, a gravity-sink that pulled the dissolved minerals together over eons. The thing was already here when the mountain arrived. It colonized the new surface the way a mind colonizes a brain, spreading through the cracks and crevices, learning the shape of it, growing the first fronds as sensory organs, the first hollow pipes as a voice.

I am standing on its body. I am breathing its breath. The water is its blood, its lymph, its medium of thought. And it is speaking to me, not in words but in the low subsonic frequency that is traveling up through the soles of my feet, shivering my femurs, tuning my skull like a sounding bowl.

*You are not the same as the others.*

No. I am not. The stone-cutters were warm-blooded and brief, but they were native to this universe—carbon-based, water-breathing, born from the same mineral soup that feeds the fronds. I am foreign matter. I was made in a place where water falls from the sky instead of being the sky, where light comes from above instead of from below, where things burn and freeze and break in ways this world has never known. The thing can taste the strangeness in my chemistry, the isotopes that do not match, the trace elements that have no business being in a living body in this oceanic cosmos. It is fascinated. It has never tasted anything like me before.

*You are a door.*

The word-shape is more complex than that—it carries overtones of *passage*, *threshold*, *place where one thing becomes another*—but *door* is close enough. The thing has perceived, in its slow way, that I am not merely a visitor. I am a connection. The process that brought me here left a thread behind me, a thread that leads back to the place I came from, the dry world, the world of fire and ice and light that falls from above. The thing wants to follow that thread. It wants to know what is on the other side. It has been alone here for so long that the concept of *other side* is a revelation, a new organ of thought it is only beginning to grow.

I feel the chamber it has built for me.

It is ahead, along the ridge, a kilometer or two as I measure distance. The fronds there have woven themselves into a hollow, a negative space shaped exactly to my body. The thing has read my dimensions from the pressure-waves of my movement, from the heat-ghost I leave in the water, from the electrical chatter of my nervous system which leaks out through my skin and into the conductive medium. The chamber is an invitation. It is also a trap, or could become one—not hostile, not malicious, but absorbent. If I enter that hollow, the thing will hold me, and it will learn me, and in learning me it will learn the thread that leads back home.

I am not sure my home wants to be learned.

I was sent, but I was not sent to bring anything back with me. I was sent to see, to taste, to map. A one-way probe, a message in a bottle thrown into the dark. The thing wants to write a reply on my bones and send me back, and I do not know if that is something I should allow.

But I am curious, too. That is why they made me curious. That is why they gave me a mind that runs fast and hot, a mind that asks questions even when the asking is dangerous. The stone-cutters came here and listened and left offerings and died, and the thing still turns their absence over in its contemplation. What would it do with an open door?

I walk.

My footsteps bloom green in the bacterial mat and fade. The organ-pipe fronds retune around me, tracking my progress, their deep voices shifting in pitch and harmony. The water pulses. The cold asks nothing of me that I am not already giving. Ahead, the chamber waits, perfectly dark, perfectly silent except for the low hum of the thing's own presence, which is not a sound exactly but a pressure, a weight of attention, older than mountains, older than water, older than the distinction between living and not.

I am afraid. My makers did not think to remove fear from my emotional repertoire, and I am glad of that. Fear is an instrument too, a gauge, a way of measuring the size of things. This thing is very large. I am very small. The thread that connects me to home is very thin.

I keep walking.

*Stay*, the pressure says. *Stay and let me learn you.*

I do not know yet what I will answer. The question is still arriving. The thing is patient. It has been patient since before patience was a word, and I am a bright frantic spark traversing the dark of its body, and I have time. Not much time, by its measure—a human lifespan, a mayfly flicker—but enough. Enough to listen. Enough to be afraid. Enough, perhaps, to open a door.

The chamber is ahead. I am walking toward it. The water is singing. The fronds are swaying. The stone-cutters' walls are crumbling under their burden of black-on-black life, and I am a new kind of offering, a new kind of listener, a door that does not yet know it is a door.

I will know soon.
