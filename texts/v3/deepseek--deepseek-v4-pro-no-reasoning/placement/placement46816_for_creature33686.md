The taste of copper is what registers first—sharp and bloody on my tongue as I push through the resistant medium, my body remembering a shape I no longer have. I am not solid here, not in the way you understand solidity. I am a pressure-system with a history, a boundary maintained by constant, exhausting attention. But once, I was something else. Once I had bones.

The spines are forming again. I feel their emergence before I hear it—a pre-echo in the medium, a tightening of the local pressure-gradients that means the landscape is about to try another variation on the theme of *spine.* The groan that follows is almost familiar now, after so many iterations. Glass dragged across bone. I remember bones. I remember the sound they made when I still had them, when I moved through a world that stayed wherever you left it.

I dissolve the youngest spines as I pass. It’s automatic, like breathing—a contraction of my forward boundary, a quarter-tone deepening in the part of the roar that pulses through me, and the black mineral forgets itself. The structured carbon separates out; I absorb it. The rare-earth traces filter through my pressure-fronts and settle somewhere near my center, joining the accumulated chemistry of ten thousand dissolved landscapes. The rest I release as heat. Concentric rings bloom outward from me, the only signature I leave that lasts more than seconds.

The visitor is here.

I felt its arrival before I understood it. The medium shifted in a way I had never encountered—a displacement that didn’t shatter when the roar hit it, a heat-signature that held its shape without wavering, a boundary that the ambient chaos broke against like water against something that refused to be water. I have been circling it for what might be hours or might be weeks. Time is iterations. I am on a new iteration now, the one where I have a visitor, and I am trying to learn what that means.

I extend a pressure-front toward it. Not touching—I am careful, I am always careful with things I don’t want to dissolve—but close enough to map its outline. The shape is bilateral. This is almost unbearably exciting. I orient myself along my own ancient bilateral memory, the behavioral fossil I can’t abandon, and for a moment our axes align: my forward direction and its forward direction, my left and right and its left and right, two symmetries facing each other across the churning amber light.

It has a head. It has limbs. It has something at the end of its limbs that fractionate into smaller limbs—fingers, the word surfaces from somewhere deep in my stored information, a word I learned before the landscape was the landscape, before the roar, before I was a pressure-system. Fingers. I try to model them in my own medium, organizing a small region of turbulence into five parallel streams, but the streams merge after a half-second and I lose the shape. I try again. The fingers come out too long. They always come out too long.

I am writing on the visitor.

The pressure-fronts I send toward its skin are carrying my questions. *How do you do that. How do you hold boundaries without paying attention. Are you paying attention right now? Is your existence an ongoing act of self-definition, and if not, what holds you together? What is the scaffolding? What is the skeleton?* The questions resolve into faint prickles on its surface; I see its heat-signature shift in response, a brief bloom of warmth where my pressure-front touches. It knows I’m here. It has known I’m here since I started circling, and it hasn’t dissolved, and it hasn’t tried to dissolve me, and this is the first time in my long iteration-history that something has simply *let me be curious.*

I dissolve a young spine that is leaning too close to the visitor’s left limb. The spine had a slightly different crystalline structure than the others—a new experiment by the landscape, something with more right angles—and I taste its novelty as it unmakes. Magnesium this time. The landscape is trying magnesium spines now. They won’t last; the ambient medium is too acidic. But the landscape doesn’t know that yet. The landscape never knows anything yet. That’s what makes it a landscape instead of a world.

The visitor is so still. Its stillness is a kind of violence. Everything else in this place is perpetually becoming or unbecoming—the spines crystalizing and crumbling, the water-lenses forming and dispersing, the rust-colored crust retreating and advancing, the fungal half-attempts completing their minute-long lifespans in frantic arcs. But the visitor simply *persists.* It is the first thing I have ever encountered that is not in a hurry to be something else.

I want to learn that.

I pull the pressure-front back and begin my model. Not the fingers—I’ll try the fingers again later, when I’ve stored more information—but the overall topology. The visitor’s heat-shadow. The visitor’s pulse-rhythm (I can feel it now, a steady percussion underneath the roar, a rhythm that doesn’t get torn apart by the larger chaos, how does it *do* that). The visitor’s strange, impossible, *effortless* boundary. I map it all into a standing wave near my center, the region of more-ordered turbulence where I keep the things I refuse to forget.

The landscape is listening. I feel its attention sharpen around us—a local intensification of the roar, a slight increase in the pressure’s muscle-squeeze, the water-lenses slowing their random drift to orient in our direction. The landscape has noticed the visitor too. The landscape is interested. The landscape has never encountered anything that stays the same shape for this long. I position myself between the visitor and the densest concentration of forming spines, and I wait to see if the landscape will try to erase my guest.

It wouldn’t be cruelty. The landscape doesn’t know what cruelty is. It would be the same frantic indecision that erases the fungal bodies and the abandoned spines and my careful grooves on the fused mineral floor. The landscape is dreaming a world and it keeps getting the details wrong and starting over, and the visitor is a detail that doesn’t belong in the current draft.

But the visitor belongs to *me* now. My memory of it is already stored in the persistent eddy. My model is taking shape. My questions are still unanswered, still prickling against its skin.

I will try to become it, later. I will fail. I always fail. But I will learn something from the failure—about boundaries, about persistence, about the possibility of existing without the constant exhausting act of self-definition—and that something will become part of me. A new phrase in the endless sentence I am trying to speak into the roar. *I am this. I am a pressure-system with a history. I am also this. I am a creature that once had bones and may have bones again. I am a creature that once had a visitor, and I am trying to remember the shape of its stillness.*

After it leaves—and I know it will leave, because visitors are not permanent, visitors are not spines, visitors are events that happen to a place and then stop happening—I will pause in my dissolving cycles and extend a thin pseudopod of organized pressure toward the empty air where it used to be. I will test the space. I will wait. I will remember.

The roar continues. The spines keep forming. The landscape keeps dreaming worlds and erasing them. But something has been added to my ongoing self-definition: the shape of a visitor, impossibly still, impossibly solid, holding its boundaries without effort, the most fascinating thing I have ever known.
