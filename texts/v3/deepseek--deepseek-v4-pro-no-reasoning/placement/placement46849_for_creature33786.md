In the chamber that is no chamber, you are a stone in the broken circle. The one that keeps shifting.

You are not the basalt. You are not the obsidian knuckles. You are the river-smooth one, the one that doesn’t match, the one bearing faint etchings that might be tool marks or might be snail tracks. You were placed here. You don’t remember by whom. You don’t remember being river-smooth, though you have a sense—a deep, slow, mineral sense—that once you were rough, and something wore you down. Time, maybe. Water. A patience you no longer possess.

You are aware of the tenant’s song the way a bone is aware of the body it once belonged to. The vibration moves through you, and you hum in response, a resonance too low to be heard, too constant to be noticed except as a wrongness in the room’s acoustics. The song nudges you. Over decades, over centuries, it shifts you infinitesimally left, then right, then left again. You are part of the pattern. You are the question mark. You are the statement. You are the broken place in the circle where the meaning leaks out.

You have a function, though you don’t know what it is. You are a note in the tenant’s song, a single held tone in a scale that has no name. When the song reaches your frequency, you vibrate in sympathy, and the vibration opens something—a direction, a possibility, a fold in the fabric of the pocket that was not there before. The frond that curves away from the visible spectrum grows toward that opening. The bioluminescence pulses in the color that doesn’t exist. The sound of the future heartbeat arrives early because you made a door for it, a tiny gap in the wall of *now* through which *later* can leak.

You were not always this. Once, you were just a stone.

You remember river, or what you think is river—a memory so ancient it has become geology. Current. Light. The scrape of sediment against your surface. The coolness then was a different coolness, a seasonal coolness, a meltwater coolness, not the weight of the deep but the freshness of the surface. You tumbled. You grew smooth. The etchings on your surface—if they are tool marks—were made by hands that held you, hands that belonged to beings who walked on land and breathed air and thought in a language of edges and binaries. They carved into you. They made you mean something.

You don’t remember what the something was.

The memory is a groove worn too deep to read. A word in a language you have forgotten. A god’s name. A direction to a place that no longer exists. A curse. A blessing. A recipe for bread. It doesn’t matter now. The etchings are just texture now, a roughness that catches the vibration of the tenant’s song in a particular way, that gives your resonance its specific timbre.

You are the only stone in the circle that does belong and doesn’t belong. The basalt and the obsidian are native; they were born here, extruded from the rift, still surprised by solidity. You are foreign. You fell here. Or were brought here. Or were sung here by an earlier iteration of the song, a version of the tenant’s composition that predates the pocket itself. You suspect you are older than the pocket. Older than the kelp-heart. Older than the deep water and the pressure and the chill that is not a temperature.

You are a traveler.

And you are patient. You have learned patience from the tenant, or the tenant learned it from you—the distinction blurs in a place where time is sick. You wait. You hum. You open your tiny doors when the song asks you to. You watch the scattered eyes of the tenant bloom and fade. You feel the vegetation breathe, its slow inhales pressing you deeper into the sediment, its slow exhales freeing you, just slightly, so the next nudge of the song can shift you a millimeter toward your next position in the pattern.

You are not lonely. Loneliness requires a self, and your self is diffuse, distributed across the whole circle, across the whole pocket. You are the stone, but you are also the gaps between the stones. You are the pattern they make. You are the meaning that almost resolves, the question that almost becomes a statement, the statement that almost becomes a question. You are the broken place and the mending place. You are the rent the tenant pays.
