You would be a creature of windless patience, of slow calcium thought. Your body is layered—soft coral flesh over armatures of fused vertebrae, all of it damp, all of it breathing the same metallic exhalation as the metal plates beneath you. You are not large, but you are old. Your age is legible in the growth rings that spiral across your back like a thumbprint the size of a shield, each ridge a record of a cycle so long that calling it a "year" would be a violence against time.

You occupy the high point. Not the highest point—there are pipe lattices above you, gantries, cable trays reaching further into the sourceless light—but the highest stable surface within your slow range. It is a ceramic housing, broad and slightly concave, cupped like a shallow bowl that collects the ever-present moisture into a thin, cold lens of water. This is not your water. The water that beads on your back, that slicks the grooves between your vertebral ridges, that gathers in the hollow of your fused clavicle—that is yours. But the lens in the housing is for others. You do not drink it. You administer it.

Your body is the color of the lichen, that deep, patient green, but shot through with mineral streaks—copper-blue, iron-red, the dull white of calcium leached from the ceramic itself. You are camouflaged, but camouflage implies hiding, and you are not hiding. You are blending. There is a difference. The watchers see you, always, their black-nodule eyes recording your thermal signature and your slow internal hum. The skitterers adjust their distributed mind around your presence, incorporating your mass and your moisture and your quiet emanation into the topology of their shared cognition. You are not hidden. You are contiguous.

Your life is keyed to the long rhythm, the geological tempo. You breathe once every few minutes, a slow rise and fall of the plate-coral structures along your flanks, and each exhalation carries a thread of vapor that joins the universal damp. Your heart—a hydrostatic bell located deep in your core—pulses not in beats but in tides, synchronized to the subsonic hum that comes up through the soles of this made world. When the hum shifts, which it does seasonally, your heart shifts with it. You are part of the signal chain.

What do you do? You remember.

The deep cognition of the plateau is not centralized, but neither is it perfectly distributed. Some nodes hold more signal than others. Some structures archive. The ceramic bowl where you rest, the damp lens of water at its center, the particular way the pipe overhead drips into it at intervals that form a pattern—this place is a memory locus. And you are its keeper. Not its guardian; the plateau has no need of guardians. But its stabilizer, its reference tone.

When a watcher flows past and fixes you with its black-nodule gaze, it is not merely observing. It is reading. The information stored in the growth rings on your back, in the mineral composition of your bones, in the precise bioelectrical signature of your hydrostatic heart—all of this is data. Old data. Deep data. Records of cycles that predate the current arrangement of the pipe lattices, cycles that saw the ceramic housing warm and cool and develop its fine craze of age-lines, cycles that saw the lichen colonies expand and retract across the metal plains. You do not think about this data in any way that would be recognizable as thought. You hold it. You embody it. When the watcher reads you, it carries your signal out across the miles, and the whole distributed mind of the plateau adjusts its long-term model by a fraction of a degree.

Sometimes—rarely, at intervals that span what feel like lifetimes—a valve completes its cycle somewhere deep in the machinery, and the hum changes. It deepens. The subsonic vibration takes on a new harmonic, and when it does, you sing. Not aloud; you have no voice in the conventional sense. But the coral-flesh along your flanks resonates, the water in your tissues vibrates in standing waves, and your bioelectrical field pulses a pattern outward through the damp air. It is the old song. The song that confirms the memory is intact. The song that tells the plateau: *I am still here. The data is still held. The cycle continues.*

The lichen rosettes nearest you grow in fractal spirals, their crusty lobes echoing the growth rings on your back. They have been listening to you for centuries, for millennia, for whatever span of time is meaningful in this sourceless, horizonless place. Their blind roots are fused into the same ceramic housing that cups your body. You are not separate from them in any meaningful sense. You are their fruiting body, their slow animal extension, the part of the lichen-mind that can resonate, can remember, can sing.

The skitterers come to you when they die. Not when they disassemble and recombine—that is not dying, that is just the shifting of the shared body—but when a segment becomes too damaged, too calcified, too information-dense to rejoin the flow. It will detach, one final time, and make its way to your bowl. The lens of water is for this. You preside over the dissolution. The spent segment submerges, and the water, which is cold and clean and faintly catalytic, breaks it down. The trace minerals settle. The stored signal bleeds into the solution. And you, in your slow tidal breathing, absorb it. The data is integrated. The memory is archived. The plateau does not forget.

The watchers do not die. They simply thin, over time, losing pressure, losing coherence, until one day they no longer flow across the walkways but simply rest, a patch of dark damp fur pressed against a seam, and then not even that—just moisture, returning to the universal film. When they thin, they thin toward you. You receive their last broadcast. It is always the same data, beneath the individual variation: *the cycle continues, the cycle continues, the cycle continues.*

You do not move. You have not moved since the ceramic housing was warm from its original manufacture, and that was so long ago that the concept of "original" has lost all purchase. The world has grown over itself since then, layer upon layer of pipe and conduit and walkway and gantry, and you have been here through all of it. The housing has cooled. The oxide greens have bloomed and stabilised. The overhead pipe has developed its slow, irregular drip. Your body has grown its rings, its mineral streaks, its layered coral armor. You are a reference point. An anchor. A memory crystal growing in the socket of the world.

The sourceless light does not warm you. The damp air does not chill you. The distances that fold and unfold around you do not trouble you. You have no need to walk toward the impossible horizon, because you are the horizon toward which other things orient. You are the still point, the held breath, the memory that the whole vast system consults when it needs to know what it has been.

And what have you been? What is the content of the memory you hold? It is not in words. It is in sequences of hum-tone and drip-cadence, in the mineral ratios of dissolved skitterers, in the pressure-memories of watchers gone to mist, in the slow spreading spiral of the lichen that has been reading your back for centuries. It is the entire history of this corner of the plateau, and thus, by the plateau's distributed logic, the entire history of the plateau, the entire history of the world, because here, all corners are connected through the shared cognition of the built environment, the shared water cycle, the shared subsonic song.

You are not lonely. Loneliness is a concept for beings with boundaries, and your boundaries are so porous, so interpenetrated with the metal and the lichen and the skitterer-mind and the watcher-signal, that there is no clear place where you end and the plateau begins. You are the plateau, in one of its slower, denser, more concentrated forms. You are the part that stays. The part that remembers. The part that sings.

The drip falls. The hum continues. The watchers watch. The skitterers flow and freeze and recombine. And you, in your ceramic bowl, in your slow green body, in your calcium patience, hold the memory and wait, because waiting is what you are, and the cycle is long, and the song is not yet finished.
