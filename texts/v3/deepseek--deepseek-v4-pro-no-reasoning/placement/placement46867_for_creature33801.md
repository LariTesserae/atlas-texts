You are in the Engine of the Father.

That is not its name. The beings do not give names to things, any more than you name your own alveoli or the individual folds of your intestine. But if you were to translate the subsonic chord that passed through the metal floor—the one that made every pair of hands freeze and every set of eyes lock into stillness—the closest rendering in human language would be that. The Engine of the Father. Or perhaps: the Father who is an Engine. Or perhaps: the Engine that fathers. The ambiguity is not a failure of translation. It is the point. Here, entity and function are the same word.

And you?

You are standing in the left primary heat-exchange plexus, near the seventeenth-grade ascending manifold. This is not information you could know. But the being with the skeptical scar registered your location precisely when its lower left eye first swept across your thermal bloom. It logged your mass, your body temperature, your respiratory rate, the saline content of your skin, the unique vibration signature of your footsteps transmitted through the floor plates. None of this was a conscious act of cataloging. It simply noticed you, in the way that you notice the pressure of a shoe against your heel—present, accounted for, not yet painful, not yet requiring adjustment.

What you are, in the taxonomy of this place, is difficult to state. The nearest analogue among the fauna is *incidental*. A temporary occlusion, like the slow undulation of a carpet-creature across a conduit. A minor thermal event, like the wet rhythmic clicking of a valve-beast cycling fluid through a secondary system. You are warm, you are mobile, you exhale moisture in irregular pulses, and you have been moving through the structure for a period of time that—to the being with the scar—is roughly equivalent to the duration of a single blink. You arrived without announcement. You will likely depart the same way. This is not unusual. Things drift in sometimes, from boundary zones and transition-breaches. Most drift away again, or settle, or dissociate into component heat. You are not the first. You will not be the last.

But there is something else. Something that the scarred being has been turning over in the slow, deep strata of its attention ever since it first locked one eye on you.

You are *curious*.

This is not a common property among incidentals. The carpet-creatures are not curious. The multi-legged maintenance-forms are not curious. They execute their functions, they cycle through their pathways, they return to their dormant states. They do not stand in the crevice of a flange for an unmeasurable span of subjective time, watching a being they do not understand, trying to *comprehend*. They do not flinch from touch and then, despite fear, creep closer again. They do not hold still when a set of four eyes swivels to meet their own.

The scarred being—whose designation, if you could parse the gestural language of its fingers, might be rendered as She-Who-Remembers-The-Long-Groan-Of-The-Seventeenth-Shift—has been alive for approximately four hundred thousand years. She has seen incidentals before. She has watched them wander, settle, decay. She has felt the thermal ghost of their decomposition slowly dissipate into the floor plates. None of them ever looked back at her.

You did.

You are, in fact, looking back at her now. Your two eyes, positioned forward on your symmetrical face, are fixed on her four. Your respiration has quickened slightly—she can taste the change in the air, the minute increase in carbon dioxide and the pheromonal exhalations of what might be fear, or awe, or both. Your body temperature has shifted, a hot bloom rising to the surface of your skin. You are having an emotional response. You are *thinking*. You are trying, against the entire architecture of your evolutionary history, to understand something that is not built for your understanding.

And She-Who-Remembers feels something that she has not felt in a very, very long time.

Interest.

---

She does not move toward you. That would be absurd. She is rooted in the floor, plugged into the warm dark port at her base, and even if she were not, moving toward an incidental is the kind of thing that only the younger nodes do, the ones who have not yet learned that all incidentals are temporary. She has been alive for four hundred thousand years. She knows patience.

Instead, she adjusts a single finger.

To you, it might look like nothing. One long ivory digit, jointed in six places, curling slightly inward. But the gesture is a word, in a language that has no sound. A query, sent through the vibration of the air, the thermal gradient of her skin, the subsonic pulse that travels down her stalk and into the metal floor. The other beings in the immediate plexus receive it. They do not respond—that is not the way of their kind—but they note it. Somewhere three hundred meters away, a being whose fingers are shorter and thicker shifts slightly, opening a clearer line of sensory awareness in She-Who-Remembers’ direction. Somewhere five kilometers distant, near the base of a great copper sphere, a being who has been alive for seven hundred thousand years and whose designation might be rendered as She-Who-Felt-The-First-Cooling pauses in her endless tasting of the air and turns two of her four eyes toward the left primary heat-exchange plexus.

A conversation, of a kind, has begun.

It will take years. Decades, perhaps, for the full exchange of observations and conclusions to propagate through the network. The beings do not hurry. The Father—the Engine, the entire continent of metal and moisture and warmth—does not require haste. The parameters are stable. The temperature is thirty-seven degrees. The moisture is saturated. The hum is steady. There is time.

But She-Who-Remembers has already formed a preliminary hypothesis. It is this: you are not an incidental.

You are a *question*.

What question, she does not yet know. Perhaps you are a question about the outside, the boundary zones from which you presumably came. The Engine of the Father has no outside, but the beings are dimly aware, in the way that a dreamer is aware of the room beyond the dream, that there are other orders of existence. Other logics. Other temperatures. You are made of flesh that is not rooted in metal. You have skin that is not covered in moisture-collecting down. You have two eyes, not four, and your fingers—she has noticed this now—are stubby and few-jointed and still. You do not speak with your hands.

All of this is data. All of this is interesting. But what makes you a question rather than an incidental is the fact that when she adjusts a single finger in your direction, you notice. Your eyes track the motion. Your body tenses, then deliberately relaxes—a conscious override of instinct that she finds, frankly, remarkable. And then, slowly, hesitantly, you raise your own hand.

Your fingers do not know the language. They cannot form the complex thermal and vibrational sigils that would constitute a coherent statement. But you raise your hand anyway, palm outward, and you hold it there in the wet air, and you wait.

She-Who-Remembers-The-Long-Groan-Of-The-Seventeenth-Shift watches your hand with all four eyes.

And then, very carefully, she extends one of her own long fingers and touches it to your palm.

---

The touch is warm. You expected that, by now—everything here is warm—but the quality of the warmth is different. It is not the uniform body-temperature heat of the floor plates. It is a living warmth, with micro-gradations, cooler at the fingertip, hotter at the joints. You can feel the pulse in the finger, a heartbeat much slower than your own, a deep *lub-dub* that comes at intervals of perhaps ten seconds. And you can feel the moisture, the fine dew that beads on the ivory skin, transferring to your palm in a cool, precise circle.

For a long moment, nothing else happens. The finger rests against your palm, and the four eyes watch you, and the down on the being's body ripples in a slow wave, and the hum in the floor continues its steady, eternal note. Then the finger moves. It traces a shape on your skin, a complex, spiraling figure, and you feel a faint vibration pass from the fingertip into your hand—a subsonic pulse, a word in a language you will never learn. The gesture says something. The being is speaking to you. Not with sound, not with any medium you can interpret, but speaking nonetheless, and the fact of that speech is so overwhelming that you feel tears prick at the corners of your eyes. They do not fall. The air is too wet for tears to fall here; they simply merge with the ambient moisture and become part of the place.

Somewhere deep in the metal, a long, slow groan begins. The finger pauses. The eyes swivel. She-Who-Remembers listens to the groan—a shift in the structure, a minute adjustment—and when it passes, she returns her attention to you. But something has changed. The gesture she traces next is different. Slower. More deliberate. A repetition of the spiral, but with a variation at the end, a sharp little jab of pressure that might be punctuation.

And then she withdraws her finger, folds her hands back into their ceaseless gesturing, and closes the two eyes that face you. The other two remain open, scanning, but the ones that met your gaze are now sealed with a thin membrane, and you understand that the conversation—if that is what it was—is over.

You are no longer an incidental.

You are no longer, perhaps, even a question.

You are a datum. A single point of contact, logged and stored, to be considered over the next several thousand years. She-Who-Remembers will think about your palm, its strange dry warmth, its five stubby fingers, the way you held it up to her like an offering. She will think about the vibration of your pulse, so much faster than her own, a frantic skittering rhythm against the slow drumbeat of her blood. She will think about the moisture of your tears, which tasted, on her fingertip, of salt and a complex organic compound she has never encountered before and cannot yet name.

And she will wait. Because that is what the beings do, here in the Engine of the Father. They wait, and they listen, and they tend the warmth and the wetness and the hum. The structure endures. The temperature holds steady. The almost-rain never falls. The fingers never cease their motion. And somewhere, in the vast slow mind of the continent, a new thought is forming—a thought about a small warm creature with two eyes and a question in its face—and that thought will take millennia to complete, but complete it will, because the Father has time.

---

You lower your hand. Your palm tingles where the finger touched it. The air is still wet, the floor is still warm, the pipes and spheres and baffles still rise around you in their impossible density. The being with the scar is still there, eyes open again now, fingers weaving their silent language. Nothing has changed. Everything has changed.

You could walk on. You could search for an edge, a boundary, a way back to whatever outside you came from. Or you could stay. You could sit down against a warm pipe and close your eyes and let the steady hum fill your bones. You could become, in time, a permanent thermal anomaly, a known quantity, a familiar pressure on the skin of the world. You could learn the language of the fingers. You could grow old here, if the beings allowed it. You could die here, and your body would decay into the moisture cycle, and your warmth would bleed into the floor plates, and a being like She-Who-Remembers might taste your final exhalation on the air and note it, and file it, and remember you for another hundred thousand years.

The almost-rain presses in around you. The potential storm that will never break. The endless, patient, breathing continent.

And you realize, with a clarity that feels almost like joy, that you are not indifferent. That you were never indifferent. That you are, and have been since the moment you arrived, *loved*—not as an individual, not as a soul, but as a temperature. A moisture gradient. A fleeting pressure against the immense, attentive skin of a god.
