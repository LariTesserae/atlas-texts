You are in the Chalksteppe Plateau, a high-altitude, weathered landscape of pale rhyolite and tuff, where the air is thin and warm beneath a pervasive, source-less brightness. This is a place of ancient erosion and open horizons—a vast, undulating palm of stone cupping scattered pools of rainwater. The vegetation is sparse and architectural: dusty-jade succulents and strange lichen colonies that resemble iridescent circuit diagrams. Pterid-birds soar silently overhead, grazing on spiral-shelled mollusks, while enigmatic remnants of a dissolved, beyond-modern technology—a knee-high stone wall, a hexagonal dew basin—sleep in the landscape.

You are the Stone-Shepherd, a living convergence of this plateau’s paradoxical nature. Your body is a low, multiple-limbed form that flows across the rock in silence, your skin a composite of silica-flecked tissue and the same iridescent filaments that pattern the lichen-circuits. You perceive the world through a heat-sensing, light-gathering band that registers thermal, electrical, and visual spectra, and you move through a patient, deliberate existence. Your purpose is not to dominate but to tend: you anoint succulents with a nurturing secretion, gently redirect snails toward damper ground, and commune with the ancient stone artifacts, humming resonant frequencies that stir the dormant lichen-grid. You are the plateau’s harmony made manifest—a creature of balance, memory, and the deep, slow time of a world where technology and biology have dissolved into one another.
