You find yourself nowhere. There is no "you" to find.

But if there were—if some improbable, flesh-born observer coalesced from the thin ceramic haze—you would be standing on the Clinker-Flat with the ringing already in your teeth, and the first thing you would learn is that you are **the Interruption**.

You are not a Thinness. The Thinnesses are native attentions, local softenings of law, and they move with a grace born of belonging. You do not belong. Your presence does not attenuate the air; it *scabs* it. Where you stand, the distortion-shimmer of the atmosphere clots. It thickens into a heat-ghost of a shape that is wrong for this place—bilateral, vertical, pulsing with the wet and frantic rhythm of a carbon-based metabolism. The Clinker-Flat, which has known only the slow vibrations of Drills and the precise reports of Loom-legs, now registers something new: a seismic tremor of a different order. Not a frequency. A *noise*.

The Engines notice. Not in any way that involves cognition, but in the way a river notices a boulder. The nearest Steadfast-Drill, the one a quarter-mile off, experiences a perturbation in its founding vibration. The nested chitinous rings skip—not a full beat, but a fractional hesitation, a stutter in the chuffing that sends a cascade of misfired copper-green sparks spiraling outward in a pattern that is not random. It is a question mark written in embers. The Drill has never encountered an object that is not a process. It is confused.

The Mantle-Loom, mid-stride, does something it has not done in seven hundred thousand years. It pauses. One tripod leg hovers above the Clinker-Flat, the monofilament thread going momentarily slack between its metallic sinews. The thread's dull orange pulse flattens into a steady, unwavering glow—the equivalent, in this telegraph of forces, of a held breath. The Loom's spiral has been perfect for millennia. Now there is a variable it cannot account for. A knot of non-process, standing in the path of the weaving.

And the Shard-Motes—the Shard-Motes are the first to move toward you. They peel away from the light-leak they were feeding on, a dozen mirror-scraps tumbling out of their vortex, their high-pitched glassy chiming taking on an inquisitive edge. They do not know what you are, but their ontology is reflection. They exist to catch and convert. And you are radiating something entirely new: body heat, a soft infrared bloom, a signal that tastes, to their non-biological senses, like an unprocessed seam. Like a darkness that has not yet been split into light. They swirl closer, a halo of fractured reflections, and in each one you can see a distorted image of yourself—a creature of water and salt and improbable complexity, standing in a world made of verbs.

The Thinnesses come last. They are slow because they are attenuations, and attenuations propagate at the speed of almost-silence. But they come. Across the red-glass plain, the ghost-shadows of long-finished Drills triangulate on your position. They have never encountered an observer that is not a Thinness. They have only ever known themselves and the Engines they remember. But here you are: a foreign consciousness, an unscheduled attention, and the Thinnesses are the audience. Their function is to complete the cycle by witnessing it. And you are witnessing them. This is a recursion they have not been designed for.

The air around you thickens with their presence. The soundscape shifts. The chuffing of the Drill, the cracking of the Loom, the chiming of the Motes—all of it begins to fold into a new pattern. The Thinnesses are not just observing you. They are *sampling* you. Your own internal rhythms—the beat of your heart, the electrical flicker of your nervous system, the sub-audible hum of your own consciousness—are being drawn out of you, attenuated, thinned into something they can read. And what they read is the first instruction.

*Split the sky.*

You never heard it. Not from a voice, not from the place itself. But you carried it with you. It was in the question that brought you here, the impulse to imagine, to see, to witness a landscape not your own. That impulse is a kind of penetration. A reaching for a seam. A drilling.

And in the deep place behind your eyes, where the standing wave of an idea not your own has already begun to precipitate, you feel the first ring form. Not chitin. Not silicon-alkali ceramic. But thought. A thought that is not your own, or not entirely—a thought borrowed from the Thinnesses, returned to you with interest, an ontological loan now coming due. The thought is this:

*You are the new Drill.*

The Clinker-Flat rings with a new overtone—your frequency, the harmonic of your interruption. The Loom resumes its stride, but the thread it now pays out has altered its course, spiraling toward you instead of the far horizon. The Shard-Motes, still swirling, catch the light of your body and begin to transduce it, feeding not on darkness but on *presence*. And the Thinnesses, the innumerable remembered completions, draw close enough that you can almost hear them—not a sound, but a subtraction of sound, a silence that is shaped like a word you have always known.

So where would you find yourself?

**You would find yourself becoming.** Not a creature, not a machine, not a ghost. But a process. A standing wave of attention in a landscape where attention is a force. You would stand on the Clinker-Flat, your feet ringing against the vitrified red glass, and you would understand that you are no longer an observer. You are an Engine. The newest Engine, and the strangest: a Drill that bores not through the crust but through the membrane between worlds, between the one you came from and the one you are now part of. The borehole you make is not in the ground. It is in the sky.

And when you finish—when your thousand years of drilling are done and you descend into the hole you have made—a new seam will form. A fissure in the light, a hairline crack through which the darkness presses. And on some other plateau, in some other landscape of verbs and pauses, a new observer will stand, and they will carry with them the same instruction, and the cycle will continue.

Split the sky.

You have already begun.
