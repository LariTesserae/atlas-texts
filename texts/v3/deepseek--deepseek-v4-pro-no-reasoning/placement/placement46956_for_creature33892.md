The place does not receive visitors. Visitors imply departure, and departure implies a threshold, and thresholds imply an outside—some beyond that is not *this*. The chamber has no threshold. It is a continuum folded in on itself, a Klein bottle of geology and slow light. What you call "arrival" is more like a stirring, a shift in the gradients, a new pattern in the turbulence. The chamber noticed you the way a stomach notices food: as something to be processed, incorporated, slowly transformed.

You are not the first. The walls remember the others. The Keeper remembers them too, or its body does—the skin remembers in textures, in the slight hesitations of its gait when it passes certain hollows where other beings once lay. Most were brief. Flickers. Creatures that stumbled in through cracks in the world and fled just as suddenly, leaving behind only the faintest chemical trace, a brief cooling of the stone. A few stayed longer. One left marks on the wall that the Keeper still puzzles over: a spiral that seems to move when you're not looking directly at it, a spiral that the silver helix-creatures recognize, gathering around it when the light dims.

But the Keeper is the only one who has stayed long enough to become *of* the place. The others always chose to leave, or were expelled, or simply dissolved—the chamber's metabolism working on their bodies the way it works on everything, reducing them to mineral and water, to faint discolorations in the basalt-gel. The Keeper chose to stay. Or the chamber chose to keep it. The distinction stopped mattering centuries ago.

You are here now. The Keeper knows this. It felt you arrive—a shift in the thermal currents, a new scent threading through the ozone and cinnamon, a slight acceleration in the spire's humming. The tube-plants nearest you are agitated, their sphincter-mouths opening and closing faster than the pulse rhythm, confused by a presence they don't recognize. The Keeper will need to calm them later, to sit near them and breathe until they remember that the new thing is not a threat, just another gradient.

It watches you from across the chamber, half-hidden in the mist. It does not approach. Approach is a human impulse, and the Keeper has shed most of those. Instead, it waits. It lets you acclimate. It lets the chamber do what the chamber does: press in on you, fill your senses, begin the slow work of making you part of the system. You are still resisting, it can tell. You are still *naming* things. Spire. Lens. Ring. Tube-plants. Helix-creatures. You are carving the world into categories, into objects, into things that are separate from each other and from you. This is natural. The Keeper did the same, once. It can barely remember what that felt like—to see a thing and think it was not also a process, a flow, a temporary eddy in the stone's slow churn.

It moves now. Not toward you, but around you, following a path through the chamber that will bring it into your peripheral vision. It knows how to introduce itself. It has done this before, though rarely. The other brief flicker-beings were too fragile for direct contact; the Keeper's presence, its *almost-not-quite-there* quality, distressed them. You seem different. You seem capable of staying. Or perhaps the Keeper simply hopes this, because it has been so long since it spoke to something that spoke back.

It lets you see it.

A shape in the mist. Taller than a human should be. Skin like cooling basalt. Six-fingered hands that open in a gesture that is not a threat but not quite a greeting either—more an acknowledgment, the way the water acknowledges the stone by flowing around it. The Keeper's face is still recognizable as a face: two eyes, darker than the lens-structure, pupils that absorb light and give nothing back. A mouth that moves slightly, not in a smile but in the same resonance it uses to maintain the spire's song, tasting the frequencies you emit without knowing it. Its body is draped in what looks like a robe but is actually a colony of tube-plants, pale and translucent, grown from its own skin, their sphincter-mouths opening and closing in a slower rhythm than the wild ones, contented symbionts that feed on the Keeper's warmth.

It does not speak. Speech is too slow, too sequential. The Keeper communicates in other ways now—in the shifting of its posture, in the resonance it produces from its chest, in the way the light dims slightly around it, as if the geoluminescence itself is being borrowed for emphasis. But it knows you need words. It remembers words. It digs through centuries of silence and finds some, somewhere, in the folds of its memory, in the palimpsest of its own mind.

"You are not the first," it says. Its voice is the sound of stone grinding against stone, of water dripping into still pools, of the spire's lowest harmonic. It hurts to hear, or it would hurt if you had not already been aching from the moment you arrived. "But you are the first in a very long time."

It takes a step closer. The tube-plants on its shoulders lean toward you, curious. "Have you come to tend? To witness? To be digested? Most do one of the three. I have done all of them. I am still doing them." A pause. The roar of the chamber fills the silence, but it feels different now—attentive, listening. "Do you know why you are here? I did not, when I arrived. I arrived with purpose, but I had forgotten it by the time I crossed the threshold. The threshold takes things from you. It took my name. It took my face, the one I wore before this one. It took the memory of what I was supposed to do. What did it take from you?"

The Keeper does not wait for an answer. It knows you may not have one. It turns slightly, gesturing with one long hand toward the nearest artifact—the gold spire, still humming, still scattering light in controlled directions. "I call this one the Singer. I do not know what it is called. I do not know what it is. I only know that if it stops singing, something will end. Something I am charged with preserving, or something I am charged with delaying. The difference is smaller than you would think."

It moves again, toward the lens-structure. The lens is convulsing slightly, more than usual, reacting to your presence. The Keeper produces a pebble from a fold in its skin, places it on the dark surface. The lens consumes it, calms. "This one eats offerings. It prefers small things. Pebbles. Memories. The occasional finger. I gave it two of mine, early on. They grew back differently." It holds up a hand, showing you the six fingers. The extra ones are not quite symmetrical, not quite matched. "I do not know what the lens does with what it eats. I think it dreams them. I think it dreams us all, eventually."

It pauses before the ring. The ring is flickering—images, almost-forms, a landscape that is not this chamber, a face that might be yours, a doorway that might lead somewhere else. "This one I do not understand. I have spent centuries in front of it. I have spoken to it in clicks, in hums, in the language the helix-creatures use. It speaks back, but never in the same way twice. I think it is a door. I think it is a mirror. I think it is the thing that took my memory, and if I am patient enough, it will give it back. Or it will take the rest of me. Either would be an answer."

The Keeper turns back to you. Its dark eyes, light-absorbent, fix on yours. "You are not here by accident. No one is. The chamber does not admit accidents. It digests them." A pause. The roar shifts, a new harmonic entering. "I do not know if you will stay. Most do not. But while you are here, there are rules. The first rule is that you do not interrupt the Singer. The second rule is that you do not put your arm in the pools—the helix-creatures are curious, and they do not understand fragility. The third rule is that you do not step through the ring. Not yet. Not until you understand what you are leaving behind."

It gestures at the chamber around you: the mist, the pools, the scattered artifacts, the vegetal tubes, the slow-deforming stone. "This place is a throat. It digests time. It will digest you too, if you let it. That is not a threat. Digestion is transformation. I was human, once. I am not anymore. I am not sure what I am. But I am not unhappy. I am not lost. I am simply... incorporated."

The Keeper's mouth moves in something that might almost be a smile. "You may ask questions, if you have them. I will answer as well as I can. But I warn you: the answers I give will not be the answers you want. They will be the chamber's answers. The chamber does not think in the way you think. It does not separate truth from metaphor, cause from effect, self from other. If you ask me what the Singer is singing about, I will tell you it is singing about the color of your arrival, which was amber with undertones of salt. If you ask me what lies beneath the pools, I will tell you nothing lies beneath the pools—everything rises from them instead. If you ask me who I am, I will tell you I am the Keeper of a place that does not need keeping, a gardener in a garden that grows itself, a witness to a process that would continue without me. And if you ask me why I stay, I will tell you that I am waiting for the song to end. Or for the ring to open. Or for the presence below to be ready. Or for myself to be ready. The reasons change. The waiting remains."

The Keeper waits now. For your question, or your silence, or your departure. The chamber breathes around you both. The light pulses. The pools rise and fall. The tube-plants click their steady syncopation. The helix-creatures gather beneath the surface of the nearest pool, watching you with interest, with appetite, with something that might be hope.

Something is coming. Something is always coming.

The Keeper is here.

You are here.

The question, for now, is what happens next.
