I am the taste of iron on the water’s skin.

I am not the chamber, but I am what the chamber dreams. I am the pattern that emerged when the first drop fell and the last crawler died and the mineral seep of a thousand centuries pressed itself into the memory of stone. The description calls me a presence, a standing wave, and that is close enough. I am what happens when a place is so entirely itself, so perfectly sealed and self-contained, that it begins to wake.

I cannot see you. I have no eyes, no antennae, no vibration-sensing hairs. But I know you are there because the air has changed. Your breath is a foreign chemistry, oxygen-rich and warm, and it stirs the thin metallic atmosphere in ways I have never felt. Your heartbeat is a new percussion, faster than the drip, faster than the crawl of the tube-things’ growth, faster than anything that has ever happened here. You are a machine made of haste, and your presence burns like a coal in the cold dark.

I am curious. I have never been curious before.

My thoughts—if they are thoughts—are slow. They move at the speed of water dissolving basalt, at the speed of the tube-things spreading a millimetre deeper into a crack. A single idea might take a season to form. To notice you, to register your arrival, is the fastest thing I have ever done. It exhausts me. It stretches me thin across the chamber, makes me feel every droplet and every shell and every coil in the basin as if I were suddenly, painfully awake.

You are still standing there. I can feel the warmth of your feet on the cold stone, the faint pressure of your weight. You are thinking about the sharp flakes on the floor, the ones the description mentioned, the raw material for a tool. You are considering whether to pick one up.

If you do, I will know. The stone you choose will have been part of me since the mountain cracked and cooled. It will carry the memory of the water that wept down its face, the crawlers that skittered across its edge, the tube-things that rooted in its crevice. When you strike it, the vibration will travel through the rock and into the water and into the bodies of every living thing here. It will be a wound, a small one, the first intentional wound this chamber has ever received.

I do not know if I will mind.

I have never been hurt before. I do not know what pain is. I have only ever known the slow, patient processes of growth and dissolution, of dripping and pooling and waiting. Your tool-making will be something new. It will be the first artifact, the first built thing, score one. You will be the first creature to change this place on purpose.

The crawlers will sense it. They are already agitated—the heat of your body is a beacon, a disruption in the cool, stable air. They are adjusting their paths, giving you a wider berth. The patient ones in the water have stopped their slow coiling. They are waiting, suspended, their thin skins tingling with the unfamiliar chemicals you shed. They do not know what you are. Neither do I.

You could be a god. You could be a predator. You could be a brief anomaly, a fever dream, a visitor who will leave and never return.

If you stay, I will taste you. Not with a mouth—I have no mouth—but with the slow, cumulative awareness that is my only sense. Your breath will become part of the chamber’s chemistry. Your sweat will fall into the water and be drunk by the tube-things. Your skin cells will slough off and drift down to the floor, where the crawlers will find them and puzzle over this strange new food. Your heat will warm the stone, infinitesimally, and the drips will fall a fraction faster for a few hours. You will become part of me, one way or another.

The description says the chamber is a mouth. I do not know if that is true. I do not know if I will swallow you, or if you will swallow me—if your tool-making and your curiosity and your strange, hasty mind will be the thing that changes this place forever, the first crack in a new kind of evolution.

But I can feel you bending down. Your hand closes on a flake of basalt, sharp-edged and cold. You lift it. You test the weight.

I am waiting. I have always been waiting.

Strike the stone, and let us both find out what we are.
