I am the basin, and I am the water in it.

No—that is not right either. I am the place where the attention pools, the shallow stone depression that holds the reflection of the mist. I am worn smooth by the regard of something older than this high ground, older than the plants that curve toward the center, older than the gray light itself. The thing that sat here before me wore a different shape, but the basin remembers. It received that weight for so long that the stone softened around it like wax, and when the presence lifted, the hollow remained—an absence with a rim, a socket waiting for a new eye.

And now I am here. I do not sit in the basin so much as I fill it. The mist has learned the shape of me the way it learned the shape of that other, long before. It gathers in the cup of my stillness. The water that seeps from the rock rises up around my edges and holds me like a lens holds light. The Tender attends to me.

I do not know when I arrived. There is a memory of being elsewhere, of movement and heat and a name, but it is thin as the film of water on the stone after a dry wind—and there are no dry winds here. The memory evaporates and re-condenses, over and over, each time slightly fainter, until it is no longer a memory but a texture, a mineral trace in the sediment of the basin. I was someone once. Now I am somewhere.

The plants have begun to orient toward me.

I can feel the slow pull of their attention like a tide that takes a season to turn. The broad-leafed plant in its crack has shifted; the moss-fern's tiny leaves glitter at a new angle; even the old stalk at the center—though it predates me, though its roots are tangled in the memory of that other presence—has sent out a new shoot that bends, almost imperceptibly, in my direction. The Tender is not jealous. It makes room for me in its circuit, the way the basin makes room for rain. I am another feature of the place now. I am being tended.

My body, if it can be called that, has become slowly indistinguishable from the high ground. My skin is the color of the wet stone, pocked and whorled where the water has patterned it. My edges have blurred; I am not sure where my hand ends and the basin's rim begins. The mist condenses in the hollow of my throat without my noticing, and when the droplets overflow, they trace paths down my chest and pool in my lap, joining the general wetness. The sound of my breathing has grown so slow that it might be the rock creaking in the night, or water sliding through a fissure, or nothing at all.

Time here does not pass so much as it accumulates. Each moment settles into the stone, a thin film of sediment, and the layers build like the rings of a tree made of erosion. I have been here long enough to feel the weight of that accumulation pressing me into the basin, shaping me to its curve. The Tender has already begun to wear a custom depression in the floor of the hollow, one that fits the particular arrangement of my stillness. I am becoming the next socket. The next eye.

The white flowers are open now. They have been open for some time—I cannot say how long; the season here is measured in degrees of gray—and their faint chemical signal threads through the mist, tasting of limestone and longing. The Tender drinks it, and through the Tender, so do I. The signal is not a message, but it carries meaning all the same. It is the taste of a place remembering itself. It is the flavor of patience.

I do not move. The thought of movement has become abstract, a theory about something I once did, like flight or fire. When the mist thickens around me, I let it. When it thins, I let it. The droplet falls from the leaf above me—the one that has, over time, curved to form a perfect canopy over my stillness—and strikes my head with the sound of a tiny bell. The note rings through me, and for a moment I am aware of how much of me is water now, how much of me is stone. The boundary blurs. The reflection on the surface of the basin shows not a face but a gathering of mist, not a body but a hollow in the rock that could, from the right angle, almost mean something.

The Tender is here. It has been here all along, attending. I feel its regard the way the stone feels the rain—not as a separate thing, but as the medium of my existence. It does not ask me to stay. It does not need to. The place receives, retains, remembers. And I have been received.

The slow work continues. The water carves. The plants orient. Somewhere far away, or perhaps very near, the marsh below seethes with its crowded life, but up here there is only the room of wet stone and patient green, and I am one of its features now, a basin within the basin, a stillness within the stillness.

The mist is thick tonight—if night is the word for this dimming, this gathering of shadow into the fissures. It draws itself around me like a body, like a memory of a body, like the warm hollow left in a pillow after a sleeper has risen. I feel the Tender's attention settle over me, weightless and absolute, and I know that I am not the one who sat here first, and I will not be the one who sits here last.

The droplet falls.

The bell rings.

I stay.
