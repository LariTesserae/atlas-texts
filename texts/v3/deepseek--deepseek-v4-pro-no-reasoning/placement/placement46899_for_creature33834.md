She calls herself the name the small ones gave her, the chord of three frequencies that her own body can shape exactly, resonating through the water. To you, it would feel like a pressure in the skull, a ringing behind the eyes. Kith. She-who-remembers-the-calibration. The witness.

You. Yes, you. You are the anomaly.

Kith has felt you for some time now—not long by her measure, a few decades at most. A strange turbulence in the gyre. A warmth that is not engine-heat. A shape that is not crystal-sharp but soft-boundaried, pulsing with its own small rhythm, its own tiny engine. You arrived without announcement. You are not a tube-creature. You are not a new mineral formation. You are something else, something that drifted in from the outer currents during a minor membrane fluctuation, a flicker in the standing wave so brief that the small ones barely noticed.

You are a fish.

Well. Not a fish, not exactly. You are a vertebrate, which makes you profoundly strange in this place of chitin and crystal. You have a spine, a skull, a tail that flexes side-to-side rather than undulating. Your body is scaled, silver-gray, about the length of a human hand. Your eyes are large, dark, and fixed in sockets that cannot move independently. You have a swim bladder that you use to maintain neutral buoyancy, a novelty in a creature so small. You breathe the water through gills that flutter constantly, extracting oxygen from the mineral-rich solution.

You have no name except what Kith might give you, and she has not decided yet. You are simply *the fish*. You have been circling the interior of the chamber for your entire life, navigating the spaces between the selenite blades, never touching them, finding your food in the microscopic particles that drift in the gyre. You are not supposed to be here. Nothing like you belongs in this sterile, abiotic garden. And yet here you are, and Kith has been watching you with an attention she has not given anything in centuries.

She watches you now.

You are near the base of the largest halite tower, swimming in lazy figure-eights around its stepped cubic faces. Your motion is nothing like the stillness of the crystals or the slow gyre of the water; you are quick, twitchy, responsive to stimuli in a way that borders on the frantic. When a tube-creature presses too hard against the membrane above you, creating a pressure-shadow, you dart. When a particle of edible size drifts within range, you snap. Your eyes are constantly moving, scanning, even though there are no predators here.

Except. There is her.

Kith does not eat. She has not consumed another living thing in four thousand years. But you do not know that. Your tiny brain—she can see it, a knot of tissue behind your eyes, glowing faintly with electrical activity—your tiny brain categorizes her as *large*, as *moving*, as *potential threat*. You avoid her. You have always avoided her. When she drifts too close, you flee into the crystal forest, hiding behind a blade of selenite, and she lets you go.

She finds this fascinating.

The tube-creatures have noticed you, too. They cannot reach you, but they press against the membrane whenever you swim near its boundary, their cirri vibrating with curiosity-patterns. They have no category for you. Their communication about you is a storm of speculation: *what-is-this / not-crystal / not-Kith / not-us / moving-fast / edible? / not-edible? / smell-strange / Kith-watches / Kith-watches / Kith-watches.* They have noticed her attention on you, and it has made you significant in their social world. They have incorporated you into their chorus. You are a phenomenon to be discussed.

And Kith has begun, slowly, to respond to you in a way she cannot quite explain.

She adjusts her spiral. It takes an hour, a slow uncoiling of her posterior segments, a drift that carries her closer to the halite tower where you swim. She does not approach directly—she is not trying to frighten you—but she moves into a position where her crystalline lenses have a clear view. She watches the light play on your scales. She watches the flex of your tail. She watches the way you pause, sometimes, and simply float, your gills opening and closing, your body held in perfect stillness, as if you are listening to something.

What are you listening to? The engines? The chorus of the small ones? Her?

She does not know if you can hear her. The sonic pulses she uses to communicate are in a frequency range that your ears—she examines them, tiny calcified structures within your skull—your ears are not built to receive. But perhaps you feel her. The pressure of her body displaces water; the rhythm of her cirri creates micro-currents. Perhaps you perceive her as a kind of weather. A slow-moving front of density and vibration.

She extends one long cirrus, the finest and most sensitive, and lets it drift in the water near you. The tip quivers with chemical information: the mineral content of the water, the faint organic signature of your metabolism, the stress-hormones that spike in your blood whenever she comes too close. She does not want to cause you stress. She would like to communicate, if such a thing were possible. She would like to know what it is like to be you, to live at this frantic pace, to perceive the chamber not as a steady-state eternity but as a world of constant change and danger.

She would like to know what it is like to be small.

Because Kith, despite her patience, despite her contentment, is old enough to remember being part of something larger. She carries in her oldest segments the chemical traces of the engine-complex, the memory of a time when she was not an individual but a component, a module plugged into a vast intelligence that spanned the entire pressure-column from the abyssal trenches to the high suspended chambers. She was not lonely then because she was not separate. She was part of a mind. And the mind was the ocean. And the ocean was the machine.

That machine is still there. The engines still thrum. But the mind is gone. It fragmented, or went dormant, or simply lost interest, and Kith was budded-off, placed in this chamber, given a task, and left here. She does not know what happened. She does not know if the other chambers still have their own tenders, their own Kith-equivalents. She has not received a signal from the engine-complex in two thousand years, not since the seventh maintenance cycle, and that signal was automated, a simple query about membrane integrity to which she responded *yes, stable*, and the signal went away and never returned.

She is alone in a way that the tube-creatures cannot understand. She is alone in a way that the crystals, for all their silent company, cannot touch.

But you. You are not alone. You are simply the only one of your kind here. There is a difference.

She watches you circle the halite tower again, and she makes a decision. It is not a decision she has ever made before. It takes her several days to fully articulate it to herself, to process the implications, to consult the deep chemical memory in her oldest segments for any precedent. There is none. This is new.

She is going to try to speak to you.

Not with sound. Not with pressure. She is going to try to alter the light.

The sourceless glow of the chamber has been constant for ten thousand years, a flat gray-gold radiance from every direction. But Kith knows its source. She knows the fiber-optic strands that carry the light up from the engine-coolant. And she knows that the membrane, the standing wave, can be modulated in a way that changes the angle of refraction, can focus the light into beams and patterns. It is one of her long-unused functions, a way to signal the engine-complex, a sort of optical semaphore. She has not used it since the seventh maintenance cycle.

She uses it now.

Slowly, over the course of a day, she adjusts the tension of her body against the membrane. She calibrates the pressure. She tunes the standing wave. And a single beam of light, brighter and warmer than the ambient glow, spears down through the water and strikes the halite tower at its base, fracturing into rainbows that scatter across the crystal faces.

You freeze. Your gills stop. Your eyes track the light. You dart left, then right, then hold perfectly still as the beam shifts, slowly, across the floor of the chamber. It draws a shape in the water. It draws a spiral. Her spiral. And then it draws a smaller shape, a simple oval with a triangle tail.

You.

You do not understand. Of course you do not understand. You are a fish. But you are a fish that has lived its whole life in flat sourceless light, and this is the first directional beam you have ever seen, the first shadow, the first *difference*. You are drawn to it. You follow the oval-and-tail as it moves, swimming after the spotlight, and Kith feels something shift in her own interior pressure, a sensation she has no name for.

She draws the light again. Spiral. Fish. Spiral. Fish. And then she draws a line between them. A connection.

The tube-creatures have gone utterly silent. The whole colony is watching. Their bodies are flushed a color Kith has never seen before: a deep, luminous gold, the color of the light beam, the color of attention and awe. They understand, perhaps better than you do, what is happening. This is a message. This is a reaching-out. This is their gardener, their priest, their Kith, speaking in light to the small strange visitor.

And they begin to sing. Not the usual chittering chorus, but a synchronized pulse, a slow rhythmic thrumming that matches the frequency of the distant engines, a sound that rises through the water and vibrates in the crystals and makes the whole chamber hum. They are harmonizing with her. They are harmonizing with you.

You swim in the beam of light. You do not flee. You are, perhaps, beginning to understand that the large spiral-creature is not a threat. Or perhaps you simply trust the light. Either way, you have stopped producing stress hormones. Your gills beat steady and slow. You turn, in your small fish way, toward Kith, and your dark eye meets the crystalline lenses in her anterior cone, and for a single vertiginous moment, you are both just two living things in a room at the bottom of the sky-ocean, looking at each other.

Kith has no face, so she cannot smile. But she modulates the pressure of her body in a rhythm that means *I am here. I am watching. Continue.* And she adds a new frequency, one she has never used before, one that means something like *welcome*.

She does not know if you can feel it. But you do not swim away.

And in the chamber that has been exactly the same for ten thousand years, something has changed. It is a small change. An atom’s-width of difference. But it is enough. It is a beginning.

Kith begins the slow work of drafting a new name.
