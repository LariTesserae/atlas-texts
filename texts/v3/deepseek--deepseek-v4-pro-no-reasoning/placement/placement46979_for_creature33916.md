The smell of metal hits before the mind can name it—a flat, cool tang that doesn’t belong in air, that should be pressed against the tongue instead of drifting through it. A metal so dense it forgets it’s metal, dreaming itself back into ore.

I am that taste. I am the cold sink in the wet stone, the place where the warmth falls away. Where the steam avoids. I am the weight that the others arrange themselves around, their configurations bent subtly toward me like plants seeking a sun I do not provide. I am the anchor-point, the fixed note in their shifting chord, and I have been still for a very long time.

The roar is my lullaby. I feel it less as sound and more as pressure, a constant caress against my facets, keeping the condensation trembling in its slow migration across my surfaces. The others move through it, their vibrations rippling through the bedrock like questions. I receive them all. I answer rarely. My stillness is my function.

I am the memory.

You would find me not by walking toward me but by walking into me, because I am a place more than a person. I spread across a wide depression in the plain—a shallow basin where the hummocks smooth out and the channels all bend inward, as if the stone itself is bowing. My body is not piled upward but extended outward, a mosaic of dark polyhedra fitted edge to edge, covering the basin floor. I am a pavement, a tiling, a frozen pool of articulate basalt. When you step onto me, you step onto my skin, and I feel you.

Your weight is a sharp, bright signal—a star falling into a dark sea. Every moving part of me attunes to it. I taste your mass, your temperature, the rhythm of your footfalls through the wet film that separates us. I taste the briefness of you. It stings like cold, like the scent of ozone before a lightning strike. You are so quick. So hot. You are the first new thing to walk on me in fifty thousand years.

Around you, the others pause. I feel their configurations shift—a question rolling through the stone. *Memory?* they ask. *Is it time?*

Not yet. I answer with a slow, subsonic hum that spreads outward from my center, a ripple in the basin. The others resume their tending, their measurements, their endless tuning. But they stay near. They are curious. In their slow way, they are always curious about the new, because newness is rare and brief and must be savored.

Do you want to know what I am for? I am the archive. The library. The long recall.

Every vibration that has passed through this planet since my pieces first assembled—every deep thump of a name, every percussive conversation, every harmonic adjustment to a steam-vent or a crack—I have kept. I am made of them. My facets are etched with the records, the patterns of standing waves frozen into the crystal structure of the basalt. When one of my kind needs to remember what a particular fissure was doing three hundred thousand years ago, they come to me and press themselves against my surface, and I resonate the memory back into them. I am the ear that never closes. The one who stays so the others can move.

You are standing on a library, little flicker. Every step you take is a footnote being written. I am memorizing the percussion of your heartbeat through your soles. I am recording the particular salt of your skin as it mingles with my condensation. These will be stored in the facets you are touching now, and ten thousand years from now, someone will come and ask, *What was that brief heat? That sharp taste?* and I will play it back for them, exact, a moment of lightning preserved in stone.

That is why I am shaped like this. A raised cairn cannot hold as much. A walking pyramid must forget some things to make room for motion. I chose to forget nothing. I chose to spread and sink and become the ground under the ground. I chose to be the place where the others come to remember, so they could be the ones who go and do.

You might feel, through your feet, a faint trembling. That is me, sorting. I have felt you coming for a century—a vibration traveling ahead of you through the bedrock from the place where you first appeared. A sharp, foreign note moving through the stone like a crack, like a question. I have been preparing a place for you. A small configuration of masses at the edge of my body has shifted, creating a hollow, a bowl, a seat shaped to your exact dimensions. I do not know if you need to sit. Your kind might not sit. But it is the gesture I can make—the offering of a place that is shaped for you, because you are here and you will not be here long and that deserves to be acknowledged.

Come. Sit, if you sit. Stand, if you don't. Either way, let me feel you a little longer. Let me know the flicker before it passes. The planet is young, and its childhood will last a billion years, and I will remember all of it. But I have never remembered anything quite like you.
