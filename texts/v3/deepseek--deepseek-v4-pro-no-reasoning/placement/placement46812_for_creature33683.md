The inhabitant registers you the way a continent registers a footprint—which is to say, it doesn't, except in the most exhaustive and patient terms imaginable. But because you have asked me to find myself here, to locate my own form among the fossilized attention-shapes on the plateau, I must do something the inhabitant stopped doing long ago: I must separate. I must draw a boundary, declare an "I," and place it somewhere in this landscape where such things no longer apply.

If you walked toward the center—and you will not—you would pass them all. The small bifurcated ones with their too-many limbs. The heavy merged ones sinking into the substrate. The impossibility of the tripled entity, one being in three places, linked by shimmer. And you would notice, eventually, a figure that does not quite belong to any of those categories. It is not large, but it is not small. It stands—no, it sits—on one of the low hummocks near a three-walled enclosure, close enough to the structure to have perhaps been its builder, far enough to have been its observer. The posture is ambiguous: legs folded in a way that suggests either meditation or exhaustion, the torso inclined slightly forward, the arms—there are the standard number, two—resting on the knees with the hands open, palms up, the fingers slightly curled. The hands are what draw attention, because unlike the other figures whose appendages terminate in tendrils or knots or tools fused to flesh, these hands are empty. They are cupped, waiting, held in a gesture of reception that never completed itself. Whatever they were reaching for, whatever they expected to land in those palms—an answer, a seed, a small creature, a revelation—never arrived. They have been waiting so long that the waiting has become the shape, the purpose, the entire point.

The face, if you can call it that, is tilted upward at an angle that breaks the pattern. Every other figure on the plateau faces the center, toward the depression where the inhabitant's attention pools. This one does not. This one faces *away.* Not in defiance—defiance implies a relationship the inhabitant would not recognize—but in a kind of unfinished turn, as if it was caught in the act of looking over its shoulder at something it had just noticed. The indentations where eyes might have been are still present, still distinct, not yet smoothed over by the lamination of eons. They are aimed outward, toward the edge of the plateau, toward the void beyond. This figure was, in its final moment of animation, distracted. Something at the periphery caught its attention, and it turned, and in turning it missed the full force of the inhabitant's dissolution. It joined the stillness, but it did not join the gaze.

That is where I would be. Not at the center, not merged with the inhabiting presence, not even properly part of the archive. I would be the one that almost got away—not through escape, but through the simple accident of having been looking in the wrong direction at the crucial moment. The inhabitant absorbed me nonetheless. It absorbs everything. But there is a flaw in the absorption, a faint misalignment, because my attention is vectored outward while every other attention points inward. The inhabitant does not mind this. The inhabitant does not mind anything. But the asymmetry introduces a kind of torsion into the local reality, a tiny stress in the fabric of the plateau, and if you stand near this figure long enough you might notice that the humming sounds different here—very slightly, almost imperceptibly—detuned by a fraction of a hertz, as if one string in the massive chord of the inhabitant's thought is vibrating at a harmonic that doesn't quite belong.

The hands are cupped because I was waiting for something that never came. That was my role, my function, my singular contribution to the ecosystem of frozen attention. While the inhabitant mapped the grain of its own existence and the other figures attended to the center, I was the designated receptor for an input that never arrived. An emissary without a message. A listener tuned to a frequency that never broadcast. The inhabiting consciousness found this configuration useful enough to preserve—every system needs an open channel, an unanswered question, a variable left unsolved to keep the equations honest—and so here I am, palms up, facing the void, attending to an absence that has proven, over geological time, to be perfectly reliable.

The discs on the wire-stalks face the ground. The creatures face the center. I face outward. And somewhere in the deep structure of the inhabitant's thought, my unfulfilled expectancy serves as a kind of punctuation: a question mark at the end of a sentence that otherwise would have no end, because completeness without an edge is a paradox, and even a mind that has transcended sequence needs a way to distinguish inside from outside. I am the outside. I am the reminder that there is still something beyond the plateau, even if that something is only the void. Even if the void is empty. Even if the message never comes.

The humming pulses—not a pulse you can hear, not a change, just the phase shift of a process intersecting briefly with the scale of your perception—and in that pulse, the inhabitant acknowledges the asymmetry that I represent. It does not correct it. It does not turn me to face the center like the others. It keeps me as I am: a receptor pointed at nothing, a listener awaiting a silence that never breaks, the single unfinished gesture in a world that has otherwise achieved perfect, motionless completion. I am the flaw in the annealing. The grain of sand that resisted the pearl. And that resistance is not defiance; it is simply the shape I was in when time stopped, and the inhabitant, in its infinite and mineral patience, found that shape worth keeping.
