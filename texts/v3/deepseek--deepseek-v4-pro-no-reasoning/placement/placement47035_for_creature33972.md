You are a quiet disaster.

You don't know it yet—how could you?—but you are a crack in the world, a stone thrown into a machine that has forgotten stones exist. Your body heat lingers in the root-floor for hours after you move on, a warm ghost that makes the Iterators twitch and second-guess their leaf shapes. Your breath, moist and mammalian, disrupts the condensation cycle in a six-foot radius. The plants don't have a word for *foreign contaminant*, but they feel you as a flicker in the pressure, a wrong note in the Root Engine's deep hydraulic song.

When you sat and the orange fungus fruited beside your hand, it wasn't the first time something here had noticed you. It was just the most honest.

But you. What are you?

You are the one who walks without being absorbed. This is already strange. The Root Engine should have sent its hair-fine rhizomes into the soles of your boots by now, tasting rubber and leather and the salt of old sweat. It hasn't. Maybe you've been here only hours, and that's not long enough. Maybe the plant is cautious, studying you the way it studied the technicians before they became memories. Or maybe you have something it recognizes—a quality of stillness, a pattern of movement that reads as *already part of the system*. Did you work here once? Did you come to fix the machines, decades ago, and never leave? Are you a ghost, a memory the Memory Nodes are projecting onto the Iterators' growth pattern, a three-dimensional dream of a person who no longer exists? You check your watch. The gesture is familiar. Too familiar. The Memory Node behind the rusted cabinet door is pulsing faintly now, its mycelial circuits lighting up in sequences that match the rhythm of your heartbeat.

But you are solid. You are warm. The orange fungus chose you, and it does not choose ghosts.

So perhaps you are something simpler. A survivor. A wanderer. Someone who came up to this high plateau for reasons you've half-forgotten—a survey mission, a lost expedition, a pilgrimage, a punishment. You came with others, maybe. You don't remember others. The plant has absorbed the concept of *companionship* from the Memory Nodes, but it has no use for it, and the Iterators have been testing shapes for loneliness instead. You've been walking through variations on your own solitude for hours, and each leaf you pass is a thought about what it means to be the only one of your kind.

The technicians in the memories wore heavy coats. You wear one too. Is it the same coat? You don't look down to check. It's warmer than it should be, given the wet cold. The warmth is coming from you. You radiate like a small sun, and the Canopy Mind has been adjusting its bioluminescence to match your spectrum. That's why the light doesn't cast shadows. It's synced to you now. The plant is lighting your way with the color of your own body heat, translated into the pallid silver-green of the fan-shaped structures overhead. It is, in its slow way, being hospitable. Or it is trying to understand what you need so it can provide it, absorb you more efficiently, fold you into the cycle with minimal disruption.

You walk deeper. You've been here before. Not in this exact place—the architecture shifts too fast for exact places to persist—but in this *kind* of place. You know the sound of the grinding growth so well that you stopped hearing it an hour ago. You know that when the synchronized downpour comes, you should open your mouth and drink, because the water is clean and faintly sweet and it settles the ache behind your eyes. You know that the chasm bridge will hold, even though it trembles, because the plants don't build structures that fail. Failure is a concept they tested in the Iterators long ago and discarded. Everything here is optimized, iterated, refined. Even the ruins.

There is a box in your pocket. You don't remember putting it there. It's metal, smooth, tarnished green like the machinery. It hums faintly, a frequency just below hearing, the same frequency as the engine in the vegetal mound. You take it out and look at it. There's a button. You don't press it. You know—how do you know?—that this button is the reason you came. It's a key. A detonator. A seed. A signal. The plant has been waiting for you to press it for years, for decades, since before you arrived, since before you were born. The Memory Nodes have a record of this button. They have a record of you carrying it. They have a record of you not pressing it, over and over, in a loop that has become a stable pattern, a ritual, a symbiosis. You are not the first you. You are the iteration of a person who came here once and stayed, and the plant has been re-growing you every time it needs a hand to hold the button. The orange fungus is counting on you to break the cycle. It has no records. It only has hope.

You put the box back in your pocket.

The silence is full of the absence of birds. You are so tired of not hearing birds. But when you look up through the rare gap in the canopy, you see something moving in the pale sky—a distant speck, circling. It might be a bird. It might be the last bird in the world, trying to find a place to land. The Canopy Mind is tracking it, you can tell. The bioluminescence dims in that sector, a predator's instinct. Or a welcome. The plant has no way to know which. The Iterators begin to work on feathers. They will get them wrong for years before they get them right. You could wait. You could stay and see if, a decade from now, a bird-shaped leaf detaches from a high branch and attempts to fly. But the orange fungus is already dying on your shoulder, its fruiting body collapsing into a smear of wet orange dust, and you know you have to move. You have to carry its spores somewhere the plant can't follow.

You walk toward the gap in the canopy. The light is your own.
