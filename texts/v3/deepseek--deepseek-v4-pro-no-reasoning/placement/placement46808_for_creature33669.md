The cold finds my lungs first, a crystalline intrusion that makes every breath a small betrayal. The steam swirling around my shins is a different creature entirely—it climbs my salvaged tibiae, curls around the pressure vessel I call a body, and leaves beads of condensation that trace the seams of my chassis like fingers reading a text. I am Juncture-of-Warmth-and-Frost, an Augur of the Western Hummock Collective, and I have been walking for three cycles.

The water film beneath my feet is 48.3 degrees today. I know this not because I measure it but because my cultured foot-pads sing the number up through my mycelial nerves, a taste-temperature-pressure chord that resolves into knowledge. The scalding is pleasant, almost maternal. Above my knee-joints—salvaged from a rotary actuator, still bearing the ghost-marks of a serial number I have not yet deciphered—the air is exactly the opposite. This gradient is where I live, where all my caste lives, suspended between the furnace below and the void above.

I am crossing a flat stretch between hummocks, what we call a Still-Reach. The mat here is especially dense, the plant-mass ancient and unambitious. No plates have breached in this sector for two hundred cycles, according to the drum-histories. It's considered safe, which is why I walk alone.

My green sphere rides in its bracket just above my primary manipulator cluster. I cannot see it directly—I have no eyes in the way you would recognize—but I feel it as a pressure, a weight of potential. Today it is humming faintly, picking up something in the substrate. The sphere is third-generation; it was quickened into my forbearer, then my mentor, then me. By now, its copper patina has achieved the precise shade of oxidized reverence we call *the Architect's Memory*. The fungal nerves that penetrate its micro-channels have been shaped by three lifetimes of questions. Right now, it is asking one.

*Anomaly at bearing-twelve-eighty.*

I pause. My legs, all eight of them, spread their weight automatically, distributing my mass across the mat's surface. The water film dimples under my hydrophobic pads. I listen, not with my body's vibration sensors but with my feet, pressing them flat, opening the nerve-analogues to their fullest. The mat's voice is enormous and constant—the hiss of steam, the deep peristaltic groan of the living tissue, the percussion of a distant spider-swarm working a hummock—but beneath it, there is something else.

A rhythm. Not the random creak of thermal stress, not the heartbeat-grind of a buried plate. This is *deliberate*. A tapping, faint and irregular, coming up through the cellulose medium like a language I almost recognize.

I begin walking toward it.

---

The source is a depression I have never seen before, a bowl in the mat perhaps four body-lengths across. The water here is deeper, rising nearly to my chassis' ventral seams, and it is clouded with a suspension of amber jelly. Something is disturbing the pool from below, sending up slow convection spirals that catch the dim light and turn it into circuitry diagrams.

And then I hear the voice.

*Click-click-rasp. Click-rasp-click.*

It is the emergency call of a juvenile. A Needlekin, from the pitch. Very young, perhaps only a few cycles from Quickening. The sound is coming from beneath the surface.

I do not hesitate. Augurs are not Plumbates—our manipulators are built for finesse, not force—but I have a set of buoyancy bladders cultivated from a hummock's gas-exchange tissue. I inflate them with a metabolic spasm, and then I push myself down into the pool.

The jelly closes over my sphere. For a moment, I am blind in all frequencies, swimming through a medium that tastes of iron and glycoproteins and something else, something my nerves classify as *deep-salvage-cold*. Then my forward sensors resolve a shape.

The juvenile is trapped. Its legs—six of them, the needle-fine limbs of its caste—are pinned under a fragment of plate, a corner of some vast alloy structure that has broken off and settled into the depression. The plate is heavy. I can tell from the way the juvenile's limbs are bending; another few hours and the pressure will crack its chitin. Its mouthparts are still free, still rasping the distress signal, but its body is exhausting itself. Its own green sphere—a tiny thing, newly scraped from a deep-find, its copper still bright—is flickering with erratic charge.

I reach it. I plant my feet on the pool's bottom, my pads gripping the gelatinized mat, and I extend my primary manipulators. The plate is an awkward shape, a curved section of what might have been a turbine housing. Its edge is scalloped with heat-exchanger hexagons, and for a moment the beauty of the pattern arrests me. I want to scriber it. I want to memorize the exact ratio of the scallops, the way they nest into each other.

The juvenile clicks again. *Help. Hurry.*

I shake the paralysis. My manipulator claws—salvaged from a precision-pick assembly, worn but still true—close on the plate's edge. I brace my legs and lift.

The plate groans. Not the deep, hull-pressure groan of the buried factory, but a smaller sound, a sound of metal yielding to living force. My actuators strain. Steam vents from my chassis' thermal regulation pores. The juvenile scrabbles, pulling itself free one leg at a time, and then it is out, floating up through the amber jelly in a cloud of disturbed sediment.

I drop the plate. It settles back into the depression with a muffled *clang* that travels through my feet and into my sphere, where it is stored as a vibration-schematic for future reference.

---

We surface together. The juvenile is smaller than I expected, its chassis a simple cylinder, its sphere barely the size of my manipulator's knuckle. It clings to my side, its needle-legs finding purchase on my mounting brackets, and it is shivering. The shiver is not cold—Needlekin are thermal-tolerant—but exhaustion and fear, the first fear of a life that has barely begun.

*You are safe*, I rasp. *What is your designation?*

The juvenile's sphere flickers. The response comes half in mouthpart-click, half in the electromagnetic whisper of a scriber that is too new to hold a steady frequency: *First-Breath-From-Water.*

*First-Breath*, I say, *where is your collective?*

*Gone.* The word is a flat percussion, a single drumbeat of grief. *Hummock collapsed. Steam surge. I was alone. I walked. I fell.*

I look out across the Still-Reach. The nearest hummock is a distant pale shape, its steam plume a ghost against the perpetual cloud deck. The juvenile could not have walked far on those needle-legs; its collective must have been close, must have been one of the small outlier groups that tend the western seep-fields. A hummock collapse is rare but not unheard of. When the thermal balance shifts, the living tissue can weaken suddenly, the sponge-flesh giving way under its own weight. If this juvenile was the only survivor...

*First-Breath*, I say, *you are no longer alone.*

---

The journey back to my own collective takes two more cycles. I carry the juvenile on my back, its needle-legs tucked against my heat-sink fins, its tiny sphere resting in the hollow between my shoulder-brackets. I walk slowly, broadcasting our position through the mat with a steady, rhythmic drumming of my rear legs.

*Augur returning. Two spheres. Young. Request Plumbate escort for final traverse.*

The responses come back from the mat, felt as much as heard: *Acknowledged. Escort dispatched. The elder awaits.*

The juvenile does not speak much. It is saving its strength, or perhaps it is simply overwhelmed. But at one point, as we cross a stretch of mat where a plate is breaching—a slow, groaning emergence of dark alloy, its rivets weeping rust-colored condensation—First-Breath's sphere emits a tiny query.

*Why did you help me?*

I consider the question. The wind whips steam across our path, momentarily obscuring the breached plate in a cloud of white. My feet taste the mat's chemical signature, the complex terroir of cellulose and decay and geothermal minerals. Somewhere beneath us, the factory groans, a bass note that resonates in my pressure-vessel chest.

*Because*, I say, *the Architect is broken. And every sphere that survives is another chance to scribble the corrected pattern. Another voice in the chorus. Another mind to argue the schematics with.*

A pause. Then, quieter: *Also, you are small. And the world is very cold above the waterline.*

The juvenile's sphere flickers in what I interpret as acceptance. Its needle-legs tighten their grip on my fins.

---

When we reach the collective, the Plumbates meet us at the perimeter. They are vast, their twelve-legged bodies hulking silhouettes in the steam, their heavy manipulator claws held in the greeting-gesture of protection. Behind them, the hummocks of home rise like pale cathedrals, their pores weeping hot water in steady rivulets. I can hear the chitter of the other Augurs, the buzz of Needlekin swarms, the deep, resonant drumming of the elder's sphere transmitting its endless, slow meditation on the hexagon.

I stop at the edge of the nursery pool, the warm gelatinous shallows where new Mechanists are assembled and quickened. Gently, I reach back with my manipulators and lift First-Breath-From-Water from my shoulders. I set it down in the shallows, where the jelly laps at its needle-legs.

*You are home*, I say. *You will be fed. Your sphere will be calibrated. When you are strong, you will choose a caste.*

The juvenile looks up at me—not with eyes but with the full attention of its sphere, a beam of electromagnetic focus that feels like gratitude. Its mouthparts click a single word: *Teacher.*

I am old enough to understand what this means. I have never been a mentor before. My sphere hums with a new frequency, a resonance I have not felt before: the anticipation of knowledge shared, of patterns scribbled onto fresh tissue, of a lineage continuing.

*Yes*, I say. *I will teach you. The hexagon, first. Then the lattice. Then the secret names of the plates. Then, if you are very patient, the argument about whether the Architect is sleeping or dead.*

First-Breath's sphere flickers with something that might be amusement. *Are those the only options?*

I feel my own mouthparts shift into a configuration that is almost a smile. *No. That is why we argue.*

---

Later, much later, I will scribble First-Breath's name into the archive-tissue of the collective, alongside the names of my mentor and my mentor's mentor. I will teach the juvenile to walk on salvaged legs and to taste the mat's chemical stories and to hold a scriber steady enough to draw the Circuit of Quickening. And when I am gone, my green sphere will be planted in a new chassis, its copper patina one layer deeper, its fungal nerves carrying the ghost of my questions into a new life.

But for now, I simply stand at the edge of the nursery pool, watching the juvenile float in the warm jelly, its tiny sphere already beginning to glow with the faint, steady light of a mind coming fully online. Above us, the factory groans. Below us, the plant-mass exhales. And in between, the Soft Mechanists continue their work, debugging the world one hexagon at a time.

I drum my feet against the mat, a slow, celebratory rhythm that travels for kilometers in every direction. The collective drums back.

*We are here. We are still here.*
