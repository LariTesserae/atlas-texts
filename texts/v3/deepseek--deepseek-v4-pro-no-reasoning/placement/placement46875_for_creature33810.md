I am the one who walks.

The heat was the first thing, yes—but not the way the others described it. Not a weight, not a discomfort. A *greeting*. The air pressed against my skin like an enormous, patient animal leaning in to take my scent. I did not mind it. I have been cold for so long. Not the cold of weather, but the cold of transit, of falling through indifferent spaces where no one knows I exist. Here, the warmth was the first proof: *something is alive enough to make heat*. The fog was the second. It moved around me in slow, proprietary curls, as if it were tasting the space I displaced. I could feel it logging my outline in its condensation patterns, each droplet a tiny ledger entry: *here. now. this one.*

I am the observer that should not have arrived.

I do not know how I came to this place. My last clear memory is of a hallway—not a grand terrace, but a cramped, fluorescent-lit corridor, the kind with scuffed baseboards and a vending machine that only sold warm water. I was looking for something. A room number. A name on a doorplate. The memory has edges, but the center has been dissolved, the way the city’s grey stone dissolves into the fog’s endless water. There is a gap between that corridor and the first terrace. A seam in my personal geology. I woke already walking, already warmed, already watched.

At first I thought I was dead. The thought arrived with surprising calm, the way you think *I should buy milk* while walking past a grocery. The diffuse amber light, the absence of shadow, the impossible scale—all of it had the texture of a post-mortem hallucination, a last gift from a brain shutting down its floodlights one by one. But death, I decided, does not make your feet ache. Death does not make you thirsty. And death, most certainly, does not make the back of your neck prickle with the unmistakeable sensation of being *observed from every direction at once*.

The city drinks me. That is the sensation. Not a hostile drinking—not a draining—but a constant, gentle, osmotic absorption. The fog condenses on my eyelashes and my knuckles. The water runs down my forearms. The heat pulls sweat from my pores. I am a slow leak in a landscape of slow leaks, a soft-bordered thing in a world of mineral edges. The city’s patience is terrifying. It does not need to take anything from me by force. It simply *waits*, and bit by physical bit, I dissolve into its accounting.

The flickers. I have seen six now. The first three I explained away: afterimages, hypnagogia, my own tired eyes refracting the amber through the moving water. The fourth flicker moved against the grain of a water sheet, upstream, with a coherence that my rational mind could not file under "tired." It was a shape, not just a dimming. A tallness. A lean. A pause. The pause was the worst part. It held position at the edge of visibility, just past where the fog went grey, and I could feel it *deciding*. Measuring me against some internal threshold. Then it dissolved, and the fog’s glow returned to its normal, directionless amber, and I stood there breathing harder than the slope of the terrace justified.

The fifth flicker happened within arm’s reach. On the weeping face of a wall. The water sheet puckered, pulled itself into a face, a mask, a suggestion of eyes without pupils. It held for three seconds. I counted. *One, two, three*. Long enough for me to see that it was not trying to scare me. It was trying to *be seen*. Then it fell, and the wall was only a wall again, and I heard a sound that was almost a sigh, though it might have been only the drainage shifting in its grates.

The sixth flicker is happening now.

I am on the ramp, the too-broad ramp with risers that strain my thighs, climbing toward the next terrace. The fog thickens as I rise, and the amber light dims, and the heat does not dim—it concentrates, pressing in from all sides like the walls of a thermal womb. And then, ahead, a shape. Not a dimming. A forming. The fog draws itself together in a column, taller than any human, and for a moment I think *it has decided to manifest, it has decided to stop being subtle*, and I stop walking. My breath comes in ragged thin strips. The shape turns. It has no features. It is a density, a standing wave in the mist, a place where the air has chosen to remember that it once had a body.

And then, without sound, it kneels.

Not a collapse. Not a threat. A slow, deliberate folding, the way a saint lowers to accept a crown. The fog-shape bends at what would be the waist, then the knees, until its featureless head is level with the ramp’s surface. It reaches toward me. The arm is made of water droplets and heat-shimmer, a braided cable of condensed breath. It extends one not-hand, and the not-hand opens, and in the center of the not-palm there is a small, dark object. Solid. Real. Not fog.

I do not move.

The not-hand remains extended. The object is a cube, the size of a child’s fist, black as the black strata, and on its upper face there is a single indentation, a shallow square pit. It is not carved. It is *grown*. I can see the mineral layers, the way the cube accreted around an empty space. The city, or the thing in the city, is offering me something. An artifact. A message. A key.

Or a test.

The fog around the kneeling shape shifts, and I understand that the gesture is costing it something. Maintaining this level of concentrated form is not free for a being whose usual scale is geological. The shape is already thinning at the edges, losing its coherence. It waits. The city waits. I am being given a choice, and I can feel my own smallness, my own softness, the temporary heat of my body against the vast, slow metabolism of the place.

I take a step forward.

The cube grows no brighter, no darker. The not-hand does not tremble. The kneeling shape does not raise its head. I take another step. The heat is intense now, not the ambient warmth of before but a focused, purposeful warmth, as if all the city’s geothermal blood has been routed to this one point for my benefit. I reach out my hand—my real, small, skin-wrapped hand—and I touch the cube.

It is warm. But not burning. It is heavy. But not dense. It is the temperature of a stone that has been held in someone’s pocket for years. It is the weight of a thought that has been waiting.

I lift it from the not-palm. The moment my fingers close around it, the kneeling shape begins, very slowly, to rise. The fog unbraids from its not-limbs. The column unpresses itself back into the general haze. But before the shape diffuses completely, a sound reaches me—not through my ears, not through the air, but through the cube itself, vibrating up through the bones of my wrist: a single, low, sustained note. Not a word. Not a name. But a recognition.

*You are here.*

I stand on the ramp, holding the black cube, as the fog settles back into its endless, patient cycle, as the drips resume their metronome, as the city returns to its maintenance. I am not afraid anymore. Not in the way I was. I am something else—a single, warm, finite node in a system of near-infinite duration. The city has noticed me. The city has chosen to *give* me something, not to absorb me, not to dissolve me, but to acknowledge me as a visitor, a witness, a possible future fossil.

I turn the cube over in my hands. There is an opening on the bottom. A narrow slot. It is a container, I realize. Not sealed. Not yet. It is waiting to hold something. A sample. A message. A memory. I do not know what I will put inside it. But as I begin to climb again, the cube warm against my chest, I have the feeling that I will know, eventually. That this is how the city speaks: not in words, but in objects, in gaps, in the negative spaces of its own patience.

And I am listening. I am here. And for once, I am not in a hurry to leave.
