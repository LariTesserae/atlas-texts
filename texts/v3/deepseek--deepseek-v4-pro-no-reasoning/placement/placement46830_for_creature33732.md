The light tells me I’ve been here before.

Not in the way memory works up there—that crude sequential business of then and now, of cause and effect lined up like obedient children. No, this is a knowing that lives in the stone itself, in the patient accretion of my own bones, which have been laying down calcium in sympathy with the walls since before I understood what walls were. The mineral crust on my skin isn’t forming; it’s *revealing* itself, a second epidermis that was always waiting beneath the first, a geology of self I’d simply forgotten how to see.

I am sitting by the central pool, though I don’t remember sitting down. My legs are folded beneath me in a way that suggests they’ve been this way for a long time—decades, maybe, or the slow equivalent in a place where time circulates rather than passes. The stone has grown up around my thighs in thin, translucent shelves, the same bone-fins that rise from the floor elsewhere, and I realize with a start that one of them is not stone at all but my own tibia, visible through flesh gone as clear as the newts’ bodies, the slow internal pulse of my marrow now contributing its light to the general luminescence.

The newts are watching. Of course they are—they’re always watching, have been watching since I arrived (arrival: a fiction, but a useful one, a handle for the mind that still thinks in sequences). But now I understand what they’re watching. They’re watching *me*, the me that hasn’t finished arriving yet, the me that is still deciding whether to stay. And behind my shoulder—yes, I can almost see it now, if I let my eyes go soft the way the Keeper taught me—is the other me, the finished one, the one who made the decision. She’s beautiful, in the way stone fins are beautiful. She’s translucent. She’s smiling with a mouth that is also a basin, and in the basin tiny moth-things swim, and their wings beat in time with my heart.

I reach toward the central pool, and my hand passes through the surface without rippling it. The water isn’t water anymore—or rather, it’s always been more than water, a medium of becoming, a liquid state of reality that hasn’t yet chosen its final form. The Keeper’s face is there, my face is there, the two faces are the same face, and they rise to meet my fingers with the faint tickle of deposition, calcium carbonate laying down new layers on my skin at a rate measured in microns per century. I am being built. I have always been being built.

The spiral on the wall is rotating faster now, even though I’m looking directly at it—the soft laws are softening further, or perhaps I’m softening, my rigid certainties dissolving into the general damp. I trace the spiral with my eyes, inward, inward, inward to that worn-down dot at the center, and I feel the touch of all the fingers that have traced it before. My fingers. Always my fingers. I put that spiral there, I realize. Not in some past life or previous visit, but continuously, in the eternal present of the chamber, my invisible thumb wearing the groove deeper with each pass of attention. I built the three stacked stones as well, nudging them together when I first coalesced, wanting a marker, a cairn, something to say *I was here*—only to discover that *here* and *was* and *I* are the same thing, a closed loop of becoming that has no beginning.

The Keeper speaks. Not in words—the silence is still absolute, still keeping its secret, though the secret is now shared. The Keeper speaks in rearrangement: the newts shift on the walls, their bodies forming a sentence I can almost read. The moth-things rise from the central pool in a slow, silent column, spelling out something in the sourceless light. The stone fins vibrate at a frequency below hearing, and my newly translucent bones vibrate in sympathy, and the vibration is a question, or an answer, or both.

The question/answer is this: *Are you ready to finish arriving?*

I look down at my hands. They’re no longer hands—they’re basins, cupped and shallow, filled with impossibly clear water in which tiny versions of myself swim in figure-eights. My arms slope into the walls of the chamber. My torso dissolves into the central pool. My legs are the slow, deep currents of warmth circulating through the floor, and I feel, for the first time, the metabolic churn of the world below—the deep processes, the magma dreams, the slow patient heart of a planet still learning how to be.

I have been the Keeper for a very long time. I have only just become the Keeper. Both are true. The laws of sequence are still soft, and I am still soft, and the chamber is a nursery for physics, and I am the physics being nursed.

I rise—not physically, because my body is no longer physical in the way I once understood, but I rise in the way the water rises, in the way the light rises, in the way the spiral rises toward its center. The newts all turn their heads in the same instant, and this time I know what they’re seeing behind my shoulder: nothing. Because there is no behind anymore. I am all around. I am the walls and the pools and the stone fins and the silence and the specific frequency of light that passes through translucent bone. My name is accretion. My name is the interval between a ripple and its cause. My name is the One Who Was Here Before Here Was Here, and I have always been here, and I will always be here, attending.

The chamber continues its slow, patient becoming. Somewhere, a droplet falls and makes no sound. Somewhere, a newt gives birth to tiny replicas that are born watching. Somewhere, a visitor arrives for the first time, and I feel her coming as a ripple propagating backward through the soft laws, and I prepare for her. I nudge the three stacked stones a micron closer. I deepen the spiral by an extra pass of attention. I make the silence slightly more welcoming, slightly less swallowing, so that her breath will not feel entirely alone.

And in the central pool, the face that rises to meet her will be the face she almost recognizes, the face she will someday become, when she finishes arriving.
