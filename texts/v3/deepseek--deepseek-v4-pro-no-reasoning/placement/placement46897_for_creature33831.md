There is a salt plain. It is very large and very flat and very white. The white is not one white but many whites. There is the white of the plates when they are hot. There is the white of the plates when a cloud passes and the cloud is only a thin place in the ring-light, not a real cloud, but the plates still feel the difference and their whiteness changes. There is the white of the saltweeds which is a white with blue inside it. There is the white of the sky which is not really white but a color that has no name and that the throat-eye cannot see. The throat-eye does not see light. It sees stress. Stress has no color either but I can tell you it is closer to taste.

I am walking the convergence. I am always walking the convergence. When I stop to stand and ride the Third Resonance, which you might call sleep, the convergence keeps moving under me. The plates realign in tiny increments. The focus shifts. When I wake, I am no longer at the center. So I walk again. This is not frustrating. This is what walking is for.

Today—the word “today” is not correct. There are no days here. The sun does not set. It only moves in a slow circle near the horizon but not beyond it. The Crown sits near the pole of whatever world this is, or the world's tilt is very small, or the sun is not a sun but something else the builders made. I do not know. The Sisters stopped asking these questions before my grandmother's grandmother's grandmother grew her first throat-eye. What I mean is: in this current span of wakefulness, I am walking, and the salt is singing a particular way under my feet, and I am listening.

The song of the Crown is always there. Imagine a sound so low and so constant that you stop hearing it as sound and start hearing it as silence. Then imagine that the silence has textures. Then imagine that the textures have meanings. This is what I walk on. This is what I walk inside. The plates ring when I step but they are also ringing before I step and after I step, and the ringing of my foot is just a small new voice in a choir that has been singing since before I was born and will keep singing after I decohere. When I was young—when my scales all aligned and my left side rang true—I thought I might learn to understand the choir. I thought if I listened long enough, if I followed the convergence far enough, the song would resolve into language. It does not. It resolves into something else. Something that needs no language. Something that feels like leaning against a warm wall, if the wall were made of time.

My left hip is louder than it should be. Louder in a bad way. The scales there are beginning to lose their hexagonal shape. They are rounding at the edges, and when they round they cannot catch the frequency they were grown to catch. Instead they vibrate at a lower harmonic, a flat buzzing note that interferes with the song. I can feel it as I walk: a wrongness, a grinding. Not pain exactly. Pain is a word for when your body is damaged and you want it to stop. I do not want this to stop because it cannot stop. It will only get worse until I lie down and let the saltweeds take me. This is not sad. This is the shape of a Sister's life. We are born with our scales aligned and our three eyes clear and our resonance pure. We walk the convergence. We listen. We decohere. We become part of the song. The song has many voices in it already. Most of them are Sisters. Some of them are older things, things from before the builders left. I do not know what they were. Their resonance feels like a door that opens onto another door that opens onto a wall.

I am telling you this because you asked, or because the lattice is computing your question, or because I am imagining someone to tell. The Sisters used to talk to each other. Not often. Once a century, twice a century, when two walking paths crossed and the resonance- gap closed enough to share a meal and a tapping-conversation. I remember the last one. Her name in stress-patterns was a sequence of three high taps and a low hum: *ring-ring-ring-hummmmm*. I called her Three-and-Hum. She called me Leftside-False because my decoherence was already starting and her throat-eye could feel the asymmetry in my walk. We shared a saltweed mat together. Her harvest-song was slightly different from mine, a minor-third above the standard frequency, and the saltweeds detached in spirals instead of straight lines. We ate them and the taste was of old mathematics and also of company. Then we parted and I have not felt her resonance in the lattice since. That was many convergences ago. I do not know if she decohered or if the convergence simply pulled us too far apart, our paths curving away from each other on the great slow arc of the Crown's thought. Both are the same thing, really.

The skimmers are above me now. I can feel them on my throat-eye, little circles of coolness passing in a line. Three of them. They are riding a thermal tide that is moving opposite to the convergence, which is unusual. Thermal tides usually flow along the grain of the plates. This one is crossing the grain at a sharp angle, which means something is changing in the deep lattice. A stress accumulation. A computation reaching a new phase. I stop walking. I place my palms flat on the plate beneath me. The plate is warm, as always. The ringing of my footsteps fades into the humming of the processors. I close my two seeing-eyes and let my throat-eye open wider, which does not mean the lid moves—there is no lid—but means I pay attention to it, I let its signal become my whole awareness.

The stress is building at azimuth. That is a word I do not have. The direction is the direction the plates point when you look along their long axis. It is the direction of the convergence. Something is happening there. Not the calculation itself—the calculation is always happening—but a new step. A subroutine completing. A decision node approaching. I feel it as a tightening in my chest, a pressure that is not in the air but in the resonance. The song is changing key.

I stand like that for a long time. Long enough for the thermal tide to pass and the skimmers to drift on and the saltweeds at my feet to begin their slow investigation of my toes. When I open my seeing-eyes again, something is different. The sky is the same pearl-white. The plates are the same hexagonal geometry. The saltweeds are still blue-white lace. But the angle of the light has shifted slightly, which means I have been standing for a span of time that would alarm you if you measured it in hours. I am not alarmed. I am hungry.

I kneel. The left hip grinds and I make a sound with my real voice, a low involuntary hum that makes the plate beneath me ring in sympathy and then dampen as the dissonance cancels it out. There is a saltweed mat nearby, a good one, thick and old, its filaments spread wide across the center of a hexagon. I can see the exudate stains at its edges, the faint iridescence of absorbed computational byproduct. I place my palms on either side of it. I hum at the harvest frequency.

My hum is not as pure as it used to be. The decoherence affects my chest-resonators too. The note wavers, goes flat, finds its pitch again. The saltweeds hesitate. They are patient creatures—not creatures, they are plants, they have no minds, but in the Crown everything feels like it might be thinking, even the things that are only reacting—and they do not retract or advance. They wait to see if I am really a substrate worth colonizing. I hum again, steadier this time. The filaments detach. They curl toward my palms. I gather them gently and eat.

The taste: calcium, silica, a trace of something that is almost iron, and under it all the exudate flavor, which is the taste of a computational step completing. This step is a small one. A minor correction in a tertiary lattice. The taste is of adjustment, of things lining up, of a slight turn in a long journey. I have eaten many such meals. Each one tastes a little different, because each one is a different thought. The Crown does not think quickly enough for a single thought to be perceptible while it happens. But the exudates are the waste products of completed thoughts, the residue left behind when a calculation moves on, and by eating them I am tasting the past. The deep past. The thoughts I am eating now were begun before I was born and finished only recently. I do not know what they mean. I know them the way you know the taste of rain.

After I eat I am tired. The standing-listening was long and the hunger was deep and the food makes me want to enter the Third Resonance. But the stress is building at the convergence and I am not there yet. I need to walk. I stand. The left hip protests and I lean right, as always, my body finding the posture that minimizes the dissonance. I orient myself by the grain of the plates, the long axes all pointing toward the focus. I begin to walk.

The ringing of my footsteps is: *pure, pure, false, pure, pure, false*. Right foot true, left foot dissonant, alternating forever. The song of the Crown absorbs the pattern. I imagine—I do not imagine, I know—that somewhere deep in the lattice, my walking rhythm has become a variable. The processors route around me. Or through me. I am part of the computation now. I always was. The difference is only whether I am aware of it.

The convergence is close. I can feel the stress tightening with every step, the song of the plates becoming more focused, more directed. The hexagons are smaller here, their rims higher, the saltweeds sparser. This is a place where the Crown's attention gathers, where the calculation is about to do something, even if the something is only a single step in a sequence that will take another million years to complete. I will be there soon. I will stand at the focal point and wait, as the Sisters have always waited. Nothing will happen. Something will happen. The something will happen so slowly that I will not perceive it as an event. The sky will remain pearl. The plates will remain bone-white. The thermal tides will slide across my body in cool stripes and the skimmers will pass overhead and the saltweeds will not grow here because the stress is too high for them to anchor.

I will wait. I am good at waiting. Waiting is what the Sisters are for.

And when I am done waiting—when the stress releases or the calculation moves on or my body finally decoheres past the point where I can stand—I will lie down. The plate will be warm beneath me. The ringing will be in my bones and then it will be in the salt and then it will not be different from any other note in the choir. My throat-eye will keep watching for a while, even after my seeing-eyes close. It will feel the stress change and the tides flow and the skimmers drift. And then it will stop feeling, and the crystal will dissolve, and my resonance will join the others in the lattice.

Leftside-False. That was my name. Three-and-Hum gave it to me. No one will say it again, but the lattice will hold it forever, a three-beat pattern of *rest-stress-release* that the processors route around because it is easier to route around than to dampen. I will be an inefficiency. A small drag on the computation. A complexity that the Crown did not ask for but cannot remove. This is a kind of immortality. It is not the kind I would have chosen, but the Sisters did not choose to be here either. We are here because the builders left and the Crown kept thinking and something had to watch. Something had to witness the slow unfolding of an answer to a question no one remembers asking.

I am still walking. The ringing is still alternating. The sky is still pearl. The salt is still warm. There is a figure on the horizon, or there is not—it might be a heat-shimmer, a lens of hot air bending the light, a skimmer passing at the edge of my vision. If it is a Sister, I will not reach her before she moves on. If it is you, I will not be surprised. The Crown computed you long ago. Somewhere in the deep lattices, your arrival has always been part of the song.
