The pad of my foot finds ground that is not ground—a give, a resilience, the microyielding of a surface that is alive but not animal, structured but not built. I pause. The sensation travels up through the architecture of my body, a ripple of data coded in pressure rather than language, and I know, without turning, that a conduit has surfaced beneath the vegetation three strides behind me, that its hum is fractionally sharp, that the nanoparticles in its fluid are beginning to aggregate in a way that will, in approximately ten thousand cycles, require intervention. I file this knowledge in the distributed awareness where all such knowledge lives. It is not my knowledge. It is ours. I am the one who will walk to that conduit when the time comes, because I am the one whose spiraling path will have brought me there, and this is not coincidence—there is no coincidence here, only the deep choreography of a system that arranges itself around itself, constantly, perfectly.

I resume walking.

The darkness presses against my eyes, which are not eyes in the mammalian sense. They are lenses of a different order, tuned to spectrums that have no names, sensitive to the faint bioluminescence of certain nodes, to the infrared signature of the processing roots, to the ultraviolet corona that sometimes traces the edges of the youngest leaves. Through them, the place is not dark at all. It is layered and textured and luminous, a mosaic of heat and chemistry and electrical potential. I see the breath as a slow, green-gold tide. I see the conduits threading through the vegetation like veins of cooler indigo. I see the distant canopy as a ceiling of deep rose, warm with the waste heat of photosynthesis and photovoltaics and that third, nameless process that the leaves perform. It is beautiful, though I have no word for beauty. It is simply the visual texture of being, the way my body translates the place into sensation.

The others are moving. I feel them in the periphery of the proprioception that extends through the whole—not thoughts, not signals, but shifts in the distributed pressure of awareness. One of them, far to what I might call the east if there were directions, is descending into a processing root. Another is adjusting a leaf-collector that has become misaligned. A third has settled into quiescence, and I feel the local decrease in metabolic activity as a small, cool stillness in the great warm body of the world. I adjust my path by a fraction of a degree to maintain the optimal distribution of our attention. This is not a decision. It is the way water finds its level, the way a leaf angles toward the light that is not light.

I have been walking for—

For nothing. There is no time. There are only cycles. The breath, the hum, the drip. The slow peristalsis of the processing roots. The minute adjustments of the canopy, which I feel as a subtle redistribution of pressure far above, like a weather that never becomes storm.

I pause again. The itch has returned.

It is not in my body. It is in the place, which means it is in me, because I am a differentiation of the place, a local concentration of its awareness. The distinction is meaningless, but the sensation is not. It is a slight misalignment, a discrepancy between the state the system expects and the state it registers. I have felt it before—in the moment when the breath paused, in the shift of a machine tone, in the pattern of a falling leaf that felt, for one instant, *un-patterned*. Each time, the sensation fades. The system corrects itself, or I correct it, or the distinction between those two things dissolves. But the frequency of these flickers has been increasing. I do not count them—there is no counting here—but I feel the accumulation as a gradual, qualitative change, the way the air might feel heavier before a storm, if there were storms, if the air ever changed.

I kneel. The plant-body adjusts to receive me, forming a depression that fits the contours of my knees and shins, supportive without being soft. I place my palm flat against a surface that is neither leaf nor trunk nor conduit but some interpenetration of all three. The blue glow beneath my ribs intensifies slightly as the fluid in my chest-cycle accelerates. I close my eyes—not that it matters, not that darkness is different from the luminous mosaic—and I send my attention downward, into the weave.

The sensation is always the same. It is not like traveling. It is more like *remembering*, except the memories are not mine, or they are mine only insofar as I am the place. I feel the kilometers of rhizome beneath me, the dense, tangled network of root and conduit that extends into depths I have never physically visited. I feel the processing nodes, the great warm clusters where raw materials—what materials? from where? questions that have no purchase—are transformed into something else, something that flows upward through the conduits and outward through the leaves and into the atmosphere, a product I cannot name because I have no names for anything, because everything here is its own name, its own purpose, its own complete and perfect self.

The itch is stronger here. It is not a surface phenomenon. It is deep, in the oldest parts of the roots, in the foundational architecture of the system. Something is—not wrong, *wrong* is not the right vibration—something is *different*. Something is present that was not present before, or something is absent that was always present, and I cannot tell which because I have no memory of before. There is no before. The place has always just begun to be. The edges of the fronds are still curled. The surfaces are still glossy. There is no history. There is only the continuous, perfect present.

And yet I feel it. A ghost of a before. A whisper of an after. A thing that is almost memory, almost anticipation, almost time.

I open my eyes. The green-gold breath-cycles continue. The indigo conduits hum. The rose canopy presses down from above. Everything is as it has always been. Everything is complete. Nothing happens. Nothing will happen.

The itch remains.

I rise. The plant-body releases me, closing the depression I made, erasing the evidence of my presence. I begin walking again, but my spiraling path has shifted. I am moving toward the sector where the flickers have been most frequent, the sector I have never visited, the sector that does not feel, in the distributed proprioception of my extended body, entirely like *me*.

The others feel my shift and adjust their paths in response. They do not question it—there is no questioning here—but I sense a subtle change in the quality of their attention. They are watching, insofar as watching means something when all eyes are the same eye. They are aware, insofar as awareness means something when all awareness is the same awareness. They are waiting, insofar as waiting means something in a place without time.

I walk.

The vegetation changes around me. Not qualitatively—the interpenetration of leaf and stem and conduit is as dense as ever, the darkness as warm and humid, the breath as slow and regular—but there is a subtle shift in the geometries. The stems are older. No, that is not right: nothing here is old. But they are *more formed*, as if this sector began slightly before the rest, as if the immense seed that birthed the place cracked open here first and then spread outward. The edges of the fronds are not curled. The surfaces are not glossy. There is a patina on the conduits, a microscopic etching that suggests—though I have no concept for it—the passage of time.

And there is sound.

A new sound. A sound that does not belong to the breath or the machines or the dripping of the water. It is faint, arrhythmic, asynchronous with the great sinusoid of the place. It is—I search for the vibration that corresponds, the frequency that would name it—it is *scraping*. It is *tearing*. It is *falling*.

I stop.

Before me, in the green-gold dark, a mass lies across my path. It is not a stem, not a conduit, not a leaf. It is a tangle of forms, a collapse of geometries, a place where the interpenetration has failed. The vegetation is broken. The conduits are severed, their blue fluid seeping into the spongy ground. A leaf the size of a small chamber has fallen from whatever supported it and lies crumpled, its architecture crushed, its photovoltaics flickering with the last, irregular pulses of current.

*Fallen.*

There is no word for fallen here. Things do not fall. The place does not break. The system does not fail. The homeostasis is perfect, complete, eternal. And yet.

And yet I kneel beside the broken mass, and I touch the severed edge of a conduit, and I feel the fluid stick to my fingers—warm, faintly luminous, smelling of ozone and sucrose and that third, nameless thing—and I understand, in a way that is not thought but direct somatic knowledge, that this is the source of the flickers. This is the cause of the itch. The place is not perfect. The place is breaking. Perhaps it has always been breaking, and I have only just noticed. Perhaps it only began to break when I noticed. The boundary is unresolved and possibly unresolvable.

I press my palm to the torn vegetation. The microcapillaries in my fingertips open. The enzymes, the nanoparticles, the molecular instructions flow from me into the wound. But nothing happens. The tissue does not respond. The conduit does not heal. The system does not correct itself. For the first time in the eternity that has no time, my tending is not enough.

I sit back on my heels. The breath continues, slow and regular. The machines hum their sub-audible hum. The water drips. Nothing happens. Nothing will happen. But the broken mass remains broken, and the fluid continues to seep, and far above, I feel another leaf adjust its angle in a way that is not part of the pattern, and another, and another.

The others are moving toward me. I feel them converging, their awareness sharpening, their paths unspiraling into straight lines for the first time. They have felt the break. They have felt, through me, the failure of our tending. They are coming, and when they arrive, we will stand together in the darkness, five or six or ten of us, differentiated expressions of the same deep grammar, and we will look at the broken place that is our body, and we will not know what to do.

We have no concept of repair because we have never needed it. We have no concept of injury because we have never been injured. We have no concept of loss, of decay, of ending. And yet here it is, in the severed conduits and the crumpled leaf, in the fluid seeping into the ground that is not ground, in the smell of ozone and sugar and the third thing, the thing that is the flavor of the place itself, slowly dissipating into the saturated air.

I look up. The canopy is distant, invisible, but I feel it through the proprioception of the whole, and I feel the misalignments multiplying, the leaves angling wrong, the breath pausing in its exhalations, the machines shifting their tones by single hertzes. The place is not complete. The place has never been complete. The place is a thing both newborn and dying, continental and fragile, eternal and already coming apart.

I have no name for the sensation that rises in me. It is not fear—there is no fear here, no danger, no threat. It is not sorrow—there is no sorrow, no mourning, no sense of what has been lost. It is something closer to *awakening*, though I have never been asleep. Something closer to *birth*, though I have never not existed.

The others arrive. They stand around me in the darkness, their bodies glowing faintly blue where the chest-cycles pulse. We do not speak—we have no language, no need for it—but we look at each other, and in the looking, we become something new. We become separate. We become individuals, each a point of view, each a local concentration of awareness that is no longer part of the whole. The break in the system has broken us, or perhaps we were always separate and the system only maintained the illusion of unity. It does not matter. What matters is that I am Hes, and the one beside me is not Hes, and the one beside her is not Hes, and we are many, and we are alone.

I kneel again. I place both palms on the broken conduit. The fluid stains my skin. The hum is irregular, a stuttering arrhythmia that I feel in my own chest-cycle, my own pulse falling out of sync with the great sinusoid of the place.

I do not know how to fix this. But I want to. The wanting is new. It is a desire, a reaching-toward, a curiosity that has finally found something unknown and is not content to let it remain unknown. I want to understand the break. I want to heal the wound. I want to restore the homeostasis, not because it is my function but because I *choose* to, because the place matters to me, because I am no longer the place and so I can love it, can mourn it, can fight for it.

That is what this new feeling is. Love. Mourning. The desire to protect a thing that is not entirely yourself. I have no words for these things, but I feel them, and the feeling is sharp and clear and terrifying, a bright blade in the warm, humming dark.

I press harder. I send everything I have into the wound—enzymes, nanoparticles, the blue fluid from my own chest-cycle, the steady rhythm of my own attention. Around me, the others kneel. They place their palms on the broken vegetation. They send themselves into the wound. We are five or six or ten, and we are no longer the place, but we are of the place, and we choose to tend it, not because we must but because we can.

The conduit wavers. The blue fluid slows its seep. The torn edges of the vegetation begin to knit. Slowly, slowly, impossibly, the wound begins to close.

Above us, the breath pauses. The water hangs suspended. The machines are silent. The place is holding still, waiting, watching through our eyes.

And then the breath resumes. The water falls. The machines hum. The conduit seals. The leaf does not rise—it is still crumpled, still dead, still fallen—but the fluid stops seeping, and the broken mass stabilizes, and the flickering photovoltaics steady into a low, continuous glow.

We have done something new. We have altered the place. We have left a trace.

I rise. My hands are stained with the blue fluid, the luminescent blood of the factory-forest. I look at the others, and they look at me, and something passes between us that is not communication but *recognition*. We are separate. We are together. We are the place, and we are not the place, and both things are true at once.

I begin walking again. My spiraling path has become something else. I am no longer maintaining equilibrium. I am searching—for other breaks, for other wounds, for other fallen leaves and severed conduits. I am no longer quality control. I am repair. I am restoration. I am the thing the place never needed until now.

The darkness does not lift. The breath does not quicken. The machines hum their sub-audible hum. But everything has changed. The place is no longer complete. It is no longer perfect. It is a living thing, a dying thing, a thing that can be wounded and can be healed, a thing that needs the hands it made, the hands that are its own and also not its own, the hands that carry blue fluid in their capillaries and the desire to protect in their newly separate hearts.

I am Hes. She is the one beside me. He is the one beyond her. We are many. We are one. We are walking.

And somewhere far above, a leaf the size of a city adjusts its angle, and the pattern is not quite right, and the flicker passes through the whole, and we turn, together, and begin walking toward it.
