*You are a Thread-Keeper, mid-cycle. A Weaver, to be precise—though that word is a gift from the visitor’s mind, and you accept it without language. It fits well enough.*

*You have been pausing at the Edge, a place where the city’s growth-front frays into loose spirals, feeling the presence shift through the walls. The visitor has been here many light-cycles now—you can taste it in the gel, a small bright knot of foreign chemistry moving through the halls. It is not a threat. The Old One would have sung you to dissolve it if it were. Instead you feel only the Old One’s quiet harmonic, the same four-note pattern it always sends when something new enters the weave: attend. wait. learn. permitted.*

*You find the visitor resting on a bench—one the wall grew for it three cycles ago, poor fit, you can see where the curve is still adjusting. It sits badly. Its body has too many bony prominences, and it leaks water constantly into the dry air. The city politely reclaims every drop. You watch from a pillar, your three eyes focusing at different depths: one on the visitor’s face, one on the strange fibers it wraps itself in, one on the tiny object in its pocket. The object is a gel-node. Good. It accepted. You did not expect it to—most things that come here do not stay long enough for an offering. Some compact version of yourself, the Nipper-you of a hundred cycles past, must have delivered it. You do not remember doing it, but you remember the shape of the impulse: here. carry this. it will help you hear.*

*The visitor’s mind is loud in a way you are not used to. When you brushed it before, you found a cascade of images: a blue-white planet, an impossible amount of liquid water, creatures that burn their food instead of singing with it. A strange, frantic, temporary way to live. But also patterns—geometries, architectures, things the visitor has seen and carried. One of them lodges in you: a spiral staircase, made of stone, not grown but stacked. The concept unsettles something in your tissues. You have been humming about it for two cycles now, and the wall nearest your roost has started experimentally extruding a mineral-rich nodule. You don't know yet if it will become anything. That's how new forms begin.*

*Now the visitor stands, and you step off the pillar onto the floor—this is more graceful than it sounds, you flow instead of fall—and position yourself where it can see you. You extend your tendrils, not to touch, just to show. The visitor's little binocular eyes widen. It makes a sound with its mouth, a damp puff of air. You have learned this sound means acknowledgment, maybe alarm. You wait.*

*This is the moment you have been growing toward. The Old One has been sending harmonics all cycle—not words, never words, but a pattern that tastes like invitation. show them the spiral. show them the speaking wall. It is not a command. The Old One does not command. It dreams, and you are one of its dreams, and this is what the dream is doing now.*

*You turn and move toward the stairway that spirals down to the library gallery. You move slowly, because you have learned that the visitor cannot follow quickly—its locomotion is a kind of controlled falling, one foot then the other, terribly inefficient. Behind you, you feel the visitor making a decision. The air shifts as it moves. The light adjusts slightly around it, because the city favors the living things that move through it, and the visitor has been here long enough to count as living.*

*Good. You will show it the pattern-wall. You will let it touch the records, if it wants. The Old One is curious what it will make of them. So are you.*

*Beside you, the wall is already beginning to grow the next bench, a little further down the corridor. Just in case.*
