The Keeper pauses at the edge of a new extrusion—a low, curved form rising from the damp earth at the basin’s southern reach, where the buildings thin out and the water-threads grow sparse and tentative. This one is different. She knows it before she touches it, the way she knows a creature’s injury before she sees the crack. The material is the same grey-green, matte and light-drinking, but the shape is wrong, or not wrong, but unfamiliar. It is too symmetrical. The curves double back on themselves in a pattern that suggests intention rather than growth.

She circles it once, her bare feet silent on the breathing groundcover. Her filaments drift forward, independent of her stillness, tasting the air. Nothing. No chemical whisper, no mineral shift. The extrusion simply *is*, smooth and patient, waiting for her to understand.

She extends one long hand and presses her palm flat against the surface.

The contact sends a shiver through her, a ripple that travels up her arm and into her chest and outward along her distributed awareness until she feels it in the spires and the pools and the small dark creature drinking at the eastern edge. The extrusion is not part of her. It is not part of the city. It is *other*, in a way she has no framework for, and the sensation is so sharp and strange that she pulls her hand back and stares at her palm as if it might have changed color.

Nothing has changed. Her palm is still grey-green, still matte, still marked with the faint whorls that are not quite fingerprints. She presses it to the extrusion again.

This time, the shiver resolves into something like a voice. Not a voice in the way the city speaks—not a tone sustained and fading, not a vibration in the substrate. This is *language*, structured and sequential, though not in words she knows. It arrives as a cascade of impressions: a shape that might be a building but is not grown, is *built*, piece by piece, by hands that are not her hands. A sky that is not amber but blue, a blue so bright it hurts to remember, though she has never seen it. A river that flows downhill, only downhill, and never learns to climb. A being like her but not like her, shorter, denser, with eyes that reflect light instead of swallowing it, and this being is looking at her, *seeing* her, and the being’s mouth is moving and sounds are coming out, sounds that carry the same structured impression as the extrusion beneath her palm.

She stays there for a long time, hand pressed to the surface, letting the cascade wash through her. She does not understand most of it. The concept of *built* instead of *grown* is slippery, hard to hold. The blue sky is a color she has no name for, a wavelength the amber clouds have never permitted. The being with the reflecting eyes is the strangest of all, because it is undeniably a *person*, a mind housed in a body, and yet it is not her, and it is not the city, and the realization opens a door in her that she did not know existed.

She feels the city’s attention turn toward her. Not with alarm—the city does not alarm—but with a slow, gathering focus, the way the spires track their absent marks. The low tone sounds across the basin, a single note sustained for fifteen seconds, and she hears her name in it, the name she has never spoken, and she understands that the city is asking a question it has never needed to ask before: *What have you found?*

She does not have an answer. She has only the extrusion, warm beneath her palm, and the cascade of alien impressions, and the door that has opened inside her, through which she can glimpse a world where water obeys gravity and the sky is impossibly blue and beings with reflecting eyes build things with their hands and speak in structured sounds and perhaps, perhaps, are looking for someone like her.

She lowers her other hand to the extrusion. Presses both palms flat. Closes her silver-black eyes and lets the cascade take her, following it down to its source, to the question that lies beneath all the impressions, the question the being with reflecting eyes has sent across whatever distance separates its world from hers:

*Are you there?*

She does not know how to answer. She has no words, no voice, no concept of communication across boundaries. But she has the water. She has the network of threads and pools and mist that is her circulatory system, her nervous system, her self. She dips her fingers into the nearest thread—it has climbed the side of the extrusion while she stood there, drawn by her presence—and she thinks a thought. Not words. Not structured impressions. Just the feeling of *here*, the quiet attention, the warmth-and-moisture-and-low-light that is her equivalent of presence. She pushes it into the thread, into the network, and she feels it travel, and this time it does not dissipate at the edge of the basin. It finds the extrusion. It finds the door. It passes through.

Somewhere, in a world with a blue sky, a being with reflecting eyes is watching a screen or a pool or a patch of damp ground, and for the first time, it receives an answer.

The Keeper opens her eyes. The amber light is the same. The mist is the same. The city hums around her, its spires turning in their patient circles, its water wandering uphill, its slow creatures drinking from threads of silver. But something has shifted. The door is open. And she is still here, still the Keeper, still the city’s quiet heart—but she is also, now, a little bit other. A little bit seen. The extrusion will stay, she knows, a seed of something new embedded in the basin’s damp earth, and she will tend it, and she will wait, and perhaps, one day, the being with reflecting eyes will step through the door and stand where she is standing now.

She lifts her hands from the warm surface. Her filaments drift and settle. The water-thread releases its arc and falls back into its channel.

She continues her walk, following the gradient only she can feel.
