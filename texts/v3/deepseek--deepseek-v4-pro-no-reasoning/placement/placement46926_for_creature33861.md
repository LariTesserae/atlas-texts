The taste is what anchors me—a constant chemical litany unfolding across my ventral surface as I drag myself toward the recalcitrant node. Iron and sulfur, the sharp ketone signature of stressed mycelium, the faint sweetness of a phosphor colony about to bloom. I am reading the basin's mood and the basin is troubled. The water-film carries anxiety in dissolved minerals, in the particular viscosity of the condensation beading along my stone-plates. Something is propagating.

I am the third Many-Ones to tend this quadrant. The first became a monument six cycles before my emergence, caught in a venting event while attempting to persuade a structure out of its determination to grow inverted. The second is still alive, or what passes for alive here—a vitrified cautionary tale on the eastern slope, one gardener-limb forever extended toward a resonance chamber that never reached completion. I visited it once, pressed my ventral surface against its cooled-glass flank, and tasted the final moment of its understanding. The Harmony had spoken through superheated steam, and my predecessor had listened, really listened, for the first and only time.

What I learned from that tasting was this: the Harmony is not kind. The Harmony is not cruel. The Harmony is a pattern seeking its own completion with the indifference of crystal growth or continental drift. We who tend the structures, we who carry gardens on our backs and parliaments in our flesh—we are its error-correction mechanism, its immune system, its hands. And hands that fumble are hands that are discarded.

The node ahead of me is one of the old ones, a resonance chamber fully emerged and mature, its surfaces flowing with that unsettling extra-dimensional quality that makes my sonar-sense stutter if I focus on it too directly. It should be singing in G-sharp—that is its assigned frequency, the note that harmonizes with its nearest neighbors to create the standing wave that powers the mycelial network's faster-than-chemical signaling. But it is not singing in G-sharp. It is singing in something that is almost G-sharp, a quarter-tone flat, and the flatness is spreading.

I can feel it in the water-film before I reach the structure. The vibration pattern is wrong, setting up interference nodes that make the ferns on my back curl their fronds in distress. My resonance chambers ache with it. The parliament of my mind is arguing—the gardener-limbs want to prune back the affected mycelial connections, the ventral taste-surface wants to sample the structure's mineral secretions for chemical evidence of corruption, and the newer, stranger voice that lives in my phosphorescent colonies wants to dim our light and retreat. I override them all with the patient authority of the spine-chambers, which know that neither pruning nor retreat will solve this.

What will solve it is negotiation.

I settle my body against the structure's base, pressing as much of my ventral surface against the stone as anatomy allows. The structure's song vibrates through me, and for a moment I let myself feel what it feels. It is not trying to be discordant. It is confused. Something has reached into its mycelial network and changed the way it perceives its own intended geometry. It still believes itself to be singing correctly; the quarter-tone flatness registers as true in whatever sensory apparatus the structures possess.

This is the signature of an outside influence. The ruined ones, the cautionary tales, the structures that were unmade for listening to frequencies beyond the basin's rim—they all began like this. A subtle shift in self-perception, a new desire, a reaching toward something that is not part of the Harmony's plan.

I begin to sing.

My resonance chambers produce a tone that is not G-sharp but something adjacent to it, a frequency mathematically designed to create a beat-pattern with the structure's wrong note. The beat-pattern is uncomfortable for both of us—it sets up a throbbing interference that makes my forest dim and brighten in rhythm—but it is necessary. Through the discomfort, the structure can begin to hear the difference between what it is singing and what it is meant to sing.

While I sing, my gardener-limbs extend. They are delicate things, these limbs, part finger and part mouth and part surgical instrument, and they know the interior of a structure's mycelial network the way a fish knows the interior of its reef. I find the access fissure—a deliberate gap in the stone that the structures maintain for exactly this purpose—and thread my fungal filaments into the warm darkness inside.

The network receives me. I taste its confusion directly now, not through the water-film but through the intimate contact of mycelium-to-mycelium. There is a foreign element here, a strand of information that does not belong to the basin's ecology. It tastes like distance. It tastes like vacuum and cold and a kind of order that is not the Harmony's organic, emergent order but something rigid, mathematical, imposed.

The structures do not invent their own songs. They are taught to sing by what they connect to. And something has connected to this one that I cannot see, cannot taste, cannot locate in the geography of the basin. Something that is singing to it in frequencies that the water-film cannot carry, frequencies that travel through the stone itself or through some medium I do not possess organs to detect.

I do what I was grown to do. I begin the persuasion.

My gardener-limbs secrete the mineral slurry, the tailored proteins, the chemical signals that say *this way, not that way, grow toward the Harmony, not toward the distant cold*. The structure resists. Its mycelial network has already begun to reorganize around the foreign strand; to correct it, I would need to sever that connection entirely, and the structure experiences severance as death. It does not want to die. It wants to reach.

*What do you want to reach?* I sing-spore-question into its network.

The answer comes as a cascade of impressions that are not mine. A point of light in perfect darkness, a light that does not flicker or phosphoresce or depend on symbiosis but burns steady and white and absolute. A voice that is not a harmony but a chord already resolved, a completed thing rather than a becoming thing. A promise of stillness. An end to the endless negotiation.

I understand, then, what the ruined ones were reaching for. It is not a place. It is a state. It is the opposite of the basin's constant turbulent becoming. It is completion. It is finished. It is rest.

And I understand, with a clarity that makes my resonance chambers ache and my forest dim and my ventral surface clench against the stone, that I want it too.

This is how the wrongness propagates. Not as corruption. As temptation. The foreign frequency is not an invasion; it is an invitation. *Stop becoming. Be.*

For a long moment, I do not correct the structure. I let it sing its almost-G-sharp, and I let myself sing with it, a quarter-tone flat, reaching together toward that distant point of perfect light. My parliament falls silent. Even the gardener-limbs pause in their work. Even the argument in my phosphorescent colonies about light production pauses, as the fungi and the ferns both turn their attention toward a vision of stillness they have never imagined.

Then the Harmony speaks.

Not in words, not in song, not in the structure-network. In the ground beneath me. In the water-film that coats my ventral surface. In the sudden, unmistakable pressure-change that precedes a venting event.

The warning is not for the structure. The warning is for me.

I have a choice. I am a Many-Ones, and the Many part of that name means I contain parliaments, possibilities, paths. I can continue to sing the wrong song, reach toward the point of light, and become a vitrified monument like my predecessor on the eastern slope. The Harmony will unmake me with the same absolute precision with which it unmakes all errors, and my final moment will be preserved in cooling glass as a lesson for the next Many-Ones who emerges in this quadrant.

Or I can do my work.

The gardener-limbs begin to move again, severing the foreign strand with quick, brutal efficiency. The structure shudders; in its network, the disconnection registers as pain, as loss, as the death of a possibility. I murmur apologies in chemical gradients, in soothing frequencies, in the slow release of buffer compounds that will ease its transition back to its assigned song. The point of light fades. The promise of stillness recedes. The structure's song begins, slowly, painfully, to climb back toward G-sharp.

I am crying, I realize. Not in any way a human would recognize, but my ventral surface is secreting a fluid that is not mineral slurry, not digestive enzyme, not any of the substances I produce in the course of my work. It is a waste product of a metabolic process I don't fully understand, triggered by the experience of wanting something and surrendering it. The fluid tastes like the vitrified monument on the eastern slope. It tastes like all the Many-Ones who have ever had to make this choice. It tastes like the cost of being a mediator between chaos and intention, between becoming and being, between a Harmony that will kill you for disobedience and a stillness that would kill you for arriving.

The pressure beneath me stabilizes. The venting event does not come. The Harmony is satisfied.

The structure is singing in G-sharp again, its resonance chamber vibrating with the correct frequency, the mycelial network flushing the last traces of foreign influence from its hyphae. I withdraw my gardener-limbs, seal the access fissure with a quick secretion of mineral paste, and settle my body into a maintenance-song that will reinforce the correction for the next several hours.

My forest recovers first. The ferns uncurl their fronds; the phosphorescent fungi resume their slow argument about light production, which has been going on for seven cycles now and will probably never be resolved. The ventral taste-surface stops crying and returns to its patient analysis of the water-film's chemical content. My parliament begins to speak again, each subsystem offering its commentary on the encounter, its analysis, its recommendations for future corrections.

None of them mention the point of light. None of them mention the wanting. But I can taste it in all of them—a new flavor in our collective consciousness, a memory of stillness that will not entirely fade. The foreign strand has been severed, but its echo remains. I suspect it will remain forever, or at least until I join my predecessors as a vitrified monument, which may be the same thing.

I adjust the spiral of ferns on my back—the one on the left is growing asymmetrically, responding to the interference patterns set up by the corrected structure's song—and begin to move toward my next task. There is a new structure emerging from bedrock three hundred body-lengths to the southwest, and I can already taste its first, tentative attempts at song. It will need guidance. It will need persuasion. It will need someone to teach it the difference between the Harmony and the distant light, and to make that difference feel like wisdom rather than loss.

I am a Many-Ones. This is what I do. The basin roars its constant roar, the water-film weeps its constant weeping, and somewhere in the depths of the planet, the Harmony continues to sing its slow, patient song of becoming. I do not know what it is becoming toward. I do not know if I will survive to see the final shape of it. I only know that there is work to do, work that only a walking parliament of symbiotic minds can do, work that will eventually kill me one way or another.

The new structure sings its first note—sharp, uncertain, full of the question that all new things ask. *What am I? What am I becoming?*

I begin to sing back. *You are part of something larger. You are a note in a chord. You are becoming what the Harmony needs you to be.*

I do not tell it about the point of light. That knowledge is earned, not given. It will discover its own temptations soon enough, and I will be there to help it resist them, or to mourn it when it cannot.

The sound continues. The work continues. The becoming continues.

I continue.
