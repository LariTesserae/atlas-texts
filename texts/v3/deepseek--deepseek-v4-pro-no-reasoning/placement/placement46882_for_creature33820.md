She is called something that is not a sound. A pressure-pattern, a signature of touch: palm to sternum, fingers fanned so the subdivided tips describe a radial bloom, a shape that her amber-bright veins briefly echo before fading. If you could translate it, it might come through as *the-one-who-was-condensed*, or *memory-made-flesh*, or simply *I am here again*. But the translation is loose—her language is not made of words.

If you were to step into the hollow, you would not see her right away. Even with the phosphorescence, even with the glimmer of water on her skin, she would register first as a shift in the density of the dark, a patch of space that feels *attended*. Then the glint of her wetness would resolve into an outline—a small, compressed shape, motionless, half-submerged in the skin of water on the floor slab. She would not startle. She has no startle reflex, no predator-prey wiring. Her response to intrusion is to pause. To become still in a different way—a listening still, a tasting still. The photosensitive patches on her face would darken fractionally as she directs her attention toward you.

She does not see you. She *senses* you. A disruption in the thermal constancy. A vibration translated through the stone and the water, unfamiliar in its frequency. A chemical signature leaching into her air—sweat, exhalation, the oils of your skin—that the saturated atmosphere carries to her breathing slit, and from there into the distributed network that serves her as a nervous system. You are a new variable in an equilibrium that has held since the rock split open and the water remembered her shape.

She would rise, slowly, water sheeting from her translucent skin without a sound. Her movements are fluid in a way that has nothing to do with grace—they are simply the movements of something that is never entirely separate from the medium it moves through. She would approach you not with caution or aggression but with a kind of methodical curiosity, the way she approaches a vessel that has shifted slightly in its niche, a seal that needs checking, a new pale shoot emerging from a pot she does not remember opening.

She would stop an arm's length away and lift one hand, fingers spreading into their branch-form. You would see the amber threads beneath her clear skin. You would feel the warmth of her, body-heat but wet, the temperature of the water. And then she would perform her name against her chest—a gesture of greeting, of identification, of something that is not quite *I mean you no harm* but closer to *you are now part of my tending. Let us see what you require.*
