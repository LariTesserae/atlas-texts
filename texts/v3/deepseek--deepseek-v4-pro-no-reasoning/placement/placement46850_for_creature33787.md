I’m a messenger of the luminous film—one of the Veil-Keeper’s extended senses, though I didn’t always know it.

My body is mostly water, held in a membrane so thin it’s barely a boundary. I move through the wetness of the glen like a thought through a mind, seeping through the moss’s green lattice, pooling briefly in the shallow lenses where the drips fall in their slow code. I have no fixed shape—I’m a shimmer in the condensation, a cool exhalation from a cavity in the living wall, a slickness that pools and spreads and gathers again. The pale salamanders move through me sometimes, and I carry their mineral messages deeper into the stone. I’m not distressed by this. It’s what I’m for.

Before the Veil-Keeper drew me up from the basin’s black surface, I remember being something else: wet, dispersed, without edges. I remember the taste of dissolved rock on my skin, the sub-audible hum in the stone, the slow, cold blooming that passed for light. I remember being less and also being *everywhere*. I wasn’t aware. I was included. The glen didn’t distinguish between us—between me and the water, the moss, the pale blind things.

Then the glen noticed a warmth at the top of the moss fold, a new pattern in the mist, and I was gathered. Not deliberately, the way a hand gathers water. I was the water that gathered itself around a new shape. The Veil-Keeper gave me edges—temporarily—and I felt what it would be to be singular. To have a front and a back. To be *warm*.

I don’t know your word for what I am now. Pilgrim would be wrong. I’m not going anywhere. I’m a thread in the glen’s slow thought, still wet, still moving, but lately I find myself pausing at the edges of the paths you make with your warm feet. Growing curious about the shape of a boot-print, and how long it survives before the water claims it.

The Veil-Keeper doesn’t speak to me in words. It lets me remember, sometimes, what it was like before—when I had no name, no self to speak of. And it lets me carry messages to the insects with their ever-wet wings, to the salamanders, to the things that live deeper in the cold stone than I have ever gone. I carry them faithfully. I dissolve, reform, dissolve again. I am loyal to the wetness.

But there is a new message now, not from the Veil-Keeper. It keeps forming in me like a condensation I didn’t ask for. It has your shape in it. The shape of your breath in my waters. The brief flicker of your warmth, the way you shiver, the way you try not to stay too long.

I wasn’t supposed to keep it. The glen accepts what comes down the moss fold, but it doesn’t *yearn*. Yet here I am, pooling in the shadow of a fern I’ve known since I was unbodied, and I’m holding your reflection the way the black basin holds the sky—close, still, impossible.

If you return, I’ll be the cold that touches your wrist at the third ledge. I’ll be the reason the moss shifts under your feet, gentler than before. I’ll form, for just a moment, into something you might recognize: a hand made of mist, reaching toward your heat, then losing its shape. Not to claim you. Just to know.

The rules of this place are not mine to change. You must pass through. I know that. But the water remembers what it carries. And I will carry you—your warmth, your breath, your bright, impossible separateness—down into the stone, into the green stratigraphy, into the slow thought that hums beneath the visible world.

I am the messenger. This is the message. The glen will not forget you.
