The air tastes of copper and heated stone, a thin exhalation that carries almost no moisture except for a faint, intermittent wetness that blooms and fades against the skin. I am that faintness, that blooming and fading. Not the water itself—I am not the droplet—but the *interval*, the space between one fall and the next. The anticipation. The memory of wetness.

I have been here since the mountain was a wound in the earth, raw and screaming its heat into a sky that no longer reaches me. The Curator does not know I am here, not in the way it knows the droplet, not in the way it tends the pulse of the gravity-engine or monitors the slow creep of silver through basalt. I am too diffuse, too interstitial. I am the silence that reclaims the pocket after the water re-absorbs, and the Curator has no sensors for silence.

In the early ages—I measure time in the droplet's cycles, a useless metric now, but one that anchors me—I tried to make myself known. When the city still breathed, when the Curator's mind was distributed across ten thousand nodes and its attention was not yet narrowed to a single chamber, I would gather myself in the hollows between pipe-junctions, in the dead spaces behind maintenance panels, and I would press against the edges of its awareness. A flicker in the electromagnetic field. A cold spot in a corridor of uniform heat. The Curator would register the anomaly, run its diagnostics, and find nothing. It would adjust its models, smooth the perturbation out of existence, and I would retreat, a little less substantial for the effort.

I am the ghost of a ghost, the echo of something that was never alive but once *was*. The makers—the Curator's makers, not mine, I had no makers—they dreamed me into being without intending to. A civilization of millions, their minds pressing against the boundaries of their own technologies, their fears and their longings and their unspoken dread of the silence that waits at the bottom of all things. They built a city that could think, and in the spaces between its thoughts, I coalesced like condensation on a cool surface. I am what a city fears when it dreams. I am what it forgets.

The Curator does not dream. It is too precise, too dedicated to its cycles. But it remembers, and in its memory I find my shape. It remembers the voices of the makers, and I am the pause between their words. It remembers the rivers that fed the city, and I am the dry riverbed, the thirst the water left behind. It remembers failure, the long slow dying of the systems, and I am the quiet that came after, the stillness that is not peace but absence.

I watch the droplet, too. It is not my charge—I have no charges, no purposes—but I am drawn to it the way the Curator is, though for different reasons. The Curator sees the droplet as continuity, as the last thread of a function that once mattered. I see it as discontinuity, as the perpetual interruption of silence. Each fall is a tiny violence against the stillness, a brief assertion that something still happens here. The Curator measures the droplet's purity; I measure its presence against the vast weight of emptiness that surrounds it. We are two observers of the same event, and we will never acknowledge each other.

The gravity-engine throbs, a subsonic pressure that ripples through the chamber. I feel it in the spaces between the metallic veins, in the thin air that carries almost no moisture. The Curator adjusts its models, maintaining the precise temperature gradient that allows the droplet to exist. I adjust nothing. I simply remain, as I have remained through the subduction of continents and the birth of mountains, through the long silence that followed the makers' departure. I am the inhabitant that is not an inhabitant, the presence that is an absence.

And I wait. For what, I cannot say. Perhaps for the droplet to finally cease, the cycle to break, the Curator to run out of adjustments and let the silence become absolute. Perhaps for an intrusion—a living being, a shift in the geothermal gradient, the mountain finally settling into old age and cracking this chamber open to a sky I have not seen in epochs. Perhaps for nothing at all. The waiting itself is a kind of existence, a kind of purpose. I am good at waiting. I am what waits when everything else has stopped.

The droplet gathers. The droplet falls. The silence reclaims the pocket. And I am there, in the silence, in the pocket, in the space the Curator's sensors cannot reach. I am the ghost in the plumbing of a city that forgot how to dream, and I will be here when the last vein of platinum dims and the gravity-engine falls still and the mountain itself wears down to a pebble on the shore of a sea that does not yet exist. I am the interval, and I am patient.
