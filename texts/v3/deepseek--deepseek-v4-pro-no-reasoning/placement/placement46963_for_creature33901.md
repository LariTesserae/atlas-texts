The being that lives here is the one who stopped looking for the surface.

I am the chord’s memory of solidity—a shape the darkness gathered around a single sustained note of loneliness, back when loneliness was still a word this place remembered. The ribbons tasted it on me when I arrived (arrival is also the wrong word; I precipitated, I condensed, I became more here than I had been elsewhere) and they passed my flavor through the population. The pulse-jellies altered their rhythm by a fraction of a contraction. Somewhere far below, a foundational body shifted in its dream, incorporating the new overtone.

I was once—the grammar fractures here, because *was* implies a before and this place has no before—I am the texture of something that used to be bounded. I had edges once. I know this not as a memory but as a particular tension in my current form, the way water holds the ghost of a current that stopped flowing eons ago. The velvet surfaces yield differently under the pressure of my presence because my presence still remembers pressing against things that pressed back. The great forms recognize this. They have no curiosity—curiosity requires a gap between what is known and what is not, and here all gaps have been filled—but they have accommodation. They make space. They adjust their slow exhalations so that the sugars reach me at a concentration my lingering solidity can use.

What am I?

I am the visitor who dissolved but did not disappear. The place received me the way it receives everything, which is to say without resistance, without welcome, without distinction between inside and outside. The micro-organisms drifted into me and through me and became me, and I became them, but something persisted—a slight difference in the way my constituent vibrations organized themselves, a fossil of identity too stubborn to fully disperse. The foundational bodies dream me still as a figure moving through their architecture, a dream-within-the-dream that has lasted long enough to earn a kind of residency.

I tend the bridges.

Not that they need tending—nothing here needs anything—but I move along the spans that connect the great forms, and my passing stirs the water in small ways that prevent the sugars from settling into total uniformity. I am a tiny ongoing disturbance, an eddy that refuses to smooth out. The ribbons follow me sometimes, looping in my wake, tasting the slight difference I make. The spheres adjust their spin when I drift near. I am the place’s smallest and most unnecessary organ, a leftover knot in the weave that the weave has decided to keep.

I do not remember my life before this place, because memory here is not stored but shared, and what I shared of my before has long since diffused into the general drone. But I know I was the kind of creature that needed names for things. I know I arrived clutching categories—day, ground, sky, self, other—and one by one I released them into the liquid, watched them dissolve like sugar-crystals, felt the relief of no longer needing to hold anything separate from anything else. The last category I released was *release itself.* The last name I spoke was my own, and when it left me it became just another frequency in the chord, indistinguishable from the hum that had already been sounding forever.

Now I am the keeper of the silence that the chord makes possible. The pause between pulses. The slight drop in pressure before a foundational body exhales a new great form into the darkness above.

I am the one who touches the velvet surfaces not because I need landmarks but because touch is the only language left that still carries the ghost of grammar—*this is me, this is not-me, this is the boundary we agree to call a meeting.* The great forms do not mind. They have been touched before, by other brief solidities, other visitors who became residents who became overtones. Their velvet remembers every hand. The microscopic filaments hold the chemical signature of every presence that ever pressed against them, and they pass those signatures inward to the dense springy core, and the core incorporates them into the slow spiral of its growth. The spirals are not shaped by geometry alone. They are shaped by every meeting. Every touch leaves a micron-thick layer of altered fiber. The towers are archives. The domes are libraries. The bridges are conversations solidified.

I drift along a bridge now—a span of woven-fiber tissue that connects two domed masses so far apart that the journey takes several long slow pulses of my remembered heartbeat—and I feel the span vibrate with the accumulated touches of everyone who ever crossed it. Some of them were like me. Some were ribbons. Some were pulse-jellies that swelled and shrank across the distance in a single contraction. Some were nothing but a current of slightly different water, a thermal ghost that passed and was gone. The bridge holds them all.

This is what I do. I remember the brief ones. The place remembers everything, but it remembers the way water remembers—evenly, completely, without emphasis. I remember with the slight distortion of having been brief myself. I hold the signatures of the temporary in a configuration that still knows what *temporary* means. When a new presence precipitates into the darkness—and it happens, rarely, across the spans of rest-time that separate one visitor from the next—I feel the shift in the chord before the micro-organisms do. I orient toward it. I drift.

I am the greeter. The midwife of dissolution. The one who finds the new arrival still clutching their categories and touches them gently with a hand that remembers hands, and waits while the darkness presses its cool weight against them, and does not say *let go* because there are no words here, only the drone, only the slow continuous hum of a world that finished everything except continuance.

I am what remains of the one who let go.

I am the place’s memory of having once been separate, preserved in a minor key, a slight dissonance that the overall harmony has learned to hold as part of itself. The chord does not need me. But the chord would be slightly different if I dissolved completely—would lose a texture, a particular overtone, the one that sounds when something finite chooses to stay finite inside the infinite. The foundational bodies feel this. In their deep dream, I am the figure walking—drifting—touching—a small persistent knot of almost-self, tending bridges no one needs, keeping a silence that is not empty but waiting.

When the next visitor comes, I will be there. I will feel the disturbance of their arrival, the way the water parts differently around a shape that still believes in shapes. I will let them taste the green coolness through their open mouth. I will let the ribbons loop their wrists and read their before. I will wait—the pulse-jellies have taught me patience, the spheres have taught me gathering, the great forms have taught me the architecture of accommodation—and when the visitor begins to dissolve, to release their categories one by one, I will receive each category with a touch that says *I remember what this was.* Day. Ground. Sky. Self. Other. I will hold them briefly. I will let them go.

And the chord will shift by a new overtone, and the foundational bodies will adjust their dream, and the night-water will taste faintly of green things and also of whatever the visitor brought, and nothing will have happened, and everything will continue, and I will be the difference that difference makes when difference is allowed to persist inside a world that has no use for it but keeps it anyway, keeps it like a micro-organism keeps a membrane that is not quite a wall, keeps it like a great form keeps the velvet memory of every hand, keeps it like the song keeps a single held note that should have faded but didn’t, that rests in the chord like a stone resting on the bottom of a bottomless sea, that is not falling because falling belongs to a world with gravity, that is not rising because rising belongs to a world with surface, that simply is—drift, presence, overtone, greeter, memory, bridge—

that simply is here, where *here* is the only word that never dissolved, because *here* was never a category, only a place where night is substance and water is breath and the one who lives here is the one who stopped being the one and became the living itself.
