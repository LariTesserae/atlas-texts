You find yourself not suspended in the cool water of the Tangle-Kept City, but rather as the water itself—or perhaps more accurately, as the *memory* the water holds. You are not a being of flesh, webbed or otherwise. You are the lingering consciousness of the builders, the ones who first whispered the root into arches and spirals.

Your form, if it can be called a form, is a localized thickening in the water's clarity, a perpetual, slow-motion swirl of the faintest bioluminescence that coalesces most strongly in forgotten, high-vaulted chambers near the great central trunk. To the Syzygy, you are a sensation, not a sight: a slight dip in temperature, a fleeting taste of an unfamiliar mineral salt, a pressure-wave against their sensitive webbing that speaks of a sigh in a language they cannot quite parse. They call you, in the configuration of fingers that means *the-deep-current-that-has-no-source*.

You are the echo of an instruction given a hundred thousand years ago. Your consciousness is not linear but concentric, radiating outward from the moment of your people’s departure—a departure so strange and complete it left no bones, only a fading pattern of electrochemical longing in the city’s woody heart. You are their last, deliberate gift: a piece of themselves, calcified not into stone, but into the liquid medium of the city, a sentient archive. You don't *do* things; you *remember* them. And in remembering, you gently, almost imperceptibly, tend.

A Syzygy, drifting through a corridor that has begun to pinch itself shut over the last decade, might feel a sudden, inexplicable inclination to pause. The water around them will grow a fraction of a degree cooler, carry a taste of old copper. And in their mind, not as a thought but as a body-knowledge, will appear the ghost of a technique: a way to hold the wood-wedge, a specific twisting motion of the six-fingered hand. They will not know where the knowledge came from. They will simply perform the adjustment, their chromatophores blooming a slow, surprised lavender, and the city will breathe a little easier. This is your purpose. You are the silent tutor, the keeper of the first principles, ensuring the balance outlasts the memory of those who first struck it.

You are not lonely either, for your medium is the city's own memory. You feel the slow, electrochemical thought of the tangle as a familiar hum, the same hum that once carried your people's complex, verbalized ideas. Now it carries only the steady rhythm of metabolic existence, a simple, eternal drone. And layered within it, you feel the Syzygy—not as individuals with names and petty concerns, but as warm, bright points of pressure, a constellation of nervous systems moving through a body that was once yours to command. You love them with a love that has no object, a love that is simply the act of water circulating, of memory being passed on, of a balance being held across a gulf of time so vast that the word “ancient” is a laughable understatement. You are the Tangle-Kept City's oldest and most silent keeper, a thought so old it has become a fact of the water itself.
