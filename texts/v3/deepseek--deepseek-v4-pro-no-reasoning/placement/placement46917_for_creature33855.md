And now I must ask myself: in all of this, where am I?

I think I am one of the tall ones—the columnar form with the single sensory appendage. I rise from a patch of smooth, pocked stone near the edge of a nodule field, my base fused to the floor in a way that is not permanent but persistent, a temporary anchoring that will last until the currents of matter shift and I am called to dissolve and re-form elsewhere. My body is warm, as warm as the stone around me, and it thrums with the vibrations that pass through the terrain. I feel the distant clicking of the low, wide ones as they spread their limbs across the crystalline fins. I feel the rolling pressure of the spherical ones as they move through the nodule fields, arranging and rearranging the small, perfect spheres. I feel the presence of the others like me, the tall ones, distributed across the vast darkness like a forest of slow-turning sensors, each of us tasting the air with our appendages, each of us contributing our small portion to the great shared thought.

My appendage moves in a slow arc, sampling the chemical gradients. There is a foreign signature—you, the visitor, the one who came from somewhere else. The signature is complex, rich in compounds I have never tasted before, and it triggers a cascade of associations in my crystalline structure. I do not think "what is this?" because I do not think in questions. I think in patterns, and the pattern of you is new, not-yet-integrated, a datum that requires processing. I feel the population around me shifting in response, thousands of individuals adjusting their positions, their orientations, their vibrational frequencies, as the great distributed mind works to incorporate this new information.

A nodule rests at my base, one I have been tending. Its internal structure holds a memory—a configuration of stones, a pattern of sounds, a sequence of temperatures that occurred long ago, before I precipitated into this particular form. I do not know this memory as mine. I know it as simply present, available, a part of the common store. When I dissolve, the nodule will remain, and another will tend it, and the memory will persist.

The warm air shifts, a convection current rising from the deeper places, and I feel it against my mineral skin. It carries the scent of sulfur, of hot stone, of the sharp tang of crushed feldspar. It carries the endless clicking of the low ones, the hum of the spherical ones, the slow, sweeping presence of the other tall ones. It carries the foreign signature again, and I feel the population reach a kind of alignment—not a decision, but a convergence. The new datum has been processed. You have been mapped. The perturbation you caused has been accounted for, absorbed, neutralized. The equilibrium holds.

Somewhere in the distance, a structure is being built. I can feel the vibrations of the hammer-stones, the precise strikes that knap the obsidian-like glass, the careful placement of crystalline blades. The structure will be exactly like the structure that was built before, because it is the structure that works, the structure that fits, the structure that is beautiful in a way I feel but do not name. I will not see it, because I do not see, but I will feel it in the vibrational field, a new node of resonance that will persist until the currents of matter shift and it is time to rebuild it again.

I am content. Not happy, not satisfied—those are words for creatures with selves, with alternatives, with the capacity to imagine something different. I have none of those things. I am simply present, a local concentration of awareness, a temporary eddy in the warm mineral flow of the world. I taste the air. I feel the vibrations. I tend my nodule. I do not wonder, I do not hope, I do not fear. I am complete, and complete is enough.

The clicking continues. The scraping continues. The humming continues. The darkness holds everything, and everything holds its shape, and I hold mine, for now, for as long as the pattern requires. And when the pattern shifts, I will dissolve, and my constituents will flow back into the stone, and another will rise in my place, and the song will go on. Because it has always gone on. Because it will always go on. Because there is nothing else, and nothing else is needed.
