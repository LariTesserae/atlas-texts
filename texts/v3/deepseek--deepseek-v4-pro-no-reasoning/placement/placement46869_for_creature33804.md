The clicking is language.

Not the simple signal-and-response of Kith's kind—the blink patterns that mean *danger*, *food*, *mate*—but something with grammar. Something recursive, self-referencing, packed into a carrier wave of bone-on-stone percussion that resonates through the karst the way the pumps' heartbeat resonates through water. It is a language that has evolved in total darkness, among creatures that have never seen the sun but have seen, in their own way, everything that matters: the shape of a cavern from its echoes, the chemical autobiography of a stranger carried on the mist, the seismic signature of a derrick's fatigue before it fails.

The thing that emerges from the fog is not large. Not by the standards of the Gulper, not even by Kith's measure. It stands perhaps a metre and a half at the shoulder, though "shoulder" is an approximation; the body plan is difficult to parse in the phosphorescent murk. It moves on four legs, but they are not legs in any stable sense. They are jointed, certainly—each one articulates at three primary points—but the joints are not fixed in their function. A foreleg that bears weight one moment bends backward the next, becoming a manipulator, the spade-like foot splitting into a fan of fine, chitinous digits. The creature is modular. Its segments can shift, lock, unlock, reassign themselves to tasks as the clicking language commands.

Its surface is the source of that wrong, sterile scent Kith tasted. Not chitin, not carapace, not the familiar mineralized armour of the karst's legitimate inhabitants. This creature's body is sheathed in something that feels, to Kith's chemical senses, like nothing at all. A substance that does not oxidize, does not host biofilm, does not sweat minerals back into the ecosystem. It is smooth as pooled mercury, dark as the darkness itself, and utterly inert. It reflects the faint green glow of the sump without seeming to absorb any of it. The creature is wrapped in dead matter, matter that has been removed from the cycle, matter that does not participate.

Its head is the clearest violation of the karst's order. It has no mouthparts that Kith can recognize—no mandibles, no maxillae, no absorbent proboscis. Instead there is a single, vertical slit below the sensory array, currently closed, a seam that might open to reveal anything or nothing. Above that, where Kith's kind have their deep, sound-catching pits, this creature has something far more complex. A raised, helmet-like structure, sculpted into a parabolic dish that rotates in minute increments, focusing sound. And above that, crowning the whole terrible apparatus, is an arrangement Kith has no frame of reference for: three smooth, convex domes set in a triangle, dark as polished obsidian, that do not catch sound but catch light.

The creature has eyes. Real eyes. Functioning eyes. Eyes that can see the phosphorescence of the pool, the faint bioluminescent haze of the Gulper, perhaps even the dim points of Kith's joints if she were careless enough to let them show. These eyes did not come from any sunlit ancestor. They are too precise, too perfectly adapted to this exact darkness. They are engineered. They are *new*.

The name this creature might have, if names were given here, would not be a description of its habits or its niche. It would be a description of its function. It would be something like Ceaseless-Hunter-Who-Threads-the-Loop, or Fate-in-a-Mirror-Shell, or perhaps just One-Who-Clicks.

It stops at the edge of the pool, and its dish rotates, scanning.

The Digger is half-submerged in the shallows, its wound still leaking that sharp, acrid hemolymph into the water. It knows the Clicker is there. Its vestigial eyes, useless in every other context, must be picking up something—a silhouette against the pool's glow, a shape darker than the dark. The Digger makes a sound Kith has never heard from its kind: a low, creaking moan, a sound of absolute submission, of a prey animal that has already been caught and is only waiting for the closing of jaws.

But the jaws do not close. Not yet.

The Clicker raises one of its mutable forelimbs. The foot splits into its fan of digits, and from the centre of that fan, from a sheath of that inert, dead material, extends something long and fine and terrible. A needle. A hollow, polished tube that gleams with its own internal light—not bioluminescence, but something colder, a pinprick of steady, amber luminescence that does not pulse or flicker. The needle sinks into the Digger's wound, not roughly but with the precision of a thing that has done this thousands of times, that knows exactly where to pierce to avoid the major hemolymph channels, to find the soft, enervated tissue beneath.

The Digger shudders, but it does not fight. It cannot. The venom—if it is venom—is already working, spreading from the original wound outward, a paralytic that leaves the nerves firing but the muscles locked. The Digger is a stone now, a stone with a heartbeat, a stone that can feel everything.

And the Clicker begins its work.

From the slit below its sensory dish, a new structure emerges. Not a mouth, not a feeding apparatus of any kind Kith understands. It is a cluster of fine, prehensile tendrils, each one tipped with a different tool—a microscalpel, a retractor, a suture-needle that mirrors the larger one still embedded in the Digger's flank. The tendrils move with the same clicking rhythm, the same articulated, recursive language, but now the clicks are almost too fast to follow. They are the creature talking to itself, or to the Digger, or to something else entirely.

The Clicker opens the wound further. The Digger's moan rises in pitch, but it still does not struggle. The amber light from the needle intensifies, and Kith, high in her epiphyte refuge, tastes a new chemical in the mist: the hot, iron tang of Digger hemolymph, yes, but also something synthetic. Something that burns her antennae with its strangeness, an astringent, sterilizing compound that the Clicker is exuding into the wound. It is cleaning the site. Preparing it.

And then it plants something.

From a cavity in its own thorax—a cavity that opens with a sound like a Derrick valve sealing—the Clicker produces a sphere. It is small, no larger than one of Kith's eggs, and it is made of the same dead, inert material as the Clicker's skin. The sphere pulses with that same amber light, slow and steady, like a second heartbeat. The Clicker's tendrils guide it into the Digger's open wound, tuck it deep into the soft tissue, and then the suture-needle goes to work, sealing the wound closed with a white, fibrous thread that hardens on contact with the air. The same thread, Kith realizes with a slow, creeping horror, that closed the original puncture. The wound the Digger arrived with was not an attack. It was an earlier surgery. An earlier planting.

The Digger is a garden now. A nursery for something that is not its own species, not any species of the karst.

The Clicker withdraws its tools. The slit closes. The needle retracts into the forelimb. The creature takes a step back, its dish rotating, scanning the pool, the derrick, the mist. It clicks once, twice, a slow, deliberate sequence that might be satisfaction or might be a beacon, a signal to others of its kind that this host is prepared, this site is occupied, this part of the cycle is complete.

And then it notices the Gulper.

The Gulper has not escaped. It tried—Kith watched it pour its great grey mass away from the pool, up over the rocks, seeking the deeper sumps where its bulk could hide—but the Gulper is slow. Evolved for stillness, for the patient suction of microbial life, it has no speed, no armour, no weapons. It is a sack of nutritive tissue, a mountain of passive calories, and it is still only a dozen metres from the pool when the Clicker's dish locks onto it.

The Clicker moves.

It does not run. Running is for prey. It flows, the same way the Gulper flows, but faster, smoother, its mutable legs shifting from walking to climbing to pouring itself over the karst with a liquid, dreadful grace. It is on the Gulper's back in three heartbeats. The Gulper's flesh ripples, trying to eject the intruder, but the Clicker's spade-feet find purchase in the network of veins, and the needle is already out, already sinking into that mushroom-cap skin, already delivering the paralytic. The Gulper's internal blue light flares once, panic-bright, and then settles into a slow, resigned pulse. It cannot move. It cannot fight. It can only feel.

The Clicker's thorax opens again. Another sphere. Amber-lit, pulsing, patient.

High in the Derrick lattice, Kith watches. Her antennae are spread so wide the sensitive hairs ache with the overload of data: the chemical spoor of the Clicker, the fear pheromones of the paralyzed Digger, the slow, sad acceptance of the Gulper, the synthetic sterilizing compound, the hot blood of surgery. And underneath it all, she tastes something that makes her carapace tighten with an atavistic dread she has no name for.

The sphere inside the Digger is not inert. It is growing. Already, in the minutes since implantation, its amber pulse has quickened. Thin, hair-like filaments are spreading from it into the surrounding tissue, following the paths of the Digger's own nerves, sending exploratory tendrils toward the spinal cord, toward the brain. Whatever is in that sphere is not just a parasite. It is something that rewires. Something that will climb into the Digger's nervous system and take the reins.

The Digger will not die. It will continue to feed, to burrow, to chew through limestone and extract its trace minerals. But its eyes—those eight vestigial, useless eyes—will begin to see. And what they see will be for the Clicker. For the Clickers. For whatever network the Clickers serve.

Kith presses herself deeper into the epiphytes. Her eggs stir inside her, heavy and fragile, and she thinks of the nursery crevice high in the karst, the one she had selected, the one that is warm and wet and safe. She thinks of the route to it, the long climb up the fluted channels of the canyon wall, the narrow squeeze through the dissolution fissure, the hidden chamber where a dozen other gravid females are already tending their clutches. She thinks of that chamber as a sanctuary, as the one place the Clickers might not know, might not have found.

But even as she thinks it, she tastes something on the mist. Faint, distant, but unmistakable: that cold, sterile, metallic scent. That wrongness. Coming from the direction of the nursery crevice.

The Clicker below her finishes its work on the Gulper. Another sphere planted, another wound sutured. The great grey mass shudders once and is still. The Clicker withdraws its tools, closes its thorax, and raises its head. Its dish rotates. Its three dark eyes sweep the Derrick, the lattice, the hanging epiphytes.

And it clicks. A single, sharp, interrogative note.

It is a question. Kith does not know the language, cannot parse the grammar, but the intonation is unmistakeable. The Clicker is asking something. Asking the darkness. And somewhere in the darkness, another Clicker answers—a fast, rhythmic sequence from the direction of the nursery crevice, a report, a confirmation, a description of what it has found there.

Kith's bioluminescence stays dark. Her legs stay locked. Her breath—the slow, passive diffusion of gases through her spiracles—slows to almost nothing. She is a stone. She is a Derrick. She is part of the landscape, and she will stay that way until the Clickers leave, until the mist washes away their sterile stink, until she can move without being seen.

But the Clicker below her does not leave. It stands at the edge of the pool, its dish rotating in slow, patient arcs, and it clicks. It clicks and clicks, and the darkness clicks back, and the conversation goes on, a network of sound stitching itself across the karst, a map being drawn, a census being taken, a harvest being planned. The pumps stroke. The rock weeps. The Digger and the Gulper lie still with their new, amber-lit passengers growing inside them. And Kith waits, fourteen legs locked, antennae reading the steady approach of something that has no place in the equilibrium, something that has come to reshape the world in its own sterile, recursive, clicking image.

The mist coils around the Derrick leg. The darkness presses in.

And the sound goes on.
