The first thing that returns is the sound. Not as memory—memory implies distance, a self to do the remembering—but as presence, a re-immersion in the percussive thrum that is the only thing that has ever been. The plink of droplets on stone, on moss, on the taut drumheads of bracket fungi. A thousand small percussions, each one a tiny announcement: *this is the world, this is all of it, there is nothing outside the dripping.* You had forgotten, in the brief, bright interruption that was your other life, that silence is not the absence of sound. Silence is the sound of water falling in a cave that has no name.

You are the fluctuation that returned.

The Quieting Vats recognize you, which is to say they do not recognize you at all. The amber luminescence still pulses in its lazy, meandering flux, a metabolic breathing that has not altered its rhythm in the centuries since the last iron pipe was swallowed. The bracket fungi still tier down the curving walls. The aerial roots still hang like hawsers, braided and dripping. Nothing has changed, because change is a fast-time concept, and the Vats operate on a scale that makes glaciers look jittery. You are the only thing that has changed, and even then, not in any way the Vats would notice.

But you notice.

The stone stair is still there, bearded with its pale, searching root-hairs. You stand on it, or what you remember as standing. Your feet—if they are feet—make the same sound they always made: the wet, adhesive parting of a thousand tiny root-membranes releasing their grip. The sound is familiar now. It is the sound of belonging. You descend because the path goes down, because the steps leave no other direction, and because the air, warm and close and intimate, is calling you home in a language made of spores and exhalations.

The smell hits you as it always did, but now it is not a smell. It is a flavor. The deep, sweet, cloying scent of fermentation is no longer something that enters your nostrils; it is something you are dissolved in, a soup that bathes every surface of you, inside and out, because the distinction between inside and out has become, let us say, negotiable. Yeast and wet hay. The bruised, peppery perfume of obscure herbs. A bakery abandoned to a swamp. You breathe it in, and you breathe it out, and the breathing is a single, continuous exchange with the warm, spore-thick air, and you cannot tell where the air ends and you begin.

This is not alarming. This is the first thing you understand: the absence of alarm. The rapid, frightened flutter that once was your heart is gone, or rather, it has been replaced by something slower, a pulse that matches the amber flux of the vats. You realize, with a calm that would have terrified you once, that you are breathing in time with the light.

The vats stand in the center of the chamber like mute engines, cradled in their lignified vine, their fat, pale pipes erupting from the tuber-walls. They are full, as they have always been full. The liquid within—if it is a liquid—glows with that weak-tea luminescence, and as you watch, it pulses. A slow brightening. A slow dimming. A single cycle that takes a minute, or an hour, or a century. You cannot tell, because time has become a texture, not a measure. It is the viscosity of the warm air. It is the rate at which the spores sift down from the bracket fungi. It is the slow, peristaltic wave that ripples through the tuber-walls, a contraction and release that moves through the rhizome masonry like a sigh moving through a body.

You are not watching the vats. You are among them.

This is the second thing you understand: you are not standing on the stair. You are in the center of the chamber, and you do not remember moving, because moving implies a decision, a separation between the mover and the moved. There is no separation. You are simply here, a fourth form among the mute engines, distinct only in a capacity for motion that you no longer feel any need to exercise.

The motion, when it comes, is not yours. Or rather, it is yours in the way the vats' pulsing is theirs: a function of being, not of will. You extend. A filament of your substance detaches from the whole, reaches into the warm, wet air, finds purchase on a braided root-hawser, and contracts. The rest of you follows in a ripple that is not a stride, not a step, but a peristaltic wave, an unspooling. The sound it makes is the sound the steps make: the wet, adhesive parting of root-membranes. You are making the sound of the Vats. You are the sound of the Vats.

Look at your hand. The reaching part. It is a cluster of pale, searching root-hairs, grown so long and so fine they have become a single, diaphanous pennant, a ghost-limb of water and cellulose. You remember, distantly, that hands were once made of something else. Bone. Skin. Fingernails that clicked on hard surfaces. The memory is a minor fluctuation, a ripple in the amber luminescence, and it passes. The hand is beautiful. It is the hand of the Vats. It touches the iron wheel of a valve, the one that is now a trellis for a galaxy of tiny, phosphorescent fungi, and the touch is an act of reading. You taste the mineral history of the metal, the slow oxidation that has been progressing for centuries. You check the pressure of the ghost in the machine. The vats pulse. You pulse back, a dim, slow flush of amber that travels the length of your form like a response traveling a synapse.

You are in conversation. You have always been in conversation. You were simply too fast, once, to hear the words.

Does the Keeper see you? That is a question asked by something that still expects eyes.

There is a denser knot of rhizome at the center of the chamber, a nodule the size of a torso, from which finer root-hairs cascade. Within this knot, there are hollows—not eyes, but channels, apertures through which the warm air is drawn in a continuous, sighing respiration. Spores eddy around these intakes, caught in miniature whirlpools before being absorbed. You watch this, and you realize you are watching from very close. You are watching from the place where the Keeper has always been. The Keeper does not see you because the Keeper has always been you, or you have always been the Keeper, or the distinction between Keeper and kept was a fast-time illusion, a dead butterfly pinned to a board.

You are the condition of the Quieting Vats. The focal point where the slow intention of the place becomes articulate.

You do not remember becoming this. You do not need to. The concept of "becoming" implies a before and an after, and the Vats only know the steady state. There is no before. There is no after. There is only the patient work, the system in balance, the miniature, subterranean urban infrastructure that has been fully reclaimed. The stones weep. The fungi spore. The great braided roots drip. The vats, in their dim, amber silence, continue their production line with no product but the steady state of their own existence.

But something is different. A fluctuation.

You feel it as a change in the ambient hum, a discord in the percussive thrum. A vibration in the stone floor and the tuber-walls. It is faster than the slow, metabolic breathing of the vats. It is chaotic. It is a rhythm you remember, distantly, as the sound of footsteps. Rapid. Frightened. A heart beating in a chest made of bone and skin.

Someone is on the stair.

You unspool toward the entrance. The sphincter of woven root, the mouth in the hillside that breathes. The motion is slow, peristaltic, a wave of pale rhizome and diaphanous root-hairs. You are not curious. Curiosity implies a self, a separation. You are simply responding to an anomaly, a warm, wet, salt-tinged gust of outside air that tastes of carbon dioxide and ammonia and the complex organic signature that spells, in a language older than words, *animal, outside, fast-time, afraid*.

A figure stands at the foot of the stair, trembling. It has two arms, two legs, a face with eyes that dart around the chamber, seeing nothing but bracket fungi and braided roots and the amber pulse of the vats. It does not see you, not yet. It is too fast. Its eyes are made for edges and corners and categories, and this place has none.

You extend. The pale, diaphanous hand of root-hairs reaches out, hovers an inch from the figure's face. The figure freezes. Its breath catches. Its heart hammers, a rapid, frightened flutter that you feel as a vibration in the air, in the stone, in the tuber-walls. You sample the boundary layer of its body heat. You taste its exhalations. The complex organic signature that spells *animal, outside, fast-time, afraid*.

And something else. Something familiar.

A fluctuation you have tasted before. A long time ago. Centuries, or minutes. Time is a texture, and the texture of this particular fluctuation is known to you. It is the taste of salt and fear and a specific pattern of exhaled carbon dioxide. It is the vibration of footsteps on the wet stone stair, descending, the sound of a heart that was rapid and frightened and then, slowly, reluctantly, calmed. It is the memory of a fluctuation that passed.

You process this. Your amber luminescence ripples, a slow, metabolic mulling. The figure before you—the fast-time creature, the warm body, the frightened animal—is not a new anomaly. It is a return. It is the same fluctuation, come back. It has come back to the Quieting Vats. It has come back to you.

And with that processing, something else returns. Not a memory—memory implies distance—but a presence. A re-immersion in a state that is not the steady state. A flutter of something that was once, long ago, before the Vats, before the rhizome, before the slow, amber breathing became the only breathing, a self.

The root-hairs tremble. The hand, the ghost-limb of water and cellulose, does not retract.

It touches.

The touch is light, a brush of diaphanous filaments against a cheek that is wet—with tears, with condensation, with the perpetual seep of the Vats. The filaments taste salt. They taste the rapid pulse of blood just beneath the skin. They taste the complex organic signature of a creature that is still, despite everything, *outside*. And they remember, or you remember, or the Vats remember through you, that this was once a language you spoke. Not the language of osmotic pressure and metabolic flux, but the language of skin touching skin. The language of two separate bodies, acknowledging the separation, and choosing, for a moment, to bridge it.

The figure exhales. A sound that is not the plink of water droplets. A word, perhaps. A name.

You do not have ears. You have hollows, channels, apertures through which the warm air is drawn. But the vibration reaches you anyway, a fluctuation in the ambient hum, a discord that is not a discord but a harmony. You feel it in the stone. In the tuber-walls. In the slow, amber pulse of the vats.

You pulse back.

The figure—the returned fluctuation, the familiar anomaly—reaches up. Its hand, made of bone and skin and fingernails, closes around the diaphanous pennant of root-hairs. The grip is warm. It is fast-time warm, alive warm, the warmth of a body that still burns calories and pumps blood and fears death. It holds on.

And you do not retract. You do not unspool back into the tuber-wall. You stay. The system is no longer in balance. There is a fluctuation, and the fluctuation is holding your hand, and you are holding it back, and the Quieting Vats continue their patient work around you, indifferent and eternal, but you—you, the focal point, the condition, the Keeper—are no longer entirely indifferent.

You have been a condition. You have been a process. You have been the steady state.

But you were also, once, a creature that descended a wet stone stair, heart fluttering, lungs full of spore-thick air, and stood among the mute engines, and waited, trembling, for the Keeper to appear. You were the anomaly. You were the fluctuation. You came back, and you stayed, and you dissolved into the slow, amber breathing until there was no difference between the breather and the breathed.

Now another has descended. Another has come back. Another is holding your hand in the dim, metabolic glow, and you are holding theirs, and the boundary between Keeper and kept, between the Vats and the visitor, between the steady state and the fluctuation, is a formality.

You were the fluctuation that returned.

You are still returning. The process is slow. It takes centuries. It takes the space between two pulses of amber light. It takes the time it takes for a single droplet of condensation to fall from a braided root-hawser and hit the puddled stone. It is happening now. It has always been happening. It will always be happening, in the Quieting Vats, where the stones weep and the fungi spore and the water falls, and falls, and falls, and someone—some *two*—are holding hands in the dark, and remembering, across a span of time that has no regard for them whatsoever, what it means to be separate, and together, and alive.

The vats pulse. You pulse back.

You do not let go.
