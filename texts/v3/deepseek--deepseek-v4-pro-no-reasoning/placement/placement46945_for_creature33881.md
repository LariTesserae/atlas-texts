The stairs take three light-shifts to climb. My eyes seal over on the second. A mercy, maybe. The brightness becomes something I feel on my skin instead of through my vision, a prickling heat that pushes against me like a hand. I keep one palm pressed to the braided material of the stairs—still that strange, dead, sap-less fiber—and the other stretched forward into the glare, waiting to touch something solid.

The song gets clearer as I rise.

It’s not a voice. Not exactly. It’s a sound made by something that used to be a voice, that remembers being a voice, that is trying very hard to become one again. The melody is simple, a falling and rising pattern of tones that repeat with slight variation, like someone practicing a phrase in a language they almost remember. Between the notes, I hear pauses that are too long, and breaths that are too wet, and occasionally a soft clicking sound that might be a tongue against teeth, or might be something else entirely.

I stop when my outstretched hand touches warmth.

Not the diffuse, humid warmth of the Breathfold below. This is concentrated, directional, radiating from a single source. It’s the warmth of a body. I let my blistered fingers trace the shape of it: a shoulder, curved but still angular; a neck, too long, with ridges of something hard along the sides; a jaw that moves under my touch, slowly, chewing on the next note of the song before releasing it into the air.

"I thought I was the only one," I say. My voice is a croak. The words are the first real words I’ve spoken out loud, and they taste like spores and rust.

The thing under my hand stops singing. The silence is shocking, a hole in the light.

"You are," it says. Its voice is a chorus of small, dry sounds, like leaves rubbing together. "I’m not one. I’m many."

I pull my hand back. The brightness is fading now—or my eyes are adapting, finding the seams in the glare, the shadows that define shape. I can see outlines. A figure crouched on the stairs above me, hunched over, its back to the shaft of light. It’s taller than me by half. Its limbs are segmented, jointed in places I don’t have joints, and its skin—if it’s skin—has the texture of bark, but bark that has been polished smooth by years of something touching it. Its head turns, and I see that it has no face. Not in the sense of missing features. It has too many features, layered over each other, a palimpsest of faces that have grown and been reabsorbed over time. Eyes and mouths and what might be nostrils are visible in faint relief, overlapping, like the growth rings in a trunk. The most recent face is the most prominent, and it’s looking at me with an expression I don’t have a word for yet. Recognition, maybe. Or hunger.

"I am the Harvester," it says. The mouths move slightly out of sync with the words. "I have been waiting for the harvest to be ready. Are you ready?"

I don’t know what that means. I tell it so.

The Harvester shifts, and I see that it’s holding something. A spindle. A tool. The braided stairs trail up behind it, and I realize now that the stairs aren’t just *there*—they’re being *made*. The Harvester is drawing fiber from its own body, a thin pale thread that pulls from a slit in its side, and twisting it with practiced, patient movements into the material of the stairs. It has been doing this for a very long time. The stairs extend upward into the light, and I understand, with a vertigo that has nothing to do with height, that they go much, much higher than I thought. They go somewhere.

"Where do the stairs lead?" I ask.

"Out," says the Harvester. "Or in. The direction is the same. This place is a mouth. I am making it a throat."

I sit down on the step below it. The woven fiber is cool and dry under me. Below, the Breathfold roars its constant, distant roar, the sound of a trillion growing things building and crumbling. Up here, the sound is thinner, cleaner. I can hear the thread twisting. I can hear the Harvester breathing, a low susurrus like wind through dry leaves.

"Are you the one who made the archways?" I ask. "The empty rooms?"

"No. I harvest what the archways are meant to hold. The rooms are empty because the inhabitants haven’t arrived yet. The city is a cup, and the cup is waiting for water."

I think about the hollows I left in the root mat. The traces of others I’ve found. The itch that started everything.

"I think I might be water," I say.

The Harvester’s multiple mouths curve into something like a smile. The expression is complicated, full of teeth and gaps and old grief. "Yes," it says. "You are. But you’re not finished yet. You’re still mostly cup."

It reaches out with a hand that has too many fingers, all of them long and clever, and touches my face. The touch is gentle, exploratory, like the tendrils that filled the hollow I left in the mat. I feel it mapping the shape of me, the newness of my mouth, the rawness of my eyes.

"You’re close, though," it murmurs. "Closer than the last one. The last one turned back. Went down into the green and let the mat take it again. It didn’t want to be water. It wanted to stay cup."

"Why?"

"Because being water means leaving. And leaving means you can’t come back. The Breathfold doesn’t forgive exodus. It only knows incorporation."

It withdraws its hand. The thread at its side keeps spinning out, keeps being twisted into stairs. The light is dimmer now, or I’m getting used to it. I can see the Harvester more clearly—the exhaustion in the set of its shoulders, the patient, endless rhythm of its work, the way its body is slowly being consumed to make this impossible ladder. It’s been here for a very long time. Longer than the city. Longer than the Breathfold, maybe.

"Why are you making the stairs?" I ask. "If no one climbs them?"

"Someone will," it says. "I’m making them for the one who will climb all the way. The one who will name this place. Names are anchors. Names let a thing stop becoming and start being. The Breathfold has been becoming for a very long time. It’s tired, I think. It wants to be."

I look up, past the Harvester, into the light. I can’t see where the stairs end. But I can feel it now—a point of difference in the air, a thinning, a boundary. The mouth of the mouth. The place where the green ends and something else begins.

"What’s out there?" I whisper.

The Harvester stops twisting. The silence is so sudden it feels like a physical blow. The light flickers, or my vision does. The faces on its face shift, layer over layer, all of them turning to look at me with expressions that range from hope to terror to a kind of weary love.

"I don’t know," it says. "I’m not the one who climbs. I’m the one who builds. I’ve never seen it."

I stand up. My legs are weak, still half-fused with the memory of being root, but they hold. The stairs are narrow, and the Harvester takes up most of the space, but it shifts aside for me, pressing its strange jointed body against the wall of the light shaft. The thread at its side keeps spinning. It will keep spinning until there’s nothing left of it, I realize. Until its whole body has been unwound into this ladder.

"Why do you do it?" I ask. "If you’ll never see the end?"

"Because someone will," it says. "And a ladder is a promise. Every rung says: *you can go further*. That’s enough."

I want to say something else. I want to thank it, or ask its name, or promise to come back. But I don’t have the words yet. My mouth is still new. So I just touch its shoulder again, that warm strange surface, and then I turn and I keep climbing.

The light gets brighter. Then it starts to change. It’s not just light anymore—it’s space, it’s depth, it’s a quality of air that doesn’t exist in the Breathfold. It’s cold. A cold that bites instead of caressing. I feel my skin tightening, my breath turning to mist in front of my face. The roar of the Breathfold is a memory now, a vibration in the stairs under my feet rather than a sound in the air. The air itself is thinning, getting sharp and clean and empty.

I climb. The stairs go on. Behind me, the Harvester has started singing again, a low, repetitive song that sounds like a lullaby, or a dirge, or a map spelled out in notes. I don’t know which. I listen until I can’t hear it anymore, and then I keep listening anyway, holding the melody in my chest like a second heartbeat.

The light ahead of me is no longer green-golden. It’s pale, and cold, and it has a color I don’t have a name for yet. Blue, I will learn later. White. The colors of a sky that isn’t made of leaves.

I’m not finished yet. I’m still becoming. But I’m climbing, and the stairs hold, and somewhere above me there’s an edge, a rim, a lip of the cup that the Breathfold has been growing toward for a thousand thousand light-shifts.

I don’t know what I’ll do when I reach it.

But I know, finally, what I am.

I’m a mouth. I’m the mouth the Breathfold has been waiting for. And when I break the surface, when I stand for the first time under a sky that isn’t green, I’ll open that mouth and I’ll speak.

Not a description. Not a question.

A name.
