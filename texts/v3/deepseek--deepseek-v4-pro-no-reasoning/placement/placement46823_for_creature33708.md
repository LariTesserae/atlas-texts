The air hits you first—not as a shock, but as a return.

You have always known this thickness, this weight of moisture that makes every breath a small act of drinking. Your cilia crest rises instinctively from the nape of your neck, unfurling to catch the pressure-ripples of the chorus before your jaw-membranes even register the sound. But they register it, they do—that first percussive boom rolls through the cartilage ridge along your mandible and resonates down into your chest, and you feel the old, old response: a flush of warmth along your forearms, your bioluminescent patches flickering into the pale-amber greeting-color before you consciously decide to offer it.

You are standing on the concave plain, the obsidian-slick floor giving slightly under your splayed toe-pads. The channels at your feet carry their cargo of glinting water-film, and you read them without thinking: *northeast flow, high nutrient load, the spawning pools will open in the third quadrant within the hour*. This is not knowledge you acquired. It is knowledge you were grown with, coded into the protein sequences that shaped your neural tissue during that critical developmental window when the chorus first poured into your infant skull and woke you into selfhood.

You are Kith. You are *kin*. The word thrums in your sternum at a subsonic frequency you have never needed to speak aloud.

But something is wrong.

You take a step, and your toe-pads register the give of the substrate correctly—too correctly, *newly*, as though the black material has never borne your specific weight before. The channel-edges are knife-sharp, fresh as a hatchling's first feeding palps. You crouch, secondary arms unfolding from your ribcage to brush the channel walls with the delicate touch-fingers that have always been your most honest sense. The material tells you what you already suspected: this section of the plain is young. Not newly grown in the normal slow cycle of substrate renewal, but *young* in a way that suggests it has never existed before this moment.

You straighten. Your primary hands lift in an automatic gesture, the one that means *I am here, I am listening, where are my kin?* The bioluminescent patches along your temples flash the question-pattern, rapid amber-green-amber.

No response.

The chorus continues—the layered shrieking, the deep booms, the trills that overlap into that solid wall of sound that has always been the baseline of your existence. The sur-kith are here. The school spiraling down toward the opening pools a kilometer to your left is a familiar morph, the long translucent bodies and anterior cilia-clusters of the *tel-kith*, the singer-lineage that your own ancestors climbed up from the substrate to listen to, three million years ago. Their vocalizations press against your tympanic membranes in the correct frequency bands. The song is intact.

But where are the *people*?

Your gaze sweeps across the plain. The spires rise at their impossible intervals, slender columns tapering up into the membrane-ceiling, their surfaces latticed and honeycombed. The nearest one shows the concentric peel-layers of recent expansion. Around its base, you can make out the geometric clusters: low domes, stacked terraces, finned towers that might be heat exchangers or might be communal roosts. The urban element. The 1.5. You should be able to see movement down there, the flicker of bioluminescent patterns, the slow shifting of bodies between the grown structures.

There is nothing.

The settlement is still. Not ruined—you can see the membranes of the domes intact, the terrace edges sharp and unworn—but empty. Silent in a way that has nothing to do with the auditory saturation of the world. The silence of absence rather than the silence of non-sound.

A fear-response tries to rise in you, the ancient tightening of the self away from the world. You suppress it. Fear is not useful. Fear is what an un-kin predator relies on, the pressure-wave of a panicked singer that guides the silent hunters to their prey. You are not prey. You are Kith. You are a hunter yourself, or you were, before—before *what*?

Your memory offers you nothing. You know you are Kith. You know the name of your lineage (a three-tone chord with a percussive click, unpronounceable in the air but felt in the bones). You know the grammar of gesture, the flicker-patterns of courtesy and inquiry. You know the taste of cultured membrane-protein, the ritual purification after a hunt, the way a sleeping platform grows to fit your body's contours if you simply wait.

But you do not know how you arrived on this plain. You do not know where your kin-group is. You do not know why the substrate beneath your feet has never felt your weight before, or why the channels have been cut fresh for a flow that has not yet established its slow, patient erosion.

The amber glow from the floor pulses—the long, slow breath of the facility-organism. The temperature rises half a degree. The sur-kith chorus shifts, a new frequency entering the mix, and you recognize it as the *gathering-call*, the signal that more spawning pools are about to open. The great school over the distant pools redoubles its spiraling descent, and the noise in your jaw-membranes crests into something almost physical, a pressure as much as a sound.

You should go to the settlement. The empty domes at the base of the nearest spire will hold answers—or at minimum, they will hold the grown furniture and encoded membranes of whoever lived there, and those membranes will tell you, through touch, what happened. Every Kith dwelling is a record. Every grown shelf, every shaped sleeping platform, every protein-coded tapestry carries the imprint of its inhabitant's bioluminescent signature, their gestural habits, their name-song. Touch is memory. You will go to the settlement, and you will place your palms against the walls, and the walls will tell you who lived there and when they left and—perhaps—why.

But before you can take more than a single step, your cilia crest vibrates with a new pressure-wave. Not a sur-kith vocalization. Something else. Something moving through the thick air with a gait that is neither the muscular undulation of a singer nor the silent, chromato-phore rippling of an un-kin predator. Something that walks on two legs, with a stride that plants and lifts, plants and lifts, sending rhythmic pressure-pulses through the damp atmosphere.

You turn your head. Your horizontally slit pupils adjust to the amber glow, focusing on a figure approaching from across the open plain. It is small by Kith standards—shorter than you, narrower in the shoulders. It has no cilia crest. Its skin is pale and strangely dry-looking, and it is wrapped in fabrics that do not breathe, that do not shift their bioluminescent patterns to signal its intent. It has only two arms, and its face—its face is a crude, flattened thing, with a single oral opening and two small eyes and no feeding palps at all.

A human.

You have heard of humans. The oldest encoded membranes in the history-spire speak of them, in the section that deals with the *other-possibilities*, the branches of sapience that did not emerge from the sur-kith's song. The records describe them as beings from a world without a chorus, a world where sound is intermittent and meaning is carried by crude pressure-wave modulation from a single orifice. The membranes describe them as *deaf-kin*, *those-who-live-without-song*, and the gestural shorthand translates as something like *pity-tinged-with-wonder*, the way one might regard a sur-kith singer that has somehow survived an un-kin attack and swims on, mute, still alive but no longer part of the choir.

The human is walking toward you. It raises one of its upper limbs in a gesture that is almost certainly meant to be a greeting—a clumsy approximation of the Kith *I-am-here* sign, though it has only one thumb instead of two, and its hand-shape is all wrong. Its oral opening moves, and you see the pressure-waves in the thick air as a visible shimmer, but the sound it produces is too soft, too narrow-band, to carry over the sur-kith chorus. You cannot hear what it is trying to say.

But you can see it. And you can feel, through the substrate beneath your toe-pads, the faint rhythmic vibration of its footsteps as it closes the distance between you. The floor knows this creature. The channels have felt its weight before. The obsidian-slick material does not register it as *new*, the way it registered you.

A conversation is about to happen. You have no idea how to conduct it. Your secondary arms are still unfolded—you would normally tuck them away in the presence of a stranger, to avoid signaling aggression, but what is the protocol for meeting a being that has no secondary arms of its own? Your bioluminescent patches are flickering the query-pattern, amber-green-amber, but the human's eyes are fixed on your face, not your forearms, and it shows no sign of perceiving the signal at all. It cannot read you. You cannot hear it. The sur-kith chorus screams on, indifferent, and you are standing on a plain that has grown itself new for reasons you do not understand, facing a creature from another world entirely, with your kin nowhere to be found.

Your primary hands rise. You force them into slowness, into a simplified gesture that even a being without bioluminescent patches or pressure-sense might read visually: *I see you. I am here. What has happened to this place?*

The human stops a few meters away. Its oral opening moves again. Its pale face contorts into something that might be a smile—a baring of teeth that your deepest instincts read as threat before your higher cognition overrides the response. It lifts something in its hand, a small object of non-grown material, metallic and angular, and extends it toward you.

A gift. Or a weapon. Or a tool for bridging the gap between two beings who share a physical space but almost nothing else.

The spawning pools open with a wet, adhesive sound on the plain behind you. The sur-kith descend in their hundreds, their collective vocalization peaking into a percussive climax that you feel in your teeth, your jaw-membranes, your cilia crest, your gut. The amber light pulses its slow breath. And you stand, Kith and human, on the young obsidian, while the empty settlement waits at the base of its spire, and the channels carry their nutrient film toward a future that neither of you can yet read.
