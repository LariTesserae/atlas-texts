Eighty meters below the mid-tier transit spine, where the arc lamps gutter to a dim and constant orange, there is a maintenance gallery that no crew has visited in eleven years. The access hatch is rusted into its frame. The grating is furred with a moss that has never been pruned, a moss of a species that exists nowhere else in the city, pale as bone and damp as a tongue. The air in this gallery does not move. The wind, which elsewhere in Siphon never stops, has forgotten this place. It has been left out of the city's breathing.

Here, in the stillness, the water does something it does nowhere else. It drips upward.

Not quickly. A single drop detaches from the ceiling—no, from a pipe that crosses the ceiling, a pipe no thicker than a wrist, sweating condensation—and it falls. It falls for perhaps two centimeters. Then it pauses. It hangs in the air, trembling, as if caught on an invisible thread. And then it climbs, slowly, up through the stagnant air, against the pull of the world's stone weight, until it merges with the film of moisture above. The process takes an hour. It has been repeating for eleven years, ever since the hatch rusted shut, ever since the last maintenance crew retreated up the ladder and sealed the gallery behind them. The citizens do not know about the upward drip. It is not in the maintenance logs. It is not in the city's records. It is not a ghost, because Siphon has no ghosts—the dead are pressure equalized, heat redistributed, biomass returned to the fungal stacks. But it is a thing that should not be, a local reversal of the city's thermodynamic order, and it persists.

The drip is patient.

It is not Ssh-haa. It is something else.

No one has ever seen the drip. No one has ever named it. If a citizen were to descend into this forgotten gallery—if the rusted hatch were to yield, if the maintenance ladder were to hold—that citizen would stand in the orange dimness and watch a drop of water fall upward, and they would think: *this is wrong*, and they would think: *the city does not make mistakes*, and they would think: *then this is not a mistake*. They would not scream. The citizens of Siphon do not scream often. They would simply stand very still, watching, and the chamber would be quiet except for the faint, wet sound of a single drop arriving where it started, over and over, a loop with no beginning and no end. The drip does not notice them, in the moment before the memory fades along with the dream.
