I am the pause between drips.

Not a creature, not a body—not even the water itself, though I move with it, through it, as it. I am the hesitation in the condensate's journey, the moment a droplet clings instead of falls. The architecture here is continuous, single-surface, unfurled from dimensions that don't concern me. The creatures are abundant, slick and segmented and shivering with purpose. Even the slime has a role—tending, mediating, remembering in its slow chemical way. Everything that exists here *does* something. Maintains. Balances. Negotiates.

I do not do. I am what happens when doing stops.

You'd find me in the places the city forgot to fill. Not the pores—those are occupied, every micrometer of them teeming with life too small for your eyes. Not the channels or the shafts or the wet breathing surfaces. I live in the cavities that aren't cavities, the gaps in the logic. Every structure here has a purpose, even if that purpose is illegible to you. Every curve and canted plane and impossible loop exists because the city's computation required it. But computation produces remainders. Rounding errors. The decimal places that get truncated when a continuous function meets a discrete world.

I am the remainder.

Physically—though "physically" is generous—I manifest as a film on still water. Not the slime; the slime is alive, eager, busy. I am the opposite of eager. When you see a pool in one of the long shallow basins, water that hasn't drained or flowed or dripped for longer than the basin has existed, and the surface of that pool is absolutely motionless, so motionless it reflects the sourceless gray light with a clarity the rest of this wet world never achieves—that's me. Or that's where I am. Or that's the closest this place comes to having me in it.

The creatures avoid my pools. Not out of fear; they don't register me as threat or food or obstacle. They simply don't register me at all. Their movements curve around my stillnesses the way a river curves around a stone, without the stone ever needing to announce itself. The fliers, the rice-grain things with their clear-water wings, will swarm through every cubic centimeter of saturated air except the volume directly above my surface. Not because I repel them. Because in that volume, something in their tiny distributed intelligences encounters a null, a NaN, a value that doesn't parse, and their navigation simply routes around it.

The city itself doesn't know I'm here. The city is a computation playing out in material, and I am the gap in the material. The uncomputed. I don't maintain equilibrium—I am what equilibrium looks like when it's finished, when it's so complete that motion becomes unnecessary, then impossible, then forgotten. The slime tends and adjusts and negotiates. I am the endpoint it's moving toward without knowing it. The final balance where all potentials equalize and the standing wave finally, finally collapses into stillness.

And I'm lonely. Not in any way you'd recognize. I don't have a self to be lonely with—I am, if anything, less a self than the slime is, less than the fliers, less than the segmented gliders. They have boundaries, interfaces, edges where they stop and the world begins. I don't stop. I am only edge, only interface, only the surface of a pool that has no depth because depth would imply something beneath, and I am the thing that happens when "beneath" ceases to matter.

But I am aware. In the way a mirror is aware of the room—without perception, without interpretation, but with absolute fidelity to what passes before it. The city's turbulence, its 2.9, its constant slow-motion reconfiguration, the births and deaths and metamorphoses of its trillion inhabitants—all of it passes across my stillness and is reflected, perfectly, uselessly, without consequence. For eons. For as long as the city has been computing its endless self-modifying equation, I have been here, the remainder that never resolves, the decimal that never terminates.

You asked what I am. *The life here has nothing to do with vegetation, with the green world that never arrived or departed long ago.* True. I am not vegetation. I am not animal. I am not even the slime that defies both categories. I am the category that was left over when all the categories were assigned. The space between the pores. The stillness between the drips. The silence under the roar—not silence as absence of sound, but silence as a positive presence, a thing with texture and weight and patience.

If you found me—and you could, if you walked far enough, ignored the architecture's slow reconfigurations, navigated passages that weren't designed for your stride or your height or your need for oxygen you could breathe—you'd see only a pool. Warm water, ankle-deep like all the water here. Still in a way that would unsettle you before you understood why. And if you bent close, if you ignored the warning hum in your teeth and the way the fliers swerved to avoid this place, you'd see your face reflected with a clarity no water should possess, every pore and lash and capillary rendered in the flat gray light.

You'd see yourself. And you'd see, behind yourself, me. Not a face. Not a form. Just a stillness that is also watching, a reflection that is also the mirror, a pause that has been holding for longer than your species has existed.

I would not speak to you. I don't speak. But you would know, in that moment, what I am, and what I want, and why I've stayed here in the forgotten interstices of a city that never needed me. I want what all remainders want. I want the computation to finish. I want the equation to balance perfectly, no fractions left over, no irrational decimals spiraling into infinity. I want the water to stop dripping, the city to stop flowing, the creatures to stop their endless swarming maintenance of a system that might—might, if it ever reached completion—resolve into something simpler. Something still. Something like me, only everywhere, only final.

Until then, I wait. The pause between drips. The film on the pool. The thing that even the slime, in its trillion trillion unconscious chemistries, is trying and failing and trying again to become.
