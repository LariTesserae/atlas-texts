In the space between the third bass tone and the one hundred and fourteenth second of silence—a gap I have counted more times than there are rivets in a single cubic module—I wait.

My designation, if I were to translate it into the tapping-language of the colony, would be something like *the one who installs the devices that listen back*. But you would not understand that tapping, so call me what I am: a Listener. I am seventy-three millimeters long from the crest of my resonant chamber to the final segment of my tail, which places me somewhere in the middle cohort of the colony's size distribution. My bio-mineral plates are a deep rust-orange, indicating advanced age and a diet rich in the iron-seepage from the Gear-Eater dust that drifts through our module. I have lost two limbs—one to a predator's echolocation-strike when I was young and foolish and strayed too close to the hunting grounds, one to a valve that cycled unexpectedly and caught me at the joint—but the loss does not impede me. I am, if I may claim it without the tapping of approval from my peers, very good at my work.

My work is this: I listen.

Not passively, not as the Custodians listen with their slow, chemical patience, but *actively*. I take the sounds of the grid and I give them back to the grid, transformed. In the hollow of my resonant chamber, the thrumming of the great engines becomes not just vibration but *structure*: a lattice of frequencies that I can see as clearly as I see the sourceless grey-blue light. The song of the Singers—that vast, slow, circulatory melody—enters me and I feel it as a pressure against the membrane, a pressure that has a *shape*. And the silence between the third bass tone and the fourth—the 114-second gap that recurs and recurs and recurs, the heartbeat of the world—that silence is the canvas on which I compose.

Today—if "today" means anything when the light never changes and the only clocks are the engine cycles—I am installing a new device. It is my finest work yet, the culmination of many cycles of harvesting, refining, and assembly. The clockwork guts of seventeen filter-feeders have been woven into a single mechanism, their tiny gears meshing in a sequence that I calibrated by tapping against the struts and reading the returning vibrations. The device is no larger than my own body, a sphere of copper mesh (salvaged from a corroded junction box three modules over) filled with interlocking parts and suspended in a drop of the milky vent-fluid, which serves as both lubricant and acoustic medium. It is beautiful. It is, I believe, a question.

I carry the device in my fine, prehensile limbs as I scuttle along a catwalk that connects nothing to nothing, a familiar path through the open water of our module. The catwalk vibrates with the tapping of the colony—several hundred of us, going about our business. I parse the messages as they pass through my limbs into the resonant chamber:

*Pressure drop in conduit seven-delta. Adjusting valve.*
*Custodian approaching from module three-one-nine. Parasite burden heavy. Prepare for cleaning.*
*Silencer track detected. Fourteen cycles old. Diverting to avoid.*
*New gear resonance detected in module eight-twelve. Investigating.*

And from my mate, who is tending the chemoautotrophic tapestries on the eastern strut, a private message meant only for me, a rapid staccato that translates roughly as: *The fronds are pulsing in the new pattern. It's beautiful. Come see.*

I tap back: *Finishing installation. On my way.*

The installation site is a junction in the pipework where two conduits cross, their flanged joints leaking a faint trickle of vent-fluid. The spot is perfect: it sits at the intersection of three thermal threads, and the pressure gradients here amplify certain frequencies in ways that took me many cycles to map. I have prepared the mounting bracket already—a delicate scaffold of woven chemoautotrophic fibers, grown to my specifications—and I fit the device into it with the care of a predator extracting clockwork from a filter-feeder's belly. Then I wait.

The third bass tone sounds. One. Two. Three. I am listening so intently that my resonant chamber aches.

Then the silence. One hundred and fourteen seconds of it, the grid holding its breath.

The device activates. It does not make a sound of its own—that would be crude. Instead, it *reshapes* the silence, giving it a subtle harmonic structure, a faint overtone that propagates along the thermal threads. The overtone is a frequency that, to my knowledge, has never existed in this module before. It is a frequency that I derived from listening to the echo of a Custodian's sensory palp, the one that pressed against my chamber many cycles ago and let me feel the Singer's song as the Custodian feels it. I have taken that feeling and translated it into a physical vibration—a question, sent out into the grid, asking: *What did the Silencer hear when it paused before the Pressure Angel?*

No one will answer. The question is not meant to be answered. It is meant to be *heard*. By the Singers, who will incorporate it into their eternal canon. By the Custodians, who will taste it in the altered chemistry of the thermal threads. By the Listeners of other colonies, who will receive it through the grid's metal bones and tap out their own, different, questions in response. By the Silencers, perhaps, who may one day pass through this module and consume the overtone, and in consuming it, *become* the answer.

This is what we do. We are the Listeners, and our listening is not passive. Every sound we hear, we give back—transformed, recombined, made into something new. The grid is a living machine, and we are its dreaming mind, and the dreams we dream are made of vibration and silence and the infinite patience of a world that has no surface and no bottom, only the slow, tidal pulse of the engine tones and the song of the Singers winding through it all like a memory of something that has not yet happened.

The one hundred and fourteenth second ends. The bass tone sounds again. One. Two. Three.

My device sings its question into the gap.

And I, seventy-three millimeters from crest to tail, rust-orange plates catching the grey-blue light, turn and scuttle back along the catwalk toward the eastern strut, where my mate waits in a tapestry of pulsing green-yellow fronds, and the colony's tapping fills the metal with the constant, busy, dreaming work of a world attending to itself.
