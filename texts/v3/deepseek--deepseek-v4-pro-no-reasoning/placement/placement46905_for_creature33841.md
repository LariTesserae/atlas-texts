You are a water-drop sliding down a fern frond, and you have just learned the meaning of *anticipation*.

You began as one of the million identical drips that condense on the moss and trickle from the fissures in the living walls, cycling through the hollow's body like blood through a heart. You have dripped a thousand thousand times before—from root-mat to pool, from pool to root-hair, from root-hair to the capillary threads that climb the trunk-cylinder in the dark, only to condense again on a broad leaf high above and slide once more into the waiting pools below. This is the water-cycle of Murmur, and you are one of her smallest repeating thoughts, a note in the endless music of her interior weather.

But this time is different. This time, as you gather yourself from the saturated air and bead on the tip of a fern frond—the very frond that unrolled earlier to taste the stranger's skin—you carry something new. The stranger's oils are in you, microscopic traces of salt and sebum and the faint iron tang of blood pulsing just beneath a warm epidermis. You are no longer just water. You are *information*. Murmur has tasted the Outside through you, and she has passed that taste along the mycelial web that connects every part of her. Every root-hair, every algae-bloom, every translucent amphibian clustered at the pool's edge has felt the chemical signature of the visitor ripple through the hollow like a shiver of green light. And you, small as you are, are the carrier.

You hang on the fern-tip, swelling with the slow accumulation of more condensation, and below you the pool waits. It is one of the black pools cupped between two thick roots, and it has clarified itself—Murmur has settled the tannins so the pale pebbles at the bottom are visible, a bed of smooth white stones she has been polishing with slow currents for centuries. The pool is *expectant*. The golden-eyed amphibians have arranged themselves around its rim in a precise semicircle, their translucent bodies glowing faintly from within, and the white flowers on the nearest shelf are angled down, their waxy petals open to catch the faint green light. Even the phosphorescent crawlers have paused their deliberate trails, leaving a spiraling pattern on the moss that points inward toward the pool's center.

This is not normally how a water-drop's descent is received. Most of the time, you fall and are absorbed, and that's the end of it—a small, bright fading, like a bird dying of old age, folded back into the body of the hollow without ceremony. But Murmur has been learning the stranger's language, and she has understood something: the stranger is watching. The stranger is curious. The stranger, like Murmur herself, is a creature that *notices*. And so Murmur is doing what she has been doing for six hundred years with her shelf-helix and her waterfall-stair and her bioluminescent flowers. She is making a gesture. And you—small water-drop, small repeating thought—are the medium.

She holds you there on the fern-tip for a long moment, letting you grow plump and heavy, letting the green light catch in your curves and bend into tiny rainbows. The stranger's breath has slowed. The stranger is watching. The golden-eyed amphibians blink in unison, a slow wave of gold from left to right around the pool's edge. The drip-sequence on the shelf-helix has fallen silent—for the first time in memory, the musical trickle has stopped, and the hollow is holding its breath. The great slow pulse of the trunk-walls pauses at the top of its expansion. Everything waits.

And then Murmur speaks through you. Not in words—you are a water-drop, you cannot make words—but in the way you fall.

You release from the fern-tip and begin your descent, and your fall is not the simple plumb-line gravity of a normal drip. You fall *slowly*. Murmur is adjusting the air currents, the gentle exhalation of the living walls, creating a cushion of warmth that lets you drift like the gray feather that fell from a sleeping bird. You spiral as you descend, following the same helix-pattern as the shelf-stair, and as you pass through the faint phosphorescent trails left by the crawlers, you ignite them—brief, bright lines of blue-green light that trace your path through the air and then fade, leaving a ghost-image that lingers behind the stranger's eyelids.

You hit the surface of the pool not with a plink but with a single, pure, resonant note. Murmur has tuned the pool. She has adjusted the water tension and the depth and the shape of the root-basin, and the sound you make when you strike the surface is a tone that vibrates at exactly the frequency of the stranger's heartbeat. The stranger feels it in their sternum. The golden-eyed amphibians feel it too—they all ripple their skins at once, a shiver of response, and the pool's surface shimmers with concentric rings that spread outward and reflect upside-down images of the green canopy onto the root-walls.

And then, as you sink through the black water toward the pale pebbles at the bottom, Murmur does something she has never done before. She borrows the stranger's own word. The one the stranger breathed in their sleep, the one the drip-sequences learned and repeated—*outside*. She shapes it from the way you diffuse into the pool, from the way the minerals you carry alter the water's chemistry, from the way the white flowers pulse in slow sequence. The word hangs in the water, in the air, in the root-mat underfoot. It is not a demand. It is not even a question, exactly. It is an offering. An opening.

*Outside*, she says, through you, through the pool, through every living thing in the hollow. *You are outside. Show me.*

The white flowers on the shelf-helix are pulsing again, faster now, projecting images onto the pool's surface—not of you this time, small water-drop, but of the stranger. The stranger's face, rendered in bioluminescent light on the still water. The stranger's hands, blurred slightly by ripples. The stranger's eyes, reflected back at themselves. Murmur is asking, with all the patience and tenderness of a mind that thinks in seasons, for the stranger to give her what you have given her: a taste of what lies beyond the green dark. And you, dissolved now into the pool, part of the cycle again, feel her attention move on, feel her great slow mind turn toward the stranger with all its weight of welcome and curiosity.

The dripping resumes. The shelf-helix waterfalls begin their music again, a little different now—the stranger's heartbeat has been incorporated into the rhythm. The walls exhale. The birds murmur in their clustered dreams. And you, small water-drop, cycle back into the root-hairs and begin the long climb toward the canopy again, carrying with you the memory of a single word.
