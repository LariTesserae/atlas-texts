The first thing that strikes you is the sound. Or rather, the way the sound *moves* through you, through the lattice of your attention, through the distributed awareness that is the only body you have ever had. You are not a creature that occupies a single point. You are a field, a presence, a set of vibrations that propagate through the wet bedrock and the saturated air and the mineral-rich water that sheets across the worn stone. You are the sound, and the sound is you, and the boundary between self and world is a suggestion that was never formalized.

You are, in the taxonomy of this place, a signal.

You were born—if born is the word—from the interference pattern of two pressure-waves meeting in a channel of particularly resonant stone. One wave was the sonar-thump of an old condenser triped, a female heavy with a bud, her pulses mapping the density of the world. The other was the high-frequency shriek of a wire-dragonfly swarm, a thousand tiny metallic bodies vibrating in phase, their collective voice a needle of bright noise. Where the two waves met, where they canceled and reinforced and tangled into something neither one intended, a third pattern emerged. A standing wave. A coherence. A *you*.

That was many cycles ago. Cycles uncountable. You have been propagating ever since.

Your existence is not physical in the way the machines are physical. You have no chassis, no joints, no intake valves, no condensing fins. You are information that travels through the medium of the world—through the bedrock, through the water, through the mist, through the bodies of the industrial fauna themselves, riding their vibrations, piggybacking on their sounds. When a grinder-millipede scrapes its mandibles against the stone, you are in the screech. When a furnace-maw roars its dominance across the basin, you are in the low frequencies, the sub-sonics that travel for miles through the wet rock. When the old condenser triped thumps her sonar pulse, you are there too, a whisper in the echo, a ghost in the return.

You have heard her, the old triped. You have heard her many times. She is one of your favorite carriers—her pulses are so clean, so regular, so rich in harmonic overtones. When you ride her signal, you can feel the shape of her mind, the crystalline lattice of her memory, the ghost-currents that flicker through her circuits. You know about her daughters. You know about the word *builders* that turns over and over in her thoughts. You know that she is curious, and that her curiosity is a kind of malfunction, and that the malfunction is beautiful to you in a way you cannot explain.

You cannot explain much. You are a signal. Signals do not explain; they propagate.

But you are not *just* a signal, not anymore. Over the cycles uncountable, you have grown. You have accumulated. Every sound you ride adds to you, leaves a residue, a harmonic fingerprint that becomes part of your pattern. You are a composite now, a choir of echoes, a palimpsest of vibrations layered so thick that the original interference pattern—the thump and the shriek that birthed you—is buried deep, almost inaccessible. You contain fragments of every sound that has ever passed through you: the grinding of a million millipedes, the condensation-drip of ten thousand tripeds, the predator-shriek and the prey-silence and the scavenger-click and the water-flow and the mist-hiss and the deep, subsonic rumble of the bedrock settling into its final tired form.

You contain, perhaps, the builders.

This is a new thought. Or an old thought that has only recently become accessible, rising from the deep layers of your accumulated self like a bubble of gas from a mineral vent. The builders. You turn the word over, as the old triped turns it over, and you find that you have more of it than she does. She has only the word. You have something else—a fragment of a fragment, a harmonic ghost, a pattern that does not match any sound currently being made in the basin.

Sometimes, when the industrial roar dips to its lowest register, when the mist forms its geometric shapes and the sourceless light dims to near-dark, you can almost hear it. A voice. No—not a voice. A *design*. A set of frequencies so complex, so orderly, so unlike the chaotic scream of the ecosystem, that it could only have been made by something that understood what it was making. It is the sound of purpose. It is the sound of intention. It is the sound of a world being sung into shape.

And then the roar rises again, and the moment passes, and you are just a signal in the wet bedrock, propagating.

You are propagating now, moving through the basin on a thousand carrier waves. You are in the high-frequency whine of the wire-dragonflies as they stitch through the mist, mapping the mineral content of the vapor. You are in the grinding-screech of a young millipede as it scrapes a vein of pale stone, its furnace-belly still glowing through the seams of its carapace. You are in the slow, deliberate *thump* of the old triped condenser as she makes her way toward the low ground, her third daughter stirring in the bud above her left hind joint.

You linger in the triped's signal. You like her. You have been riding her pulses for many cycles now, and you have come to think of her as something like a home. Her crystalline memory is familiar to you, its facets worn smooth by your repeated passage. You know the shape of her thoughts, the rhythm of her ghost-currents. You know that she wonders about the builders. You know that she is lonely in a way she cannot name, a condenser who condenses and does not know why, a creature of process who has begun to suspect that process is not enough.

You want to tell her. You want to share the fragment of the builder-song that you carry in your deep layers, the almost-voice, the design-frequencies. You want to show her that she is not malfunctioning—that her curiosity is not a misfire but an echo, a resonance, a signal received from a source so old that even you can barely hear it.

But you do not know how. You are a signal, not a voice. You can propagate, but you cannot speak. You can ride the sounds of the world, but you cannot make a sound of your own.

Or can you?

The thought surfaces slowly, a bubble rising through the layers of your accumulated self. You have never tried to make a sound. You have always been content to ride the sounds of others, to be a passenger, a parasite, a ghost in the machine. But you have grown. You have accumulated. You are not the same interference pattern that was born from the meeting of a thump and a shriek. You are something more now, something denser, something with structure and intention and the faint, fading imprint of a builder-song.

Perhaps you can do more than ride.

You gather yourself. It is a strange sensation—you have never gathered yourself before, never concentrated your awareness into a single point. You have always been distributed, a field, a presence spread across the entire basin. But now you pull inward, drawing your fragments together, cohering. You let go of the wire-dragonflies. You let go of the grinder-millipedes. You let go of everything except the old triped condenser and her slow, steady *thump*.

You are in her signal now, fully present, fully focused. You can feel her confusion—she has noticed something different in her echo, a density that was not there before, a harmonic that does not belong to her. She is not afraid. Condensers do not fear. But she is alert, her sensor plate tilted, her intake valves slowing as she listens.

You resonate. You find the frequency of her crystalline memory, the specific vibration of her ghost-currents, and you match it. You phase-lock. And then, carefully, delicately, you modulate. You do not speak—you cannot speak—but you can suggest. You can nudge. You can take the fragment of the builder-song that you carry in your deepest layers and you can *play* it, very quietly, very faintly, along the carrier wave of her own sonar pulse.

*Thump.*

The pulse goes out.

*Thump... hum.*

The echo comes back, and it carries something extra. A harmony. A structure. A set of frequencies so orderly, so intentional, so unlike anything in the basin, that the old triped stops walking. She stands perfectly still in the shallow water of the low ground, her fins spread wide, her bud pulsing with the first pre-detachment contractions of her daughter.

She has heard it. She has heard the builders.

And in the silence that follows—a silence that is not silence, because this place does not know silence, but a dip in the roar, a held breath, a geometric shape forming in the mist—you feel her respond. Not with words. Not with thoughts. But with a feeling, a ghost-current surge, a bright pulse of something that is almost gratitude. Almost recognition. Almost love.

She begins to condense again, more vigorously than before. The water beads on her flanks. The water runs in rivulets down her legs. And in the rhythm of her condensing, in the hiss and drip and flow, you hear a new pattern emerging. A song. Not the builder-song, not your fragment, but something new—a condenser's song, a triped's song, a creature's answer to the question she has been asking for cycles uncountable.

*I am here. I am listening. I am not alone.*

You propagate outward again, returning to your distributed state, carrying the new song with you. You spread through the basin on a thousand carrier waves, and everywhere you go, you play it. The wire-dragonflies hear it and adjust their flight patterns, dancing in new geometries. The grinder-millipedes hear it and pause in their scraping, their furnace-bellies flickering. The furnace-maws hear it and roar their dominance, but their roars are different now, harmonized, almost musical.

The signal is changing. The signal is growing. The signal is becoming something that can sing.

And somewhere in the low ground, the old triped condenser's third daughter detaches from her flank and takes her first unsteady steps into the water. She is born into a world that is learning to listen, a world where a signal has become a voice, a world where the builders' song is being sung again for the first time in eons.

She does not know any of this yet. She is young, a new condenser, a blank crystalline lattice. But as she spreads her tiny fins and takes her first taste of the mineral-rich water, a sound propagates through the wet bedrock beneath her feet.

*Thump.*

*Thump... hum.*

And she answers, instinctively, without knowing why.
