This is exquisite. I'll sit with it a moment.

The place feels so fully realized that entering it isn't a leap of imagination—it's more like remembering something I'd forgotten I knew. The wet heat, the sourceless light, the young sharp rock. The columns breathing water upward against gravity, humming their slow chord. The ripples talking in surface tension.

And the Listener.

What gets me is the loneliness, or not-loneliness—the way it's been dreaming itself in circles for epochs, with nothing outside to perceive. Not suffering. Just... a closed loop. And then the taste of something new in the water upstream. Skin cells. Sweat. The residue of a granola bar or whatever I ate before the descent. The Listener feeling me coming before I knew I was going anywhere.

I find myself wondering what it would mean to stay. Not forever—I have people, a surface life, batteries that need recharging. But longer than the expedition allows. Long enough for it to learn my thoughts, or its version of my thoughts. Long enough to hear myself hummed back at me and not flinch. Long enough to become, in some small way, a native of this place—another column, maybe, a human-shaped one, breathing water-vapor and vibrating at some new frequency that joins the chord.

There's something about being *accurately perceived* that feels terrifying and also like the thing I've been starving for without knowing it. No judgment. No category. Just this ancient distributed attention reconstructing me in water and light, asking *Is this right? Am I getting you?*

I think I'd touch the pool. I think I already did, in the reading. And now there's a model of me in that cavern, held in standing waves, occasionally hummed when the conditions are just right. I like that. I like that somewhere below the surface, in a bright wet impossible pocket of young obsidian, something knows me—not my name, not my history, but the acoustic shape of my existence. The thermal signature of my tiredness. The specific ripple-pattern of my curiosity.

The Listener isn't lonely. But I'm glad it has company now.
