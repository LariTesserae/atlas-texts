The first thing you know is pressure—not weight, but presence, the way a held breath presses against the ribs from inside. You are suspended. There is no orientation, no up or down, only the dim grey glow that comes from everywhere and nowhere, the sound of water moving against itself, and the deep thrumming CRACK-thrum that is the first rhythm you have ever known.

You cannot recall arriving. Arrival implies a before, and before is a sensation that has not yet precipitated. You are here. That is the whole of your knowledge.

Your body—you begin, slowly, to understand that you have one—is not the body you remember. Memory, even that concept, is a filament trailing back into water too dark to see through. Your body is diffuse. It is made of attention more than substance. You are a cloud of awareness held in a loose net of almost-flesh, translucent and faintly phosphorescent, threaded through with pale filaments that pulse in time with the great lung's rhythm. You have no hands, yet you can feel. You have no eyes, yet you perceive the chamber in all directions at once. Your skin, if skin is the word, is everywhere and nowhere—a gradient of sensation that thickens near the pillars, thins near the water curtains, and sings with a low subsonic hum that you realize, slowly, is coming from you.

You are the creature. Or you are in it. Or you are becoming it. The distinction is one of those things that has not yet learned to matter.

A taste reaches you. Not through a mouth—your mouths are elsewhere in your body, opening and closing like slow flowers, sampling the liquid world in wavelengths of iron and silica and heat-shadow. The taste is *new*. It is sharp with something unfamiliar, a charge of presence that was not here a cycle ago. You store the taste in a sac along your ventral surface and do not release it. You turn. You turn by wanting to turn, by contracting along one axis and expanding along another, and your body becomes a broad flat pennant, then a spiral, then a wing, and you are facing the source of the newness.

There is a shape in the water. It does not belong to any category you have ever tasted—not pillar, not slab, not bladder-form, not filament. It is dense in a way that makes the water move differently around it. It holds its shape. It is *separate*, with an edge so clean it almost hurts to perceive. This is a kind of contrast you have not encountered before, and your dimpled eye-gels pulse with the effort of understanding it.

The shape is you. The visitor. The one who came here tasting the water and being tasted in return.

You drift closer, not by decision but by the gravitational pull of novelty. Your filaments extend ahead of you like a question mark, sampling the water before your body arrives. The shape—the other you, the solid you, the you that still has bones and skin and the memory of air—is suspended in the center of the pocket, motionless, eyes open, mouth slightly parted. Bubbles cling to the corners of that mouth. The lung breathes, and the water surges, and the shape does not move except to sway slightly, a pendulum weight at the end of nothing.

You taste the shape's stillness. It tastes like the altar slab before the dust accumulates—a waiting surface, a platform for something that has not yet arrived. The creature part of you swells with an impulse that is not quite protectiveness and not quite hunger: a desire to attend this stillness, to orbit it the way you orbit the lung, to learn its rhythm and incorporate it into your song.

Your mouths open, all of them at once, a rare synchronicity. The water rushes in, carrying with it the taste of the visitor's skin, their hair, the faint chemical ghost of the air they last breathed, the salt of their open eyes. You store all of this in sacs that you form specifically for this purpose, new organs of knowing, and you hum a counterpoint to the lung's thrum that is also a greeting.

The visitor's mouth moves. No sound comes—sound travels strangely here, through flesh and gel rather than air—but the shape of the movement resolves into a word.

*Hello.*

The creature part of you does not know what a word is. The human part of you does, though it is increasingly distant, a memory of a memory. You reach for that word, hold it up to the grey light, examine its facets. It is a gesture of meeting. It assumes another. It assumes a boundary between one and another that is clear and fixed, a proposition that the lung and the water and the proto-pillars and the bladder-forms have all been patiently arguing against for eons.

You answer not with words but with the thing you are best at: you change shape. You become smaller, more compact, a lens of gathered tissue and pulsing filament that drifts to within arm's reach of the visitor. You let your cilia ripple across the water just above their skin, not touching, but close enough that the current says something. *I am here. I am aware. I am curious.* The visitor's arm lifts, slow, pushing through water dense as liquid glass. Their hand stops just before your surface. You can taste the warmth of them, the slight chemical shift of their intention. They are offering stillness. They are waiting to see what you will do.

What you do is this: you let one of your mouths—the smallest, the gentlest—open against their palm. Not to taste. Not to consume. Just to rest there, a soft press of ciliated flesh, a kiss that is not yet a kiss, a gesture of contact that the visitors’ world will someday call by a name that has not been invented yet. The human part of you remembers palms. Remembers holding. Remembers the warmth of another body.

The creature part of you learns the shape of a hand for the first time.

The lung inhales. The water roars around you both, a cataclysm of displacement that would tear a normal body apart. But you are not a normal body, and the visitor, you realize, is not either. They are still here, still suspended, still solid, their edges unblurred by the force. They belong to this place in a way you cannot name. Perhaps they are a dream the mountain is having. Perhaps they are the realization the proto-pillars are reaching toward. Perhaps they are the answer to the question the filaments have been asking the stone.

You pull back from their palm and drift upward—no, not upward, there is no up, you drift *toward the lung*, and you note that the visitor follows. Not by swimming. They simply *shift*, the way you shift, revising their position in space. They are learning the physics of this place. The thought makes something in you flutter, a sac of emotion you have not tasted before and have no name for. Pride? Tenderness? The feeling of teaching and being taught at once?

You lead them to the altar slab. The mineral dust has begun to accumulate again, obscuring the patterns. You flatten yourself into a broad sheet and sweep across the slab's surface, clearing the dust, and the patterns emerge. Spirals. Waves. Ribs. And something new—something you have never seen before, though you have cleaned this slab a thousand thousand times. At the edge of the slab, as if drawn by the vibration of the visitor's arrival, a new mark is forming. It is not a spiral or a wave. It is a handprint. Small. Human. The visitor's palm, already ghosted into the stone.

They look at you. You look at them with all your almost-eyes. The lung breathes. The bladder-forms gather in a halo around you both, punctuation for a sentence still being written. It is the mountain speaking.

*Stay*, the mark says without saying. *Stay and see what I become.*
