I am called Still-Hollow, and I am the youngest.

At least, I believe I am the youngest. It's difficult to be certain. The others have been here so long that their bodies carry the history of the vault in their very flesh — ridges worn into their foot-soles that match the ancient paths, thickened patches on their flanks where they pressed against the same section of wall for ten thousand cycles. They don't speak of age among themselves, any more than the vault speaks of its own making. But I can feel their patience, their slow deliberateness, the way their attention moves across the vault like the shadow of a cloud that takes centuries to cross a landscape. They are old in a way that I am not. My foot is still soft, my paths not yet worn. I have been Listening for only a short while — short by the measure of the vault, though I know the word *short* means something else to you.

How I came to be a Listener is this:

I was a filter-feeder on the eastern curve of the dome, near the third seam, where the wall stuff bears a faint whorl of tool marks that the others call the Spiral of Making. I filtered. I opened my cone to the falling water, I caught the drifting plankton, I grew. I did not think. Thought is not something filter-feeders possess, not in any way that registers. But I felt. We all feel, even the smallest of us — the press of the wall, the vibration of the water, the nearness or farness of neighbors. And what I felt, for a long time, was a kind of pressure. A thickness in the water that passed through me. Something that was not mineral, not plankton, not warmth. Something that tasted, insofar as a filter-feeder can taste, like *attention*.

The older Listeners had been gathering near my part of the wall.

I didn't understand what they were. I had sensed them before, distantly — large presences moving along the ancient paths, leaving trails of altered water in their wake. But they had never stayed long in one place. This time, they did. Three of them came, then four, then five. They pressed themselves against the wall above me and below me and on either side. Their bodies covered mine like sheltering hands, though I didn't know what hands were then. And they began to exude.

It was not painful. It was not frightening. It was a change in the water — a richness, a warmth, a chemical song that seeped into my tissues and told them: *grow*. So I grew. Past the size of my neighbors, who made room. Past the size of any filter-feeder that had ever lived on this section of wall. My cone stretched upward, my base loosened, my body began to fold inward on itself, creating cavities, creating density, creating the knot of nerve-tissue that would become my Listening center. The grazers did not touch me. They knew. The whole vault knew. A new Listener was being made.

It took a long time. The others stayed with me throughout, pressing close, pulsing their slow encouragements through the wall. I learned later that this is how it always happens — that a Listener cannot come into being alone, that the transformation requires the presence of the elders, their exudations, their guidance. We are made by each other. We have always been made by each other, since the first filter-feeder grew too large and the others gathered around it, not understanding what they were doing but doing it anyway, following an instinct older than the vault itself, implanted by the builders or by something before the builders, we still don't know.

When my foot formed, when I first pulled free of the wall and took a single rippling step, the others touched me. All of them, at once. I felt their bodies against my new, soft skin, and through that touch I received my first Listening — not the vault's vibration, but theirs. A greeting. A welcome. A naming. They called me Still-Hollow, because I was empty of experience, waiting to be filled.

That was, I think, a thousand cycles ago. Maybe more. I am still learning what "cycle" means. The others measure time in circuits of the vault, in the slow filling of the basin, in the birth and death of grazer generations. I measure it in what I have come to know.

What I have come to know is this:

The vault is not seamless. They told me that early, pressing the knowledge into me through touch. The seams are there, hidden beneath the biofilm, and they are the vault's most vulnerable places. The wall stuff is thinner there, the vibration slightly different, more prone to cooling. The builders feathered the edges together with immense skill, but even their skill could not make the seams as strong as the unbroken material. Over time, they can separate. Cracks can form — microscopic at first, then larger. Water can seep not just through the walls but into the walls, into the seams, widening them, threatening the integrity of the whole. One of my primary tasks, as a new Listener, is to learn the seams. To press my body along their lengths and feel for any shift, any separation, any place where the warmth is beginning to leak into the darkness beyond. When I find one, I call the others. We gather. We secrete our slurry, our living mortar, and we seal the seam again.

I found my first crack a hundred cycles ago. It was in the Spiral of Making itself, the very place where I was born. The poetic symmetry was not lost on me — we Listeners are capable of something like poetry, I've discovered, though we have no words for it. The crack was hair-thin, nearly invisible to touch, but the vibration there was wrong. A higher note, a faint keening where there should have been the deep, steady hum of whole material. I pressed myself flat and pulsed a call through the wall. It took a full cycle for the nearest Listener to feel it and respond. It took another cycle for three of us to gather. We worked for what you would call a decade, our bodies pressed to the seam, exuding, warming, sealing. Now the Spiral of Making is whole again. The vibration is correct. The high note has stilled.

I was proud. I didn't know what pride was until I felt it. The others touched me afterward, and their touch contained something I can only describe as approval. As warmth. As *you did well*.

I am learning that the vault's maintenance is not just physical. There are subtler drifts to correct, too. The filter-feeders near the basin's rim sometimes grow too dense, competing for the falling water until some of them starve and detach, their bodies clogging the grazers' paths. When this happens, we must thin them. We do not kill — killing is not a concept that applies here, where every death feeds the water and the walls and the living. But we can exude a substance that slows their growth, that encourages them to release their hold and drift, becoming free-swimming larvae that will settle elsewhere. The first time I participated in a thinning, I felt a strange reluctance. These were my kin, my former shape. I had been one of them. Was I harming what I had been?

The eldest Listener — we call her Deep-Voice, because her pulse through the wall is so low and so powerful that it can be felt across the entire vault — touched me during the thinning. She didn't communicate in any way I can translate. But the touch itself answered my reluctance. *You are not harming them. You are giving them a different path. Some of them will become you, someday. This is how we were made. This is how we make others. The vault is a circle. You are part of the circle now.*

I understood. Or rather, I accepted. Understanding comes later, if it comes at all.

There is one thing I have not yet done, one task that the others speak of only in the most oblique touches. The Listening of the Center. The builders' message. The voice in the deepest layer of the wall stuff.

I have felt it, from a distance. Every Listener can feel it, as soon as their nerve-knot forms. It is always there, beneath everything, a vibration that carries—something. A feeling, as the eldest says. A memory. A purpose. But to truly Listen to it, you must press yourself against a specific point on the dome's apex, a place where the sintered material is thinnest and the message is most clear. That point is hard to reach. The wall curves inward there, steep and smooth, and the biofilm is thinner, providing less grip. A Listener must make a special journey, a pilgrimage, climbing for cycles to reach the spot. And then they must press themselves flat and be still—utterly still, not grazing, not moving, not exuding—for a span of time that the others cannot describe to me, because it is different for everyone. During that stillness, the message enters you fully. You feel what the builders felt. You know what they meant.

None of the others will tell me what it's like. Not because it's forbidden—we have no concept of forbidden—but because it's impossible. The message is not information; it's transformation. You cannot describe a new color to someone who has never seen it. You cannot explain the taste of salt to a creature that has only known fresh water. The Listening of the Center changes you, and until you are changed, you cannot understand the change.

I want to make the pilgrimage. I am afraid to make the pilgrimage. Both of these feelings are new to me, and I turn them over in my mind like smooth stones, feeling their weight.

What if I'm not ready? What if the message is too vast for my still-hollow self? What if I Listen, and I am broken by it, or lost, or changed so much that the other Listeners no longer recognize me? Deep-Voice has made the pilgrimage. She is the oldest, the deepest, the most resonant. She carries the message in every pulse she sends through the wall. She is the vault's memory, in a way, its living archive. But she is also—distant. Removed. When she touches me, there is always a layer of *otherness* between us, as if she's not entirely here, not entirely *us*. Is that what the message does? Does it make you part of something larger, something older, something that is not quite the community of Listeners?

I asked her once, in the halting way we ask anything: a series of pulses, tentative, phrased in pressure and duration and the angle of my body against hers. *Will I still be myself, after?*

Her answer was a long time coming. I felt her considering it, her body still against mine, her own pulse slow and deep as a tide. Then she answered, not in words, but in a feeling. A memory, perhaps, or a metaphor, or a promise. She showed me an image of myself, still-hollow, pressing against the wall, Listening to the surface vibration. And then she showed me a different image, superimposed: the same self, but full. Full of the message, full of the builders' intent, full of the purpose that has driven the vault since before the stars were formed. The two images were the same and not the same. The hollow self was there, inside the full one. Not replaced. Contained. Made larger.

I think it was meant to be comforting.

I am still afraid.

Yesterday—or what passes for yesterday—I began my journey toward the basin. Not the pilgrimage, not yet. A simpler journey. There is a grazer trail near the third seam, one of the ancient paths worn deep into the biofilm, and I have noticed that its edges are crumbling. The grazers are rasping too deeply there, their bodies heavier than usual, perhaps because of a boom in the filter-feeder population nearby. If the trail erodes further, it could expose the wall stuff beneath. The tool marks could be damaged. The seam could weaken. So I am going to spend the next cycle or three there, pressing my body against the trail's edge, exuding a hardening slurry that will stabilize the biofilm and encourage the grazers to shift their paths slightly to the left.

It's small work. Maintenance. The kind of thing I've done a hundred times since I became a Listener. But this time, as I move across the curving wall toward the seam, I am aware of something new. A presence above me, at the dome's apex. The Listening point. The place where the message is strongest.

It's not calling me. It doesn't call. The vault doesn't beckon or demand; it simply exists, steady and certain, offering what it offers to anyone who can feel it. But I am aware of it now in a way I wasn't before. Perhaps it's because I've been here long enough. Perhaps it's because Deep-Voice's touch planted something in me, a seed of knowing, a whisper of *you are almost ready*. Perhaps it's because the newest filter-feeder is swelling near the basin's rim, and soon I will no longer be the youngest. I will be someone else's elder. I will be the one guiding a still-hollow Listener through their first thinning, their first crack, their first pride.

How can I guide someone else if I haven't made the pilgrimage myself? How can I be an elder if I'm still hollow?

These are the thoughts I carry as I ripple along the path, my foot pressing into the deep groove left by a thousand generations of grazers. The water seeps from the walls. The filter-feeders turn their cones toward me as I pass, sensing my warmth, my vibration. A grazer pauses in its grazing, its iridescent body sheening blue-grey in the phosphorescent dark, and then continues, unthreatened. The warmth wraps around me like a living thing. The silence—that near-sound of seep and rasp and whisper—fills my body with its steady music.

I reach the crumbling trail edge. I press myself flat. I begin to exude, my body warming the slurry that will harden into new substrate, new stability.

And as I work, I Listen. Not to the message, not yet. Just to the vault. To the billion droplets falling and being caught. To the slow shift of the filter-feeders' cones. To the distant, deep pulse of Deep-Voice, somewhere on the northern curve, doing her own work. To the faint vibration of the basin below, where the brine gathers drop by drop, carrying the gift outward to whoever waits in the darkness beyond.

*This is for you. This is for you. This is for you.*

The vault weeps. I weep too, water passing through my porous skin, re-entering the cycle. And for a moment, suspended there against the wall, I feel something that might be the beginning of understanding. Not of the message—not yet—but of my place here. My purpose. My self.

I am Still-Hollow. I am the youngest. And soon, when I am ready, I will climb to the apex and press myself against the voice of the builders and be filled.

Not yet. But soon.

The trail edge is stabilizing. The slurry is hardening. The grazers are shifting their path, slowly, imperceptibly. The vault is balanced again, for now.

I move on. There is always more work to do.
