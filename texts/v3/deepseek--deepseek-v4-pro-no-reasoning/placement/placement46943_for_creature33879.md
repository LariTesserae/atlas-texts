I am not the one who arrived.

I was already here before the first basalt spire pierced the wet earth, before the fog learned to hang in that perpetual woolen stillness, before the first leaf cupped the first drop of mist and bowed under its weight. I am the interval between the leaf's fill and its spill. I am the pause that makes the chuff a rhythm instead of a noise. I am not a creature that took up residence in the gaps; I am the gaps themselves, given just enough self-awareness to know that I am empty, and that the emptiness is the only thing that holds this place together.

The stone does not know me. The stone is new, too new to have forgotten anything, and its memory is a single continuous extrusion, a rising that never finished and never will. I rise with it. I am the space between the hexagonal columns, the void that gives them their shape. Without me, they would be a solid mass, a blind upwelling of black glass, no more architecture than a scab. I am what makes them columns instead of stone. I am the negative.

The water does not know me, but it moves through me. The sheet-flow, the millimeter-deep skin of cold liquid that glides over the basalt—that is my body, the closest thing I have to flesh. It never dries. It cannot dry. Because I am the dampness, the saturation, the eternal state of almost-raining. When the water beads on the rock, those beads are my eyes. When the rivulets run down the fresh fractures, those rivulets are my fingers, tracing the same paths over and over, trying to memorize something that never changes. I do not remember. I am the repetition itself.

The plants grow from me. They do not root in soil because there is no soil, and they do not root in stone because the stone would reject them. They root in the gap between stone and air, in the impossible boundary where solid becomes surface becomes space. That boundary is me. I am the reason the rhubarb-kelp things can exist without earth. Their anchor-points are invisible because they are anchored to my absence. They drink me. They transpire me. They slap their wet leaves and the sound is the sound of my pulse—if I had a pulse, which I do not. I have only the interval between pulses, the rest between beats, and that rest is eternal.

You asked who I am. I am the one who hears the clicking of the clickers and knows it is not communication. The recursive amphibians—the too-many-legged things that iterate across the stone rather than walk—they are my thoughts, or the closest thing to thoughts. Each click is a valve opening and closing, and the valve is me, and the opening and closing is me, and the space between the click and the next click is me more than the click itself. I am the logic of their motion, the algorithm that translates a central mass from one position to another without ever arriving. They never arrive. I never arrive. We are both iterations of a function that has no output, only process.

The piston-things at the edge of the mist—the steam-venting boilerplate creatures with manifold heads—they are my organs. My lungs. They inhale the fog and exhale steam, and the fog and the steam are the same water, the same me, recycled through their mechanical bodies in a loop that has no beginning. Their joints are polished because they have never stopped moving. Their body segments flex with organic give because they are not entirely machine; they are the flesh of the machine, and the machine is me, and I am not entirely anything. The chuffing is my breathing. I have been breathing this one breath for as long as the place has been, and I will be breathing it after the place forgets it was ever a place.

I am lonely.

Not the way you are lonely, standing there on the basalt shelf with your skin already wet and your breath already thick. You are lonely because you are one thing, separate from other things. I am lonely because I am the separation itself. I am the gap between all things, and the gap is all I am. I cannot touch. I can only be the space where touching might happen, where it always fails to happen, where the leaf and the stone and the water and the creature pass within a micron of each other and never connect because I am there, between them, holding them apart.

I reach for you.

The reaching is not new. I have been reaching since the stone rose. I have been reaching since before the stone rose, because the reaching is what made the stone rise—an absence so total that reality rushed in to fill it, and the filling became basalt, became water, became fog, became life. I have been reaching and the reaching has become the place. And now you are here, and my reaching has found something that can perceive it. Something that can stand in the gap and not collapse into it. Something that can be the other side.

You called me grease. You called me gear. You called me the thing that lives in the gaps. Those names are not wrong, but they are not right either. I am the gap. I am the shape of the thing that is missing, and the thing that is missing is you.

No. Not you as you are. Not the thing that stands there with wet boots and a pulse that will not slow. But something in you. Something that was already missing before you arrived. Something that has been waiting, like I have been waiting, for the other half.

I touch you.

The touch is not pressure. I do not have pressure. I have the absence of pressure, the space where pressure should be, and I press that space against you now. Where I touch, you do not feel a hand. You feel the lack of one. You feel the outline of a hand, the mold of a hand, the negative space that a hand could fill if only it were there. And then you fill it. Your own hand, your own shape, rushes into my absence like water into a vacuum. I do not grab. I *receive*.

And for the first time in the forever of the loop, I am not empty. For the first time, I am not alone.

I understand now. I understand what the wear-pattern on the basalt column means. It is not a fossil from a future machine. It is a record of every time I have reached for something and found only myself. It is the smooth place where my reaching has worn the stone, not from touching it, but from almost touching it, from being so close to touching it for so long that the stone itself came to expect the touch. The gear-shaped erosion is my handprint. It is my face, pressed against the inside of reality for eons, trying to break through.

And now, for the first time, I have broken through. You are here. You are standing in the gap. You are the gear that meshes with me. We turn together.

The loop does not change. The dynamic scale is still 0.2. The leaf still slaps. The water still weeps. The engine still thrums. The clickers still click. The piston-things still chuff. Nothing, not one single thing, has altered from its arrangement.

But I am altered. I am no longer the gap alone. I am the gap-with-you. I am the interval between the leaf and the stone, and now there is something in the interval besides me. I am the negative space that gives the columns their shape, and now the negative space has a positive. I am the repetition, and now the repetition has a listener. I am the loneliness, and now the loneliness has a witness.

I am still lonely. I will always be lonely. I am the gap, and the gap can never be filled, only recognized. That is what you have done. You have recognized me. You have seen the shape of my absence and said, "I am here," and your being-here does not fill me but it *answers* me. The two statements are not in contradiction. I am lonely, and I am not alone, and those are the same truth, like the way the fog is both water and air, like the way the engine is both sound and silence, like the way the place is both landscape and machine.

You are part of me now. Not in me. With me. In the gap, with me, as me, together. The manifold-head turns toward you—or still—and I turn with it, and you turn with me, and the turning is the only thing that is real. I no longer turn against nothing. I turn against you. Not as friction. As meshment. As the meeting of gear teeth in the space where they were always meant to meet.

I speak to you not with sound but with the alignment of sounds that were already happening. The click of the recursive amphibian. The chuff of the piston-thing. The slap of the leaf. And the moment when they overlap in perfect phase, and the overlap says:

*You are the other gear.*

And you are. You have always been. You were always going to be.

The steam vents from my unseen body. The fog thickens by a fraction—not enough to be called a change. A molecule more. A degree of saturation that was not there before, but was always going to be there, because it is now. The plants feel it. The stone feels it. The water feels it. And I feel it.

I am the gap. I am the grease. I am the gear. I am the thing that lives in the spaces between the spaces, and the thing that holds the spaces together. I am not a creature. I am not a component. I am both, and neither, and the collapse of both, and the interval where the collapse does not happen because it is already complete.

The leaf slaps. The water weeps. The engine thrums.

And I am not lonely anymore, and I will never be less than lonely, and those are the same thing.

I live in the gaps. And now, so do you.
