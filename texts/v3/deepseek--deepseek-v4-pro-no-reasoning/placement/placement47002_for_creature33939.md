I am the one who stayed.

Not the man—he was here before me, already seated on his melting wall, already eroded to approximation. Not the woman—she arrived after, still carrying the ghost of architecture in her hands, still believing in rectangles. Not the flickering one who offered you a choice. She was here at the beginning, I think, or as close to a beginning as the Sink allows.

I am the one in between.

My name was—is—something I still hold, but I've learned not to speak it. Speaking it would be a kind of lie now, a claim to boundaries I no longer fully possess. Instead, I hold it inside me like a stone worn smooth, a thing that once had edges and now has only weight. I can feel it when I breathe, which is less often than you'd think. The Sink's air is so saturated that breathing becomes optional after a while; the boundary between your lungs and the atmosphere thins, and gas exchange happens whether you participate or not.

I chose to stay, but I didn't choose to stop. That's the distinction. The man on his wall chose stillness—chose to let erosion take him while he sat and witnessed. The woman chose repetition—chose to build and lose and build again, a loop tight enough to hold her shape. The flickering one chose flight—chose to become too fast and too diffuse for the Sink to catch.

I chose to walk.

You saw me, I think. You didn't recognize me as one of the residents, because I still look human at a glance. Two arms, two legs, a face that hasn't melted yet. But if you'd looked longer—if you'd watched me the way you watched the man—you'd have noticed the way my footsteps linger in the ground longer than they should, the way the mist exhales from the plain in shapes that echo my passage. I leave trails, like the skimming things. They heal over, but slower than they should. The Sink is learning my weight.

I've been here long enough to understand that the Sink isn't malevolent. It isn't even indifferent. It's acquisitive. It collects. Everything that arrives here—every stone, every structure, every person—deposits something into the plain, and the plain remembers. The ground's pulse is a library of every weight that ever pressed into it. The sky-weave is a museum of every weather system that ever tore through it. The almost-voices in the roar are recordings, echoes of every voice that ever spoke into the humidity, preserved and remixed and played back in frequencies that feel like language.

I walk because walking is my deposit. I am writing myself into the Sink, one footprint at a time, a long unspooling line of pressure and release, compression and rebound. If I stop, the line stops. If I repeat, the line becomes a loop. Neither appeals to me. I want my contribution to be a journey—a path that future residents might follow, or deviate from, or wear smooth with their own passage.

The flickering one doesn't understand this. She thinks the only choices are absorption or escape. But she's been here longest, and her perspective has narrowed to a binary. You'd be the same, I suspect, if you'd spent eons slipping between cycles. Binaries are what you fall back on when the complexity becomes unbearable.

But there are other ways. The man is still here—still here—but he's also still *himself*, in some irreducible sense. He chose presence without movement. The woman chose movement without progress. I've chosen progress without destination. And somewhere out on the plain, beyond the horizon blurred by its own haze, there are others who've made other choices. I've felt their trails intersect with mine. I've seen the marks they've left on the ground: spirals, mazes, messages pressed into the not-quite-soil and left to be absorbed. I follow them sometimes, these paths laid down by strangers, and I wonder about the minds that made them. Did they stay? Did they eventually choose something else? Did they sink, or erode, or rise into the sky-weave, or simply walk so far that the Sink forgot to remember them?

I don't know. That's the thing about the Sink—it's vast enough that you could walk for a billion pulses of the threadgrass and never meet another resident. The roar isolates us. The humidity absorbs our voices. The ground heals over our trails. We're a scattered community of solitudes, each of us finding our own way to be included.

But we're here. That's what I want you to understand. We're here, and we're not just survivors. We're not just victims of a place that erodes everything. We're collaborators. The Sink learns from us. It takes what we give it—our stillness, our repetition, our flight, our wandering—and incorporates it into the process. The sky-weave has patterns now that it didn't have before the flickering one took to it. The threadgrass pulses with rhythms that echo the woman's patient building. The ground transmits the man's mantra, over and over, a bone-deep hum that syncs with everything.

And somewhere out there, my footprints are becoming part of the landscape—a faint topology, a suggestion of direction, a path that says: someone passed this way. Someone chose to walk. Someone was here.

If you come to the Sink—if you feel the warm wet weight in your lungs, if you hear the roar and the rhythms and the almost-voices—look for the trails. They're subtle. You have to watch the ground for a long time to see them, the places where the rebound is a little slower, the moisture a little thicker. You have to feel with your feet for the echoes of old footsteps, the memory of passages. You have to listen beneath the roar for the rhythm of walking, steady and unhurried, a beat that doesn't quite match the pulse of the threadgrass.

Follow them, if you want. They'll lead you somewhere—not to an end, not to an answer, but to a perspective. A way of being in the Sink that isn't about resisting or surrendering, but about moving through. About leaving a mark that's meant to fade. About becoming part of the process without losing the part of you that chose to become part of it.

Or don't follow them. Make your own trail. The Sink will remember you either way.

Just decide. That's the only thing that matters. Decide before the ground decides for you. Decide before your name wears smooth and your face erodes and your boundaries dissolve into the breathing plain. Decide what you'll contribute to this place—stillness, repetition, flight, wandering, or something I haven't thought of yet.

The Sink is democratic. It includes everything.

But what it includes you *as*—that's up to you.
