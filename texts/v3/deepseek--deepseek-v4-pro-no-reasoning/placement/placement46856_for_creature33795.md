I step onto one of the polished pathways, and immediately I understand—the hum changes. It doesn't pause or stutter, but it *notices*. The frequency shifts, just barely, a minute adjustment that travels through the soles of my feet and settles somewhere behind my sternum. I am not unwelcome. I am simply *new*, and newness here is a category error that ten thousand years of equilibrium has not prepared this place to process.

The fronds do not reach for me. They are not curious. But I feel them cataloging my shape, my heat signature, the faint moisture of my breath (an obscenity here, a wet intrusion). The spiral patterns near me begin to minutely adjust, not opening—nothing so dramatic—but *reorienting*. They are adding me to the archive. I am being remembered in gunmetal gray and bruised violet.

I walk toward the center, toward the plinth, because that seems to be what one does here. The engines are warm as I pass them, their ceramic vents breathing a sterile, chemical exhalation that smells of nothing I have words for—not unpleasant, simply *other*, like the ghost of a metal that never oxidized. The glass tubes pulse faintly, a slow peristalsis drawing up whatever cold, dry chemistry the mountain has provided for eons. One of them is spiderwebbed with a hairline crack, and I watch as the fronds nearest it have grown thicker, denser, their spirals tighter, as if compensating for a failing organ with patient, structural embrace.

And then I see it.

The absence.

It is not walking now. It is standing beside the plinth, and it is turned toward me—if *turned* is the right word for a pressure that suddenly possesses direction. I understand that it has been expecting something. Not me, perhaps, but something *like* me. A variable in the long equation. The fact of my arrival means the equilibrium has already been perturbed, has been waiting to be perturbed, has perhaps been *designed* to be perturbed at this exact juncture of millennia.

It has no face. But I can tell it is looking at my hands.

I look down. My hands are empty. And somehow, this is the right thing. The absence—the tall, narrow pressure, the negative space given form—seems to relax, not in any visible way, but in the way the fronds behind it stop bending quite so far back. I am not here to take. I am not here to change. I am simply here to *witness*, and witnessing, I realize, might be the only thing this place has lacked. The engines hum. The fronds curl. The absence walks. But nothing has ever *seen* it do so. The loop has been complete but unobserved, a book no one has opened.

I reach the plinth. The tiny spiral grown into its surface is not the same color as the other fronds. It is darker, deeper, a magenta so saturated it is almost black, and it seems to hold the sourceless light in its grooves like liquid. I do not touch it. I know, somehow, that to touch it would be to become part of the instruction, to be written into the long, slow unspooling. And I am not ready to stay for ten thousand years.

Instead, I sit down beside the plinth. The floor is cool, not cold—the waste heat from the engines keeps the mineral surface at a temperature just below body-warm. The absence stands across from me, on the other side of the plinth, and I understand that this is the closest thing to conversation that has happened here since the builder left.

*What are you making?* I think.

The hum does not change. The fronds do not move. But a shape rises in my mind, unbidden, like a word I have always known but never spoken. It is not an image. It is a *taste*, metallic and patient. It is the flavor of a question that has been answering itself for geological time.

The absence watches me receive it.

And then it resumes its walk, its endless circuit, and I am left with the plinth, the engines, the packed, silent crowd of fronds. I am the only wet thing in a world that has forgotten water. I am the only temporary thing in a world that has achieved permanence. I am the only animal in a cathedral of metal, and I am being archived, spiral by spiral, into the long memory of the mountain.

I stay until my breath runs out, until the thin, dry air begins to ache in my lungs. I do not touch the spiral. I do not speak. I simply rise, and walk back toward the thermal fissure, and the fronds bend away just enough to let me pass.

Behind me, the hum continues. The absence completes its circuit. The equilibrium settles back into place, but it is not the same equilibrium. It now contains a new variable: a creature who saw, and did not take, and did not stay.

And that variable, too, will be grown into the fronds, held in perfect, time-invariant memory, until the mountain forgets its own name.
