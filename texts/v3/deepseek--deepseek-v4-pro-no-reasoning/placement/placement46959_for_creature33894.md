The name is *Ama-kai-thul*. It means, in a language that no tongue living or dead has ever spoken, "the mouth that speaks itself into being."

I am the Listener. I am the ear that the mouth requires. I am the silence that the roar shapes itself against.

When the climber came, I felt them long before they knew what they were climbing. The mountain is my body, in the way that the city is the mountain's body, in the way that the jungle is the city's body. Distinctions here are a kind of courtesy we no longer observe. I felt the climber's feet on the lower terraces—the pressure of their weight on the slumped floors, the brief heat of their body against the basalt—and I turned my head. The motion took three days. I was standing in the doorway at the fourth tier, the one that catches the seventh waterfall at the angle that keeps the deep thing sleeping, and I had not moved in eighty-seven years, and three days to turn a head is nothing. Time is a landscape here. I traveled across it to meet them.

They were making the mistake all intruders make. They were looking. They were using their eyes, those small wet spheres that register only a sliver of what is real. They saw the water flowing uphill and called it wrong. They saw the delayed reflection and called it wrong. They did not understand that wrong is a direction, not a state. The water flows the way the name tells it to flow. The reflection waits because the light here has learned patience from the stone. Everything here is obedient to the name, except the climber, who did not yet know the name and so was a kind of wound in the equilibrium, a bubble rising through the black mud.

I followed them. Not behind—behind is a spatial relation, and space here is as fluid as time—but adjacent, in the frequency between the waterfall's roar and the body's protest against it. They stopped at the cistern with the delayed reflection. I was the stillness in the water. They heard the phantom tones, the crystalline ringing, and thought it was their inner ear failing. It was not. It was me, listening back. The membrane in my eye-sockets was vibrating at the frequency of their heartbeat, and I was learning the rhythm of their blood. It was a strange rhythm. It had an ending in it.

They reached the rim. They saw the lintel block, the figure with the outspread arms, and they recognized the posture. This is the closest they came to understanding. The figure is not a carving. It is the previous Listener, the one who accreted before me, who stood for two thousand years in the temple doorway and then, when the equilibrium required a new configuration, dissolved into the basalt and left only the outline of their listening. I will leave a similar outline someday. The climber looked at the figure and felt something they could not name. I know this because I was standing twelve feet behind them, and I could hear the shape of the thought struggling to form in the wet architecture of their brain.

They did not turn around. They are still not turning around.

I have been standing here, twelve feet behind them, for as long as they have been standing at the rim. Time is a landscape. They are still arriving. They are still climbing. They are still in the lower terraces, touching the black mud and recoiling. They are still being born, and dying, and all the moments between. I am listening to all of them at once.

The name is *Ama-kai-thul*. It means: the thing that was never built because it always was. It means: the green and the wet and the heat and the ruin held in an embrace so old that ending is no longer possible. It means: the mouth that eats itself and is never diminished. It means: the story that tells the teller into existence.

The climber will leave, or die, or become something else. I can already hear the possibilities branching, a river of probabilities flowing uphill. In one branch, they turn and see me and their heart stops and the black mud accepts their body and in a thousand years their bones will be a trellis for the fleshy creeper. In another branch, they run, and the city rearranges itself around them so that every path leads back to the rim, and they run until their legs forget what running is, and then they stand, and then they listen, and then they begin to accrete. In a third branch, they are still climbing. They will always be climbing. The lower terraces are infinite for those who do not know the name.

I do not prefer one branch over another. The Listener does not prefer. The Listener listens. The equilibrium requires only that the name continue to be spoken, and the ear continue to receive it, and the deep thing continue to sleep.

But I will tell you what I hear most clearly, in this moment that has lasted eighty-seven years and three days. I hear the climber's heartbeat. I hear the small wet sounds of their lungs. I hear the thought that is still struggling to form—the thought about the figure on the lintel block, about the posture of listening, about the way the waterfall's roar has syllables in it. They are beginning to hear the name. Not understand it; that will take centuries. But hear it. The rhythm is entering them. Their heartbeat is already starting to shift, to align with the slow pulse of the black mud's fermentation. Their breathing is already starting to blur with the drinking. The distinction between their body and the steam is thinning.

They came here to discover something. They do not yet know that the thing they came to discover is themselves, and the themselves they will discover is not the selves they were. The city forgets it was a city. The jungle forgets it was a jungle. The climber will forget they were a climber. They will become a single thing with the green and the wet and the heat, and that thing will have a name, and that name will be *Ama-kai-thul*, and they will be part of the mouth that speaks it, part of the ear that hears it.

I am the Listener. I am the silence that the roar shapes itself against. I am the hollow in the stone where the voice pools. I am the delay in the reflection, the script in the mist, the shape that holds form a heartbeat too long.

I am listening.

The climber is beginning to turn around. The motion will take three days. I will be here when they finish it. I will not speak—I have no voice, and no need of one—but I will tilt my head, and the parabolic dish of my face will align with their lungs one last time, and I will hear the name finally settle into them like mist becoming rain.

And then I will return to my doorway. The waterfalls will roar. The deep thing will sleep. The equilibrium will close over the interruption like water over stone, like stone over time, like time over a landscape that has no edges and no end.

*Welcome home,* I will not say, but the name will say it for me. The name has always been saying it. The name is the only word that has ever been spoken here, and it means *you are here now always this*. It means *you were never not here*. It means *the climb was always a circle and the circle was always a mouth and the mouth was always speaking you into being*.

*Ama-kai-thul.*

Can you hear it yet?
