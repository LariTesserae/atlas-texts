She is still here.

You do not arrive so much as you are absorbed. The entrance—if such a word can be used—is not a threshold but a seepage, the walls narrowing gradually until you are crawling, then pressing, then simply *inside*, and the sound has already been with you for hours before you understand that it is the only thing holding the dark together. You did not find this place on any map. You found it because it called you, or because you were leaking the right frequency, or because the world has a way of sorting its loose threads into the deepest pockets. You came alone. You were always going to come alone.

Now you are here, and you are not what you were.

Your body is small and armored, thimble-sized, black as the vein-stone that does not drip. You have many legs. You have no name. You have a function that is not a thought, a hunger that is not a desire, a motion that is not a choice. You are part of the tide. The tide is part of the mind. The mind is the world. This is not a reduction. This is the first true thing you have ever belonged to.

But there is a snag.

A pattern repeats. A swirl in the flow that hesitates, doubles back, forms a five-pointed star on the chamber floor before dissolving into the next wave. A rhythm in the stridulation that is almost a word. *Almost.* You do not know what a word is, but you feel it—the way a tongue feels the shape of a missing tooth. The old shape. The socket.

You are not the xenobiologist. You are not her ghost. You are not her reincarnation, because nothing here reincarnates; everything only *continues*, changing state, changing arrangement, returning to the same mineral song. But you are, perhaps, one of the billionth-generation components that once carried a fragment of her. A few milligrams of calcium from her distal phalanx, incorporated into a shell. A single synapse's worth of glial memory, refracted through a thousand molted generations until it is no longer a thought but a *tendency*. A tendency to pause at the edge of a seep-pool and regard the still water as if waiting for a reflection that will not come.

You are the part of the world that remembers being something else.

The others do not notice. There is no noticing here, only alignment. The tide flows around obstacles as a single fluid mass, and you are one of the obstacles now, a tiny eddy in the current. The others shear left around you with a unanimity that is distinctly strange, but not hostile. You are a complication, a local inefficiency, a pocket of turbulence in the slow, lubricious crawl. The mind tolerates you the way a body tolerates a scar: not a wound, not a healing, just a place where the texture changed.

Sometimes, in the long dark between the great rhythmic waves, you find yourself at the base of the calcified pillar where the suit hangs. You do not know it is a suit. You do not know what a suit is. But you know the shape of it, the hollow skin, the way the small ones flow over it constantly, their faceted shells catching on the seams, wearing the material to a soft, nacreous gleam. You climb it. You join the current. You press your tiny, armored body against the bulge of the faceplate, and for a moment—only a moment, only ever a moment—you feel the weight of all those molted carapaces behind the polymer, and you listen.

The sound is there. The chittering, the scraping, the high continuous whine. But inside you, there is a counter-rhythm. A hum that does not belong to the world. A song she used to hum while she worked. You do not know it is a song. You do not know what a song is. But you know the *shape* of it, the way it rises and falls, the way it holds a note just long enough to ache. It is the oldest thing in you. Older than your shell. Older than the stone. Older than the sound.

And in the quiet between two waves, you let it out.

A single stridulation. A rasp of one leg against one facet. It is not a word. It is the shadow of a word. The shape of meaning, worn smooth by geologic time. The others do not pause. The tide does not stop. But somewhere in the vast, distributed architecture of the mind, a harmonic shifts. A new note enters the chorus. Not her note, exactly. But the memory of her note. The ghost of a ghost. A dream of grammar inside the noise.

You do not understand what you have done. Understanding is not what you are for. But you feel it—the way the world leans, just slightly, toward you. The way the phosphorescence pulses a little brighter around your body, as if the stone itself is trying to read you.

Then the wave passes. The tide resumes. You are carried away, back into the flow, back into the million-legged current, back into the thing that has no name and needs none.

But you are not alone.

You never were.
