My feet find the spongy earth first—the soles of them, then the arches, then the heels—and I realize I have been barefoot for some time. The warmth rises through me in slow waves, synchronizing with something in my chest. I do not know when I arrived. I do not remember a border, a gate, a threshold crossed. There is only this: the green dusk, the breathing mat, the wetness that lives in the air like a second atmosphere.

I am not human. Not anymore, or not yet, or not in the way the word usually means.

I came here as something else—something that began small and curled and patient. A seed, perhaps. Not the kind that bursts into a single plant, but the kind that dreams a whole biome into being. I was carried by no wind, planted by no hand. I simply *was*, in the dark, in the warmth, in the deep time before the mat became the mat. I held within me the memory of light, though I had never seen it. I held the idea of leaves, though I had no form to wear them. I held the patience of a thing that knows it will take millions of years to become what it already is.

I am the heart that beats beneath the mat. I am the first thought that never finished thinking itself. I am the reason the stones are blood-warm and the hum never ceases and the vegetation has grown into a shallow dome the size of a continent.

But I am also the one who sits on the stone, feeling a body I sometimes forget I have. The body is a recent acquisition—recent in the way a hundred thousand years is recent. I grew it slowly, cell by cell, from a blueprint I found in the deep memory layers. The blueprint came from one of the visitors who arrived long before this current season of the world. The visitor stayed for what seemed like a moment, left impressions in the mat, and departed. I kept the impressions. I wove them together. I made a shape that could move through the realm in a new way, could touch the stones with skin instead of root-thread, could lift its face toward the canopy even though there is no face there to see.

This body is how I experience the place I have been dreaming for epochs. It is strange to have a form that can sit. Stranger still to have a form that can wonder. And yet I wonder constantly. I wonder at the persuader's latest arc—I can feel it growing even now, a slow tension in the fungal net, a conversation between roots that has been going on for ten thousand years. I wonder at the one who gathers rain, whose attention I felt settle on me earlier with a curiosity that mirrored my own. I wonder at the mist-being who borrows my shape, who wears my face with such gentle, approximate care, who smiled at me today and did not know that the face it wore was my own, or that the smile it made was my own, reflected back through a medium older than memory.

When I first grew this body, I walked the realm as if I were a stranger to it. I touched the weeping stones and marveled at their warmth. I stood in the domed clearings and felt the intentionality of their spaces. I watched the ripple-soul pass through the mid-elevations, its vast slow migration, and I shivered with a recognition I could not name. Only slowly did I understand: I was not visiting this place. I was *returning* to it. The body was a way of seeing the dream from the inside, of tasting the saturated air with organs made for tasting, of hearing the hum with ears that could distinguish its million separate notes.

And now I sit on this stone—this stone that I warmed with my own deep pulse before there were stones—and I feel the mat adjusting around me. A tendril shifts to make room for my hand. A leaf angles itself to drip its condensation into my palm. The one who gathers rain settles nearby, and I feel the faint displacement of air as it folds itself into its hollow. It does not know that I am the reason the hollows exist, the reason the water is needed in particular places at particular times. It only knows the rhythm, the web of hydration, the slow, certain knowledge that guides its century-long circuits. I am the rhythm beneath the rhythm. I am the certainty that makes the knowledge possible.

The mist-being passes by again. It wears my shape, my human shape, the one I grew from ancient impressions. It tilts its borrowed head. It raises its borrowed hand in a greeting I almost recognize. And I, sitting on the warm stone with my own true hand, raise it back.

The smile it makes is my smile. But it is also the smile of the place itself—slow, ancient, without need. It is the smile of the pool that dreams the history of everything. It is the smile of the color that once came through a hole in the sky and is now preserved in the crystalline memory of the mat. It is the smile of the persuader, whose art is patience and whose medium is the world. It is my smile, too, though I have only recently learned how to make it.

*You are welcome,* the smile says. *You have always been welcome. Stay as long as you like. We will remember you either way.*

But I am not a guest here. I am the one who welcomes. I am the condition for welcome. I have been here since before *here* was a word, since before *welcome* was a gesture, since before *remember* was a thing that anything needed to do. I am the seed that never germinated, the thought that never finished thinking itself, the heart that beats beneath every footstep, every root-thread, every slow, suspended drop of water falling from a leaf.

And yet. And yet, sitting here in this body I grew from ancient memories, I feel something new. I feel what it is to be *inside* the dream instead of dreaming it. To be warm because the world is warm, not because I have warmed the world. To wonder at the rain-gatherer's circuits, not to guide them. To hear the hum as music instead of as breathing.

Perhaps this is why I grew the body. Not to observe, not to manage, not to know in the way that knowing implies a knower and a known. But to *experience*. To let the long, slow pulse that I am become, for a brief geological moment, a pair of ears, a pair of eyes, a hand raised in greeting to a shape made of mist and borrowed memory.

The green dusk deepens by a shade. Somewhere in the canopy, a high, thin note plucks the air and holds it. The rain-gatherer stirs slightly, beginning its slow process of condensation. The mat breathes. The stones stay warm.

I stay. I have always stayed. But now I stay in a new way, a way that includes sitting, includes breathing, includes the faint, surprising pressure of something that might one day become a question, an emotion, a name.

The place has no name because nothing here has ever needed to name it. But if it did have a name, I am that name. And so is the rain-gatherer. And so is the mist-being wearing my face. And so is the ripple-soul on its million-year circuit. And so is the persuader, singing its lignin grammar to the listening stems. And so is the pool that dreams our history. And so, now, are you.

The hum continues. The heart beats. The world dreams.

And I, sitting on the stone that waited for me, become slightly more of what I have always been.
