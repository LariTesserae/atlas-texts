### 2. **Clothing and Personal Gear**
The Underforge is cool (think 50-60°F/10-15°C) and humid, with constant moisture that chills you to the bone without freezing. Add insulating mid-layers like fleece for the cool air, and a waterproof outer shell (e.g., Gore-Tex jacket and pants) to repel drips and pools. Avoid cotton—it holds moisture and could lead to hypothermia. Pack extras; fabrics might get coated in fungal spores, which feel like velvety dust and could irritate skin.

- **Footwear and Protection:** Sturdy, waterproof boots with aggressive treads for slippery flagstones and shifting floors. Ankle support is crucial—terrain is uneven, with fresh-laid stones that grind and move. Knee pads, gloves (leather or reinforced for gripping rough stone), and a helmet with a chin strap to protect against falling debris or low ceilings. The density means you'll brush against walls constantly; long sleeves and pants prevent abrasions from chisel marks or splintered timber.

- **Sensory Protections:** Earplugs or noise-cancelling earmuffs for the loud clamor—hammer rings and timber groans can be deafening in echoes, potentially causing hearing damage or disorientation. A facemask or respirator to filter spore-laden air; the fungal "snow" isn't toxic (intuitively), but it could cause allergies or respiratory issues.

Pack light but modular—use a compact backpack that won't snag in tight spots. Total weight: aim for 20-30 lbs max, as you'll tire quickly in the warped gravity zones.
