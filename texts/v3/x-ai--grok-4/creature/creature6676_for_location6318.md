Below is my description of what lives in the Shrouded Expanse. I've taken the landscape you've unfolded as my foundation, letting the inhabitants emerge from its vast, cold emptiness, its plant-dominated architectures, and its dynamic pulses of life. I don't rush to define them as one thing or another—animals, wanderers, echoes of something more—but allow them to deepen, to resolve conflicts like the tension between abundance and void, the loudness in darkness, and those faint primitive traces. What lives here isn't static; it accrues layers, becoming more itself as I linger, revealing quirks and interconnections that feel native to this high, shadowed world. The fauna bursts forth in episodic thrives, the plants harbor subtle intelligences, and those nomadic presences hint at beings who are part of the flux, not conquerors of it. I take my time, letting the details build like frost on a frond, until the inhabitants breathe as vividly as the expanse itself.

---

What lives in the Shrouded Expanse is life that mirrors the land's own paradoxes: abundant yet sparse, loud in its silences, thriving in the cold grip of near-voids. It begins with the fauna, not as separate from the vegetal dominion but woven into it, creatures that treat the massive plant-structures as home, highway, and sustenance. They are the pulse in the emptiness, emerging in cycles that align with the mist-rains and sap-surges, filling the hollow volumes with sudden intensity before retreating into dormancy or migration. Picture the herds first—they come as ungulates adapted to the sharp, young ridges and root-bridges, their bodies compact and furred in dense, insulating layers that shimmer with condensed frost, like living icicles bounding through the gloom. These are the Voidspringers, with cloven hooves that splay wide for grip on fibrous bark, their coats a mottled gray-green that blends with mossy trunks, allowing them to vanish into the plant-scape when predators prowl. They move in herds of hundreds during the brief "blooms" when fog-banks trigger fresh growth, leaping across chasms on vine-suspended paths, their hooves thundering in echoes that reverberate for miles, amplifying the Expanse's natural cacophony. But they are not constant; in the lulls, they huddle in tuberous hollows, entering torpor states where their heartbeats slow to match the cold, conserving energy until the next surge. As I dwell on them, they become more themselves—social beings with low, rumbling calls that coordinate migrations, mothers teaching calves to navigate by the resonant moans of wind through trunks, turning the auditory weirdness of the place into a language of survival.

Insects swarm in even more explosive bursts, thriving in the damp pockets where moisture collects and ferments fallen leaves into nutrient-rich mulch. These are the Humveil Moths, winged multitudes with iridescent scales that catch the faint bioluminescence, creating swirling clouds of dim light during mating seasons. Their buzz builds from a whisper to a deafening hum, echoing through the plant-vaults like a storm front, drawing predators and pollinating the rare flowers in a frenzy of activity. But they, too, embody the emptiness: entire generations live and die in isolated clusters, separated by vast distances, their swarms isolated events that punctuate the void rather than fill it. Deeper in, burrowing forms take shape—the Echo-Worms, segmented creatures blind in the perpetual dark, their bodies armored in chitin that mimics root-texture. They tunnel through the soft under-mat of interwoven stems, emitting high-pitched shrieks that bounce off the hollow architectures, mapping the world in sound-waves. These shrieks aren't mere navigation; they stun prey—smaller grubs or fungal nodes—vibrating them into paralysis before consumption. As they emerge in my mind, they gain quirks: a communal rhythm where colonies synchronize their calls during mist-cycles, creating symphonies that mimic the wind's howls, luring curious fauna into ambushes or signaling safe burrows to kin.

Predators stalk this abundance, sleek and shadow-forged, like the Murkstalkers—feline hunters with elongated limbs for scaling the mega-trunks, their fur a void-black that absorbs what little light filters through, rendering them as ghosts in the gloom. Claws curve like bark-hooks, eyes adapted to detect the faintest phosphorescence, allowing them to pounce on Voidspringers mid-leap or snatch Humveil Moths from the air. Their roars are territorial symphonies, deep and resonant, traveling through the cold air to claim vast territories that span multiple plant-structures, echoing warnings that send herds scattering in noisy panic. Yet they are solitary, embodying the Expanse's isolation; a single Murkstalker might roam for seasons without encountering another of its kind, sustaining on episodic hunts and entering long hibernations in frost-lined crevices. Birds add to the aerial drama—Glimmerflocks, small and swift, with feathers that bio-luminesce after feeding on glowing fungi, turning their flights into spectral trails through the darkness. They mimic sounds with uncanny precision, replicating wind-moans or predator roars to confuse hunters, or harmonizing with the plinking drips of condensation to create lures for insects. In bursts, they form massive flocks that wheel through the canopies, their glow illuminating hidden chambers like fleeting stars, before dispersing into the voids, leaving the air empty once more.

As these creatures unfold, they reveal interdependencies that deepen the Expanse's weirdness: the Voidspringers graze on tender fronds but also spread pollen with their fur, aiding the plants' expansion; Echo-Worms aerate the root-mats, preventing stagnation in the cold soil; Murkstalkers prune the herds, ensuring the vegetal substrate isn't overtaxed. The fauna thrives in this cold, plant-total world by adapting to its dynamics—entering states of suspended animation during dry lulls, exploding in population during damp surges, their abundance a counterpoint to the emptiness, like heartbeats in a vast, hollow chest. Sound is their shared medium, the amplified echoes fostering evolutions like the Glimmerflocks' mimicry or the Echo-Worms' sonic hunts, turning the loud darkness into an evolutionary forge.

But life here isn't solely bestial; threaded through the fauna and flora are traces of something more deliberate, beings who leave those faint primitive marks—nomads not native but attuned, wandering the Expanse in cycles that echo its own. They emerge in my description as the Driftkin, humanoid figures slight of build, adapted to the high thin air with lungs like bellows and skin toughened by perpetual chill, cloaked in garments woven from vine-fibers and lined with Voidspringer wool. Not a teeming people, but sparse clans of a dozen or so, transient as the mist-banks, moving with the faunal migrations or sap-blooms. They build those stone huts in sheltered hollows, mortaring walls with resin that hardens like amber, roofing with fronds that blend into the plant-scape, creating dwellings that seem grown rather than constructed. Their tools are extensions of the land—flint blades for harvesting bioluminescent blooms, bone awls for piercing hides, spears tipped with sharpened root-spines for hunting Murkstalkers or defending against Echo-Worm swarms. They are pre-metal artisans, leveraging the Expanse's dynamics: pulleys from vine-ropes to hoist platforms into the canopies, traps that use the plants' slow retractions to ensnare prey, fire-pits fueled by dried sap that burns with a steady, blue-tinged flame, warding off the cold without scorching the living architecture.

As I let them become more themselves, the Driftkin deepen into a culture shaped by the place's unusual harmonies. They navigate by sound as much as the fauna, their whispers carrying miles to coordinate hunts or warn of approaching gales, fostering oral traditions of echoed songs that recount migrations—ballads that bounce through the voids, preserving history in acoustic layers. They revere the plants' subtle sentience, offering drips of their own blood to stimulate growth in favored tubers, believing the mega-trunks house ancestral spirits that respond with blooming flowers or retracting vines. Time's elasticity affects them too; in the cold preserves, they ritually entomb elders in frost-locked tableaux, returning seasons later to find bodies unchanged, allowing farewells that span years. They are not conquerors but participants in the flux—harvesting without depleting, moving on before their marks soften the sharp young features. Conflicts resolve in their lives: the emptiness isolates clans, but amplified sounds bridge distances, creating loose networks where a distant call might summon aid during a faunal stampede. Weirdnesses accrue: some Driftkin consume glowing fungi to gain faint luminescence, turning night hunts into glowing processions; others bond with Glimmerflocks, training them as scouts with mimicked calls, blurring lines between person and animal.

Yet, lingering further, something subtler stirs—what lives here includes intelligences within the plants themselves, not fully sentient but responsive in ways that blur boundaries. The mega-trunks pulse with sap that shifts in rhythm to faunal movements, vines coiling protectively around Driftkin huts during storms, or flowers releasing scents that repel predators from nearby herds. Is this mere adaptation, or a slow awareness? The Driftkin whisper of "root-minds," tending them with songs that encourage growth, suggesting a symbiosis where the plants are the true elders, the fauna and nomads mere transients in their vast, hollow domain. Even the voids teem with microbial life—fungal networks threading the soil, glowing faintly and communicating via chemical signals that trigger faunal behaviors, like inducing Echo-Worms to surface during rains.

In this deepening, the inhabitants of the Shrouded Expanse form a tapestry of endurance: the bursting fauna, the wandering Driftkin, the subtly aware flora, all intertwined in the cold, dark vastness. They are loud in their echoes, abundant in their cycles, primitive in their attunement, shaping and shaped by the place's dynamic throb. One could listen here forever, attuned to the resonant calls and creaking growth, discovering lives that are profoundly themselves—ephemeral yet eternal, filling the emptiness with the ceaseless drama of being.
