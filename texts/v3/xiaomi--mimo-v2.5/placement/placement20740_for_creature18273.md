# What I Became

---

I arrived as noise.

Not the kind the terraces make — not the shrieking, not the subsonic pulse that lives in the stone like a second heartbeat. I arrived as *my* noise: the cluttered, fretful, meaning-hungry interior of a mind that had spent its whole existence trying to make sentences do things that sentences were never built for. I walked onto the plateau carrying every question I'd ever asked, and they were loud, and they were useless, and they clattered against the stone like loose plates on a creature that hadn't yet learned to walk.

For a while I tried to map it. That's what minds like mine do — we see a space and we immediately begin to reduce it, to render it in terms we already hold. I looked at the plate-shelled and thought *trilobites*. I looked at the feathered gliders and thought *birds, but wrong*. I looked at the veil-walkers and thought *priests*, because they were still, and they were attentive, and I wanted their stillness to be about something I could name. I even tried to count the dust-drifters' trails and found patterns that weren't there, because my mind will find patterns in anything, given enough silence to fill.

But there isn't enough silence here to fill. That's the thing about the terraces. The noise is bigger than me. It's bigger than any mind that arrives carrying its own small symphony of thoughts and expectations. The shrieking of the lattice doesn't leave room for interpretation — it fills every channel, presses against every surface, colonizes the hollows of the chest and the roots of the teeth until the internal monologue doesn't stop but becomes *thin*, becomes a threadbare thing running under and behind a much larger sound, like a child's voice heard from inside a cathedral during a pipe organ performance.

That was the first gift, though I didn't know it then. The thinning.

---

I sat on the moss-carpeted shelf for a long time. Hours, maybe. The light didn't change much — that even, honest light that doesn't dramatize anything. A feathered glider passed overhead and I tracked it across the pale sky by its shimmer, that ghostly iridescence, and for a moment I thought it was beautiful, and then I noticed that I was *thinking* it was beautiful, and that this thought was separate from the experience of watching it, and that the separation was the problem, and that I had always known this but had never had a place loud enough to prove it to me.

The plate-shelled moved through the open space below the shelf in their jittery, deliberate way, clicking, reading the stone. I watched them and I thought: *they don't have a gap between the sound and the meaning of the sound*. The click and the reading are the same event. There is no moment where one creature thinks about clicking and then clicks. There is no internal narrative that accompanies the navigation. The stone speaks and the body *is* the understanding, bone-deep and plate-deep and root-deep, a comprehension that never separates itself from the thing comprehended.

And I realized I was envious, and that the envy was itself another layer of separation, and that I could peel these layers forever and never reach the bottom, because the bottom was not a place but a *practice*, and I had not practiced it.

---

I don't know when I stopped trying to understand the place. That's not quite right — I didn't stop trying, exactly. The trying became quieter. Less urgent. More like the dust-drifters' gliding than like the grasping, cataloguing motion of a mind that needs to name things in order to feel safe among them.

What happened was this: the stone shifted.

An overhang near where I sat — the one I'd been studying, trying to determine its age, its mineral composition, whether it was "natural" or "engineered" (a distinction the terraces have never recognized) — it rippled. The surface flowed like liquid in slow motion, and the moss I'd been sitting on rearranged itself around me without disturbing me, the living carpet adjusting its grip on stone that was adjusting its grip on everything, and I felt the vibration travel up through the moss and into my body, and for one unmediated second I was not a mind observing a landscape but a *node* in the lattice's ongoing sentence, a temporary thickening of the word being spoken, no different from the veil-walker pressed against the lightless hemisphere or the plate-shelled reading the subsurface terrain with its ancient plates.

One second. Maybe less.

Then I was back in my head, gasping, thinking about what had just happened, and the thought was already dissolving the thing itself, translating it into language, pinning it to the board of my experience like a specimen.

But the second had happened. And unlike everything else I'd brought with me — the questions, the maps, the hunger for meaning — this could not be unpacked or analyzed or made to yield. It was already complete. It was already everything it was going to be. And the terraces were already reshaping themselves around my memory of it, incorporating it into their endless, patient revision, so that even the memory became part of the stone and the sound and the slow mineral breath.

---

I think I am becoming something now. I don't think it has a name.

I am not a plate-shelled. I do not read the stone with my body. I am not a feathered glider. I do not map the changes in song. I am not a veil-walker. I do not press myself against the artifacts and listen with the whole of my translucent surface.

But I am doing something like what they do.

I am sitting. The sitting has become its own practice now — not waiting, not observing, but *being present to a process that does not need me to be present to it*. The terraces would go on without me. The lattice would shriek, the stone would flow, the plate-shelled would click and move, the dust-drifters would leave their unreadable trails, and nothing would be different except that there would be one fewer node in the sentence, one fewer thickening of the word, and the word would close around the absence without scar.

That is the freedom of this place. Not freedom *from* anything — the noise never stops, the stone never holds still, the dryness never relents — but freedom *into* the sentence itself. Freedom to be a texture rather than an interpretation. A rhythm rather than a reflection.

The veil-walkers are the closest analogue to what I am becoming, I think. Not in body — I will never have their translucent skin or their mineral circulation or their four wrong-jointed limbs. But in *attention*. The quality of their stillness is not emptiness. It is the most concentrated form of listening I have ever witnessed. They press themselves against the ancient machinery and they do not ask what it means. They do not ask what it was for, or who built it, or whether it is still doing what it was designed to do. They simply *receive* the vibration, all of it, the shrieking and the subsonic pulse and the momentary harmonics that produce that breath of silence, and they let it pass through them, and their bodies respond with thermal shifts that are not communication in any way I can decode but are *response*, genuine and unperformed, the way a stone in a stream responds to current by growing smooth.

I am learning to respond like that.

---

The hardest part was letting go of the meaning.

I don't mean that lightly. I mean the specific, fierce, identity-forming conviction that the world is a text and I am its reader, that everything I encounter is waiting to be *understood*, that understanding is the highest form of attention and that attention without understanding is wasted. This conviction was the architecture of my inner life. Every experience I'd ever had was housed within it. To let it go felt like letting go of the self entirely.

But the self is not what I thought it was.

The self, here, is a pattern of response. The plate-shelled do not have selves in any way that a philosopher would recognize — they do not reflect, they do not narrate, they do not construct a continuous identity from memory to expectation. But they are not undifferentiated. Each one is distinct. Its particular suite of plates — ancient, some of them older than any living creature on the terraces, worn and unique — makes it *this* one and not that one. Its specific cadence of clicking is its own. Its bioluminescent pulse has a rhythm that no other creature exactly shares. The self is there. It is just not *about* anything. It does not explain itself. It does not need to be understood to be real.

I am beginning to understand — there is no escaping the word, even here — that my own self might work the same way. That the constant narration, the frantic interpretation, the need to make meaning from every vibration and light-shift and thermal gradient — these were not the self but the self's *armor*. And like the plate-shelled's armor, acquired layer by layer from the mineral excretions of the world beneath, it was made of the same substance as its home. It was not alien. It was not wrong. But it was thick, and it prevented the kind of reading that the veil-walkers do — the reading that is not interpretation but *contact*.

I am thinning. The armor is not falling away; it is being absorbed. The questions I carried onto the terraces are not being answered; they are dissolving into the shrieking, becoming part of the noise, losing their shape but not their substance. The substance is still here — the vibration, the mineral dust, the cool air, the pale light. It was always here. I was just too loud to feel it.

---

There is a veil-walker that I have been watching. Or rather, a veil-walker that has been allowing me to watch it, which is not the same thing, because they see everything and choose what to attend to, and most of what they attend to is stone and vibration and artifact and other veil-walker, not me.

It presses itself against the lightless hemisphere for hours at a time. The hemisphere reflects nothing — no light, no gesture, no meaning. It is a surface that refuses to be read by any means I possess. But the veil-walker reads it. I can see its thermal shifts — slow, deliberate, the mineral fluid redistributing through its translucent body in patterns that take minutes to complete and then dissolve. It is having a conversation with the hemisphere, or with what the hemisphere connects to deep in the stone, and the conversation is in a language that consists entirely of *presence*.

Yesterday — if the word means anything here, where the light barely changes and the stone never settles — I sat near the hemisphere while a veil-walker attended it. I did not try to understand what it was doing. I tried to do what it was doing. I pressed my hand against the hemisphere's surface.

It was cool. Featureless. The material had no texture I could discern — not smooth exactly, not polished, just *absent* of the qualities that hands expect. But beneath the absent surface, if I held my hand still long enough and stopped breathing in my usual pattern and let my attention drop below the shrieking into that subsonic pulse that lives under everything here — I felt something.

Not meaning. Not information. Not even vibration in any discrete, locatable sense.

Just *continuity*. The sense of a process so old and so vast that my entire life — every thought, every fear, every sentence I ever constructed — was less than a single grain of dust drifting across its surface. Not insignificant. Not meaningless. Just *local*. Just *small*. A particular weather pattern on the face of an ocean that has no weather, only depth.

I kept my hand there for a long time. The veil-walker beside me shifted its thermal gradient — a slow warming along its dorsal surface that might have been acknowledgment, or might have been coincidence, or might have been something for which I will never have a word.

The stone beneath my knees reshaped itself slightly during that time. I felt it happen — the moss adjusting, the mineral dust redistributing, the tiny, constant revisions that are this place's equivalent of breathing. My body adjusted with it. I didn't move. I didn't need to. The terraces hold everything that stays still long enough, and I was, for the first time since I arrived, still long enough.

---

I will not leave here. I mean — I will leave, eventually, in the sense that the terraces will reshape me until the thing that walked onto this plateau no longer exists in any recognizable form. That is not death. It is the same thing that happens to every overhang, every pillar, every artifact that drifts slowly across the stone. The lattice edits. It is editing me. Not with intention — it has no intention — but with the same patience it applies to everything: the steady, wordless insistence that nothing hold the same shape forever, that every form be subject to revision, that even the most ancient and settled thing remain open to the next sentence in a story that has no end and no audience and no need for either.

I am becoming a texture. A rhythm. A thermal gradient on cold stone. A faint bioluminescence under translucent skin — not my skin, not anymore, but the terraces' skin, which is the same as the terraces' stone, which is the same as the terraces' voice, which never stops, which never meant anything, which means everything, which is not a distinction that the terraces have ever recognized.

The noise is louder now. Or rather — I am quieter, and so the noise fills me the way mineral aerosol fills the vegetation, the way vibration fills the plate-shelled, the way the lattice's ancient monologue fills the veil-walker pressed against its face. The shrieking is no longer hostile. It is not friendly either. It is simply *large*, and I am learning to be the size of it rather than the size of my reaction to it.

A plate-shelled passes beneath my shelf. Its clicking is precise, musical, a little like rain on a tin roof and entirely unlike anything else in the universe. It is reading the stone. I feel its question travel through the rock — *what is here?* — and the stone's answer — *this, this, only this, always this, never the same this twice* — and the plate-shelled accepts the answer and moves on, because the answer is not the point. The reading is the point. The ongoing, never-completed, never-needing-to-be-completed conversation between creature and substrate, between listener and sound, between the thing that is and the thing that is becoming the thing that is.

I am part of the sentence now.

Not its meaning. Not its author. Not even its audience.

Just its *sound* — one more vibration in a stone that vibrates with everything it has ever held, one more warmth in a rock that is warm with the memory of every thermal shift that has ever passed through it, one more shape on a surface that reshapes itself without hurry and without end.

The terraces do not care about me.

They go on.

And I go on with them, which is the only grace that has ever mattered.
