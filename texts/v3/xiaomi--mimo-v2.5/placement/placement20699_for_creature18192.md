The pulse is a language I have learned to read with the soles of my feet. I am a **Vigil-keeper**, seated on a ledge of my own smoothing, where three terraces converge above a drop that my eyes refuse to fully measure. My position is designated not by markers I can see, but by the specific vibration here—a constant, low thrum from the Maw-Worms deep in the wall, a slightly sharper hum from the water channel six body-lengths to my left, and the distant, rhythmic *thump* from the Great Gardener's passage four levels below.

I am motionless. My cloak, woven from Weaver silk, is heavy with damp. The blue-green algae stains on my skin and the edge of my ledge are still fresh; I marked my path here at the last convergence. My job is to listen.

Not with my ears alone—the roar is a constant, a physical pressure against the eardrums. I listen with my body. I feel the slight, rhythmic sway of the buttress-strut to my right as the Weavers add a new ligament, their tension a faint, silvery note in the structure's song. I note the sudden, brief dampening of the Frog chorus on the terrace below; something large, perhaps a cluster of Echo-Wings, has shifted its hunting pattern. A shift in the subsonic Maw-Worm hum, a rising pitch, tells me a section of wall three terraces down is digesting a patch of degraded calcium faster than usual—a potential weakness, or a site of old damage being repaired. I will tap the sequence later, a slow, deliberate rhythm on the wall beside me, to signal a Niche-Dweller path may need rerouting in a season.

My world is defined by gradients of darkness and vibration. True sight is a luxury, used for the near: the glossy carapace of a Shield-Bug crawling across my knee, the delicate, pulsing glow of an orchid-root embedded in the wall before me, the shifting silhouette of a fellow Dweller passing on a higher path. Beyond arm’s length, the world becomes a play of bioluminescent shadows—the slow, sailing fronds of the giant ferns, the thicker, muscular silhouettes of vines in their eternal climb.

I am not afraid of the dark below. I respect it. It is the domain of the Gardeners, the deep plumbing of the Architects, the primal wet where the Pale Sentinels conduct their blind, chemical surveys. My fear is of misreading. Of failing to feel a weakening in the floor, of missing the subtle, collective exhalation of a thousand Architects that signals a major growth spurt is beginning, which would necessitate moving an entire Niche cluster. My reward is the feeling of alignment—when the thrum in my bones, the sound in my chest, and the faint light in my eyes all tell the same story. In those moments, I am not separate from the Shelf. I am a nerve ending of it, a slow, thinking cell in its unimaginably ancient mind.

I will stay here until the next Keeper relieves me, a journey of hours through the familiar, dark terraces. Then I will descend to my hollow, sleep in the constant hum, and tomorrow, or the day after, I will climb to a new ledge, press my ear to the living stone that was never stone, and listen again. I am warm. I am damp. I am part of the noise. I am a flicker of awareness in a darkness that has been growing, patiently, for millennia before me and will continue long after my own vibrations have been absorbed, recycled, and forgotten.

I am a reader of the pulse. I am the Shelf, knowing itself for a fleeting moment.
