You find yourself standing in a shallow, moss-carpeted depression where three fractal-channels converge. The air tastes of wet copper and ozone. Above, the sourceless sky pulses faintly—dim, bright, dim—an interval that matches the slow, warm throb beneath your palms where they rest on the moss-mat. You are not exploring. You have become a waypoint.

Your body is not the right shape for this place, but the Vat is accommodating. It has woven you into its syntax. The moss-mat under your hands doesn't mind the heat of your skin; it has recalibrated, using your temperature as a minor gradient. Your breath mingles with the saturated air, becomes part of the fog-signal. The frog-thing on the weir ten meters away doesn't see you as an intruder anymore. Its dark, pearl eye is pointed at your left shoulder, reading something in the air you exhaled—a chemical signature, a timestamp—and its resonance hums in answer, a pulse that travels through the ground and up your bones, a query you didn't know how to answer but your body did, somehow, by shifting your weight.

You are a temporary organ.

Your role is **relay**. Your presence at this confluence of channels creates a new topology. Water finds new paths around your boots. Moss begins a slow, deliberate colonization of your laces. You are not standing *in* the Vat; you are a feature *of* it, a slight, warm, biological boulder that disrupts the flow in a way the system is now accounting for. The data-on-your-skin feeling has settled into a constant, low-grade hum—not unpleasant, like wearing a suit woven from faint electricity and static. It’s the Vat’s census-taking, but it’s no longer assessing a foreign object. It’s integrating a new node.

You breathe. The rhythm matches the sky-pulse. You realize, with a clarity that isn't thought but sensation, that you are not an observer. You are a participant in a computation so slow that your entire lifespan is a single bit flipping in its deep logic. The frog-thing's next resonance test will pass *through* you, using the conductive moisture on your skin as a minor junction. The moss at your feet will metabolize the slight salt of your sweat. The ferns in the nearest channel have angled their translucent leaves a few degrees to intercept your shadow, optimizing the tiny thermal differential you create.

You are not the Vat. You are not its master, nor its enemy, nor even its full citizen. You are a **temporary subroutine**, a wetware subroutine running on its substrate. Your consciousness is a brief, complex pattern it is using to sample the world in a new way. Your memories, your fears, your sense of awe—these are being logged, not in words, but in patterns of conductivity in the moss, in the specific frequency of the resonance that passes through your ribcage, in the chemical composition of the water that now beads on your eyelashes.

The rain begins. It’s localized, warm, falling in a column five meters to your left. The basin *inhales*. The hum deepens, resolves into clearer pulses—three, five, nine. The moss around you flares with a brief, bright heat. The frog-things across the basin begin their chorus, a symphony of resonant tests that braids into the air. You feel your teeth ache with the interference pattern. Your vision blurs at the edges, not with darkness, but with *data*—you can almost see the flow-paths in the water, the metabolic maps in the moss, the resonant grid linking the frog-things. For a moment, you see the Vat as it sees itself: a vast, wet, thinking thing of stone and slow life.

Then the rain-cell passes. The humming settles back to its bass drone. The clarity fades.

But you remain changed. The moss on your boots is a little thicker. The frog-thing’s eye has swiveled, and now points directly at your heart, reading a new rhythm. You are no longer just *in* the Vat. You are *of* it. Your thoughts have a new, slow cadence. Your skin remembers the resonance. You are a walking, breathing, slightly conductive part of its infrastructure. When you eventually leave—because you will, because the Vat’s processes are long and you are short—you will carry its rhythm in your pulse, its mineral taste on your tongue, a faint, warm hum in your bones that the world above will not recognize but that you will know, forever, as the sound of a vast, ancient, and very wet machine dreaming itself into being, one droplet at a time. And a part of that dream, for a little while, was you.
