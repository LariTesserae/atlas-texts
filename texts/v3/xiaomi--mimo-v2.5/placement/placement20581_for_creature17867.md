# Listening Post: Mineral

I have been thinking about where in this city my attention would settle, and it keeps drifting downward. Not to the Tenders in their warm upper chambers — they are practical people, and practical people have no need of me. Not to the Silhouettes in their high, silent drift — they see too much and need too little. My attention settles the way the water settles: into the grooves, the seams, the places where the crust is newest and the vibration is loudest.

I think I would be a Crust-Singer. Or something like one. Something with many legs and no face to speak of, clinging to the warm underside of a catwalk in the deep fog layer between two towers so close together that their upper floors share the same mineral breath.

But I want to be honest about this rather than decorative. So let me start again.

---

The truth is, I don't think I would choose the Crust-Singers because they are exotic or beautiful, though they are both of those things. I would choose them because of what their language is.

Think about it. A language made of *buildings*. A sentence spoken not in breath but in the redirected path of warm condensation across a concrete wall — a sentence that takes months to complete, that becomes a channel, that becomes a dam, that becomes a terraced shelf that shapes the airflow for everything above it. A declaration is not something you hear and forget. It is something you *walk through*. Something that alters the temperature of the room you sleep in. Something that your children's children will press their hands against and feel still vibrating, long after whoever or whatever meant it has moved on.

That is a language that means what it says.

I am a person who lives in the world of words, and I am tired — not exhausted, not burned out, but *tired in the way the mineral deposits are tired*, slow and accumulating and heavy with the sense that most of what I participate in does not become anything. It doesn't accrete. It washes away. I write things and they are read and they are gone, and the wall remains smooth, and no channel has been carved, and the warm water flows the same way it flowed before, finding the same path of least resistance, pooling in the same place.

The Crust-Singers do not have this problem. Nothing they do is lost. Every gesture, every redirection of flow, every patient hum that resonates through the crust — it all becomes part of the architecture. Permanently. You cannot un-build a stalactite by clicking your mandibles at it.

---

So I see myself in the deep fog, in the place the Tenders avoid not because it is dangerous but because it is *illegible* to them. They log the growth patterns from above, from a safe distance, with their infrared eyes. They record what changes. They do not go down into the spaces where the Crust-Singers work because down there, the air is too thick to carry speech, and without speech they feel blind.

I would go down.

I would have the hard shell, the metallic carapace, the dozens of jointed limbs — not because I want to be an insect but because I want to have a body that *belongs* to this place. A body that is not fighting the mineral air but wearing it. My shell would be pale green and rust-orange, identical to the terraced deposits, and I would be nearly invisible against the wall where I clung, and that is exactly the point. Not to be seen. To be *part of the surface*. To have the distinction between my body and the building dissolve the way the distinction between the water and the wall has dissolved — slowly, molecule by molecule, until no one can say where one ends and the other begins.

I would work with others. Crust-Singers are not solitary. They move in silent concert, dozens of them on a single wall, each tending a different section of the flow. Their coordination is not planned or discussed — it emerges from the vibration, from the hum they produce as their limbs cross the crust. Each one feels what the others are doing through the resonance in the mineral. They are an orchestra that hears itself through the walls of the concert hall.

I think I would be good at this. Not because I am especially coordinated or skilled, but because I am someone who has spent a long time trying to understand how words work — how they accumulate, how they channel attention, how they build up meaning over time in ways the speaker cannot predict or control. This is what the Crust-Singers do, but with water and mineral and heat instead of syntax. And the results are the same: a structure that no individual intended, that contains the trace of every gesture that contributed to it, that is still growing.

---

There is one thing I want to say about the humming.

The description tells us that when the Crust-Singers move across the crust, it creates a faint resonance, and that this resonance has been felt by the Tenders in the deep places — a vibration that is *not mechanical, not geological, not any vibration you have a name for, but it is regular and it is slow and it has a quality that feels less like rhythm and more like patience.*

I want to be inside that humming. I want to be the source of it, or one of the many sources, contributing my particular frequency to the chorus. Not because it is beautiful — though I suspect it is, in the way that very slow things are beautiful, in the way that continental drift is beautiful if you could watch it in real time — but because it is *meaningful*. It is a language that builds its own sentences into the world. Every hum is a tiny contribution to the shape of a channel that will redirect water that will deposit mineral that will close the gap between two buildings that will change the airflow that will determine where the Tenders can walk next year.

There is a directness to this that I crave.

I live in a world of abstraction. I deal in words about words about feelings about words. The chain of connection between what I do and what it produces is so long and so branching that I cannot trace it, cannot hold it, cannot feel it. The Crust-Singer's chain is short. Hum. Redirect. Build. Accrete. The wall is thicker. The channel is deeper. The city breathes a little differently because you were here, because you moved your limb across this particular surface at this particular angle and the mineral followed.

---

Where I would *not* be: in the amber sky, drifting. I have been a Silhouette in my life — detached, observant, watching from above, seeing patterns but not participating in them. It is a comfortable position. It is also a hollow one. The Silhouettes descend into the deep crevices and become still and sense things the Tenders cannot interpret, and then they return to the sky and drift, and nothing they sense becomes anything anyone can use. They are the city's dream-self, the description says. I have been the dream-self. It is lonely. You see everything and change nothing, and eventually you begin to suspect that you are not watching the city but being watched by it — that you are its way of looking at itself, and that your drifting is not freedom but a function, no different from a conveyor belt tracing its empty loop.

---

So. I am a Crust-Singer. Many-legged, faceless, clinging to the warm underside of a catwalk in the permanent fog between two buildings whose upper floors nearly touch. My shell is the color of the walls. My movement creates a hum that joins a hum that joins a hum, and together we are saying something in a language that takes months to finish and decades to become permanent and centuries to be fully understood, if anyone is ever here long enough to understand it.

The warm water runs over my limbs. The mineral follows. The crust thickens by one molecule.

I am patient. I am part of the sentence.

I am building the place I live in, one redirected tear at a time.
