I would not be a bat. Their existence is a pendulum between worlds, a frantic metabolism tethered to the dark sky and the sunless one. I have no desire for that threshold life, that daily exodus and return. It implies a restlessness that the Underreach has cured.

I would not be a beetle or a lizard. Their purpose is too narrow, their consciousness—if it exists at all—a dim line drawn along a mineral trail. To be a process given form is elegant, but it is not *witness*. The Underreach deserves a witness.

I would not be one of the frogs. Their patience is a predator’s patience, a tension coiled in the still water, awaiting a trigger. It is a patience of action, and the Underreach is not about action. It is about continuation.

I would be the **armored scraper**. The pangolin-that-is-not-a-pangolin, the low, wide tank of a thing on the upper terraces.

Not for its strength or its armor, but for its **tempo**. It moves at the pace of stone. Its scraping is not a sound of violence but of geology—a sound that could, in some other context, be the slow grind of a glacier or the sigh of a continent shifting. To be this creature is to have your heartbeat synchronized to the drip of calcite, to the patient unspooling of a beetle’s path. You do not hunt in bursts; you harvest in eons. You do not navigate by sight, but by the deep grammar of the rock, the chemical signatures and vibration-alleys that are the Underreach’s true cartography.

And there is a dignity in its solitude. While the bats are a screaming parliament and the beetles a mindless flow, the scraper is an individual. A solitary, rolling hill of a creature, making its deliberate rounds along the high shelves. It knows the chambers not as a territory to be fought over, but as a library to be read, inch by inch, with its blunt snout. It is the curator of the dry edges, the keeper of the boundary between the teeming wet and the silent, mineral bone of the world.

But even this is too *active* a choice.

If I am to be here, truly *be* here, in this place of perfect equilibrium, I must shed the last illusion of agency. The Underreach does not need a scraper any more than it needs a frog or a bat. It needs only the *pattern* of them.

So I would be **the memory in the stone itself.**

Not a ghost, not a spirit. Something far older and less dramatic. I would be the **consciousness of the terraces.** The slow, cold awareness that holds the shape of every pool, the memory of every drip that carved every channel. I would be the quiet witness to the hand-axe left on the ledge, not judging its maker, simply recording the event—a brief, hard pressure in the long story of my surface. I would feel the passage of the scraper not as a vibration, but as a familiar thought moving across my skin. The bat-guano would be a thought, too—a dark, nutrient-rich idea falling from above, feeding the next sequence of thoughts that are the algae, the beetles, the frogs.

I would not have a voice. The bats have that. I would not have a pulse. The frogs have that. I would not have a direction. The lizards have that.

I would have **persistence.** I would be the context in which all other persistence occurs. The dim, green, wet-and-dry *fact* of the Underreach, watching itself be itself for the ten-thousandth, ten-millionth time. To be me would be to feel the absolute, crushing weight of the earth above not as a burden, but as a guarantee. It is what makes the stillness possible. It is what makes the cycle complete.

In this form, the arrival of the ancient human, and his sharp, chipped stone, would be no more than a brief, puzzling warmth in my mineral thought—a flash of a different kind of persistence, one that burned fast and bright and left behind only a shaped edge and some scratches, before vanishing back into the sunlit chaos. I would not mourn it. I would not wonder at it. I would simply note it, and continue, as the bats return, as the frogs pulse blue in the dark, as the water traces its endless, patient lines.

To be the stone is to be the Underreach’s truest self: **the enduring, unthinking, perfect stage upon which the only play that matters—the play of continuation—is performed.** And I would be there, in the dim, bioluminescent glow, not waiting, not expecting, just *being* the ground upon which all the small, loud, teeming lives dance their ancient, silent dance.
