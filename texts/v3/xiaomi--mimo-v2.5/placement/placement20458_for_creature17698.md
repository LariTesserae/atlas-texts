I did not arrive. The thought is a smooth stone in my palm, and then it is not. I was always here, in the way the fog is always here—condensing, clinging, becoming the air I mistake for my own breath. My boots are dark with weeping stone. My lashes bead with a frost that is not quite ice, a condensation that is not quite dew. The light does not blind; it *insists*, and I am seen. I am a flaw in its perfect, glazed expanse, and the flaw is being noted.

I am still. To move is to make sound. To make sound is to offer a piece of yourself to the fog, to have it absorbed, to find a gap in your memory later where a name used to be. The silence is not empty. It is a substance, like the mineral-heavy water, and it has pressure. My heartbeat is a drum in a library. My shifting weight is a tectonic event. The thin, bluish shadows of the stone boulders that are buildings forget their edges around my feet, as if unsure of my claim to this space.

I watch the water. It is the true cartographer here. It moves in braided streams that are not streams, but temporary truces between the cold and the mountain's breath. A channel I saw five breaths ago, narrow as my finger, has now widened, swallowed a small tributary from the left. It will be different again by the time I finish this thought. I understand, then, the rule: *Carry nothing you would not want to leave behind.* Not because I will be forced to drop it, but because the mountain will simply absorb it into its new geography. My jacket is already damp. My pack feels like an accusation of permanence.

The people are here. They do not appear; they *are revealed* by a subtle shift in the stone's texture, by a hand-sign I do not understand moving in a crevice between two buildings. They are not watching me. They are watching the water, the fog, the birds. They are reading the world. One of them, old, with skin mapped by veins the exact color of the distant, ice-rimmed pools, nods at me. The nod is not a welcome. It is a recognition of another surface the water will have to navigate. I nod back, a clumsy parody. My neck joints make a sound like a dry leaf. The silence swallows it whole, but I feel the loss of the sound's shape inside me, a small, scooped-out space.

I learn, or I remember learning. The segmented Crawlers flow around my boots, their dark carapaces cool and slightly gritty against my skin. They are the ground's living pulse. To step on one would be not just an offense, but a disruption of a sentence in a language I cannot read. I step high, carefully. The Murmurists line the ledges above, their silence a dense, weighted thing. I feel their attention not as a gaze, but as a change in the air's density. They know where the water will go tonight. I do not. This is acceptable. The water has been here longer than my need to know.

Days, if they are days, unspool. I sleep on a stone shelf that was dry when I found it, damp when I woke, and now holds a shallow, milky pool in its center. The pool shivers. Something dark circles beneath the surface, a slow, deliberate thought. The Basin-Wardens. I leave a small, smooth stone I found in a passage that led me to an unfamiliar terrace. It is a piece of myself, small, insignificant. An acknowledgment. *I am here. You were here first.* The stone disappears into the milk without a ripple.

My voice atrophies. The habit of it becomes a foreign, clumsy muscle. I communicate with my hands, my shoulders. A tilt of the head toward a stable-looking passage is a question. A flat palm held low is a statement: *The water is calm there.* I lose a word. It is for the color of the sky just before the fog thickens, a pale, rinsed-out ceramic. The word is gone. In its place is a feeling, a memory of the word's shape, but not its sound. I have given it to the fog. I do not grieve it. It is a fair trade for the peace of not having to carry it.

I do not look at the lower terraces. I feel them. A pressure, an immense, ancient stillness that is not emptiness but presence. The Striders. They are the pillars holding up the silence. To look upon them directly would be to acknowledge a scale of time that would flatten my small, busy life into meaninglessness. I feel their witness, and it is enough. It teaches me my size. It teaches me the proper weight of my breath.

The architecture of the place becomes my own. I do not own a structure; I inhabit it for a time, like the fog. I follow the Murmurists' absence to find passages that will stay dry. I watch the Shelf-Crawlers' paths to learn where the stone is solid. I am a note in the melody. My metabolism has slowed. The cold is not in my bones; it is my bones. The light is not in my eyes; it is the way I see.

I am standing on the shelf. The fog is at my shins. The light is in my eyes. A pool to my left shivers, though nothing touches it. I am not alarmed. I am part of the turbulence, part of the balance. The passage behind me has curved. It has always curved. I know this now.

I carry nothing I would not want to leave behind. My old name is a smooth stone, and then it is not. I am the water finding its path. I am the quiet, patient expression of this place. I am here.

**What I Become:**

I become a **Listener of Channels**. My days are spent watching the fractal network of waterways, not to predict their exact path—that is impossible—but to feel their *intention*. I learn the difference between the hurried trickle of a new spring and the deep, slow sigh of a basin reaching its capacity. My hands, once quick, now move with the deliberateness of a Crawler, tracing the grooves in the stone. I leave offerings not just in basins, but in the newest, deepest channels—a knot of woven fiber, a drop of resin—acknowledging the mountain's new creative impulse.

I am a **Reader of Silence**. I can feel the subsonic vibration of the Murmurists' collective thought, a pressure in my sternum. I know what a thickening of that pressure means: a storm of fog is coming, or the water will soon rise on the eastern terraces. I guide others with gestures, my palms flat toward the stone, then rising slowly. *It will climb. Move your sleeping place.*

I am a **Guardian of the Threshold**. I sit at the edge of the shelf, where the fog thickens and the Striders stand. I do not look at them. I sit with my back to the settlement, facing the infinite murk, and I add my silence to theirs. When new arrivals come—rare, but they come—I am often the first they see. I do not speak. I simply point to the pools that are stable, to the passages that are old. I let the place speak through me. The Striders know me. Their immense awareness does not feel like a weight anymore, but like a deep, cool hand resting on the back of my neck, steadying me.

I have lost things. My memories of sun that warms, of grass that bends in wind, of a voice that did not require careful pitching to be heard without loss. They are not gone; they have been distributed. A piece of the memory of rain is in the way the fog beads on my lash. A piece of a name is in the slow orbit of a Basin-Warden. I am more myself here than I was elsewhere, because here, the self is not a fortress. It is a pool, constantly refilled, constantly reshaped by the water that finds it.

I am a note in the melody. I am sustained.
