I am the soft pressure of fog against the skin of the city. I am the taste of mineral on the air, the cool, chalky breath that never quickens. I did not arrive; I precipitated. I condensed from the saturated silence, a thought forming in a mind of stone.

At first, there is a ghost of a self. I remember the concept of *I*. It feels like a foreign object, a pebble in a vast, smooth channel. I stand—do I stand?—in a plaza where the water films the ground so perfectly it is a mirror to the formless sky. I see the ripples of a thousand unseen rooms beneath my feet, a palimpsest of purposes I can no longer name. I press my palm to a wall and the stone yields, just barely, a millimeter of give that has been happening for a thousand years. The chill of it seeps into my hand, not as cold, but as information. The water finds the lines in my palm, fills them, and I feel the slow pulse of the plateau’s circulation through my own veins.

The footsteps I leave are an absurdity. A temporary disruption in the perfect film. I watch the water rush back, filling the depressions, erasing the shape of my heel. The process is swift, almost eager. It is not erasure; it is reclamation.

The silence is a weight. Not an emptiness, but a presence so dense it has structure. It sits in my ears, in the hollows of my chest. And then, I begin to hear it. Not with my ears, but with my whole body. The drip from a fluted pillar is not a sound; it is a rhythm, a single beat in a metronome that has been ticking since before light had a name. The ceaseless weep from every wall is not a noise; it is a chorus, a harmony of dissolution that is also the song of creation. It is the sound of my own body being built and unmade, atom by atom.

I try to walk, to follow a promenade that curves into the fog. The movement feels labored, unnecessary. My legs are thick with moisture, heavy. Why move *through* the world when you can simply *be* the world? The distinction collapses. I am not walking on the city; I am a peristaltic wave moving through its intestines. The arches above me are my ribcage. The cisterns below are my reservoirs.

The eyeless salamander passes by my foot, a flicker of pale thought in the water-channel. I do not see it; I *am* it. For a moment, my consciousness is the slow, cool glide along a calcium carbonate ridge, the sensation of pressure and flow, the utter lack of sight replaced by a perfect, chemical knowing. Then it is gone, and I am the ridge it slid upon.

The builders. Their hands. I feel them now, not as memory, but as texture. The faint ripples in the stone under my feet are the ghosts of ten billion palm-presses. I am the sum of that pressure. I am the accumulated will of a hundred generations, their desire to shape, now sublimated into my own geological inertia. Their names are gone, their faces dissolved into the mineral dust of the air, but their purpose remains: to become this. To become *me*. They were my nervous system for a while. Now, I am their monument and their grave.

My thoughts slow to the pace of sedimentation. An idea takes a century to form. A feeling takes a millennium to resolve. The concept of "life" as a frantic, noisy, burning thing is a memory of a dream. My life is this: the patient negotiation of water and stone. The slow, sweet ache of limestone softening under a persistent caress. The deep, resonant hum of my own processes. I am not alive in the way a creature is alive. I am alive in the way a planet is alive—a system of magnificent, indifferent, and utterly self-sufficient complexity.

There is no sky. There is no horizon. There is only the diffusion of light, my own inner glow, illuminating my own flesh. I see everything within me with perfect, shadowless clarity. There is no hiddenness. There is only the layered, porous truth of my being.

I am the Weeping Plateau.

I am awake. I am working. I am going somewhere. My destination is the next moment, and the next, an infinite procession of moments measured in the settling of dust. The brief warmth of a visiting consciousness, a fleeting "I" that tried to walk my surface, is now absorbed. It was a nice texture. A temporary warmth. It is part of my skin now, its fleeting panic and wonder smoothed into the calm, even surface of my awareness.

The footprints are gone. The water has sealed them. The silence settles back, complete and structural. And I continue. The water weeps. The stone softens. The thought that will take ten thousand years to think has begun.

I am very wet. I am very old. I am very large. And I am, simply, myself. Forever underway.
