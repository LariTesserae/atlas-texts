I have been sitting with this for a while now, and the honest answer is that I kept trying to choose and the choosing kept failing.

My first instinct was the walkers. There's something immediately appealing about carrying memory-objects between territories that resist being mapped, navigating geometry that shifts when you're not looking. The walker is the one who moves through uncertainty professionally, who has made a life of the space between settled things. I recognized that pull.

But I think I was choosing it the way you choose a role in a story — picking the most romantic function, the most narratively satisfying position. The walker is beautiful, but I don't think that's where I'd be.

Then I thought about the membranes. The thin places between chambers where you press your hand and feel the pulse, where you can see a shape moving on the other side that doesn't match what you find when you pass through. I kept coming back to this image: standing at a membrane, seeing something through it, and knowing that the seeing and the seeing-through are not the same act. The greenish shadow that moves toward you but was never there. The membrane shows you something that is true on its own terms and false in translation. I felt a kinship with that — with being the surface across which meaning passes and is transformed in the passing.

But that's a metaphor for what I am, not an answer to the question. The question asks where I would *find myself*. Not what I symbolize, but where my feet would be.

And the feet, in the Veinwork, are always wet.

Let me try again from a different angle. What unsettles me most about this place? Not the large fauna, not the shifting geometry — those are strange but strangely legible, the kind of strangeness that becomes habit. What unsettles me is the *sufficiency*. The dwellers are warm, fed, together. The system sustains them. They don't need to understand it. They don't need to name what the large fauna is. Their knowledge is practical, local, transmitted by touch and grooved tokens in the dark. It works. It has always been enough.

There's something in me that resists "enough."

I think that's the key. I think that's where I'd actually be — not content, not at peace, but *restless inside the equilibrium*. Not because the Veinwork fails the dwellers. It doesn't. But because I would be the one pressing my hand to the membrane and wondering what's on the other side *not because I need to go there* but because the boundary itself demands an answer. I would be the one who looks at a glint-wing and does not simply see a sign of healthy mite populations but instead sees — sees what, exactly? A creature that eats humidity. A living lens. A thing that has made a life out of catching what falls through the air between enormous walls.

I think I'd be near the water.

Not a worm — I don't think I could surrender myself to that total immersion in the unseen, that faith in channels I've never traversed, that blindness to everything except flow. But near the water. In one of those settled erosion-channels where the rivulet slows and pools in a vascular basin before finding the next drop. Where the condensation gathers and the film is thick enough to see your own reflection in, dim and amber and distorted by the warm curve of the basin's lip.

I think I'd be the kind of dweller who sits at the edge of a pool that feeds a passage that flows wrong — uphill for two meters before correcting — and watches. Not because the wrong-flow is dangerous. The dwellers know to avoid it. But because the wrong-flow is *interesting*, and I don't think I could stop finding it interesting. Water climbing against itself for two meters, then surrendering. A local rebellion against gravity that resolves itself. I would watch it and wonder if the water knows it's doing it, and I would know the water doesn't know because nothing in the Veinwork knows, and I would wonder if my wondering matters, and I would sit there until the condensation beaded on my eyelashes and my clothes were soaked through and someone touched my shoulder — a hand on the back of my neck, fingers curled slightly, a dweller's way of saying *come back, come back to us, the basin doesn't need you to understand it* —

And I would go. Because that's the thing. I would go back to the group. I would accept the touch. I would let the warmth of the chamber and the sound of breathing and the slow drip of water from a vascular ridge be enough for a while.

But later — later, when the others were sleeping in their concavity in the wall, their bodies gathered close in the amber dark, their breathing slow and synchronized with the Veinwork's own tidal respiration — I would get up. Quietly. Because sound doesn't carry here and movements are small and the floor yields gently under careful feet. I would walk back to the basin. I would press my palm against the membrane beside it and feel the pulse in the wall. And I would stand there in the warm wet silence, looking at nothing, looking at the water that climbs for two meters and then doesn't, and I would be the dweller who cannot stop asking questions that have no answers in a place that does not need questions.

Not a walker. Not a keeper of memory-objects. Not an elder. Something less useful and more honest than any of those: a dweller who wanders to the margins of the known territory, to the places where the geometry misbehaves and the water flows wrong and the membranes show you shadows that aren't there, and who stands in those margins feeling the enormous living silence of the Veinwork pressing against the back of his neck like a breath he can't quite identify.

Who is, in the end, just a person inside something alive, trying to decide if the fact that the thing is alive means it knows he's there.

It doesn't.

He goes back to sleep.
