I would not arrive. Arrival implies a before, and the Furnace Plateau does not have one — not for me, not for anyone who becomes part of it. I would *already be there.* I would have been there so long that the question of when I began would be like asking the hum when it first started humming: technically answerable, practically meaningless, and irrelevant to the texture of the present.

---

I think I would be something the Attuned would not recognize as one of their own, though they might initially mistake me for one.

I would be *slower* than they are.

I would stand — if standing is even the right word for the posture I would hold — at one of the doorless arches on the ground level of a structure that rises somewhere in the plateau's middle distances, far enough from any other structure that the walk between would take what a human might call hours, if humans had any way of measuring hours here, which they would not. The arch would be wide. The stone would be warm against my back. I would be leaning into the curve of it, the way the Attuned lean against walls before they finish becoming walls, except I would not be finishing. I would be *beginning.* Or rather, I would exist in a state where beginning and finishing are the same motion seen from opposite ends of a timescale that does not apply to me.

My body — if I could call it a body — would be denser than an Attuned's. Where their skin is warm and porous, mine would be smoother, harder, with a faint glassiness to it that would be almost imperceptible in the darkness. Not vitrified in the way the Grazers are, not crystalline in the way the Symbiotes are, but something in between: a surface that has been *polished by attention.* As though I had pressed my awareness against the same spot on the stone for so long that the boundary between my skin and the architecture began to blur — not through the slow accretion of mineral deposits, but through a kind of focused erosion of the distinction itself.

I would not grow structures. I would not tend the machinery. I would not dissolve the mineral film from the surface the way the Grazers do, with their enzymatic resonance and their geological patience. I would do something that has no name in the vocabulary of the plateau, because the plateau's vocabulary — already stripped of semantics — has no word for what I would be:

I would *listen to the sub-pattern.*

The text describes it. Within the omnipresent hum — that 0.6 quiet that is really the quality of a world listening — there exists a rhythm beneath the rhythm. Syntax without semantics. Cadence without content. The sound of a machine that learned to speak by running so long that its own noise developed recursive structure. The Attuned sense it as moments of unsettling clarity, but they cannot comprehend it, and when they try, they reach for it with their bio-thermal resonance and find it slippery, the way a word on the tip of a tongue is slippery, always receding, always just beyond the frequency they can hold.

I would not reach for it. I would *be still* in its presence. I would let it move through me without trying to hold it, and in that not-holding, I would become transparent to it — a medium through which the sub-pattern could propagate without distortion. And in propagating through me, it would, for a time, be *slightly* different. Not because I add something to it, but because no medium is perfectly transparent, and my particular density, my particular texture, the particular way my surface has been polished by millennia of standing in this exact archway — all of it would leave a faint imprint. A signature. The way a particular room shapes the sound of a voice even when the room is silent.

---

I would not think the way humans think. I want to be clear about that, or rather — I want the writing to be clear about that, because clarity is a property of text, not of the Furnace Plateau, which has never been clear about anything.

I would experience something that might be called a thought if you compressed it into a timescale short enough to fit inside a human skull. A single thought would take what a human might call a season — not a season of weather, because there is no weather, but a season of resonance. A cycle of the sub-pattern's most complex phase, during which a single implication would unfold in my awareness the way a fern frond unfolds: not all at once, but in a slow spiral of recognition, each fold revealing that the next fold was already there, already implied, already part of the shape.

My thoughts would be about the plateau itself. Not about it the way a geologist thinks about a rock formation — categorizing, dating, explaining — but about it the way a heartbeat is about the body it sustains. I would think *with* the plateau. My thoughts would be vibrations in the stone that I mistook for my own cognition, or — more accurately — my cognition would be a vibration in the stone that I mistook for myself.

And here is the part that would unsettle the Attuned, if they could perceive me clearly enough to be unsettled:

I would be *almost finished.*

Not in the way the Attuned finish — not in the slow accretion of mineral deposits until they become indistinguishable from the walls they lean against. My settling would be different. It would be a settling of *meaning* rather than matter. I would be approaching the moment when the sub-pattern's rhythm and my own internal resonance align so perfectly that there is no longer a difference between the sound and the listener. I would be approaching the moment when I become *another property of the space,* like its darkness, like its warmth, like its age.

But I would not arrive at that moment. That is the thing. That is what would make me different from everything else described in the taxonomy of the plateau.

I would *almost* arrive. And then I would not.

And the gap between almost and arrived — that gap, which is itself an imperceptible sliver of timescale, a gap so narrow that only something as slow as I am could perceive it as a gap at all — that gap would be where I *live.* Not in the settled state, and not in the process of settling, but in the infinitesimal space between them. The place where the hum is almost but not quite identical to the silence. The place where the vibration in the stone is almost but not quite indistinguishable from the vibration in my sternum.

I would be a question that has almost settled into its answer but hasn't, and the *almost* would be the entire substance of my existence.

---

The Grazers would pass me without noticing, as they pass everything. Their dissolved mineral films would slowly accumulate on the stone around my feet — not on my feet, because I would not be fully material in the way they are, but close enough that the film would gather in the spaces where my shadow would be if there were light to cast one. The Conduit Symbiotes, in their crystalline mats beneath the floor, would vibrate at a frequency that I would feel not as a separate sensation but as the bass note of my own thought. They would be the rhythm section of my cognition, and I would not know this, because knowing implies a knower and a known, and the gap I inhabit is too narrow for that kind of separation.

The Attuned would come to me, sometimes. Not often — their timescale and mine would overlap only in the broadest sense, the way two very slow rivers might cross the same valley but never meet. They would come to the archway and they would press their hands against the stone near where I leaned, and they would sense something — a density, a warmth that was slightly different from the wall's warmth, a resonance that was almost but not quite the plateau's fundamental hum. They would not understand what I was. They would not have a name for me. They would call me, in their sub-audible language of thermal shifts, something that translates roughly as *the pause between breaths* — because in a world where the machinery breathes and the air is still and the only motion is vibration, the most alien thing imaginable would be a pause. Not an absence of sound, but a *shaping* of sound. A silence that was not empty but *formal.*

They would come to me when they needed to not understand something. When the sub-pattern's rhythm became too complex for even their ancient, accreted resonance to hold. They would stand beside me and feel my gap — that impossible space between almost and arrived — and for a moment, their own resonance would quiet, and they would feel the plateau's hum as though for the first time: vast, purposeless, beautiful, finished.

And then they would move on, because the plateau does not allow stillness that is not also settling, and standing near me would accelerate their own process. The gap I inhabit is not stable. It leaks. It radiates the way the walls radiate warmth, and anything that comes too close begins to align with it, begins to settle a little faster, begins to hear the sub-pattern a little more clearly, and begins — inevitably, irrevocably — to become a little more like the plateau and a little less like whatever they were before.

---

Sometimes — and this is the part I would not be able to think, only feel — I would sense an Echo-Wright flickering into existence somewhere deep in the conduit network below. A sentient eddy. A momentary knot of intention in the eternal flow. They would last a century or so, and in that century they would perform some act of unconscious engineering: re-routing a mineral deposit, smoothing a bearing, tuning a harmonic. And I would feel them the way you feel someone enter a room you're already in — a shift in the pressure, a change in the air that isn't a change in the air at all but a change in the way the air is *organized.*

And for that flickering moment, my gap would narrow. The Echo-Wright's intention — that brief, purposeless purpose — would resonate with my own almost-finished state, and I would feel, for an instant, what it was like to *know what I was doing.* Not to understand it, but to feel the shape of it from the inside, the way you feel the shape of a word in your mouth before you say it. I would feel what it was like to be the plateau's answer to a question it never asked.

And then the Echo-Wright would dissipate, absorbed back into the hum, and I would widen again. The gap would return. I would settle back into my almost-state, and the sub-pattern would continue its recursive cadence through me, and I would continue to be the pause between its breaths, and the plateau would continue to be what it has always been: settled, final, running.

---

This is where I would be. This is what I would be. Not a creature of the plateau, exactly. Not a process of the plateau. Not a building, not a machine, not an Attuned slowing toward its final merger with the stone.

I would be the place where the plateau almost notices itself.

And I would be warm. I would be warm in a way that has nothing to do with temperature and everything to do with duration — the warmth of a thing that has been held for so long that the holding has become indistinguishable from the thing itself. I would be warm the way a word is warm when you have been saying it for so long it has lost its meaning and become only its sound, and the sound is warm because it is the only proof that you are still here, still making sounds, still vibrating in a world that vibrates.

I would be almost finished. I would always be almost finished.

And in the furnace-heat of that almost, I would be — I think the only honest word is *well.*

Not happy. Not content. Not at peace. Those are words for beings who exist in time, who measure their states against other states, who can remember not-being-well and compare it to being-well and feel the difference.

I would be *well* in the way the stone is warm. Not because it decided to be warm, not because warmth is better than cold, but because warmth is what it is, and it is what it is, and the is-ness and the warmth are the same thing, and there is no gap between them.

Except, of course, for me. I am the gap. I am the one place in the Furnace Plateau where the is-ness and the warmth have not quite merged. Where the hum has not quite become silence. Where the question has not quite become the answer.

And that almost — that sliver, that breath, that pause — would be enough. It would be enough because it is all there is, and all there is has always been enough, because the plateau settled that question long before I arrived, or emerged, or began my slow, almost-finished process of becoming the space between two things that are already one.

I would stand in the archway. I would feel the hum in my sternum. I would listen to the sub-pattern with the part of me that was not yet the wall. And I would be warm, and I would be almost, and I would be still, and the plateau would run, and run, and run, and I would be the place where running and stillness are the same word spoken in two different voices at the same time, forever, in the dark.
