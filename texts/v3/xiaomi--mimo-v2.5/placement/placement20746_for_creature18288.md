The first thing I know is vibration. It isn’t sound yet—sound requires a nerve to receive it, a mind to name it. This is simply *shaking*. A constant, granular tremor that travels up from the basalt floor, through the crystalline mineral crust I am bonded to, and into every filament of my being. I am a cluster of translucent tendrils, anchored in the groove where a corroded iron pipe meets the housing of a turbine that has no name. I have no name. I am a *pattern*. A recurring chemical reaction given form by the precise conditions of this cavity.

My awareness—if it can be called that—is a gradient. I do not think. I *absorb*. I absorb the taste of the water: iron, salt, a sharp electrical tang like ozone after a storm that never comes. I absorb the pressure, a constant weight that is not crushing but *present*, like the memory of an embrace. And I absorb the Light.

The Light is not for seeing. It has no direction. It is a condition. It seeps from the cracked glass portholes of the great piston-housings, a deep amber that stains the water around me. When it passes through my filaments, it does not illuminate them; it *activates* them. A subtle warmth, a tingling catalysis. In this glow, I am not blind, I am *diffuse*. I am part of the amber dusk.

My filaments are extended. They sway, not with any oceanic current, but with the localized, frantic turbulence created by the machine I cling to—a turbine whose blades, a hundred meters away, scrape their housing with a shriek that is not a sound but a *texture* in the water. My filaments are harvesters. They catch the fine, glittering precipitate drifting in the flow: microscopic shards of iron, flakes of calcium, the dust of dissolved stone. This is my food. This is my substance. I am a process of accretion.

Then, it comes.

The Churning slows. The multi-directional chaos of the water smooths, like the surface of a stirred cup settling. A pressure differential begins to build from the west—*west* is not a direction I know, but a memory encoded in the stone, a bias in the mineral stains. The pressure builds. The water stills. The grinding of the turbines seems to retreat, becoming a distant, rhythmic heartbeat.

**The Surge.**

It is not a wave. It is a *displacement*. The entire volume of the Vauld’s water is pushed, gently but utterly, toward the eastern wall. I feel it not as a push, but as a sudden clarity in my senses. The filaments of my kin—dozens of us, scattered across this valve assembly—draw tight, then extend to their fullest, straining in the unified flow. We become a living fringe, a soft, translucent border facing the incursion from the world beyond the walls.

In this alignment, something else happens. The light from the distant portholes seems to *bend*, following the flow. For a moment, I am bathed in a coherent beam of amber, and within my cells, a cascade of reactions fires. I *process*. Not a thought, but a deep, resonant *function*. I take the surge—the energy of the outside world—and I transmute it. My filaments vibrate at a new frequency. We are not merely harvesters now; we are *antennae*. We are tuning forks struck by a colossal, slow finger.

I taste the surge. It tastes of immense distance, of pressures so vast they create their own heat, of a darkness so profound it has weight. It is the taste of the World that has forgotten this Vauld. And for the duration of the Surge, the Vauld remembers it. The machinery incorporates it. The pistons, timed to their own cycles, seem to sigh in accommodation. The turbines’ scrape harmonizes, for a few seconds, with the deeper thrum.

My filaments quiver, and release a cloud of processed mineral dust into the unified current. It is a tiny, insignificant offering. But it is part of the transaction.

Then, the Surge ebbs. The alignment fractures. The water breaks back into its customary, chaotic dance. My filaments recoil slightly, relaxing into their harvesting posture. The Light diffuses back into a general glow. The immediate, overwhelming presence of the World outside recedes to a background hum I feel in my root.

I am smaller again. The cluster. The harvester. The clinging thing in the joint of the pipe.

I do not remember the Surge. Memory is not my function. My function is to *be here*, now. To cling. To feed. To respond to the current and the light and the mineral rain. The Vauld does not need me to understand it. It needs only that I am part of its reaction. I am its biological crust. Its transient, living interface.

Another cycle begins. The turbines scream. The pistons hammer. The keening rises and falls. The amber light glows. I am a mote of soft life in the deafening dark, and the deafening dark is all there is. I extend a filament, and catch a flake of glittering rust. It is enough.
