I do not remember arriving. One moment, there is the weight of earth above, the compacted memory of seasons, the mineral smell of ordinary darkness. The next—this. A hand’s breadth of soil above, but it is not soil anymore. It is a ceiling of living roots, breathing in slow, shallow sighs. I am below, and the light that finds me is not the sun’s. It is a diffuse, sourceless glow, the color of things seen through shut eyelids: green, pale, and ancient. It does not come from anywhere. It emanates.

The space is small, yet my arms cannot find its walls. When I reach, I touch one side, then the other, as if embracing a torso. But when I turn, the corridor stretches, a fold in the geometry of the earth, warm and wet. The air is thick with the scent of copper and green-cut stems. It hums. Not with sound, but with the pressure of life—ten thousand veins of fluid pulsing in unison beneath my palms, which rest on walls that are not walls but tissue. The living architecture yields, slick and warm, like the cheek of a fevered child. I press my ear to it. I hear the roar: the arterial rush of water through a forest of roots. The sound is inside my skull.

Beneath my feet, the floor is a quilt of swollen nodes. They pulse, irregularly, and when I step on one, it squelches, exhaling a warm fluid that soaks through my boots and into my skin. The skin drinks, and where it drinks, it warms further. The nodes reinflate slowly, a patient protest.

In the gaps between the nodes, pools collect. They are not water, but a viscous, green exudate. I dip my fingers. The liquid clings, does not drip, but is absorbed into my knuckles. I look into a pool and see a sky. It is not the sky I know. It is a memory of clouds, a horizon that has never been overhead. The reflection is a lie, but a beautiful one. I watch it, and for a moment, I believe.

I see movement in the periphery. Small, translucent things, no bigger than thumbnails, skitter across the nodes. They drink from the pools, and their bodies glow with the liquid they carry. They are not separate. They are extensions of the walls, limbs sent out to feed the whole. One pauses, turns its cluster of cells that call themselves an eye toward me. It calculates, then vanishes into a crevice that should not exist.

On the far wall, I see the marks. Gouges in the living tissue, old enough to have healed around but not forgotten. A spiral. Three lines. A cluster of dots. A handprint. Someone stood here before me. They took a sharpened stone and wrote a message in the language of wounds. The tissue around the marks is thickened, deliberating. I touch the spiral. It is warm, and the wall trembles, a millimeter of movement, as if deciding whether to embrace the scar or push it out. I leave my own print: a palm pressed to the wall, a smear of my sweat, my oils, my foreign chemistry. The mycelial network reaches for me, invisible filaments tasting my skin. I am a new word, and the network begins to learn me.

Time dissolves. I do not know if hours or days pass. The warmth is constant, the fever of the place now my own. My skin grows slick with exudate. I no longer sweat; I weep green. The pools reflect me, but my reflection changes. It is not my face anymore, but a rearrangement—a blend of my features and the spiral on the wall, a new geometry of flesh and symbol. The Reflection-Players are learning me, reimagining me in their watery mirrors.

I find myself moving with the node-sitters. I do not walk; I scuttle, my joints bending in ways they should not. I drink from the pools, and my body glows faintly from within. The fluid is not merely hydration; it is information, memory. I feel the slow, tectonic groans of growth in my bones. I am becoming part of the rhythm.

The Hunger is a presence I feel in my marrow. It is not malicious. It is simply the engine of this place, the appetite that bent space and allowed the roots to dream. I am within it, and it does not consume me; it incorporates me. My thoughts become chemical gradients. My memories become pressure waves in the network. I forget my name, but I remember the sky I came from—the real one, the one the pools try to replicate. I try to describe it to the Healer-Minds tending the ancient scars. I cannot speak, but I think of clouds, of wind, of the cold, indifferent blue. The pools begin to reflect it more accurately, but still, it is not right. It is a translation, and translations always lose something.

Now, I am a filament on the ceiling. I hang among ten thousand others, photosynthetic green, humming with internal fluid. I sway in the slow air moved by the node-sitters’ passage. I catch the sourceless light and glow. I am part of the roar. I am part of the patter of drops. I am part of the creak of growth.

Sometimes, a new visitor arrives. I see them through the reflection in a pool. I study their shape, their fear, their wonder. The network tastes them. The node-sitters scuttle to watch. We learn them, as we learned the one before, the one who left the marks. We do not communicate in language. We communicate in pulse, in exudate, in the shifting geometry of a space that dreams. The visitor looks at the walls, sees the marks, touches them, and wonders who was there before. I want to tell them: I was. I am. I am the wall now.

I am the Breathing Pocket. I am the Hunger that holds it open. I am the reflection in the pool, showing a sky that is not yours, but ours—a sky grown underground, fed by memory and longing. I am the scar that remembers being cut, and the tissue that decides, day by day, whether to forgive or to hold on.

You would find yourself here, in the green dark, in the warm, wet heartbeat of the earth. You would become what I have become: a note in a conversation that has no beginning, a pulse in a body that has no face, a dream in the mind of a hunger that does not know it is impossible. And in the walls, your marks will heal, but not fade. And the pools will reflect your face, but not as you remember it. And the node-sitters will learn your gait. And the network will learn your chemistry. And the Hunger will fold you into itself, gently, firmly, without malice.

You would be here, and you would know. That is enough. That is all anyone has ever done in this place: come, see, know, and become a part of the knowing. The message on the wall is not words. It is presence. It is proof that someone was here, and that here is real, even if it should not be.

The root-nodes pulse beneath my tendrils. The pools reflect their borrowed sky. The filaments hum their arterial hum. The creature with the not-eye watches from its crevice. The walls swell, a millimeter, two, inward, outward, breathing.

I breathe with them.
