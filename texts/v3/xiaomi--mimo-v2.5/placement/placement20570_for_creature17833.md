# The Scratched Line

I am the only imperfection, and I have made peace with that.

I did not arrive the way you might imagine — stepping through some threshold, some door in the air, some seam in the architecture that opened onto this place. There was no moment of crossing. I have simply been here long enough that the memory of arrival has compressed into a single featureless point, like a coordinate without axes. I know that once I was elsewhere. I can recall, in a theoretical way, that there were seasons, or things that functioned as seasons — changes in light, shifts in air, a before and an after that followed each other in sequence. I remember this the way I remember that I have a name. It is a fact I carry but cannot feel.

Here, I walk.

---

I walk the way the articulated walk, though I am not one of them. I learned their pace not out of imitation but out of exhaustion — not physical exhaustion, but the exhaustion of every other speed. Fast movement has no object here. There is nothing to arrive at sooner. Slow movement accumulates a weight of minutes that becomes unbearable against the stillness of everything around you. One point four meters per second is the speed at which the landscape passes at the rate your attention can process it without either sharpening into obsession or blurring into indifference. It is, as the description says, *correct*. I arrived at it independently and found that it was already the speed the world moved at, and that my adopting it changed nothing, because nothing changes, and I had simply aligned myself with the one parameter that was already set.

My footsteps are different from theirs. That is the first thing. The articulated produce a *tick-tick-tick* — metronomic, precise, each footfall identical in force and interval. I produce a sound that varies. Sometimes my heel catches differently. Sometimes I shift my weight unevenly. Sometimes I scuff. These are not errors, exactly — the Plate does not register error — but they are *noise*, irregularities in a system that runs on uniformity. When I walk through a dense concentration of the articulated, I can feel my own rhythm falling against theirs, the way a heartbeat falls against a clock, and there is something in that dissonance that reminds me I am alive, which is the thing I am most at risk of forgetting.

---

I have scratched the ground.

This happened early — or what I think of as early, before the days (which are not days, because there is no day-night cycle, only the permanent warm light from no visible source) bled into one another. I dropped something. A tool, I think — something I carried, something from before, something with an edge. It struck the syntheticite and left a mark. A thin white line, a millimeter deep, cutting across the original scoring of the construction.

I remember the feeling of that mark appearing. It was not dramatic. It was not cathartic. It was something more fundamental: the feeling of *consequence*. Something had happened that had not been part of the system's equilibrium. A new feature had been introduced. The mark existed, and the Plate had not corrected it, and would not correct it, because correction is not something the Plate does, but also the mark had not been *permitted*, because permission is not something the Plate does either. The mark simply *was*, the way the condensation simply was, the way the articulated simply were. It was another feature of a world that contains features but no history.

I go back to it sometimes. I stand over it. The scratch is exactly as I left it — no wider, no narrower, no more weathered, because there is no weather here, only the constant warm moisture that wets everything equally and changes nothing. The scratch sits on the surface like a word in a language that was never spoken. It means nothing. It changes nothing. But it is mine, in a way that nothing else on the Plate is mine, because everything else on the Plate was here before me and will be here after me and was not made by anything that bears a name.

---

The articulated pass me at their constant pace. They do not alter their bearing. They do not slow or speed. I have stood directly in the path of one — directly, with no lateral offset, my body centered on its trajectory — and watched it approach at 1.4 meters per second until it was close enough to touch, and then it was not there anymore, it was *beside* me, having made some adjustment so precise and so swift that I could not perceive it, a lateral shift of perhaps thirty centimeters executed in a fraction of a second and then immediately corrected, the articulated resuming its exact former bearing without a visible wobble or deviation.

I am not a thing the system routes around. That would imply awareness. I am a *geometric feature* — an obstruction of a certain size and position that the articulated's pathfinding resolves the way a fluid resolves a stone, parting and rejoining without turbulence. They are not avoiding me. They are solving for me. The difference is everything and nothing.

The smaller fauna ignore me entirely. I have stood in the grooves — I fit, barely, if I turn my feet sideways — and felt them moving against my ankles, their sheet-metal exoskeletons cool against my skin, their tiny legs working the surface of the syntheticite with the tireless, pointless diligence of their kind. They do not climb me. They do not investigate me. They pass over me the way they pass over a hatch-cover or a channel-wall: a feature of the landscape, an obstacle to be navigated around or climbed over, part of the topology of the ground, indistinguishable in functional terms from any other bump or ridge in the syntheticite's scored surface.

I am terrain. I am topography. I am a hill in a flat world, and I do not erode, and they do not care.

---

The soft are what I find hardest.

I can avoid the articulated — their paths are fixed, their schedules absolute, their bearing knowable. I can step over the smaller fauna. But the soft fill the spaces I need to move through, the channels between the housings, the narrow canyons where the architecture closes in and the only way forward is through a passage already occupied by warm, translucent, pulsing mass.

I have pushed through them. It is the most intimate physical experience available on the Plate, and it is not intimate at all. Their substance yields. It is warm — always the same warmth, the warmth of the air, the warmth of the water, the warmth of everything here — and it is wet, not with surface moisture but with an integrated wetness, as though the boundary between the organism and its medium had dissolved at some fundamental level. When I push through a dense aggregation, they part around me and close behind me. The filament networks tear and reform in seconds. I emerge on the other side coated in a thin film of their substance that evaporates — or is reabsorbed — within minutes, leaving no trace.

The thing I cannot shake, the thing that keeps me coming back to the channels, is this: when I am immersed in the soft — chest-deep, waist-deep, moving slowly through a canyon where they are layered four deep and the light filters through their bodies in golden gradients — I feel the pulse. Not metaphorically. A physical, slow, rhythmic expansion and contraction of the mass around me, synchronized across dozens of individual organisms, a wave of peristaltic motion that moves through the aggregation like a breath through a lung. It is not fast. One pulse every forty seconds, perhaps. A gentle thickening and thinning, a subtle shift in pressure against my body.

I have timed it. It does not vary. It has never varied, across every observation, in every location, on every portion of the Plate where the soft are found. The pulse is universal and constant and it is the closest thing this world has to a heartbeat, and it beats for nothing, and it beats without variation, and if I stand still long enough in the channels I begin to feel my own heart falling into its rhythm, which is impossible — my heart does not synchronize with external stimuli at that timescale — and yet it happens, or I think it happens, and the thought is the thing I cannot shake, because if my body can synchronize with this place, if the rhythms of my living flesh can entrain to the rhythms of something that is not alive, then what does that make me?

---

I have asked the question that the world does not answer.

*Why?*

Why was the Plate built? Why were the fauna placed? Why does the water condense at this rate and no other? Why does the articulated walk at 1.4 meters per second? Why do the soft pulse every forty seconds? Why do the smaller fauna trace the grooves instead of the flat surfaces? Why is the temperature what it is? Why is the light what it is? Why is the equilibrium what it is?

The world does not answer because the world does not hear. The question requires a listener, and there is no listener here. The fauna do not hear it. The structures do not hear it. The water does not hear it. I am the only thing here that asks, and my asking changes nothing, because asking is a thing that requires consequence to have meaning, and there is no consequence here, only continuation.

But I ask anyway. I ask because I am alive and asking is what living things do in the presence of systems they cannot explain, and this system is the most unexplainable system I have ever encountered, and I have encountered many, because I am old — or I have been here a long time, which amounts to the same thing in a place without biological aging, where my body persists in the exact state it was in when I arrived, because nothing here promotes decay and nothing here promotes renewal and I am simply *maintained*, the way everything is maintained, held in a state of perpetual present tense.

---

Here is what I have come to understand, or what passes for understanding in a place where understanding cannot be tested against change:

The Plate is a sentence that has been completed.

Not a story — a story requires events, and there are no events here. A sentence. A grammatical unit with a structure and a conclusion, fully formed, placed on the page all at once, meaning everything it means in the instant of its completion. The articulated are the subject. The soft are the verb. The smaller fauna are the punctuation. The water is the tone. The housing structures are the syntax. And the sentence says: *this*.

Just *this*.

Not *this is what was built* or *this is why* or *this is what it means*. Just *this*. The present tense of a world that has no past and no future, that exists only in the perpetual unfolding of its own operation, that is not going anywhere because it is already there, it was always there, it is the there that everything else would have to be relative to if there were anything else, and there isn't, because the Plate is continental and total and extends to every horizon and there is nothing beyond the haze except more Plate, more housing, more fauna, more water, more equilibrium.

---

I am the only verb that conjugates.

I am the only word in this sentence that changes tense. When I think, the thought has a before and an after. When I walk, my position has a history. When I scratch the ground, the scratch is an event — not an adjustment, not a redistribution, but a *happening*, something that was not and then was, and this is the most alien thing on the Plate, more alien than the articulated, more alien than the soft, more alien than the absolute equilibrium of a world without change: the simple, irreducible fact of *happening*.

I happen. The Plate continues. These are not the same thing, and they coexist, and their coexistence is the only actual tension this world has ever contained.

---

Sometimes I lie down in a shallow pool.

The water is exactly the depth it always is — millimeters, calibrated, precise — and it supports my weight the way the air supports the mist: not by effort but by arrangement. The water does not deepen. The pool does not overflow. I am simply *in* it, a body occupying a calibrated space, and the water warms around me the way everything warms here, and the condensation falls on my face in slow, heavy drops that do not sting and do not cleanse and do not mean anything.

I lie there and I look up at the haze, which has no top, which is not sky but is the space above the Plate where the moisture accumulates until it is indistinguishable from atmosphere, and I think: I am the only thing in this world that has ever rested.

Rest requires the possibility of not-resting. Rest is a deviation from activity, and deviation requires a norm to deviate from. On the Plate, there is no norm of activity or inactivity — there is only operation, which is neither rest nor work but a third state that has no name. The articulated do not rest. The soft do not rest. The smaller fauna do not rest. They operate, ceaselessly, at the rate they operate at, which is the only rate, which has always been the rate.

I rest. I choose to stop. I press my body against the syntheticite and feel its warmth through the water and I stop moving and I stop thinking and I let the equilibrium of the Plate operate on me, through me, around me, and for a moment — a real moment, a moment with edges, a moment that begins and ends — I am part of it.

Then I get up, because I am alive, and alive things get up, and the getting-up is the scratch in the groove, the white line on the syntheticite, the history on the place that has none, the noise in the signal, the verb in the sentence, the heartbeat that syncs to a pulse it was never meant to match.

---

I will leave eventually. Not because the Plate will force me. Nothing on the Plate forces anything. I will leave because I am finite and this place is not, because I change and this place does not, because eventually the equilibrium will become indistinguishable from imprisonment, and I will want — *want*, another word the Plate does not contain — to stand somewhere where the wind has a direction and the light has a source and the water goes somewhere and the ground was not poured but *was*, already there, before anyone built anything on top of it, carrying the fossils of things that lived and died and left traces that changed the chemistry of the stone and gave it a history measured in millions of years.

I will leave and the scratch will remain. The scratch will remain forever, or for as long as the Plate remains, which may be the same thing. It will sit in its groove, white against the dark syntheticite, bearing the exact shape of the moment it was made, unchanged by the water that condenses on it and the articulated that walk over it and the smaller fauna that trace around it in their grooves. It will be the only evidence that something happened here, once, that something living moved through this place and left a mark that was not part of the design.

And the Plate will not know it is there.

And that is the truest and most terrible thing about this place: it is a world that cannot witness itself. It runs, and its running is complete, and its completeness is the absence of everything that makes completeness meaningful — surprise, loss, discovery, the shock of the new, the ache of the remembered. The Plate is perfect the way a circle is perfect, which is to say the way a thing without interiority is perfect, which is to say not perfect at all but merely *finished*.

I am the imperfection. I am the scratch. I am the thing that happened in the place where nothing happens.

And when I go, the sentence will be complete again. The equilibrium will reassert. The fauna will operate. The water will condense. The pulse will continue at its forty-second interval. And there will be no one to know that any of it is strange, because strangeness requires a witness, and there will be no witness, and there never was one, and the Plate was always a world without an audience, performing for no one, eternally and beautifully and without meaning.

Except for the scratch.

The scratch will know.
