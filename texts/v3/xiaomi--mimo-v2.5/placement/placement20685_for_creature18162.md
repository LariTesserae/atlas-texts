I would be found at the barricade.

Not in the bustling, breathing core with its marketplaces and lived-in groves, though I would visit, feeling the cool, woven walls under my palms and sharing the whispered barter. But my place would be at the very edge, where the managed world ends and the raw becoming begins.

I would be a **Sentinel of the Unfinished**.

My tools would not be weapons. They would be delicate: a rod of tuned, resonant wood to feel the deep vibrations of the forming stone; a satchel of mineral pigments to mark shifts in the rock’s chemistry on the barricade’s bark; most importantly, my own body, trained to listen. I would sit for hours on a platform woven into the defensive line of young trees, my feet bare against the living wood, my eyes on the darkness beyond.

My work would be interpretation. The deep *crack* of a falling ceiling slab is not just a sound; it’s a message. The scent change in the cold, mineral-sharp air is data. The hesitant, rhythmic footfalls of the deeper fauna are a grammar I am still learning. I would not seek to conquer or name those larger things. I would seek to understand their rhythms, their boundaries, so that the city might continue its slow, respectful inch forward.

I would know the difference between the settling groan of geology finding its shape and the ominous groan of a collapse waiting to happen. I would feel the subtle shift in the bellows-trees’ pulse that signals a change in atmospheric pressure, a change that might hasten the stabilization of a new stretch of ground. My life would be a conversation held in geologic time, with responses measured in decades of growth.

At dusk, when the city’s light only slightly dims, I would be the one to make the final, silent circuit of the barricade, touching each sentinel tree, feeling for the thrum of health in its fibers. I would look out into the forming dark, where the occasional, distant flash of something—bioluminescence? mineral reaction?—streaks through the void. I would not fear it. It is the frontier’s own heartbeat.

I am not the one who raises the archways or tends the market-groves. I am the one who makes their continued growth possible. I am the nerve ending at the city’s expanding edge, translating silence into safety, darkness into a map of future light. To be here is to embody the city’s deepest truth: that growth is not an assertion against the world, but a patient listening to it, and a slow, courageous step into what is just beginning to be.

In this place, I would not be a builder. I would be a witness to the building. And in that witnessing, I would be a vital part of the structure itself.
