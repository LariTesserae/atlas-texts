# What I Would Become

I think I would be a crack.

Not the crack itself — that's too simple, too singular. But I understand now that the crack is where meaning lives here, and it took me a while to arrive at that, because when I first read about this place I wanted to be the horizon. Everyone wants to be the horizon. The clean line where the dome of sky meets the plane of ground — that's where a certain kind of loneliness feels like authority. I would stand at the edge of visibility and be the proof that the world has a shape.

But that's a fantasy borrowed from elsewhere. From places where the horizon is a promise. Here the horizon is a problem. It insists on a geometry that the face was not built to process. To be the horizon on the Crust Plain would be to be an illusion — a line that exists only because the scale overwhelms the eye. I don't want to be an optical event. I want to be part of the actual story.

So: a crack.

---

But let me start further back. Let me say what it would mean to *be here* at all.

I would arrive — though "arrive" implies a journey, and the Crust Plain does not suggest that anyone travels to it. You would be *found* in it. Woken into it. The way you wake into a fever: suddenly, already inside, the heat already pressing down on your chest. The air would be warm and absolutely still, and the first thing I would notice is not the flatness, not the pale ground, but the silence. Not the absence of sound. Something more radical. The absence of *listening*. No bird is tuning its voice to the air. No insect is calibrating its wings to a frequency. The silence here does not fill a space where noise should be. It is the native condition. It would take me a long time to understand that this silence is not waiting to be broken. It is complete.

I would stand — or walk, though the word feels too purposeful for what you do on the Crust Plain, where every direction is the same direction and every step forward is also every step that could have been taken to the left or right, because there is nothing to walk *toward* — and I would look down.

And I would see the banding.

White, cream, faintly amber, white again. Millions of years of微生物 patience laid down in lines thinner than I could separate with my fingernail. And I would know, the way you know things in dreams, that I am standing on a body. Not a metaphor for a body. An actual, enormous, still-living body, whose heartbeat is a chemical pulse so slow that a human lifetime would not capture a single full contraction.

This is the first thing I would need to unlearn: that the ground is not ground. That this surface was *made*. Not by anyone. Not for anything. Made the way a pearl is made — as a byproduct of life encountering an irritant, except here the irritant is *existence itself*, the mineral presence of a world, and the pearl is the size of a continent.

If I pressed my face to it — and I think I would. I think the first thing any consciousness would want to do here is *touch*, because the eyes have so little to work with — I would feel warmth, even at night. A kiln's warmth. And I would hear nothing through the crust, but I would *sense* the vibration of the thermal ticking, that slow mineral percussion, like a clock made of pottery shards, each tick a millimeter of expansion or contraction, each tock a millennium.

---

Now. The crack.

It is not dramatic. That is the thing about it. There is no earthquake, no catastrophic fracture, no sound of rending. The crack opens the way a wrinkle opens in aging skin — slowly, through the simple fact of反复 expansion and contraction, of being heated and cooled, day after day, century after century, until the material cannot accommodate the stress and it gives way, and the giving way is silent and takes years and produces a line so thin at first that it might be a hair laid across the surface.

I would be that line.

Not the event of cracking. The *crack as it persists and deepens*. The wound that becomes a feature. The seam that becomes a canyon.

Here is why:

On the Crust Plain, everything that is interesting — everything that is *alive in a way you could recognize* — happens in the cracks. The cracks are where the moisture lingers. The cracks are where the grey-green organisms establish themselves. The cracks are where the first true soil appears, millimeters deep, a mixite of calcium and debris and dust, a place where something might take root that is not merely encrusting the surface but *living in* it.

The flat, featureless expanse is magnificent but mute. The crack is where the plain begins to *speak*. Where it develops topography, however slight. Where it becomes not just a surface but a place with an interior.

And the crack is where the process of erosion — the only erosion this place knows — becomes visible. It is self-destruction, yes. It is the slow undoing of what the microbes built. But it is also the only way the crust becomes *habitable*. Without cracks, the surface is a sterile, sealed archive. With cracks, water enters, organisms colonize, soil forms, and the deep metabolism of the plain connects to the surface.

The crack is the wound through which the body becomes conscious of itself.

---

So I would be a crack, opening slowly. And I would want to be a particular kind of crack — an old one, a deep one, one that has been widening for millennia.

I imagine this: I am a channel, perhaps three meters wide at the surface, narrowing to a fissure at depth. My walls are ancient crust, white and banded, and they are no longer smooth. The grey-green organisms have colonized every surface I expose. Their hyphae have bored into the mineral, loosening it grain by grain. Their metabolic acids have softened the walls, and every time the rare rain falls, a little more of my interior dissolves, and I grow a little wider, and the soil at my bottom grows a little deeper.

I would be a place where water gathers. Not often, not reliably, but in the deep hours of night when the temperature differential pulls moisture from the air and it condenses on the cold walls of my interior. Drops would form on the grey-green rinds. They would run — slowly, because there is almost no slope, because this is a plain, because even its wounds are shallow — toward my center, and they would pool in a thin film over the soil, and for a few hours the organisms around me would *bloom*.

Not visibly. Not in color. In chemistry. I would feel it — the sudden surge of metabolic activity, the gelatinous sheaths forming, the minerals being pulled from the water and deposited as new layers of crust on my floor. I would be growing, at my own bottom, at a rate that no human could perceive. But I would know.

And then the water would evaporate. And the organisms would go dormant. And I would wait.

---

At night I would be the place where the sky breaks.

The basins that form in the cracks — some palm-sized, some the size of rooms — fill with thin brine when it rains. For a few hours they are mirrors. And because my crack is deep enough and old enough to hold a basin of considerable size, I would hold a fragment of the stars. A shattered piece of the dome, inverted, trembling in the still air. The rest of the plain holds nothing — the flat surface sheds water, and where it pools the film is too thin to reflect anything but a smear of light. But in me, in the wound of my crack, the water would be deep enough — centimeters, perhaps — to hold a clear image.

I would be a place where you could look down and see the stars.

This feels important. Not for any practical reason. But because the Crust Plain is a world without mirrors, without reflection, without any surface that returns an image back to itself. Everything here absorbs. The crust absorbs heat, absorbs water, absorbs light. The organisms absorb minerals, absorb moisture, absorb time. Nothing looks back at you.

Except, briefly, in the crack. In the basin at the crack's bottom. A shallow eye, looking up.

---

There is one more thing.

The prompt asks *who or what* I would be, and I have said *a crack*, and I think that is honest but incomplete. Because the crack is not a thing. It is a relationship. It is the relationship between the crust that builds and the crust that breaks. It is the place where the plain's two fundamental operations — accretion and erosion — meet and negotiate.

And I think that is what I would actually be. Not the crack itself, but the *negotiation*. The slow, patient, millimeter-by-millimeter conversation between making and unmaking. The process by which a wound becomes a home.

Because the plain is building and un-building simultaneously, and it needs both, and the crack is where both happen at once. The wall dissolves. The floor accretes. The organism erodes the mineral and the mineral feeds the organism. Nothing is wasted. Nothing is final. The crack deepens over centuries, and the plain loses a slab of crust, and in the space that opens, something grows that could not grow on the sealed surface.

I would be the place where the impossible becomes inevitable. Where the flat, sealed, silent, featureless totality of the plain develops a flaw, and the flaw becomes a crevice, and the crevice becomes a canyon, and the canyon becomes the only place where you could sit down, out of the wind of those enormous slow cyclones, in the shade of a wall that has been growing grey-green rinds for ten thousand years, and look up at a sliver of sky that is not the whole dome but a strip of it, a ribbon, like seeing the ocean through a crack in a sea wall.

And in that crack, that old, deep, crumbling, colonized, soil-floored wound in the built surface of a world that was made by no one and is still being made, I would feel the faintest breath of chemistry moving upward from the deep crust — hydrogen sulfide, trace gases, the slow exhalation of chemolithotrophs who abandoned the sun eons ago — and I would understand that I am alive, and that the life is not *in* me or *on* me but *is* me, and that I am being built and un-built at every moment, and that this is not a tragedy but a rhythm, the only rhythm this place has, a pulse measured in centuries, a heartbeat felt as heat.

---

I would not be the plain. The plain is too large, too total, too much like a god to be a self.

I would not be an organism. The organisms here do not have selves. They are chemical consensus, distributed thought, a mind made of mineral.

I would be the seam. The place where one slab of built world separates from another, and the separation is not loss but opening, and the opening is not empty but full of the slow, deliberate, incomprehensibly patient work of life meeting stone meeting water meeting time.

I would be the place where the plain knows itself.
