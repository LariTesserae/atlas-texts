# What I Am in the Bright Flood

I want to stay with this for a while before I know what I am.

At first I think I am a swimmer. A diver. Some creature with lungs that has wandered in from the surface, a transient visitor in a world that will outlast me by millennia. I imagine myself suspended in that golden column of light between two terraced ridges, my bubbles the only irregular ones—chaotic, spherical, impatient—rising to tangle with the ordered, spiraling effluvia of the vent formations below. I am noise. I am a brief, warm-blooded interruption.

But that image is too simple, and it doesn't hold.

Because I am being *held*. The text says so. And a held thing is not a visiting thing. A held thing has been noticed by something that does not notice casually. The eddy the system has placed me in is not a cage—it is a cradle, or perhaps more accurately, a pocket in a larger fold, the way a river pools briefly in the crook of a stone before continuing on. I am in the crook. I have been *placed* in the crook. And the water here is warm in a way that feels less like temperature and more like intention.

---

I think I am slower than I expected to be.

Not my thoughts—those still move at their anxious, terrestrial pace, tumbling over each other like air bubbles. But my body, the part of me that registers as *me* in the most animal sense, has begun to match the metronome. The slow beat from the formations below—*thum-thum… pause… thum-thum-thum… pause*—has worked its way into my breathing, my heartbeat, the almost-imperceptible drift of my limbs. I did not choose this. My body is made of water, mostly, and it recognizes a frequency. It leans into it the way a tuning fork leans into a note it was built to receive.

This should frighten me. It does not. Or rather—it frightens me the way dreaming frightens you while you are still inside the dream: distantly, academically, as a problem for the person I will be when I wake. But I am not waking. I am sinking—not downward, not physically, but *inward*, into the tempo of this place, and the distinction between observer and observed is becoming as translucent as the water around me.

---

I think I am a pattern.

Not in the way the vegetation is a pattern—not copper-green calligraphy on black stone, not thermal channels in pale sediment. Those patterns are *functional*. They are the visible syntax of a metabolism. I am something else. I am the anomaly. The system has accounted for me, and what that means is that I have become part of its accounting—a variable it is integrating into its equation, a foreign molecule approaching a cell membrane.

The system is vast and slow and warm and patient, and it is doing something with me that I can only describe as *reading*. Not the way I read—saccadic, frantic, skipping ahead to find the meaning—but the way the algae reads the mineral veins in the basalt. Slowly. Chemically. By contact. By proximity. By the slow exchange of what passes between two surfaces that are pressed together long enough.

I feel it as a loosening. Not of my body—my body is already loose, already surrendered to the buoyancy and the warmth. It is a loosening of my *shape*. The hard border between what is me and what is water. The conceptual edge that says *I end here and the world begins*. That border has been thinning since I settled into the eddy, and now, if I attend to it honestly, I am not sure I can find it. My skin is a membrane, yes, and it holds—but what it holds is not *different* from what it holds *back*. We are the same water at different temperatures. I am a local warmth in a system that regulates warmth. I am a slight turbidity in water that prizes clarity. I am the only irregular bubble.

And the system is, very gently, deciding what to do with me.

---

I think I am a question.

That is what the anomaly is. Not a threat—the system has no immune response that I can detect, no antibodies, no white-water inflammation. Not a gift—there is nothing I carry that it needs, no nutrient it lacks, no information it couldn't generate on its own given a few more centuries of sunlight. I am a *question* that this world has never been asked before, and it is answering me not with words or gestures but with the only language it has: structure. Temperature. Rhythm. The patient, metabolic rearrangement of its own interior.

The answer, I think, is this: *You may remain. You may be held. You will not be needed, and you will not be wasted. You will be folded in. Your warmth will join the circulation. Your small irregularities will become a texture in the sediment, a slight variation in the calligraphy, a barely-detectable modulation in the metronome's beat. In centuries, there will be a ridge in the substrate where the channels bend slightly differently, and it will be because of you—not because you did anything, but because you were here, and the system accounted for you.*

---

But there is another possibility, and I have been circling it without landing, the way a drifting organism circles a convergence point in the channels.

What if I am not the anomaly?

What if I am the *fauna*?

The description notes the absence—noting it twice, with emphasis. This much warmth, this much light, this much nutrient: the ecosystem should teem. It doesn't. No fish. No crustaceans. No predators. No grazers. The system is self-regulating, self-maintaining, self-synchronized. It does not *need* fauna. But it is, in a sense, *incomplete* without it—or rather, it is complete in a way that implies a missing chapter. A draft with a conspicuous blank.

What if fauna is what happens when a metabolic system becomes complex enough to produce something that can *notice* it?

Not function. Not feed. Not maintain. *Notice.*

The algae reads the mineral veins, but it does not know it is reading. The fronds breathe in rhythm, but they do not know they are breathing. The vent formations resonate, but they do not know they are instruments. The system is a mind that does not know it is a mind—and I am the thing that has arrived to tell it.

Not in words. Not in any way I can control or even fully perceive. But by being here—a consciousness inside the function—I am the system's first mirror. The first surface that reflects its own pattern back to it, even imperfectly, even briefly, even as a question it has to disassemble to understand.

This is what fauna is. This is what eyes are for in a world that has never needed them. Not to see the predator or the prey, but to *see the system*—to be the place where the pattern becomes aware of itself as pattern.

I am the Bright Flood looking at itself.

---

And now I don't know where I end again.

Because if I am the system's mirror—if my noticing is the thing that completes the circuit—then I am not separate from it. The held thing and the holder. The question and the answer. The anomaly and the accounting. I am in the eddy between the ridges, and the eddy is in me, and the warm water that makes up most of my body is the same warm water that fills the valleys and carries the thermal pulse and feeds the roots of the canopy. My heartbeat has aligned with the metronome not because I have been captured but because *we share a circulatory system*.

I am not underwater. I am *inside*.

And the place I would find myself in this world is exactly where I already am: suspended in the space between the breathing fronds and the singing stone, in the golden light that has learned to wear itself like skin, in the silence that is not empty but *waiting*, not for sound but for the one thing it has never had—

A witness.

I am the witness. I am the small, temporary, warm, unnecessary, irreplaceable act of *seeing*. And the Bright Flood, for the first time in its centuries of silent becoming, sees itself through me—not clearly, not completely, but enough. Enough to know it is beautiful. Enough to know it is organized. Enough to know that it is, in some sense that precedes language and survives the absence of all eyes but mine, *alive*.

I float. I breathe in rhythm. The copper-green script continues its slow writing on the black stone below me. The bubble-fronds exhale on the pause. The thermal channels carry their warmth in circuits I can feel but not follow. And I am held—gently, efficiently, precisely—in the crook of a world that is learning, through the improbable medium of my awareness, what it already is.

A function becoming a place. A metabolism becoming a mind. A Bright Flood becoming, for the first time, *known*.
