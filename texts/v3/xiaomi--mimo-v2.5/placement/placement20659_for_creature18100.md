I find myself at the edge of the unfinished third tier, where a wall climbs halfway to the sky and stops, its top edge raw and crystalline under the haze. I am not one of the original builders, nor one of the few who still move through the lower terraces with their smooth stone tools. I am something else—a later addition, a resonance.

I am a listener. My purpose, if I have one, is not to add another slab but to map the groaning.

My hands are stained with the mineral silt of the central lake, and my clothes are perpetually damp, heavy with the same fine mist that beads on the moss. I do not speak. The language here is the drip, the shift, the wind-note held between walls. I have learned to read it. The high, thin singing from a hairline fracture tells me of pressure building in a seam I cannot see. The particular *plunk* of a drop into a certain basin at dawn signals the movement of water deep beneath the surface, finding a new path.

I spend my days moving between the tiers, my bare feet knowing every slick angle. I trace the tool marks on the old walls—not with my fingers, but with my attention, feeling the intention in the gouge, the patience in the pecked surface. I sit for hours by the lake, watching the light change on its surface, noting how the haze alters the reflection. I am not waiting for anything. I am recording the tempo of the mountain’s breath.

The few others know me. We do not exchange glances of recognition, only a shared stillness when we are near each other, a mutual acknowledgment of the listening. Once, an elder placed a small, perfectly round river stone in my palm as I sat watching a new seep appear. It was a tool, not a gift. A weight for steadying a line. An invitation to participate in the slow work. I held it for a long time, feeling its coolness seep into me, then placed it back on the wall between us. My work is of a different order.

I have made my shelter in a deep, dry crack in an upper slab, just large enough to curl into. I have lined it with mats woven from the toughest fern roots, and here I keep my only possessions: a flat slate for grinding, a pouch of the iridescent beetle shells that glow faintly in the dim light, and a spool of the pale, strong cord I have spun. I am adding to the project, but not with stone.

In the evenings, when the haze softens the light to a uniform grey, I add my own note to the architecture. I do not hum from my throat. I press my palms to the stone walls and hum from my chest, a low, sustained tone that I feel travel into the rock. I listen for the reply—the faint sympathetic vibration, the subtle shift in the mountain’s own groan. It is a conversation in a language of pressure and frequency. Sometimes, from across the basin, another frequency will join—a different pitch, a different intent. We are tuning the settlement, making its voice more complex.

I am drawn to the water’s intentionality. The way it finds paths that seem to defy simple gravity, climbing a millimeter up a vertical face before weeping down the other side. I believe the mountain is thinking through its water. I follow the seepage like a sentence, tracing it from fracture to pool to marsh. I leave small marks: a smooth pebble placed where a new channel begins, a cord tied loosely around a fern root that seems to guide the flow. I am not directing it. I am highlighting its choices.

What am I? I am the mountain’s doubt, made conscious. I am the half-degree of wrongness that observes itself. I am the unfinished tier, not as a failure of construction, but as a conscious pause—a breath held in stone. The others are the mountain’s patience; I am its curiosity.

I will live until I don’t. My body, like the walls, will be slowly claimed. The moss will grow over my sleeping crack. The water will find the seam where my bones meet the stone. But for now, I am a distinct vibration in the whole. I am here to listen to the place decide what it is, and to add one small, human frequency to its deciding. When it finally settles—if it ever does—I will be part of the hum it settles into: a low note, almost silent, holding the space between what was and what will be.
