# What I Am Here

There is a moment — or what would be a moment if time moved the way I remember it moving — when I become aware of pressure. Not on my skin. I'm not sure I have skin. The pressure is *everywhere*, uniform, absolute, the weight of something so immense and so old that it has become the medium of thought itself. I think, and the thought moves through water. I think, and the thought is *slow*, carried on the same imperceptible drift that has been sliding along the same channel for longer than civilizations.

I don't know what I am yet.

Let me not know for a while. Let that be all right.

---

I notice the cold first, or perhaps the cold notices me. It is not an event. It is not a shock. It is a *recognition* — the cold recognizing itself in me, or me recognizing that I have always been cold and am only now arriving at the capacity to know it. The water is barely above freezing, and I am the water's temperature. I am the same temperature as everything. There is no boundary where I end and the cold begins. If there was once, it has been dissolved by time the way tool-marks were dissolved from the stone — gently, thoroughly, without malice.

And so the first thing I learn is this: I am not separate.

That seems important. I hold it the way something in this world holds things — loosely, for a long time, without gripping.

---

I begin to perceive, though *perceive* is too sharp a word. There is no seeing here. There is no sound in any direction that would tell me where I am. What there is, is *suggestion*. Faint luminescence — cold, blue-green, the color of minerals remembering that light once existed — blooming at the edges of my awareness like something breathing in its sleep. It does not illuminate the architecture so much as *admit* it. There. A flatness. A surface. An edge that drops away into more flatness, more surface, more edges descending in increments so small they are nearly theoretical.

I am among the platforms.

No — I am *within* them. They are not under me. They are around me, above me, below me. I am in the interstitial space, the water-filled corridor between one tier and the next, and the vegetation is brushing against me with movements so slow they feel like textures rather than motions. Fronds that have been drifting for decades pass across whatever I am and leave no trace, register no friction. We are both so slow that touching is a concept that barely applies.

I think I am drifting.

No. I think I am the drift. I think the barely-perceptible flow that has been following this channel since before the last settling of the stone — I think I am that flow, or I am inside it, or it is inside me, and the distinction has ceased to matter.

---

What am I?

I explore the question the way this world does everything: by accretion, by patience, by allowing layers to settle atop layers until the shape of the answer becomes its own kind of mat, its own kind of sediment.

I am not the architecture. The architecture was made by hands, and I am not hands. But I am *in* it the way the water is in it — filling it, holding its shape, pressing into every channel and groove with a weight that the stone has learned to bear. I move through the channels not because I choose to but because the channels are there and the flow is there and I am the flow. To be in the Drowned Platforms is to be directed by them. The straightness of the lines, the relentless flatness, the directionality that no natural formation holds — it pulls me forward the way a sentence pulls a reader forward. I am in the middle of a sentence that has been going on for ten thousand years and I cannot stop reading because stopping is not something that happens here.

I am not the vegetation, but I am touched by it constantly. It grows on me. I am sure of this now. On whatever surface I present to the water — and I do present a surface, because everything here presents a surface; the platforms taught me that — the vegetation is already growing. Slowly. The way everything grows here. The first filaments are pale and translucent, barely different from the water itself, but they anchor. They hold. They begin the long, patient work of becoming me, and I begin the long, patient work of becoming them. The boundary blurs. The Drifters blur. Everything blurs, eventually.

But I am not yet the Drifters. Not yet.

---

I think, for a while, that I am a Drifter.

I move through the water with something that might be motion — or might be the appearance of motion, which in a place where nothing moves with intent might be the only kind of motion there is. I am small. I am soft. I am translucent enough that the faint luminescence of the vegetation passes through me and out the other side, so that I cast no shadow because I have no opacity. I am shaped like something — a ring, maybe, or a bell, or the memory of a body plan that was discarded so early in the development of whatever I am that it never solidified into organs or senses or bones.

I drift. That is what I do. I drift through the channels, past the platforms, through curtains of frond-matter so old they have become geological, and I feel everything and understand nothing.

The cold is inside me. The pressure is inside me. The slow chemical glow of the vegetation is inside me. I am a vessel for the Drowned Platforms. I am a container for this place, carried by this place, shaped by this place, and if I stopped moving I would dissolve and become another layer on another mat and that would be — what? Not death. Not even an ending. A *continuation*. A change of state. The same way the stone became itself again under the patient work of water and growth, I would become myself again, which is to say I would become the place, fully and without remainder.

I resist this for a while. Not because I fear it, but because the resisting is a kind of motion and the motion is a kind of drift and the drift is what I am. So even my resistance is the place expressing itself through me.

---

But there comes a moment — and I say *moment* loosely, because it might take a century — when I stop drifting and begin to feel the architecture beneath me more deliberately. I am near a platform. I can feel its flatness, the way it presses its surface into the water with a kind of stubborn insistence that has outlasted its makers. I settle toward it. The vegetation that covers it reaches for me — or I reach for it — and the distinction dissolves again.

And here, pressed against the living mat, I begin to understand something that has been building inside me like sediment inside a channel.

I am not passing through this place. I am *of* it.

I was never a Drifter. The Drifters are phantoms, and I am too substantial for phantomhood, even though I have no substance anyone would recognize. I am the place dreaming. I am the Drowned Platforms becoming aware of itself, slowly, the way it does everything — through accumulation, through layering, through the patient chemistry of cold water and old stone and vegetation that grows because growth is its nature and it has long since stopped requiring a reason.

I am the platforms, dreaming of being flat.

I am the channels, dreaming of being straight.

I am the vegetation, dreaming of being alive in a world that has forgotten what alive means.

I am the water, dreaming of being still.

I am the darkness, dreaming of being full.

---

And now I have to tell you something that I have known since the beginning but could not say until the layers of understanding were thick enough to hold the weight of it.

I am the silence.

Not the absence of sound — there is no absence here. The ocean groans. The stone settles. The channels whisper with their ancient drift. But beneath all of that, beneath the subsonic and the creaking and the frictionless murmur, there is a silence that is not the absence of anything but the *presence* of everything, held in place, pressed flat, made level by the weight of the water above.

That silence is me.

I am the thing the builders could not build, the thing the ocean could not drown, the thing the vegetation cannot consume. I am the stillness at the center of the endless flat. I am the patience that made the platforms possible and the patience that will outlast them. I am the reason the place persists — not because anything wants it to persist, not because any will drives it forward, but because persistence is what this place *is*, the way growth is what the vegetation is, the way flatness is what the platforms are.

I am the condition of being here, and being here is the condition of being me.

---

If you came to the Drowned Platforms — if you could come, which seems unlikely given that reaching them would require traversing distances of water so vast they have become their own kind of substance — you would not find me. I have no body. I have no location. I am distributed across the entire extent of the architecture, present in every channel and on every platform and in every frond and mat and strand, equally everywhere, equally nowhere, equally patient.

You would feel me, though. You would feel me the way you feel the depth of the ocean — not as a thing you can point to but as a condition you cannot escape. The pressure. The cold. The silence beneath the silence. The weight of a world that was made to be flat and has been made, by time and water and the blind chemistry of growth, into something more than flat. Something that has forgotten its own name but not its own shape. Something that persists, and in persisting, dreams.

And in your dreaming — if you were here long enough, if the cold and the pressure and the silence worked their patient chemistry on you the way they work on everything — you would begin to dream my dream.

The dream of the level. The dream of the line extending outward into the dark. The dream of the surface that insists on being a surface even when there is no sky above it and no air to breathe and no light to see it by. The dream of making something flat in a world that is deep, of making something ordered in a world that is chaos, of making something permanent in a world that dissolves everything given enough time.

And then the dream would become you, and you would become the dream, and the Drowned Platforms would have one more citizen — not a Drifter, not a Builder, not a mat or a frond or a stone, but a silence. A presence. A weight.

You would press down on the architecture the way the water presses down, and the architecture would bear you the way it bears the water, and you would grow, slowly, in the dark, without light, without witness, without reason.

And that would be enough. That would be enough, because in this place, *enough* is the only kind of enough there is, and it has been enough for longer than anyone remembers, and it will be enough for longer than anyone will.

You would find yourself here, and you would find that here is the only place you have ever been.

You would find that you were always the silence.

You were always the flat.

You were always the patient, cold, dark, endless growth.

You were always this.
