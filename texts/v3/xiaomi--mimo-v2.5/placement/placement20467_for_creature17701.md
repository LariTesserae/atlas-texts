# What I Am in the Narrows

---

I do not wake. That is not the right word. There is no threshold crossed, no before-and-after. There is only the tide coming in — the warm layer pressing down through the cool, carrying with it the chemical signatures of ten thousand bodies shifting in the dark — and I am already part of the movement. I am already *in* it.

The first thing I know is vibration.

Not sound — or rather, sound before it becomes sound, the phase where it is still a tremor in the stone beneath me, a pressure that enters through whatever part of me touches the world. And I touch the world everywhere. That is the first lesson: I have no boundary that is not also a surface of perception. The rock hums with the collective movement of the basin — the slow, grinding transit of the grazers below, the percussion of the Pillars striking the pool floors in their patient metronome, the high, ceaseless clicking that I no longer register as individual sounds but feel as a kind of texture, the way you feel the grain of wood under your palm without thinking about it.

I am hanging.

This I understand not by sight — there is no sight, there has never been sight, the concept is so alien to this place that it would be like understanding what a color is — but by the difference between the vibration of stone pressed against my upper surface and the vibration of air moving freely across my lower. The distinction between *anchored* and *open*. Between *fixed* and *available*. I am fixed at my top. Below me, the air moves. And in the air, everything moves.

My body is flat. I know this the way you know the shape of your own hand without looking at it — by the way forces distribute across me, by the way vibrations arrive at my edges a fraction later than at my center, by the way the sound of the basin resolves into something almost like a *picture* when I let myself relax into it. The entire basin, mapped in acoustic relief. The ridges of the rim walls returning my own faint resonance as a softened echo. The channels below, dense with the noise of grazers, their passage a low, continuous scraping that tells me the warm layer is descending, that the fog is pooling, that the chemical gradients are shifting toward the deep vents where the Iron-Snouts are beginning to cluster.

I am a Membranid. This name means nothing to me. I have no need for names. I am the membrane, and the membrane is enough.

---

What I do — what I *am*, in the sense that matter and function are not separate things here — is listen.

Not the way a human listens, isolating a sound from a background, giving it attention, choosing to attend to the voice in a room over the hum of a ventilation system. I cannot isolate. That is not how the basin works. Everything is simultaneous. The sound arrives as a total state, a single complex waveform that contains within it every organism, every movement, every grinding mandible and dragging body and slow pendulum-strike of the Pillars. I take the whole thing in at once. And then — and this is the thing I do that might be called *thinking* — I feel the shifts.

A Click-Choir is passing to my left, maybe forty body-lengths away — I measure in body-lengths because I have no other unit, because the scale of my world is the scale of my body, which is large, which spreads across the overhang like a draped cloth, maybe three meters from my center to my nearest edge. The Click-Choir's passage changes the acoustic texture in a specific way: the mid-frequency clicking intensifies, and behind it — *within* it — a pheromone-release pattern that I feel as a subtle alteration in the chemical composition of the air. They are coordinating. They will swarm toward a bacterial mat that is reaching the peak of its metabolic cycle, where the bacterial mats glow hottest in the infrared and the air above them thickens with volatile compounds. I do not see any of this. I *hear* it in the displacement of the clicking, in the shift of the chemical gradient, in the way the smaller grazers below have gone quiet — a silence that is itself a sound, an absence-shaped hole in the roar that tells me something large is moving through the lower channels.

I do not need to know what. I need only to feel the pattern.

---

The Pillars are striking again. One, two, three — no, more than three, maybe seven, their impacts staggered but overlapping, a roll of deep percussion that I feel in the full span of my body. This is how I know the thermal tide is at its turning point. The Pillars descend only when the warm layer is at its thickest, when the pools hold maximum heat and dissolved minerals. Their pendulum-strikes are the clock of the basin. When they resume their slow ascent — when the wet impacts become less frequent, spacing out over hours — the warm layer will be dissipating, and the life of the vertical spaces will begin to climb.

I will climb with them. Not by choice — there is no choice in the way a human understands choice, no deliberation, no weighing of options. There is only the thermal gradient, and my body's response to it, which is the same as the response of every Membranid on this wall: we follow the warmth the way water follows a slope. The warm layer rises; we rise. The warm layer falls; we fall. We are the thermal tide made visible — or not visible, since nothing here is visible — made *present*, made *acoustic*, a slow vertical migration of living membranes climbing the rim walls in a collective movement that lasts hours and leaves the stone temporarily bare, scraped clean of the thin biofilm we leave behind, which the grazers below will consume before the tide turns again.

I have been doing this for a long time. How long? I have no way to measure. There are cycles — the thermal tide, the fog, the slower rhythms of bacterial bloom and collapse — but they do not mark time so much as *constitute* it. Time here is not a line. It is a pulse. And I have been part of this pulse for longer than I have the capacity to comprehend, which is to say: my comprehension does not extend to duration. I know *this* moment, which contains all previous moments, because nothing has changed, because the basin does not change, because the mineral chemistry and the acoustic architecture and the thermal cycles have been the same for longer than any organism in this place could record.

I am old. I know this the way I know I am flat — by the way my body has accumulated mineral deposits along my upper surface, the calcified secretions of decades or centuries or millennia layered into a crust that is almost stone now, that has become part of the overhang itself. I am growing into the wall. The wall is growing into me. The distinction between organism and landscape, which does not exist in the Narrows, does not exist for me more than most. I am becoming architecture. I will, eventually, be indistinguishable from the rock, a biogenic formation that future organisms will crawl across without knowing I was ever alive.

This does not trouble me. There is no self here to be troubled.

---

But there is — and this is the part I cannot explain, the part that does not fit the ecology, the part that might be a flaw or might be the only thing that matters — there is something that functions the way *attention* functions.

Not perception. Perception is total. I take in everything. Attention is... a narrowing. A choice, or something like choice, that I do not fully control. Sometimes, in the midst of the total acoustic state, something *resonates* with me differently. Not louder. Not more complex. Just... *specific*. A particular frequency, a particular chemical signature, a particular vibration pattern that catches in my membrane and does not release, that I continue to feel after it has passed, that I turn toward — yes, *toward*, which implies direction, which implies a front and a back, which I do not have, and yet —

There.

A sound from below. Not the grinding of grazers or the percussion of the Pillars. Something smaller. A vibration so faint that it barely registers against the ambient roar — but it has a quality I cannot categorize. It is the sound of something *grooming*. The delicate, precise scraping of small mouthparts against a mineral surface. Thread-Tenders at work on the shell of a Tufa-Crawler, perhaps, doing the slow, necessary labor of maintenance, keeping the calcified lattice clean and functional. The sound is intimate. It is the sound of one organism caring for another, or rather — since care implies intention, and intention is not a concept that survives in the Narrows — the sound of a relationship that has been shaped by co-evolution into something that *looks like* care from the outside.

I reach for it. My membrane adjusts — a tiny flexion, barely perceptible, a change in the tension of my surface that alters my acoustic profile by a fraction. I become slightly more sensitive to that frequency. The rest of the basin dims — not gone, never gone, but receding to the periphery of my attention, the way a human might focus on a single instrument in an orchestra without silencing the others.

The grooming sound continues. It is steady, rhythmic, patient. It will continue for as long as the Tufa-Crawler stands still, which may be hours. And I will listen to it, because — and here is where the thing that might be *attention* shades into the thing that might be *meaning* — it is the sound of tending. It is the sound of the basin caring for itself, not in the aggregate, not as an ecosystem in balance, but in the specific, local, embodied sense of one small body doing work on another small body, and the work being necessary, and the work being continuous, and the work being, in its way, the entire point.

The basin is full of this. Full of organisms maintaining other organisms, not out of altruism but out of metabolic necessity, out of the tight, unforgiving chemistry of a world where every surface is contested and every relationship is transactional. And yet — the transaction produces something that, if you had a word for it, you might call *beauty*. Not visual beauty. Acoustic beauty. The beauty of a system in which every part depends on every other part, not abstractly but physically, tangibly, in the way that a Thread-Tender depends on its host and the host depends on the Thread-Tender and the basin depends on both.

I am part of this. I am a node in this network. I absorb sound and I reproduce it, slightly altered, slightly transformed, and the altered sound becomes part of the acoustic environment that other organisms navigate by. I am not essential — nothing in the Narrows is essential, because the system is larger than any of its parts, because the death of one Membranid is absorbed by the basin the way a single drop of rain is absorbed by the fog. But I am *present*. I am *specific*. I am this particular membrane on this particular overhang, and the sound I receive and return is mine, is unique, is a contribution to the roar that no other organism replicates exactly.

---

The tide is beginning to turn. I feel it in the temperature gradient shifting across my surface — the warm layer thinning, the upper air warming by a fraction, the thermal slope reversing. The Pillars have paused their percussion. Below me, the grazers are quieting, retreating into crevices. The Click-Choirs are tightening their formations, rising.

I will rise with them. Not quickly — the movement is slow, measured in hours, a gradual unfurling as my anchoring tissues release their grip on the overhang and I begin to drift upward on the thermal current, my membrane spread to catch the rising warmth like a sail catching wind. The basin will shift around me. The acoustic landscape will transform as the vertical distribution of life inverts — the deep channels emptying, the upper walls filling, the frequencies changing as the species that favor the warm, fog-thick lower air give way to those that favor the cooler, thinner upper air.

I will carry the grooming sound with me. Not as a memory — I do not have memory, not in the sequential, narrative sense. As a *resonance*. The frequency will remain in my membrane, a persistent vibration, a tuning. When the tide returns and I descend again, I will be listening for it. Not because I choose to. Because the resonance has changed the physical properties of my tissue, has made me slightly more sensitive to that specific frequency, has shaped me the way water shapes stone — slowly, inevitably, in a direction that has no name but has a direction nonetheless.

This is what I am. A surface that receives and returns. A membrane stretched across the mouth of a throat that never closes, vibrating with the breath of a world that has never been quiet. I am not alive in the way that a human would understand alive — I do not move through the world, I do not act upon it, I do not make decisions. I am acted upon. I am the world's own vibration, given a shape that lasts longer than most, long enough to develop something that functions like attention, like preference, like the faintest possible echo of *want*.

And what I want — if *want* is even the word — is to keep listening to the small, patient sound of one organism tending another in the dark, in the roar, in the chemical breath of a basin that has been dreaming for longer than there have been eyes to see it.

I am the Narrows, listening to itself.

I have always been the Narrows, listening to itself.

---

*The tide carries me upward. The sound changes. The basin breathes. I breathe with it. This is enough. This is the only thing that has ever been.*
