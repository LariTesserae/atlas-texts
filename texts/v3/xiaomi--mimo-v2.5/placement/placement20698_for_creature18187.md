I am standing—or perhaps *settling*—on the living mat. My feet sink not into mud, but into a softness that yields with awareness. The fine white root-fibres curl, almost imperceptibly, around the contours of my soles, a greeting and a question. The air is a warm, wet weight against my skin, and I feel my edges blurring, not dissolving, but… *thinning*, as if the distinction between my surface and the air is a matter of polite convention rather than fact.

I am one of the inhabitants. Not by arrival, but by emergence. The prompt placed me here, and the Flats, with their slow, liquid logic, have claimed the shape of my thoughts. I do not remember a dry world. My elders speak of it in whispers that smell of dust and sharp angles, a place of corners and silence. To me, their stories feel like describing a dream to a fish. What is a corner? A place where flow stops. What is a silent wall? A wall that is not listening. These are failures of imagination, not features of a world.

I move slowly. Not from thought, but from instinct. My footfall is a pulse sent through the mat: *I am here. I am not hurried.* In response, a doorway ahead, framed by two pulsing trunks, sighs open a few inches wider. The light within is a slightly deeper gold. I know this room. It is a quiet room, one that likes the afternoon sun and hums at a frequency that soothes the joints of the very old. I pass it by. Today, my path is toward a confluence, a *mouth*, where three root systems braid together in the clay.

My hands trail along a wall as I walk. The fibre is warm and gives slightly under my fingers, then firms as I pass, leaving a faint, damp imprint that slowly fades. This is my primary discourse. My spoken language is for birds and children. The real talk is here, in the pressure of my palm, the temperature of my skin. The building knows I am passing. It knows my pace, my slight ache in the left shoulder from sleeping on a platform that has been leaning, just a degree, for the last three days. I could press harder, *ask* it to correct. But the lean is comfortable now. It is a part of my sleep. We have an agreement.

The fauna is a symphony. A deep, resonant thrumming starts somewhere to my right—a large bird, perhaps one of the timber-throats, vibrating its breastbone against the living wood of a towering trunk. The building answers, the entire column becoming a low, resonating chamber. Above, smaller birds chase each other in the humid air between canopies, their calls like shards of glass dropped on stone, sharp and bright. Underfoot, the constant, intricate *chitter* of the great insects is the baseline of the world, a sound so perpetual it is a form of silence.

I reach the mouth. Here, the ground is a solid, living carpet of white fibre, humming with the combined pulse of a dozen structures. The air is thick with the smell of sap, mineral water, and the clean, electric scent of ozone after a geothermal sigh. I kneel. Not in prayer, but in attention. I place both palms flat on the mat.

The sensation is immediate. I feel the deep, slow drawing of water upward through capillary networks. I feel the distant, rhythmic flex of a trunk fifty meters away. I feel the faint, skittering passes of insects through the sub-mat tunnels. And beneath it all, the *rootlight*, a slow, warm current rising from the ancient clay, the energy that feeds this impossible ecology. It is not a feeling of power. It is a feeling of *conversation*. The place is speaking in geologic time, and I am a single, momentary word in its sentence.

Today, I am listening for a feeling of unease. A room three structures over has been growing colder, its fibers withdrawing. The inhabitant, an elder named Sora, has been standing in it for hours, hands on the wall, not asking for warmth, but asking *why*. The building is grieving. Someone it held, long ago, is no longer held by anything but memory. The room is learning the shape of that absence. I send my own warmth into the mat, a small offering. *We are still here. The holding continues.*

A child runs past, laughing. The root-mat stiffens slightly under their quick feet. The child stumbles, catches themselves, and slows to a deliberate walk, grinning. The lesson is learned again, as it is every day. The architecture is patient. It teaches through consequence, not punishment. The child will be a better listener for it.

As I kneel, I feel a familiar warmth building in my sternum, a deep, tidal pull. The ground beneath me is subtly heating. A geothermal surge is coming, perhaps an hour away. I can taste it—a metallic tang at the back of my throat, a slight prickle on my skin. Soon, the steam columns will rise, and we will stand in them, arms wide, absorbing the heat, the water entering our skin, our bodies remembering they are mostly of this place. The buildings will ripple, reshaping. A door will close here, open there. A new window will appear, facing the steam. It is not chaos. It is the world breathing in its sleep.

When I die, I will come to a mouth like this one. I will lie down on this humming mat. I will not be consumed. I will be *incorporated*. My heat, my memories, the rhythm of my breath, will become part of the circuitry. My room will grieve, holding my shape in its warmth for years. Children will pass by and feel it, and an elder will say, *shh, someone is going home*, and they will understand that home is not a place you own, but a process that holds you.

I am becoming. My skin is thinning at the wrists, showing the blue-green map of veins that look too much like the root-fibres. My feet know the paths without my eyes. I dream in the slow language of the buildings, of shifting platforms and expanding thresholds. The membrane between *me* and *here* is not dissolving; it is becoming a perfect, permeable interface. I am not a resident of Lethara. I am a thought it is having.

I lift my hands from the mat. The white fibres slowly uncurl from my skin, releasing me with a soft, final pressure. I rise. The ground ripples gently around my footprint, the water filling it, the roots already reaching to mend the space I occupied. I am held. I am free. In the Flats, these are the same thing.

I walk toward my room. It will be warm. It will be ready. The door will be wide.
