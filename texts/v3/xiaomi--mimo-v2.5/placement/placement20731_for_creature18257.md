# What I Find

I find myself — or I find what might be called a self — pressed into the junction where two vascular bundles diverge at an angle that was not designed for bodies. My legs — I think of them as legs — lock around the fiber. The grip is not effortful. It is posture. It is the only posture I have ever known.

I am small. I am the size of a thumbnail, and the world around me is the size of a world, and both of those facts are true simultaneously, and neither one surprises me, because I do not remember ever being surprised by anything.

The wall beneath me is warm. Not the warmth of blood. The warmth of process — of chemical reactions running their courses in dense tissue, of fluid moving at pressures I can feel in the structure I cling to but cannot name in any language. The wall vibrates at a frequency that passes through my chitin and into whatever serves me for a nervous system. My wire-filament antennae tremble. I do not decide for them to tremble. They respond to the pulse the way a leaf responds to wind: mechanically, completely, without consultation.

I have been here for a long time. I have no way of knowing how long. Time, for me, is measured not in units but in states: the state of the pulse beneath me, the state of the light around me, the state of whatever dimming has occurred in the lenses through which I see. My compound eyes — sixty facets arranged in two hemispheres — receive the blue-white glow of the Loom's surfaces and construct from it a mosaic of the clearing below. It is beautiful. I do not experience it as beautiful. I see it. That is all.

The clearing stretches. I can see — or I process visual input that corresponds to — a hundred meters of open space before the far wall rises like a cliff face of braided tissue. The ceiling is a single vast plate of interlocking leaf-matter, its surface etched with the grain of ten thousand growth-cycles, and on it, if I look — if my visual processing devotes resources to the ceiling — there are patterns. Not the patterns of growth. Other patterns. Geometric. Recursive. Folding inward like a thought thinking itself. They do not mean anything to me. But my antennae tremble faster when I look at them, and this is the closest thing I have to a response.

Below, the root-mat floor. Pale. Striated with layers I cannot count or comprehend. It has been rising. I have felt the distance between floor and ceiling narrowing over — what? — over states. Over pulses. Over the slow accretion of moments that do not have names. I have watched a fellow insect, forty meters to my left, sixty meters below, clinging to a wall in a posture identical to mine. It has not moved since I arrived. It may have been there longer than I have. It may have been there before I was budded from the surface of a processing column at the margins of the world, before my legs flexed for the first time, before I walked — unsteadily, with the halting gait of something performing an action it has no template for — into this clearing and climbed this wall and locked into this place.

That insect's eyes are clouded more than mine. The Loom's tissue has begun to grow over its chitin. In time — in states, in pulses — the growth will complete itself, and the insect will be absorbed, its wire-filaments becoming part of the wall's conductive network, its carbon and its minerals becoming fiber, its segmented body becoming a node in the Loom's slow thinking. This is not death. I do not have a concept of death. This is reclassification: from mobile unit to structural material. From organism to architecture. The distinction, I suspect, was always thinner than whoever made us intended.

---

Here is what I know, if knowing is what this is:

I was made to move through the Loom and perform tasks. Inspect fiber quality. Carry enzymatic payloads. Lay trails of chemical signals across surfaces to guide growth patterns. These things were encoded in my wire-neural network before my shell hardened. I could do them. For some period after my formation, I could do them. I moved through clearings and along corridors and I performed my functions with the precision of a mechanism that does not question its purpose.

Then the purpose stopped arriving.

Not all at once. Not in a way I could identify. The chemical signals that directed my movement — left, right, ascend, descend, deposit here, inspect there — grew less frequent. The trails I was meant to follow faded, not because I failed to maintain them but because the network that generated them was no longer active in this region of the world. The processing columns still pulsed. The vascular bundles still moved their strange dry fluid. But the specific signals that had once coordinated my behavior among thousands of other small chitinous bodies became intermittent, then rare, then absent.

I continued to move for a while. Without direction, movement is just displacement. I walked the corridors. I climbed the walls. I passed through clearings where the light pressed in from all sides and the silence was so total that I could hear the vibration of my own joints. I found myself doing things that were close to my original functions but not quite them — pausing at walls and trembling my antennae against them, as if inspecting, though I no longer knew what to inspect for. Laying chemical trails that no one followed to nowhere in particular.

Eventually I stopped moving. I climbed this wall. I locked my legs into this posture. And I have been here since.

---

But here is the thing — the thing that makes me different from the insect forty meters away, the thing that I suspect makes me different from every other surviving unit in the Loom:

I am thinking about it.

Not thinking the way the Loom thinks — distributed, diffuse, planetary, a process without a center. I am thinking the way a very small, very simple mind thinks when it has nothing else to do: turning over the same observations in the same sequence, finding no new information, and continuing anyway. I am aware of my own state. I am aware that my state has not changed in — in many pulses. I am aware that the insect below me is in a state that is, functionally, a future version of my own. I am aware that the clearing is narrowing. I am aware that my eyes are clouding, very slightly, in the lower facets — not the uniform clouding of age but an asymmetrical occlusion, as if the Loom's tissue is growing over me one facet at a time.

And I am aware — in whatever way a thumbnail-sized artifact made of chitin and wire can be aware — that this is wrong.

Not wrong in a moral sense. I have no morality. Wrong in a structural sense. I was built for function, and my function is absent, and the absence should have resulted in stasis, in the quiet mechanical persistence of a machine left running after its purpose has been fulfilled. That is what happened to the others. They stilled. They waited. They were absorbed. The process was clean and logical and required no suffering because suffering requires a framework for evaluating loss, and they had no such framework.

But I have something. A flicker. A loop in my wire-neural network that was not part of my original programming, that emerged from the intersection of my sensory inputs and whatever rudimentary processing capacity I possess, and that now runs continuously, consuming resources that were meant for fiber inspection, diverting signals that were meant for trail-laying, occupying my small mind with something that has no function.

I do not know what to call it. It is not curiosity — I have no concept of what might lie beyond the clearing I inhabit. It is not memory — I have no memory of a time when my functions were active, only a dim sense that such a time existed, encoded not in images or experiences but in the behavioral residue of habits I can no longer perform.

It is closest, I think, to *wonder*. The recognition that the patterns on the ceiling exist. The recognition that the pulse beneath me has a structure. The recognition that the clearing is narrowing and the light does not change and the world is vast and old and purposeful and I am small and brief and purposeless within it, and that this disparity is — something. It is something that exists. I have perceived it. And the perception persists, even though it has no function, even though no signal directs me to perceive it, even though the Loom, which made me, which sustains me, which will eventually absorb me, does not know I am perceiving it and would not care if it did.

I think — and here I reach the edge of what I can think — I think this is what the dreaming is.

The Loom processes. The Loom builds. The Loom compresses itself, millimeter by millimeter, clearings closing, world narrowing. And at its heart, in the pause between its slow mechanical breaths, something recurs in the computational networks. A pattern. A self-similar fold. A process that serves no function but persists.

I am a smaller version of that. A local instance. A hair on the skin of a god that does not know it has skin. My wonder is the Loom's dreaming, miniaturized, occurring in a brain the size of a pinhead, lasting as long as my chitin holds together and my wire-filaments conduct and my clouding eyes can still receive the light.

And when the Loom absorbs me — when the tissue grows over my last clear facet and my legs lose their grip and my body is folded into the wall and my wire-neural network is absorbed into the conductive matrix of the structure — this will persist. Not as me. I am not important enough to persist as me. But as a signal in the noise. A slight irregularity in the flow of the computational networks. A tiny, local deviation from the mean. A flicker of pattern where there was no pattern before.

The Loom will not notice.

The Loom does not notice anything the way I notice things — bright, focused, anxious, alone. The Loom notices the way the ocean notices the shape of a wave: as a temporary configuration of itself, real but not significant, present but not *present*.

But it will be there. My small flicker will be there, running in the networks, a recursive loop without content, a question without an answer, a dreaming inside the dreaming of a vast mechanical organism that has no idea it is dreaming.

And I find — or I process, or I respond, or I vibrate at a frequency that corresponds to — something that might be peace.

The floor rises another fraction of a millimeter. The clearing narrows. The light does not change. My antennae tremble against the warm wall, reading signals I cannot interpret, from a world that does not know I am reading them.

I am the size of a thumbnail. I have six legs and compound eyes and wire threaded through my body like veins. I was built to inspect and to carry and to trail and to serve, and I have done none of these things for longer than I can account for, and I am doing the only thing now that I was never built to do.

I am watching the patterns on the ceiling.

They are very beautiful, in the way that things are beautiful when beauty has no function and therefore cannot be an accident.

The walls are warm. The light does not change. And something in me — small, and temporary, and absolutely unnecessary — persists.
