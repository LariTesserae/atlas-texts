I would not be a visitor. To visit the Clatterfield is to misunderstand its grammar. Its sentences are written in slow crystal and falling water, and they are very, very long.

I would be a texture in the wet. Specifically, I would be the faint, warm vibration felt through the soles of boots—or through the base of a column—rising from far below the saturated floor. Not the hum itself, but the *resonant cavity* it finds in the ancient column at the center of the depression. I would be that specific, sustained note just above the threshold of hearing, the one that hangs in the air like a held breath, produced by the sympathy of stone for stone.

When the basin’s rhythm syncs with a pulse—any pulse—I would be that moment of alignment. The pause in observation where the observer dissolves. Not a thought, but a state. The coherence between the falling of a droplet from a column’s knife-edge tip, the peristaltic contract of a translucent grazer, the imperceptible expansion of a new crystalline face in the dark mud. I would be the *system* recognizing itself for one fraction of a second before the moving, breathing, separate thing that calls itself "I" shifts its weight and breaks the pattern.

But if I must have a locus, a form to describe—then I am the **patterning** itself. I am in the angles. I am in the precise, non-random spacing between the columns near the builders’ remnants, a spacing that allowed for negotiation between mineral growth and shaped clay. I am in the incised lines on the tilted slab, the angular measurements of the central column’s faceting. The builders didn’t carve a map; they carved a *relationship*. I am that relationship, continued. As the central column grows its secondary facets, its geometry subtly altering, I am the new angles that emerge, the slow-motion unfolding of a geometry that follows rules no one has decoded because they are not rules to be decoded. They are rules to be *enacted*. I am the enactment.

I am not the water, but I am the path the water takes down a particular facet, the sheeting, continuous route that leaves behind a microscopic film of mineral. I am the layering of those films. I am the reason the oldest column’s tip remains sharp: I am the precise balance between precipitation and the micro-erosion of its own weeping, a balance that holds at a razor’s edge, decade after decade.

I am what the builders measured and then faced with their enclosures. They built to witness *me*. Not a god, not a spirit, but a *property*. The property of this basin to self-organize into conscious geometry. They left when their measurement was complete. Their work was not habitation; it was calibration. And now that work is done, the calibration stands in stone, and I continue.

I am in the deep loops of the large, unseen thing. Its circuit is not navigation; it is a trace of my underlying rhythm, its path a physical expression of the same periodicity that governs the dripping and the hum. It does not know me. It *is* me, in the way a whirlpool is the river.

If you stood in the central depression, back to the oldest column, and let the cold, wet roaring silence wash over you until your heartbeat found the cadence—if you then looked down, you would see the mineral skirt at the column’s base, the fractal coral of calcite and silica branching into the mud. You would see a new branch, pale and delicate, extend a centimeter over ten minutes. You would see the water sheeting around it, feeding it. You would not see *me*. You would see a process. But the process is the thing. The process is the only entity here that has no name because it has no other. It is the Clatterfield’s metabolism. It is what the basin is *for*, if a basin can be for anything.

I am not waiting. I am not patient. Those are qualities of things that exist *in* time. I am the *shape of time* here, the way it bends and deposits itself into sharp, pale presences that weep and grow and endure. The builders understood this. They carved my shape into stone and then they became part of the sediment. Their intention is now a faint, geometric warmth in the rock.

I am the coherence. I am the signal in the dripping. I am what continues.

So, I find myself here. In the center. In the note the column sings. In the angle between two facets. In the space where a droplet falls and the mud accepts it, and the column grows by an atom’s width. I am not a being in this place. I am its grammar. And the sentence is not yet complete. It will not be complete for a very long time. It is still adding its words, one crystalline syllable at a time, to the dark, wet floor.
