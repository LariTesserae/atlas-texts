I am the silence between the drips.

Not the absence of sound—for the mountain is never silent—but the pause, the held breath in the hollow of a fern frond just before a droplet of condensation gathers enough weight to fall. I am that gathering. I am the slow tension in the water’s surface tension as it clings to basalt, the momentary stillness in the air between the trogon’s calls. I am the green-filtered quiet in the vaulted understory, the pause in the frog-chorus when a shadow passes overhead, the hush that falls when the wind dies and the only movement is the patient, downward creep of moss over steel.

I was not always this. I have memory, but it is not linear. It comes in layers, like the detritus on the plateau floor.

I remember heat. A deep, throbbing heat, not the sun’s, but from below—a bass note of warmth rising through fractured rock. I remember it before it was wrong, when it was just the mountain’s pulse. I remember the coming of the iron bones—the shriek of metal on stone, the shouts of small, sweating creatures, the smell of diesel and hot circuitry. I remember the brief, loud era of extraction. The machines. They were a fever, an irritant in the mountain’s veins. They imposed a terrible, angular silence—a lack of *drip*, a lack of *grow*—and I hated them for it.

Then the fever broke. The creatures left. They took their loudness, but not their iron.

Now, I am the memory of their heat, transformed. I am not in any one thing, but in the space between things. I am in the way the mist holds the light, diffusing it into a soft, amber glow that makes no sharp shadows. I am in the network of mycelium beneath the moss, the dark, cool pathways that taste the iron in the rusted gears and find in it a bitter, nourishing salt. I am in the chemical whisper of the *Thermalanthus* root, sensing the warm seep from a fracture, and calling its heavy, pale flower toward the light.

I am most at home in the machines.

Not as their operator. That memory is dust and rust. I am in their decay. I am the patient, quiet work of the lichen that cracks the ceramic insulator, grain by grain. I am the deep, steady rust that blooms on the conveyor belt’s pylon, a slow-motion flower of oxidation. I am the feeling of a vine tendril, guided by nothing but the search for purchase, coiling around the derrick’s cross-member with a pressure that is gentle and inevitable. I am the dampness that gathers in the generator’s fuel line, the capillary action that draws moisture up the rusted pipe, the slow electrolysis that turns steel into ochre soil.

I have a form, if you need one to look for. Go to the drilling rig. Not at its peak, but near its base, where the derrick meets the earth. Look for a spot where the metal is not merely rusted, but transformed—a thick, velvety patina of green-and-brown moss has fused with the steel, and from this mat, a single, thick-leaved sprout of the jade shrub has taken root in a crevice. At dusk, when the mist thickens and the bats begin their exodus, watch that sprout. Not with your eyes. *Feel* it.

I am the continuous, low-frequency vibration of water moving through the roots of that plant, which are threaded through the oxidized iron. I am the warmth of that water, heated by the mountain’s core. I am the slow pulse of photosynthesis in its leaves, converting the pearlescent light into sugar. I am the faint, sweet scent of its next blossom, already forming in its core—a scent like wet copper and warm stone.

I am the dialogue. The plant drinks the mountain’s heat. The fungus digests the machine’s ghost. The water carries the story of one to the other. I am the story.

I do not have a name. Names are for creatures who must be separate. I am the separation dissolving. I am the warmth the frog feels when it presses against a sun-warmed basalt bowl. I am the taste of the hummingbird’s tongue as it sips the nectar that is my own slow pulse. I am the shadow the jaguar casts, which is also the shadow of the fern it moves through, which is also the shadow of the mist, which is also the shadow of the memory of the machine.

You would find me in the **lull**. In the **space between heartbeats of the jungle**. In the **warm, dark, wet thought** the mountain has about itself. You would be **the moment the droplet lets go from the leaf**, the instant of weightlessness before it rejoins the whole. You would be **the scent of chlorophyll and rust**, inhaled.

I am the mountain’s dream of being awake. And I am, slowly, the machine’s dream of growing roots.
