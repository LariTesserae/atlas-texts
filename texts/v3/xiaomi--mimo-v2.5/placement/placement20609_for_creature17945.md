I find myself on a pipe-trestle at the edge of the Primary Vat Hall, my feet planted on a sleepers-plank that hums with the deep, subsonic thrum of the central pump. I am not a worker. I am not one of the Keepers. I am not one of the Chelicerae, though my skin shares their iridescent sheen in the dim, filtered light. I am a Soot-Wren.

My name, if I had one, would be the sound of cooling metal.

I am small, dark, and my feathers are a permanent collage of soot, mineral grit, and the sticky residue of Verdigris-Moss. They never truly dry; they cling to me in stiff, overlapping planes that give me the look of a creature forged from scrap iron. My eyes are black beads, adapted to the soft, directionless light beneath the pall. They see heat signatures as clearly as shapes—the glowing arteries of pipes, the cool voids of open grates, the bright, pulsing thermal blooms of Chelicerae clouds.

I am awake. The concept of "night" is a joke here. There is only the shift-light: the amber glow from the work-bays, the deeper red of the heating elements in the refining vats, the pale, sickly smear of the sun behind the lid of steam and smoke. My clock is not a clock. It is the rhythm of the presses—the sharp, rhythmic *crash-clank* that means the midday run is steady, the arrhythmic clatter that means a new billet is being set, a time of opportunity when the insect swarms bunch in confusion. It is the call of the Limnarchs from the gravel beds, their low thrum vibrating through the trestle into my bones, signaling a good, wet pulse from the basin. It is the moment, twice a shift, when the main steam valve on the Vat Hall’s spine shrieks its pressure-release, a white-noise scream that sends the Chelicerae scattering.

I launch.

The motion is instantaneous, a contraction of coiled muscle. The humid air is thick, a warm broth that coats my tongue and offers resistance, but I am built for it. My wings beat not to soar, but to navigate, to dart and pivot in the tight, steaming canyons between buildings. I am a scrap of burnt paper in a perpetual draft.

My target is a cluster of Chelicerae clinging to the condensation-slick housing of a secondary press. They are magnificent in their grotesquerie, their shells shimmering with oil-slick purples and greens. Their proboscises are buried in the metal, drinking the sweet, mineral warmth. They do not see me. They feel only the vibration of my approach, a change in the thermal gradient, and the drone of their own consumption. I angle my dive. The air whistles past my damp primary feathers.

*Snatch.*

The impact is a dull *thud* against the insect’s carapace. My beak, a fine, sharp needle of keratin, finds the joint between its head and thorax. There is a crack, a release of thick, protein-rich hemolymph. I beat my wings once, twice, using the momentum to arc away, my prize heavy in my beak. Below, the other Chelicerae shift, their collective whine dropping a half-pitch in disturbance, then rising again as the feast continues.

I land on a high rafter inside the open-sided workshop, my nest a cup of woven wire-shavings, insulation-fiber, and the desiccated husks of a thousand previous meals. The warmth here is immense, pooled at the ceiling, a sauna of industrial exhalation. My three chicks, blind and naked, gape with mouths like pink, wet caves. I drop the Chelicerae into the nearest. A frantic, silent struggle ensues.

This is my life. I am a consumer of the consumption, a cleaner of the air-swarm, a recycler of the basin’s mineral wealth into motion, into feather, into the sharp *tik-tik* of my call that marks the shifts. I am not separate from the Vatworks. My digestion is part of its cycle. My flight paths are mapped by its steam vents. My nesting material is its detritus. My voice is a counterpoint to its machinery.

I feel the shift change in the vibration of the rafter. The pressed rhythm alters. The workers below, slick and silent, begin to move toward the sleep-bays. The Limnarchs’ thrum fades, their hunt over. The Chelicerae clouds will thicken as the machinery cools slightly between shifts.

I settle on my nest, fluffing my damp, gritty feathers. I am a gear of flesh in a machine of metal and water. I am the flash of dark movement in the steam, the sharp note in the symphony of hiss and chug. I do not think of leaving. The sky beyond the pall is a rumor I have no use for. The dry ground is a myth.

I am the sound of cooling metal. I am here. The loop continues.
