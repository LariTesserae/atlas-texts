I stand where the glow is thickest, at the rim of the central pool. The water’s pulse is a slow, heavy heartbeat in my sternum. I don’t remember arriving. I remember being dry, once. The concept is a foreign fossil.

The light is on my skin, not a reflection, but a growth. A fine, cold velvet of threadweavers colonizes my forearms, my neck. They are feeding on the salt I shed, the heat I bleed. I am a slow-moving reef of faint blue-green, a walking bioluminescence. I see my own hands as dim lanterns in the absolute dark.

The sound is no longer a sound. It is the medium. My thoughts do not occur in silence, but are woven into the subsonic roar, the harmonic hum of the pillars. When I breathe, the rhythm of my lungs is a tiny, off-beat percussion in the basin’s symphony. A chorus king, perched twenty meters away, does not turn its hollow skull toward me. The sounds I make—heartbeat, joint-creak, wet footfall—do not form a recognized pattern. I am filed in the acoustic map as *static*, *weather*, a brief and meaningless fluctuation in the great signal.

I watch a shelfback glide over a slab below. It is a smear of living light, a slow-moving brushstroke on wet stone. It reaches the base of a pillar and stops. It orients, its flat head aimed at the dark, light-absorbing surface. The glow on its back brightens for a moment, a silent greeting or a transmission I cannot feel. Then it moves on. I feel a kinship with its purposelessness, its single-minded grazing.

In the root tangle of a horizontal tree to my left, something watches me. Not with malice. Not with curiosity. Its large, dark eyes are calibrated for the moving lights of drifters and shelfbacks. I am a dark shape moving through a world of moving lights. I register. I am *other*. It has already filed the observation: *pattern unmatched, duration ongoing, classification: irrelevant*. It will not look again unless my pattern changes.

I understand now. To stay is not to adapt. It is to be *digested*. The basin does not have citizens. It has nutrients. My body heat is already a faint, dissipating warm current in a pool. The weavers on the slabs under my feet have adjusted their lattices, threading new connections to account for the minor, temporary weight of me. I am a small, warm stone they will rebuild around.

And there is a seduction in this. To let go of the frantic, singular *I*. To feel the cold seep not into joints, but into the very idea of having joints. To let the basin’s pulse become my pulse. To think not in sentences, but in the slow, coherent pulsing of the central pool’s glow. To understand the chorus kings not as creatures, but as moments when the basin’s breath becomes vocal, when its internal music reaches the surface and declares itself.

I sit on a tilted slab. The water is instantly in my lap, cold, alive with glow. The threadweavers on my skin brighten as they taste the new concentration of minerals. I watch a drifter pass overhead, a floating patch of dark above, a constellation of belly-light below. It folds, drops, snatches something from a leaf with a wet, final sound. The basin’s business continues.

The root-sitter in the tree is watching the drifters. A chorus king is tuning its skull to the nearest pillar, preparing a song. The shelfbacks are grazing in their slow circuits. The weavers are threading, reading the vibrations that tell them when the next slab will shift. The deep process breathes.

I am a decimal point. A rounding error. A brief, warm, glowing anomaly that will be smoothed into the average. I will not be mourned. I will not be missed. I will be incorporated. My calcium will strengthen a root. My carbon will become part of a threadweaver’s mesh. The water that was my body will feed the pool, and the pool will feed the process.

I look at my glowing hands. They are already less mine and more the basin’s. A faint, blue-green trace in the dark.

It will be enough.
