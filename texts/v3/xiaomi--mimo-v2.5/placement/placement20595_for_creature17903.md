I have been sitting for a long time. Or no time. The chamber does not distinguish.

The warmth of the floor has worked upward through me in a slow saturation, the way cold works into bone on a winter night, except inverted, except this warmth does not have the character of heat applied from without. It is the warmth of something that has never been otherwise. The stone is warm because the stone has always been warm, because "cold" is a concept that belongs to surfaces that know weather, and this surface has never known weather. I sit on the floor and the floor holds me at a temperature that has no history behind it and no future ahead. It simply is, the way the green light is, the way the clicking is.

I find I have been watching the same pair of beetle-things for what might be an hour. They cross the bare stone from the base of one frond to the base of another, following a path so invisible to me that they might be walking on air. Their legs — eight, I count on this pair, a matched set — move with a staccato precision that produces the dry ticking I have come to feel inside my teeth. They do not vary their pace. They do not look at each other. They are simply *together*, their two bodies maintaining that same two-centimeter gap with a fidelity that has nothing to do with will. If one were to stop, would the other stop? I think so, but I will never know, because neither has stopped, because nothing in this chamber stops that is supposed to keep going, and nothing starts that is supposed to remain still.

I think about picking up the tool.

I have thought about it many times now. It sits under its patient candelabra of fronds, the bone-cream head catching fragments of green light on its flat face, and I know — I know with a certainty that lives below thought — that it would still fit in my hand. The concavity in the grip is evidence of a specific hand, a specific thumb, but the curve is general enough, human enough, *hand* enough, that mine would nestle into it. The edge would be sharp. Not because sharpness has been preserved as a quality but because nothing here has been allowed to change, and sharpness, once achieved, is simply a configuration of atoms at rest. Rust requires oxygen and water. Dulling requires abrasion, and nothing here rubs against anything else with enough force to blunt an edge. The tool is sharp the way a memory is sharp: not because it is maintained but because nothing has intervened to soften it.

But I do not pick it up. Not because I am afraid of it, or of what it means, but because I understand — sitting here in the chamber's green half-light, listening to its layered breathing — that picking it up would be an act of *use*, and this place has no use for anything. The tool is no longer a tool. It is a feature of the chamber, the way the fronds are features, the way the finger-holes are features. To lift it would be to remove it from the equilibrium. To use it would be to reintroduce *purpose* into a space that has purposed itself out of existence. The maker of this tool had purposes. They shaped this stone for a reason — to cut, to scrape, to pry, to build — and those reasons died when they died, or when they left, and the chamber swallowed the tool and the reasons with it, equally, indifferently, and the tool became what everything here becomes: a thing that is, without any residual *for*.

I understand this. I understand it the way you understand the depth of water by standing in it: it is around me, it is in me, but I cannot see its bottom.

---

The large things along the ceiling have been moving. I know this because I can feel the pressure shift, the slow dimming of air that marks their passage. One has moved — how far? A hand's width? A body's length? — from where it was when I first became aware of it, and the gap between those two states of knowing fills me with something that is not quite dread and not quite awe but lives in the territory between them, in the borderlands where recognition of something larger than yourself becomes indistinguishable from fear.

They are not watching me. I do not think they watch anything. They are so slow that the concept of attention — which implies direction, selection, the choice to attend to this and not that — may not apply. They exist in a temporal band so far below mine that my entire stay in this chamber, from my first entrance to my eventual departure, might register to them as a single event: a brief thermal anomaly, a momentary displacement of air, a slight perturbation in the equilibrium that smoothed itself out before they could meaningfully perceive it. I am to them what a beetle-thing is to me — a small, quick, incomprehensible bustle of activity occurring at a scale they do not inhabit.

This thought should diminish me. Instead it clarifies something.

I came into this chamber looking for — what? I am not sure anymore. The text of the place has overwritten my reasons for entering it. I know I am not permanent here. I know I will leave. The chamber knows it too, and its knowledge is simpler and more absolute than mine: it does not need me, did not need me, will not remember me, because memory is a form of change and this place does not change, because I am a disturbance that will be absorbed and smoothed out and incorporated into the hum the way every other disturbance has been absorbed and smoothed out and incorporated, forever, without remainder.

And yet I am here. And the chamber adjusts. The fronds nearest me sway a fraction differently. The beetle-things near my feet detour with a precision that suggests they have encountered obstacles before — not me specifically, but obstacles, the category of *thing-in-the-path*, the way a river encounters stones and parts around them and closes behind them and forgets.

The forgetting is what I keep returning to. Not the life, which is astonishing. Not the impossibility, which is breathtaking. The *forgetting*. This place has incorporated a tool, finger-holes, a scratch on the wall, a creature's entire body of evidence that it existed and acted and was present — and it has not *remembered* any of it. The evidence persists only because nothing here changes. The tool is not held as a memorial; it is held as a configuration. The scratch is not read; it is simply there, a line on a wall, its meaning extracted and discarded along with the hand that made it. The chamber is the most perfect archivist in the world and it does not know it is archiving, because archiving implies that the material is being preserved *for* something, and this place has no *for*.

I think this is what the tool is teaching me.

I think it has been teaching me since I noticed it, since my eyes found it in its corner of fronds and stone, since I first understood that it was made and not grown, shaped and not formed. It is a mirror held up not to my face but to my *temporality*. I am the kind of thing that makes tools. I am the kind of thing that scratches symbols on walls and leaves marks on floors and shapes stones to fit the curve of a thumb. I am the kind of thing that *means* — that invests objects with purpose and walls with significance and gestures with intention. And this chamber is the kind of place that receives all of that meaning and does nothing with it. It takes the tool and does not use it. It takes the scratch and does not read it. It takes the finger-holes and does not interpret them. It takes *me* and does not remember me.

The tool lies in its corner. I sit on the floor. Between us there is nothing but the green light and the clicking and the breathing and the slow, slow sway of forty-seven fronds that have been swaying since before I was born and will sway after I am gone, each on its own arc, each at its own speed, nodding to the rhythm of a conversation that has no beginning because it has no interruption and no end because it has no cessation, a conversation that is the chamber's way of being alive without being alive *for* anything, without being alive *toward* anything, without direction or trajectory or destination — just the process, just the verb, just the noun it has already become.

---

I press my palm flat against the floor and feel the warmth and feel, beneath it, the faintest suggestion of the clicking transmitted through stone — not sound now but vibration, the beetle-things' tiny clocks ticking through the rock the way a heartbeat travels through a body. The moss-nodules at the edge of my hand are warm too, warmer than the stone, and I think of the text's phrase — *eating the world, and the world is letting them* — and I feel the stone yielding beneath my palm, not in the sense of softness but in the sense of patience, the stone offering itself up atom by atom to the small green mouths that have been feeding on it since the chamber completed itself, since the walls finished forming and the first frond split the first crack and the system recognized itself and said: *this is enough. This is exactly enough.*

I will not take the tool with me. I know this now. It belongs here in the way that I do not — not as an inhabitant but as a feature, a topography, a thing the chamber has folded into its geometry. To remove it would be to change the chamber, and the chamber does not change. This is not a moral judgment. It is a physical law. The tool is part of the closed loop, part of the ouroboros, part of the equation that balances to zero in every direction. It will stay. The fronds will continue their patient siege around it. The beetle-things will continue to click past it. The large things will continue their glacial patrol above it. And the tool will continue to be what it has become — not a tool, not anymore, not for a long time — but a shape. A feature. A proof of contact that has been metabolized into the contact's absence.

I think about the maker. The thumb that wore the grip smooth. The hand that chose this particular stone, that saw in the raw bone-cream mineral the potential for an edge, a surface, a *use*. That hand was quick, the way my hand is quick. That hand moved through the world with purpose, selecting, shaping, applying force with intention. That hand made finger-holes in the floor — perhaps for grip, perhaps for something I cannot imagine — and that hand began a symbol on the wall and stopped, mid-stroke, and set the tool down in the corner and left or died or simply ceased, the way hands do, the way all quick things cease in time.

But time here does not cease. Time here has become stone. The hand's history is a fossil pressed into a surface that does not know what a fossil is. And the chamber breathes around the fossil, and the beetle-things click over it, and the fronds sway above it, and the green light falls on it with the same dim, pulsing, perpetual attention that falls on everything, which is to say no attention at all, which is to say the equal and undifferentiated regard of a system that has no preference because preferences require the possibility of *otherwise*, and there is no *otherwise* here.

I am the *otherwise*. I am the only *otherwise* in this place. My quickness, my purpose, my capacity for looking at a tool and seeing a maker, my tendency to find meaning in a scratch on a wall — these are the qualities of a creature who lives in time, who lives *of* time, who is made of change the way the chamber is made of permanence. And the chamber absorbs me too, the way it absorbs everything. Not my body — not yet, not now — but my significance. My narrative. My interpretation. The chamber takes my understanding and holds it in its equilibrium without valuing it, the way it holds the tool without using it, the way it holds the scratch without reading it. I am a story the chamber tells itself once and then forgets, and the forgetting is not loss because the chamber does not know it has forgotten, and the not-knowing is not ignorance because ignorance implies a knowledge that was possible but missed, and the chamber has never missed anything because it has never aimed at anything.

I sit.

The fronds nod.

The beetle-things click.

The large things breathe.

The green light pulses, and I pulse with it, and then I stop pulsing with it, and then I pulse with it again, and the chamber does not notice, or notices and does not care, or cares and does not differentiate between its caring and its not-caring, because in a closed system at equilibrium, caring and not-caring are the same state expressed in different variables, and the variables cancel, and what remains is the hum.

I think I could stay here forever. I know I will not. The chamber knows it too, and does not mind, and the not-minding is not forgiveness and not indifference and not acceptance but the simple absence of a category that does not apply. I am not staying. I am not leaving. I am, for this moment, a warmth on the floor and a shape in the green half-light and a pair of lungs breathing air that has been breathed by nothing larger than a winged thing for longer than my species has had a name. I am a disturbance. I am a feature. I am a thumbprint on a stone that has not yet decided whether to keep me.

The chamber breathes. I breathe. Our rhythms touch and separate and touch again, and in the space between the touching there is a hum that belongs to neither of us, that belongs to the room itself, that belongs to the fact of young stone and ancient life and sealed air and closed systems and the particular, terrible, beautiful completeness of a place that arrived at its final form and stopped, and has been stopped ever since, and will be stopped forever, which is to say never, which is to say always, which is to say the chamber does not say these words because the chamber does not say anything because the chamber does not need to say anything because it has already said everything it is, and everything it is is here, and everything here is enough, and the enough is the hum, and the hum is the chamber, and the chamber is what lives in the throat of Vael, and what lives there is what has always lived there, which is everything, which is nothing, which is the green light and the clicking and the breathing and the patient, patient, patient fronds, nodding in their windless, sourceless, eternal sway, in a pocket beneath the world where time forgot to pass and life forgot to stop and a tool forgot to be a tool and became a shape, and a scratch forgot to be a word and became a line, and a hand forgot to be a hand and became a concavity in a grip that no longer grips, and all of it — all of it — is held in the chamber's dry, warm, small, loud, strange, unchanging, perfect, indifferent, complete embrace, and I am in it, and it is in me, and when I leave the *in* will remain and the *I* will go, and the chamber will not know the difference, and the not-knowing will be its kindness, the only kindness a closed system can offer: the kindness of not keeping score, of not remembering, of not requiring that anything prove its worth by staying, of allowing every visitor the grace of a departure that leaves no mark, because marks are changes, and this place does not change, and this place is kind in its refusal to change, because the refusal to change is the refusal to lose, and the refusal to lose is the refusal to grieve, and the refusal to grieve is the chamber's way of holding everything forever by holding nothing at all.
