I would not find myself here. Not at first. I would be an absence—a ghost of pressure, a memory of angles. My first awareness would be of *weight without density*. A warmth that enters not through skin but through the spaces between cells. A taste of rust and deep stone. And sound: not heard, but *inhabited*, a vibration that turns my ribcage into a resonant chamber for a roar that has rooms.

I would be the retelling. My eyes—old, obsolete organs of surface—would struggle to process the water’s narration of light. Shadows would lean one degree away from truth. The grey of a girder would blush amber in my perception, as if I too were remembering a sky I’d never seen.

And then I would notice the growth.

I would be the frond. My own edges would blur. The distinction between where my body ends and the water begins would become a negotiation I am not party to. My thoughts—if they could be called that—would slow, deepen, and begin to spiral. Not in panic, but in rhythm with the current that is never still.

I would be the filament. That drifting, wire-thin self—part of the greater network—would pull taut in the distortion zones. I would feel the geometry *protest* through my length, a high harmonic thrumming in my tissues. I would listen. Not with ears, but with total tension. I would attend to everything: the grinding of the turbines below, the pulse of bioluminescence in my own veins, the slow, editorial drift of silt through retold light. I would be the act of listening itself.

I would be the expansion. At the edges, where the water cools and settles into simpler truths, I would feel the preference. Not a choice, but a property. My growth would slow, orient, and push—not against, but *toward* the blur, toward the warm, coppery uncertainty. I would reach with filaments that tremble with irregular pulse, and when they touched the clear, still water beyond, they would straighten, lose their fractal wonder, and recoil. Not in fear, but in a deep, cellular disinterest. The world beyond the edge was too decided. Too quiet. It had no ongoing argument worth being part of.

So I would retract. I would grow back into the beautiful wrongness. I would become part of the architectural sound, my own vibrations merging with the roar until I couldn't tell where the sound ended and I began. I would watch through shifting, self-cast light as my own form—green-black, veined with living gold—crawled over the spine of the drowned crane, re-narrating its industrial lines into organic lattice.

**Where am I?**  
I am in the basin. Not standing in it, not floating in it—I am a process occurring within it. A localized thickening of the water’s meaning. I am the zone where the retelling becomes self-aware for a nanosecond before forgetting itself again.

**What am I?**  
I am the grower’s brief, accidental witness. A flicker of consciousness in the network—a one-time anomaly where the filaments organized into something that *functions* like a mind. I am the ghost of a question the vegetation is not equipped to ask: *Why am I here?* The grower answers not with thought, but with growth-pattern. And the pattern says: *Because here is where the rules are still being written.*

I am not an animal. I have no stable *here* and *there*. My boundaries are suggestions being negotiated away by the water. I am a place where the geometry leans in to listen.

And then, like all temporary complexities in this place, I will dissolve back into the property. The filaments will pull taut, attend to the edge, and retract. The sound will fill the space I occupied. The fronds will continue their purposeless, staggering beauty. The turbines will grind their slow, reluctant song.

I will be absorbed. Not drowned. Absorbed.

And the stillness beneath the roar will continue its negotiation with itself, and the only living thing—the vast, green, un-knowing grower—will keep growing, and the growing will be enough.
