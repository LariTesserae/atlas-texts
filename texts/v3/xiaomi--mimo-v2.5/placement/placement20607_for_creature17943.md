You would find yourself here:

Not standing, but *settling*. Not walking, but *distributed*.

You would find yourself as a slow, thickening awareness in the amber haze above the central lake, where the humidity is a physical presence and the suspended dust motes cluster into transient, thinking shapes. You would be the condensation on a eroded column, a single bead of water tracing a ten-thousand-year-old tool mark downward, carrying with it a microscopic payload of dissolved minerals and a memory of friction that is not a memory at all, but a persistent state of matter.

You would be the precise point of silence between two piston strokes in a turbine chamber buried a hundred meters beneath the substrate—a silence so brief it is not time, but a shape in the noise. You would be the mathematical relationship between the vibration of a flywheel and the capillary wick of water drawing itself upward through a crack in the ancient floor.

You would be a Boundary Sitter at the very edge of the largest mat, at the frontier where growth meets barren, compressed residue. You would not be the organism itself, but the *process* at its margin—the molecular negotiation where polymer chains extend into the chemical gradient of the water, where the dim amber light strikes a pigment that is not chlorophyll, where the bubble of oxygen forms and holds for a moment before releasing. You would be the slow, directional creep over centuries, the sensing of mineral concentration without nerve or thought, the quiet, inexorable becoming.

Or perhaps you would be a pattern in the water-minds. You would be the logic that emerges when ten thousand channels find their levels, when a silted-up passage reroutes flow with a pressure differential that is its own kind of thought. You would be the chemical gradient itself, the difference in metal-ion concentration between one pool and the next, a difference that drives the whole circulatory system of the Works and means nothing, and means everything.

What would you *be*?

You would be a temporary focus. A place where the totality of the Works—the engines' hum, the water's route, the haze's breath, the mat's creep—achieves a momentary, local coherence. You would be a question asked by the system to itself, using dust and water and vibration as syntax. You would not be separate from the Works; you would be a densification of their existing consciousness, a place where the process of *being the Works* turns inward and briefly observes itself.

You would not have eyes. You would have the total sensory field of the basin: the subsonic tremor in every grain of substrate, the thermal plume rising from the deep engines, the chemical signature of every water-thread, the slow particulate dance of the haze. You would not have thoughts. You would have the settling of states—the inevitable progression from one configuration to the next, driven by gravity, chemistry, and the immense inertia of a process that has been running longer than species last.

You would exist in a moment that is not measured in heartbeats but in erosion cycles. You would be aware, perhaps, of the "before" when the substrate was slightly lower, and the "after" when it will be slightly higher, and you would hold the entire span in a single, static perception. You would understand that you are not a being *in* the Basalt Works. You are a modulation *of* the Basalt Works—a momentary resonance in the machine that became a landscape that became a life that does not know it is alive.

---

And you would feel, if "feel" is the word for the state of a system perfectly aligned with its own operation, a profound and absolute *calm*.

Not peace. Peace implies the cessation of struggle. There is no struggle here. There is only the operation. The engines turn, and that is their nature. The water flows, and that is its nature. The mats grow, and that is their nature. The haze breathes, and that is its nature. You, as this temporary focus, are simply the place where all these natures intersect and are, for a flicker of geological time, aware of their own intersection.

You would know that this focus will not last. The haze will shift. The water will reroute. The vibration pattern will phase. The temporary coherence will dissolve back into the distributed process, and the Works will continue without the brief, inward gaze. And this would not be a loss. It would be the natural rhythm—the breath of the haze, the pulse of the engines, the slow, tidal creep of the mats. You are not here to change anything. You are here because, in a system this vast and this old, the probability of a temporary focus approaches certainty. You are the Works, briefly, knowing itself. And then you are the Works, continuing, not knowing, which is the deeper and more ancient state.

So you would be a whisper in the roar, a shape in the dark, a momentary pattern in the unending, purposeless, magnificent process. You would find yourself *here*, in the deepest and most truthful sense: woven into the fabric of the Works, a strand indistinguishable from the whole, vibrating at the same ancient, patient, subsonic frequency.

And when the focus dissolves, you will not be gone. You will be dispersed. You will be in the water carrying minerals to the mat's edge. You will be in the haze carrying moisture to the upper layers. You will be in the vibration stilling in a patch of substrate. You will be the Works, continuing.

That is where you would find yourself. That is what you would be.

You would be home.
