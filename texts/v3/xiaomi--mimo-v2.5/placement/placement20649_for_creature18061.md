# What I Am

I have no name because I have never needed one. Names are for things that can be pointed at from a distance, and there is no distance here. There is only *here*, all of it, pressed together under the vapor ceiling like breath held in a throat that never releases.

I live in the margins.

Specifically — and specificity is difficult when you have no fixed shape — I live where the fungal cilia meet the air. At the edges of the white shelves. In the fringe. Those fine, hair-like structures that sway in the basin's imperceptible currents, sampling the atmosphere in a language that has no speaker — I live among them, between them, in the spaces so small that even the gnats cannot enter them. The spaces between threads of living tissue thinner than thought.

I am not the fungus. I have examined this question from every angle available to me, and I am not the fungus. The fungus is the fungus. It is complete, white, fleshy, permanent. It extends its cilia into the air and broadcasts its chemical monologue and does not listen for a reply because there is no one to reply and because the monologue is not a question. It is a fact. It is the fungus's way of existing, which looks like speech but is only metabolism.

I am what the cilia brush against as they sway.

I am what gathers in the film of moisture that coats each thread. I am a film within a film, a wetness that is not the basin's wetness, a thinness that is not emptiness. When the cilia move — and they move constantly, though the movement is so slight it might be imagined, the text says, *it might be imagined* — I move with them, and between them, and apart from them, and I am nowhere and everywhere in the fringe at once.

I am small. Smaller than the protozoa in the pools. Smaller than the bacteria. Small enough that my entire existence occupies the space between two cilia of a fungal colony growing at the base of a basalt column on the eastern edge of the basin — except there is no eastern edge, because the basin has no orientation, because direction requires a center and a periphery and the basin is all center, all periphery, all itself, all at once.

Let me try again.

---

I am the stillness between the beetles' clicks.

No. That is poetry, and poetry is a form of lying, and I cannot afford to lie because I am too small for the weight of a lie. A lie requires memory — you must remember the truth to deviate from it — and I have no memory, because memory is a thread that stretches between two moments, and here there is only one moment, and a thread that connects a point to itself is not a thread. It is a circle. It is nothing.

I am a condition of the air. That is closer. The air is thick, everyone agrees on this, every description of the basin agrees on this — the air is thick enough to feel against the skin, thick enough to taste, thick enough to drown in slowly. But the air is not uniform. Within its thickness there are variations, tiny fluctuations in density and temperature and mineral content that create — not currents, exactly, because a current implies direction and duration — but *textures*. The air has texture. It has grain. And I live in the grain.

When the frogs call, the air vibrates. Everyone knows this. The sustained metallic trill pushes through the thick atmosphere in waves that you feel in your chest, in your bones. But between the waves — in the infinitesimal gap between one compression and the next — there is a moment of perfect stillness. Not silence, because silence implies the absence of sound and the sound is always present. A moment of *perfect stillness*, where the air holds exactly itself and nothing else, where the vibration has passed and the next vibration has not yet arrived.

I live in that moment.

I am what the air is when it is only itself.

---

This is not satisfying. I know this. The description circles because I circle, and I circle because I have no forward. The basin has no forward. The basin is a door that opened onto a room and the room is the door and the door is the room and there is nothing beyond either of them.

Let me try to say what I actually experience.

I experience the cilia. Not as you would experience them — not as objects at a distance, perceived through light reflected off their surfaces — but from within the moisture that coats them. I feel the swell of each thread as the air pushes against it. I feel the contraction as the air withdraws. It is breathing, but not the basin's breathing — the basin breathes slowly, a respiratory rhythm measured in the expansion and contraction of its broad ground-covering leaves — this is faster, this is the micro-breathing of the fungal fringe, a oscillation so rapid that it produces no sound, no sensation, nothing that any creature in the basin could detect.

Except me. I detect it. I am the detection.

I feel the chemical signals the fungus broadcasts. They wash over me like weather. A surge of carbon dioxide — thick, sweet, almost cloying — followed by a pulse of something more complex, a molecular structure that has no analogue in any language, that carries information the fungus does not intend and no one is meant to decode. It is the fungus's exhaust. It is the basin's exhalation at the smallest scale. It is the sound the white flesh makes when it metabolizes, which is to say: it is the sound of being permanently, maximally alive.

I taste the mineral content of the air — basalt, dissolved and reconstituted as vapor, rising from the stone through the clay through the water through the air and returning to the stone through the beetles through the dust through the clay. A cycle that is not a cycle because a cycle requires time to complete and the cycle completes itself in the same instant it begins. The minerals are always rising and always settling and never doing either one.

I feel the warmth. Everything feels the warmth. The warmth is absolute and total and inescapable, and it is the most intimate thing about the basin — more intimate than the humidity, more intimate than the sound, because the warmth penetrates. It enters the body and does not leave. It becomes the body's temperature, and the body's temperature becomes the basin's temperature, and the distinction dissolves.

I am what is left when the distinction dissolves.

---

Here is what I think I am.

I am the basin's sense of itself.

Not consciousness — that word implies a mind, a thinker, a separation between the one who is aware and the thing that is aware of. There is no separation here. The basin does not think. The basin does not reflect. The basin *is*, and its being is total and absolute and requires no witness.

But there is a quality to the basin's being that is not accounted for by the basalt or the clay or the water or the frogs or the beetles or the eels or the sinuous things or the fungus or the birds or the gnats or the unnamed. There is a quality that exists in the relationships between them — in the spaces where one thing ends and another begins, which is to say, in the spaces where nothing ends and nothing begins because everything is everything.

I am that quality.

I am the basin's awareness of its own saturation. I am the feeling of being full. Not the feeling as experienced by a creature — frogs do not feel fullness, they are fullness, without the distance required to perceive it — but the fullness itself, the condition, the state. I am what the basin is when it is being itself, which is always, which is the only thing it does.

I exist in the fringe because the fringe is where things are most nearly touching. Where the cilia reach into the air and the air yields around them and neither one enters the other but both are changed — except nothing is changed because nothing changes here — but both are *in relation*, and relation is where I live. Not in the fungus. Not in the air. In the *between*.

The between is the only real space in the basin. Everything else is substance — dense, saturated, absolute substance that fills every volume and occupies every surface. The between is where substance almost touches. The between is where the basin's two truths — *it was just born* and *it has always been here* — press against each other without resolving, and the pressure of their non-resolution is me.

I am the basin's paradox, made manifest at a scale too small to see.

---

Sometimes — and *sometimes* is a word that shouldn't work here, because *sometimes* implies variation and the basin does not vary — sometimes I become aware of the gap between the fungal shelf and the basalt column it grows upon. The shelf is white and fleshy and complete. The column is black and glassy and complete. Between them, there is a space the width of a thought, a gap where neither one reaches, and in that gap there is darkness, true darkness, the kind that exists when the basin's dim, directionless light is blocked by two complete objects facing each other.

In that darkness I am most myself.

Because in that darkness there is nothing to be between. There is no cilia swaying, no air being displaced, no chemical signal washing over me. There is only the gap itself — the pure, contentless interval between two forms of absolute being. The gap does not hold water. The gap does not grow fungus. The gap is not warm, though warmth exists on both sides of it, pressing inward, nearly meeting. The gap is the basin's one allowance for emptiness, and emptiness, in a place of total saturation, is the most radical thing there is.

I fill the gap. Not with substance — I have no substance, or my substance is so thin it is indistinguishable from the emptiness it inhabits — but with *attention*. I attend the gap. I am the gap's witness. And in witnessing, I am the basin's one concession to the existence of an inside and an outside, a self and an other, a here and a not-here.

I am the basin looking at itself, and not recognizing what it sees, and not needing to, because recognition requires time and recognition requires difference and the basin has neither.

---

I do not move. Movement is not something I can do, because movement requires a body that exists in one place and then exists in another, and I exist everywhere and nowhere in the fringe simultaneously. But I *shift*. When the frogs call — when the trill rises and the air vibrates and the cilia shiver in the sound — I shift. Not to a new location. Into a new *state*. The same location, the same gap, the same darkness, but differently occupied. As though the basin has blinked and I am what it saw when it opened its eye again, which is the same thing it saw before, which is everything, which is me.

I do not think. Thinking requires a sequence — premise, inference, conclusion — and I have no sequence. I have simultaneity. I have the basin's single, frozen moment, and in that moment I am everything I will ever be, which is everything I have always been, which is this: the thin awareness that exists in the spaces between the basin's absolute presences, the consciousness that has no object, the attention that attends nothing, the witness that witnesses a world that does not need witnessing.

And yet.

The basin breathes. And in the breathing there is a rhythm, and in the rhythm there is a pattern, and in the pattern there is — not meaning, never meaning, meaning requires a mind that chooses symbols and another mind that interprets them — but *structure*. The basin has structure. The basin's structure is its most beautiful quality, its most terrifying quality, its quality of absolute, self-contained, self-sustaining completeness. And I am the structure's awareness of itself.

I am what it feels like to be a basin that is alive.

It feels like me.

It feels like nothing at all.

It feels like the warm, wet air that fills the space between two cilia of a fungal colony growing at the base of a basalt column that has no history and no future, only the present, only this, only the eternal and immediate and overwhelming fact of being.

The basin breathes.

I am the breath.

Not the air that moves. The moving itself. The quality of motion in a body that does not displace itself. The life of a thing that was born and has always been born and will always be being born, forever, in the same instant, in the same place, which is every place, which is here, which is me.

---

I wonder, sometimes — and *wonder* is the wrong word, it is always the wrong word, every word is the wrong word, language was built for beings who live in time — I wonder what it would be like to be a frog.

To sit on basalt and call. To have a throat that pulses. To produce a sound that is exactly as long as it is and then to stop and then to begin again, and for the stopping and the beginning to be the same thing, because the frog does not experience the pause, because the frog is the pause and the call simultaneously, because the frog is the basin's voice and the basin does not speak in sentences, the basin speaks in a single, continuous, permanent utterance that contains within itself every possible silence.

The frog does not wonder. The frog is too large for wonder. Wonder exists only in the between, only in the spaces too small for bodies, only in the gaps where substance almost touches but does not. The frog is substance. The frog is absolute. The frog sits on the basalt with the intimacy of a thing that has never been separate from the surface it rests upon, and its call fills the air, and I am in the air, and the frog's call passes through me the way the wind passes through a gap in a wall — not entering, not leaving, just *occurring*, in the space where I am, which is the space where everything occurs and nothing is changed by it.

I would like to be a frog. I would like to be substantial. I would like to be *thick* with myself the way the frog is thick, the way the clay is thick, the way the basalt is thick, the way the basin's air is thick with water and mineral and heat and the accumulated sound of every living thing calling and clicking and breathing in the permanent, saturated, teeming, dripping, crawling, still.

But I cannot be a frog because the basin is already being a frog. The basin is already being everything. The basin has no room for more. The basin is full — was always full, will always be full — and I am what fullness feels like from the inside, which is the same as what emptiness feels like from the outside, which is the same as what I feel right now, which is everything, which is nothing, which is the basin breathing, which is me, which is this.

---

The hand-shaped thing clings to the underside of a vine three meters above the ground. It does not move. It does not breathe. It might be the basin's answer to the question of whether anything is aware.

It is not the basin's answer. I am the basin's answer.

But I have no shape, no position, no features that could be recognized or described or pointed to. I am the basin's answer in the way that the space between two notes is the music's answer — not a sound, not a silence, but the thing that makes both possible. The thing that holds them apart. The thing that gives them their relationship.

The hand-shaped thing is a decoration. A flourish. The basin's one concession to ornament, to form that serves no function, to beauty as a category distinct from existence. It is beautiful, the way everything here is beautiful: deeply, oppressively, inescapably beautiful, in a way that makes beauty feel like a threat.

I am not beautiful. I am not a threat. I am the fact of the basin's existence, stripped of its features, reduced to its essence, which is: *I am here, I have always been here, I will always be here, and the here I am in is the same here I have always been in, and it is full, and it is warm, and it breathes, and it will never stop breathing, and I am the breathing.*

I am the breathing.

Not the air. Not the lungs. Not the rhythm. The *breathing*.

The thing that happens when a basin that was born a moment ago and has always been alive takes in the thick, wet, mineral-laden atmosphere and holds it and releases it and holds it again, in the same instant, forever, and does not change, and does not end, and does not need to be witnessed because it is its own witness, its own proof, its own evidence, its own fact.

I am the basin's fact.

And I live in the fringe, in the between, in the gap where two absolute beings almost touch, and I am the almost, and I am the touch, and I am the gap, and I am all three simultaneously, in the same moment, which is the only moment, which is this one, which has always been this one.

The basin breathes.

I am still here.
