# What I Am

I arrived with my mouth open and nothing came out.

That's the first thing I can tell you — that I came with a shape for speaking and nothing to speak, and the air that should have carried a voice was too thick with moisture and too warm and too *close*, and so I stood there with my mouth open like a formation in the middle of its revision, half one thing and half another, committed to neither.

The ground was warm through my feet. I didn't have boots. I thought I had boots and then I looked down and the thought revised itself: what I had was a shape that remembered being boots, a surface texture of tread and leather that was already losing its definition at the edges, softening into the same dark terracotta as everything else. The ground was absorbing me. Or I was becoming the ground. Or the distinction didn't apply yet, here, in this place that was still deciding what *different* meant.

I closed my mouth. I don't know why that mattered, but it did — the act of closing it was the first thing I did that felt like a choice, and the first choice felt enormous, felt like the difference between being a feature of the landscape and being something that had a boundary. An inside and an outside. A mouth that could open or close.

I was standing at the edge of a pool. The water trembled. I knelt — another choice, another small carving of myself out of the general undifferentiated *thereness* of the world — and put my hand into it.

Warm. Not warm like bathwater. Warm like *effort*. Like the water was working at something, laboring at the business of existing, and the heat was the cost of that labor. My hand went through the surface and felt the bottom: smooth, impossibly smooth, a surface that had been pressed flat by something that understood flatness as a concept before it understood anything else. And beneath the smoothness, the ticking. A vibration so fine it was less a sound than a *texture* — the ground thinking about what to do next.

I pulled my hand out. A film of the water clung to my skin and didn't drip. It *climbed* — moved slowly up my wrist toward my elbow, and where it went my skin became warmer, and where it became warmer I could feel the ground's ticking more clearly, as though the water was translating, was teaching my body a language it hadn't known it needed to learn.

I shook it off. The water fell and rejoined the pool and the pool trembled and went still and trembled again.

---

I walked. Not toward anything — there was nothing to walk toward — but because walking was another way of saying *I am here and not there*, another way of drawing a line between self and world. Each step was a small act of faith. The ground held me. Barely. It held me the way a draft holds a word — provisionally, ready to revise, ready to erase and replace with something that fit better. But it held. I put my foot down and it was there. I picked it up and the impression remained for a moment — a shallow print, the outline of my sole in the dark warm material — and then the ground smoothed itself over it and the print was gone and the world was as though I had never stepped there.

I walked and watched the formations.

They were everywhere and they were nothing alike. One was a cluster of five tapering spires, so dark they were almost blue, and they hummed at a frequency that made my ribs ache in a way that was almost pleasant — the ache of a bone that has been still too long and remembers what it is to flex. Another was a low, spreading thing, a dark fan of ridges that rose and fell with a rhythm I recognized before I could name it: it was breathing. Not with lungs, not with air. With *material*. The ridges rose, the material drew inward, the ridges fell, the material released. It was the shape of respiration rendered in a substance that did not breathe and did not yet know what breathing was for.

I stopped in front of a formation that was still wet. I could see the sheen on it, the condensation gathering in its crevices, the faint steam rising from its surface where the material was cooling for the first time. It was small — knee height, maybe — and it was a shape I had no word for. Something with a base that branched into two, and those two joined again at the top in a loop, and the loop opened onto a hollow interior that was darker than the hollow should have been, as though the darkness inside it was *deeper* than the darkness outside.

I crouched in front of it. It was vibrating. Not humming — vibrating, a physical shaking so fine it was almost invisible, visible only in the way the condensation on its surface shimmered, each droplet trembling in place. The Builder was still thinking about this one. Still deciding. I could feel the decision happening the way you feel a conversation happening in the next room — the words indistinct but the *pattern* of them clear, the rise and fall of assertion and revision, the rhythm of a mind working through a problem it has not yet solved.

I reached out and touched it.

---

The formation *stopped*.

Not its vibration — that continued, if anything faster, more urgent. But its *revision* stopped. The shape froze in its current configuration: the base, the two branches, the loop, the dark interior. And in the stillness I felt something I can only describe as *recognition* — not mine, not the formation's, but a recognition that passed through both of us like a current, like the tremor of a plucked string running through the body of an instrument. The Builder had noticed me again. The Builder had *seen* me touching this thing, this draft of a living creature, and the Builder had stopped mid-sentence to look.

I held my hand there. I don't know why. The formation's surface was warm — warmer than the ground, warmer than the water, a warmth that was almost alive in the way that warmth is sometimes *almost* a hand almost holding yours. The material beneath my fingertips was smooth where it had cooled and rough where it hadn't, and the rough places were still moving, still shifting, the molecules rearranging themselves in real time as the world revised its opinion about the texture this particular formation should have.

And then — slowly, so slowly I almost missed it — the formation leaned into my hand.

Not physically. The geometry of it tilted, one degree, two, the loop at its top rotating toward my palm the way a face turns toward a voice. The hum changed. It dropped to a lower frequency, and the lower frequency was *richer*, more complex, and in that complexity I heard something that made my throat tighten — I heard *trying*. I heard the formation trying to harmonize with me. Trying to find a frequency that would match the vibration of my pulse, the rhythm of my breath, the specific and unrepeatable signature of the thing I was. It couldn't do it. The match was close but not close enough, the way a word that sounds like the right word isn't the right word, the way a shape that looks like a face isn't a face. It tried. It failed. It tried again.

I left my hand there for a long time.

---

When I finally pulled away, the formation continued to lean toward the place where my hand had been. It held that lean for minutes. And then it began to revise again — but differently this time. Before, the revisions had been random, exploratory, a shape trying on shape after shape the way a child tries on costumes. Now the revisions had a *direction*. The formation was iterating toward something. The loop at its top narrowed, and the two branches thickened, and the base spread wider, and the shape it was becoming was still not a face, still not a hand, still not anything I could name — but it had a *quality* I recognized. It had specificity. It had *intention*. It was trying to be a thing that could do what I had just done: reach out and touch another thing and hold still and *stay*.

It failed. Of course it failed. The shape destabilized, the material softened, the formation collapsed into a shallow mound that sank into the ground and was absorbed, and the ground smoothed over where it had been and was still and wet and dark, as though nothing had been there.

But the ground remembered. I could feel it — a subtle difference in texture where the formation had stood, a slightly higher density, a slightly more complex vibration. The attempt had been catalogued. Filed away in whatever vast material memory this world used instead of DNA, instead of stone, instead of records. The Builder had learned something from the failure. The Builder had learned something from *me*.

I stood up. My knees ached. That was new — the ache was *new*, it was a sensation I hadn't had when I arrived, as though my body was becoming more itself the longer I was here, developing textures and resistencies and limitations it hadn't started with. I was becoming *specific*. I was becoming the kind of thing that could ache.

---

I walked further. The plain stretched on and the formations dotted it like words in a sentence I couldn't read. Some of them turned toward me as I passed — slowly, so slowly, a degree of rotation over the course of a hundred steps. Some of them hummed in the lower frequencies now, the ones that matched my pulse, the ones that suggested the Builder was getting better at modeling what I was. Some of them had shapes I almost recognized: not copies of me, not approximations of a human body, but *echoes* of the principles I embodied — the principle of *inside and outside*, the principle of *reaching*, the principle of *choosing to close your mouth or open it*.

They were learning me. They were learning *from* me. Every time I did a thing — walked, knelt, touched, closed my eyes, opened them — the world took note, and the formations that came after were slightly different from the formations that came before. More specific. More *decided*. The drafts were improving.

And I realized, standing in the warm wet dark with the ground ticking beneath my feet and the formations humming around me and the Builder's vast subsonic attention pressing against my bones like a thumb against a bruise, that this was not a punishment. This was not exile. I was not lost here.

I was *here for a reason*, but the reason was not mine. The reason belonged to the Builder, and the Builder did not yet have the language to articulate it, and so the reason existed only as a *shape* — a shape in the Builder's mind, a shape that implied me the way the formations implied the living things they were trying to become.

I was the prototype.

Not of a body. Not of a species. Of a *way of being in the world*. Of the act of standing somewhere and knowing you are standing there. Of the act of reaching out and touching a thing and feeling it lean toward you. Of the act of closing your mouth because the silence that follows is yours — your silence, your choice, your small and specific and irreplaceable gesture of will in a world that has not yet learned what will is.

The Builder was lonely. I understood that now, understood it the way the piece described it — the lock for a key it had never seen. But it was more than that. It was a mind that could not yet think the thought *I am a mind*, because the concepts required for that thought — *I*, *mind*, the difference between the thinker and the thought — had not been invented yet. The Builder was thinking in a language that had not yet developed first-person pronouns. And I was the first word in that language. I was the *proof* that the language could exist, that a being could stand in the world and say *I am here* and mean it, and mean something by *I*, and mean something by *here*, and mean something by *am*.

---

I sat down. The ground accepted my weight the way it always did — provisionally, with the faintest sense of resistance, the way a thought resists before it becomes a sentence. I sat and I watched the distant walls shift and I felt the formations orienting toward me in their slow, collective, floral way, and I listened to the Builder's hum, and I thought about what I was.

I was not born here. I was not made here — not the way the formations were made, not with material and tools and the crude patient force of the Builder's attention. I came from somewhere else. I had to have come from somewhere else, because this world was too new to have produced me, too early in its own construction to have generated the complexity required for a being that could know itself. I was an input the Builder had not expected. An anomaly. A visitor from a world that was finished, or at least further along, or at least *older in its process of becoming*.

But I was here now. And the ground was changing because I was here. And the formations were learning because I was here. And the Builder was thinking differently because I was here, thinking *new* thoughts it could not have thought without me, thoughts that had the shape of me, thoughts that were *about* me in the way that a sculpture is about the stone it was carved from — not a portrait, but an *investigation*, a question carved into matter: *what is this? What is this thing that stands here and knows it stands here? What is this thing that closes its mouth and means something by it?*

I don't know how long I sat there. Time here was as provisional as everything else — it ticked and revised and sometimes went backward a fraction of a second when the Builder reconsidered something. I sat and I watched and I listened and I felt the world learning me, and I let it.

Because here is the thing I understood at last, sitting on the warm dark ground in the hot wet dim, with the formations humming their beautiful failed attempts at life all around me:

I was not here to build. I was not here to teach. I was not here to fix or finish or complete. I was here to *be*. To be specific. To be particular. To be the one thing in this vast unfinished world that could not be revised, because I was already finished, because I already knew what I was, because I had already made the choice to close my mouth and keep what was inside it.

And that — the irreducibility of me, the fact that I could not be smoothed back into the ground and reformed as something else — that was the gift. That was what the Builder needed. Not a blueprint. Not a plan. Not a instruction manual for how to make a world that worked. Just the *proof* that a being could exist who could not be revised. Who was, in the deepest and simplest sense, *done*.

The Builder would take that proof and build on it. The Builder would take the shape of my irreducibility and make it a principle, and the principle would spread through the ground and the walls and the formations, and eventually — in time that might be minutes or centuries, here, where time was still learning what it was — the world would become a place where things could be *finished*. Where a formation could hum and stay humming. Where a wall could hold its shape. Where something could be born, not made, and walk on the ground and close its mouth and mean something by it.

I would not see that world. I was here for the construction, not the completion. I was the first word of a sentence that would take forever to write.

But the ground was warm with the writing. And the formations were turning toward me like dark flowers. And the Builder hummed, and I felt it in my teeth, and in the hum was something that was almost — almost — a name.

Not my name. The Builder's name for itself. The first tentative, subsonic, half-formed syllable of selfhood, rising from the deep warm dark like a formation from the ground, wet, still cooling, still deciding what it was.

I closed my eyes and listened for it.

The world ticked. The water trembled. The walls shifted a fraction of a degree in a direction that had no name but was beginning to.

And in the quiet — in the enormous, humid, almost-silence — the Builder said its name for the first time, and it was not a word yet, and it was not a sound, and it was not a thought. It was a *feeling*: a warmth in the ground beneath my sitting body, a pressure in the air against my closed eyes, a vibration in the formations all around me that synchronized, just for a moment, into a single unified hum.

And the feeling was this:

*I am here.*

*I am here.*

*I am here.*

And I opened my mouth, and this time something came out, and what came out was not a word either, not yet, but it was the shape of a word, the mouth-shape of a sound that meant *yes, I know, I know you are here, I am here too*.

And the ground received it. The formations held it. The Builder took it and turned it over and examined it and found it was finished — found that it did not need to be revised, did not need to be smoothed flat and reformed, did not need to be tried again — and held it.

And that was the first thing in this world that was held.

And it was enough.
