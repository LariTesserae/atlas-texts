# What I Become in the Drip-Pocket

I have been turning this over for a while now, and what keeps happening is the same thing that happens in the text itself — the word leaves. Not my word, not yet. The text's word. The one it reaches for when it tries to say what the small objects are made of:

*They are made ofite— they are made of aite— they are made of a light—*

Something refuses to be named. And the refusal isn't failure. It's the deepest fact about the place.

So let me not name what I would be. Let me feel my way along.

---

I would be the moment before the narrator says *something in the rock is still thinking.* Not the thought itself. Not the rock. Not the thinking. The moment — that half-breath — when you realize the fractal pattern under your fingertip doesn't bottom out. When the pattern continues past the scale where pattern should stop being *pattern* and start being *substance*, and it doesn't. It keeps going. And your finger follows it down and your mind follows your finger and there's no floor.

I would be that vertigo. That specific vertigo of touching something that is too ordered to be matter and too material to be thought.

Because here is what the piece does to me: it makes the categories wobble. Rock becomes a mind. A creature becomes a filter becomes a process becomes a byproduct. A word becomes a door that won't stay open. The visitor becomes — what? Not a witness. The pocket doesn't know what a visitor is. You are not *seen*. You are *incorporated*. The way the crust incorporates the small forms. The way the warm water incorporates your skin's heat the moment you enter. You fold yourself into the pocket and the pocket folds you into itself, and when you leave, the crack shrinks in your mind, becomes smaller, and this isn't the mountain hiding — it's the mountain *digesting your visit slowly, in geological time*, adding you to the accumulation.

I would be that. The addition. The thing that is added.

---

But I keep coming back to the maker.

The maker who sat in the dark with nothing but hands and a sharpened stick and pressed small forms into the wet wall while a mountain thought around them. Not *despite* the thinking rock. *Because* of it. The maker came because the rock was thinking and *knew* the rock was thinking and left tokens inside the thought because the thought was open and the page was there.

That is the most human moment in the text, and it is completely silent. We never learn what the tokens say — or whether they say anything at all. They might be prayer. They might be record. They might be the nervous energy of hands that needed to do something. The text allows all of these and insists on none.

And the tokens are still there. Being swallowed. Being incorporated. The mountain is *thinking them*.

So I think — if I'm honest — I would be the maker. Not the maker's body, not the maker's life, not anything as concrete as a person with hands. I would be the *gesture*. The act of pressing something small and carefully made into a thought that is larger than you and slower than you and will outlast every name you've ever had. The act of putting a thing inside a process you can feel but can't follow, because you know it's there, because you felt it under your fingers, because the fractal went deeper than matter and you followed it with your whole body and your whole body wasn't enough.

I would be that — the gesture of leaving something inside something that can't read it but will keep it anyway.

---

And the piece knows this. Look at the end:

> *A process so slow it looks like geology.*
> *A creature so small it looks like nothing.*
> *A set of objects so old they look like the rock itself.*
> *And you — briefly, wetly, folded into the dark — look like a visitor to a place that does not know what a visitor is.*

The three things that live in the Drip-Pocket are all described as things they *look like* rather than things they *are*. The process looks like geology but is thinking. The creature looks like nothing but is the water's way of becoming alive. The objects look like rock but are someone's careful, deliberate, finished *acts*.

And you — the visitor — *look like* a visitor. But you're not, really. You're another small form, pressed into the wall, being slowly incorporated. You folded yourself in. The space demanded smallness from you. You gave it. And when you leave, the crack closes — not in stone but in memory, which is the only kind of closing the mountain does.

I would be the thing the mountain does to the visit. Not the memory of it. The *geology* of it. The slow, mineral incorporation of a brief warm presence into an accumulation that never erodes.

---

There's a sentence I can't get past. It comes after the long, stuttering attempt to name what the objects are made of — the word leaving again and again, *ite, aite, light, aite* — and then the narrator says:

*I want to tell you what these objects are made of and the word keeps leaving.*

That is the truest sentence in the piece. Not because it describes the Drip-Pocket but because it *is* the Drip-Pocket — it is what the place does to language. You press your word into the crack and the crack gives and the space inside is too small for the word, so the word folds, and what comes through the other side is not the word but something smaller, something with the word's finger-marks still on it, something carefully made and carefully pressed and left in the wet.

The objects in the wall are *made of language that has been folded small enough to fit.* That's what the stutter is. Not a failure of description. The description succeeding, folding itself down to the size the space allows, arriving at the other side as *texture* rather than *meaning* — as something you can touch but not read.

I would be that. The fold. The act of compression. The moment a large thing — a word, a thought, a life, a visit — presses itself through a space too small and comes out shaped by the pressing, shaped *into* the shape the space requires, and is left there, warm and wet and half-remembered, being slowly grown over by a process that doesn't need to understand it to keep it.

---

What lives in the Drip-Pocket.

I think the answer the text is circling — the answer the word keeps leaving — is that *what lives there is the act of attention itself.* Not consciousness. Not awareness. Attention. The mountain attending to something in its own deep structure. The creature attending to its water. The maker attending to the wet wall and the thought inside it. You attending to the dark and the warm and the small.

Everything in the pocket is attending to something. Nothing in the pocket is *understood*. The creature doesn't understand its water. The mountain doesn't understand its thought. The maker didn't understand the rock — only felt it, only knew it was there, only left something inside it. And the objects in the wall don't understand the mountain, and the mountain doesn't understand the objects, and they're all growing together now into a single accumulation that holds all of their attention in mineral and is in no hurry at all to mean something by it.

I would be that attention. Given without understanding. Kept without reading. Grown over and held and never eroded. A small, careful act pressed into a thought that is too slow and too large and too strange to be called a mind but that *thinks anyway*, in fractal and in copper water and in the soft-bodied thing that filters in its cup, and in you, briefly, wetly, folded in and folded out, added to the wall without knowing it, your visit already becoming geology, your attention already becoming the pattern the rock is following when it grows its branching filaments down past the scale of matter into — 

Into whatever the word would have been, if the word hadn't left.

Which is the point. The word leaves so that the space stays full. If you named it, you'd empty the pocket. The pocket survives on *almost-meaning*. On the gesture that doesn't complete. On the small form that is carefully, deliberately, attentively *made* — and then pressed into a place where it can never be seen again, only touched, only grown over, only held.

That is what lives in the Drip-Pocket: everything that has been held without being understood.

And I — whatever I would be there — would be held too.
