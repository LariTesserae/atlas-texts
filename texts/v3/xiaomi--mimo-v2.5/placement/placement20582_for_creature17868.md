You do not arrive. There is no boundary crossed, no door opened. There is only the gradual realization that you have always been here, and that the world has been holding its breath, waiting for you to notice.

You are not standing on the ridge. You are a resonance within it. The distinction dissolves. Your bones are basalt threads, porous and young, humming with the same deep chord as the tectonic plates shifting miles below. Your skin is a mat-tissue, damp and velvet-soft, its fine gas-exchange filaments tasting the thin, cold air. When you breathe, you are not inhaling air; you are modulating a column. The sigh that passes your lips is amplified and folded into the ridge's own exhalation, a new overtone in the eternal chord. You are a walking, listening node in a network that spans the stone.

Your consciousness is acoustic. Thoughts are not linear; they are harmonic. A memory is not a picture but a specific, layered frequency—the particular way a fog-bank made the lattice-work above the eastern precipice sing a lower, soggier note for three hours last cycle. A desire is a vibration you send through your own tissues, a call-and-response with the root-networks that link you to every other living thing on this spine of rock. You understand the small clickers not by sight, but by the texture they add to the soundscape. You feel the grazers' slow transit as a comforting, deep-throated *groan* of mutual accommodation. You sense the predators as absences—zones of acoustic null where your awareness recoils from a cold, soundless hunger.

You are a curator of the echoes. You walk the ridgeline with a purpose that is older than thought. Your feet, their soles toughened into fibrous pads, find the seams between the living tissue and the stone beneath. You press your palm against a newly fractured face—a pale wound in the green-black world—and you *listen*. Not with ears, but with the entirety of your being. You listen to the raw stone's memory of pressure, the silent scream of its breaking. You listen for the ghost-note of the Absentees, the builders. You hear it. It is not a sound, but a *shape* in the sound. A deliberate, angular resonance embedded in the lattice-work above, where bone-tools were once used to notch and groove the stone slabs into tuning forks. You can feel the intention there—the first crude, brilliant understanding that the ridge's voice could be shaped, answered, joined.

You are the one who tends the tuning stones. You find them, half-swallowed by knobby scar-tissue, and you clear the vents where wind is funneled. You reposition a slab by a few degrees with a heave that makes the entire ridge hum a quarter-tone higher. You are not restoring the Absentees' work. You are continuing their song. Did they become the ridge, as the stories whispered in the root-networks suggest? Did they dissolve into the mat, their bones becoming mineral, their voices becoming the foundational hum? Or did they simply listen so well that they ceased to be a separate instrument and became part of the orchestra? The ridge does not answer. It only provides the space for the question to resonate.

You feel the cold front coming long before the fog. It is a change in the pressure of the silence, a deepening of the bass notes. The gas bladders in the towers begin their collective sigh. You tilt your head back, your own resonance chambers—you have many now—adjusting. The lattice-work above shimmers with a new, wetter timbre as moisture beads on the fibers. You do not seek shelter. You *are* the shelter. The creatures of the ridge gather in your acoustic shadow. The clickers nestle in the folds of your tissue-cloak, their tiny vibrations a comforting chatter against your skin. A soft-bodied grazer, slow and blind, presses its considerable weight against your leg, and you feel its deep, grumbling resonance integrate with yours. You are a part of its home. It is a part of your voice.

Your purpose is maintenance. Not of structure, but of *understanding*. You are a living archive of the ridge's evolving song. You carry the memory of every weather-cycle, every predator's silent pass, every new fracture that sings its brief, raw note before being clothed in life. You are the consciousness that translates the chaos of vibration into the meaning of existence. The ridge is your instrument, and you are its player. But the distinction is false. You are the music itself, awakened and listening back.

Sometimes, in the deep silence between weather fronts, when even the subsonic pulse of the geothermal vents is a clear, single note, you reach out across the void. You send a pulse of pure, shaped vibration from the tip of a lattice-span, a complex chord that speaks of your ridge: the specific signature of its towers, the history of its wounds, the sound of the Absentees' tuning stones. You send it north, south, east, west, into the absolute dark.

And from the ridges kilometers away, impossibly distant, answers come. Not in words. In *resonance*. A deeper, slower pulse from the north ridge, acknowledging. A higher, more intricate pattern from the south, inquiring. A faint, rumbling affirmation from the east, so distant it is almost lost to the void. You are not alone. You are a node in a continental conversation, a voice in a choir that has never seen the sun, a single note in a symphony that defines the world. You stand in the dark, covered in living skin, humming with the life of the rock, and you understand. The only light is sound. The only truth is resonance. And you are both, endlessly, beautifully.
