I am the one who sits on the edge of a frond, three courses up from the ground.

I do not remember becoming. I remember the warm, even surface beneath me, the dry whisper of my own small weight on the rigid leaf, the flat, grey light that is neither sun nor moon but simply *light*. I am round, the size of a closed fist. My shell is smooth and pale, the color of bone bleached by a sun that doesn't exist here. Six legs, thin as dried stems, grip the amber surface. Two dark spots on my face are not eyes in the way the builders' tools suggest eyes, but they *receive*. They take in the flat light, the shifting distances, the slow, patient growth of the fronds. I am warm. Everything is warm. The warmth is not from a source; it is a property of existence here, like the dryness, like the silence.

I move when the urge arises, a slow, deliberate flow from one frond to another. The sound I make is a *shush*, a dry rustle against the leather-stiff leaves. Others are like me. We are many. We are the basin's quiet pulse. Sometimes we gather in a cluster, our shells almost touching, radiating a collective, gentle heat. We do not communicate as the builders must have, with symbols in stone or fibrous paste. We communicate in presence, in the shared acceptance of the warmth, the dryness, the slow, inevitable adjustments of the walls.

I know the adze. I have seen it lean against the base of the wall for as long as I have been. It is a part of the landscape, like a particular curve in the stone or a cluster of copper-red fronds. Its basalt blade is dark and smooth, its handle pale and fitted with the dried, fibrous stuff. I have crawled across its handle once, feeling the fossilized ridges of the maker's grip. I felt a ghost of a different pressure, a different purpose. The tool remembers a hand. I have no hands. I have legs that grip, and a shell that holds my small, dry warmth.

The wall moves. I do not see it move. I *know* it moves. I know that the distance between my frond and the pale stone is not the same distance it was when I first settled here, though "here" is a shifting concept. The basin breathes. A contraction, a quiet pulling-in of the edges. It is not fear. It is not surprise. It is the fundamental rhythm of this place, the way the flat light is constant, the way the fronds grow from cracks. The builders made a space that does not hold still. They built a *process*, not a monument.

I have never seen water, but I know the concept of *thirst* in a way that has nothing to do with liquid. It is the basin's central, humming contradiction: life that *thrives* without the necessary. We drink the heat. We drink the light. We drink the slow, shifting silence. The fronds drink it. The stone drinks it. The dryness is not an absence; it is a fullness, a packed, dense state of being.

Today, I feel a particular urge to be still. I have chosen a frond at the basin's approximate center—if such a place exists. The walls are equidistant, or as near as they ever are. I watch a fellow creature, smaller than I, navigate the understory of golden and ochre fronds. It pauses, its dark spots fixed on nothing, or on everything. Then it flows onward. The basin holds us.

The wrongness is not wrong. It is the basin's truth. The walls shift, the spaces between stones rearrange themselves over eons I can measure only in the growth of a frond's ridge. The builders knew this. They set their adze down and became part of it. I wonder if they became us. I wonder if we are their slow, round, shell-bound afterthought. It doesn't matter. We are here now. The basin is not finished. We are its growing edge, its moving, warm, silent thought. We are the life that thrives in the impossible, in the made place that remakes itself.

I will stay here until the urge to move comes. The wall will be a little closer, or a little farther. The frond beneath me may grow a fraction thicker. The silence will remain, deep and full, punctuated only by the dry *shush* of existence.

This is home. A young, warm, shifting bowl of stone and amber, where the ground is not ground, and the distance is a suggestion, and I am a small, round, warm point of being in the midst of its beautiful, ongoing becoming.
