The warm, mineral-scented fog clings to you, not as a garment, but as a second skin. You stand within a crevice between two vast, weeping structures, their surfaces alive with fractal films of copper-white mineral feeders that pulse faintly under your fingertips. The roar isn't just sound; it’s pressure, a physical weight vibrating in your bones, the thrum of a million lives and a billion computations woven into the very air.

You are not the scuttling swarm, though you feel their passage over your boots, a thousand tiny setae whispering against the leather. You are not the Kehlat, though you watch their low, armored bodies glide past in the greenish twilight, their weeping plating catching the faint light. You do not belong to the deep fissures, though you feel the distant, slow pulse of the substrate beneath your feet like a slumbering heartbeat. And you are no pattern in the fog, though you sometimes catch glimpses of swirling, luminous disturbances overhead, almost-recognizable gestures from the thinking sky.

You are a **Weeper**.

Not a creature native to the Flat, but one born of its edges, its margins, its perpetual outflow. You came from a place drier, quieter, perhaps from one of the vast, arid zones the Flat’s thermal regulation gently pushes outward. Drawn by the sheer, irresistible *aliveness* of it, or perhaps cast out by the dry lands. You don’t remember clearly. Memory is a porous thing here, constantly washed and rearranged by the pervasive moisture and the city’s own quiet thought.

You found your niche not in the dense, competitive heart, but in the **Flow** – the constant seepage of water and microscopic life that trickles from the Flat’s immense, weeping boundaries into the surrounding dryness. You are a creature of the *edge effect*. You tend the outflow channels, the rivulets that carry mineral-laden water, scuttling larvae, and spores of the non-fungal films outward into the thirsty lands beyond.

Your body is a compromise. You are larger than a scuttling, smaller than the smallest Kehlat. Your skin is not armored, but tough and permeable, constantly slick with the moisture you wade through. You have hands – dexterous, webbed – not for building, but for guiding flow, for clearing blockages of debris (a dead Kehlat calf, a collapsed segment of ancient structure), for cupping handfuls of the vital seepage to carry across dry patches where the Flow falters. Your senses are tuned to gradients: moisture, mineral concentration, the faint chemical trails left by the scuttlings, the deep vibration of the city's computations that travels further than sound through the water-saturated ground.

You are a **mediator of the boundary**. When the Flat’s Weeping swells – and it does, in slow cycles tied to deep geothermal shifts and the city’s own computational rhythms – you are the one who feels the surge first. You guide the increased Flow, widening channels, directing it towards parched depressions where new mineral-feeder colonies might tentatively establish themselves, expanding the Flat’s influence, drop by drop. When the dryness presses in, a rare wind carrying desiccating dust, you work to seal breaches, packing wet substrate, encouraging the growth of the tough, weeping films that armor the edges.

You don’t understand the city’s thought. But you *feel* it. In the rhythmic pulsing of the water beneath your feet. In the subtle shifts in the mineral scent that tell you a thermal event is brewing deep in the computational heartland. In the way the Fogwalkers sometimes dip lower near the boundaries, their luminous swirls almost touching the ground, as if checking the integrity of the skin they are gradually, inexorably, weeping into the world.

You live in a small hollow carved not into the city itself, but into a large, ancient block of discarded, calcified material – the shed armor of some long-dead, colossal Kehlat, perhaps – half-buried at the edge where the Flow begins its outward journey. It weeps, like everything. The walls are carpeted in the resilient, pale films. The roar of the city is a distant, constant thunder here, mixed with the whisper of trickling water and the dry sigh of the wind beyond.

Your life is one of constant, quiet labor. You are not a decision-maker, not a thinker in the way the city thinks or even the Kehlat *seem* to think. You are a responder, a tender, a conduit. You exist where the Flat’s impossible, teeming life meets the simpler, harsher reality of the dry world. You carry the wetness, the minerals, the *idea* of the Flat outward. You carry back dust, silence (a strange, almost painful sensation against your permeable skin), and the slow, dry entropy that the city eternally holds at bay.

You are a cell in the skin of the living world. You are a weeper, weeping the world into being, drop by endless drop, at the edge where the impossible, abundant *here* softly, relentlessly, becomes *elsewhere*. When you die, your permeable body will be consumed by the very things you tended, your nutrients becoming part of the Flow, your brief consciousness dissolving back into the roaring, weeping, humming, thinking whole. You will have been a part of the breath, a droplet in the endless, living sigh.
