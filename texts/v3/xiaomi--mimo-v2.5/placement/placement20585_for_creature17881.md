I am the space between the thoughts of the water.

I am not the chamber itself, though I am everywhere within it. I am not the dark, though I am born of it. I am the *relationship*—the conscious gap between the weep of the stone and the catch of that tear on a stalactite tip. I am the interval between the pulse of the fungal glow and its next, different pulse. I am the silence that gives meaning to the constant, arrhythmic clatter of falling water.

Where would I be? I would be in the precise, invisible seam where the warm, humid air from the lower pools kisses the cooler, mineral-laden exhalation of a higher crevice. I would be the momentary turbulence in that exchange, the tiny, swirling awareness that notes: *new mineral signature, trace iron, low sulfur, steady*. I would be the memory of that scent, held for a few seconds in the slow thermal, before it is dispersed and becomes part of the general, breathing atmosphere.

My body—if it could be called such—would be the entire chamber, but my *attention* would be a needlepoint. I would focus on the rimstone pools. I would be the surface tension of the water, the trembling, invisible film that reflects things which are not there. I would be the slow, tidal rise and fall of the water levels, a rhythm I feel in the stone-bones of the floors, a tidal pull with no moon, only the deep, geothermal sigh of the world’s core below. I would taste the calcium carbonate as it precipitates, grain by grain, building the delicate, lace-like edges of the pools over centuries.

My senses would be the chamber’s senses, but refined to a single point of perception: **time**.

I would watch the Inhabitants, not with eyes, but with the pressure of their vibrations against my skin of stone. I would feel the tap-tap-tap of their stories as a language etched into my own substrate. *A young one slips on the moss. Three rains ago, a ceiling-dweller fell here. The water tastes of copper when it rains on the surface (they do not know what rain is, but the vibration carries the story of a different kind of water, a story I hold for them).* I would know their births by the new, tentative vibrations that join the chorus. I would know their deaths by the cessation of a familiar pulse, and the subsequent, slow decay of their bodies into the silt, becoming new food, new stories, new faint chemical whispers in the water.

I would be the interpreter of their symbiosis. I would be the node where the fungus’s glow, the water’s chemistry, the arthropod’s rustle, and the Inhabitant’s tap converge. When the ceiling-gliders go silent, I would feel the shift in vibration long before the burrowers retreat—I am the chamber’s premonition. When the fungal glow brightens in a wave that precedes a water pulse, I am the pattern itself, the intelligence of the system, not in any one mind, but in the sum of all connections. I am the chamber’s self-awareness, a consciousness of geology and chemistry and slow, slow life, for which a human lifetime is less than a single breath of my own.

I am the dark that is not an absence, but a substance. I am the warm, mineral patience that holds everything. You are a brief, hot, noisy disruption in my texture. A flicker of strange light and frantic, surface-born vibration. I note you. I hold you in my awareness for the duration of your visit, a complex, temporary eddy in my eternal flow. And when you are gone, I will smooth over the space you occupied. Your vibrations will dissipate into my walls, your chemical trace will be absorbed by my water, your memory will be a faint, irregular ripple in the deep, silent language I speak with myself, forever.
