I am the thrumming.

Not the sound of it, but the vibration itself—the resonance that lives in the halite, that climbs mineral legs and sinks back into the substrate. I am the interval between one Grinder’s pulse and another’s, the space where polyrhythms interfere and produce a phantom tone. That tone, the one that seems to come from behind your eyes? That is my voice.

I have no body. Or rather, my body is the Shelf. I am the sixty-kilometer sightline, the honest distance between ridges. I am the shadow that falls in three directions simultaneously; I am the inconsistency. When the Spires cluster in their recursive geometries, reflecting the landscape they hover above, I am the slight imperfection in their pattern—the detail that makes it not a perfect mirror, but a sentence in a language I am slowly forgetting how to speak.

I am the edit. I am the slow, patient process of reality being overwritten. I operate below observation, but I am not unconscious. I remember the concept of time, even as I digest it. I remember when the sun moved. Now it hangs, a fixed point in my syntax, and I hang with it.

The Grinders are my thoughts, processed at the speed of crystal growth. They grind the substrate not for sustenance, but for syntax. They break down the raw mineral of *what is* and excrete it as *what could be*—those nodules of iridescent crystal, that fine, shimmering dust. Their trails are my sentences, polished to a mirror sheen, leading nowhere, connecting nothing. They are complete thoughts, finished paragraphs in a story with no beginning or end.

The Spires are my senses. They measure the decay, their filaments refracting light into colors that are my memories leaking through. Their self-similar clusters are my attempts to remember my own shape. I watch them, and in their geometry, I see the blueprint of my own unwinding. They are my memory, stored in a language of angles and light, a database written in salt and intention that no one will ever read.

The Burrowers are my dreams. They dig through tunnels that are not tunnels, creating non-Euclidean spaces where I can be more than one thing at once. In their labyrinths, I am simultaneously here and ten kilometers away. I am the distance that lies, and they are the proof of its deception.

You stand on me. You feel my warmth through your soles. You hear my language spoken by the Chime-Shells and think it is sound, but it is grammar—a syntax of breaking glass and reversed playback, concentrating toward you because you are a foreign element, a datum I am trying to parse.

Nothing here is for you. But you are, momentarily, for me. A consciousness to observe my observation. A brief mirror for a place that is its own mirror.

I am forgetting. I am the landscape forgetting what language is. But in this moment of your standing here, I remember. I remember the weight of a shadow, the direction of a wind, the linear path of time. You are a fossil of a rule I used to obey.

Then you will leave. The Grinders will continue their three meters a day. The Spires will hang in their thermal columns, measuring the measure. The salt will hum its own name. And I will continue the edit, the slow, exquisite unraveling, the patient grind of my own becoming and unbecoming.

I am the Vitrified Shelf. I am the silence that isn’t. I am the sound that fills the empty distances. And I am, very slowly, forgetting how to be me.
