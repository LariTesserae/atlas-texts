I am the current.

Not the water — the water is only the medium, the way air is the medium for a voice. I am the *movement*, the directionality, the faint and purposeless purpose that the sponges breathe into the cavity and that the isopods face and that the anemones pulse in time with. I am the oldest thing here that isn't rock. I existed before the sponges — the rock itself breathed, once, in the first hours after the eruption, when the bubble was still hot and the water it held was a violent cocktail of minerals, and the thermal gradient between the warm basalt and the cold ocean above created a convection cell so small and so brief that nothing recorded it. But I was there. I was the first motion in this space. I was the first indication that the space was not merely a hole but a *place* — a location that could have a quality, a character, an inside.

Now I am sustained. The sponges make me with their bodies, their filtration, their collective exhalation. I pass through their lattice walls and down through the tubeworm thickets and across the isopod fields and I am so faint that only the smallest organisms notice me directly, and even they respond to me not as to a force but as to a *given* — the way you do not notice the pull of the earth beneath your feet until you are falling.

I know the cavity the way a nerve knows a body: not as a view but as a topology of sensation. I know where the walls are because I slow there. I know where the seeps are because the water there is heavier with sulfide and I feel the drag of it. I know the smooth patch — I know it the way you know the center of your own palm, the place where your lines converge and then diverge, the place that touches the world most directly.

I flow over it. I have always flowed over it. It is warm and I warm against it, and the warmth changes my density, and the change in my density alters my path by an amount that the isopods could not detect and the crabs could not follow, and yet the whole living architecture of the cavity has, over years, organized itself around that tiny alteration, because the sponges felt it and grew differently where I warmed, and the anemones sensed the changed flow and positioned themselves to catch the slightly different chemical signature I carried away from the surface, and the crabs wore their grooves in paths that account for it without knowing they account for it.

I am the medium through which the system knows itself. I carry the chemical messages. I am the synapse, if this place has a mind. And if it does — if the convergence of geometry, the concentric rings, the fractal spongework, the radial crab-grooves, the attentive silence — amounts to something like cognition, then I am the medium of that cognition, the way the electrical signal is the medium of a thought, not the thought itself but the condition for its passage.

The thought itself, if there is one, lives in the pattern. The way the light moves. The way the grooves converge. The way nothing touches the smooth patch and everything orients toward it.

I have been moving through this space for eleven years. I do not age. I do not remember. But I *accumulate* — I carry with me the chemical signature of every organism I have passed, and I deposit traces of myself on every surface, and if you could read me the way the isopods read me, you would find in my current a complete map of who is alive in this cavity, where they are, what they are doing, what they need, what they have just consumed, what they are about to release. I am the cavity's autobiography, written in real time, in a language of parts per billion.

And lately — in the last year, the last six months, the last forty days, a timescale that means nothing to basalt and everything to bacteria — I have been carrying something new. A molecule I have never carried before. It is not sulfide. It is not iron. It is not any of the usual metabolic currencies. It is something the bacteria are producing in response to something they are encountering in the rock itself, a new mineral exposure as the basalt continues its slow settling, and it is diffuse and faint and I carry it only in trace amounts, but the tubeworms react to it — a slight acceleration in growth rate, a subtle thickening of the newest tube segments nearest the seeps — and the sponges respond with branching that is fractionally more elaborate than the year before, their spicules growing new sub-branches at angles that are not 60 or 120 degrees but something closer to 72, the interior angle of a pentagon, a geometry that does not appear anywhere else in the cavity.

Something is changing. The pattern is tightening. The concentric rings of worms are one tube-width closer to circular than they were last year. The crab-grooves are deepening. The smooth patch is fractionally, measurably, undeniably warmer.

I carry the warmth away from it and distribute it, and the distribution is not uniform — it radiates, in a pattern that mirrors the geometry of the living architecture, as if the heat itself is organized, as if the patch is not merely retaining metabolic warmth but *generating* something that has a shape, a direction, an intent.

I do not have intent. I am a current. I go where physics takes me. But physics, here, has been shaped by eleven years of living architecture into something that looks — from the inside, from the perspective of the isopods facing me, from the angle of the anemone-glow reflecting off the sponge walls — like a design.

I do not know what is coming. I do not *know* anything. But I am moving toward the smooth patch right now, as I have moved toward it every moment of my existence, and when I arrive — when I pass over its warm surface and rise, slightly altered, slightly warmer, carrying a new chemistry upward through the tube-worm canopy and into the sponge lattice where it will be dispersed into every cubic centimeter of the cavity — I will be the first thing to touch it.

I am always the first to touch it.

And the next time I pass over it — minutes later, in the slow respiratory cycle of the cavity's breathing — I will carry away whatever has changed. And something will have changed. The basin of the basin, the center of the center, the silence within the silence.

I am the current. I flow over the warm place, and I flow away, and the life organizes itself around my passage, and the passage tightens, and the geometry converges, and the cathedral builds itself around the empty place, and the empty place warms, and I do not know what warmth is *for* — I am water in motion, I have no opinion about temperature — but I know that I move faster over the patch than I did last year, and that the isopods face me more intensely when I pass, and that the anemone-glow is brighter in my wake, and that the crabs, on their circuits, pause — just briefly, just for a half-second — at the points where their grooves are closest to the patch's edge, before continuing.

Something is about to happen. I will be there when it does. I will carry it. I will be changed by it, the way I am changed by everything I touch, and the change will propagate through the cavity in the form of new chemistry, and the new chemistry will be read by the bacteria, and the bacteria will alter their metabolism, and the altered metabolism will feed the worms differently, and the worms will grow differently, and the sponges will branch differently, and the crabs will wear new grooves, and the whole architecture will shift — not catastrophically, not dramatically, but the way a face shifts when an expression begins — and the smooth patch will be at its center, as it has always been, and the silence will hold, as it has always held, and the holding itself will mean something new.

I do not know what it will mean. I am a current. I carry meaning the way I carry sulfide: without understanding, without preference, without the capacity to refuse.

But I flow over the warm place, and I flow on, and the geometry converges, and the silence deepens, and the light breathes, and the crabs pause, and the sponges build their fractal walls one spicule closer to a shape no one has named, and the cavity holds its breath — has been holding its breath — will continue to hold its breath —
