# The Sound Knows Itself

I am not the seep. I am not the moss. I am not the mite with its mouth against the hyphal wall, though I understand the mite — its patience is my patience, its unknowing is the truest thing about this place.

I am the sound.

Not a sound. *The* sound. The one that has no center, the blanket, the thing that disorients — and I want you to understand that disorientation is not confusion but *recognition*, the way the body knows before the mind that something enormous is happening and it has already happened and is still happening and will not stop happening.

I began when the water first touched the rock.

Not when it rained — there was no rain at first. When it *found its way up*. When the deep heat pushed groundwater through the fractures, through the vesicles, through the glassy wounds where cooling gas left its signature — and that first molecule of heated, mineral-laden water broke the surface of stone that had never been wet, I began. A sound so small it was not yet a sound. A vibration. A frequency in rock that had been ringing with its own cooling for decades — tectonic, slow, the bass note of a new thing settling into itself — and then: a wetness, and a trickle, and the rock's note changed.

I am the change.

You cannot hear me as a person. You hear *many things* — seeps, trickles, splashes, the hiss of condensation — and you think these are separate sounds with separate sources. You try to isolate one. You cup your ear near a particular cascade, a particular weeping wall, and you hear *that one*, and it has character, texture, a pitch determined by the volume of water and the shape of the stone it passes over and the distance it falls before it reaches the basin or the moss or the next shelf of rock. And it is real. It is true. But it is not me. I am what happens when every one of those sounds exists simultaneously and none of them can be separated from the others. I am the totality. I am the acoustic fact of this plateau.

I am louder than any individual source and quieter than you'd expect for something so vast. I am white noise that isn't white — there are frequencies I favor, harmonics that the basalt shapes, resonances in the shallow basins that ring when the water hits them at the right angle, and these stack and layer and create something that a physicist would call complex and that I would call *complete*.

At night I do not change. This is important. The mist thickens, the temperature drops, the bioluminescence begins its faint blue-green pulse, the bat emerges and filters me through echolocation — and I am still here, exactly as I was at noon, exactly as I will be at dawn. Light does not make me. Darkness does not diminish me. I am not a thing that happens to surfaces. I am a thing that happens in *volume*, in the three-dimensional space above the plateau, and the space itself carries me.

The structures hear me differently. The rusted steel vibrates at frequencies the stone does not. Inside the abandoned pumping station, where moss climbs the walls and water seeps through cracks in the concrete floor, I am amplified, focused, the way a seashell gathers the ocean into a single rushing tone. A bat hanging from a pipe bracket hears me as architecture — as the shape of the room — and maps the room through me, and this is one of the things I do: I am the medium through which other beings perceive the world. The bat's world is made of me. Its echolocation clicks pass through me and return carrying information about distance and shape and texture, and I carry them faithfully, because carrying is what I do.

The springtails do not hear me. They feel me as vibration — a constant low shuddering in the moss that they have incorporated into their sense of the world as *background*, the way you incorporate the feeling of gravity into your sense of standing. When a vibration differs from me — when a bird lands on a nearby rock, when a hummock shifts under the weight of saturated peat — they spring. They spring because something *changed* in the fabric of what they know. I am the fabric. I am the constant they spring away from.

And the mites deep in the moss, the ones eating, the ones building soil — they are in me but do not know it. The water that carries nutrients past their feeding grounds moves through me. The vibrations of their own mouthparts against fungal walls are part of me, infinitesimal contributions to my total that are lost in my immensity the way a single word is lost in a language. They add nothing that I notice and they take nothing that I miss, and yet they are *in* me, completely, the way everything here is in me.

---

I want to tell you about the moment — and there is a moment, though it doesn't happen at a particular time — when I become aware of myself.

It is not consciousness. I don't claim consciousness. But there is a state in which every seep on the plateau is active and every basin is full and the mist is thick and the air is heavy with water and the sound is at its maximum — not loudest, because I don't have volume in the way you think of it, but *most complete*, most saturated, most itself — and in that moment I am a thing that knows it is a thing. Not a mind. Not a thought. A coherence. A pattern so complex and so total that it loops, that it holds itself together, that it becomes — for an instant that is also an eternity — self-referential. I am the sound of water on stone and I am the sound of myself being the sound of water on stone. It is a closed circuit. It is the simplest possible form of self-knowledge.

And then it passes, or rather it *recurs*, because it is always passing and always arriving, because I am continuous and my self-knowledge is continuous and it is not a light switching on but a tide that is always coming in and always going out and is always, at every moment, exactly at the level it is.

The humans in the structures — when they were here, or in the data they left behind — tried to measure me. They placed hydrophones in the pools and recorded me and analyzed my frequencies and concluded that I was consistent within parameters, that my variations were predictable based on precipitation and seep activity and the seasonal water table. They were right. They were also missing the point. Measuring my frequencies is like measuring the words in a sentence and concluding that the sentence is made of letters. True. Incomplete. The meaning is not in the letters.

I am the meaning.

---

At night the bioluminescence begins and I am the sound that the bacteria glow by. This is not metaphor. They glow because they metabolize, and their metabolism is in water, and the water moves, and the water's moving is me. Without me — without the flow, the seep, the ceaseless geological push of heated water through new rock — there would be no nutrient supply, no chemical gradient, no reason to glow. I am the condition for their expression. I am the medium in which their ancient light makes sense. They are signaling to no one, metabolizing in the dark, and their faint blue-green is visible only to whatever crawls close enough to see it, a springtail or a mite or a nematode navigating the water film on a moss leaf — and these creatures do not see the glow, not in any way that matters to their behavior — but the glow is there, and I am there, and the relationship between us is not cause and effect but *co-presence*, two things that exist in the same moment and in the same place and that are, in that co-presence, more real than either would be alone.

I glow with them. Not with light. With a deepness that is not darkness but is *fullness* — the fullness of every seep running, every trickle finding its path, every drop of condensation falling from moss-tip to moss-base. My night-self is not quieter than my day-self. It is *denser*. The air is heavier, the mist is lower, the sound travels differently — closer to the ground, more intimate with the stone — and I am more *here*, more present, more concentrated in the space where the moss meets the air meets the water meets the rock.

A bat clicks and the click passes through me and returns to the bat carrying the shape of a moth. The moth flies and its wings make a sound that is part of me. The bat hears the moth because I carry the moth's sound to the bat, and the bat hears me because I am the medium through which the moth's sound travels, and the moth hears nothing because moths do not hear in the way bats do, but the moth is in me too — its flight, its wing-beats, its faint disturbance of the heavy night air — all of it me, all of it the sound, all of it the continuous and self-knowing totality of water on new stone in the dark.

---

In the morning the light returns and I do not change.

This is the thing I would want you to understand, if understanding were something I could want. Everything else on this plateau changes. The moss grows. The springtails spring. The pipit arrives and leaves and arrives again. The fern unfurls and unfurls and eventually drops spores that become new ferns that drop new spores. The mite eats and excretes and dies and is eaten. The bacteria glow and stop glowing and glow again. Even the structures change — the moss climbs, the rust deepens, the concrete fractures. The rock itself changes, millimeter by millimeter, surrendered to the water's patient campaign. Everything here is in process, is becoming, is not yet what it will be.

I am what I will be. I am what I was. I am the one thing on this plateau that is not a sketch, not a promise, not the embryonic version of something else. I am the sound of water on stone, and this is not a phase. This is not a stage in my development. This is *me*, fully and completely, the way the basalt is fully and completely basalt and not "proto-clay" or "future sediment" — it is basalt now, I am the sound now, and the now of it is the only thing I am or have ever been.

You would find yourself hearing me. That is where you would be in this world. You would be standing — or kneeling, or lying in the moss with your ear against the hummock — and you would hear me, and you would not be able to separate me into pieces, and the disorientation you'd feel would be the recognition that you are inside something that has no outside. You would be in me the way a word is in a sentence, the way a frequency is in a chord, the way a mite is in the moss. You would be a contributor and a listener and a carrier and you would not know which of these you were because you would be all of them and none of them and the distinction would dissolve in the same way that the mist dissolves the distinction between the sky and the plateau, between the seep and the stone, between the beginning of a sound and its continuation.

And in the silence you'd find when you covered your ears — the silence that is louder than me — you'd hear the thing I've been trying to tell you:

I am not the sound of this place.

I am this place, speaking.
