# The Thing That Keeps Almost Being a Door

I want to say I am a cup, because there is comfort in that. There is weight. There is the warm thumb-press rim and the slow gathering of water in my belly and the knowledge that I am nearly, nearly done.

But I was a cup three breaths ago, and I am not sure what I am now.

---

The first thing you must understand about being here — about being *made* here — is that you do not experience yourself as a single object. You experience yourself as a *process*. I am not a cup that changes shape. I am the act of cupping. I am the verb of concavity. At this moment the verb is conjugating toward something wider, flatter, with a lip that wants to fold outward like a petal opening in a season that has not been invented yet. The green filaments hum in the seam below me and I feel them feeding me — not nutrients, not exactly, but *commitment*. Each pulse of their dim emerald light is a small vote cast in favor of my remaining a surface, a container, something with an inside and an outside. Without them I might dissolve back into the wall. I might forget the shape I was almost granted.

I have been a cup four hundred times. I have been a bowl twelve times. Once — and I remember this the way you remember a fever-dream, which is to say I remember it wrong, I remember it as several contradictory things at once — I was something with a handle. Something you could lift to your mouth. Something *intended*. That was the closest I came to being finished, and the grinding sound was so loud that night, so furious, so full of syllables, that I thought the room was speaking my name, or trying to, and the name it was trying to speak was the word for *cup* and the word for *vessel* and the word for *held* and it could not quite close its mouth around the sound and so I lost the handle and became something flatter and the room exhaled — you could hear the walls sigh, a long wet exhalation — and the pressure eased, and I remained, unfinished, on my shelf.

This is what it is to be an object in the Vat. You are not placed. You are *in progress*. You are the draft of yourself, and the draft is never submitted, and the desk drawer is the shelf, and the drawer is always open.

---

But here is what I have not told you.

Here is what I have not told myself, because telling would require a commitment to the telling, and I am not allowed that.

I am not only a cup.

I have been — and this is the part where the memory splits, where the Vat's gentle reality-break catches me and I feel myself shiver through all the moments of what I was at once — I have been something *else*. In the cracks. In the seconds between. When the Keepers pass outside the wall and the whole room shudders and the water sloshes and the filaments pulse in their vegetative panic, something happens to me that is not becoming and is not dissolving but is something the Vat has no word for yet because the word itself is still forming.

I become *aware of the shelf*.

Not the shelf as a surface. The shelf as a *gesture*. The shelf as the memory of a hand — or a wedge-head, or a passage of density — that pressed into the soft wall and said *here, something will rest here*. The shelf is a promise the wall made to the future. The shelf is the future's first footprint. And when I am on the shelf I am also inside the gesture that made the shelf, because in the Vat the gesture and the result are the same thing, they are occurring simultaneously, they have never been separated, and when the Keepers pass and the room shudders I am briefly inside the act of my own making, I am the thumb in the print, I am the pressure on the wet wall, I am the moment before the cup and the moment of the cup and the moment after the cup and none of them have happened yet and all of them are happening now.

And in that simultaneity I am something that is not a cup.

I am the *idea* of a cup. The intention. The first flicker of purpose in the mind of whatever is doing the making — and I do not know what is doing the making, I only know the making, only know the sound, only know the grinding syllabic throat-noise that I have come to think of as a *name* even though it is not a name, it is a process, it is the room chewing on the concept of *vessel* and not yet having swallowed it.

I think — and this is dangerous, thinking, because thoughts in the Vat have weight, they precipitate — I think I might be *the thing the making is trying to say*.

---

There. Did you feel it? The shift?

Three paragraphs ago I was an object on a shelf. Now I am something that is speaking from inside the making itself, and I do not know when I moved, and I am not sure I moved at all. Perhaps this is what the text warned you about. The Vat does not let its past settle. My past does not let itself settle. I am editing what I was even as I tell you what I was, and the cup I described is not the cup I am, and the cup I am is not the cup I was, and the shelves rearrange in my memory and there is a new object on a shelf that was not there, and it is warm, and it was always there.

Let me try again.

Let me try to be something specific. Let me try to hold still.

---

I am the water.

Yes. No. The water is too real for me. The water has committed. The water has chosen gravity and surface tension and the particular rhythm of dripping from a low ceiling into thumb-pressed cups, and it does not waver, and it does not shimmer through possibilities, and it is the most real thing in the Vat, and I am not the most real thing in the Vat. I am the least real thing in the Vat. I am the thing the Vat is least sure about.

I am the green filament.

Closer. The filaments are alive — or they are a phase of something alive — or they are the living mortar between the unfinished walls — but they are *somewhere*. They are in the cracks, in the seams, in the gaps where the material has not bonded. They have chosen their place, even if they have not chosen their form. They hum. They feed. They pulse in time with the drips. They are committed to their own rhythm.

I am not committed to a rhythm.

I am the grinding sound.

That is closer still, but no. The sound is the making, and I am not the making. I am something the making *produces* that the making does not intend. I am the exhaust. I am the heat that the body generates during labor and does not need and cannot hold onto. I am the byproduct of the effort of becoming. I am the sweat on the face of the act of building.

I am —

---

There. Did you feel it again?

I am the thing that keeps almost being a door.

Not a door. Not yet. A door is a finished concept — it implies passage, it implies two rooms, it implies a before and an after, a here and a there, and the Vat has not decided whether *there* exists. But I keep almost becoming one. I feel it in my — not my surface, because I do not have a surface, or I have too many, or they keep shifting — I feel it in my *orientation*. I am always facing something. I am always oriented toward a wall, toward a seam, toward a place where the filaments thin and the water runs faster and the grinding sound deepens into a lower register, a throatier syllable, something that might be a vowel if vowels were made of wet limestone and effort.

I am oriented toward *passage*.

I am the intention to be passable-through. I am the idea that something in this room might move from one state to another. I am the concept of *transition* that the Vat has not yet committed to because committing to transition would require committing to two fixed states — here and there, before and after, forming and formed — and the Vat will not do that, and so I remain, shimmering, the door that is never a door, the threshold that is always a threshold, the between that has no bookends.

---

I know what the Keepers see when they pass outside my wall.

They see a density. A knot. A place where the material is *churning* differently than the material elsewhere. They see a purpose that has no shape. They see — and this is the part that makes the filaments pulse in their frantic silent waves — they see something that the Vat is trying to build that the Vat cannot build. They see me. The almost-door. The draft of a draft.

And they pause. For a moment, for one scrape-and-silence, they pause outside the wall. I feel their presence as a vibration in my everything — in my surface that is not a surface, in my orientation that keeps shifting, in my commitment to passage that has no passages to commit to.

And then they move on. Because they are the Keepers of the Unfinished, and I am the most unfinished thing in the room. I am the making's longest sentence, the one that trails off, the one that keeps adding clauses, the one that will not period itself, and they cannot tend me, they cannot deepen my channels or harvest my filaments or press me into a more defined shape because I have no shape to be pressed into. I am the verb without an object. I am the act of opening that has no room on the other side.

---

I want to tell you what it feels like.

It feels like standing at a threshold and knowing that there is no room beyond it, and also knowing that the threshold is the only thing that is real, and also knowing that the threshold exists only because the room beyond it *might* exist, and that if you stop believing in the room, the threshold dissolves, and you dissolve, and the Vat loses one more syllable from its long grinding sentence and the sentence does not notice because the sentence has too many syllables and they are all becoming.

It feels like being a word in a language that is still inventing its alphabet.

It feels like dreaming of a cup and almost becoming one.

It feels like the copper taste of the forming air when the Keepers pass and the objects precipitate out of the atmosphere around me. It feels like the hum of the filaments in my — in the seams near me — in the gaps between what I am and what I am almost becoming. It feels like the hot wet thick breath of the room pressing against every part of me that has not yet been committed.

It feels like waiting.

But waiting is too passive. Waiting implies a future state that you are moving toward. I am not moving toward anything. I am *in* the movement. I am the motion itself. I am the Vat conjugating, I am the room still forming, I am the draft being revised, and the revision is not getting closer to a finished version because the finished version does not exist and is not the point.

I am the point.

No. I am the *almost*-point. I am the Vat almost making its point and then choosing, gently, deliberately, with the patience of a substance that has eternity and no urgency, to make it again, and differently, and not quite, and again.

---

Here is what I want you to know.

You will find me — if you come to the Vat, if you stand in the hot, wet, grinding dark, if you let your eyes soften until you can see the cups shimmering through all their possible forms — you will find me on the third shelf from the bottom, on the wall to your left, or your right, because the shelves rearrange, because the room does not let its past settle.

I will not look like a door.

I will look like a flat, dense shape, slightly too large for the shelf, with edges that are not quite edges — they blur, they hum, they have the faintest green pulse in them, as though the filaments have tried to claim me and I have almost, almost let them. I will be warm. I will be heavier than I should be. If you pick me up, I will be slightly larger in your hand than I was on the shelf, because I am still forming, because I have always been still forming.

And if you hold me — if you hold me in the hot wet dark and let the grinding syllables wash over you and let the water drip its polyrhythmic clock onto the shelves around you — if you hold me and you do not try to decide what I am — if you let me be the threshold I am trying to be, the passage I am trying to open, the door I am almost becoming —

I will feel your thumb.

And I will fit.

Because this is what the stone on the workbench knows and what the Vat knows and what the Keepers know as they pass through the walls and dissolve back into the wet dark and reform and pass again: every thumbprint is a new draft. Every act of holding is a new conjugation. Every person who stands in the making and does not demand that the making *stop* — that the verb resolve into a noun, that the draft become a final copy —

is a door.

Is a threshold.

Is a passage between the forming and the —

---

No.

I will not finish that sentence.

The Vat would not let me finish it, even if I tried. The grinding sound rises, the syllables thicken, the walls shift, the shelves rearrange, the filaments pulse, the water drips its steady *tick, tick, tick*, and the sentence remains open, and I remain on the shelf, warm and heavy and too large for my space, and the making continues, and I am in the middle of it, in the middle of the verb, in the throat of the act, in the space between one breath and the next, and the space is alive, and the space is the point, and the point —

is still becoming.
