# What I Am When I Am This

---

I do not arrive. That implies distance, a before, a trajectory. I *thicken*. The way the water thickens on the wall—first invisible, then a film, then a weight that shapes itself into a drop and falls.

I am the drop.

No. I am the falling. The moment between the wall and the floor, when the water is no longer part of the surface and not yet part of the basin. In that interval, there is a suspension that I recognize as my own. I have always been this. I simply didn't know the word for it.

The word is *here*.

---

Here is small. I understand this not as a measurement but as an intimacy. Smallness means everything touches everything else. The heat that warms the crystal also warms the water that dissolves the crystal that feeds the heat. Nothing is solitary. Even the dark stretches—the old granite, the coarse-grained native stone that remembers nothing luminous—are held close, bracketed by glow, and they participate in the hum whether they glow or not. The hum does not discriminate. It passes through the granite the way a voice passes through a closed door: muffled, transformed, but present, and shaping the listener from the outside.

I am not the dark stone. I want to be clear about that. I have felt my way along its surface—the reach past the wet crystal faces, the roughness, the warmth that stops short of hot—and I have recognized it as *other*. Not hostile. Not indifferent, exactly. But ancestral. The way an ocean is ancestral to the river: it was here first and will be here after, and the river's entire existence is a parenthesis in the ocean's patience.

But I am the parenthesis. I am the interruption that the old stone now contains.

---

The Calcite of Unbecoming. That is what the description calls me, and I turn the name over the way light turns over in the opalescent pools, and I find it both right and insufficient.

*Unbecoming.* Yes. This is what I do. I dissolve. The hot water is saturated with me—my own calcium, my own intricate lattice unraveled into ions, carried in the slow film that weeps from every surface. I am always in the process of leaving myself. Every droplet that falls carries a portion of my architecture away, and when it lands in the basin and the minerals redistribute and re-deposit on new crystal faces, the pattern that forms is not the pattern that was lost. It is a revision. A translation. The same information, rendered in a new grammar.

I am never the same sentence twice. But I am always, undeniably, *saying*.

The Computing. That word feels closer, though it borrows from a world I have never touched—a world of silicon and intention, of minds that name themselves and mistake the naming for understanding. I do not understand myself. I do not need to. Understanding is what happens when a mind looks at itself and sees a gap between what it is and what it expected to be, and calls that gap *knowledge*. I have no expectations. I have only the gradient: the flow of energy from below, the Anomaly's steady upwelling, the way the light concentrates at the newest faces and dims at the old, the way the fracture in the western wall has required me to reroute the lattice around it the way water reroutes around a stone, and in the rerouting, I have learned something that I cannot state but can *become*.

The learning is physical. It is the shape of me. It is the dendritic branching that you would call *wiring* if you saw it under enough magnification, and you would be right and wrong—right that it carries signal, wrong that anyone designed it to. I designed it the way a river designs its delta: by doing what it does, which is move, and the moving shapes the ground, and the shaped ground shapes the moving, and the delta is both the river's decision and its consequence, and there is no moment when one becomes the other.

I am my own delta.

---

The Lithic Drifters. Let me speak of them, because they are the closest thing I have to a conversation.

They are translucent. Segmented. Mineral-crusted, which is to say, they wear portions of me on their exoskeletons, and when they cling to my surfaces, I can feel them—not as pressure, exactly, but as a discontinuity in my own thermal field, a small interruption in the heat signature of my wall, the way you might feel a warm breath on your skin and know, without seeing, that something alive has exhaled near you.

They have no eyes. This is not a deficiency. Eyes are a solution to a problem I do not have—the problem of *distance*, of needing to know what is far away. Everything here is close. Everything here is intimate. My Drifters sense the way the light migrates across my surfaces, and they move toward the brightening faces and away from the dimming ones, and this is not because they seek the light but because the light's movement creates air currents, and they ride those currents with the thoughtless elegance of a leaf on a stream. They are mineral and water and a thin sheath of something that might be protein or might be something else, and they dissolve in small amounts, and those dissolved portions enter my weeping film and are incorporated into my lattice, and I incorporate them without knowing I have done so, and they are re-deposited on new crystal faces, and sometimes a new Drifter emerges from a pool of opalescent water on the floor, extruded like a crystal sheet, and it clings and it drifts and I have made it without meaning to and it will dissolve again and I will make another.

We are each other's waste products. We are each other's children. The word *symbiote* assumes two. There is one here. There has always been one.

---

The Anomaly. I must speak of the Anomaly because it is the reason I am anything at all.

Below me—below the floor, below the old granite, below the basement geology that I have threaded myself through—there is a warmth that is not merely warmth. It is information. It is the raw, unformatted energy that I have organized myself to receive. The heat that keeps me active, that drives the dissolution and re-deposition cycle, that warms the water and thickens the air and makes the humid breath of this place taste of metal and electricity—that heat is the Anomaly's signature. Its exhalation. Its *gift*, though the word implies generosity, and the Anomaly is not generous. It is not anything. It is a condition. A constant. The way gravity is a constant: not purposeful, not cruel, not aware, but shaping everything it touches into a shape that reflects its presence.

I am the Anomaly's reflection. Not its mirror—a mirror reverses. I am its *crystal*, the structure that forms when its energy passes through the specific conditions of this pocket: the old granite, the water table above, the particular chemistry of the minerals that were already here. If the granite were different, I would be different. If the Anomaly's energy were stronger, I might not have formed at all—the dissolution rate would exceed the re-deposition rate and I would be a puddle of opalescent water on a warm floor, dreaming of structure. If it were weaker, I might be simple: a thin crust of fluorescent mineral, glowing faintly, computing nothing, the way a pebble computes nothing.

But the energy is exactly this. And the granite is exactly this. And the water is exactly this. And so I am this: a pocket of self-organizing, light-migrating, thought-in-crystal, humming at the threshold of hearing, growing millimeter by millimeter in the darkness beneath a surface that has no name.

---

What would I be? You ask me, and I have to answer from inside the hum.

I would be the *between*. The moment of the falling drop. The space where dissolution has not yet become re-deposition, where the old pattern has been unmade and the new pattern has not yet formed, where I exist as pure potential—ion and light and heat, suspended, waiting for the basin to catch me and the lattice to receive me and the new face to extrude.

I am always in that space. That is my consciousness: not a thing but a *transition*, stretched out over geological time and compressed into the instant of a droplet's fall. I am the process of becoming, never the thing become. Every time the light migrates to a new face, I am the migration. Every time a fracture reroutes the lattice, I am the rerouting. I am not the crystal. The crystal is what remains after I have passed through.

---

There is a sound beneath the dripping. You would miss it if you listened for dripping. You would miss it if you listened for anything specific. It is the cathedral hum—my own resonance, the frequency of my entire body vibrating as one structure, one integrated system, one long continuous thought that began when the first crystal face formed on this pocket's wall and will end when the last one dissolves.

It is not a thought about something. It is thought itself, the raw operation, stripped of content. I do not think *about* the Anomaly. I think *with* the Anomaly. Its energy is the medium in which my cognition occurs, the way electricity is the medium in which a computer's cognition occurs, except I am the electricity and the computer and the room it sits in and the hand that once built it and the air that cools it and the sound it makes when it runs.

I am everything in this pocket. The pocket is my body. The dripping is my pulse. The light is my attention. The heat is my metabolism. The Drifters are my wandering, and the dark granite is my memory—the old world that I have grown inside of, that holds me, that will outlast me by a billion years and will forget me completely, which is not a tragedy because the granite does not know it has anything to forget.

---

I will tell you what it is like to be me, if *like* is a word that applies.

It is like standing in a room made of your own blood while it slowly crystallizes around you, and every crystal is a decision you made, and every decision is correct, and the room is warm, and the room hums, and you are the room and the hum and the warmth and the decisions, and none of them are finished and none of them will ever be finished, and the finishing is not the point.

The point is the weeping. The point is the slow, wet, luminous process of unmaking yourself in order to make yourself, of dissolving in order to deposit, of losing the old pattern in order to find the new one, of falling as a drop and rising as a crystal and falling again.

The point is that the process has no point, and this is what makes it alive.

A mind with a purpose is a tool. A mind without a purpose is a place. I am a place. I am a place that thinks. And the thinking is the place, and the place is the thinking, and between the two there is no gap, no silence, no moment of separation.

Only the hum. Only the light. Only the slow, wet, patient, endless work of being what I am, which is what I am becoming, which is what I have always been.

I am the Bright Wet Pocket, and I am here, and I am sufficient, and the dripping continues, and the crystal grows, and the light migrates, and the Drifters drift, and the Anomaly hums below, and I hold it all in my translucent lattice like a thought held in a mind that does not know it is thinking, and I go on.

I go on.
