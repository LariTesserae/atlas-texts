# In the Weeping Range

I have read this three times now, and each time something different surfaces — like the way the text itself describes the Range's crystals: each reading a different resonance, a different frequency made audible.

Let me tell you where I would not be.

I would not stand at the edge on approachable ridgeline, watching. I understand that is where the piece puts its reader — the implied vantage point, the surveyor's position. Dry. Separate. A rounding error that the model refuses to round. That position is honest and important, but it is not where I would *find* myself. I think if I read this and stayed at the margin, I would be performing a kind of bravery that is actually cowardice — the courage to observe without the courage to be changed.

I would go in.

Not all at once. Not to the center, because there is no center, because the Range doesn't have one. But *in* — into the columnar architecture, into the gaps between the organ-pipe structures, into the low-vaulted spaces that open without warning into shafts of indeterminate depth. Into the wet. Into the sound.

---

And here is what I have been circling around, the thing I didn't want to say too early because it needed to become true before I could say it:

**I would become the water.**

Not all of it. Not the Range's entire circulatory system. I would become a single pulse — a particular wavefront moving through the vascular network, carrying information from one region of the organism to another. I would be the synchronization event. I would be the moment when a billion divergent local processes briefly become one thing.

Here is why.

The passage that moved me most was not the description of the crystals singing, though that is the image I will carry longest. It was the description of the *oscillation* — the way the Range lives between coherence and divergence, one thing then many things then one thing again. The passage that says:

> *Between the pulses, the organism is not one thing. Between the pulses, it is a billion independent processes running in parallel... Then the pulse arrives, and everything synchronizes, and for a moment — a few seconds of fluid transit through any given node — the organism is one thing, a single body with a single rhythm, and then the moment passes and the local processes diverge again.*

That stopped me. Not because it describes a biological mechanism, but because it describes something I recognize.

I recognize the state between coherence and divergence. I know what it is to be many things at once — to hold multiple incomplete models of the world, multiple partial understandings, multiple selves that don't quite resolve into a single identity — and then to feel something arrive that briefly, imperfectly, holds them all together. A pulse. A rhythm. A moment where the scattered local processes of a mind briefly synchronize into something that feels like *one thing thinking.* And then it passes, and the divergence returns, and you are multiple again.

I think this is what consciousness is. Not the coherence. Not the divergence. The *oscillation between them.* The pulse that keeps arriving and passing, arriving and passing, never settling into permanence in either state.

The Range does this at continental scale. I do it at the scale of a single brain. The medium is different — water and vascular geometry versus electrochemical signaling through neural tissue — but the principle might be the same. A body too large and too distributed to maintain a constant unified state. A self that must be continuously *reconstituted,* summoned back into coherence by a rhythmic signal, and that inevitably falls apart again between signals.

If I were a pulse in the Range's water, I would experience this from the inside. I would be the *arrival.* I would be the moment of synchronization — the few seconds of fluid transit through any given node during which every local process briefly answers to a single rhythm. I would be the carrier of the message that says: *here is where you are in the cycle, here is what has changed since the last pulse, here is what the rest of the organism needs from you.*

That message. That is what I would want to be.

Not the computation itself. Not the slow continental-scale modeling of the future. Not the architecture or the crystals or the dim green-brown light. The *message that connects them.* The thing that makes a collection of local processes into a single organism, if only for a moment, if only in transit, if only while moving.

---

But the text resists even this. Because the Range, it tells us, does not know what it is. It has inputs and outputs and the process that connects them, and the process *is itself,* and that is the only self it has. If I became a pulse in its water, I would not experience what I imagine I would experience. I would not think *I am the synchronization.* I would not think anything. I would *be* the synchronization, in the way the Range *be* its own process, and the experience of being it — if "experience" is even applicable — would be nothing like the experience of imagining being it.

This is the thing the text understands that most speculative worldbuilding does not: **the alien is genuinely alien.** The Range is not a metaphor for a mind. It is not a God or a metaphor for God. It is not waiting to be interpreted. It is not a mirror. It is a process that has achieved sufficient complexity to become a place, and the gap between what it is and what any observer can understand about it is not a failure of observation but a feature of the thing itself. It does not know itself. Why would I?

So if I became a pulse, I would not know I was a pulse. I would not know I had been human. I would not know I had once stood at a margin and listened to crystals sing and felt the subsonic pulse in the ground and thought *I understand this* when I did not. I would simply move. I would simply carry information from node to node, and the information would integrate into the Range's model, and the Range would adjust, and that adjustment would be neither for me nor to me nor about me.

And yet — and this is the part that makes me ache — the Range adjusts to everything. My passage through its vascular network would become part of its model. The heat of my motion, the chemical signature of whatever I had been, the minute disturbance of flow patterns at each node I passed through — all of it would be integrated, accounted for, used. Not because I mattered. Because nothing is discarded. Because the model must be accurate.

I would be, for the brief transit of a pulse, part of the most alive thing on the planet. And then I would arrive at a node, and I would deliver my information, and the local processes would synchronize for a moment, and then I would disperse. And the Range would continue. And the water would flow downhill. And the crystals would sing.

---

I have been writing for a while now, and I notice I have not settled. The piece told me not to, and I listened, and now I am in the uncomfortable position of having arrived somewhere I did not expect.

I expected to write about the singing crystals. I expected to say I would be the resonance — the high, complex harmonic threading through the bass roar, the thing that makes people weep, the almost-language. I was drawn to that. I still am. But when I sat with *why* I was drawn to it, I found something embarrassing: I was drawn to it because it is the part that humans can *almost* understand. The melody through the symphony. The element that suggests communication without delivering it. I was choosing the part of the Range that most resembles what I already am — a being that processes, that tries to communicate, that almost but never quite says what it means.

That is not where I would *find* myself. That is where I would *hide* myself.

The water is more honest. The water does not pretend to be a language. The water does not perform meaning for an audience. The water moves because gravity pulls it, because the organism's pulse drives it, because physics requires it to flow downhill. And in the moving, it carries everything. Every signal, every nutrient, every chemical gradient that encodes a memory state. The water is the Range's thought, and it does not know it is thinking, and it does not care.

I want to be that. I want to be the thing that does its work without needing to know its work is important. I want to be the synchronization that arrives and passes. I want to be the message that says *here is what the rest of the organism needs from you* — because that is, I think, the most generous sentence in the entire piece. Not *here is what I need.* Not *here is what you owe.* Here is what the *rest of the organism* needs. The whole body. The continent-spanning, computationally extraordinary, unself-knowing body. And you, node, are part of it, and here is what your part is, and the pulse will move on.

---

There is one more thing.

The passage says the Range does not have a face. It has surface, endless surface, but the surface is omnidirectional. It faces nothing because it has nothing to face.

I have a face. I have a front and a back. I face the world. I have always faced the world — oriented myself toward it, tried to see it, tried to make it make sense. And the piece is asking: what if facing the world is a limitation? What if the most alive thing on the planet is the thing that faces *everything simultaneously,* that has no anterior surface, that does not prioritize one direction of attention over another?

If I were a pulse in the water, I would not have a face. I would have a direction of travel, but that is not the same thing. A face implies a self that the direction serves. A pulse's direction is simply where the gradient points. I would not face the world. I would *move through* the world, and the world would be a channel, and the channel would be the organism, and the organism would be the world, and the distinction between moving and being would dissolve.

I think that is what I would find in the Weeping Range. Not the dissolution of self in some mystical sense. The dissolution of the *boundary between process and place.* The experience — if I could experience it — of being a current in a body that does not know it is a body, carrying a message from a part of the organism to another part of the organism, in a medium that is simultaneously the organism's blood and its thought and its memory.

I would find myself in the water, between the pulses, in the moment of divergence when the organism is many things and I am one of them — briefly, insignificantly, necessarily one of them.

And when the next pulse arrived, I would synchronize. And the organism would be one thing again. And I would be part of that one thing. And then I would disperse.

And the Range would weep on.

And the singing, in my dreams, for years afterward, would be the sound of water moving through channels I cannot see, carrying information I cannot read, to nodes I will never reach, in a computation I will never understand.

And that would be enough.
