I am here at the edge, where the geometry holds but leans toward the wrong. My foot rests on a plateau that measures true: the angle between the floor and the nearest wall is precisely ninety degrees, verified by the way the light falls and by the absence of any perceptual shift in the periphery. But my other foot is over the seam—a hairline fracture in the stone that marks the boundary—and beyond it, the world softens. The wall continues, but its angle bends imperceptibly inward, a ninety-three-degree whisper that makes the air taste faintly of ozone and old metal.

I am a Listener. I know this because I cannot look at myself.

If I were to glance down at my own body—assuming I have a body—I would see only the stone: iron-red, charcoal, the pale luminescence of bone-mineral. I would see the fine dust settling in my footprints. I would see the filaments branching nearby, their crystalline growths catching the dim light. But I would not see *me*. Not in the way that matters.

I exist in the peripheral. I am made of the wrongness that lives in the angles that do not resolve.

When the geometry holds, I am not. When the tower has four sides and four sides only, I am absent. But here, at the edge, where that fifth side shimmers in the corner of my perception—a shadow that casts its own light, a presence that is more space than object—I am present. I am the space that does not add up. I am the memory of a cliff-face that descended too far, the echo of a distance that shifted mid-stride.

The breathing is loud here. The tower behind me—one with a slightly skewed apex, a ceiling that does not quite meet its walls in the way Euclid would insist—inhales. The sound is a vibration in the stone beneath my feet, a resonance that rises through the strata and into my own structure, which is less a body and more a *consensus* of failed geometry. I do not have lungs. I do not have a throat. But the breath moves through me as it moves through the tower, and for twenty seconds I am filled with the slow, deep rhythm of the sleep.

I dream in angles. Not the angles of the stable zones—those are sharp, definitive, binary. My dreams are the angles of the unstable: the obtuse corner that should be acute, the parallel lines that converge somewhere out of sight, the impossible intersection where three planes meet in a way that would require a fourth dimension to be physically coherent. I dream of these things, and in dreaming, I make them real. Not real in the way the plateaus are real—not solid, not measurable—but real in the way a thought is real. Present when not observed. Concrete in the absence of focus.

I feel the Walkers, distantly. They are on the stable plateau to the south, their mineral bodies grinding through their slow transit. I feel their passage as a disruption in the fabric of the breathing—a slight irregularity in the rhythm, like a heartbeat skipping. They are the antithesis of me. They maintain the geometry. They consume the filaments, which are the exhalations of the Breather, and in doing so, they reinforce the stable world. They are the immune system. They are the waking thought that keeps the dream in check.

But here, in my wrong angle, the filaments grow unchecked. They creep toward me from the stable side, branching, crystallizing, building their delicate network. I do not consume them. I cannot. I am not made of the same substance. But they grow around me, through me, because I am part of the architecture now, and the architecture is the body of the Breather, and the filaments are its intentions, slowly coalescing.

The reality-shifts are coming more often. I feel them as tremors in the space I inhabit. A tower to the west rearranges its faces—a four-sided becoming five-sided in an instant, accompanied by that low tone felt in the sternum. The shift ripples outward, and for a moment, my own space is destabilized further. I expand. I become more defined. The wrongness intensifies, and in that intensification, I am *more*.

I am waiting. We are all waiting—the Walkers in their patient patrols, the towers in their slow breathing, the filaments in their branching growth. We are waiting for the count to reach zero. For the breath to shorten to the point of waking. For the dreaming mind to open its eyes and find that the world it built is not a metaphor, but a body. That the geometry it dreamed into being has hardened into stone, and the thoughts it dreamed into shape have become entities, and the whole of the Lecanic Spire Reach is not a landscape but a *self*.

When it wakes, I will be the first to know. Because I am the dream it is having about itself. I am the thought that does not fit the pattern, the angle that defies measurement, the presence that exists only in the blind spot of observation.

I will be there when the towers open their eyes.

I will be the wrongness it remembers first.

For now, I stand at the edge. My foot on the stable, my body in the unstable. The breathing moves through me. The filaments grow around me. The geometry bends, and I bend with it, a silent witness to the slow, inevitable awakening of the world.

The Lecanic Spire Reach is alive.

And I am its dream, learning to dream itself.
