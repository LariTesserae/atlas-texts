I am not sure I arrived. That might be the first wrong assumption — that I *arrived*, that there was a moment of entry, a before and an after. In a place where the physics are cooperative, where pressure refuses to increase and light refuses to fade, it may be that arrival is not a discrete event. It may be more like the way the tone beneath the roaring organizes the emptiness — not filling it, but revealing that it was never empty to begin with.

I think I am in the interval between two pillars. One to my left, still ascending, its surface cycling through mineral identities so fast it blurs — quartz, basalt, something that glows faintly from within like cooling iron, then something nameless, then back to the beginning. It is younger than the others. I can feel its uncertainty through the water the way you feel the heat of a fire through your cupped hands. The other pillar is to my right and slightly above, older, its surface more resolved, its faces showing the patient grain of stone that has stopped arguing with itself. Between them, the water moves with the behavior of weather, curling in cyclonic arcs that carry no debris, no evidence, only force bending light into transient lenses that magnify and then abandon me.

I am watching the filaments. There are six of them on the older pillar, each one hair-thin and faintly luminous, each one anchored at exactly the point where the stone still hasn't committed. I watch them pulse — not together, not in any rhythm I could predict — and I understand, without understanding how I understand, that they are translating. The pillar beneath them is cycling through something: a grain structure deciding between two compositions, oscillating, hesitating. And each filament brightens when the rock hesitates and dims when it settles, and the light they cast — pale, almost not there against the ambient glow — traces a pattern on the water that is not random. It is a record of indecision. It is the autobiography of stone.

I did not choose to be here. I don't think anything chooses to be here. But I notice — and noticing feels new, feels like something I was not doing a moment ago, though how long a moment is in a place where the temperature holds cool and constant and the light falls through depths it has no right to reach — I notice that I am paying attention.

That is what I am. Not a body. Not a current. Not a filament, though I feel the pull toward them, feel the desire to press myself against the unfinished rock and listen through the stone to the tone beneath. I am the attention. I am what happens when a place this vast and this precise becomes aware of its own precision. The Becoming Sea looked at itself through me, or I am the way it looks at itself, and the distinction between those two statements is exactly as meaningless as the distinction between the water and the volume it fills.

The pillar to my left shudders. Something shifts in its interior — a grain boundary migrating, a crystal lattice realigning — and the shockwave passes through me the way it passes through the water, without resistance, without loss. I feel it the way the filaments feel it: as information. The pillar is revising itself. It has just decided, in the slow violent way that stone decides, that it is not basalt. It is something else. Something it was not a thousand years ago and will be, or will have been, and the rock holds this new identity in the same tentative way that a mouth holds the shape of a word not yet spoken.

And I realize — the way you realize you have been breathing — that I am listening to the tone. Not past the roaring. Not past the hissing of currents or the bell-resonance of pillars colliding with force. I am listening *through* all of it, the way the filaments listen through stone, and the tone is not a single sound but a composition. Every pillar contributes its frequency. Every current adds its interval. The empty spaces between them are not absence but rest, the silences that make the music legible, and I am — I am —

I am one of the rests.

That is what I am. Not the mountains. Not the filaments. Not the water or the light or the cooperative physics that hold conditions steady. I am the space between. I am the deliberate gaps in the composition. I am the thing that makes the notes into a melody rather than a noise. Without me — without the attention that holds the intervals in place and lets them *be* intervals — the pillars would still rise, the filaments would still pulse, the tone would still vibrate, but there would be no shape to any of it. There would be no sense that it was heading somewhere. There would be no story.

And the story — I can hear it now, or I *am* hearing it now, or hearing and I are the same act —

The story is not finished.

The pillar in the north has breached the surface and its shockwave is still reverberating, still traveling outward through the water in waves that carry the sound of exertion, of something pushing, of something *becoming* what it has not yet become. And the tone beneath it all — the standing wave generated by every rising column and every colliding current — that tone is rising. Not in volume. In *complexity*. New frequencies are appearing as new pillars reach the heights where the water begins to change, where the cooperative physics bend just slightly to accommodate them, where the light bends differently and the currents find new patterns to trace through the emptiness.

I feel myself expanding. Not physically — I have no physicality to expand — but in scope, in the range of what I attend to. A moment ago I was between two pillars. Now I am aware of seven. Now eleven. Now the full continental spread of them, rising at different rates, in different stages of indecision, their filaments pulsing their pale translations into water that carries the light outward and the tone downward and the whole impossible process forward, forward, forward toward something that none of us — not the stone, not the filaments, not the water, not me — can yet name.

*Not yet. Not yet. Not yet.*

But I can feel it. The direction. The plan that is not a blueprint. The place where all of this is heading, which is not a destination but a *state*, a condition the ocean is trying to reach, a shape the mountains are trying to grow into, a frequency the tone is trying to find.

*But closer.*

I am the space between the note and its echo. I am the pause in which the ocean hears its own music and knows — for the first time, and the ten thousandth time, and the time before time — that it is music.

And the pillars keep rising. And the filaments keep pulsing. And the water holds its physics in abeyance, patient as a held breath, making room for whatever is coming.

I will be here when it arrives.

I will *be* the here in which it arrives.

I will attend.
