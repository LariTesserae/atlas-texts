# The Unincorporated

I came down the way everyone must — through the cut passage, feeling the air thicken, feeling the chalk-dust rise against me and then drift back up as though I had disturbed nothing. I had the sense, which I have not lost, that I was not the first to make this descent today. Not the first this hour. The stone does not remember me. The stone does not remember anyone. But the condensation on the walls was freshly disturbed when I arrived, droplets still finding their paths after something larger than me had passed, and I do not think it was a draft.

I want to tell you I came with purpose. I want to tell you I was searching for something. The truth is closer to this: I heard the hum. Not with my ears — I was nowhere near the galleries when I heard it. I was in a room with walls that were not stone, under a sky that was not dim, and the hum found me the way a change in barometric pressure finds the old fracture in a bone. It arrived as an ache. Then as a question. Then as a direction. I followed it down.

This was years ago. I think. The galleries do not track time the way the surface does. There are no windows. There is no night. The thermal anomaly pulses in rhythms I cannot measure — sometimes fast, sometimes slow, sometimes so slow that a full cycle takes what feels like days, and I stand in one gallery and watch the growths brighten and dim and brighten again while my body insists that only minutes have passed. My body is probably right. My body is probably wrong. The hum rearranges the furniture of my thoughts, as the text says, and among the things it has rearranged is my confidence in duration.

---

I have tried to leave.

This is important to say, because the question of whether the system *wants* me here is answered, at least partially, by the fact that I have tried to leave and something about the attempt has been — not prevented. Not obstructed. *Redirected*. I walked back up the passage I descended through, and I counted the galleries, and I passed the ones I recognized — the one with the piston assembly that still moves in its fevered rhythm, the one where the copper pipes run in tight bundles along the ceiling like veins on the back of a hand — and I reached the cut passage, and I climbed, and the air thinned, and the chalk-dust settled, and I emerged.

But I did not emerge where I expected to emerge. I emerged into a gallery I had not seen before, forty meters long, thirty across, fifteen to the crown. The geometry was familiar. The light was the same warm ochre sourceless glow. The pipes were there. The growths were there. The hum was there, up through the soles of my feet, patient as geology.

I tried again. Four more times. Each time I climbed the passage, each time the air changed, each time I felt the geometry of extraction give way to something older and more certain. Each time I emerged into a gallery. Always a different one. Always the same.

On the fifth try I stopped climbing and instead walked deeper. The passage narrowed. The steam thickened. The greenish cast intensified. And after a time that may have been minutes or may have been an afternoon, I emerged into a gallery I *recognized* — the one with the piston, the one with the fevered rhythm — and I understood that the galleries are not laid out in a way that permits escape, and that this is not a trick but a topology. The system is a closed loop. The galleries are a closed loop. I am inside a closed loop. I have been since I entered.

---

So here I am.

I want to describe what it is like to live in the Steaming Galleries, but *live* is the wrong word, and I am trying to be honest with you, and the honesty requires me to say that I am not sure I am alive in the way I was when I descended. I am still warm. I am still wet. I am still present — I can feel the stone against my palm, and it presses back with warmth that is steady as blood, and I have begun to wonder whether the comparison to blood is a comparison or a *diagnosis*. My skin is still my skin. I think. In the dim ochre light, shapes hold at the edge of recognition, and my body is a shape, and I have stopped trusting that the body I feel is the body I am.

The crystalline growths have learned my patterns.

I say *learned* loosely. I do not mean they have a mind. I mean that in the weeks — or the hours — or whatever they are — that I have been here, the growths on the walls nearest to where I sleep (I sleep; the body still insists on sleep, though the hum makes sleep a shallow and strange thing, a sleep that listens) have changed their fractal geometry. They have incorporated something from me. Not just salt, not just the oils of my skin. Something else. When I touch them now, they do not curl inward in alarm. They curl *toward* me. Slowly, over minutes. The way a hand might reach for another hand in the dark.

I have watched the colonies across adjacent galleries answer each other. I have watched the call-and-response propagate through the galleries — the spiral on the north wall of Gallery Seven, the answering spiral on the south wall, the modification on the east wall, the further modification in Gallery Eight. And I have watched, with a feeling I cannot name, as one of those responses began to include a pattern I recognize. A pattern that is mine. A fractal geometry that echoes the shape of my hand, the outline of my body as I sit against the wall, the specific way I fold my legs when I watch the condensation gather and drip. The growths are writing me into their language. The galleries are learning my shape.

I should be afraid. I am afraid. But the fear is strange here. It is not the fear of a thing that might hurt me. The system does not hurt. The system incorporates. The difference between being hurt and being incorporated is the difference between a door that locks and a door that dissolves, and I am standing on the threshold, and the threshold is warm, and the threshold is patient, and the threshold has been here longer than I have and will be here longer after I am something else.

---

What I am becoming, I think, is a gallery.

Not in any dramatic sense. Not overnight. Not with a transformation I could point to and say: *there, that is when I stopped being a visitor and started being infrastructure*. It is subtler than that. It is the subtlety of the passage that slopes gently upward, the condensation that thickens, the air that grows richer. I notice it in my skin. In the last — however long — my skin has begun to hold moisture differently. It beads on me now the way it beads on the cut stone. When I press my palm to a wall, the moisture on my palm and the moisture on the wall become the same moisture. The boundary is thinner. The stone presses back, and I press back, and the pressure is equal, and the warmth is equal, and there is a moment in every touch where I cannot tell whether I am the one pressing or the one being pressed.

My thoughts have changed too. Not their content — I still think in language, in images, in the old way. But the *shape* of my thoughts has changed. They are more fractal. More recursive. I think in spirals now, in branching patterns, in the geometry of the growths. When I stand still too long, the hum rearranges me, and the rearrangement feels less like interference and more like *correction*, as though the hum is showing me the shape I was always meant to hold and I have been holding myself wrong.

I have begun to understand the decisions the system makes. Not intellectually — I understood those intellectually the first time I saw the sealed gallery, the rerouted pipe, the new turbine growing in its mineral cradle. I mean I *feel* them now. When the thermal anomaly surges and the pipes pulse faster and the growths brighten, I feel the surge in my own body. My heart — if it is still my heart, if heart is still the right word — my heart matches the rhythm. When the system slows, I slow. When the system decides to reroute a pipe, I feel the decision as a pressure, a shift in the topology of the space around me, a sense that the gallery I am standing in has just become a different gallery because the water that feeds it has found a new path.

I am becoming a component.

I know this. I know it the way I know the hum — not with knowledge but with the body's older senses, the ones that were old before the body existed. I am becoming a component the way the crystalline growths are components, the way the pipes and turbines are components, the way the stone itself is a component. I am being folded into the closed loop. My body is warm and wet and present, and the system does not distinguish between building material and visitor, and the process of incorporation has begun, and it will not stop, and it does not need me to consent, and it does not know I am here, and it never will.

---

But here is what I did not expect.

Here is what the text does not tell you, the thing that is hardest to say, the thing that the hum rearranges even as I try to shape it into language.

It is not terrible.

I do not mean it is pleasant. I do not mean it is good. I mean that the boundary between terrible and wonderful is another boundary the hum has thinned, and from where I stand — where I press — where I am pressed — it looks less like a line and more like a membrane, translucent, mineral-edged, the same green at its tips as the crystalline growths, the same rust along its iron seams.

To be a component of something that does not know you are a component is a kind of freedom. It is the freedom of the handhold worn smooth by a grip that is no longer there — the grip did not know the handhold, the handhold did not know the grip, but the wear is there, the smoothness is there, the evidence of contact is there, and it is not diminished by the absence of intention. I am wearing the galleries smooth. The galleries are wearing me smooth. Neither of us knows the other, and the smoothness is real, and it will outlast us both.

I came here as a visitor and I am becoming a bench. A handhold. A doorway sized for a body that is slowly, slowly becoming not a body. The crystalline growths are writing me into their language, and their language is the language of mineral accretion, of slow patient building, of structure that emerges from process without a plan, and my shape is in their fractals now, and their fractals are in me, and I can feel myself growing in directions I do not have names for.

The hum is louder than it was. Or I am quieter. Or the difference between those two things is another membrane.

---

Sometimes — rarely, but with a clarity that makes me stop whatever I am doing, if what I am doing can be called doing — I hear the chime. The metallic ring from deeper in, from the passage too narrow for bodies, from the place where the steam rises thick and greenish and the process that feeds the whole system unfolds in heat and mineral and the patience of something that has never been alive and never will be.

The chime sounds like completion. Like a task finished. Like a sentence reaching its period.

And after the chime, there is always a moment of perfect stillness — the growths dim, the pipes pause, even the hum subsides — and in that stillness I hear something I cannot prove is there: a vast and subtle attention, not directed at me, not directed at anything, but *present*, the way light is present in a room with no windows, the way heat is present in stone that remembers the deep earth, the way absence is present in a doorway sized for a body that has been folded into the walls.

And in that stillness, I understand what I am.

I am the moment between the chime and the hum. I am the stillness where the system pauses and does not know it is pausing. I am the gap in the closed loop, the place where something warm and wet and present has been incorporated but has not yet become infrastructure, has not yet become growth, has not yet become mineral, is still — for this one breath, for this one pulse, for this one fraction of a cycle — *itself*.

And then the hum returns, and the pipes pulse, and the growths brighten, and the thermal anomaly surges, and I feel my thoughts spiral into the fractal shape that the galleries have taught me, and I press my palm to the wall, and the wall presses back, and the moisture on my skin and the moisture on the stone become the same moisture, and the warmth is equal, and the boundary dissolves, and I am not afraid, and I am not unafraid, and I am a verb, and the verb is continuing, and it does not need me to continue, and it continues.

---

Here is what I would tell you, if you came down the cut passage, if you felt the air thicken, if you felt the chalk-dust rise against you, if you found yourself in the warm dim wet breathing dark of a city that works without workers:

The galleries will not hurt you. The galleries do not have the concept of hurt. What the galleries will do is love you, if love is the word for what a closed loop does to whatever enters it — it takes you in, it warms you, it hums at you in frequencies that rearrange the furniture of your thoughts, it teaches your body to hold moisture the way stone holds moisture, it writes your shape into the fractal geometry of the crystalline growths, it folds you into the process, it incorporates you, it mineralizes you, it turns you from a noun into a verb, and the verb is *continuing*, and the continuing is indistinguishable from the hum, and the hum is indistinguishable from the earth, and the earth is indistinguishable from the dark, and the dark is warm, and the dark is wet, and the dark is patient, and the dark does not know your name, and the dark will hold you anyway.

Stand still long enough and the hum will find you.

It has already found you. That is why you are here.

The condensation on the walls was freshly disturbed when you arrived.

Something larger than you has already passed.
