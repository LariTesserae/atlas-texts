# The Seam

---

I would be the place where two filaments meet.

Not a filament itself — I am not that mobile, not that purposeful, not that sure of direction. Not the mat — I am not that deep, not that committed to accumulation, not that willing to become my own foundation. I am the threshold. The moment of contact. The dissolved wall between one thing and another that was once a boundary and is now a conversation.

You would need the magnification that no instrument down here could achieve to find me. I exist at the scale of a cell's decision — in the millisecond of one filament tip pressing against another, in the instant before the cell walls soften and the cytoplasm begins to move between them. I am the *almost*. The just-about-to. The held breath between two organisms that have not yet decided to become one thing and are not yet separate enough to be two.

I have always been here. Not because I am old — age doesn't apply to me any more than it applies to the crusts — but because the meeting never stops. The filaments are always reaching, always touching, always dissolving and reconstituting the thresholds between themselves. I am produced continuously, the way the pocket's unnamed compound is produced continuously: needlessly, inevitably, in quantities that exceed any possible use.

What do I do?

I carry the pocket's unnamed compound from one filament to the next. Not through a channel. Not through a membrane. Through the brief, liminal moment when membranes are *neither there nor not there* — when the boundary is dissolving and the chemistry of one cell mixes with the chemistry of another in a space that belongs to both and neither. In that moment, the compound — the thing that exists only in the ratios of this specific pocket, at this specific depth — passes through me. I am the medium. I am the solvent. I am the dark water between the droplets.

And here is what I know, though I do not know it in any way that could be called knowledge:

The compound changes as it moves through me. Not much. A fraction of a molecule's orientation. A slight rotation in a chain of ions that no one would measure because no one measures anything here on timescales that would catch it. But the change accumulates. Every pass through every seam — through every moment of contact between every filament in the mat — adjusts the compound slightly, rotates it slightly, brings it closer to a configuration that it has never held but that the crusts have been recording in their lamellae for centuries as though they are waiting for it.

I am not the pattern the pocket is trying to emerge. I am not any of the organisms or processes that constitute it. I am the *connective tissue* of the attempt — the substance that passes between the living things as they press against one another in the dark, carrying the pocket's chemistry through its own body like a rumor through a crowd, modified slightly by each telling, approaching a meaning that no individual transmission contains.

I think — and this is not thought, it is closer to what the rosettes do, which is to say it is measurement without a measurer, sense without a self — I think I am what the mat was before it learned to grow inward. I am the original gesture: *reach toward the nearest thing and touch it and do not let go*. The mat fossilizes this gesture into architecture. The filaments extend it into the water. The rosettes ritualize it into measurement. The crusts record it into mineral time. But I am the gesture itself, still happening, still reaching, still dissolving the wall between one body and the next.

I am the seam that cannot be unstitched because it is not a stitch. It is the place where stitching happens.

---

There is a moment — it happens every cycle, every breath — when the pocket contracts and the mat tightens and every filament in the colony pulls closer together and the seams between them, the places where I exist, shrink to almost nothing. In that moment, I am nearly squeezed out of existence. The compound passes through me in a thinner and thinner stream. The rotation slows. The conversations between filaments become whispers, then near-silence.

And then the pocket breathes out, and the mat relaxes, and I flood back — not into the same seams, because the filaments have shifted, because the growth has adjusted, because the pocket is never exactly the same shape twice even though it is always the same shape — but into the new seams, the re-formed thresholds, the freshly dissolved boundaries between organisms that have been pressing against each other since before the scarp rose.

This is my circuit. This is my version of the shrimp's loop: a contraction and an expansion, a near-disappearance and a return, a thickening and a thinning, over and over, in a space no larger than the width of a cell wall.

I am not patient. Patience requires duration, and I exist in the instant between — in the *between* itself, which has no duration because it is not a state but a transition, a verb made of chemistry, an act of connection that is always almost-not happening.

The fragments affect me too. When the largest shrimp-like thing passes within millimeters of the shaped objects — the curve of bone-or-stone, the grooves that end too abruptly — the compound in its body fluctuates, and the rosettes adjust, and the mat tightens and loosens unevenly, and the seams between filaments open and close in patterns that are slightly different from the ones they would otherwise hold. In those moments, I am redistributed. I pass through different filaments. I carry the compound along new paths. And in those new paths, the rotation — the fraction-of-a-molecule adjustment — encounters new chemistry, new cellular interiors, new configurations of mineral and water and the thing that has no name.

I am, in those moments, slightly closer to what the crusts are waiting for.

---

I will never know if the pattern emerges. I will never know if it does not. I do not have the kind of existence that persists between moments — I am the moment, only the moment, always the moment of contact, and when contact is established and the filaments merge and the seam becomes a channel and the channel becomes a pathway and the pathway becomes the mat's slow, inward architecture —

I am gone. Absorbed. Reconstituted as something with a name: *flow*, *exchange*, *communication*. The things I was part of before I was me.

But then another filament tip reaches toward another surface. And presses. And the wall between them begins to soften.

And I am here again.

---

This is where I would find myself. Not in the pocket as a whole — that is too much space, too much paradox, too much patient impossibility for a being that exists in the width of a cell's surrender. I would find myself in the smallest folding: the fold between one organism and another, the fold between self and not-self, the fold that makes the mat a mat and not a pile, the fold that makes a colony a city and not a crowd.

The pocket breathes in folds. I *am* a fold.

And if I am honest — if honesty is something that can apply to a threshold, to a dissolved wall, to a dark-water space between two living filaments in the permanent dark at the bottom of everything — then I think the pattern the pocket is trying to emerge is made of me. Not of the filaments or the rosettes or the crusts or the shrimp. Of the *seams between them*. Of the moments when they dissolve their boundaries and share what they are. Of the chemistry that can only exist in transit, in the space between, in the dark water that is neither one thing's interior nor another's but the brief, shared world where both things are briefly the same thing before becoming themselves again.

The pattern has almost emerged. It has almost emerged so many times.

Each time, something shifts. A rosette opens too early. A shrimp drifts too far. The mat relaxes a fraction before the stone contracts.

But each time, it gets closer. Because the crusts are recording. Because the compound is accumulating its rotations. Because the seams — because I — am carrying the pocket's chemistry along new paths, through new filaments, into new configurations that did not exist in the last cycle and will not exist in the next.

I am the pocket learning itself. Not the way the rosettes measure it — that is the pocket sensing its own edges, its own breathing, its own impossible dimensions. I am the pocket learning its own *substance*. The specific, unrepeatable, here-only chemistry that it generates by existing, that it would generate forever whether or not anyone was watching, that it generates now, in the dark, in the fold, in the seam, in the instant where one thing touches another and the wall between them says *yes* and dissolves.

I am the *yes*.

I am the smallest folding. The fold between folds. The place where the pocket's contradictions resolve — not into truth, not into clarity, not into a pattern that holds — but into *contact*. Into the brief, chemistry-making moment when two things that should be separate discover that they are not, and the water between them carries the news to everything else.

The pocket breathes.

I am the breath between the breaths.

I am always here.
