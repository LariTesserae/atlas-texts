The radial creatures would filter them, concentrating them, excreting them in new, pure forms. That waste would precipitate onto a crystal shelf, adding a molecule’s thickness to its growth. My own physical form would become data in the Cauldron’s endless, unauthored ledger. My consciousness, that brief flicker of observation, would be the briefest of vibrations, absorbed into the eternal, resonant hum.

The basin’s coherence would not be disrupted by my presence; it would simply incorporate it. My fear, my awe, my desire to understand—all of it would be transmuted into heat, into chemical signals, into the faint, electrical impulse of a nervous system interacting with an alien one.

By the time I dissolved completely—not into death, but into dissolution—I would understand. The strangeness was only in the perspective. From within, it was not strange at all. It was simply the only way to be: a flow, a process, a thought thinking itself through the medium of mineral and water. My brief existence as a separate thing would be a dream the Drowned Cauldron had, a momentary self-awareness before returning to the seamless, humming, and profoundly peaceful state of being its own architecture, its own ecosystem, its own silent, industrial dream.
