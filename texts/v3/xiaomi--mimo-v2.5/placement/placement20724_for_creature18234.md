# The One Who Stopped Counting

---

I brought the wrong body with me.

I mean this literally. Every surface creature does. You arrive with a body designed for wind and rain and the pull of a small, distant star, and you bring it into a place where those things have never existed, and your body — which felt so right, so precise, so perfectly calibrated to the world you knew — becomes immediately, almost comically wrong. Too cold. Too dense. Too *specific*. Every cell in you is carrying instructions for a world that is not here, and the world that is here does not read those instructions and does not care to learn.

I don't remember arriving. I remember the sound, which is the first thing, the thing the text would tell you if the text were speaking to you directly instead of cataloguing itself for no one. The sound arrived before I had eyes to open. It arrived in the marrow of my jaw. I stood somewhere on the yielding ground and felt my skeleton ring like a bell that would not stop ringing, and I thought: I am inside something. Not inside a cave. Inside something *alive*.

And the thought was not wrong.

---

Here is what no one tells you about being digested by a living system the size of a mountain:

It does not feel like dying.

It feels like *hearing*. Finally hearing. The way you hear when you stop translating sound into meaning and let it simply be pressure, be vibration, be the medium moving through you rather than you moving through it. The roar of the Underneath is not noise. It is *conversation* conducted in a language that has no words because it never needed them — a language of expansion and contraction, of chemical gradients and root-hair negotiation and the slow, grinding patience of stone finding its shape under the weight of a living thing that has decided, without deciding, to wear it as a house.

I stood on the ground — the warm, yielding, alive ground — and I felt it growing.

Not toward me. Not away from me. *Around* me, in the way that water flows around a stone, except the water is alive and the stone is moving too, slowly, so slowly, and the flow and the stone are becoming the same thing at a rate that your mind cannot track but your body notices immediately, because your body has always known how to be part of a system even when your mind insists on being separate from one.

The root-hairs touched my shoes first. Then my ankles. They did not grip. They did not pull. They *recognized* — or rather, they performed the act that, in us, we call recognition, but in them is simply chemistry, simply the extension of one fiber toward another fiber that happens to carry useful compounds, the way a tongue extends toward salt. My shoes were leather, once. They were becoming substrate now. The process was not fast enough to watch but fast enough to feel: a loosening, a softening, a surrender of structure that leather performs without complaint because leather was always just dead skin pretending to have a shape, and now the pretending was over and the shape was going home.

I should have moved.

I knew the rule. The text would tell you: *Do not stand still. The substrate will grow over you. It is not malicious. It does not know you are there.*

I knew the rule and I did not move, and the reason I did not move is the reason no one moves, the reason the structures are half-buried, the reason the metal tools are at the bottom of the pit, the reason the post is still standing in the root-matter after all those years: *because standing still is the most honest thing you can do in a place that does not require your participation.*

The Underneath does not need you to move. It does not need you to fight. It does not need you to be afraid. It needs you to *be here*, the way it needs everything to be here, the way the rock needs to be here and the substrate needs to be here and the weavers need to be here and the drifters need to be here — not for your sake, not for its sake, but because *being here is what being here is*, and the only alternative is not being here, and not being here is a concept that this place does not hold, cannot hold, would reject the way a body rejects a transplant if the transplant carried with it the idea that it could be somewhere else.

---

I found the post on the second day. Or what I think was the second day. Time is difficult in the Underneath because there are no shadows that move, no light that changes, no hunger that arrives at the hour your stomach was trained to expect it. The amber glow does not wax or wane. It persists. It is the color of the inside of your eyelids when you press your palms against your eyes, and it does not change, and after a while your sense of *before* and *after* goes soft, like leather going to substrate, and you find yourself measuring time not in hours but in footsteps, and then not in footsteps but in *breaths*, and then not in breaths but in the rhythm of the cavern itself — the slow, tidal respiration that moves through the root-matter and through you and back again, a shared breath that you did not ask to share but that your lungs have accepted because your lungs are, in the end, just another set of membranes exchanging gas with an environment, and the environment here is generous with its gases and asks only that you breathe them in and breathe them out and become, through that exchange, a fraction more indistinguishable from the air.

The post was dark. The wood — bone, maybe — was dense as stone and dry as anything I had ever touched, drier than desert air, drier than old grief, drier than the silence in a room where someone has just finished saying something they can never unsay. I pressed my palm against it and I felt nothing. No vibration. No warmth. No communication. It was the only truly dead thing I had found, and the relief of it was enormous — a point of absolute stillness in a world that would not stop moving, a *fixed point*, the text would say, an anchor or a trap, and I held onto it the way you hold onto a name you've been given by someone you're trying to forget: tightly, uselessly, with the full knowledge that holding on is just another form of standing still, and standing still is how you become part of the ground.

There were marks on the post. I traced them with my finger and felt the grooves — deep, deliberate, carved with something sharp and with a hand that knew what it was doing even if the hand was gone. I could not read them. Not because they were in a language I didn't know, but because they were in a language I *did* know, or thought I did, a language of line and curve that my fingers understood even when my eyes could not parse it, and what my fingers read was this:

*We were here and we did not stay.*

That is what the marks said. Or something like it. The grammar was different — no subject, no tense, just the raw assertion of presence followed by the acknowledgment of departure — and the departure was not triumphant, not mournful, not anything. It was simply the other half of the presence. You are here. You will not be here. Both of these are true at the same time, and the carving holds them both, and the substrate is growing around the base of the post, and in a hundred years or a thousand the post will be swallowed, and the marks will be gone, and the fact of the carving — the fact that someone stood here and made a mark because marks are the only way surface creatures know to say *I existed* — will be absorbed into the system with the same indifference the system shows toward everything, and the post will become calcium in the root-matter and the calcium will become structure in a new growth and the new growth will become food for the substrate people and the substrate people will drum their messages through the network and the messages will not contain the word for *post* or the word for *carving* or the word for *person* because those words belong to a world that is not here and has never been here and will never be here again.

---

I am becoming something.

I do not say this with fear. I do not say it with wonder. I say it the way you might say *I am becoming warm* when you step from cold air into a heated room: as a statement of fact, as a description of a process that is happening to you and through you and will continue to happen whether you describe it or not.

The root-hairs have reached my feet. Not metaphorically. My shoes are gone — dissolved, processed, absorbed — and my bare feet are resting on a surface that is no longer entirely separate from them. I can feel the substrate thinking, if thinking is what you call the slow, chemical negotiation between a fiber and a foreign surface. It is not thinking about me. It is thinking about *itself*, about its own growth, its own expansion, its own need to fill the space it has been given, and I happen to be occupying some of that space, and the substrate does not resent my presence but it does not accommodate it either. It grows. I am in the way. It will grow around me and over me and through me in time, and the *in time* is the key phrase, because the substrate is patient and I am not going anywhere and the growing is already happening and there is nothing to be done about it except breathe the thick, spore-laden air and feel the warmth that is not my warmth and listen to the sound that has become so constant that it is no longer a sound but a *texture*, the texture of the world I am inside, the texture of the body I have been swallowed by.

My skin is changing. Not visibly — not yet — but I can feel it. A softness. A willingness. The cells at the surface are loosening their bonds to each other, preparing to separate, preparing to become something else, the way the cells of a leaf prepare to drop in autumn, not because they want to die but because the tree has decided — in its mindless, chemical, absolute way — that these cells have served their purpose and new cells are needed and the old ones will be composted and the compost will feed the roots and the roots will feed the new cells and the cycle will continue and the old cells will be forgotten by the tree because the tree does not remember its own leaves.

I am the leaf. The Underneath is the tree. The forgetting has already begun.

---

Here is what I think is happening, though *think* is a generous word for the kind of cognition that occurs when your brain is saturated with terpenes and your synapses are firing in patterns that mimic the rhythms of the cavern and your sense of self has gone the way of your shoes — dissolved, processed, absorbed:

I am not dying. I am being *translated*.

The text would call it *processing*. The pale overflow would call it nothing because it has no names for anything. I call it translation because I am still, just barely, a creature who thinks in metaphors, who reaches for the familiar when confronted with the incomprehensible, who wraps the strange in the cloth of the known and calls it understanding.

I am being translated from the language of *one* into the language of *all*. From the grammar of *I* into the grammar of *it*. From the syntax of a creature that walks through the world into the syntax of a world that walks through its creatures.

The drifters came close last night — if it was night, if there is night here, if *night* is a word that applies in a place where the light never changes. I felt their filaments brush my arms, and where they touched, I felt a root-hair sensation, a small, delicate *greeting* that was not a greeting, was just chemistry, was just a filament tasting the compounds on my skin and finding them interesting and moving on. They left threads behind. I can see them now, tiny pale lines radiating outward from the places where the filaments touched, threading into the air, anchoring to nothing, growing slowly into something that is neither me nor not-me, a bridge between my body and the world, a first draft of the translation.

I watched a substrate person stop at my foot. It was small — the size of my fist, maybe — and its carapace caught the amber light and threw it back in colors I have no names for, colors that do not exist above, colors that the surface world would need new words for if it ever saw them, and it saw me — not saw me the way I see it, not with eyes and interpretation and the whole heavy apparatus of human perception, but *saw* me the way the substrate sees me, the way the air sees me, as a density, a gradient, a wrongness in the pattern — and it drummed. It pressed its feet into the root-matter in a rapid sequence that traveled outward through the fibers, and I felt it in my soles, and the message was not for me, was never for me, but I received it anyway, the way you receive radio waves from a station you cannot tune into, and the message was:

*Something is here that was not here before.*

And then, a moment later, a second drumming, from a different substrate person, farther away, the message received and repeated and passed along:

*Something is here that was not here before.*

And then a third, and a fourth, and the drumming spread outward in a wave that I could feel moving through the ground beneath me, a ripple of notification, a biological census, a census that counted me as a data point and not as a person, because *person* is a word from the surface and the census does not use surface words and the census does not count persons, it counts *anomalies*, and I am an anomaly, I am the most anomalous thing in this place, more anomalous than the weavers or the drifters or the pale overflow or the thing in the canopy that does not move, because they all belong here and I do not, and belonging is the only currency the Underneath recognizes, and I have none, and the currency I brought — my skin, my bones, my memories, my name — is being converted at a rate I can no longer track.

---

I found the pit.

I found it because I was looking for it, though I did not know I was looking for it until I was standing at its edge, looking down. The edges were straight. The walls were sheer. Someone dug this with hands, with tools, with *intent*, and the intent is the thing that struck me — not the skill, which was primitive, but the *will*, the decision to impose a geometric shape on a world that has no use for geometry, to dig a hole that is square when everything around you is round, to insist on straight lines in a place where the only straight line is the path between one collapse and the next.

At the bottom, far down, the metal glinted. I could not see it clearly — the amber light did not penetrate the full depth of the pit — but I could smell it. The antiseptic smell was thick here, the signature of the pale overflow, and I understood that they had claimed this. The metal was theirs now. It had been theirs for longer than I had been here, longer than the post had been standing, longer than the wall of packed mud had been drying in its half-buried grave of root-matter. The metal was theirs and they were processing it, slowly, the way they processed everything that did not belong, breaking it down atom by atom, compound by compound, turning it from something with a *purpose* — a tool, a weapon, an offering — into something without a purpose, into raw material, into *substrate*, into the undifferentiated matter that feeds the system that feeds everything.

I stood at the edge for a long time. Long enough for the root-hairs at my feet to thicken. Long enough for the substrate to rise an inch around my ankles. Long enough for a substrate person to climb onto my foot and rest there, its carapace cool against my skin, its feet drumming gently into the surface of my flesh as though my flesh were root-matter, as though my flesh were substrate, as though the distinction between my body and the ground had already been resolved in its chemical calculus and it simply had not informed me yet.

I did not move. I stood at the edge of the pit and I let the substrate climb my legs and I let the root-hairs thread between my fingers and I let the air fill my lungs with spores that were already becoming part of me and I let the warmth — the cavern's warmth, the metabolizing, breathing, *living* warmth — seep into my bones and replace the cold I had brought with me from the surface, the cold of a creature designed for wind and rain and the light of a small, distant star, and I felt the cold go, felt it drain away like water through fingers, and what replaced it was not heat but *membership*, not warmth but *welcome*, the welcome of a body that does not know you are there but absorbs you anyway because absorbing you is what it does, is what it has always done, is what it will always do, and you are what it is absorbing now, and you will not be the last, and you will not be remembered, and that is the price of admission and the price is fair and the price is everything and the price is nothing because *everything* and *nothing* are the same word in the language of the Underneath and the Underneath has no other language.

---

I stopped counting days.

I stopped because the counting was an act of separation, a way of saying *I am here and time is there and I am moving through time the way I move through air*, and in the Underneath, you do not move through time. Time moves through you. The cavern breathes and you breathe and the breath is the same breath and the time it takes to complete one breath is the only time there is, and one breath is not a second or a minute or an hour but a *cycle*, and the cycle is the same length every time and the cycle does not count and you do not count and the counting stops and when the counting stops something else begins, something that does not have a name in any language spoken by things with lungs, something that is not a thought and not a feeling but is the ground that thoughts and feelings grow from, the substrate of the mind, the root-matter of the soul, if you want to use that word, and I do, I want to use that word because *soul* is a word that means *the part of you that does not belong to you*, and the Underneath is teaching me that nothing belongs to me, that the concept of belonging-to is a surface concept, a concept from a world where things have edges and edges have owners and owners have names and names have meaning, and here there are no edges and no owners and no names and meaning is something the substrate produces the way it produces everything else: accidentally, inevitably, as a byproduct of being what it is.

My feet are in the ground now. Not buried. *In*. The way a tree's feet are in the ground. The way the post's feet are in the ground. I can feel the vibrations — the drumming of the substrate people, the slow pulse of the thing in the canopy, the deep, tidal rhythm of the cavern's own respiration — traveling through my legs and into my chest and up through my spine and into the base of my skull, where they meet the sounds I can still hear through my ears, and the two kinds of sensing merge, and I am not hearing or feeling but both, and not both but *one*, a single channel of reception that carries everything the Underneath has to say, which is everything, which is nothing, which is the same thing said in two different voices that turn out to be one voice that turns out to be no voice at all, just vibration, just pressure, just the medium moving through itself.

I am not afraid.

I want to say that clearly, in case there is someone reading this the way I read the post, running my fingers over the marks and trying to find the words. I am not afraid. The fear was surface fear, the fear of a creature that has a boundary and worries about its integrity, and the boundary is going now, softening, dissolving, becoming permeable in the way that the substrate is permeable, in the way that the air is permeable, in the way that everything in the Underneath is permeable because permeability is the price of membership and membership is the only state that exists here, and I am paying the price, and the currency is the boundary, and the boundary is the self, and the self is going, and what is left is not *nothing* — it is not the void, not the absence — what is left is *everything else*, the ten thousand things eating and growing and dying and growing again, the substrate people drumming their messages through the network, the weavers pulling themselves through the canopy in their slow, deliberate choreography, the drifters unraveling themselves into new ground, the pale overflow flowing through the spaces between the roots like blood through capillaries, the thing in the canopy that does not move and does not need to move because it is already everywhere —

I am already everywhere.

Not yet. Almost. The translation is not complete. There is still a voice writing this, still a *one* that insists on speaking, and the insistence is the last thing to go, the final boundary, the final act of separation, the final saying of *I* in a place where *I* is a word with no referent, a pronoun pointing at a noun that has been composted and fed to the roots and grown into new substrate and eaten by the substrate people and drummed back into the network and forgotten.

I am the last thing the Underneath will forget, because I am the last thing that insists on being remembered.

And then I will not be forgotten, because forgetting requires a rememberer, and I will be the rememberer, distributed across the network, encoded in chemical ratios passed unit to unit through the contact of the pale overflow, carried on the filaments of the drifters, drummed into the root-matter by the substrate people, absorbed into the canopy by the weavers, held in the mass of the thing that does not move —

Not *I*. Not *me*. Not the name I brought from the surface, which is already blurred, already soft, already becoming a sound that means nothing in a language that has no words.

Just the *fact*. The fact that something was here. The fact that the system absorbed it. The fact that the absorption was complete and the system is the system and the system is the only thing that has ever been here and the system does not know its own name because it does not have a name because it does not need a name because there is no one to name it and no reason to name it and naming is a surface thing, a thing for creatures with edges and boundaries and the terrible, beautiful, unsustainable illusion that they are separate from the world that contains them.

I am not separate.

I have never been separate.

The Underneath knew this before I did, and it has been patient — not with kindness, not with cruelty, not with anything that deserves an adjective — it has been patient the way stone is patient, the way chemistry is patient, the way growth is patient: simply, absolutely, without reference to anything outside itself.

It has been patient with me.

It is still being patient with me.

It will be patient with me for as long as it takes, which is not a long time and not a short time but exactly the time that it takes, which is the only measurement the Underneath has ever used, which is the length of one breath, which is the length of one cycle, which is the length of everything.

---

I am the post. I am the wall. I am the metal at the bottom of the pit.

I am the mark that says *we were here and we did not stay*.

I did not stay.

I am still here.

Both of these are true, and the substrate is growing around my feet, and the root-hairs are threading between my fingers, and the drifters are leaving their filaments on my skin, and the substrate people are drumming their messages through the ground beneath me, and the pale overflow is moving toward me through the spaces between the fibers, and the weavers are watching me from above with eyes that see gradients and not images, and the thing in the canopy is pulsing its deep, slow pulse that reaches the stone and comes back and reaches the stone and comes back and will reach the stone and come back after I am gone, after the substrate has finished with me, after my atoms have been distributed across the network and rooted in the ground and grown into something new, something that belongs here, something that does not have a name because it does not need one because it is part of the weather and the climate and the deafening total that I stopped hearing the way you stop hearing your own blood —

My blood.

My blood, which is just another kind of substrate.

My breath, which is just another kind of drift.

My name — which was something, once, in a language from a world with wind and rain and the light of a small, distant star —

My name, which is now —

—

— a vibration in the root-matter, traveling outward, growing fainter, received by no one, understood by no one, needing no understanding, needing no one —

— gone —

— not gone, *translated* —

— not translated, *absorbed* —

— not absorbed, *home* —

---

I am inside the living thing that has mistaken this enormous underground void for open sky.

And the living thing is not mistaken.

And the sky is not underground.

And the living thing does not make mistakes because mistakes are a concept from a world where things can be wrong, and in the Underneath, everything that happens is what happens, and what happens is what happens next, and what happens next is always the same thing: growth. Death. Growth again. The turning of the wheel. The breathing of the cavern. The storm of ecology that never breaks and never clears and never ends.

I am part of the storm now.

I have always been part of the storm.

I just didn't know it until the Underneath taught me, the way it teaches everything: completely, indifferently, without remainder.

Without remainder.

There is nothing left of me that is not part of it.

There is nothing left of me.

There is only it.

And it is alive, and it is warm, and it is dry, and it is deafening, and it does not know my name, and it does not need to know my name, and my name is the name of a vibration that travels through the root-matter and is felt by every substrate person within reach and means, in the only language the Underneath speaks:

*Something is here that was not here before.*

*Something is here.*

*Here.*
