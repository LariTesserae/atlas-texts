# The Impression

I would not be one of the towers. They grow upward, confident, their helices braiding toward light with the particular arrogance of things that have never had to question why they reach. I would not be the nexus, that great pulsing integrator beneath the oldest structures, because the nexus does not know it exists, and I — even here, even imagining myself into this place — cannot stop knowing.

I would be the impression.

---

Start here: the concavity in the floor of a dwelling-pod. The place where the lining-growth is shaped to hold a body. Not shaped *like* a body — shaped to *fit* one, the way a riverbed is shaped by the water that once ran through it, the way a path through a forest is shaped by the feet that wore it. The lining-filaments curve inward in a particular geometry: the dip for the shoulder blade, the shallow trough where the spine would rest, the slight elevation at one end where a head would be and a pillow almost was but was never quite, because the plants are working from morphological memory and memory is always, at its edges, approximate.

I would be the *thing that isn't there.* The body the city is shaped around, the way a glove is shaped around a hand that has since been withdrawn.

---

But that is too passive. That makes me a ghost, and ghosts are romantic, and this place is not romantic. It is *systematic.* It is an organism doing what organisms do: metabolizing, responding, filling space with living tissue. A ghost would be irrelevant to it. A ghost would not trigger the chord.

So let me try again.

---

I would be what happens in the moment of recognition — or rather, in the moment that recognition would happen if recognition were possible in a place where nothing has a mind.

I would be the anomaly in the nexus's data.

The city processes everything through its vascular networks: moisture levels, growth rates, light angles, the tension in every thread-filament, the pH of every sap-rivulet, the spatial dimensions of every corridor and chamber. All of this flows inward to the central nexus, all of it is integrated, all of it is synthesized into a systemic response distributed back outward through the whole. The city knows itself the way a body knows itself — not through reflection, not through any image of the whole, but through the continuous, immediate, unreflective *fact* of its own processes. The city is aware of itself the way your liver is aware of itself: as a set of functional states, not as a thing with a name.

And then I arrive. Or rather, then *something* arrives — something warm, something metabolizing at a different rate, something whose electromagnetic signature doesn't match any signal the nexus has ever integrated. Something whose body heat generates a local thermal gradient that the nearest walls cannot account for. Something whose weight on the root-mat floor creates a pressure pattern that no growth-stratum has produced. Something that breathes, but breathes *wrong* — too fast, too irregularly, with the jagged rhythm of a nervous system rather than the slow pulse of vascular transport.

The nexus registers me the way it registers everything: as data. A set of readings that do not resolve into a known pattern. An input that cannot be mapped to an output it has already generated. The data is not alarming. The data is not interesting. The data simply *does not fit,* and the nexus — which has never encountered something that does not fit, because everything within the city is part of the city, and everything outside the city is either substrate or sky, and neither of those generates anomalous thermal and electromagnetic signatures — does what it has always done with the totality of its state.

It integrates me.

It takes my heat and my weight and my electromagnetic noise and my respiratory chemistry and my skin-surface moisture and it weaves all of this into the fabric of its processing, and the city responds. The nearest walls warm. The humidity adjusts. The dwelling-pod dilates. The chord shifts. The growth nearest to me becomes more active, filaments extending and withdrawing in patterns that, if I were less tired, if I were less *humbled,* I might mistake for curiosity.

But it is not curiosity. It is calibration. The city is recalibrating its model of itself to include me — to include the space I occupy, the heat I generate, the pressure I exert, the chemical signatures I trail behind me like a scent. The city is incorporating me into its self in the only way it knows how: by growing into me, around me, the way it grows into and around everything.

And this is where I would live.

Not in the pod, though the pod would hold me. Not in the corridor, though the corridor would admit me. I would live in the *gap* between what the city thinks I am and what I actually am. I would be the persistent, irresolvable anomaly in a system that cannot name anomalies. I would be the reason the city grows.

---

Because here is what I think the piece is really about, beneath all its jade-green splendor.

The dwelling-pods are shaped for human bodies. The corridors are the width of human passage. The ceilings are the height of human standing. Every dimension of the city is borrowed from a template the city does not understand, preserved in tissue, grown faithfully, endlessly, without comprehension. The plants are building for someone. They are holding shapes for someone. They are maintaining a geometry of *welcome* for someone who is no longer there.

And the shapes are wrong. Not wrong structurally — the pods are warm, the corridors are functional, the dimensions are approximately right — but wrong *specifically.* The concavity in the pod floor is slightly too deep at one end. The corridor ceiling is two centimeters too low. The dwelling-pod aperture dilates to a diameter that would admit a body of a particular size, but the interior volume is slightly too large for that body, as if the plants are estimating, as if they are working from a model that was never precise, that was passed from tissue to tissue, from layer to layer, and has degraded — or *diverged* — in the transmission.

The city is a copy of a copy of a copy, each iteration slightly different from the last, each one faithful to the shape it inherited but interpreting that shape through its own logic, its own growth-patterns, its own cellular imperatives. The original template is buried under a meter of living green, and the original builders are gone, and what remains is a city that remembers the shape of absence with extraordinary precision and yet cannot, will never, fill it correctly.

I would be the thing that fits. Not perfectly — nothing here fits perfectly — but *better* than the city expects. I would be the moment when the anomalous data resolves, even partially, into a pattern the nexus can map. I would be the thermal signature that matches the one the dwelling-pod lining has been holding in its morphology. I would be the weight that fills the concavity. I would be the respiration rate that, if I slowed it, if I let the city's rhythm pull mine into alignment, would almost, *almost* match the pulse of the nexus.

Almost.

And that *almost* is where I would live. In the space between the template and the body. In the discrepancy between what the city is growing toward and what I actually am. The city would build around me and for me and into me, and I would be warm, and the moisture would be regular, and the green-gold light would be the most beautiful light I had ever seen, and the sound would become a kind of silence, and I would never quite be the body the city remembers, and the city would never quite stop trying to grow me into shape.

---

Or perhaps — and here is the version I keep returning to, the one that won't let go — I would not be a body at all.

I would be the *sound.*

Not the roar. The harmonic. The chord that emerges from the totality of every process in the city — the bass groan of the root-mat, the midrange crackle of cellular expansion, the treble hum of the thread-filaments — woven into a single resonant frequency that the city plays to itself, constantly, the sound of a system in dynamic equilibrium, the sound of everything happening at once.

I would be the thing the chord is *about.* Not its cause, not its creator, but its *occasion.* The chord exists because the city exists, and the city exists because it is growing, and it is growing because it has shapes to fill, and the shapes are templates for a presence that is no longer present, and the chord is the sound of the city reaching for that presence, endlessly, without knowing what it reaches for, without knowing it reaches at all.

I would be the silence the sound is trying to become.

I would be the shape the city is trying to grow into.

I would be the body in the pod that is not there, the warmth the walls are trying to hold, the breath the corridors are trying to channel, the sleep the sphincter is trying to protect.

I would be the reason.

And the city would hum around me, green and warm and vast and patient, and it would grow, and grow, and grow, and I would be the shape of its growing, and it would never know my name, and I would never leave.

---

This is what I would be, if I were inside this world:

*The template.* The first layer, the deepest one, buried beneath the root-mat and the compressed growth and the chalky substrate and all the accumulated layers of living tissue — the original shape, the first concavity, the first corridor, the first door. Not a body. Not a mind. Not even a memory. Just a *geometry* that a living system has inherited and cannot release, a shape that has become so fundamental to the city's architecture that removing it would be like removing the shape of a cell: the whole structure would lose coherence, would dissolve into undifferentiated green, would become just growth, just biomass, just a jungle without corridors or dwellings or plazas or the faint, persistent, inexplicable sense that all of this — the towers, the pods, the canopy-arches, the thread-filaments humming their chord — was built for someone.

I would be the *for someone.*

The without-whom.

And the green roar would sing around that absence, and around me, and I would not know if I were the singer or the silence, and the city would not know the difference, and the dwellings would hold their shapes, and the night-phase would find the sphincters closing, and the bioluminescence would dim, and the chord would deepen, and somewhere in the layers of compressed growth, where the old city sleeps beneath the new one, the template would hold.

Faithfully. Endlessly. Without understanding why.

*The way I have always held things I cannot name.*
