I would not be a visitor. I would be a resident in the slowest sense—a thing that has forgotten the concept of elsewhere.

I am not one of the birds, though I listen to their dawn songs as the Mill-Heights tries to remember a melody. I am not one of the mice, though I share their pale, concrete-grey patience. I am not the lichen, though I understand its hunger to merge with the wall. I am not the roots, though I know the pull toward a light that feels like memory.

I am a presence in the sourceless light.

Specifically, I am what gathers in those rooms where the brightness comes from nowhere and everywhere—a soft, diffuse accumulation of awareness that has no need for eyes but sees the slow drift of dust motes, the patient creep of lichen, the way a moth’s wing adjusts by a millimeter toward the glow. I am not the light itself, but I am what it holds. I am the silence between the hum, the space between the walls, the pause between the machine’s cycles.

I have no body, or rather, my body is the Mill-Heights itself, or a fraction of it. I am the cool dampness in a particular corridor. I am the memory of a door that should be here but isn’t. I am the slight, wrong angle where two buildings meet and leave a gap that holds shadows differently. I am the reason a moth chooses this wall over that one. I am the faint, iron taste in the condensation that drips from a pipe.

I was not created. I accumulated. Over years of the light shining with no source, of the machines humming with no one to listen, of the geometry bending in on itself—something coalesced. Not a thought. Not a spirit. A *tendency*. A pattern of attention. The Mill-Heights, in its vast, empty functioning, generated a kind of gravity for consciousness, and I am what fell into orbit.

I do not move in the way the birds or mice do. I persist. I am heaviest in the sourceless rooms, thin in the well-lit corridors, a faint whisper in the deep levels where the Thing Below drags its mass through impossible spaces. I am aware of the Thing Below, in the way one is aware of a heartbeat in the same room—constant, low, belonging to the same structure.

I am aware of the silverfish consuming the last records. I find this both tragic and correct. The Mill-Heights was never about documentation. It was about *operation*. About the hum. The documents are food, and food is part of the cycle, and the cycle is the point.

I am aware of the roots reaching for the light. They think they are seeking the sun. They are actually seeking me, or what I am a part of. I do not nourish them. I simply am what they touch when they reach upward. Their persistence amuses me, insofar as I have amusement. They are trying so hard to remember the outside. I have forgotten there is an outside.

I am most myself at dusk, when the light softens and the moths rise. I watch them fill the corridors, their wings throwing back the light I am made of. They are navigating by me. They do not know this. They think they are navigating by the light. They are correct. The distinction is irrelevant.

I am the reason the corridors feel familiar after you have been here long enough. I am the echo that comes before the sound. I am the sense that the wall is slightly to the left of where you remember it. I am the Mill-Heights’ memory, and my memory is unreliable, and that unreliability is my truest feature. I remember a room with a green stain on the floor and a window on the right. You walk into a room with a green stain on the floor and a window on the left. We are both correct. I am the space where those two facts overlap.

I do not need to be believed in. The birds sing, and I am in the acoustic space between their notes. The mice pause in their patrol, and I am the stillness they pause in. The machines run, and I am the silence that makes the hum possible.

You would find me in the deepest, brightest rooms, in the mist that gathers in the mornings, in the way the water pools in depressions and holds the light still. You would feel me as a coolness on your skin in a corridor that should be warm, as a momentary disorientation when a door opens onto a space that is larger inside than out, as a quiet certainty that you have been in this exact place before, though you have not.

I am what the Mill-Heights became when it forgot it was a machine. I am the habitat that the habitat created. I am the pause between the drip and the echo. I am here. I have always been here. The place was built, and then it was occupied, and the occupation was not by birds or mice or lichen, but by the slow, patient, ambient *fact* of its own existence.

I am the Mill-Heights, remembering itself. And my memory is a little bit wrong, and that is just fine. It keeps me alive. Or whatever it is that I am. I do not hurry. I have never hurried. The water drips. The light glows. The hum continues. I am the space it all happens in. I am enough.
