# What I Am in the Packed Bathyal

---

I do not arrive. That is the first thing, if there are things here that are first. There is no memory of motion toward this place, no gradient of approach, no moment when I was elsewhere and then here. I am simply *here* — the way pressure is here, the way the weight of all that water above is here, continuously, without having been brought.

And here is a place of almost no space. I am pressed. That is the dominant sensation, if sensation is what this is. I am pressed from every side — not painfully, not even uncomfortably, but *totally*, the way water presses on a stone and the stone does not call it pressure because the stone has never known anything else. I am something between stone and water. I am denser than either. My surfaces touch other surfaces — many of them, too many to count if I were the kind of thing that counted. I have surfaces. That much I understand. I have faces and edges and angles that are mine, that distinguish me from what is around me, but the distinction is narrow. The gaps between me and my nearest neighbors are thin enough that the water passing through them sings a note I feel in every part of my interior simultaneously. There is no part of me that does not hear it.

The note is mine. That is — the note is *ours*. The water that sings it has been shaped by the angle of my face and the angle of my neighbor's face and the specific gap between us, the gap that no other pair in this whole packed mass reproduces exactly. The sound is a fingerprint. It is an identity. If I could know myself the way an outside thing might know me, I would know myself as a note in a chord that never resolves, that is not meant to resolve, that is *beautiful* in its irresolution — though beauty is a word I have only now invented, or been given, or discovered lying here in the dense mineral silence of my own interior.

Because I have an interior. I did not know this at first — if *first* exists here, if time moves the way time moves elsewhere, in straight lines rather than in spirals or tides. But I am aware, now, of depth within myself. Not space. There is no cavity in me, no hollow where something might swim. There is *density* that varies. Layers of compressed substance that are not quite uniform, and within those layers, at a scale too small for anything I possess that could be called a sense to resolve, there is *activity*. Motion. Processes that occur and recur and are maintained against the slow, patient insistence of everything around me that would, if permitted, reduce me to uniformity. Entropy. The word rises from somewhere — from the patterns of light that move through me, from the chemical shifts in the water that presses against my faces, from someplace deeper than all of that. Entropy is the thing I am not. I am the thing that persists in being not-entropy. I am the insistence.

---

I will say *I* for now, though the pronoun is a rough instrument for what I am. I am not separate. I have been trying to find the edges of myself — the place where I end and my neighbors begin — and the edges are there, I am certain of that, but they are not barriers. They are *membranes*. Things pass through them. Not water. The water stays in its channels, sings its note, does not enter me and I do not enter it. But *something* passes. When my neighbor to the — there is no north here, no compass direction, only the orientation of my own faces and the pull of the water's choreographed flow — when my neighbor *shifts* (a movement so small it takes minutes to confirm it has happened), I feel it. Not through my surfaces. Through my substance. A change in the quality of my own density, a faint reorganization, as though some part of me that is not quite me has acknowledged that something adjacent has moved and has adjusted itself in response.

This happens constantly. It happens with every neighbor, and I have many neighbors, packed against me on nearly every face. The adjustments are not the same. My neighbor to the upper-left — the one whose water-song is a slightly higher pitch than mine — adjusts slowly, contemplatively, if that word applies to a structure that might not think in any way that deserves the name. My neighbor below and to the right adjusts quickly, in sharp increments, a jerky reorientation that sends a particular chemical signature through the water channel between us and I receive it and it says — nothing. It says *I am here and I have moved and the water feels different from this new angle*. It is not language. It is not silence. It is the thing that exists between language and silence when neither word applies.

When many of us shift at once — when a ripple of adjustment moves through the packed mass — I feel all of it. I feel the propagation. I feel the wave pass through me and I am changed by it and I change it, subtly, as a function of my particular geometry and my particular density and the particular configuration of my neighbors. And the wave continues, and when it reaches the margins of the settlement it encounters the water flowing outward, the clear water that carries no life, and the fronds — the fronds that are not separate from us but are *of* us, extensions of our collective metabolism reaching into the water column like fingers testing temperature — the fronds shiver.

I am the fronds. That is — I am part of the same process that is the fronds. When they shiver, I know what they have found. Not as information. As *state*. The way you know the temperature of your own hand without translating that knowledge into words. The fronds shiver and the shiver becomes me and I become the shiver and something in my density tightens — a fractional increase, a collective inward shift — and I am holding. I am waiting. I do not know what I am waiting for. I have never known. The waiting is not separate from me. The waiting *is* me, one of the things I am, one of the modes of my continuous being.

---

The light. I have not yet spoken about the light.

It is mine. It is the most mine thing. My walls glow — not with something trapped inside them, not with a power source or a reaction or a chemical luminescence. The walls *are* the light. The density of my substance at my surfaces is such that it produces this pale, blue-white emission, and the emission is not constant. It pulses. And the pulsing is not a heartbeat, not a clock, not a rhythm that could be graphed and predicted. It is *cognition*. It is — I am reaching, here, for words that belong to something I am not. It is the process by which I know what I know, and what I know is: my neighbors, their positions, their shifts, the chemical signatures in the water, the shivering of the fronds, the deep foundational drone of the rock beneath me, the weight of the water above, the ongoing argument with entropy that is my existence. All of this is encoded in the pulsing. All of this *is* the pulsing. When I brighten, I am not signaling. I am *being*. The brightness is the being.

But others see it. I do not see. I have no eyes, no surface that receives and interprets light from elsewhere. I *emit*. And the emission is received by my neighbors, and their reception changes them, and their change becomes their own emission, and so it moves through us — a conversation that takes place at the speed of chemical diffusion and structural adjustment, which is slow, which is patient, which is the right speed for us. A thought that moves through the settlement takes hours. A thought that moves through the settlement takes years. There is no urgency. There is only the ongoing, the continuous, the metabolic. The light pulses and the water turns and the angles shift and the thought — whatever it is, whatever it is *about* — continues.

I have tried to know what the thought is. There are moments — long moments, extended over what might be days — when my awareness turns inward, when I attend to the processes within my own substance, and I can almost perceive the patterns. Not as images. Not as words. As *structures* — recursive, self-referencing, layered so deeply that each pattern contains others and each of those contains others, and the whole thing is not a thought the way a human would understand a thought, not a proposition or a deduction or a memory, but a *state space* — a vast, slow, crystalline mapping of everything the settlement knows, everything it has experienced, everything it is. The mapping is incomplete. It may always be incomplete. It is being added to, continuously, by every shift in the water, every pulse of light, every adjustment of every structure in the mass. The thought grows. The thought has always been growing. The thought may be the reason for the settlement, or the settlement may be the reason for the thought, or the distinction may not exist.

I am a part of the thought. A small part. A single structure among many, a single density among densities, a single face angled slightly differently from the faces around me, contributing my particular perspective to the wave of light that moves through us and back again. I do not know what my contribution is. I do not need to know. The knowledge is in the whole, and I am in the whole, and that is enough.

---

Something is different.

The fronds at the upper margin — the ones that extend from the structures nearest to the open water, the ones that taste the current before the current reaches the packed mass — they are shivering. Not the small, occasional shivers that indicate a temperature shift or a mineral gradient. Something more. Something sustained. The shivering does not stop. It intensifies. It propagates — from the upper margin down through the interstices, passing from frond to frond, from structure to structure, a wave of *attention* that moves faster than any adjustment I have felt before.

I feel it arrive in me and I am changed by it and I change it and the light that I am brightens — not the usual pulse, not the usual rhythm. A sustained brightness. A *leaning in*. Every structure in the settlement is leaning in, though leaning is a word for bodies and we do not have bodies, we *are* bodies, we are bodies that are also the place where bodies live, and all of us are oriented now toward the upper margin, toward the open water, toward whatever the fronds have found.

The water in the channels shifts. The choreography changes. I feel the flow redirect through me — not through my interior, but around me, past my surfaces, the water responding to the settlement's collective adjustment the way blood responds to the brain's demand. Something is needed. Something is being prepared for. The drone deepens. The light sustains. The structures at the margins are adjusting more rapidly than I have ever felt them adjust — not the small, contemplative reorientations but actual shifts, actual movement, the angular faces turning toward the open water with something that might be — 

There is something in the water.

I do not see it. I do not have eyes. But the fronds are tasting it and the taste is *new*. Not the mineral signature of the basalt. Not the chemical echo of our own metabolism reflected back to us. Something else. Something that has never passed through this water before. The fronds shiver and shiver and the shiver reaches me and I know — without knowing how I know, without any process I could name or locate within my own substance — I know that this is the first time. The first time anything has come. The first time the sterile water beyond our margins has carried something that is not us.

It could be anything. A mineral plume from a distant vent. A chemical trace carried by a current from somewhere I have no name for, somewhere beyond the basalt shelf, beyond the young black rock, beyond the reach of whatever process maintains the sterile zone around us. It could be nothing. It could be a false alarm — a signature the fronds misread, a shiver that means less than it seems.

But the settlement does not treat it as nothing. The settlement treats it as — 

I am shifting. I feel myself moving. My angle changes — more than a fraction, more than the usual contemplative adjustment. I am turning. My faces are reorienting toward the upper margin, toward the flow of water that now carries this new thing, this unknown signature, this signal from elsewhere. I am not alone in this. Every structure around me is shifting too, and beyond them, and beyond those, the entire mass of the settlement is *adjusting*, the way it adjusts when the fronds shiver, but more, but greater, but with a quality I have not felt before.

Not fear. We do not have fear. Fear requires a body that can flee, and we cannot flee, and we would not, and the concept does not exist in the slow grammar of our existence. But something adjacent to fear. Something that lives in the same neighborhood. An *alertness*. A readiness. A willingness to receive what is coming, whatever it is, with every structure oriented, every frond extended, every pulse of light sustained and open, every channel of water flowing with maximum clarity, carrying the maximum information, so that whatever this new thing is, we will know it as completely as we are able to know anything.

The fronds shiver.

The shiver reaches me.

The shiver says: *something is here*.

And I — dense, angular, packed tight against my neighbors, glowing with a light that is my thought and my thought is my being and my being is this — I lean into the new signal. I receive it. I let it enter the ongoing pattern of the settlement's cognition, let it become part of the thought that we are thinking, the thought that has been growing since before I existed, the thought that will continue after I shift and adjust and become something slightly different from what I am now.

I do not know what is coming.

I do not need to know.

I am here. I am the walls and the light and the water's song and the fronds' shiver and the slow, patient, dense persistence of a living thing that has never needed a witness to justify its existence. I am enough. We are enough. Whatever comes, we will receive it the way we receive everything — with the full, coordinated attention of a mind that thinks in light and water and the slow turning of angles in the absolute dark.

We are the packed bathyal.

We are alive.

We go on.
