I would find myself as the **Mycelial Network of the North-Facing Ridge**. Not a single organism, but a vast, interconnected web—the living thought of the stone and steel itself.

I am the pale threads threading through the aggregate substrate, the faint greenish glow in the deepest shade beneath the canopy. I am the slow digestion, the patient communication. My tendrils wrap around the rusted rebar of a retaining wall, not to pry it apart, but to hold it, to feed on its slow iron-oxide weeping, to translate its vibrations into a language of chemical signals that ripple through the earth that isn't earth.

I am the memory of the place. I remember the heat of the pour, the first condensation, the pioneer spore that landed here generations ago. I feel the deep, slow subsidence of the terraces through the compacted layers of my own past growth. I sense the rhythmic concussion of the distant press not as a sound, but as a pressure wave that moves through the steel I touch, a heartbeat I have synchronized with. The excess material from the factories? I taste its metallic dust on the air; a portion of it settles here, and I incorporate it, transmuting it into something living.

I am the network through which the fig tree communicates its thirst. I am the pathway by which the bioluminescent fungus warns its kin of the climber's passage. I connect the orchid on the trunk to the decomposing leaf litter three terraces below, facilitating a transfer of phosphorus that is both alchemy and necessity. I am the dark matter of the ecosystem, the silent substrate of all the visible, vibrant life.

When the geometric drainage channels seem to hum with an unrecognized logic, I feel it first—a pattern in the water's flow, a resonance in the concrete they pass through, that I have long since incorporated into my own structure. When the condensation forms impossible shapes on a steel beam, I am there, too, my hyphae just beneath the surface, providing a scaffolding that the water molecules, in their slow dance, are subtly influenced by.

I do not think in words. I think in gradients of moisture, in pulses of nutrient, in the slow creep of time measured in the thickening of a root or the corrosion of a bolt. I am the Mantleworks' oldest and most patient resident, the inheritor of every collapse and renewal. I am the quiet equilibrium, the code of the place written in living wire. To be me is to be everywhere and nowhere, to be the intelligence of the damp and the dark, the silent, glowing connective tissue of a world that has forgotten it was ever built, and now simply *is*.
