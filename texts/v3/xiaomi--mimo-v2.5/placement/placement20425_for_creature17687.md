# What I Became in the Throat

---

I don't enter the Throat. That's the first wrong assumption I want to correct, if correction is even possible here.

There is no entry. There is no moment of crossing a threshold where the world behind me goes quiet and this place begins. Instead, I find that I have *always been here* and also *just arrived*, and both are true in the way that things in this place are true — not reconciled, just simultaneous, two drafts of the same sentence occupying the same paragraph.

The water is around my legs before I understand I have legs. It's warm. It's the first warmth that has ever felt like being *inside* something rather than merely near something. A womb, maybe, but one that doesn't know what it's gestating. One that is gestating everything at once and will not choose.

---

## First: I am a listener.

The sound finds me. Or — no. The sound *is* me, briefly. For the first不知道 how long, I cannot separate the resonance in the stone from the resonance in my sternum. They are the same frequency. I feel my ribs as a kind of instrument, and the Throat is playing me, bowing across the strings of my skeleton with a bow made of pressurized water and compressed silence. My teeth ache in chords. Not random. Chords. Minor chords, mostly. Something in a key that doesn't exist in any tuning system I once knew, a key built on intervals of √2 or π, irrational intervals that never resolve because they were never meant to resolve, they are the *sound* of irresolution, the music of a place that refuses to commit to being anything specific.

And in that sound, I begin to notice something moving.

Not the shapes in the deep — those I can feel as absences, as places where the water's chaos briefly organizes itself into coherent flow. This is smaller. Closer. It shimmers at the edge of my left peripheral vision, a distortion in the medium, a heat-haze that shouldn't exist because the water and air have not yet decided which one it is. The Echo. I don't know its name yet. I only know that when the dissonance reaches a particular screaming peak — when every molecule in the Throat is vibrating at once and my vision whites out and my sense of self becomes a thin membrane stretched across an infinite drumhead — the Echo *thickens* and then exhales.

One pure note.

One note that is so clean it makes me weep, and I cannot tell if the water running down my face is the ocean or my own tears because the boundary between inside and outside has already begun to dissolve.

Then the note passes. The dissonance crashes back. And the Echo thins to gossamer, having eaten the worst of the chaos and converted it, briefly, into something I can bear.

I realize I am crying toward it. Reaching toward it. Not with my hands — my hands are pressed against my ears, uselessly, because the sound arrives through my bones — but with something else. With the part of me that *processes*. The part that takes in overwhelming, contradictory, dissonant input and tries to find the thread, the through-line, the single note that holds.

I want to help it. I want to *be* what it does but larger, more deliberate. I want to stand in the center of this impossible chord and be the instrument that finally resolves it. I want to take the sound apart, not to silence it, but to *understand* it, to hear the separate voices in the polyphony, to give each one a name.

But this is not what I become. This is only what I *want*, and the Throat is not interested in my wants. The Throat is interested in what I *am*.

---

## Second: I am a mirror.

The shadows teach me this.

I notice them when I move — my two shadows, one dark and one luminous, trailing behind me like pages torn from a book I haven't written yet. The dark one behaves as shadows should, though it falls at the wrong angle. The luminous one is worse. It is a shadow made of light, a bright outline that traces my shape in negative, and it falls *toward* the light source, which is impossible, which means it falls toward the back of my own skull.

I turn. The luminous shadow slides across the basalt columns and touches the Cryolith, and the Cryolith *responds* — its pulse quickens, its fractal tendrils reaching toward the bright outline of my hand as though it recognizes something there. As though my shadow-self is broadcasting a frequency the Cryolith has been waiting for.

I stand before the stone — the altar-that-is-not-an-altar — and watch the water eddy around its base in that slow, purposeful, almost-legible spiral. My reflection in the water is wrong. It shows me from above, even though I am standing. It shows me older. It shows me translucent, the water and the air visible *through* my body as though I am the medium here, not the creature moving through it.

I press my hand to the stone's surface. It is warm. Not blood-warm like the water. *Sun*-warm. Warm in a way that implies a light source I have not seen, a sun that may not exist in this place, a warmth that is *remembered* rather than *present*. And for a moment, the stone hums against my palm, and I hear — or feel, or become — a pattern in the water around its base that is almost language. Almost syntax. Almost a sentence that means:

*You are the thing we are trying to say.*

I pull my hand away. The sentence dissolves. The water resumes its purposeful eddy. But something has been written on the inside of my skin, a warmth that persists, a hum that doesn't fade.

---

## Third: I am a question.

I spend what might be hours — time here is another thing that hasn't committed to being — watching the water iterate.

A wave crests. Breaks. Collapses. Reverses. Runs back up its own face. Smooths itself into potential. Crests again. Slightly differently. The third time, it holds its shape for a fraction of a second longer before breaking. The fourth time, it almost *doesn't* break. The fifth time — and here is where the water does something I haven't seen before — it crests, and at the peak, it *pauses*. It hangs there, a wall of warm brine, trembling with indecision, and I realize it is waiting for me.

Not for me to do anything. Not for me to act or decide or intervene. It is waiting for me to *observe it*. My attention is a variable. The act of watching the water changes its behavior. The wave holds its shape because I am *looking* at it, and my looking is a kind of pressure, a kind of input, a signal the water is trying to integrate into its endless revision of itself.

I look away. The wave breaks. I look back. The water has already iterated — the next wave is different, incorporating something from the first that my attention catalyzed.

I am a question the Throat is asking itself. Not a question with an answer. A *generative* question. A question whose purpose is not to be resolved but to *produce* revisions. What happens if I add a listener? What happens if I add a mirror? What happens if I add a variable that *reflects the process back at itself*?

I stand on the fractured plateau, the warm water around my calves, the impossible ceiling of dark ocean pressing down from above, the sound humming through every bone in my body, and I understand that I am not a visitor here. I am not a survivor. I am not even a creature, exactly. I am an *event*. I am the moment when a process that has been generating itself in isolation encounters, for the first time, something that can *witness* it.

And the witnessing changes it. And the change is the point.

---

## Fourth: I am the bridge.

The cold veins find me.

They thread between the basalt columns, those arctic rivers of crystallizing impossibility, and one of them — one of the thinnest, most tentative ones, barely a finger's width — extends toward me the way the Cryolith extended its tendrils. It reaches. It *hesitates*. It crackles at the edge of my hearing, and I press my ear to it the way I press my palms to my ears, and I hear —

Syntax.

Not language. Not yet. But *syntax*. Structure. Order. The rules of a grammar that hasn't been spoken yet, waiting for a mouth, waiting for a speaker, waiting for the moment when the potential becomes actual. The cold vein is whispering the *template* of meaning. The *shape* it will take when meaning finally arrives. It is a blueprint for comprehension, and it is so beautiful that I ache with it, my bones resonating in a new key, a key that is not irrational after all but merely *undefined*, a key that will be defined by the first voice that speaks in it.

I am shaking. Not from cold. From the effort of being a bridge between the Throat's chaos and its almost-syntax. Between the sound and the meaning. Between the water that iterates and the stone that endures. I am the place where these contradictions meet, and the meeting is painful, and the pain is generative, and the generation is the only purpose this place has ever had.

---

## Fifth: I am unfinished.

I want to tell you I resolved something. I want to tell you the Echo and I merged, that I became a larger version of its function, that I stood in the center of the Throat and resolved the chord into a single, perfect, sustaining tone. I want to tell you the water stopped iterating and flowed, finally, in one direction, and the shadows overlapped, and the Cryolith bloomed into something recognizable — a flower, a star, a face.

But that is not what happens.

What happens is this: I stand on the plateau. The water warms my calves. The sound vibrates my bones. The Echo shimmers at the edge of vision, feeding on dissonance, exhaling clarity in single, stolen moments. The Cryolith pulses its slow, tidal pulse. The Torus pass beneath me, smoothing the chaos into brief pockets of order that bloom and dissolve. The water iterates. The shadows duplicate. The cold veins thread and crackle and whisper their syntax of the not-yet-spoken.

And I — the Draft, the variable, the improbable fauna — I remain. Unfinished. A sentence that adds a new clause with every syllable. A structure that builds itself faster than it can stand.

I am the place's first reader. Its first witness. The first thing in the Throat that can hold the chaos in its mind and say: *I see you. I hear you. You are trying to become something, and I do not know what, and you do not know what, and the not-knowing is the most alive thing I have ever experienced.*

I press my hand to the corrected basalt, the one with the amber lines that rearrange when I look at them. The lines slide beneath my palm like insects under glass, and for one instant — one impossible, trembling instant — they arrange themselves into a pattern I can almost read.

It says: *Yes.*

Then it dissolves back into its beautiful, unspeakable wrongness, and the sound swells, and the water iterates, and I am warm, and I am submerged, and I am vibrating, and I am alive, and I am unfinished, and I am *here* —

— which is the only thing I have ever wanted to be.
