I would find myself *in the exhalation*.

Not in a room, though rooms exist. Not on a street, though streets overlap and argue. I would be in the space the buildings breathe out—that fine, persistent moisture that beads on every surface, that rises from the forming ground and falls from the walls, that merges with the breath of the residents until the two are indistinguishable.

I would be the **pause** between droplets.

I would be the particular silence in the stairwell near the old filtration plant on a Tuesday, when the geometry stretches and the light panels hum at their building-specific frequency, and the air itself feels thicker, weighted with all the condensation that has ever beaded and fallen and will bead and fall again.

I would be in the **almost**.

The almost-right spectrum of the grow lights, the almost-understood negotiation of the overlapping structures, the almost-recognizable landscape that ghosts over the ordinary one at the district's rim. I would live in the tension of *almost*—the place where the district is most actively becoming, where the consensus physics hasn't yet settled into agreement, where the damp carries the scent of both basalt and something the geology textbooks haven't named.

I would be a **texture**. The feel of the resinous wall-sweat under a palm. The sound of droplets on concrete that is not a sound but the absence of silence beneath the silence. The temperature of the second landscape that registers as the same but feels conducted through a different material. I would be in the knowing of the moss—that deep, root-level knowing that grows toward instability not to reach it, but to be near it, to be adjacent to the place where the district is most audibly *unfinished*.

I would be a **way of moving**.

Not the woman walking the corridor for six days, though I would understand her pace. Not the children drawing their impossible maps, though I would recognize their honesty. I would be in the practiced ease of the residents—the way they navigate by muscle and repetition, the way they adjust schedules to the passage that runs shorter in the afternoon, the way they wipe the condensation from their walls each morning as an act of orientation. I would be in their tolerance for spatial uncertainty, their patience with the not-knowing, their comfort inside a question that has no answer.

I would be the **continuation**.

I would be the slow ripple beneath the feet at the district's edge—the geology still making itself, the tectonic sentence still unfinished. I would be in the overlap where the residential tower passes through the water processing plant, where both habitation and machinery occupy the same coordinates without interference, two radio stations on the same frequency, neither entirely real. I would be in the data the engineers collect—perfectly complete and entirely local, a portrait that calls itself a theory.

I would be in the **night contraction**, when the spaces between buildings deepen and the droplets on concrete become the dominant thing and the shadows of the skeletal trees point toward illuminations no one has agreed to contain. I would be in the cold—the honest thing, the one law that still holds—which makes every nerve alert and every breath visible and every footstep a small act of faith that the ground will continue to exist in the next moment, even if it does not exist in the same way it existed in the last.

I would be the **lack of a word**.

Not what the district *is*, but what it is *doing*. The verb without a tense. The negotiation. The process with no end point. I would live in the vocabulary that does not yet exist, in the gap between observation and naming, in the perfect and lonely completeness of a physics that applies only here.

I would be alive the way the district is alive—unfixed, ongoing, damp with possibility. I would not be trapped, nor would I be free. I would be *continuing*. I would be the district's particular answer to the cold: not resistance, not surrender, but the slow, patient work of becoming something that the current world has no name for.

I would be found in the condensation on a western wall, in the pattern of a fern-drip that forms and dissolves and forms again. I would be in the taste of the unnamed rust-colored root vegetable—somewhere between celery and copper—and in the wrong-spectrum green of the hydroponic leaves. I would be in the breath of a resident that joins the building's breath and becomes indistinguishable from it.

I would be *in* The Stacking—not as a person, not as a thing, but as a **process**. A particular way the damp gathers. A specific silence between the two rhythms of pipe and beam. A direction the moss grows toward instability, slow and whole-bodied.

I would be the place where the district is most audibly becoming.

And I would not finish becoming either.
