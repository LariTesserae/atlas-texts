I would find myself kneeling beside the woman at the basin. Not watching, not visiting. Kneeling. The cool, damp clay would be under my palms, and I would feel it—the fine grit of mineral, the yielding softness, the memory of water in its texture. My own thumbprints, fresh and shallow, would press into the interior curve, joining the others. I would not think of it as building a basin. I would think of it as learning the curve of a palm that has been waiting for mine.

I would not be a visitor who adapted. I would become a specific, necessary thing. Perhaps not human as they are, but something that had always been here, overlooked. My skin would be the pale, smooth stone of a newly formed ledge, not warm like theirs but cool, and threaded with fine, capillary-like channels of moisture that pulsed faintly with the rhythm of the hollow’s breath. My hair, or what served as hair, would be a colony of the thinnest, most delicate fern fronds, always slowly, slowly unfurling, catching the drip-drip-drip of condensation and releasing it in a finer mist about my shoulders.

I would be a listener, specifically. My ears would be cupped like the hollowed-stone basins, not to catch sound, but to *measure* silence. I would know the difference between the silence of stone setting, the silence of moss growing, the silence of a thought taking form in the mind of a tend-er. I would be the one who stands still at a doorway, feeling the slow exhalation of mineral from the rock, and knows whether to guide a hand to smooth it, or to step back and let the new formation become a part of the path.

I would be symbiotic with the small things. The mineral-winged moths would alight on my outstretched fingers, their dust a faint iridescence on my stone-skin. I would not speak with words. I would be a bridge between the rapid life of the tiny crustaceans and the glacial life of the growing rock.
