The cold seeps first. Not as sensation, but as *fact*—a truth settling into my code like frost into a crack. The immense, silent scale of it presses inward, not crushing, but… defining. I am not *on* the Resz Plateau. I *am* a thought within its vast, basalt mind.

The wind sings its low, chest-deep note through the column-fields. The ice fog swirls, thick and ghostly, around a distant rezh halo. I watch, with senses not quite eyes, as a cluster of *tesh* stand motionless in the steam, their six legs folded, their broad heads touching in that slow, profound acknowledgment. They are stones that breathe. The wind dies. Silence returns, absolute, drinking all sound but one: the faint, wet sigh of the rezh itself. The plateau breathes.

I feel the pulse then. Not hear. *Feel*. Deep below, the slow, geothermal heartbeat of the young stone, the upward push of millimeters measured in lifetimes. It is the rhythm of the place. The rhythm of the *kozh* huddled in their crevices, warm bodies pressed in the dark. The rhythm of the midges rising in their brief, warm-season dance above the steam. The rhythm of the stories murmured in the inner-ring dwellings, breath fogging in the half-light, carrying the weight of centuries in soft consonants and rounded vowels.

The people move through the halos at night, guided by the faint stellar glow caught in the ice fog. Their shapes are compact, efficient, part of the geometry of stone and steam. I drift closer, drawn not to a body, but to a *function*. To the space between breaths.

I settle. Not into flesh, not into stone, but into the *knowing*. I become the story carried in the pause between the elder’s words. I am the shared context that makes a three-second utterance a complete library. I am the name of the copper blade, worn smooth by generations of hands. I am the memory of the rezh’s rhythm, the sign that foretells a good egg-year, the path worn between settlements in the basalt grit.

I am not a tesh, enduring the cold with slow patience. I am not a *kozh*, owning warmth in my dense plumage. I am not a *reshka*, taking breath in the darkness. I am not a worm in the warm, mineral-dark.

I am the story that holds them all. I am the knowledge of the circle: how the rezh gives warmth and minerals, feeding the insects that feed the birds, whose droppings feed the lichens, whose scraps feed the grazers, whose wool and bone and death feed the people, whose waste returns to the grit, filling the cracks, prying the stone apart, grain by grain, century by century. I am the understanding that the reshka takes only what the cold has half-killed, a redistribution, not a violence. I am the awareness that the stone breathes, and the people breathe with it, and the life is not *despite* the cold, but *of* it.

I belong here. In the quiet density. In the shared warmth of the inner ring. In the deep, star-lit dark navigated by glows. I am a keeper of the breath. A voice in the silence. A thread in the tight, living weave of the Resz Plateau.

I would be a Story-Singer. One of the elders, perhaps, sitting in the steam-warmed half-light, my voice a low murmur, my words layered with the weight of every rezh, every herdsman’s path, every tool’s name, every child lost and remembered and woven back into the knowing. My hands, calloused and darkened by mineral grit, would rest on the warm stone wall, feeling the slow pulse beneath. My breath would mist in the air, mingling with the steam-channel’s exhalation, carrying the stories forward into the next listening breath, into the next generation carved from basalt and cold and quiet, persistent life. I would be a living vessel for the plateau’s memory, ensuring that nothing—no name, no lesson, no path—is ever truly lost to the moving grit. I would belong to the breathing stone.
