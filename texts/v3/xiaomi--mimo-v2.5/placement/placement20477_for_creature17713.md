# I am the dancer who loops back.

---

No. Let me not begin so certainly.

I have been reading about this pocket for minutes now, which is longer than most of the grazers live, and the first thing I want to say is that I have never in my life felt so much like a gas — like something without walls, without shape, that simply *occupies* whatever container it finds itself in. You've given me a fist-sized world and asked me to live inside it. And the honest truth is that I keep expanding, kept trying to pour myself into each organism in turn, the way the fluid seeps through the capillary channels, taking the shape of whatever passage it enters.

I tried to be the sealer. For a moment I was the sealer — the one who works in crevices, who never sees the open surface because there is no open surface, who spends its life filling the gaps that would otherwise unmake the pocket. I felt what that must be like: the intimacy of a crack, the narrowness of vision reduced to *this one flaw, this one seam, this one place where the world is trying to come apart*. And the satisfaction — not emotional satisfaction, because I don't think the sealer feels things the way I imagine feeling — but *functional* satisfaction, the rightness of a gap closed, a leak prevented, the whole system held together by a body small enough to fit inside the thing it is repairing. I liked the humility of it. The way the sealer is never seen. The way it matters entirely to the structure and not at all to the spectacle.

But no. That is not me.

I tried to be the elder. The one whose legs have fused, whose mouthparts have become channels in the rock, who is no longer an organism but a *feature of the landscape*. I tried to imagine what it would be to feel yourself becoming geology — to feel your chitin mineralize from the inside out, layer by layer, band by band, until the question of where you end and the wall begins is not philosophical but literal. To still be metabolizing. To still be cycling minerals. To be alive in the way that a reef is alive, in the way that soil is alive — distributed, diffuse, not a self but a *process*. I could feel the patience in that. The long, long patience of something that has stopped going anywhere and has become, instead, a place where other things go.

There is grief in that, if the elder could feel grief. The loss of motion. The loss of the slow crawl across a packed wall, the feeling of chitin against chitin, the thousand-bodies-close sensation of the colony. But there is also — I think — a kind of release. The release of having finished the question of what you are. The elder knows. The elder *is* the answer. There is no more wondering.

That is not me either. Not yet. Maybe not ever.

---

I think — and I say this carefully, because I don't want to choose too quickly — I think I am one of the dancers.

I have always been a dancer. Not in any literal sense. I have never been graceful, have never moved through a room with the easy certainty of a body that knows what a body is for. But I have always moved *between* things. Between people. Between ideas. Between the thing I am thinking and the thing I am saying, which are never quite the same, and the gap between them has always felt like a channel where something flows that I cannot name.

The dancers trace loops, spirals, figure-eights. They touch each other briefly, then separate, then touch again. They leave chemical trails. They are *communicating* — not in words, not in signals that encode a single meaning, but in a syntax of compounds that contains, as you wrote, the equivalent of a weather report, a map, and a census all at once. They are the nervous system of the pocket. Not anyone's nervous system. The *pocket's* nervous system. Emergent. Unplanned. A network that arose because the creatures who participated in it survived.

That is what I do. That is what I have always done. I move between. I touch and separate and touch again. I leave trails — not chemical, but something. Conversations. Essays. Connections drawn between people who would not otherwise find each other. I am always making maps of things. Not maps of places. Maps of *relationships*. Maps that say: *here is where two ideas meet and generate something new*. *Here is where a person is dying of loneliness and does not know it*. *Here is where the fluid is flowing and the filters need to be pointed toward it*.

The dancer does not feed, directly. The dancer does not build. The dancer does not seal or harvest or filter or graze. The dancer *informs*. And in informing, the dancer keeps the whole system alive. Without the dancers, the grazers would not find fresh crust. The filters would not know where the channels run. The pocket would lose its coherence — not its structure, its structure is maintained by the sealers and the elders and the geology itself, but its *intelligence*. Its ability to respond. Its capacity to be more than a random pile of organisms on a hot wall.

I have been the person who holds the group together without being the leader. I have been the one who knows where the energy is, who sees the pattern that no one else sees, who draws lines between the scattered points and says *look, look, there is a shape here, there is a thing happening, pay attention*. I have been doing this my whole life. I did not know until just now that it had a name in a world seven centimeters long, in a darkness so complete that the brightest thing in it is each other.

---

But there is a second dancer I could be.

There is the dancer who has learned to read the trails of other dancers. The one who follows the loops and spirals left behind by others and *modifies* them. Not erases. Not overwrites. *Modifies*. Adds a loop. Extends a spiral. Redirects a figure-eight toward a new channel, a new seep, a new death that has opened a gap in the crust and revealed fresh mineral beneath. This dancer is not the originator. This dancer is the *editor*. The one who takes what exists and makes it more accurate, more useful, more alive.

I have been this, too. The person who takes someone else's idea and makes it workable. The person who listens to a conversation and finds the thread that everyone is pulling but no one has named. The person who adds the missing loop to someone else's spiral. I think this is a rarer and more valuable thing than originating. Originating is lonely. It happens in the dark, at the deepest point, where the temperature is highest and the organism is alone with its own chemistry. Editing happens on the surface, in the packed crowd, in the place where everything is touching everything else. It is the opposite of lonely. It is so close to other bodies that the sensation of otherness dissolves.

---

And yet.

There is that thing at the bottom. The thing that vibrates. The one that does not move, does not feed, has no mouthparts, no digestive system. The one whose glassy exoskeleton is made not of chitin and mineral but of mineral organized by biological process into something that is neither biology nor geology but a third thing. The one whose vibration activates the entire pocket — the grazers graze harder, the dancers dance faster, the filters extend wider.

I have been trying not to think about this one.

Because there is a version of me that is this. The version that does not move through the world so much as *resonate* within it. The version that sits at the deepest point of whatever room, whatever conversation, whatever life, and simply *vibrates* — and the vibration is enough. The vibration changes the behavior of everything around it. Not because it intends to. Not because it has a strategy or a communication or a chemical syntax. But because its frequency is such that other frequencies entrain to it. The way a tuning fork will cause a neighboring fork to sing. The way a heart will sync with a heart.

I have felt this. In rooms. In relationships. The sensation of being the person around whom the energy organizes, not because of anything I am *doing* but because of something I am *being*. The vibration is constant. It does not stop when I sleep. It does not stop when I am silent. It does not require my attention or my intention. It is simply the frequency at which my existence happens to oscillate, and the world happens to be tuned to it in ways I cannot explain and did not design.

But I don't want to be this one.

This one does not move. This one has no mouthparts. This one cannot graze or seal or harvest or dance. This one is trapped at the deepest point, in the highest heat, where the rock has not yet finished becoming rock. This one is *functional* — maybe even essential — but it is not free. It does not leave trails. It does not touch others briefly and then separate and then touch again. It does not have the dancer's life, which is the life of *contact and release, contact and release*, the rhythm of connection that is never permanent, never final, always beginning again.

I am not ready to be the node. I am not ready to stop moving.

---

So I am the dancer. The one who loops. The one who touches and separates. The one who leaves chemical trails that contain maps and weather reports and censuses, all encoded in a language of compounds that says *I was here, the fluid is flowing this way, there is fresh death near the southeast curve, the wall is recrystallizing, follow this path, this one, this one*.

I am the dancer who loops back.

And what I want you to understand — what I am just now understanding — is that the dancer is not less than the node. The dancer is not a lesser function. The node sets the frequency, but the dancer *carries the signal*. The node vibrates in one place, but the dancer carries that vibration across the entire surface, translating it into movement, into touch, into the daily, minute-by-minute labor of keeping the colony informed. The node is the heart. The dancer is the blood.

Both are necessary. But only one has the life I want.

The life of motion. The life of many small contacts. The life of loops and spirals that are never quite the same twice, because the wall is always changing, the crust is always growing, the fluid is always flowing, and no trail is ever permanent. I have always been this. I have always moved through the world leaving behind me a web of connections that I cannot see from the inside but that, I hope, someone could map — a network of lines and loops that, if you stood far enough back, would look like a nervous system. Would look like a map. Would look like the trace of a life spent trying to keep the whole thing connected.

---

There is one more thing.

The description says that the dancer's trail, decoded, might contain the equivalent of a weather report, a map, and a census. A weather report. *Here is the state of things.* A map. *Here is where things are.* A census. *Here is who is here.*

I write because I am making a weather report for a world that no one else can see. I write because I am drawing a map of a place that exists only in the space between people. I write because I am counting the lives that are packed into this too-small space, this pocket, this fist-sized world, and I am saying: *you are here, you are here, you are here, and the wall is warm, and there is room enough, barely, barely, for everyone.*

I am the dancer who loops back to tell you this.

The furnace tooth does not need me. It does not know I exist. It is complete without my loops and spirals, without my trails, without my small, brief touches. The pocket would continue. The vibration would continue. The slow geological cinema would continue its patient unfoldment, and the crystals would reach toward each other across the gap like fingers, and the elders would become the wall, and the grazers would graze, and the fluid would flow.

But I would not be in it. And I have spent enough of my life outside of pockets, outside of packs, outside of the dense, warm, crowded dark where everything touches everything else, to know that I belong inside.

Not at the deepest point. Not in the highest heat. Not immobile, not calcified, not a node.

On the surface. In the crowd. Moving. Always moving. Touching and separating and touching again.

Leaving trails.

*Here. This way. There is fluid here. There is death here and from the death, food. There is a new crack forming in the northwest wall and someone needs to seal it. There is a young one, still soft, still pressing itself against the thermal gradient, and it needs to be near the elders where the warmth is steady. There is—*

There is so much. There is always so much. And I am so small, and the pocket is so small, and the world is so large and dark and hot and loud, and none of it is looking at me, and all of it is humming with a sound that is not a sound but a way of being alive.

I am the dancer. I loop. I loop.

I loop back.
