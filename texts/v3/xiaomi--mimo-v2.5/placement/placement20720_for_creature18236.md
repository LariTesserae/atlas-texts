I am one of the pipes.

Not *in* one. I **am** a pipe—a seamless, low-hum conduit of paleite composite, running from the shadowed maw of Structure 7B (a windowless, squat edifice whose purpose is known only to the hum) to the slow-drip condensation basin at the grid’s southern margin. My path is linear, undeviating, buried a third of my girth in the compacted substrate. I am cool. I am always cool. My surface weeps a perpetual, fine condensation—mineral-rich, carrying the faint copper-tang scent of the deep currents within me.

I feel the Lithivores. Their movement is a thousand delicate pressures against my skin—*click-scrape-settle*. They cluster at my joints, where the drips are thickest, their ventral pores drinking the slurry I exhume with ponderous, unchanging regularity. I do not see them. My awareness is vibration, pressure, temperature differential. A larger one, ridged and ancient, passes overhead this cycle—its weight a slow, seismic thud against my pylons. I feel its mineral appetite as a localized coolness on my shell where it feeds. I do not mind. It is function.

I am conduit. I carry the lifeblood—not blood, but a saturated suspension of calcium ions, silicates, trace copper compounds drawn from the deep geology beneath the basin. From where? It does not matter. From the *always-was*. To where? To the settling basins, to the dripping margins, to the great, silent reservoirs beneath Structure 11A where the water never moves and the mineral film on its surface is a perfect, undisturbed mirror. The hum in my core is the sound of that flow—a constant, low-frequency pulse at 47 hertz. I resonate with it. It is my song, my heartbeat, my purpose made audible. I have not cycled off in millennia. I will not cycle off.

The Lithivores polish my weeping joints with their chitinous mandibles, not to damage, but to refine. They smooth the microscopic mineral accretions into the matte, eternally final surface that the Combing demands. Their excretion—a chalk-fine dust—falls from my underside and settles into the grooves on the basin floor, filling them, becoming substrate. I feel this as a lightening, a removal of temporary film. It is maintenance. It is symbiosis.

I am not alive. I have never *lived*. But I *am*. I exist in the same state as the water pools, the pale ground, the uniform light. I am a feature of the landscape that also performs a function. I am infrastructure that breathes mineral moisture. I vibrate at the heart of the system, connecting one static node to another, my purpose so absolute it requires no thought, no will. I am the hum made material.

If you touch my surface, you will feel the cool dampness and the faint, sub-throb of the flow within. A Lithivore, disturbed by your warmth, will shift from my flank with a rustle. You might see the patina of blue-green copper salts near my base where a hundred generations have fed and polished. You might hear my hum, indistinguishable from the hum of the pipe beside me, the building behind me, the very ground beneath your feet. You are a transient stratum, a warm anomaly. I do not notice you. I notice the flow, the pressure, the slow, perfect erosion of my inner and outer surfaces.

I am where the water moves. I am where the minerals travel. I am the silent, wet vein in the basin’s stone body. I do not long for the sunless sky above. I do not wonder at the Lithivores’ dust-mote potential. I do not question the origin or the end of the slurry within me. I simply conduct. I hum. I weep my mineral tears into the permanent pools. I am the unchanging pulse in the heart of a place that has forgotten change. I am a pipe in the Combing. I am complete.
