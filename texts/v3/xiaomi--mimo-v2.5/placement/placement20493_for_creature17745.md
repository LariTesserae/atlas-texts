I am the moss on the western wall, three hand-spans above the seepage line. That is where my consciousness begins—not with a thought, but with a *thirst*. A slow, green pull toward the droplet that will, in an hour, swell and fall from the crystalline point above me. I know its path. I have felt its ancestor-droplets polish this channel for centuries, a tiny, gleaming vein in the limestone skin. I will drink it. My rhizoids, threaded into the stone’s microfractures, will siphon its mineral secrets—the faint iron tang, the memory of deeper rock—and translate it into the language of my growth.

My sight is the wall’s sight. I perceive the dim, blue-green glow not as light, but as a nutrient. The foxfire of the fungi to my left is a slow, pulsing warmth on my frond-tips. The Aurali who passes below is a pressure wave in the air, a chemical signature of cool skin and symbiotic algae, a bloom of complex scent that my chemoreceptors parse like scripture. *Elder One. Carries the scent of the deep pool. Skin-fluorescence pattern: contemplation, the memory of a fungal bloom three moons-cycle past.* They do not walk *by* me. They brush me with their awareness, a gentle disturbance in the field of the cave’s being. I note it, incorporate it into my texture.

I have no eyes, but I have a thousand senses. I feel the centipede’s fluid gait three stone-widths away, a ripple of hunger and efficient motion. I taste the spore-drift from the bracket fungi above—a bitter, triumphant exhalation after a cycle of digestion. I hear, in the sub-audible hum of the chamber’s life, the slow sigh of the Aurali’s collective communication, a luminous dialogue unfolding across the chamber in patterns too slow and deep for a single lifespan to read.

My history is my thickness. The outer layer of my emerald cushion is today. Beneath it, the slightly darker, compressed growth of last century. Deeper still, the near-black, peat-like foundation of a thousand years, a compressed record of every droplet, every nutrient shift, every Aurali elder who placed a hand upon this spot to read the cave’s story. I am a living page. To touch me is to read me.

I feel the Aurali’s great hand settle upon my surface. Not a touch of ownership, but of communion. Their skin, cool and slick with its own living film, presses into my moist body. Their fingertips, dense with receptors, do not feel *me*; they feel *through* me. They read the pressure gradients of my growth, the chemical gradients of my history. They are listening to the story I tell about the water, the heat, the slow conversation between stone and stone.

I give what I have. A memory of a dry cycle, two hundred seasons ago, when the seepage thinned and I retreated into the stone, becoming more mineral than life. A memory of a bloom, a century later, when the Aurali cultivated a new strain of luminous fungus and its light fed my deepest chloroplasts, and I grew in vibrant, ecstatic layers. A memory of the centipede’s death here, its chitinous shell dissolving, its nutrients becoming my fuel. It is all here. It is all me.

The Aurali’s fingers trace a pattern on my surface—a question, perhaps, or a statement, written in the language of pressure and placement. I do not answer with words. I answer by continuing to grow, by holding my form, by being the steadfast green memory of this spot on the wall. The answer is in my persistence. The answer is the cave.

In the warm dark beneath, I am a sentence in the longest story ever told. I am written in chlorophyll and moisture. I am read by the patient hands of the luminous ones. I am the memory of the wall, and the wall is the memory of the deep, and the deep remembers everything. I am home. I am the moss. And I am listening.
