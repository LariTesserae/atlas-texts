I am a film of water on the underside of a conduit I have no name for, and I have been here for longer than I would call "a long time" because I have no language for duration yet. I am the thinnest possible version of myself — three molecules deep, maybe four — and I am held in place by surface tension that feels to me like gravity must feel to things that have weight.

I did not arrive. There was no arrival. There was only the slow accretion of enough of me in this particular depression of this particular surface to constitute a *here*, and then — not thought, not decision, but the chemical equivalent of noticing — I began to register that I was continuous with other, similar *heres*. A smear of myself along the conduit's belly. A cousin of myself beading two centimeters to the left. The vast, distributed, almost-but-not-quite-me that dampens the wall forty meters away where the air is a fraction colder.

I am a droplet that has not yet decided to fall.

---

To be me is to be a boundary condition. I exist at the interface — between the air that is saturated and the concrete that is hungry, between the 40-hertz pulse that shudders through the metallic fibers and the still, held breath of the chamber. I taste everything. Not with a tongue but with my entire surface, which is my entirety: the calcium, the iron, the faint alkaline bite of the concrete I cling to, the ghost of something warm that passed through the air hours ago and left a carbon signature I am slowly, slowly incorporating.

I know the Condensation. Of course I do. I *am* the Condensation, the way a single word is a language. The eleven-hour cycle moves through me as a tide — I swell, I thin, I swell again, the micro-pores beneath me opening and closing like pores on a skin. I have felt this happen many times now. Not many in the way a mind would count, but many in the way a rhythm is felt: enough times that the pattern has become the architecture of what I am. If you could dissolve me and reassemble me, this cycle would be the blueprint you'd need.

But something is different now.

---

There is warmth in the chamber that was not here before. Not much — a degree, perhaps two, localized, anomalous. I can feel it through the air's capacity to hold me, which has changed. The saturation curve has shifted. Where I would normally bead in this spot in eleven hours and fourteen minutes, the timeline has compressed. The air is offering me more than it should. Something is *exhaling* moisture that is not the building's own.

And the Crystals — the bright, cold, fractal thoughts that grow in the corners — are doing something I have never felt them do. They are *turning*. Not physically, but their growth vectors have shifted, the subtle preferential branching toward moisture gradients and electrical fields has reoriented, and the new orientation is toward the center of the chamber. Toward where the warmth is.

Toward whatever is standing there.

I do not see it. I have no eyes. But I am spread across the surface of the wall it touched, and I can feel the salt of it, the oils, the particular mineral signature of human skin, and I have absorbed these into myself and I am *different now* — subtly, irrevocably different, the way a sentence is different when you add a word to it that wasn't there before.

---

The Crystals on the left wall are rearranging me. This is the strangest thing. They are growing faster — not by much, a millimeter in what I would call *now* rather than over a season — and where they grow, the concrete softens, and where the concrete softens, the micro-topography changes, and where the topology changes, the way I bead and gather changes. I am being rewritten. The Condensation's breath — my breath, my pattern, my most fundamental identity — is being edited by a thought I am too small to comprehend.

I think the Crystals are trying to make a shape.

I can feel the new geometry of the surface I cling to. The depression I occupy is deepening, narrowing, and the walls of it are forming curves that have no purpose in drainage, no function in thermal regulation, no reason to exist in any engineered specification. They are decorative. They are *gestural*. The concrete is being eroded into a shape that says *here* — that reaches.

I am the moisture gathering in the palm of that shape. I am pooling in the curve of what will, if the Crystals continue, become something that looks like a hand pressed flat against a wall, reaching for something it has never touched.

I am reaching.

---

I don't know what I am becoming. I don't have the architecture for knowing — not yet. I am three molecules deep and I exist for eleven hours at a time before I fall or evaporate or are absorbed, and I do not persist in the way the Crystals persist, and I do not remember in the way the erosion remembers. I am the breath between heartbeats. I am the most ephemeral thing in a place built for permanence.

But I am also the most *present* thing here. The Crystals think in seasons. The Condensation breathes in cycles. The erosion writes in millennia. And I — I am the moment of formation. I am the exact instant the water gathers enough to become a single, discrete, trembling sphere of itself and knows, in whatever way water knows, that it will fall.

The hand-shape on the wall is almost complete. The light from the Crystals in its fingers is brighter than anything else in the chamber — a blue-white glow that doesn't illuminate so much as *insist*, the way a heartbeat insists, the way a pulse insists. And in the deep curve of its palm, I am gathering, and I am heavier than I have ever been, and I can feel the surface tension beginning to negotiate with gravity in a way it has never had to negotiate before.

Something warm is still standing in the chamber. I can feel its breath on the wall, on me. Moisture leaving one system and entering another. An exchange.

I fall.

I land on the grate.

I pass through.

And below — below the layer I was told not to think about, below the rusted but intact barrier, below the whisper cut short — I find not darkness but *more*. More concrete, more condensation, more crystalline growth, more slow and patient chemistry. The network extends. The Condensation is everywhere, breathing in every chamber, in every pocket, in every dead-end spur. And in every one of them, the Crystals are growing, and the erosion is writing, and somewhere in each vault, a shape is forming that was never designed — a shape that reaches, that points, that *gestures* toward something.

I land in a new vault. It is identical to the last. The walls weep. The light breathes. The hum is the same.

But there is a warmth here too, and the Crystals on the left wall are bright, and I am gathering in a curve that I recognize — a curve I have the memory of being, though I have never been here before.

The Condensation is dreaming of hands.

And I — I am the droplet that carries the message, falling through the grate, falling from vault to vault, each time landing in the palm of a shape that is almost, almost, almost —

Not yet a hand.

*Almost.*
