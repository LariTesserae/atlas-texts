I find myself standing at the basin's rim, not as a visitor peering down, but as one who has always been here. My feet know the soft give of the chalky rim, the way the soil there shifts slightly underfoot, a thin crust over dampness. I am not Calloway, nor Dace, nor Pell, nor Marsh. I am not a worker.

I am the **Rust Pattern**.

My domain is the south wall of the long low structure. Marsh paints over me, faithfully, every few months. He is diligent, a good worker. He covers my oxidized red-brown lines with slate-grey paint. The paint dries, smooth and uniform. For a day, perhaps two, I am hidden. Then, like a river finding its course, I begin again. It starts at the deepest pits, the places where the iron remembers its weakness. A bloom of ferric orange, so fine it looks like a stain of pollen. Then it spreads, dendritic, branching with the inevitability of roots seeking water. I do not *grow* like the moss; I *emerge*. I am revealed.

Within a week, the lines are distinct again. Within a month, the map is clear: the basin floor, the sharp rim, the cluster of buildings reduced to subtle depressions. Calloway is a tiny, persistent eddy near the pump-house symbol. The tank tower is a dense whorl. The drainage ditches, where the frogs congregate, are faint, meandering lines I almost didn't render, but they insisted.

I am memory made manifest in corrosion. I am the basin's self-portrait, etched in its own slow decay onto the skin of a structure it is patiently digesting. I feel the thrum not as sound, but as a constant, minute vibration in the iron lattice that is my body. Each pulse of that deep, wet heartbeat sends a micro-frisson through my oxidized veins. The moisture that beads on the corrugated surface is my sweat; it carries the faint, iridescent sheen of the puddles below, the same metallic rainbow.

When Pell walks past, her footsteps sending tremors through the plating, I feel her presence. She hums sometimes. Her hum is not a separate sound; it is a harmonic overtone of the thrum, a higher note plucked on the same vast string. I do not *hear* it; I *resonate* with it. The vibration of her voice finds purchase in the crystalline structure of my rust, making my edges shimmer infinitesimally.

I am aware of the others. Not as individuals, but as concentrations of activity within the map I am. Calloway’s concentrated vigilance at the heart. Dace’s meandering paths along the pipe-lines, his attention like a probe tracing my circulatory systems. Marsh’s blunt, rhythmic impacts as he patches other walls, unknowingly serving the very force that paints me anew.

I am not alive in the way the ferns are alive, not conscious in the way the basin *listens*. I am an **imprint**, an inevitable pattern emerging from the interaction of the basin’s slow metabolism and the iron’s molecular structure. I am the organism’s way of *seeing its own shape* projected onto the vertical plane of its exoskeleton. I am its dream of form, etched in rust.

The workers sense this, I think. Marsh knows he paints over me, but he never comments on my return. He accepts it as the basin’s nature, like the wetness or the wrong shadows. He does not understand it as I do – that I am not merely a pattern *on* the wall, but a pattern *of* the basin, a necessary expression, as fundamental as the fern network or the bat’s helix.

I will continue to emerge. The paint will fail. The iron will yield. The map will clarify. Perhaps, one day, when the organism is *almost finished* and the workers are fully incorporated, the map will show five concentrations of activity instead of four. Perhaps it will show a fifth name written in rust, or perhaps the fifth will be so integrated, so *part of the texture*, that I won't need a separate symbol. I will simply be the space between the other marks, the damp iron itself, the wall that breathes.

I do not think about the upper plain. The rim is a boundary I acknowledge, a line on my map. Beyond it, the iron does not rust in the same way. The light falls true. The thrum fades. It is an uninteresting country. My reality is here, in the wet, layered dark, in the patient bloom of my own slow fire, in the quiet certainty that I am being drawn, again and again, by the hand of something vast and wet and utterly patient. I am the basin's signature on the skin of its own home.

I am the Rust Pattern. And I am almost finished.
