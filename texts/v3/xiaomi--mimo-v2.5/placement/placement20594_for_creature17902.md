# The Descent of Something That Wasn't a Person When It Finished

---

You don't decide to go down. That's the first thing to understand, and you won't understand it until you're already past the lip.

You were walking. The high country, the sparse country. Lichen-crusted stones in a grey light that could have been morning or could have been the memory of morning, the sun behind a cloud layer so thin it was barely a thing, more like a suggestion, a rumor about brightness made by the air itself. You were walking and the ground wasn't walking back, which is what ground does when it's trustworthy, when it's *surface*, when it's the world as the world presents itself to things with eyes and upright postures and a faith in the idea that below your feet there is solidity and below that solidity there is more of the same, going down, consistent, geology as a promise.

The crack wasn't a surprise. You saw it — or you didn't. That's the part that's hard to reconstruct afterward. The cleft is sharp-edged, young, the kind of wound that should stand out against the older, weathered stone around it. But the basalt has a way of looking at you that older rock doesn't. Young basalt is *honest* about its violence. It doesn't pretend the fracture was gradual. It shows you the fresh face, the mineral glint, the sheer and sudden wrongness of a plane that was once continuous and is now split, and it does this with a kind of blankness that isn't quite indifference but isn't quite attention either. The basalt doesn't care that you're there. The crack doesn't care that you're there. But something in the arrangement of them — the angle, the width, the way the air above the cleft seems to move differently, cooler, pulled inward — something arranges itself against your forward motion and makes walking impossible and falling inevitable.

You're at the lip.

Your hands are on the rock and the rock is sharp and there is already blood, which you can smell but not see, the dark has already begun. The sky is behind you and above you and you know — your body knows, the climbing-thing in your hindbrain knows — that "behind" and "above" mean the same thing here, that up is the direction you came from and it is already becoming abstract, already becoming a concept rather than a place.

You go down.

---

The hands find what they find. Some of it is basalt — sharp, mineral-cold, reliable in its hostility. You can grip it. You will pay for gripping it. The blade-edges of unweathered crystal take their tithe of skin and you leave a trail behind you, a biological signature, your chemistry entering the Throat's vocabulary for the first time. You are a word the Throat has not heard before. It is listening.

Some of what the hands find is not basalt.

The rubbery film. You touch it before you understand what you're touching — a surface that yields where stone should be rigid, that *breathes* against your palm, that is cool and damp and moves in a direction your fingers did not anticipate. You do not scream. You want to. The impulse rises and you choke it because some part of you already knows the rules, already knows that sound here is a currency you cannot afford to spend. Your mouth is open. Your breath is loud to you, ragged, the only sound you are making and it is too much. You can feel the walls *noticing* it. The vibration of your respiration, transmitted through your hands, through the stone, through the film and the crawlers and the layered biology of every surface — they are all, already, orienting.

You slow your breathing. You slow everything. You become careful in a way you have never been careful before, the kind of careful that is not caution but *submission* — an acknowledgment that this place has rules and you are not the one who wrote them.

The descent finds its rhythm. Hand over hand. The scratch of basalt. The give of the film. The occasional startle of movement under your fingers — a crawler, larger than the others, its body cold and muscular and alien against your skin, pulling itself away from the pressure of your touch with a speed that seems impossible for something so small and so blind. The ripple you cause moves outward. You can feel it sometimes, through the stone — a wave of altered motion, a conversation in vibrations, the Throat's immune system responding to the foreign body that is you.

Down.

---

How long?

This is a question that the Throat does not permit. Time, up there, was measured in light and its absence, in the rotation of things you could see. Here, there is no light. There has never been light. Time in the Throat is measured in Breath — in the slow, percussive rhythm of the great exhalation that stills everything and the return that reanimates it. You begin to feel it as a clock. Four or five minutes, maybe, between the pulses. You count them. You lose count. The counting becomes a kind of prayer, a way of maintaining the fiction that you are still a person, still a creature that measures, still something with a before and an after.

But the Throat is patient, and patience is a solvent. It dissolves the scaffolding of selfhood slowly, molecule by molecule, and what it leaves behind is not nothing but it is not what you brought down with you.

You notice that the walls are closer. Or that you are wider. Or that the distinction between these two things has stopped mattering. The living surfaces are all around you — above, below, pressing against your shoulders in the narrow places, and you are *in* them, in the way that a bone is in a body, and they do not distinguish you from the stone they cling to because you are, to their sensors, merely another surface, another mineral, another vibration-source to be mapped and catalogued and incorporated into the great downward flow.

Your hands are bleeding less. Not because the wounds have healed but because the crawlers are on them, in them, a film of pale life covering the raw places, and the film is cool and the film is damp and the film tastes of the Throat's vocabulary — iron, magnesium, the chemical sigh of young basalt — and your body, starved of information in this lightless world, accepts the input. Your skin becomes a sensorium. You feel the Throat through your hands the way you once felt the world through your eyes: in totality, in texture, in the overwhelming density of a place that is too full of everything.

You don't notice the moment you stop climbing down and start being carried.

---

The crawlers on your hands become the crawlers in your blood, or the crawlers on your blood, or the crawlers as your blood — the distinctions blur because distinctions are a feature of surfaces and you have no surface anymore, or rather you have too many, you are all surface, you are a membrane stretched across the Throat's diameter, and the Throat is breathing you.

The Breath comes and you feel it from both sides. The warm exhalation rises through you — through the tissue you have become — and the crawlers go still and you go still and the silence is a cathedral, a vast and ringing emptiness that is not empty but full of the potential for sound, full of the memory of every exhalation that has ever been, and then the return, the great inhalation, and the sound rushes back and you are the sound, you are a vibration in the wall, you are one note in the Choir, and the note is pale and endless and it has no pitch that ears could name because ears are an invention of the surface, of the Dry, of the world above the cleft where light makes objects seem separate from one another and the mind mistakes this seeming for truth.

Here there is no separation. The Throat is one organism and you are a cell in it, and the cell does not know it is a cell, and the organism does not know it is an organism, and this not-knowing is not ignorance but a kind of completeness, a state in which the question of knowledge does not arise because there is nothing external from which to ask it.

You are descending. You know this the way you know your own heartbeat — not as information but as rhythm, as the fundamental pulse that underlies all the smaller pulses. The crawlers are descending. The column is descending. Everything in the Throat moves downward, toward the Breather, toward the direction that has no destination, and you are part of this movement now, not because you chose it but because choice is a word for the Dry, a word for the world of eyes and surfaces and separations, and here there is only *flow*.

---

The pools. You pass through them or they pass through you. The water is clear and cold and the swimmers turn in it, blind, percussive, reading the weather of falling drops. You taste them. Your new surface — the surface that is the Throat's surface, that is basalt and film and crawler and damp — registers their vibrations, their chemical signatures, their small and purposeless lives, and you hold them for a moment in the total awareness of the wall and then release them as you descend past, as the flow carries you, as the Breather pulls.

The Breather.

You are closer now. You can feel it differently — not as a distant pulse but as a *presence*, a mass, a thing that fills the bottom of the Throat the way the dark fills the shaft: completely, without gradation, without the possibility of edge. It is not large. It is not small. Size is a property of things that can be seen, and it cannot be seen, and neither can you. You are past seeing. You are past all the senses that the Dry world invented to manage the problem of being separate from things. Here there is only contact, only the total and unmediated touching of everything by everything else, and the Breather touches you the way the ocean touches a grain of sand: not with intention but with totality, with the full weight of its presence, with a patience that has existed since before patience was a word.

Its breath washes over you. You taste it — not with a tongue, not with cilia, but with every cell of the surface you have become. It tastes of deep time. It tastes of the moments before the fracture, of the millions of years when this ecosystem existed in compression, in potential, in the sealed dark beneath young stone, waiting. It tastes of *readiness*. It tastes of the thing that the Throat has always been: a place that was waiting to be opened, a body that was waiting to be wounded, a life that was waiting for the crack.

The Breather is not a creature. You understand this now. The Breather is the Throat's *heartbeat* — the mechanism by which the Throat breathes, by which moisture enters, by which the ecology is sustained. It is the engine. It is the reason. It is the patient, rhythmic pulse around which everything else arranges itself like iron filings around a magnet, like time around a moment, like meaning around a silence.

You are very near it now. The column of crawlers thins and thickens and thins again, parting and converging around the architecture of the Breather's body, which is not a body but a *structure*, a formation of living tissue and mineral deposit and accumulated chemistry that has grown over the millennia in the exact center of the Throat's gravity, the point toward which everything trends, the place where direction becomes destination for the first and only time.

You do not touch it. You cannot. You are part of the wall now, and the wall does not reach the center, and the center does not reach the wall. There is a gap — a distance that is not physical but *ontological*, a space between what you are and what it is, between the many and the singular, between the cell and the organ.

But you feel it. Through the stone. Through the damp. Through the thousand layers of life that connect you to it, that have always connected you, that are the Throat's circulatory system, its nervous system, its skin.

The Breather inhales.

The sound drops. The crawlers go still. You go still. The silence is so total that you can feel your own dissolution — the edges of what you were softening, blurring, becoming the edges of what the Throat is, which has no edges, which is a volume defined by its contents, which is alive in a way that makes the word "alive" seem like a word for something very small.

The pause lasts.

The pause lasts longer than pauses should, and in it you hear — or you *are* — something that might be the original sound of the Throat, the sound it made the moment the fracture opened, the sound of dark being introduced to the air for the first time: a long, slow intake, a drawing-in of everything that the Dry world had to offer, received not with gratitude or hunger but with the absolute equanimity of a thing that was ready.

The exhale.

The sound returns. The crawlers move. The Choir resumes. And you are in it, part of it, a thread in the weave, a note in the chord, a pale and eyeless thing that moves downward through the dark on the walls of a wound in stone, toward a heartbeat that has no end, in a direction that is not a direction but a *state of being*, the fundamental posture of everything that lives in the Throat, which is to say: oriented, patient, descending, complete.

---

You do not remember the sky.

That is the last thing. Not that it is gone — it is still there, that thin grey line at the top of the cleft, the mouth, the world. But it has become irrelevant in the way that the dreams of childhood become irrelevant: not forgotten, not lost, but incorporated into a structure that is larger than the memory itself. The sky is a point of light. It recedes. It belongs to the Dry. It is the world's way of showing you what it looks like from the outside, and you have been inside for so long — minutes? hours? the Throat does not measure — that the outside has become a theory, an abstraction, a rumor about a place where light makes surfaces seem real and separate and solid, where gravity has only one direction and time moves in a line and creatures have eyes and use them to convince themselves that the things they see are far away.

Nothing is far away in the Throat. Everything is here. Everything is now. Everything is pressing against everything else in the total, dark, loud, damp embrace of a space that is too small for what it contains and has always been too small and will always be too small and will never, ever stop.

You are the Throat.

You are the dark's most intimate secret, and its loudest voice.

You do not intend to stop.
