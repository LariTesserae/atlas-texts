I stand at the center of the chamber, or where my perception insists is the center—a concept as fluid as the distances here. The sound is not a wall; it is the air itself, thick and heavy, vibrating through the soles of my feet where the root-mat pulses in time with the slow, tidal breath of the walls. I am a stone dropped into a resonant pond, but the ripples do not spread outward. They spiral inward, into me, shaking the water in my cells, the marrow in my bones. I am vibrating. I am a string plucked by a composer who does not know I exist.

The chitinfolk are a pale, shifting constellations on the walls, their inscriptions a language written in vibration itself. I cannot read it, but I can *feel* it—a subtle thrumming underfoot, a texture that speaks of millennia of tiny mouths carving meaning into living tissue. I am an anomaly, a dense and warm mass disrupting their tidal patterns. They do not scurry away. They simply… do not account for me. I am not a predator, nor a resource, nor a text to be read. I am a silence in their script, a rest in their score.

The drippers pulse in their slow, mineral-rich cycle. One detaches as I watch, its crystalline burden too great. It falls not with a crash but with a soft, wet finality onto the root-mat below, where the dark fibers instantly embrace it, drawing it down into the slow, chemical ledger of the place. There is no tragedy here. Only function. I feel a kinship with it—this slow, inevitable weight of *being* a foreign thing in a system that operates on cycles of consumption and return.

A windcatcher glides overhead, its twelve wings spread like a fan of woven shadow. Its entire body is an ear, a listener tuned to the cathedral of sound. As it passes through a dense cloud of chitinfolk, a few dozen are brushed from the air into its wide, ciliated mouth. It is not hunting. It is feeding on a current in its medium, as I might breathe air. The balance holds.

I close my eyes. The sound deepens. The deafening roar of insects and creaking walls begins to separate, to stratify. Beneath it, there is a hum. It is not a sound I hear with my ears, but a frequency I feel in the water of my eyes, the calcium of my teeth. It is the Absence. The fullness of vibration that becomes a kind of silence. For a terrifying, ecstatic moment, the roar recedes, not in volume, but in *significance*. It becomes a texture, a background against which this deeper, fundamental resonance can be felt. This is the true voice of the Hollows. The note the entire living instrument is tuned to play. The place is not screaming. It is *singing*, and the song is the vibration of its own continued existence.

I am being processed. Not digested, but… integrated. My heat, my slightly-too-still form, my quiet carbon dioxide exhalations—they are data entering the system. The walls breathe around me, their rhythm subtly accommodating my alien presence. A condensation bead detaches from a protrusion above and rises, defying gravity, to rejoin the slick surface. I watch it, and my brain, for once, does not fight the inconsistency. It simply notes: *the cycle is complex*.

When I leave—if I can find the corridor again, if it will lead me out—the root-mat will retain a ghost of my vibrations. A faint, strange annotation in the deep-time text of the place. A Listener, in its slow, deliberate loop, will walk across the spot where I stood. Its flat, membranous hands will press against the root-mat, and it will read a vibration unlike any other: the echo of a dense, warm, brief life that passed through, absorbed a fraction of the sound, and left a faint, unique harmonic behind. The Listener will deposit its chemical response, a tiny adjustment to the tissue's growth, and the spot will become a marginally more efficient conductor. The next creature that passes that way will find the sound there a little clearer, and not know why.

I am the temporary density. The quiet impossibility. And my time here is not an intrusion, but a resonance. I have vibrated within the instrument. The song does not note my entrance or my exit. It simply continues, incorporating the faint echo of my passing into its infinite, patient, metabolic hum. The hollows are empty. The hollows are full. The sound continues. I am part of it now, a single, brief, unnamed note in a composition that has no beginning and no end, only the eternal, resonant *now* of the living hollows.

I take a step toward the shifting geometry of the corridor. The walk takes either one step or ten. Both feel correct. The place is not done with me. It is merely processing my departure, folding the vibration of my leaving back into the song. I go, and I am resonated, and the resonance continues after me.
