## Multiverse Travel Preparation Protocol: Grindstone Plateau

**Core Hazard Profile:**
*   **Atmospheric:** Constant near-saturated humidity (100% relative), persistent corrosive mist (pH unknown, rich in unknown mineral/organic compounds), unpredictable turbulent winds, ozone/copper scent. Temperature: Consistently cold, penetrating "back-of-the-skull" chill.
*   **Geological/Environmental:** Instability of substrate (living/dead composite organism), "Breakdown Zones" where local physics coherence fails, unpredictable topography (eroded basins, knuckles), standing mineral pools (unknown reactivity/toxicity), persistent low light.
*   **Biological:** Ubiquitous, potentially aggressive/defensive fauna (large arthropod analogs, amphibious worm-thing predators), unknown allergen/pathogen profile, potential for parasitic attachment or ingestion via contaminated water/air.
*   **Perceptual/Safety:** Disorientation due to featureless light, warped distances, time perception distortion. Constant loud, disorienting noise (industrial roar, cracking ice, wind). Risk of accidental entry into Breakdown Zones.

**Mandatory Preparation:**

1.  **Suit & Sealing:**
    *   **Base Layer:** Moisture-wicking, thermal synthetic (merino wool blend preferred). *No cotton.*
    *   **Mid Layer:** Insulating fleece or synthetic down, focused on core warmth.
    *   **Outer Shell:** **Critical.** Heavy-duty, fully seam-sealed, waterproof/breathable hard shell (e.g., Gore-Tex Pro, eVent). Must withstand constant abrasion and moisture. Integrated storm hood with stiffened brim and face gasket.
    *   **Gloves:** Layered system: Liner gloves (dexterity), waterproof insulated gauntlet gloves (main protection). All seams sealed.
    *   **Footwear:** High-cut, waterproof, aggressive-lugged boots with integrated gaiters or dedicated gaiters. Prioritize ankle support on uneven terrain. Expect constant dampness inside despite best efforts.
    *   **Sealing:** All suit openings (jacket cuff, collar, hood, boot tops) must have secondary seals (e.g., neoprene cuffs, tape). Tape critical seams pre-trip. Expect constant condensation *inside* suit; moisture management layers are vital.

2.  **Breathing & Sensory Protection:**
    *   **Respirator:** **Non-negotiable.** Full-face respirator with combination cartridges: P100 particulate filter (for mist droplets, spores, organic particulates) + Multi-Gas/Vapor cartridge (for ozone, copper, unknown organics). Ensure a tight seal. Carry spare cartridges (minimum 2). Consider supplemental oxygen if respiratory exertion is planned.
    *   **Eye Protection:** Integral goggle system within the respirator helmet/facepiece. Anti-fog coating is essential. Spare lens inserts.
    *   **Hearing Protection:** Custom-molded or high-NRR foam earplugs (worn *under* headset). Essential for long-term exposure to constant 80+ dB roar. Over-ear electronic headset/comms doubles as protection and communication.
    *   **Skin Protection:** **All skin must be covered.** No exposed areas. High-neck base layers, gaiters, sealed gloves, full-face respirator. Consider chemical-resistant gloves if handling substrate/pools is necessary (avoid if possible).

3.  **Navigation & Awareness:**
    *   **Primary:** Hardened, waterproof GPS with pre-loaded topography *and* potential Breakdown Zone mapping (if available - expect low data reliability). Compass with declination adjustment.
    *   **Secondary:** Physical topographic maps in waterproof case. High-visibility markers (chem lights, flagging tape) to mark safe paths/zones *if* movement is required and conditions allow.
    *   **Perceptual Aids:**
        *   **Light Meter:** To gauge true illumination levels.
        *   **Chronometer:** Robust, backlit analog or multi-function digital watch to anchor time perception.
        *   **Directional Microphone/Decibel Meter:** To gauge sound sources/intensity and potentially map safe zones.
        *   **UV/Fluorescence Light:** To better visualize bioluminescence and potential bio-active zones.
    *   **Breakdown Zone Detection:** Experimental tech recommended if available:
        *   **Gravimeter/Inclinometer:** To detect subtle shifts in gravity/orientation.
        *   **Local Field Anomaly Detector:** (Quantum flux? Reality coherence sensor? - *Multiverse Tech May Vary*). **Treat any anomalous reading as immediate evacuation trigger.**
        *   **Visual Reference Wand:** Carry a straight, marked rod (e.g., titanium). Regularly check horizon and ground for warping relative to the wand. **If warping is visible, evacuate immediately.**

4.  **Tools & Survival Gear:**
    *   **Shelter:** Ultralight, high-strength tarp (bright orange for visibility) and emergency bivvy. *Tent stakes will not hold.* Plan to anchor with heavy substrate chunks or specialized deadman anchors.
    *   **Lighting:** **Redundant.** 2x high-lumen, waterproof headlamps (with red-light mode), 1x handheld spotlight. **All batteries lithium primary (non-rechargeable preferred)** - extreme cold kills rechargeables. Carry spares inside suit for warmth.
    *   **Water:** **DO NOT CONSUME ANY NATURAL WATER SOURCE.** Carry minimum 4L per person per day in insulated bladders to prevent freezing. Electrolyte tablets essential.
    *   **Food:** High-calorie, non-perishable, no-cook rations (freeze-dried, energy bars, jerky). Expect appetite suppression from cold/stress. No cooking flames (oxygen levels unknown, fire risk).
    *   **First Aid:** Comprehensive kit focused on:
        *   Severe hypothermia treatment (chemical heat packs, vapor barrier liner, mylar blankets).
        *   Abrasion/puncture wound care (antiseptic, irrigation syringe, non-adherent dressings).
        *   Anti-parasitic/anti-microbial broad-spectrum medications (consult multiverse biohazard specialist).
        *   Eye/skin flush kits (sterile saline).
        *   Pain management.
    *   **Multi-tool & Repair Kit:** Heavy-duty knife, pliers, duct tape, gear repair tape, zip ties, spare seals/gaskets.
    *   **Signaling:** PLB (Personal Locator Beacon) with satellite uplink (if functional in this reality), signal mirror, whistle. Assume radio comms will be unreliable due to terrain/atmosphere.

5.  **Behavioral & Operational Protocols:**
    *   **Movement:** Slow, deliberate, test footing constantly. Avoid living patches if possible – unpredictable reactions. **Never step into Breakdown Zones.** Mark and bypass.
    *   **Observation:** Stand still frequently. Let eyes/ears adjust. Avoid touching substrate/fauna directly. Note fauna behavior to predict movement paths. Minimize sound generation beyond what's necessary.
    *   **Time:** Operate on strict external chronometer schedule. Do not trust internal sense of time.
    *   **Communication:** Maintain constant visual and radio contact with team (if any). Use pre-defined hand signals due to noise. Verbally announce movements and observations.
    *   **Mindset:** Accept profound discomfort, disorientation, and alienness. Focus on procedure. The plateau is indifferent. Anthropomorphizing is dangerous. **Your goal is observation and safe return, not interaction or understanding.**
    *   **Contingency:** Pre-plan evacuation routes and rally points. Agree on immediate withdrawal triggers (e.g., suit breach, Breakdown Zone proximity, anomalous readings, team member distress). Assume rescue is not possible; self-reliance is paramount.

6.  **Pre-Departure:**
    *   **Vaccinations/Anti-Parasitics:** Consult xenobiologist for recommended prophylactics.
    *   **Gear Testing:** Test ALL gear extensively in simulated cold/wet conditions *before* departure. Ensure perfect function of respirator seals, lighting, navigation.
    *   **Physical Conditioning:** Focus on cold tolerance, endurance, and fine motor skills with gloved hands.
    *   **Mental Briefing:** Visualize the environment. Rehearse protocols. Understand the profound sense of scale, age, and otherness you will encounter. Brace for sensory overload.

**Final Warning:** The Grindstone Plateau is not hostile; it is fundamentally *alien* and operating on a scale and principle incompatible with human biology and perception. Your preparation is not for conquest or deep understanding, but for survival while glimpsing a reality that has long since passed its own context. Proceed with extreme caution, absolute respect for the protocols, and the expectation of perpetual, profound discomfort. Your suit is your world; the outside is a beautiful, dying machine.
