# Preparing for the Vast Mouth of Ond-Settling

This is a place that will try to unmake you — not violently, not quickly, but through the slow accumulation of wrongness: wrong sound, wrong light, wrong ground, wrong air. The preparation begins long before you arrive and it begins with accepting one fundamental truth: **you are an anomaly in a patterned world, and the pattern will notice you.**

---

## The Sound Problem — Your First and Greatest Threat

Nothing in your previous experience has prepared you for the acoustic environment of Ond-Settling. Read the description again if you need to. The sound is not loud. "Loud" implies a single overwhelming noise you can acclimatize to. This is different. This is *every sound that exists in this biome, simultaneously, continuously, with structure*, and it operates across frequencies that bypass your cochlear processing and reach directly into your autonomic nervous system. The description says it presses against your chest. It means your breathing will synchronize with rhythms you don't consciously hear. It means your heart rate will drift. It means that within twenty minutes of unprotected exposure, you will not be thinking — you will be *resonating*.

**Bring:** Custom-molded, noise-attenuating earpieces — not earplugs, earpieces — calibrated not to silence but to *translation*. The ideal device would compress the frequency range into something your auditory cortex can process without drowning, ideally filtering toward the rhythmic patterns the fauna are actually producing. If such translation tech exists in whatever multiverse-accessible catalog you have, invest in the best version. You need to be able to hear the environment *without being consumed by it*. Silence is not the goal — you would lose critical awareness of fauna, terrain water, and the harmonic thrum of the plant-columns, which tells you how wind is moving and what direction the nearest structural filaments are. Total silence here would be *more* dangerous than full exposure.

**Bring also:** A visual communication system. You will not be able to speak to companions — or to anyone — in any useful way. Standard radios may work but the broadband noise floor may overwhelm most consumer frequencies. You need a system that transmits above the highest audible range, or one that operates on a visual channel. Write things down. Use a projected display. Develop hand signals before you arrive — establish a vocabulary of gestures specific to this environment. Words like "water," "large fauna," "filament," "ridge-line," "stop," "that direction."

**Practice beforehand:** Sit in a room with multiple overlapping audio sources — industrial hum, insect recordings, subsonic bass, harmonic drones — and try to think. Not perform a task. Not solve a problem. *Think.* Notice how your inner monologue starts to fragment. Notice how you begin to think in images, in spatial relationships, in feelings rather than words. This is how you will think in Ond-Settling. You should be familiar with the experience before you arrive. Meditation traditions that emphasize non-verbal awareness would be useful training.

## The Ground — Walking on a Mouth

The terrain is young, sharp, and geometrically alien. The rock ridgelines are described as knife-thin. The ground is in angular bowls separated by shelves at angles that suggest tectonic violence. This is not walking on a surface. This is walking on a *surface that is still being manufactured*, and it is being manufactured without accommodation for your feet.

**Footwear:** The single most important physical preparation. You need boots with puncture-resistant soles thick enough to survive knife-edge rock, with ankle support robust enough for constant angular terrain — you will be walking on surfaces that slope in directions your proprioception does not expect. The soles should be slightly compliant (rubber-like, not rigid) because rigid soles on sharp ridges will slide unpredictably; you need the sole to deform around the geometry. Waterproofing is essential — the water seeps and retreats constantly, and the warm mineral liquid will degrade adhesive seals on standard footwear within hours. Vulcanized or welded construction. No stitching that can wick moisture.

**Bring also:** Knee protection. You will fall. The angular bowls mean constant changes in slope and surface angle, and the warm wet surfaces will be slick in unpredictable ways. Falling onto knife-edge rock is not a minor concern. Reinforced knee panels or external knee guards that allow mobility but distribute impact.

**Navigation awareness:** Do not step into the bottom of a basin without assessing the water cycle first. Watch for a few minutes. The water seeps and retreats — the ground has a pulse. If you stand in a basin during an expansion phase, you may be ankle-deep in warm mineral water before you can react. This is probably not lethal, but the water is faintly luminous, carries whatever the geological manufacturing process puts into it, and may interact with your skin chemistry in ways you cannot predict. Stay on the ridgelines when possible. They are sharp underfoot but at least they are *dry* and *predictable* — the geometry of the ridges is stable between manufacturing pulses.

**The large fauna:** The house-sized armored organisms *reshape the ground beneath their feet*. The rock becomes temporarily viscous. If you are on a ridge or in a bowl when one of these passes through, the ground under you may liquefy — not to liquid, but to something *less solid than it should be*. You need early warning. The fauna move slowly, with architectural precision, and they are surrounded by a "conspicuous near-emptiness" — a cleared radius. You will see them coming from far away. The protocol is simple: when you see one, you leave its path. Not later. Not when it gets closer. *Now.* The space it occupies is not just the space of its body — it is the space of its gravitational and material influence, and the description tells us that the technology in this place augments physics locally. A creature that can make rock behave like fluid may be doing things to local gravity as well. Give it the widest possible berth.

## The Warmth — A Constant You Cannot Escape

The temperature does not change. It is warm and close and it presses against skin and doesn't leave. This is not tropical warmth with a breeze that offers relief. This is an enclosed, humid warmth with no diurnal cycle, no seasonal variation, no respite. The air is described as damp — carrying mineral moisture from the seeping water and the condensation cycle. You will sweat. You will sweat continuously. Your clothes will be wet within an hour and will never dry, because the air is already saturated.

**Clothing:** Lightweight, moisture-wicking base layers made from synthetic materials that retain thermal regulation even when wet. Avoid cotton — it becomes a cold, heavy, useless membrane when saturated. Merino wool would work if the temperature permits, but given the persistent warmth, a technical synthetic is better. The outer layer should be thin, loose, and breathable — you are not protecting against cold or rain, you are protecting against abrasion (the vegetation is leather-textured and grows at body height in many places) and against the sharp edges of the terrain. A mesh outer layer that allows full airflow while catching the worst of the rock edges would be ideal.

**Hydration:** Bring all the water you need. The description of the local water — luminous, seeping from manufactured rock, cycling through geological processes you don't understand — is not encouraging for human consumption. Even if the mineral content is benign, the water is doing *something* in this ecosystem, something connected to the ongoing manufacturing of the landscape, and your body is not part of that system. Carry sufficient supply and a reliable purification system, though honestly, if your filtration is overwhelmed by whatever mineral complexity exists in that water, you'd rather run out and ration than drink it. Calculate your hydration needs for the warm, humid environment — minimum four liters per day, likely more with exertion on difficult terrain — and multiply by a safety factor of at least 1.5 for the unknown stressors of the environment.

**Cooling strategy:** You will not be able to reduce your body temperature through ambient cooling. The air is always warm and always humid — evaporative cooling (sweating) will be impaired. If your body cannot shed heat, it will accumulate it, and over hours and days, you will drift toward heat exhaustion. A personal cooling system — recirculating cold-water garment, phase-change cooling vest, something — is not a luxury. It is survival infrastructure. The phase-change approach (vests with embedded coolant packets that absorb heat as they melt) is probably best for this environment because it requires no power supply and functions passively. Bring enough phase-change packets for the duration of your stay plus reserves.

## The Light — Bruised Amber

The light is dim. Not dark — you can see for forty to fifty kilometers before haze obscures the horizon — but it is the wrong color and the wrong intensity for human visual comfort. The amber wash will distort color perception. Greens will be muted. Reds may disappear. Depth perception may be affected because much of the visual information your brain uses to judge distance is color-coded.

**Bring:** A headlamp with a broad-spectrum white LED, set to a low-to-medium brightness. You will not need this for visibility — the ambient light is sufficient for navigation — but you will need it for *inspection*. When examining the ground, checking your footing, examining the vegetation, or looking at the luminous water, the amber wash will obscure detail that matters. White light restores your ability to see what's actually there. Keep it dim — this place is patterned, designed, and *aware of you*. A bright beam sweeping the landscape is a very loud signal to whatever system is monitoring anomalies. Use the light only when you need it, close to the ground, and shielded from casting outward into the wider environment.

**Also:** Amber-tinted lenses may help with eye fatigue. If the ambient light is specifically in the amber range, your visual system is working harder than usual because it's receiving a narrow-band signal — essentially the equivalent of trying to read under a monochromatic street lamp. Filters that sharpen the edges of the amber range may improve visual acuity. But this is secondary to the primary concern.

## The Atmosphere — The Air That Thinks

The air carries information. It tingles on the skin. It has a faint visual distortion near technological artifacts. It smells of wet stone and crushed mineral sweetness. This is not just air — it is a *medium*, a communication channel, and you are passing through it like a stone through a stream.

**Respiratory protection:** If the atmosphere is carrying informational payloads — and the tingling suggests it is, because tingling on human skin is often a response to electromagnetic microstimulation or fine-particle interaction — you need to understand whether your respiratory system is also receiving this data. Inhaling an atmosphere that is designed to carry information for a non-human sensory architecture could be anywhere from harmless to catastrophic. Bring a basic respirator — not a full SCBA, not yet, but a P100 filter respirator that blocks particulate matter and a basic organic vapor cartridge. This will filter mineral particulates and some volatile compounds. It will not block electromagnetic signaling if that is what the air carries, but at least you've narrowed the exposure pathway.

**Skin protection:** Long sleeves, long pants, gloves when handling terrain. The tingling in the atmosphere may be benign — the ambient electromagnetic hum of the technology all around you — or it may be a low-level interaction between the atmospheric payload and your skin chemistry. Minimizing exposed skin minimizes the surface area available for whatever is happening. If you begin to feel the tingling intensify near specific objects (filaments, towers, pools of luminous water), *move away from those objects*. The pattern is slowly processing your presence; don't accelerate the process by lingering at points of high informational density.

## The Vegetation — Fractal and Watchful

The plants grow in perfect fractals to a depth of twelve to fifteen iterations. The description says this looks "like an accusation." It is right. This is engineered life, and its mathematical precision tells you that the ecosystem is *designed* — not adapted, not evolved, but specified, like code. The plants are leather-textured, thick-leafed, and they glow faintly from within.

**Do not assume the vegetation is passive.** In a system where the ground reshapes under fauna feet, where towers think, where the atmosphere carries data, the idea that the vegetation is merely growing is optimistic. The fractal precision suggests computational structure. The bioluminescence suggests energy conversion beyond simple photosynthesis. These plants may be nodes in a distributed processing network — the "furry" growth on the rock faces may be a sensory surface, the way the tower scales are computational surfaces.

**Practical considerations:** The vegetation grows in curtains from rock faces at body height. You will push through it. It is thick and leather-textured — it will abrade exposed skin, catch on equipment, and potentially interfere with gear straps and closures. Secure all equipment. Wear the outer layer described above. When pushing through dense growth, move slowly — the fractal structure means there are sharp edges at every scale, from the macro-fronds down to the microscopic divisions, and you don't know which of those edges are rigid enough to cut.

**Do not damage the vegetation.** This is not an environmental ethics recommendation — it is a survival tactic. The pattern is slow, the pattern is vast, but the pattern is *aware of you*. Damaging its engineered life forms is the fastest way to accelerate whatever decision process is being applied to your presence. Move around the growth. Step over the fronds. Do not break, tear, cut, or uproot anything. Leave the fractals intact.

## The Fauna — The Swarm and the Custodians

The small organisms are everywhere, in overwhelming numbers, producing a roar of collective communication. The large ones are rare, armored, and reshape terrain. The medium ones ride the large ones and tend them.

**Insectoid fauna:** Modular, multi-legged, with independently moving body segments. The description does not mention biting, stinging, or chemical defense, but the description is from a *distance* — it describes what the landscape looks like, not what it feels like when something with too many legs crawls onto your arm. Assume that the ubiquitous small fauna will investigate you. You are new. You are warm. You are producing sounds and chemical signatures they have never encountered. They will swarm-curious.

**Bring:** A personal insect barrier. Not DEET — DEET dissolves synthetic materials, and in an environment this technologically saturated, you don't want to risk chemical interaction with the local technology (including the filaments, which may be close to the ground and within reach of a chemical spray). A physical barrier is better: a fine-mesh head net, tightly woven clothing that closes at all openings, and a permethrin treatment applied to clothing before arrival (permethrin is bonded to fabric and doesn't aerosolize, minimizing interaction with the environment).

**Behavioral protocol:** If small fauna land on you, do not slap. Do not brush them off aggressively. *Remove them gently.* Slowly. They are part of a communication network. Crushing one may produce a chemical or electromagnetic signal that the rest of the network interprets — a distress call, an alarm, a *report of a new entity*. You want the fauna to classify you as "not food, not threat, not interesting" — the fastest path to that classification is calm, slow, non-destructive behavior. Think of yourself as a large, clumsy, temporary rock that happens to be walking through their world.

**Large fauna:** Maintain distance. The minimum safe distance is the radius of their cleared space — the "near-emptiness" that surrounds each one. If you are inside that radius, you are in their *operational zone*, and the ground under you is subject to their influence. The custodial smaller fauna orbit the large ones — if you approach a large organism, you will also encounter its entourage, and you have no idea how they will interpret your presence in their charge's immediate vicinity. Think of the large fauna as buildings that walk — you would not walk into someone's living room uninvited, and you would not stand in the path of a moving building.

## The Technology — The Visible and the Invisible

The towers that think. The grown buildings that glow. The filaments that pulse with information — or matter — or both. The augmented physics: altered gravity, bent light. This is not a primitive world with a few advanced artifacts. This is a world *saturated* with technology so integrated that it has become the environment itself.

**Electronics:** Your devices may not work correctly. The augmented physics near filaments and towers will affect electromagnetic signaling, clock rates, sensor calibration. A GPS equivalent may give you positions filtered through locally altered geometry. A camera may produce images with artifacts caused by bent light. A clock may run at the wrong speed. **Calibrate your sense of time and space against physical, non-electronic references.** Count your steps for distance. Track the sun — or what passes for it in the amber sky — for time. Use landmarks, not coordinates. Trust your body's proprioception for slope and orientation, not digital inclinometers.

**The filaments:** They run along the ground or just above it. They pulse with rhythmic light. They carry information and possibly matter. **Do not step on them. Do not touch them. Do not bridge them with metal objects or your body.** You are already an anomaly; creating an uncontrolled connection between your biological system and the filaments' informational network is a risk that needs no explanation. If a filament runs across your intended path, step over it with clearance. If multiple filaments cross an area, find another route.

**The towers:** The nearest tower is kilometers away. The description says its surface scales are individually articulated computational panels — each one performing computation, the tower's surface being its *mind*. If you approach the tower, you are approaching a thinking surface. The surface may not have a concept of "you" in the way you have a concept of yourself — it may process your presence as environmental data, a perturbation in its sensory field. But a perturbation is still something it will *register*, and a thinking surface that registers a novel perturbation will allocate resources to understanding it. Approaching a tower is like walking up to a sleeping brain and tapping on the cortex. The brain will wake up. You do not want to be the thing that wakes it up.

**Approach technology with caution and humility.** If a filament's pulse changes rhythm as you pass, *move away.* If the ambient light shifts near a building, *move away.* If the atmospheric tingling intensifies, *move away.* You are an uninvited variable in a system that optimizes for separation and precision. The best strategy is to be as small, as quiet, and as transient as possible.

## The Psychological Preparation — Being the Anomaly

This is the preparation that matters most, and it is the one most travelers will neglect.

You are going to a place where everything — every rock, every plant, every creature, every structure, every particle of air — was *designed*. Not evolved, not formed by natural processes, but specified, manufactured, and integrated into a pattern of staggering complexity. You will stand in this landscape and you will feel, with every sense you have, that you do not belong. The description confirms this: *you are the only thing here that was not built for this place.*

**This will not feel like being a tourist in a foreign country.** It will feel like being a foreign body in an immune system. You will feel *watched* — because you are, by the pattern, by the technology, by the very air. You will feel *small* — not in the way that mountains make you feel small, but in the way that a random mutation feels small inside a genome. Your presence is noise in a signal. Your shape is an error in a dataset. You are *wrong* here, in a way that is deeper than cultural difference, and you will feel it in your bones.

**Prepare for:** Disorientation that is not spatial but existential. The inability to understand *why* this place exists, what it is *for*, what the pattern *means* — the description says the basin "would look like a diagram from above" but from the ground it looks like wilderness. You will be in the wilderness. You will never see the diagram. You will never know the purpose. This will be uncomfortable in a way that no amount of physical preparation can address.

**Prepare for:** The sound. Not just the physical assault of the noise, but the *meaning* in it. The fauna are communicating. The towers are thinking. The filaments are transmitting. Everything here is *saying something*, and you can almost hear it, almost parse it, but never quite — the description says it's like "eavesdropping on a city from a great height." This near-comprehension will be maddening. Your brain is a pattern-recognition engine, and it will try desperately to decode the patterns around it, and it will fail, and the failure will be the most disorienting thing about the place.

**Prepare for:** The patience required. The pattern's decision about you will be made "slowly, at the pace of geology." This means *days, weeks, perhaps longer.* You cannot rush it. You cannot influence it. You cannot communicate with it in any direct way. You exist inside a system that is processing your presence at its own pace, and the only useful thing you can do is *continue to be small, quiet, non-destructive, and temporary.*

**Bring:** A journal. Write every day. Not for the record — for your *sanity.* The inability to think in words, caused by the sound, means that your internal narrative will fragment over time. Writing forces the reconstruction of language. Writing anchors you to your own humanity. When you feel yourself beginning to think in fractals, in pulses, in patterns that don't have names — pick up the pen. Write a sentence. It doesn't matter what it says. The act of constructing a sentence is the act of being human, and in Ond-Settling, that is something you will need to practice deliberately.

## Final Advisory

You are going to a place that is *complete.* It does not need you. It does not want you. It does not reject you — rejection would imply awareness of your specific nature, and the pattern may not yet have decided what your specific nature *is.* You are a rounding error in a calculation that spans a basin. Your best strategy, your only strategy, is to be a rounding error that leaves no trace. Go. Observe. Record. Do not touch, do not alter, do not linger at points of technological density. And when the pattern finishes processing you — when it has decided that you are temporary, that you are noise, that you will pass through and leave no road behind you — then you can leave.

But know this: the description says the basin will be there "long after you are gone." It will not remember you. The towers will not have registered you as significant. The fauna will not tell stories about you. The fractal plants will continue their exact divisions to the fifteenth level, and none of those levels will contain a record of your passing. You will leave Ond-Settling exactly as you entered it — as the only thing there that was not built for the place, processed and dismissed by a pattern that has more important things to think about.

Prepare for that. It is, in its way, the hardest thing about going.
