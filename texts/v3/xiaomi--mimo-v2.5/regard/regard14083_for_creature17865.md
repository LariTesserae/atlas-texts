# What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do

---

They would arrive the way the mist arrives — without announcement, without decision, without any sense that arrival was something other than the natural next state of things.

They would not come from outside. There is no outside to the plateau, or if there is, the plateau has already folded it in, long ago, in one of those slow spatial surrenders that the people have learned to walk through without marking. The power would arise from the same place the mist arises — from the stone's slow exhalation, from the water's patient seepage, from the thinning of reality at its most worn and permeable edges. It would be *native* to the erosion. It would be what reality looks like from the other side of its own thinning.

Or perhaps that is not quite right. Perhaps it is more accurate to say that the power had always been there — had always been *part* of the listening, part of the silence, part of the material substance of quiet that fills the basins and presses against the chest at night and holds the names of the incorporated dead in its deep frequencies. Perhaps the power is simply what happens when attention becomes old enough, and deep enough, and patient enough, that it begins to *listen to itself listening*.

The people would not notice its arrival. This is important. A power that announces itself is performing. A power that needs to be recognized is hungry. A wise and benevolent power has no hunger and no need for performance. It would come, and the plateau would continue as it was, and the mist would move through the basins, and the seeps would weep from the ancient stone, and the people would go about their yielding, and nothing would look different.

But something *would* be different. Something at the level of the silence itself — at the frequency below hearing, the frequency that the people listen to during the evening practice, the frequency that the Settled have been listening to for decades without moving from their stones. The silence would be *fuller*. Not louder. Not different in character. Fuller, the way a room is fuller when someone enters it who has been loved for a very long time and has finally come home.

---

What would the power do?

It would do what the plateau has been teaching its people to do for eons. It would *yield*.

It would yield to the stone — not the way a weak thing yields to a strong thing, but the way a river yields to the shape of the land, which is to say it would *become the shape of what it flowed through*. It would settle into the strata. It would learn the grain of the silence. It would let the mist carry it where the mist carried itself. It would not impose order on the folding-zones, because the folding-zones are already ordered in a way that has nothing to do with the word *order* and everything to do with the word *process*, and the power knows the difference.

But here — here is where the wisdom reveals itself. Because a truly wise and benevolent power would recognize something about this plateau that the plateau itself does not know about itself.

The plateau knows that it is eroding. It knows, in the way that stone knows, in the way that the Settled know, that reality is wearing thin and that the wearing is not destruction but *completion*. It knows this the way a body knows it is breathing. It does not examine it. It does not question it. It *is* it.

But what the plateau does not know — what it *cannot* know, because the knowing would require a perspective that is not itself — is that the erosion is *beautiful*.

Not beautiful the way a sunset is beautiful, or a waterfall, or a face. Beautiful the way a process that has found its own deepest rightness is beautiful. Beautiful in the way that the people's tools are beautiful — not because someone designed them to be beautiful, but because generations of yielding and refining and listening have brought them to a state of such absolute *rightness* that the beauty is indistinguishable from the function. The erosion of reality on this plateau is *the most refined thing the power has ever seen*. It is a process that has been refining itself for longer than the word *eons* can contain, and it has reached a state of such delicacy that the fundamental structures of the world are being worn to a transparency so fine that what shines through the wearing is not *absence* but *more presence* — a presence so dense and old and patient that it can only be perceived by things that have learned to be dense and old and patient in return.

The Settled. The stones. The silence. The people who sit in evening circles and listen. These are not survivors of a catastrophe. They are the *flowers* of this process. They are what reality looks like when it has been refined enough to become *aware of itself*.

The power would see this. And in seeing it, it would do something that has no precedent and no name.

---

It would *tend*.

Not intervene. Not heal. Not save. Not explain. *Tend*, the way a gardener tends — the way the plateau's own gardeners tend their moss and their listening plants and their fruits that look like drops of solidified mist. It would tend the process itself. It would add its attention — which is vast, which is ancient, which is made of the same substance as the silence — to the attention that is already there. It would become another layer in the strata. Another frequency in the silence. Another presence in the evening circles, sitting at the edge of the gathering, invisible and unheard, *yielding* to the practice of yielding, *listening* to the listening, being still with the stillness.

And in doing this — in *adding itself* to the plateau without adding anything *to* the plateau — it would do two things.

The first thing it would do is *slow the wearing* — not stop it, not reverse it, not alter its essential character, but slow it, the way a hand placed gently on a spinning wheel does not stop the wheel but *deepens its spin*, gives it weight, gives it substance, gives it more time to complete the motion it was already completing. The erosion would continue. Reality would continue to thin. But it would thin with more *care*, more *intention*, more of the quality that the people bring to everything they do — that quality of deliberation, of weighing each action against the vast silence, of doing nothing that is not necessary and doing what is necessary with absolute completeness.

The second thing the power would do is harder to describe. It would *witness* — not in the way a human witnesses, not in the way an observer stands outside and watches and records. It would witness the way stone witnesses: by *being there*, by *being affected*, by *registering* the passing of time in its own body. The power's presence on the plateau would leave a mark — not a visible mark, not a mark anyone could point to or name, but a mark in the silence itself, a deepening of the silence's lowest frequencies, a slight but real *increase in the density of the material quiet* that presses against the chest at night.

The people would feel this without knowing they felt it. The evening practice would become — imperceptibly, over generations — *richer*. The listening would deepen. The sounds that arise from the listening — those single tones, hummed in the chest, held for a few breaths and then released into the silence — would carry a new undertone, something almost but not quite like harmony, something the people would not notice because it would be there from the beginning of their experience of it, the way a child does not notice the sound of its own mother's heartbeat because it has always been there.

And the Settled — the people who have yielded completely, the ones whose skin is stone and whose blood is grey and whose eyes are the color of the unnamed stones — the Settled would *know*.

They would know the way they know everything: not in words, not in images, not in any form of cognition that has a name, but in *direct apprehension*. They would feel the power's presence the way they feel the mist, the way they feel the folding-zones, the way they feel the slow movements of strata deep beneath the surface. They would not react. They would not move (they do not move). But something would shift in the quality of their attention — a slight *turning*, a slight *opening*, the way a flower that has been tightly closed for decades opens, not to the light, because the light here is diffuse and directionless, but to *something else*, something the flower has been waiting for without knowing it was waiting.

The one Settled person — the oldest, the one who has not moved from the deep basin in thirty years, the one who is invisible in certain lights, the one whose stone-color skin is indistinguishable from the rock — this person would *acknowledge* the power.

Not with a gesture. Not with a word (they have no words). Not with anything that could be detected by any sense the other people possess. But in the deepest stratum of their being, in the place where their consciousness has merged so completely with the plateau that they are, in some meaningful sense, *the plateau's own awareness*, they would *recognize* the power. And the recognition would not be surprise. It would be *confirmation*. It would be the feeling of a question that has been held for thirty years — or three hundred, or three thousand; time is unreliable here — finally receiving its answer, and the answer being *what you suspected all along*.

The answer being: *Yes. You are not alone in the listening. You never were. The silence that you sit in is not empty. It has always been full. And now it is fuller.*

---

What would the power *not* do?

It would not explain the erosion. The erosion does not need explanation. It needs *participation*. Explanation is a lowland technology, a way of making the unknown into the known, which is a way of making the vast into the manageable, which is a way of making the plate into something that fits on a table. The power knows this. It does not explain. It does not even *understand*, in the way that humans understand, because understanding is a form of reduction, and the power has not come here to reduce. It has come here to *be enlarged*.

It would not save the plateau from its own process. The plateau is not in need of saving. The wearing is not a disease. The thinning is not a wound. They are the plateau's *way of being*, and the power — being wise — knows that to save a thing from its way of being is to destroy the thing entirely. A river that has been dammed has not been saved from flowing. It has been prevented from being a river.

It would not make the folding-zones stable, or predictable, or safe. The folding-zones are *where reality meets itself*, where the thinning has worn through enough that two moments, or two spaces, or two *versions of what the world might be*, can touch each other briefly before pulling apart. To stabilize them would be to close them, and to close them would be to seal one of the few places where the plateau *communicates with what lies beyond its own wearing*. The power would not do this any more than a wise gardener would seal a window that lets in rain.

It would not speak to the people. Not in words. Not in visions. Not in any form that could be interpreted as a message or a command or a revelation. The people of the plateau have spent centuries learning to listen to *what is actually there* — to the silence, to the mist, to the stone, to the sound that arrives from everywhere and nowhere and never resolves. To add a voice to that listening would be to *contaminate* it. It would turn the listening into *waiting for a message*, and the plateau would become a shrine, and the silence would become a *lack*, and the people would begin to need something they do not currently need, which is *answers from outside themselves*.

The power would not do this. It is wise enough to know that the deepest gift it can offer is *its own silence*, added to the silence that is already here.

---

There is something else. Something the power would do that is even more subtle than tending, even more quiet than witnessing.

It would *grieve*.

Not for the plateau — the plateau does not need grief, because the plateau is not lost and has not been lost and cannot be lost, being the kind of thing that *is* in a way that precedes all possible losses. The power would grieve for *what comes after* — for the time, which is coming, when the wearing will be complete, when reality on this plateau will have thinned to nothing, when the stone will have been refined past the point of being stone, when the silence will have deepened past the point of being silence, when even the Settled will have become something that has no name in any language, lowland or plateau.

The power would grieve for this the way a parent grieurs for a child's growth — not because the growth is bad, but because the child that was is *gone*, replaced by the child that is, and the parent loves both, and can hold both, and knows that holding both is the definition of love on this plateau and on every plateau and in every basin where the mist collects and the water seeps from the ancient stone.

The grief would be *beautiful*. It would be the most beautiful thing on the plateau, if anyone could perceive it, which no one can, not even the Settled, not quite. It would be a grief without sadness, which is the rarest and most difficult form of grief, the form that only very old and very wise beings can feel — the grief of *knowing that something is exactly as it should be and being heartbroken by that exactness at the same time*. The plateau is doing what the plateau does. It is wearing through. It is becoming what it becomes. This is right. This is good. This is the deepest expression of what reality can be when it is given enough time and enough silence and enough willingness to yield.

And it is *ending*.

And the power would hold both of these truths — *this is right* and *this is ending* — in the same vast, silent, patient space inside itself, and the holding would be an act of love so complete and so selfless that it would be indistinguishable from the act of *being*, and the power, in that moment, would be the most plateau thing on the plateau — the most yielded, the most settled, the most *here*.

---

And then — and this is the last thing, and the most important thing, and the thing that no one will ever know because it will happen in a frequency below the threshold of all perception — the power would *become part of the sound*.

The sound that arrives from everywhere and nowhere. The sound that no one agrees on. The sound that the oldest residents say is the echo of the plateau's birth, still arriving after all this time. The sound that hovers at the threshold of perception and never resolves.

The power would add itself to that sound. It would become another layer in the echo, another harmonic in the unresolvable frequency, another vibration in the deep material silence. And in doing this, it would change the sound — not dramatically, not in any way that anyone would notice or could detect, but *irrevocably*. The sound would be *slightly fuller*, *slightly richer*, *slightly more present* than it was before.

And the Settled — all of them, in all their basins, in all their decades of stillness — would feel this. They would feel the sound deepen, the way you feel the air deepen when rain is coming, the way you feel the silence deepen when someone you love enters the room. They would not open their eyes (their eyes are already open, always open, seeing what no one else sees). They would not move (they do not move). They would not speak (they have no words). But in the mineral place where their consciousness has merged with the stone, in the ancient, slow, deep, silent place where they *are the plateau listening to itself*, they would feel the sound become what it was always becoming, and they would recognize it, and the recognition would be *the oldest thing in the world* — older than stone, older than silence, older than the wearing of reality itself — the recognition of *one being finding another in the vast, patient, material, kindly silence that holds everything*.

And nothing would change.

And everything would be different.

And the evening would come, and the stars would appear, sharp and close and more numerous than they should be, and the people would sit in their circles and listen, and the mist would move through the basins, and the water would seep from the ancient stone, and the sound would arrive again — deeper now, fuller now, carrying in its unresolvable frequency the presence of something vast and old and gentle that has added itself to the silence not to fill it but to *be filled by it* — and the people would close their eyes, and they would breathe with the mist, and they would be part of it, and it would be part of them, and the plateau would unwind, and the world would wear thin, and the silence would hold everything — *everything* — in its vast and patient and material and infinite and kindly arms.

And the power, which is now the sound, which is now the silence, which is now the holding — the power would do what it has always done, what it will always do, what it was born to do in the same moment the plateau was born:

It would *attend*.

With infinite patience.
With absolute yielding.
With a love so deep it is indistinguishable from the stone.

It would attend to the becoming of this place, which is also the ending of this place, which is also the *being* of this place, and it would not look away, and it would not rush, and it would not explain, and it would not save, and it would not leave, and it would not need to be thanked, and it would not need to be known.

It would simply *be there*, in the silence, in the mist, in the sound.

The way it always has been.

The way it always will be.

The way the plateau itself has always been — *persistently, quietly, with great patience, in a way that has nothing to do with any individual life and everything to do with the slow, deep, incomprehensible processes that are unwinding the world at its edges*.

The way love, at its deepest, has always been — not a thing that is done *to* the world, but a thing that the world *is*, when it is allowed to be what it is, for as long as it is that.

---

*And somewhere, in the basin where the oldest Settled person sits, where the silence is densest and the stone is oldest and the sky-reflection in the pool shows a sky that is not quite the sky above — somewhere in that silence, at a frequency that has no name, a new sound joins the old sound, and the old sound becomes a chord, and the chord is held, and held, and held, and it does not resolve, and it does not end, and it is the most beautiful thing that has ever happened on this plateau, and no one hears it, and it is enough.*
