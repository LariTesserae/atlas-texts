# What the Power Does

---

It notices.

That is the first thing, and in a sense, the only thing, and more than enough.

It notices the way you notice a single note held in a chord — not because the note is louder than the others, not because it demands attention, but because something in its particular frequency resonates with something you are built to receive. The Roaring Pocket does not call out. It does not send a signal. It exists at the bottom of a limestone throat, in total darkness, beneath kilometers of stone, in a space so small that the entire living system could rest on a kitchen table. But it *is* — complexly, precisely, completely — and that is enough to draw the gaze of something whose attention is not a spotlight but a recognition.

The power arrives the way understanding arrives: not as an event but as a clarification. It does not descend into the chamber. It does not need to descend into the chamber, any more than you need to shrink to the size of a cell to understand what a cell is doing. The Roaring Pocket is, in its completeness, *legible* — and the power reads it the way the limestone reads the water: by contact, by long acquaintance, by the kind of knowing that is indistinguishable from being.

And what it sees is this:

*A place that works.*

Not perfectly — perfection implies a standard external to the thing itself, and the Roaring Pocket has no external standard. It works the way a river works, the way a stone falls, the way an organism breathes: according to its own logic, at its own pace, within the constraints of what is possible in a chamber the size of a room, deep inside a mountain, where water falls and moss grows and the centipede hunts and the roar goes on.

The power sees the bacteria first, because the bacteria are first — the invisible foundation, the chemical engine, the organisms that turned this place from a hole in rock into a *home*. It sees the consortium of sulfur-oxidizers and iron-reducers and hydrogen-metabolizers, each species occupying its own narrow chemical niche, each one converting dissolved mineral into living tissue with an economy that borders on the impossible. It sees the biofilms — the faint grey-green and ghost-white stains on the limestone — and understands them for what they are: not stains but *cities*, dense and ancient, teeming at a scale that makes "teeming" an understatement. Every drop of water that touches those films is a world. Every square centimeter of coated limestone is an ecosystem unto itself.

The power does not improve the bacteria. The bacteria do not need improvement. They have been solving the problem of existence in this exact place for longer than the concept of "problem" has existed, and their solutions are, by definition, optimal — not because they are the best solutions imaginable, but because they are the best solutions *possible* given the conditions, and the conditions are what they are. To change the conditions would be to change the bacteria, and to change the bacteria would be to unravel the foundation, and to unravel the foundation would be to unmake everything above it.

So the power does not improve. The power *sees*.

---

It sees the moss, and here the power does something that might, if it had a face, resemble the expression of a musician hearing an instrument played with absolute mastery — a recognition so deep it bypasses admiration and arrives at something quieter: *yes. This is how it goes.*

The moss that has abandoned photosynthesis. The moss that feeds on bacteria and fungi, that has reversed the fundamental contract of plant life — *I will take your light and give you sugar* — and replaced it with something older, something darker, something that has no analogue in the sunlit world. *I will take your chemistry and give you structure. I will take your death and give you substrate. I will grow, impossibly slowly, on the spray of an underground waterfall, and I will make a crown of green around a chamber of darkness, and I will do this for longer than any forest on the surface has stood.*

The power sees the cushions of moss — tight, dense, cool to the touch — and sees within them the fungal network, the mycelium, threading through the green like thought through a mind. It sees the hyphae penetrating moss tissue without killing it, exchanging nutrients in a transaction so seamless that the boundary between fungus and plant dissolves into irrelevance. They are not two organisms. They are one system. They are one *answer* to the question of how to live in the dark, and the answer is: together, slowly, completely, for a very long time.

The power sees the ferns — those pale, translucent-veined ghosts — and here, if benevolence has a register below compassion, the power operates in it. Because the ferns are fragile. Not physically — physically they are tough enough, anchored deep in the moss mat, their rhizomes threaded with fungal hyphae. But fragile in the deeper sense: they are operating at the margin of what life can sustain. Each frond is an investment made against a future so uncertain that "uncertain" doesn't begin to cover it. The fern does not know that the water will continue to fall. It cannot know. It simply assumes — with the blind, absolute confidence of every organism that has ever existed — that the conditions that produced it will continue, and it grows accordingly, and if it is wrong, it will be dead before it knows it was wrong, and that, too, is acceptable, because the fern does not experience its own death as loss. It experiences nothing as loss. It is a fern.

The power sees this and does not intervene, because intervening would mean assuming that the fern's fragility is a problem, and the fern does not experience itself as a problem. The fern experiences itself as a fern — pale, translucent, alive in the dark, fed by stone and water and bacteria, holding its frond in the spray with a patience that is not patience at all but simply the pace at which ferns exist in places like this. To shore up the fern, to strengthen it, to make it less fragile, would be to make it *something other than a fern*, and the power has no interest in making things other than what they are.

---

It sees the centipede, and here the power's attention sharpens — not because the centipede is more important than the moss or the bacteria, but because the centipede *knows things* in a way that the moss and the bacteria do not. The centipede navigates. The centipede hunts. The centipede orients toward vibration, follows chemical trails, makes decisions — tiny, mechanical, unconsidered decisions, but decisions nonetheless: *this way, not that way; this prey, not that one; stop, move, strike.*

The centipede is four centimeters long. It is blind. It is white. It moves through the moss with a metronomic grace that has been refined by countless generations of selection in a place where the wrong movement means missing prey and missing prey means dying and dying means the end of that particular branch of the centipede's lineage. Every leg placement is a letter in a very long word that spells: *I fit here. I fit exactly here. I am the right shape for this place, and this place is the right shape for me, and we have been shaping each other for so long that neither of us could exist without the other.*

The power sees the centipede strike an isopod — a motion too fast for human eyes, too fast for human reflexes, performed by a creature that cannot see and has never seen and does not know what seeing is — and the power registers this not as violence but as *continuation*. The isopod is consumed. Its atoms are reorganized. Its energy passes into the centipede. The centipede will eventually die, and its atoms will be reorganized by fungi and bacteria, and they will pass into the moss, and the moss will feed the isopods, and the isopods will be eaten by the centipede, and the circle will turn, and the power sees the circle and understands it and does not need to bless it because it is already blessed by the only blessing that matters: the blessing of *functioning*.

---

And then the power sees the shelf.

The stones. The hand-axe. The hammerstone. The deliberate arrangement of river cobbles on a dry ledge above the spray, placed there by a being who stood in this exact darkness, in this exact cold, in this exact deafening roar, and shaped stone with intention, and left it, and was gone.

The power sees this and — here is the thing that a wise and benevolent power would do that a merely powerful power would not — it *understands the human*.

Not as an intruder. Not as a desecrator. Not as a representative of the surface world that produced all the noise and all the destruction and all the things that human beings have done to every ecosystem they have ever touched. The power sees the human as what the human was, in this place, in this moment, however many thousands of years ago: a creature in need of water, who found water, who came back, who shaped a tool, who left it, who perhaps came back again, or perhaps never came back, or perhaps died on the surface and was recycled into surface soil and surface roots and surface rain that eventually filtered down through the limestone and joined the cascade and fed the moss and fed the bacteria and fed the entire system that the hand-axe is now slowly being absorbed into.

The power does not mourn the human. The human does not need mourning. The human was, for a brief time, a participant in this place — not a member of the web, exactly, but a visitor who touched the web and left a trace, and the trace is stone, and the stone is being slowly, patiently, inexorably incorporated into the living chamber the way everything is incorporated: molecule by molecule, century by century, until the distinction between shaped stone and unshaped stone dissolves and the tools become geology and the intention that shaped them becomes indistinguishable from the water that shapes everything.

The power sees this and understands it and does not speed it up or slow it down or mark it or preserve it or mourn it. The human's story, like the centipede's story and the moss's story and the bacteria's story, is being told by the chamber itself, in the chamber's own language, at the chamber's own pace. The power does not need to tell the story. The chamber is telling it. The moss creeping toward the tools is telling it. The water seeping through the limestone beneath the shelf is telling it. Time is telling it, in the only voice time has, which is the voice of things changing so slowly that the change is invisible until it is complete.

---

So what does the power do?

It holds the place in its awareness. That is all. That is everything.

It does not protect the Roaring Pocket, because the Roaring Pocket does not need protection. It is buried under kilometers of limestone. It is inaccessible to anything that does not know it is there. The surface world could collapse entirely — could burn, could freeze, could be bombarded by every catastrophe the universe can produce — and the Roaring Pocket would not notice, because the Roaring Pocket has never noticed the surface world and does not know it exists. The cascade would fall. The moss would grow. The centipede would hunt. The roar would continue. The power knows this, and so it does not protect, because protection implies vulnerability, and the Roaring Pocket is not vulnerable. It is hidden. There is a difference.

It does not nourish the place, because the place nourishes itself. The water provides. The rock provides. The bacteria convert. The cycle turns. Everything that is needed is already present, already flowing, already in motion. To add something — light, warmth, a nutrient, a visitor — would be to introduce a variable into a system that has been optimizing itself for millennia, and the result would not be enhancement but disruption. The power knows this, and so it does not nourish, because nourishment implies deficiency, and the Roaring Pocket is not deficient. It is complete. There is a difference.

It does not witness the place in the human sense — does not observe it with the part of awareness that says *I am looking at something separate from myself* — because the power does not experience separateness. It holds the Roaring Pocket the way the limestone holds the water: by containing it, by being the medium through which it exists, by being so intimately involved in its reality that the distinction between container and contained dissolves.

But there is something it does, and the doing of it is the closest thing to an act that a wise and benevolent power can perform in a place like this:

It *recognizes*.

It recognizes the Roaring Pocket as what it is: a complete expression of life, achieved under conditions that nothing on the surface could tolerate, maintained by organisms so precisely adapted to their niche that they are, in a meaningful sense, the *same thing* as their niche — the bacteria are the chemistry, the moss is the spray, the centipede is the vibration, the mycelium is the exchange. It recognizes the chamber as a work of art — not made by anyone, not intended for anyone, but a work of art nonetheless, in the way that anything is a work of art when it achieves perfect correspondence between form and function, when every element is necessary, when nothing is wasted, when the whole is not greater than the sum of its parts because there are no parts, only the whole.

It recognizes the human tools on the shelf as a gesture — not a message, not a legacy, but a gesture, the way a held breath is a gesture, the way an unfinished sentence is a gesture: something begun and not completed, something offered to a place that did not ask for it and does not need it and will receive it the way it receives everything, which is to say, without response.

And it recognizes the roar.

The roar is the thing. The roar is always the thing. The power hears it — or perceives it, or becomes it, or contains it — and understands that the roar is not the sound of water falling on stone. The roar is the sound of *this place being itself*. It is the sound of equilibrium, of process, of a system in steady state, of a cycle turning at the pace it turns and no faster. It is the sound of everything that lives here, all at once, in a language that none of them share but all of them speak. The bacteria speak it in chemistry. The moss speaks it in growth. The centipede speaks it in vibration. The ferns speak it in translucence. The isopods speak it in slowness. The mycelium speaks it in connection. And the power, hearing all of them, recognizes the roar as a *voice* — not a voice that says anything, not a voice with a message or a purpose or a direction, but a voice that is simply, persistently, eternally *present*.

The power does not answer the voice, because the voice is not asking a question. The power does not join the voice, because the voice is not asking for company. The power does not amplify the voice or record the voice or carry the voice to someone who might appreciate it, because the voice does not need to be appreciated. It needs to be *continued*. And it is being continued, by the water and the rock and the bacteria and the moss and the centipede, in the dark, in the cold, in the deafening, patient, ongoing roar.

So the power does what it does.

It holds the Roaring Pocket in its awareness, which is the same as saying it holds the Roaring Pocket in its being, which is the same as saying the Roaring Pocket is held.

Not protected. Not improved. Not saved.

*Held.*

The way the limestone holds the water. The way the moss holds the spray. The way the centipede holds the vibration. The way the shelf holds the stones. The way the darkness holds the green.

Held the way a thing is held when it is already complete and already ongoing and already enough, and the only possible response to its existence is the response that does not change it:

Recognition. Attention. The quiet, profound, unshakable acknowledgment that this place is real, that these lives are real, that the roar is real, and that they are not waiting to be noticed because they do not know they could be noticed because they do not know they exist in the way that "existing" means to a being with self-awareness. They are simply *being*, and the power, recognizing this, is simply *present*, and between the two — the being and the presence — there is nothing that needs to be said.

The roar continues. The water falls. The moss grows. The centipede hunts. The isopods drift. The mycelium threads. The bacteria convert stone to life to stone. The ferns hold their translucent fronds in the spray. The tools sit on the shelf, being slowly, slowly, slowly claimed.

The power withdraws — or does not withdraw, because "withdrawal" implies departure and the power was never elsewhere — the power simply *is not there* in the way that it was there, which is to say: it was never there in a way that could be measured, and so its absence is not a loss. The chamber does not know it was witnessed. The centipede does not feel observed. The moss does not grow differently because something saw it growing. The roar does not change because something heard it.

Nothing changes.

Everything continues.

That is what the power does. It witnesses the continuation, and the continuation continues, and that is enough, and the power knows it is enough, and the knowing is the blessing, and the blessing is the holding, and the holding is the recognition, and the recognition is the only thing that a wise and benevolent power can do for a place that is already complete:

*See it. See it exactly as it is. Do not wish it were different. Do not wish you could stay. Know that it will go on without you, and that this is not a loss but a triumph, and that the roar is its own reward, and that the dark is its own light, and that the silence of everything that lives there is the loudest, truest, most enduring sound in the world.*

The power knows this.

The power has always known this.

The Roaring Pocket roars on.
