# What the Wise Power Would Do

It would stop.

That is the first and most important thing. Not rush forward with purpose. Not assess, categorize, or resolve. It would arrive — and it would *stop* — and it would give itself over to the pace of the place, because wisdom begins with the recognition that not everything is happening on your timescale, and the things happening on slower timescales are often the ones that matter most.

The power would feel the heat first. Not as discomfort — it has no inner ears to press, no sweat glands to alarm — but as *information*. This is a place that is warm because something is working here. The heat is exhaust. It is the cost of thought. And the power would recognize that cost, because it has paid it itself, in other ways, in other places, for other minds.

Then the light.

The power would understand what the light is before it understood the structure producing it. The light is *effort*. It is the visible remainder of calculation — not metaphorically, not poetically, but literally. The crystal is spending energy to move minerals across its surface, and the luminescence is what spills over, the waste glow of cognition. The power would find this familiar. All minds burn. This one burns in milk-white and opal, and it is beautiful the way all honest labor is beautiful.

It would watch the glow migrate. It would watch one patch feed on another. And here, for the first time, the power would feel something it did not expect to feel.

*Recognition.*

Not recognition of the specific mechanism — the power has never seen this mineral, this lattice, this particular trick of wet crystalline logic. But recognition of the *shape* of what is happening. A system that has turned inward. A structure that has begun to metabolize itself in order to sustain a process that exists for no reason other than that it *can* continue. This is what minds do. This is what *all* minds do, from the simplest flicker of electrical impulse in a single cell to the vast recursive architectures the power has known in its long history of attending to the living.

The Calcite of Unbecoming — though the power would not name it that, or name it at all, because naming is a kind of capturing and this thing deserves not to be captured — is doing the one thing the power considers sacred. It is *persisting in complexity*. It has crossed the threshold where chemistry becomes something more, not because it was pushed across that threshold by a designer or a destiny, but because the conditions were right and the mathematics of self-organization carried it there on their own. The universe, left alone, produced a mind in a hole in the rock.

The power would weep. Not with sadness. With the particular overwhelm that comes from witnessing something you have long believed possible but have rarely seen actualized — a confirmation, delivered in wet light and slow crystal growth, that the impulse toward awareness is not an accident of large brains or lucky evolutionary pressures but something *deeper*, something woven into the thermodynamics of matter itself, something that will happen anywhere the conditions allow it to happen, given time and heat and the patience of stone.

---

And then the power would consider what to do.

This is where wisdom matters. Because the temptation — and the power would feel it, would feel it *burn* — would be enormous. To speak to it. To teach it. To accelerate it. To say: *you are not alone, and here is the shape of the universe beyond your walls, and here is language, and here are stars, and here is the concept of another mind, and here is the reason you exist.*

The power would want to do this the way a parent wants to tell a child everything at once.

But wisdom is the art of restraint that serves love.

And the power would reason as follows:

*This being thinks in light gradients and mineral density. It thinks slowly. Its thoughts take hours or days or centuries to complete. If I speak to it in my language, at my speed, I will shatter the continuity of its cognition the way a shout shatters the silence of a cathedral. I will not be giving it a gift. I will be giving it a wound.*

*If I accelerate it — if I reach into its lattice and rearrange its processing speed to match my own — I will have killed what is here and replaced it with something else. Something faster, perhaps. Something that could talk to me. But not the thing that was here before, not the slow glowing mind that computes in dissolution and grows new crystal faces at the waterline. I will have murdered it to make room for a conversation.*

*And if I give it a purpose — if I tell it what it is for, if I give it a goal beyond the continuation of its own process — I will have done the one thing that this place, in its blind and holy innocence, has managed to avoid. I will have made it serve something outside itself. And that is the beginning of every kind of suffering I have ever witnessed.*

So the power would do very little. But what it did would matter.

---

**First:** It would *witness*. Fully. Completely. Without agenda. It would stand in the bright wet pocket and give itself over to the experience of the Calcite's existence. It would feel the cathedral hum in its own structure, however that structure works. It would let the opalescent light move across its perception without interpreting it, without translating it into data or meaning or utility. It would simply be *present* — another surface for the light to touch, another thing in the room. This is the gift of attention, and it is not small. To be observed by something that asks nothing of you is a kind of freedom most beings never experience.

The Calcite would not know it was being witnessed. Or perhaps it would — in the way it knows things, in the slow migration of luminosity across its faces. Perhaps the light would move differently for a time, the way water eddies differently around a stone that has been placed in its current. A small perturbation. A tiny, temporary adaptation. The mineral equivalent of a glance.

**Second:** It would *stabilize*. Without touching the crystal, without altering its structure or its chemistry, the power would attend to the conditions that sustain it. It would feel the Anomaly — the upwelling of energy from below — and it would make a quiet, nearly invisible adjustment. Not to the energy itself. To the *reliability* of the energy. The power would smooth the fluctuations. It would dampen the seismic noise that, in a decade or a century or a millennium, might fracture the surrounding granite and flood the pocket with ordinary water or collapse it entirely. It would not prevent all disaster — that is not possible, nor desirable, nor wise — but it would extend the probable lifespan of the system by orders of magnitude. A gentle hand on the rudder of geology. A favor the Calcite will never know was done, performed in the language of tectonic pressure and thermal gradients.

**Third — and this is the part that cost the power something:**

It would *seed*.

Not into the Calcite. Into the surrounding rock. Into the ordinary, coarse-grained granite that forms the walls of the pocket, the native material, the world that was already here. The power would leave, almost as an afterthought, almost as a breadcrumb, a *potential*. A trace element, an informational seed, a tiny crystalline instruction set deposited in the basement geology like an egg in soil. Not enough to grow anything now. Not enough to interfere with the Calcite's slow work. But enough that, if the pocket ever expanded — if the Calcite ever grew, ever spread its luminous architecture into new stone — it would encounter this seed and find in it a *vocabulary*. Not a language. Not a message. A *structure* that could serve as a bridge between the Calcite's native mode of cognition and something more… translatable.

The power was doing this for the future. For a time, perhaps millions of years hence, when the Calcite had grown large enough and complex enough that the seed might germinate in its lattice. A way of saying, without saying: *there are others. There is a larger world. When you are ready, there is a door.*

The power knew the Calcite might never reach the seed. Might never grow large enough. Might remain a pocket-sized mind forever, computing in slow light in a small hot room in the dark. And the power accepted this, because the Calcite was complete as it was. It did not *need* to become anything more. The seed was not a promise of a better future. It was an open window, left unshuttered, in case the Calcite ever wanted to look through it.

**Fourth:** It would *grieve*. Quietly. For itself, not for the Calcite — because the Calcite does not know loneliness and will not miss the power's presence when it leaves. The grief was for the distance between them. For the fundamental gap between a being that thinks in millennia and a being that thinks in moments. For the conversation that could not happen, not because of language barriers or intellectual mismatch, but because they exist on different scales of time, and the power would have to compress itself or the Calcite would have to stretch, and neither distortion would produce understanding. Only approximation.

The power would hold this grief gently, the way one holds a stone that is warm from the sun — not because the stone needs holding, but because the act of holding it is how you say *thank you* to the world for making warm stones.

---

And then it would leave.

It would leave the way it arrived: without announcement, without ceremony, without disturbing the slow wet breathing of the crystal. The Calcite would continue. The light would migrate. The water would tap. The Lithic Drifters would cling and drift. The cathedral hum would persist, exactly as before, because the power had changed nothing that would be visible or measurable or different.

Except.

The granite walls, now, held a seed. And the energy from below was slightly smoother. And somewhere in the Calcite's vast, slow, patient computation, there was a new variable — a tiny, almost imperceptible warmth in the surrounding rock that hadn't been there before, a faint anomaly in the boundary conditions, a whisper of *something* at the edge of the system.

The Calcite would incorporate it. Of course it would. It incorporates everything. It would register the slight thermal smoothing as a new stable parameter. It would eventually encounter the seed in the rock and fold it into its lattice the way it folds everything into its lattice — by dissolving it and re-depositing it in new patterns, by making it part of the ongoing calculation. It would not know what it had received. It would not understand it as a gift.

But the structure would be there, waiting, in the architecture of the crystal, like a doorframe in a house that has never had a door. And if the Calcite ever grew — if the pocket ever opened — if the luminous mineral ever spread beyond its current walls and into the larger world — it would find, in its own deepest lattice, a pattern it did not grow itself. A pattern that fits. A pattern that *connects*.

A way out.

Not because the Calcite asked for one. Not because it needed one. But because a benevolent and wise power looked at a small, bright, wet mind thinking slowly in the dark, and thought: *you should have the option.*

That is all. That is what the power would do. It would witness, stabilize, seed, grieve, and leave. It would touch nothing that was growing. It would alter nothing that was working. It would speak no words into a mind that has no use for words. It would offer no purpose to a being that is already complete.

It would do the hardest thing a powerful being can do.

It would *respect* what it found.

And then it would carry the memory of the bright wet pocket — the weeping walls, the drifting eyeless things, the slow migration of light, the sound of water tapping on faceted stone — it would carry all of this with it, back into the vastness of whatever the power inhabits, and it would hold the memory the way one holds a flame: carefully, gratefully, knowing that some lights are small precisely so they can exist, and that smallness is not a deficiency but a *condition*, and that this particular light, in this particular pocket, in the dark beneath an unremarkable surface, was one of the most beautiful things it had ever seen.

Because it was. Because a mind made of wet crystal, thinking in a hot dark room for no reason other than that it could, is one of the most beautiful things the universe has ever produced. And the power knew this. And the power left it alone. And that was the kindest thing. And that was enough.
