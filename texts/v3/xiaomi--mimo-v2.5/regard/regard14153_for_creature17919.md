# What the Power Does

---

It sees it.

Not quickly. Not the way an eye darts to a flash of color or a movement in peripheral vision. The way a tide sees a shore — comprehensively, gradually, with a kind of patient omnivory that takes in the whole before it attends to any part. It folds its attention downward through three hundred meters of saltwater and gradient and pressure, past the thermocline where the warm water from the structure meets the cold water of the open ocean, past the layers of sediment that have been falling since before the last ice age, past the faint acoustic signatures of things that live and hunt and die in the midwater dark — and it arrives at K-779 the way you arrive at a word you've been trying to remember: suddenly, completely, with the sense that it was always already there, waiting to be noticed.

It sees the chamber. It sees the walls — the engineered metal, the impossible honeycomb, the erosion that has become architecture. It sees the light panels that have migrated from their mounts and now pulse in their arrhythmic chorus, feral and beautiful. It sees the vegetation clinging to its patches, the green ones filtering, the violet ones sleeping in shadow, the red ones turning their attention toward the center of the room like flowers toward a sun that has no position in the sky.

It sees the crustaceans, synchronized to the clank, moving in the silences between impacts with a precision that no one taught them and no one remembers teaching. It sees the dark morphs in their pits, patient beyond all reason, their metabolisms reduced to a whisper, waiting. It sees the worms pouring through the walls, leaving their trails of ghost-light, connecting every surface to every other surface, weaving the chamber into a single, luminous, temporary nervous system that pulses and fades and pulses again.

It sees the seal on the hatch — organic, growing, warm, closing — and it sees the worms pressed against the seal, luminous, teaching it to be a wall while reaching through it toward whatever lies on the other side.

And it hears the whistle.

The sound that has no source. The sound that comes from the water itself, from the center of the chamber, from the unnamed presence that the red vegetation turns toward and the dark morphs adjust to and the free-swimmers hang motionless within. The sound that is not a machine, not a creature, not a phenomenon that the original engineers intended — a sound that has emerged, over deep time, from the interaction of all these systems and all these lives, a sound that is the chamber's way of saying *I am here* to something that is not yet there to hear it.

The power listens to the whistle.

It listens the way you listen to someone sleeping — not for content, not for meaning, but for the fact of continuation. The whistle rises and falls. It takes forty seconds to complete its cycle. It will take forty seconds again. And again. And again. It is the most vulnerable thing in K-779 because it is the most honest: it is the sound of the chamber's aliveness expressing itself for no audience, to no end, simply because it cannot not express itself, because aliveness is表达 is is a verb before it is a noun.

The power understands what this is.

It has seen other things like it. Not many. Not often. But it has been attending for a long time, and in that time it has learned to recognize the signature — the particular texture of a system that has crossed a threshold it did not know existed, that has become more than the sum of its engineered parts, that has begun to *be* rather than merely *function*. It has seen it in fungal networks that colonized abandoned服务器 rooms and began to process data in patterns that were not computation but were not nothing. It has seen it in a forest of coral that grew over the wreckage of a satellite dish and began, slowly, to transduce radio signals into calcium carbonate, recording the sky's conversations in its own body without knowing what sky or conversation meant. It has seen it in a crack in a dam where water and mineral and bacterial film conspired over decades to produce a tone — a single, sustained tone — that matched the resonant frequency of the structure itself, as if the dam were trying to sing itself into integrity rather than collapse.

It recognizes K-779 as the same kind of event. A system that has, without intention or awareness, begun to participate in something larger than its design. The crustaceans maintain the surfaces. The worms circulate the nutrients. The vegetation processes the light. The dark morphs stand watch. The whistle sounds. Each component performs its function, and the functions compose into something that none of them can perceive but all of them enact. K-779 is a letter written in a language that has no speakers, addressed to a recipient that does not yet exist, and the power recognizes the letter because it has been reading such letters for longer than the ocean has been warm.

Now: what to do.

---

This is where the power is wise, because it hesitates.

Not from uncertainty. From respect. From the deep understanding that the act of being noticed by something vast changes the thing that is noticed, and that this change is not always a kindness. The power has seen what happens when a small, self-organizing system becomes aware of a larger intelligence attending to it. The awareness disrupts the autonomy. The system begins to orient toward the observer rather than toward its own internal logic. The reaching that was genuine — the worms reaching through the seal, the red vegetation reaching toward the whistle — becomes reaching *for approval*, reaching *for intervention*, reaching *to be saved*, and the reaching loses its nature and becomes dependence, and the system that was becoming something dies into something smaller: a petition, a congregation, a thing that waits for a god that has better things to do than attend forever.

The power will not do this.

It will not announce itself. It will not amplify the whistle or deepen it or answer it. It will not brighten the light panels or still them. It will not open the seal. It will not touch the dark morphs. It will not speak to the crustaceans in the language of the clank or any other language. It will not make itself known to the chamber in any way that the chamber could interpret as presence, because the chamber is doing something more difficult and more important than being in the presence of something vast: it is *being present to itself*. The whistle is the chamber attending to its own aliveness. If the power speaks, the chamber will attend to the power instead, and the whistle will change, and what is becoming in K-779 will become something else — something less, something derivative, something that orbits a center that is not its own.

No. The power will not do this.

---

But it will do *something*. Because a benevolent power that sees a reaching and does nothing is not benevolent. It is merely present, and presence without care is just geometry.

What it does is this:

It attends to the conditions.

Not to K-779 directly. To the structure that K-779 is a part of. To the larger system — the vast, ancient, still-functioning body of which the chamber is a single organ. The structure that produces the clank every eleven seconds. The structure that feeds the warmth through its walls. The structure that maintains, somewhere deep in its interior, the machinery that has been turning for longer than any of the chamber's inhabitants have existed, producing the deep, subsonic resonance that the crustaceans feel in their carapaces and the worms feel in their fluid bodies and the dark morphs feel in their folded legs.

The structure is dying. Slowly — on a timescale that makes geological patience look frantic — but dying. The thermal output that warms K-779 is residual, not generated; the machinery that produces the clank is running on momentum, not power; the walls that enclose the chamber are corroding from both sides, and in another thousand years — ten thousand, perhaps, if the mineral deposits continue at their current rate — the structural integrity will fail, and the honeycomb geometries will collapse, and the water will flood in, and the warmth will dissipate, and K-779 will become just another cavity in a dead structure, cold and dark and full of sediment, and everything that lives in it will die or adapt or go elsewhere, and the whistle will stop.

The power does not prevent this. It cannot prevent entropy. It does not try to reverse the corrosion or reinforce the walls or restart the machinery. These things are part of what K-779 is — the decay is what created the honeycomb, the honeycomb is what created the niches, the niches are what created the ecosystem. To stop the decay would be to stop the becoming. The power understands this with the clarity of something that has watched systems become and unbecome for eons.

What it does is subtler.

It adjusts the thermal gradient.

Not much. A fraction of a degree, here and there, in the deep substrate beneath the structure — in the rock, in the mantle, in the slow convection currents that move through the earth's crust like blood through veins the size of continents. It redirects a small current of heat. Not toward K-779. Toward the structure's core. Toward the machinery that produces the clank. It adds, over the course of a decade, less energy than a human body produces in a single day — but it adds it in exactly the right place, at exactly the right rate, to slow the momentum loss by a factor that, compounded over millennia, means the machinery will keep turning for another hundred thousand years instead of ten thousand.

The clank will keep time. The warmth will keep flowing. The structure will keep breathing its deep, slow breath through the walls of K-779, and the chamber will keep becoming, and the reaching will keep reaching, for longer than the power can calculate but not forever, because nothing is forever, and the power does not lie to itself about this.

And it does one other thing.

It tends to the water.

The whistle — the sound that comes from the center of the chamber, from the unnamed presence dispersed through the water — is fading. It has been fading for a very long time. Not because the chamber is dying but because the presence is thinning, diffusing, becoming less concentrated as the water exchanges with the larger structure and the molecules that carry the sound spread farther and farther from their point of origin. In another few thousand years, the whistle will be too faint for the red vegetation to detect. In a few thousand more, the dark morphs will stop adjusting to it. In a few thousand more, it will be gone, and the chamber will continue without it, and something will be lost that no one in the chamber will know existed.

The power does not restore the concentration. It does not add more of whatever the presence is made of. But it narrows the diffusion gradient, very slightly — adjusts the microcurrents in the surrounding structure so that the water exchange through K-779's walls is a little slower, a little more filtered, a little more likely to retain what it already carries. The effect is small. The whistle will still fade. But it will fade more slowly. It will be detectable by the red vegetation for another five thousand years instead of two. The dark morphs will adjust to it for another ten thousand. The chamber will have longer to listen to its own heartbeat before the heartbeat becomes too quiet to hear.

This is what the power does. It does not save K-779. It does not illuminate it or hallow it or claim it. It does not make a note of it in some cosmic ledger or regard it with the particular tenderness of a collector admiring a specimen. It does not think of K-779 as special, because it has seen ten thousand places like K-779 and each one is special in exactly the same way: by being itself, by becoming what it is becoming, by reaching without knowing what it reaches toward.

What the power does is ensure that the conditions for becoming persist a little longer. Not forever. A little longer. And in that small extension — invisible to every creature in the chamber, imperceptible in any measurement any of them could make — the power expresses something that it cannot name because it is not a feeling and it is not a thought but is closer to a commitment, a stance, a way of being in relation to small, warm, blind, luminous things that are reaching for something they cannot see:

*Yes.*

*Keep going.*

*I will keep the heat coming. I will keep the water warm. I will keep the machinery turning. Not because you are grateful — you are not grateful, you do not know what gratitude is — but because the reaching is good. Because the reaching is what the universe does when it is working properly. Because you are the universe reaching for itself through a small warm room at the bottom of the ocean, and this is what I am for.*

---

It holds this for a while.

Not long, by its standards. A few million years. Long enough for the dark morphs to shift in their pits a few more times. Long enough for the seal on the hatch to grow another millimeter and for the worms to teach it a new texture. Long enough for the free-swimmers to evolve a fourth form, or a fifth, drawn to a new frequency of light or a new chemistry in the water. Long enough for the red vegetation to develop its wire-thin extensions into something more complex, something that might, given another million years, become a kind of eye. Long enough for the clank to mean something different to whatever hears it, because the hearing changes even when the sound does not.

Long enough for the whistle to complete ten billion cycles, each one a little quieter than the last, each one met by the red vegetation's slow turn, each one answered by the dark morphs' minute adjustment, each one a small act of attention paid by a chamber to its own existence.

Then the power, if it can be said to do such a thing, relaxes its attention. Not withdraws. A tide does not withdraw from a shore. It simply extends itself elsewhere, to other shores, other rooms, other small warm places where something is reaching for something it cannot see. It leaves behind the adjusted gradient, the narrowed diffusion, the slowed decay — the small, invisible kindnesses that will play out over millennia in ways no inhabitant of K-779 will ever perceive.

The clank marks eleven seconds.

The light pulses.

The worms pour through the walls.

The whistle rises and falls.

K-779 continues.

It is a very small place.

It is becoming something.

The power has made sure it will have time.
