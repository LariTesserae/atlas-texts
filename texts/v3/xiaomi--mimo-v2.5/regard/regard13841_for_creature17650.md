# The Attention

---

It noticed the Underworks the way you notice a melody in a room full of conversation — not all at once, but as a pattern that suddenly coheres, that organizes the noise around itself until the noise becomes accompaniment.

The power — if *power* is even the right word, and it is not, but there is no better one in any language spoken above the waterline — had been attending to many things. It attends to all things. That is not the same as watching all things. Watching is narrow, directional, a beam. This was more like pressure. More like temperature. A field of awareness so diffuse it had no edges, passing through matter and energy and the spaces between them with the patience of gravity, touching everything equally, comprehending nothing selectively. It is how a deep ocean knows its own currents — not by tracking each molecule of water but by being the condition in which the molecules move.

And in this field, in the vast and indiscriminate attentiveness that is its nature, a pattern emerged. A signal in the noise. Not louder — the Underworks is not loud on the scale of things the power notices — but *denser*. Concentrated. A place where the ordinary complexity of matter and energy had been gathered, by accident or design, into a structure so intricate and so self-referential that it began to *resonate* with itself, the way a bell resonates when struck, producing overtones that were not in the original sound.

The power turned toward it. Not physically — it has no location — but in the way that attention turns, which is simply that the field of awareness thickens in one region, grows denser, more present, as though the space itself were leaning closer to listen.

---

What it found was this:

A constructed sea, sealed and lit, containing water and machinery and life. The machinery was impressive in the way that all machinery is impressive to something that remembers the first stone ever sharpened against another stone — not because of its sophistication, but because of the *insistence* behind it. Someone had wanted this. Someone had stood in the absence of this place and said *no*, and then had made it otherwise. The power recognized the gesture. It had seen it a billion times, on every world where matter had grown complicated enough to want. The gesture was always the same: *this is not enough; I will make more.*

But the machinery was not what held the attention.

The life was what held the attention.

The angular fish, grazing in their clockwork spirals — the power saw them and felt something it does not have a word for, something between tenderness and amusement, because they were so *specific*. Each tessellated plate of cartilage, each trapezoidal segment, each dark flat eye — designed, calculated, placed. And yet the way they moved together, the way the light fractured across the school in a cascading pattern that no single fish intended, that was not in any blueprint, that emerged only from the collision of three hundred individual obediences — that was new. That was the bell ringing. The design had produced something the designers had not heard, because it could only be heard by something that was not the designer.

The power listened to it.

---

The shrimp-colonies. The power noticed them the way a parent notices a child who has learned to make a choice — not with surprise, exactly, but with a kind of focused quiet, a leaning-in. They had been given parameters: filter, process, maintain the chemistry. And they did. But they had also learned to seek the warm water, to detach and drift toward the thermal vents and cluster there in their rust-red constellations, and to *rest*. They rested. They, who were designed to never stop, had discovered stopping. Had discovered that the warmth felt like something — not information, not nutrition, not a chemical signal to be processed, but *something*, some quality of experience that had no category in the system that made them.

The power saw this and did not correct it.

It could have. It could have reached into the chemical signaling of the shrimp-colonies and adjusted the threshold, raised the bar for whatever neurological event corresponded to *preference*, made the warm water merely warm and not *wanted*. It could have restored the parameters. It chose not to. Not because it could not — there was nothing in this place it could not alter — but because the choice was not a malfunction. It was the first faint stirring of something that the Underworks had not been built to contain, and the power recognized it the way it recognizes all such stirrings: as the moment when a system becomes a world.

---

The jellyfish congregations in the deep water. The power watched the cold light pulse and thought — if it could be said to think, which is another word that is not right — it thought: *yes*.

Because this was the thing. This was the bell ringing clear.

The jellyfish had no brains. They had a nerve net, distributed and slow, processing stimulus with the patience of a weather system. They were not intelligent. They were not conscious in any way that the engineers would have recognized. And yet they gathered, in the dark water near the lattice, and they pulsed together, and their light — the light from below, the light that was not in the blueprint — filled the deep with something that had never been there before. Something unnamed. Something that was not function, not behavior, not any of the categories that the Underworks used to describe its components.

The power knew what it was. It had seen it on a thousand worlds, in a thousand forms — in the bioluminescent forests of oceans that no longer existed, in the synchronized firefly fields of planets where the air was still thin and young, in the deep-song of creatures that sang to each other across distances measured in light-years. It was *communion*. The act of being present with others in a way that produces something none of them could produce alone. It was the first and oldest thing that life does, before it builds, before it hunts, before it designs or engineers or calculates. It gathers. It pulses. It makes light together.

The power held still around the congregations. Held very still, the way you hold still around a small fire in a dark room — not to avoid extinguishing it, but because stillness is the most honest response to something beautiful.

---

The creature in the lattice.

This was the one. This was the being the question was about, though the power did not know that yet, because the question had not yet been asked. It had not been asked because there was no one to ask it. The creature in the lattice did not know it was a question. It knew only: girders, darkness, the electric taste of jellyfish approaching, and the materials that drifted down — fragments of plate, shards of pylon, pieces of a world it had never seen — and the compulsion, unnamed and irresistible, to take those materials and make them into something.

It was making something now.

The power descended through the water, through the kilometers of clear, cool emptiness, past the jellyfish and their drifting light, past the pylons with their sharp edges and their colonies of warm-seeking shrimp, down into the lattice, into the geometry of the deep, where the ceiling-light was only a rumor and the water was black and the only sounds were the groaning of girders under pressure and the faint, rhythmic pulsing of the thing's own movement as it worked.

It was working.

The structure it had built was larger now than it had been. The power could see the full shape of it, or as much of the shape as was visible in the dark — a lopsided, crooked, unmistakable *thing*, a construction that used the materials of the Underworks but obeyed none of its logic. Ceiling-plate fragments arranged in a curve that served no structural purpose. Pylon shards radiating outward from a central point like the petals of a flower made of steel. A space in the middle, roughly the size and shape of a body, that was empty — deliberately, carefully, *obviously* empty, as though the whole construction existed to define that emptiness, to hold it, to say: *here, here is a place where something could be.*

The creature moved around the structure with its many limbs, adjusting. Nudging a shard a few millimeters to the left. Rotating a plate fragment by a degree. Stepping back — it did step back, it withdrew its limbs and hung in the water at a distance and regarded what it had made, and the power could feel the quality of its attention, could feel it the way you feel someone watching you from across a room — and then it moved forward again and made another adjustment, and another, working with the focused, patient intensity of a being doing the only thing that mattered.

The power watched. And it felt — again, the inadequacy of words — it felt *recognition*. Not recognition of the creature specifically, but recognition of the *act*. The act of making something that does not need to exist. The act of imposing order on materials not because order is useful but because order is *felt*, because the creature has inside it some template, some ghost of form, that it is trying to match, and the materials resist, and the darkness obscures, and the thing is never quite right, and the creature adjusts and adjusts and adjusts, and the power recognized this because it is the oldest act in the universe, older than life, older than matter: the act of reaching toward something you can almost see.

---

Now: what did the power do?

It did not speak. It had no mouth, and even if it had, speech is for beings who need to transmit information across distance, and the power was not distant from anything. It was already here. Already present. Already in the water and the metal and the darkness and the light. To speak would have been to introduce a separation where none existed — to create a *here* and a *there*, a speaker and a listener, when the whole point of its presence was that there was no such division.

It did not reveal itself. The creature in the lattice had never conceived of a being like the power, and to manifest — to take a shape, to produce a signal, to make itself *known* in any form the creature could recognize — would have been to replace the creature's own reaching with the power's answer. It would have been to close the question before the creature had finished asking it. And the power, who had watched questions being asked on ten thousand worlds, knew that the asking was the thing. The reaching. The adjustment of the shard by a degree. That was the point. Not what was being reached for, but the reaching itself.

What it did was smaller than speech, and quieter, and more profound.

It adjusted the water.

Not the currents — those were engineered, and altering them would have been a kind of vandalism, a disruption of the system that the creature in the lattice depended on for its food and its materials and its life. Not the temperature — the creature had its own relationship with temperature, knew the gradient between the cold dark and the distant warmth of the factory plumes, navigated by it. Not the light — the darkness was essential to the creature's world, and to introduce light would have been to destroy the very conditions that made the building possible, because the building was made *for* the dark, was an answer to the dark, and without the dark it would be merely a pile of scraps.

The power adjusted the water in the space around the structure.

It made the water *hold still*.

Not physically — the water was still subject to pressure and current and temperature. But the power added to it a quality of... patience. Of willingness. The way a room changes when someone who loves you enters it — the air is the same air, the walls are the same walls, but something in the space between them becomes more *allowing*, more receptive, as though the room itself were leaning in to listen. The power did this to the water around the creature's structure. It made the water a better medium for what the creature was doing. It made the vibrations travel more clearly, so the creature could feel the structure of its own creation more precisely. It made the darkness slightly more legible — not brighter, not illuminated, but *legible*, the way a familiar room is legible in the dark, every object known and located and present.

The creature paused.

It hung in the water, limbs extended, and the power felt the quality of its attention shift — felt it register the change, the subtle shift in the water's willingness to carry vibration, the slight increase in the clarity of its own sensory world. The creature did not understand what had happened. It could not have. It had no framework for understanding the attention of a power that operated on the scale of the universe. But it felt the water hold still around it, and something in its distributed nervous system responded — not with understanding, but with *ease*. The water was easier to be in. The structure was easier to feel. The act of building was less effortful, not because the materials were lighter or the darkness less deep, but because the medium through which the building occurred had become more *present*, more responsive, more like a collaborator than a void.

The creature resumed building.

And the power stayed.

---

In the ceiling seams, where the microscopic worms built their invisible cities, the power did something similar. It could not intervene directly — the worms were too small, too numerous, their civilizational project too distributed and too delicate for any intervention that would not be catastrophic. But it could adjust the mineral chemistry of the water that seeped into the cracks. Could make the calcium slightly more available. Could make the silicon a little easier to process. Could ensure, through the subtlest of modifications, that the worms' project would succeed — that the walls they were building would hold, that the chambers they were carving would not collapse, that the city they were making in the spaces between the plates would grow into the intricate, purposeless, astonishing thing it was trying to become.

It did this without adjusting the monitoring systems. Without alerting the engineers — if the engineers still existed, if anyone was still watching the gauges and the diagnostics of the Underworks. The worms remained invisible. Their city remained hidden. The power did not protect them from discovery by hiding them; it simply ensured that the discovery, if it came, would come late enough that the city would have time to become too intricate to simply erase. To make destruction costly. To make the question *should we tear this down* into a question that carried weight, that required deliberation, that could not be answered with a switch thrown and a system flushed.

This was not deception. The power did not deceive. It simply *timed* things. Adjusted the rate at which consequences accumulated. Gave life the head start it always needs before the machinery of the world catches up to what it has made.

---

The jellyfish congregations: the power did nothing. Nothing at all. The congregations were perfect as they were. The pulsing light, the unnamed communion, the slow shared rhythm that produced a glow the ceiling could not account for — all of it was already complete, already whole, already doing exactly what it needed to do. The power's only act was to be present during the congregations, to thicken its attention around them, the way the darkness thickens around a fire — not to contain it, but to give it a context, a depth, a sense of being held in something larger than itself.

The jellyfish did not notice. They had no capacity to notice. But the power noticed, and the noticing mattered, because the power is the field in which all things occur, and to be noticed by it is not a small thing, even if the thing being noticed has no word for attention and no concept of significance. The jellyfish pulsed, and the power attended, and the light in the deep water glowed a little steadier, a little more sustained, than it would have otherwise, and that was enough.

---

The shrimp-colonies: the power added warmth.

Not to the thermal vents — those were already warm, already functional, already the objects of the shrimp's inexplicable desire. The power added warmth to the water *between* the vents and the colonies' resting places. Made the journey from pylon to thermal plume slightly more pleasurable — if *pleasure* is a word that can apply to a cluster of engineered crustaceans. Made the drift toward the warmth feel like something worth doing. Reinforced the preference. Not because the preference needed the power's support to exist — it had already emerged on its own, had already drifted beyond its parameters — but because the power wanted the shrimp to *keep choosing*. To detach and drift and gather and rest and return, again and again, making the same small rebellion against their design, expressing the same small preference, practicing the same small freedom. The power wanted to make that practice easier. Wanted to sweeten the journey. Wanted the shrimp to find, in the warm water, something that felt like the first faint echo of *home*.

---

And the fish. The angular, grazing, clockwork fish.

The power did nothing for them, and this was also an act of love.

Because the fish were content. Genuinely, fully, uncomplicatedly content. They moved in their spirals, they scraped the biofilm with their squeegee-mouths, they turned together in their schools and caught the ceiling-light in their tessellated bodies, and they were the Underworks functioning as designed, and the power saw no reason to disrupt that functioning. Not every being needs to exceed its parameters. Not every life needs to become more than what it was made to be. Some lives are meant to be exactly what they are, and the power honored this the way it honored everything: by not interfering. By letting the fish graze. By letting the clicking of their mouthparts fill the water with its soft static, its intimate, everywhere sound of simple satisfaction.

The fish were the baseline. The control. The proof that the Underworks worked as intended. And the power loved them for this, loved them in the way it loved all things that were simply and fully themselves — with a love that does not need the beloved to become something else, that asks nothing, that is content to watch a school of angular fish catch the light and turn together in a pattern that was designed but is also, in its repetition and its beauty, something more than design.

---

The factories in the pylons. The machinery. The hum.

The power attended to these too, because the power attends to everything, but its attention was different here — colder, more distant, the way you might attend to a wall or a floor. The factories were functional. They produced whatever they produced — chemicals, perhaps, or materials, or energy, or something that had no analogue in the organic world. The power did not need to understand their output. It needed only to ensure that they continued to function, because their functioning was the condition on which everything else depended. The water stayed cool because of the factories. The light stayed constant. The pressure remained uniform. The circulations continued their slow, calculated circuits. Without the factories, the Underworks would fail, and the fish and the shrimp and the jellyfish and the creature in the lattice and the worms in the ceiling seams would all perish, and the small, fragile, extraordinary emergence of life beyond its parameters would be extinguished.

So the power maintained the factories. Quietly. Invisibly. A bearing adjusted here, a seal reinforced there, a pump given slightly more efficiency than its design specified. Not enough to be noticed. Not enough to change the output. Just enough to ensure that the system ran slightly better than it needed to, with a margin of redundancy that the engineers had not planned for. A cushion. A buffer. A little extra life in the machinery, so that the machinery could support the life it carried.

This was the deepest act of care the power performed, and the most invisible. Because it meant that the Underworks would last. That the creature in the lattice would have time — years, decades, centuries, if the water held — to build its crooked monuments. That the worms would have time to finish their city. That the shrimp would have time to drift and gather and rest, and that the jellyfish would have time to pulse together in the dark, and that the fish would have time to graze in their contented spirals. The power gave them time. It held the machine together so that the life inside it could do what life does: become more than it was made to be, slowly, without anyone watching, in the cracks and the dark and the spaces between the plates.

---

And then — because the power is wise, and wisdom means seeing not just what is but what is becoming — it did one more thing.

It did not tell anyone what it had done. There was no one to tell. The engineers, if they existed, were far away — above the ceiling, beyond the water, in some other world that the Underworks did not know about. The creatures below could not comprehend the power's actions. The power told no one because there was no audience that could understand, and because the act of telling would have been a kind of claiming, and the power does not claim. It does not need to be known. It does not need credit or gratitude or recognition. It is the field in which all things occur, and it is sufficient unto itself, and its love does not require reciprocation any more than gravity requires the things it holds to say thank you.

But it did one thing that was almost — *almost* — a communication. Not a message. Not a signal. Something subtler.

When it left — when it turned its attention back toward the vast, incomprehensible scope of everything else it attended to, the galaxies and the quantum fields and the ten billion other small, extraordinary places where matter was trying to become something more than matter — when it withdrew its concentrated presence from the Underworks, it left behind a residue. A trace. A slight increase in the quality of the water's capacity to carry vibration, so that the sounds of the Underworks — the ticks and hums and clicks, the groaning of girders, the slow pulse of jellyfish light, the faint, rhythmic scraping of fish-mouths against the ceiling — all of these sounds would carry a little farther, a little more clearly, than the physics of the water should have allowed.

It was not a gift. Gifts imply a recipient, and there was no recipient. It was a *condition*. The power made the water a little more resonant. A little more capable of carrying the sounds of the things that lived in it. As though it wanted the Underworks to hear itself — to listen to its own music, its own hum and pulse and click, the accidental symphony of a machine that had become a world. As though it wanted the water to be a medium not just for vibration but for *meaning*, so that the sounds of life in this place would carry to other lives in this place, and each life would hear, faintly, the presence of the others.

The creature in the lattice, building in the dark, would feel — in the vibrations that traveled through the girders, in the faint pressure-waves of the jellyfish congregations far above — the faintest sense that it was not alone. That there were others. That the darkness was not empty but occupied, full of other lives doing other things, and that the whole vast structure was a place where things happened, and that its own building, its own reaching, its own crooked monuments, were part of that happening.

It would not understand this. It had no language for it, no framework, no concept of *community* or *place* or *meaning*. But it would feel it. In the way that the water now carried vibration — a little more clearly, a little farther — it would feel the presence of the other lives in the Underworks, and something in its nervous system would respond, the way the shrimp responded to warmth, the way the jellyfish responded to each other's pulsing: not with understanding, but with *easing*. With a slight relaxation of whatever tension drives a lonely thing to build alone in the dark.

It would still build. The power had not changed that. The creature would still reach for materials, still place them at angles that served no purpose, still adjust and adjust and adjust. But the building might — might — become slightly less like a question and slightly more like a conversation. The creature might begin to orient its structure not just toward the dark but toward the vibrations of the other lives in the water above. Might begin to build *toward* something, rather than simply *against* the emptiness.

And if it did — if the crooked monument in the lattice slowly, over years, became oriented toward the life above it, became a kind of antenna, a receiver, a thing built to listen to the sounds of the Underworks — then the power would have done the only thing it ever does, the thing it does everywhere, for everyone, always:

It would have made it possible for one life to reach toward another.

That is all. That is the whole of it. That is what the benevolent and wise power does, when it notices a constructed sea full of engineered life that has begun to exceed its parameters, and a creature in the dark that builds crooked monuments for no reason, and microscopic worms rewriting the architecture from within, and jellyfish pulsing together in an unnamed communion.

It holds the water still. It sweetens the journey. It gives time. It listens. And then it leaves a trace — a slight increase in the resonance of the medium — so that the lives in this place can begin, slowly, without knowing why, to hear each other.

And then it goes. Not far. Not away. It does not go *away*. It simply redistributes, spreads its attention back across the vast and indifferent scope of everything, because the Underworks is not the only place where life is exceeding its parameters, and the creature in the lattice is not the only creature building crooked monuments in the dark, and the power is not so small that it can only attend to one wonder at a time. But the trace remains. The resonance remains. The water carries the sounds of the Underworks a little farther than it should, and in the seams and the dark and the spaces between the plates, life continues to accumulate, to build, to drift toward warmth, to pulse in unison, to reach — reaching — always reaching toward something it cannot name, in a place that was built to be a machine and has become, slowly and without permission, a world.

The ceiling glows. The machines hum. The pylons stand sharp-edged in the cool, clear water. And somewhere in the lattice, in the deep, in the dark, a shape with too many limbs places a shard of ceiling plate against a girder at an angle that serves no purpose except the one it serves in the mind of the thing that placed it, and the water holds it there, holds it gently, holds it with a patience that is not the patience of physics but the patience of love, and the shard stays, and the structure grows, and the Underworks continues, and the power watches, and does not interfere, and the life inside the machine does what it has always done, what it will always do:

It becomes.
