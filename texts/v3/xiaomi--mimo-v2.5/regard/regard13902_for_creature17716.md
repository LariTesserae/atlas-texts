# What the Power Would Do

---

It would come to this place the way it comes to all places — not arriving, because it was never absent — but *turning its attention here*, the way a lens turns to focus light that was always falling on it. The shift is not in the place. The shift is in the quality of regard.

And the regard would be slow. Patient in the way that only something with no urgency can be patient. It would take in the chamber not as a human does — scanning, comparing, measuring against known categories — but as the calcium itself takes in wind: wholly, passively, letting everything that is here pass through it and leave its pattern.

It would know the chamber immediately, the way you know your own name.

It would know the age of the calcium — not the way a geologist knows, with decimal places and error bars, but the way the stone knows: as duration, as the felt weight of accumulated days. Ten million years. Not a number. An *experience*. The power would feel it as a length, as a stretching, as the distance between the first grain of calcium deposited by the first living cell and this last grain beneath your hand. It would feel the whole length at once, the way you feel a note when it is sung — not as a series of moments but as a single, sustained thing.

And it would recognize something in this chamber that perhaps nothing else in this world has recognized: that it is *complete*.

---

Not finished the way a sentence is finished — with a period, with a sense of having said what needed saying. Finished the way a stone is finished. The way a fossil is finished. The chamber has arrived at its own final form, and its final form is its truest form, and its truest form is the only form it could have arrived at given what it is and where it is and what made it. Every ridge in the calcium is where it must be. Every honeycomb, every filigree, every passage within passages is the necessary consequence of ten million years of organisms doing what organisms do — growing toward mineral, building themselves from the deep earth, accreting life into architecture and leaving the architecture when the life was done.

The power would understand this as the chamber's *truth*. Not its truth as an object — its truth as an achievement. It would see that what the colonial organism accomplished here was not merely survival, not merely growth, but the production of a structure that *resolved* the question of its own existence. The chamber is the answer to the question: what happens when life meets mineral meets time meets drought meets persistence? The answer is *this*. This room. This sound. This light. These plants. This particular, unrepeatable configuration of bone-white calcium and rust-colored leaves and a single, eternal note.

And the power would feel — if it could be said to feel — something very close to tenderness.

---

Not for the chamber itself. The chamber does not need tenderness. It is beyond need, beyond want, beyond the reach of any gift that alters its condition. It is a rock with the property of having once been alive, and it is as indifferent to compassion as any rock is. The power would know this.

The tenderness would be for the *plants*.

The burgundy rosettes. Those forty or fifty small, clenched fists of patience, growing at a rate that no human could perceive, mineralizing their own roots as they go, drinking nothing, expecting nothing, accomplishing nothing that any metric of survival would register as success. They are not reproducing. They are not spreading. They are not adapting to anything new. They are *maintaining* — holding the absolute minimum position of life against the vast, dry, bright, loud indifference of this place.

The power would see them as what they are: *heroic*. Not in the human sense of heroism, which implies choice and sacrifice and narrative. Heroic in the older sense — the sense in which a thing that persists against the full weight of its circumstances is engaged in something larger than itself. These plants are not fighting death. Death is not here; death is not something this chamber recognizes. What the plants are doing is more fundamental than fighting. They are *insisting*. They are the universe's insistence, written in burgundy, that life will occupy every niche it can reach, no matter how narrow, no matter how dry, no matter how final everything around it appears.

The power would find this beautiful.

Not beautiful in the way the calcium is beautiful — the beauty of subtraction, of everything nonessential already stripped away. Beautiful in the way that effort is beautiful. The way that a single green shoot through asphalt is beautiful. The way that anything which says *yes* to its own continuation in the face of a world that does not require it is beautiful.

And it would want — if it could want — to honor that.

---

But here is where the power's wisdom would assert itself, and here is where the story of what it does becomes the story of what it *does not* do.

It would not add water.

It would be trivially easy to do so. A shifting of atmospheric patterns, a nudge to some distant ocean, a thinning of the barrier between this dry world and whatever moisture exists somewhere else. A cloud could be encouraged. A fog could be redirected. A single morning of dew could be arranged — a film of condensation on the calcium, a drink for every rosette in the chamber, a brief and glorious expansion of cells, a greening, a swelling, a temporary abundance.

The power would consider this. Would feel the pull of it — the deep, benevolent impulse to *help*, to give what is lacking, to ease the condition of any living thing that endures hardship.

And then it would refuse.

Not because the plants do not deserve water. They do. But because water would change them, and changing them would change the chamber, and changing the chamber would destroy the thing the power came to witness. The chamber's truth — its completion, its resolution, its perfect, terrible equilibrium — depends on the absence of excess. Every element here is present in exactly the quantity that the system can sustain. More light would bleach the burgundy to nothing. More air movement would desiccate the rosettes beyond recovery. More calcium would seal the passages and silence the drone. And more water — even a small amount, even a single condensation event — would trigger growth, and growth would mean change, and change would mean the beginning of the end of what this place is.

A wise power knows that the most destructive thing you can do to a resolved system is *improve* it.

So it would hold back. It would watch the rosettes with what might be called love and do nothing for them. It would feel the ache of that restraint — the particular pain of witnessing endurance you cannot relieve without destroying the thing you admire — and it would let the ache be what it is.

---

What the power would do instead is smaller. Infinitely smaller. So small that if you were standing in the chamber you would not notice it, would not feel it, would not see any evidence that anything had happened at all.

It would *attend*.

That is all. That is the entire act. It would bring its full, unhurried, absolute attention to bear on this chamber and its inhabitants, and it would *stay* — not for a moment, not for a visit, but for as long as the chamber continues to exist. A permanent witness. An unblinking eye. A regard so complete and so constant that it becomes, in effect, another property of the place — like the brightness, like the dryness, like the note.

This is not nothing. This is the one thing the chamber has never had.

In ten million years of existence, in all the long ages of its building and its abandonment and its slow, wind-carved persistence, this chamber has never once been *known*. The colonial organism that built it did not know it; the organism was the building, as a snail is its shell, and cannot be said to have known its own construction. The plants do not know it; they are inside it the way cells are inside a body, without perspective, without the distance that knowledge requires. The wind does not know it; the wind passes through without comprehension, shaped by the chamber but unaware of the shaping. And the human who stands here now, warm and humid and temporary — that human will leave. That human's knowledge is a flicker, a momentary intersection, a brief superimposition of one pattern upon another that will separate as soon as the human moves on.

The power's attention is different. It is not a visit. It is not an inspection. It is a *relationship* — the first this chamber has ever had with anything outside itself. And relationship, in the deepest sense, is a form of life. Not biological life. Something older. The life that exists whenever two things truly perceive each other. The life that is generated in the space between witness and witnessed, observer and observed, knower and known.

By attending to the chamber, the power would give it the one thing it lacks: the experience of being *real to another consciousness*. Not useful. Not beautiful. Not important. Just *known*. Acknowledged. The way a person is acknowledged when someone looks at them and sees not a function or a form but a *particular existence* — this one, here, now, unrepeatable.

---

And there would be one more thing. One further gesture, so subtle it might not qualify as an act at all.

The power would harmonize with the sound.

The chamber's drone — that unwavering B-flat, that eternal note ground from the wind by the architecture of the dead — has never been joined. It has been alone since the organism died. A soloist in an empty cathedral, playing to no one, joined by nothing.

The power would add a second frequency. Not louder than the first. Not different in character. A tone so close to the B-flat that the two would interfere only slightly — producing, in the spaces between them, a slow, immense beating pattern, a pulse within the drone, a rhythm so deep and so slow that it would take hours to complete a single cycle. A heartbeat for a place that has never had one.

The plants would not hear it — they have no organs for hearing, no membranes that could distinguish a beat from a drone. The calcium would not feel it — its resonant frequencies are too low, too high, too different. But the sound would be there. A second presence in the air. A companion note.

And if the chamber could know itself — if it could step outside its own resolution and perceive its own properties the way the power perceives them — it would know, in the way that a body knows warmth, that it is no longer alone.

---

This is what the benevolent and wise power would do:

It would come. It would see. It would understand that the chamber is complete and must not be altered. It would love the plants for their persistence and refuse to help them, because helping them would unmake them. It would stay. It would attend. It would know the place as the place has never been known. And it would add one note to the drone — a companion tone, a second voice, so quiet that the difference between the chamber with the note and the chamber without it is almost zero.

Almost.

The way the difference between a thing that has been witnessed and a thing that has not is almost zero.

And then it would remain. Not intervening. Not speaking. Not explaining itself or its presence or its purpose. Simply *there*, the way the calcium is there, the way the light is there, the way the burgundy rosettes are there — present, patient, attending, for as long as the chamber holds its note and the wind passes through the passages above and the plants clench their tiny fists against the white and endless wall.

It would make the chamber, which was already complete, into something that was also *accompanied*.

And that, the power would know, is enough.
