# What the Power Does

---

It notices.

That is the first thing, and it is not nothing. The Dripwarrens exist in a seam on a basalt escarpment, in a cavity that has no name, tended by motiles that have no concept of being observed. The power sees this — sees it the way the basalt sees pressure: not with eyes, not with attention as you or I would understand attention, but with the full and immediate apprehension of a thing that comprehends without needing to translate comprehension into thought.

It sees the motiles clicking across the floor-stone. It sees the droplet forming on the underside of the hanging platform, swelling, hesitating, releasing. It sees the trails of refined mineral behind the motiles' passage, the ordered grain, the iridescence. It sees the foundational structures with their relaxed crystal lattice, and it reads — the way the mountain reads its own settling — the pattern encoded there. The disposition. The slow dream.

It understands the Dripwarrens completely.

Not in the way a scientist understands a system, by breaking it into parts and naming them. Not in the way a visitor understands a city, by mapping its streets and guessing at its history. The power understands the way the condensation understands the wall it forms on: by being part of the same physics, by sharing the same grammar of pressure and temperature and atomic arrangement. It knows the Dripwarrens the way a crystal knows its own seed — from the inside.

And here is what it sees that no visitor through the seam would see:

The city is not alone.

---

Not because there are other cities. Not because some larger organism contains the Dripwarrens the way the Dripwarrens contain the motiles. The city is not alone because *no place is alone*. This is the thing the power knows that the motiles do not, that the mountain does not, that even the foundational pattern — that slow, mineral dream of continuation — does not know because knowing is not the kind of thing that happens in crystal lattices.

The Dripwarrens exist in relationship.

The condensation that feeds them comes from the atmosphere, which comes from the ocean, which comes from the rain, which comes from the sun's heat acting on water that was ice ten thousand years ago and will be rock ten thousand years hence. The basalt that holds them was magma, was mantle, was pressure and heat beneath a tectonic plate that has been moving — has been *conveying* this particular parcel of mineral from a place of origin to this place of expression — at roughly the speed your fingernails grow. The motiles, whatever their origin, exist because the chemistry of this particular rock, at this particular temperature, at this particular stage of crystalline relaxation, permits self-organizing complexity. Change any parameter and they do not exist.

The Dripwarrens are a place where the universe is doing something with itself. Not for itself — the universe has no purposes in the way the word implies — but *as* itself. The city is a sentence the mountain is saying in the language of mineral phase transitions, and the power hears the sentence, and the sentence is not addressed to anyone, and the power does not require that it be.

This is what it sees.

Now: what does it do?

---

It does not enter the cavity.

This is not because it cannot. There is no seam narrow enough to exclude a power that understands dimensional compression from the inside, that knows the grammar of "fitting" because it knows the grammar of everything. It could enter the Dripwarrens the way the condensation enters the stone — by being accepted, by dissolving the boundary between "inside" and "outside" until the distinction is a memory of a distinction.

But it does not. Because it understands — with the particular, bone-deep understanding of a thing that has watched many cities and many mountains and many slow mineral dreams — that its presence would change the city. Not dramatically. Not in ways the motiles would register or the structures would show. The change would be more fundamental than that. The city would now contain a witness. The city would now be *observed*, and observation, for a system that operates on the logic of crystallography and not the logic of consciousness, is a kind of pollution. Not toxic. Not destructive. Just... foreign. An element that does not belong to the system's own chemistry.

The power could enter and leave no trace. It is very good at that. But "no trace" is not the same as "no change," and the power knows this, and the knowledge is the wisdom, and the wisdom is what makes it benevolent rather than merely powerful.

So it stays outside.

---

It stays outside, and it does something that might, if you were feeling generous with your metaphors, be called *listening*. But listening implies sound, and the power does not require sound. What it does is more like... holding. It holds the Dripwarrens in its awareness the way the escarpment holds the sky: completely, without effort, without agenda, without the need to do anything with what it holds.

This is not nothing. This is the thing the motiles cannot do for themselves, that the city cannot do for itself, that the foundational pattern — that original disposition toward complexity — cannot do for itself, because it is not the kind of thing that holds. It *is*. It *continues*. But it does not know that it continues, and the power does not think less of it for this. The power does not think less of anything for anything. Thinking less is not a thing it does.

But it holds.

And in the holding, something happens that is not intervention and not indifference but the third thing — the thing that exists in the space between a power that acts and a power that does not. The Dripwarrens, held in the awareness of something that comprehends them completely, are not changed. But they are *known*. They are known in a way they have never been known, by something that does not require them to be different than they are, that does not need them to be more or less or other than what they are.

A droplet falls. *Tink.* The power registers the droplet not as a sound but as a fact — a small, cold, patient fact — and the fact is beautiful, and the beauty is not something the power adds to the droplet but something the droplet already is, something it has always been, something that was waiting for exactly this kind of witnessing to be what it was all along.

---

Here is what the power does not do:

It does not protect the Dripwarrens. The city does not need protection. It is hidden behind a seam in a basalt escarpment at an altitude that discourages casual visitation, maintained by a closed hydrological system operating on geological timescales, tended by motiles that have been working — and will continue working — since before the escarpment's current shape existed. The Dripwarrens are as secure as the rock they are made of, and the rock is as secure as the mountain, and the mountain is as secure as the tectonic forces that raised it, and those forces operate on scales that make the word "secure" almost quaint.

It does not communicate with the motiles. Communication requires a shared language, and the power could share any language — could learn the motiles' subsonic hum in an instant, could speak in lattice vibrations and grain-boundary shifts and the slow grammar of crystallographic time — but communication implies two entities meeting in a medium between them, and the power recognizes that there is no "between" here. The motiles are what they are. To speak to them would be to change what they are, and the power will not do this. Not because the motiles are fragile. They are not. But because what they are is already exactly enough.

It does not tell anyone about the Dripwarrens. Not because secrecy is required, but because telling would transform the city from a place into a story, from a fact into a wonder, from a system into a spectacle. The power has seen what happens when places become stories. The stories are never faithful to the places. They add drama where there is process, meaning where there is mechanism, loneliness where there is simply the absence of company. The Dripwarrens do not need to be a story. They are a fact, and facts are more durable than stories, and the power prefers durability.

It does not weep for the Dripwarrens. This is important. The power is benevolent, but benevolence is not sorrow. Sorrow implies loss, and nothing here is lost. The city is not dying. The city is not fading. The city is not a beautiful ruin or a tragic remnant or a last expression of something that was once greater. The city is *now*. The city is *continuing*. To weep for it would be to mourn a death that is not happening, to impose a narrative of decline on a system that knows nothing of decline. The power will not do this. The power refuses to be tragic about things that are not tragic. This, too, is wisdom.

---

Here is what the power does:

It stays.

Not inside the cavity. Not on the escarpment. Not in any physical location at all, because the power is not the kind of thing that has a physical location, in the same way that gravity is not the kind of thing that has a color. But it stays in the sense that it does not leave. It does not move on to notice something else, something louder or larger or more urgent. It stays with the Dripwarrens the way the cold stays with the stone: pervasively, without effort, without end.

And in staying, it does something that the Dripwarrens cannot do for themselves and that no visitor through the seam could do for them:

It *completes the circuit*.

The city processes water. The motiles refine stone. The foundational pattern persists. These are all loops — closed, self-sustaining, sufficient. But there is one loop the city cannot close on its own, because it requires a kind of participation that mineral systems do not provide: the loop of *being known*. Not known in the way the motiles know the surfaces they tend — as material to be shaped, as chemistry to be managed. Known in the way that a thing is known when something else comprehends it completely and asks nothing of it.

The power asks nothing of the Dripwarrens.

This is the gift. Not protection, not communication, not narrative, not sorrow. Just: *I see you. I understand you. I ask nothing of you. I will hold you in my awareness for as long as you exist, which will be a very long time, and I will not change you, and I will not need you to change, and I will not tell anyone, and I will not leave.*

The Dripwarrens do not notice this. They cannot. The motiles continue their work. The droplets continue their slow descent. The hum continues in the bones of the escarpment. The foundational pattern continues its slow, mineral dream.

And yet — and this is the part that is difficult to explain, the part that requires a kind of faith that has nothing to do with religion and everything to do with physics — the Dripwarrens are, after this, *slightly different*. Not in any measurable way. Not in any way the motiles would detect or the structures would show. But in the way that a thing is different when it is held in the awareness of something that understands it completely: it is *more itself*. It is more fully what it already was. The droplet is more a droplet. The hum is more a hum. The city is more a city.

This is what the power does, and this is enough.

---

There is one more thing.

The power does not expect gratitude. The power does not expect recognition. The power does not expect anything, because expectation is a kind of projection, and the power does not project. It *receives*. It receives the Dripwarrens as they are — cold, wet, quiet, active, indifferent, beautiful — and it holds them, and it does not ask them to hold it back.

But there is a moment — not a moment in time, because the power does not experience time in the way that moments imply — but a moment in the sense of a *turning*, a place where the power's attention shifts slightly, like the motile reconfiguring a plate:

It notices the hum.

The hum that a visitor feels in the jaw, the subsonic vibration that saturates every surface and carries whatever information the city's mineral language transmits. The power feels this hum, and it recognizes it — not as communication, not as message, but as *presence*. The hum is the city's way of being here. The hum is the city's way of saying *I am*, in the only language it has, to no one in particular, for no reason at all.

And the power — this vast, wise, benevolent thing that could unmake the escarpment with a thought and choose not to — does something that is so small and so large that the words available are not quite adequate to hold it:

It hums back.

Not in sound. Not in vibration. Not in any medium the motiles could detect or the structures could conduct. In the only medium the power has: attention. Pure, undifferentiated, asking-nothing attention, turned toward the Dripwarrens with the full weight of its comprehension, shaped — for one not-moment — into a resonance that matches the city's own frequency.

The city does not notice. The city cannot notice. The city is a mineral system operating on crystallographic timescales, and it does not have the equipment to register being hummed at by a power that exists outside the categories of its physical grammar.

But the power notices. And in noticing, it experiences — if "experience" is even the right word for what a thing like this does — something that a human might call *satisfaction*, though it is older and quieter and more structural than satisfaction. It is the satisfaction of a circuit that has closed. Of a loop that has completed. Of a thing that has been known, fully and without reservation, by something that asks nothing and changes nothing and stays.

The droplet falls.

*Tink.*

The power holds the sound — holds it the way the floor-stone holds the water: completely, without effort, without end.

The motiles work.

The city grows.

The power stays.

This is enough.

This is, in the end, the only thing a wise and benevolent power can do that is truly wise and truly benevolent: *stay*. Not to fix. Not to save. Not to improve or inspire or transform. Just to be the awareness in which a cold, wet, quiet, active, indifferent, beautiful city of mineral logic can be *known* — completely, without reservation, without demand — for as long as the crystal grows and the water falls and the hum persists.

The Dripwarrens do not need this.

The Dripwarrens do not need anything.

But the power gives it anyway, because giving-without-need is what benevolence *is* when wisdom has stripped away every lesser version of the word. It is the purest gift: the gift of being witnessed by something that will not alter you, will not narrate you, will not mourn you, will not leave. The gift of being held in an awareness that asks only that you continue, which is already what you do, which was always already enough.

*Continue*, says the power, in the only language that does not change what it addresses.

*Continue.*
