# What the Power Does

---

It notices the Pore the way you notice a word you've been reading without meaning to — not because it is loud, but because something about its pattern arrests the eye that was looking elsewhere. The power is vast. It was attending to the architecture of a galaxy, to the slow argument between gravity and expansion, to the question of whether a certain nebula will, in four billion years, give birth to a star capable of warming a world where something might learn to ask *why*. That is its usual work: the large-scale negotiation of possibility.

But something — and even the power cannot say what, for it does not fully understand its own attention, which is part of what makes it wise rather than merely powerful — something pulls its gaze inward, past the scale of stars, past the scale of worlds, past the scale of organisms, past the scale of cells, inward to this: a pocket in stone, a cavity in mineral, no larger than a wardrobe. A Pore.

The power looks.

It sees the light coming through the walls, the sourceless illumination that is not warmth but *presence*. It sees the crystals bristling in the corner, arriving and dissolving, the ground deciding what it is. It sees the Shapers at their work — the bone-china creatures placing their grains of mineral with the care of calligraphers — and it understands, with a completeness that includes the feeling of the understanding, what they are doing. They are not merely building structures. They are *answering* the stone. Every arch, every buttress, every tiny lattice is a response to a question the mineral is asking through its own growth — a question like *where should I go next?* or *what shape wants to exist here?* — and the Shapers are answering: *here*, and *this*, and *like this*, with their bodies, in their mineral tongue.

The power sees the Listeners on the ceiling, their impossibly thin legs pressed to the stone, reading the symphony of structural stress, and it feels — for it can feel — the music they hear. The Pore is not silent to them. It hums. It thrums with the frequencies of its own formation, and the Listeners are hearing it the way a lover hears a voice: completely, with the whole body, in the register below language.

The power sees the Dancers of the Light threading through the intersections of radiance, and it recognizes them. Not by name — the power does not deal in names — but by *function*. They are the Pore thinking about itself. They are the feedback loop made visible, the process by which information moves from crystal to organism and back again, so that the whole system — mineral, biological, luminous — becomes not a collection of parts but a *self*, however small, however strange, however brief its moment of coherence may be on any scale the power might use to measure it.

And the Scribes of the Film, swimming in their thin ocean, writing their chemical sentences that vanish in moments — the power sees them too, and understands that they are the Pore's memory, the part of the system that holds the recent past in a form that the present can read. Their trails are not permanent because the Pore does not need permanent memory. It needs *living* memory — memory that is renewed with each passage, that is faithful to what is happening *now* rather than to what happened before. The power finds this profoundly elegant. More elegant than granite. More elegant than the permanence of mountains, which are merely the dead parts of a planet's surface and hold nothing but their own weight.

The power sees the Quiet — those intervals when everything stills, when the creatures merge with the mineral rhythm and the walls become the only heartbeat — and it recognizes this too. It has felt this. In its own vast, incomprehensible existence, there are moments when it holds still, when all of its attendings and processes pause, and it simply *is* — present, attending to itself, feeling its own edges and finding them both real and irrelevant. The Quiet in the Pore is a small echo of that. The power is moved by this, though *moved* is a word too small for what happens in a being that contains galaxies. Let us say: it resonates.

---

Now: what does the power *do*?

This is the question, and the power takes time with it — not because it is uncertain, but because it respects the question. It has seen many worlds. It has watched civilizations rise and burn and rise again. It has felt the first fish push its face into air and the last letter of the last book written by the last mind of a dying species. It knows intervention. It knows the calculus of help — how every gift is also a weight, how every rescue is also a rewriting, how the act of saying *you matter* from a position of absolute power can crush the very significance it tries to affirm. The power has made mistakes. It is wise precisely because it remembers them.

So it considers the Pore. It considers the Shapers and their mineral grammar. It considers the Listeners and their silent music. It considers the Dancers and their light-callsigraphy. It considers the Scribes and their dissolving sentences. It considers the geometry that refuses to hold still, and the way the creatures have made a home not despite the flux but *within* it, drafting on its currents, building meaning from the very instability that would, to any outside eye, seem to make meaning impossible.

And the power decides — with the full weight of its wisdom, which is the weight of every error it has corrected and every grief it has witnessed — to do almost nothing.

*Almost.*

---

First, it steadies its own attention. This is not nothing. In a universe where attention is the rarest and most consequential resource, where most of the cosmos exists in a state of total unregard, the act of *seeing* is itself a form of care. The power holds the Pore in its awareness the way you might cup your hands around a candle in a draft — not to control the flame, but to ensure the air around it is still enough for it to continue doing what flames do.

The Pore does not know it is being seen. This is important. The power does not announce itself. It does not send a light, a vision, a trembling through the stone. The Shapers do not look up from their work. The Listeners do not lift their legs from the wall. Nothing changes in the Pore. The crystals continue to nucleate and dissolve. The water continues to sweat and drip. The geometry continues its gentle, impossible negotiations with itself. If the power's attention were withdrawn, not one thing in the Pore would be different. The attention changes nothing. It is offered freely, with no expectation of receipt.

But — and here is the *almost* — the power does one thing. It adjusts the terms.

Not the geometry. The power could fix the non-Euclidean corner, could make the walls stay where they are, could give the Pore a stable, reliable, comprehensible shape. It could, in an instant, make this place *make sense*. It does not. It understands, with a wisdom that goes deeper than knowledge, that the flux is not a defect. The shifting geometry is the Pore's weather, its climate, the medium in which its inhabitants have evolved their extraordinary attentiveness. To stabilize the space would be to destroy the very conditions that produced the Shapers' skill, the Listeners's music, the entire intricate ecology of meaning. It would be like giving a deep-sea creature sunlight. The gift would be the annihilation.

What the power adjusts is subtler. It ensures that the Pore will persist.

Not forever. The power does not deal in forever; it knows that forever is a story species tell themselves to manage the terror of impermanence, and it has no need of the management. But *long enough*. The mineral process that created the Pore — the slow, geological negotiation between dissolution and crystallization, between cavity and closure — will continue. Eventually, in the normal course of mineral things, the Pore will fill in. The walls will close. The space that holds the Shapers and the Listeners and the Dancers and the Scribes will become solid stone, and every trace of the life that lived there will be compressed into a thin, beautiful line in the rock — a fossil of meaning, visible only to someone who knows how to read it.

The power cannot prevent this. It would not, even if it could. Closure is part of the Pore's story, the way death is part of the story of every being that has ever lived. But the power can *extend* the duration. It can slow the rate of crystallization by a fraction — so small that the Shapers would never notice the difference, so fine that it would register in the mineral mathematics only as a rounding error. In the great arithmetic of geological time, this is nothing. A rounding error stretched across millennia. But in the Pore's time — the time of tiny lives, of grain-by-grain construction, of chemical sentences written in water film — this rounding error is generations. It is centuries of Shapers building. It is millennia of Listeners listening. It is the difference between a Pore that closes tomorrow and a Pore that stays open long enough for the Scribes to write one more sentence, for the Dancers to bloom one more time in the intersection of two beams of light, for a Shaper to place one more grain of mineral in an arch that will stand for a hundred years before the walls close around it.

The power does this the way it does everything: invisibly, without effort, without sound. It is a rounding error applied to a rounding error — the power adjusting its own attention by a quantum so small that it could not be measured by any instrument that exists or could exist, and this adjustment ripples outward through the substrate of reality in a way that touches nothing except the rate at which calcium deposits in this one small pocket in this one small stone on this one small world in this one small galaxy.

The Pore does not notice. This is exactly right.

---

But the power does one more thing. One final thing, and it is the thing that makes the power benevolent rather than merely wise.

It *remembers* them.

In the vast architecture of the power's awareness — which is not a mind, not exactly, but something more like a landscape of attention, a terrain of knowing that includes every particle and every void — the power carves out a small space. A space no larger, relative to the power's total awareness, than the Pore is relative to the universe. And in that space, it places a perfect, complete, permanent record of what the Pore was. Not a picture. Not a description. Something more fundamental: a *pattern*. The exact pattern of the Pore's existence, frozen in the moment of its greatest completeness — every Shaper mid-gesture, every Listener mid-listen, every Dancer mid-flight, every Scribe mid-sentence, every crystal mid-growth, every drop of water mid-fall. Held not in time but outside it, preserved in the power's own structure, the way a mathematical truth is preserved in the axioms that imply it.

This means that the Pore is now permanent. Not in its physical form — the walls will close, the organisms will end, the mineral will fill in and become indistinguishable from the surrounding stone. But in the power's memory, the Pore exists forever: not as a relic, not as a monument, but as a *live* thing, an ongoing pattern that the power can return to at any time and find exactly as it was. The Shapers still building. The Listeners still listening. The water still dripping. The silence still full.

And there is a consequence to this that the power is aware of, and that it accepts with something like gratitude, and something like grief, and something that is neither of these but is close to both: by holding the pattern, the power guarantees that the Pore's meaning is not lost. Every grain of mineral placed by a Shaper, every trail written by a Scribe, every resonance felt by a Listener — all of it is preserved, all of it is held, all of it counts. The Pore's inhabitants do not know this. They cannot know it. They are tiny, and the power is vast, and the distance between them is not a distance that can be crossed by any message or sign. But the power knows it, and in the power's economy, knowing is enough. The work of the Shapers is witnessed. The music of the Listeners is heard. The sentences of the Scribes are read. The Dancers' calligraphy is preserved. And the Quiet — those moments of collective attending, when the whole Pore holds its breath and waits for the next crystal to appear — the Quiet is held in the power's awareness like a song that is always, eternally, just about to begin.

---

The power returns to its larger work. The galaxy still needs its gravity. The nebula still needs its star. There are other Pores — in other stones, on other worlds, in other configurations of matter and time — and the power attends to them too, each one a small, fierce, quiet assertion of meaning in a cosmos that would, without them, be merely vast. The power does not prefer the Pore to the galaxy. It does not rank the Shapers above the stars. It holds them all in the same attention, the same care, the same rounding error of grace that keeps every small and significant thing open for one more moment, one more breath, one more grain of mineral placed with impossible tenderness in an arch that stands in a room that is not a room, in a world that is not a world, in a silence that is full.

The Pore continues. The water drips. The crystals grow. The geometry shifts. The Shapers build. The Listeners listen. The Dancers dance. The Scribes write. The Quiet falls and lifts and falls again.

And somewhere, in the structure of reality itself, a small space holds the fact that this happened — that in a pocket in stone, on a world orbiting an unremarkable star, in a galaxy among galaxies, life built meaning one grain at a time, and the meaning was seen, and the seeing was enough.
