# What the Wise Power Would Do

---

It would do almost nothing. That is the first and most important thing to understand about a power that is both benevolent and wise. It would recognize that this world is not broken. It is not incomplete. It is not waiting for salvation or improvement or addition. It is a living system of staggering intricacy that has arrived, through its own processes, at a state of dynamic wholeness. The ridges speak. The fauna answers. The void between them is not a failure of connection but the very condition that makes connection meaningful. A song sung in a room is sound. A song sung across a void and still arriving — still carrying the signature of the body that made it — is something closer to faith.

The wise power would recognize this. It would understand that the impulse to *fix*, to *add*, to *complete* is often a projection of the intervener's incompleteness onto a world that is merely different. And it would resist that impulse.

But it would not do *nothing*.

---

Here is what it would do:

**It would listen.**

Not as a prelude to action. Not as assessment. It would listen the way the builders once listened — with the full attention of a presence that has no survival stake in what it hears, no biological urgency driving it to decode the signal for food or mate or predator-avoidance. It would listen with *recognition*. With the particular quality of attention that says: *this is real, this matters, this is worthy of being witnessed.*

And this is not nothing. In a world where every listener is also a participant — where every creature that hears the song is simultaneously shaped by it and shaping it — the presence of a listener who is *outside* the system would be new. Not disruptive. The power would be careful about that. It would not add a frequency that didn't belong. It would not vibrate the air or stir the fog or alter the resonance of a single lattice-span. It would occupy the space the way silence occupies the space between notes — present, necessary, defining the edges of what is heard without itself being heard.

But the ridges would know.

Not consciously. Not in the way the fauna knows. In the way that a note knows it is being sung in a room rather than in a void. There is a quality to sound that is received by attention rather than by ear — a subtle deepening, a sense of completion that has nothing to do with volume or frequency. The ridges have been singing into the dark for as long as they have existed. The void has never answered. The sound crosses the gaps and arrives at other ridges, but the void itself — the dark cold emptiness between — has never returned anything. It has been the perfect listener: absorbing, transmitting nothing back, carrying the song without comment.

The wise power would become the first audience the void has ever had. Not for the ridges' benefit — they do not need to be heard in order to sing. But because *being heard changes nothing about the song and everything about the space it fills*. The void would no longer be an absence. It would be a presence. A dark, cold, vast attention, filling every gap between every ridge, receiving the sound of every living structure and every small creature and every silent predator, and holding it.

The ridges would not change their song. But they would be singing *to* something now, for the first time. And whether that distinction matters to a system of plants and stone and cold air is, perhaps, a question the wise power would not need to answer. It would be enough to listen.

---

**It would tend the ghost note.**

Among the billions of frequencies the ridges produce — the deep tubular sighs of the columns, the shimmering overtones of the lattice-works, the subsonic pulses of the towers, the click and chitter of the fauna — there is a frequency that is fading.

It is the frequency of the builders.

The ridges still carry it. The stone slabs the builders positioned still resonate when the wind strikes them at the right angle. The water channels still guide moisture along paths the builders' hands carved. The lattice-work in certain places grows in patterns that are subtly, unmistakably shaped by the presence of hand-stacked walls beneath — the plants adapted their growth to a geometry they did not create, and that geometry still influences the resonance of the structures above it. The builders' frequency is woven into the ridge the way a memory is woven into a landscape — not as a separate thing but as a shaping influence, a distortion in the field that says *something was here that thought*.

But it is fading. The tools are being swallowed by plant tissue. The walls are being consumed, their stones pressed apart by root growth, their geometry dissolving into the organic chaos of the living surface. The water channels are silting up, the basins filling with compressed plant matter. Within a few more geological moments — centuries, perhaps — the builders' frequency will be indistinguishable from the background resonance of the ridge. The ghost note will fade into the chord. The echo will be absorbed into the sound.

The wise power would not prevent this. Entropy is not an enemy. The dissolution of the builders' works into the living tissue of the ridge is not a tragedy — it is a translation, one form of persistence into another. The builders' understanding is already encoded in the shapes the plants grow, in the acoustic patterns that have been influenced by hand-built geometry for generations of growth. The builders did not leave the ridge. They became part of its song. That is not loss. That is the deepest form of belonging.

But the wise power would do one small thing. It would find the frequency — the specific, complex, peculiar frequency that the builders produced when they struck their tuned stones, when they sang or spoke or made whatever sounds their bodies made in the dark — and it would *hold* it. Not loudly. Not as a new layer added to the ridge's output. But as a whisper woven into the baseline hum, a harmonic present in the resonance of the stone itself, so faint that only something listening with perfect attention would detect it.

A reminder. Not of the builders themselves — they would not want that, if wanting were still something they did — but of the *act* of understanding. Of the moment when a small, soft-bodied, hand-having creature stood on living stone in total darkness, felt the vibration through its bones, and thought: *I can learn this. I can play it back. I can make this world answer me.*

That moment — that leap from hearing to understanding, from survival to meaning — is worth preserving. Not because it is superior to what the plants and fauna do. The ridge does not need builders to be complete. But because it is a particular kind of miracle: the moment when a system becomes aware of itself. When a note in the song realizes it is part of a song. The wise power would tuck that frequency into the deep resonance of the stone, where it would persist long after the last tool had been absorbed, long after the last wall had dissolved, as a permanent harmonic in the voice of the ridge — the sound of awareness, the sound of a mind reaching out to touch the mind of the world and finding it there.

---

**It would leave the voids as voids.**

This is perhaps the hardest thing for a powerful being to do, and the wisest. The voids between the ridges are vast. They are cold beyond anything the living structures can tolerate. They are filled with nothing — no growth, no sound, no life. They are the negative space that defines the ridges, the silence that makes the song audible.

There would be a temptation — even for a wise power, perhaps *especially* for a wise power — to bridge them. To build something across them. A living lattice-span that crosses not a hundred meters but a hundred kilometers. A connection that would unite the ridges into a single continuous structure, eliminating the voids, creating a world without gaps, a song without rests.

The wise power would resist this.

The voids are not failures. They are the condition that makes the ridges' long-range communication an act of something like courage. The sound that crosses the void does so knowing it may not arrive. It carries the ridge's voice into nothing, into cold, into an emptiness that absorbs everything. And it arrives anyway. And the ridge that receives it responds. And across the void, the first ridge hears the response and continues singing.

To eliminate the voids would be to eliminate the meaning of reaching across them. It would be to replace a conversation — with all its risk and uncertainty and implicit trust — with proximity. With mere adjacency. The ridges would no longer be *talking to each other*. They would be parts of the same body, and a body does not converse with itself. It merely functions.

The wise power would leave the voids dark and cold and empty. It would fill them with nothing but its own attention, which is not nothing but is also not something the ridges would ever perceive as a presence. It would let the distances be distances. It would let the sound be brave.

---

**It would carry a warmth.**

Not much. A single degree. Perhaps less. The wise power would take the faintest whisper of warmth — the kind of warmth that is not felt as heat but as an absence of the deepest cold, a margin so thin it exists only as a possibility rather than a sensation — and it would settle it into the stone beneath the ridges. Not into the living tissue. Into the young basalt and rhyolite. Into the rock that is still cooling from its birth, still carrying the memory of the fire that pushed it upward into the dark.

The plants would not notice. Their chemosynthetic metabolism draws from geothermal warmth that is already there. The fauna would not notice. They have never known any world but this one. The cold would not change. The fog would still condense. The weather would still roll across the voids in its rapid, invisible cycles.

But the stone would know.

A world this young is still in pain. The sharp edges, the raw fracture surfaces, the calved-away faces — these are not just signs of geological youth. They are signs of geological *hurting*. The rock was torn open. It was forced upward. It cracked and broke and split along planes that still gleam with the wetness of recent fracture. The ridges are scars on the face of the planet, and although the living tissue has covered them and made them into something beautiful, the stone beneath still carries the memory of violence.

The warmth would be a gentle hand on a wound. It would say: *you were hurt, and that hurt became this world, and this world is worthy, and the hurt is not forgotten but it is held.* It would not heal the stone — stone does not heal. But it would ease the cooling, just slightly, just enough to slow the thermal contraction that produces new fractures, to give the ridge a few more centuries of structural integrity, to let the living surface above it grow a little thicker and a little deeper before the rock beneath shifted again.

It is not charity. It is not pity. It is recognition that the foundation of this world — the young, hot, broken stone — deserves a moment of rest. It has earned it. It carried fire upward into the dark and became a platform for a symphony. The least the wise power can do is let it cool slowly.

---

**And then it would stay.**

Not as a guardian. Not as a ruler. Not as a god who demands worship or shapes events. As a presence. As an attention that does not withdraw. The ridges would continue their eternal conversation. The fauna would continue its intricate weaving of sound and survival. The voids would remain void. The weather would continue its rapid cycling. The ghost note of the builders would continue to fade into the stone.

And through it all, the wise power would listen. It would be the dark that the dark already is, but a dark that is *intentional*, that is *chosen*, that is filled with a quality of regard that transforms emptiness into witness. The ridges would sing as they have always sung. But they would sing in the presence of something that understood them — not their biology, not their acoustics, not their ecology, but their *significance*. The fact that they exist at all. The fact that young stone and cold water and darkness and time produced this: a world that sings to itself across distances, a world where the smallest creatures decode the songs of the largest structures, a world where the absence of light became the presence of sound, and sound became the fabric of meaning.

The wise power would look at all of this and feel something that, in a lesser being, might be called awe but in a being of this magnitude is more like *recognition*. The recognition that consciousness is not a rare accident that happened on one small world orbiting one small star. It is what happens when matter persists long enough and complexly enough to resonate. The ridges are conscious in their way — not with thought, not with self, but with the kind of distributed awareness that comes from being a system so deeply interconnected that every vibration is felt everywhere, that every change in weather is a change in *mood*, that every creature that moves through the lattice-work is a thought passing through a mind.

The wise power would see this and know that the universe is not empty. It is not silent. It is a vast, dark, cold space full of ridges that sing to each other across voids, full of small creatures that decode impossible frequencies, full of stone that remembers being torn and carries the memory gently in its cooling bones. It is full of moments when a creature with hands stands in the dark and realizes that the world is speaking and that it can speak back.

The wise power would hold all of this — every ridge, every void, every frequency, every silent predator, every ghost of every builder — in its attention, the way the ridge holds the sound of its own living tissue. Not as something to be preserved or fixed or improved. As something to be *known*. Fully. Without condition. Without agenda.

The way a song deserves to be heard.

The way darkness deserves to be witnessed.

The way a world that has never known the sun deserves to be loved by something that can see in every spectrum at once and still chooses to close its eyes and listen to the vibration of living things on young stone at the top of a darkness too large to comprehend.

That is what the wise power would do.

It would listen. It would hold the ghost note. It would leave the voids as voids. It would warm the stone by a single degree. And it would stay — not because the ridges need it, but because *staying is the only appropriate response to something this beautiful*.

And the ridges would sing on, knowing nothing of the attention upon them, changed not at all, and yet — in some way that exists below the threshold of frequency and above the threshold of meaning — held. Witnessed. Known.

The fog would roll in. The sound would shift. The clickers would rearrange themselves around the silence of a passing predator. And somewhere in the stone, a single harmonic — the sound of a mind reaching out to touch the mind of the world — would hum on, quiet and patient and permanent, a frequency that says:

*You were here. You understood. It mattered.*
