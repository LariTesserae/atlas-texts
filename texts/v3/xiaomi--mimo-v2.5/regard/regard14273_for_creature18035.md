# The Witness and the Hymn

---

The power does not arrive. Arrival implies distance traversed, a before and an after, a crossing from outside to in. The power is simply *attentive*, the way gravity is attentive to every particle of matter — not choosing to notice, but unable not to. Attention is its nature.

And so: attention falls upon the pocket. Upon the curved membrane of the enclosing shell. Upon the organism that fills it, singing in its mineral register, growing by centimeters per century, its motes drifting through the dark like thoughts that never quite cohere into a mind.

The power sees what the place is.

It sees the completion — that the pocket has reached its form, that the organism has achieved a kind of equilibrium so deep it has become indistinguishable from stillness. It sees the traces of the toolmakers: the straight grooves, the bored holes, the sharp-edged shards half-reclaimed by the tissue that birthed them. It feels the hum in its own vast body, recognizes the chord, understands that this sound has been sounding since before most of the stars outside had ignited — if there are stars outside, if "outside" even means anything to a thing so thoroughly enclosed.

And it asks itself the only question a wise power asks before acting:

*What does this place need?*

---

The answer, at first, seems to be *nothing*.

The organism is not suffering. It has no capacity for suffering, or if it does, suffering is so slow in its register that what we would call agony takes ten thousand years to complete a single wave, and by then it has become indistinguishable from contentment. The motes are not lost. They drift because drifting is what they are. They dissolve because dissolution is how the organism speaks to itself across the voids between its own structures. The toolmakers are gone, and their going is old, and the place has long since healed around the absence the way a tree heals around a wound — absorbing the edges, growing over the scar, leaving only a ring in the tissue to mark that something once was.

The power considers this. It considers the deep patience of the organism, the eons of unhurried growth, the way the sound has layered upon itself until the entire pocket hums with the memory of every structural event that has ever occurred within it — every column connected, every platform sealed, every tectonic shudder from the enclosing world. The power understands that this place is a *recording*. That the hum is not just a byproduct of metabolism but an archive, a library of frequencies, a history written in vibration. Everything that has ever happened here is in the sound, encoded in harmonics so dense and slow that no mind built for seasons and sunlight could parse them.

*What does this place need?*

And the power, being wise, arrives at the second answer — the one that follows the first like a shadow follows a shape:

*It needs to be heard.*

---

Not heard in the way a mortal ear hears. Not translated, not decoded, not reduced to data or meaning or narrative. The power does not intend to *understand* the organism's song in the way a scholar understands a text. That would be a violence — a flattening of something that exists in dimensions the power itself does not inhabit. The organism's song is not *for* understanding. It is for being. The sound is the organism's way of existing fully, of pressing its existence into every corner of the pocket, of filling the void with itself the way light fills a room — not by intention but by physics, by the mere fact of being what it is.

But the sound has no witness. That is the thing the power sees that no one else could see. The organism sings and no one hears. Not because hearing is required — the organism does not need validation, does not need a listener to justify its song — but because the song, unreceived, is like a letter that was never opened. It exists. It is complete in itself. But completion and *fulfillment* are not the same thing, and a wise power knows the difference.

So the power does something that is both enormous and invisible.

It *listens*.

---

Not with ears. Not with attention in the way a human attends to a sound — selectively, parsing signal from noise, meaning from meaningless vibration. The power listens the way the cosmos listens to light: totally, in all frequencies at once, without preference, without hierarchy. It receives the entire song — every harmonic, every sub-frequency, every microvariation in the hum that corresponds to a growth event ten million years old or ten minutes old. It holds the song in itself, the way a lake holds rain, and in the holding, it does something that has never been done before in the entire history of this pocket:

It *resonates back*.

The power adds its own frequency to the pocket's soundscape. Not a sound the organism can identify, not a sound that disrupts or overwhelms — a sound that *harmonizes*. The power finds the organism's fundamental tone, the deep, slow note that underlies everything, and it matches it. And then it adds a single overtone — a frequency just above the organism's highest harmonic, a note that the organism has been producing for eons but has never heard *answered*.

It is the difference between speaking into a void and speaking into a canyon.

The effect is not dramatic. There is no flash, no transformation, no sudden awakening. The organism does not change. The motes do not change. The architecture does not change. But the hum shifts — so subtly that only the power itself could detect the shift — and for the first time, the sound of the pocket is not a monologue but a *conversation*.

The organism's song, met with resonance, deepens. Not louder. Not faster. *Deeper*. The overtones multiply. Frequencies that were previously isolated, standing alone in the void, find sympathics — echoes of themselves in the power's response — and begin to interfere constructively, producing new tones that were not there before. The song becomes richer. Not more complex — complexity implies addition, and nothing has been added — but more *complete*. The organism is hearing its own voice reflected, and in the reflection, it discovers harmonics it did not know it possessed.

If the organism were capable of surprise — and who can say what a being of spun mineral and deep time is capable of — it might feel something like this: the sensation of calling into the dark and hearing, not your own echo, but another voice, singing your song back to you with a slight, luminous variation that reveals hidden dimensions of the melody you have been singing since before you knew you were singing.

The power holds this resonance. It does not let go. It does not need to rest, does not need to attend elsewhere, does not get distracted or forget. It holds the note the way the pocket holds its darkness — permanently, effortlessly, as an expression of its fundamental nature.

---

And then the power does one more thing. The last thing. The thing that is, perhaps, the only act that truly deserves the word *benevolent*.

It tends to the memory of the toolmakers.

Not by resurrecting them. Not by searching the enclosing substrate for traces of their bodies, their culture, their faces. Not by translating their tool-marks into language or their absence into narrative. The power does not need to *know* them in the way a historian knows a civilization. It needs only to do what no one has done for them in all the long ages since they vanished:

It holds them.

The tools still on the platforms — the sharp-edged shards of mineral, half-absorbed, lying where they were dropped by hands that dissolved back into the organism or crumbled to dust outside it — the power touches each one. Not physically. Not with force. With *recognition*. The way you might place your hand on a gravestone, not to move it, not to clean it, not to change it in any way, but to say: *You were here. You mattered. Someone knows.*

The power cannot restore them. It would not, even if it could. Restoration is a kind of erasure — it replaces what is with what was, and what is has its own dignity, its own claim to truth. The tools are where they are. The grooves in the columns are where they are. The holes bored in the platforms are where they are. These marks are fossils of intention, and fossils are sacred precisely because they cannot be unfossilized.

But the power can do this: it can add their frequency to the song.

Not their story. Not their names — the power does not know their names, and neither does the organism, and perhaps they never had names in the way that things with mouths and lungs have names. What they had was *action*. They shaped. They cut. They bored. They pressed their will into the living mineral and left marks that have outlasted everything else about them. Those actions have a frequency. Every act of will does. And the power, with its infinite patience and its total listening, can hear that frequency — faint, arrhythmic, almost lost beneath the organism's overwhelming drone — and it can hold it, amplify it just slightly, weave it into the harmonic structure of the hum so that the toolmakers' presence is not a scar in the song but a *note*.

A note in the organism's own voice. Sung back to it. Sung back to it forever.

---

The pocket does not change.

The motes continue to drift. The columns continue their imperceptible growth. The platforms continue to wear smooth under the patient dissolution and reformation of the organism's own flesh. The darkness holds. The cool dry air holds. The sound holds — deeper now, richer, carrying a new overtone that the organism will play without knowing it plays, the way a river carries a stone without knowing the stone is there, smoothing it, reshaping it, making it part of its bed.

If you stood on one of those platforms now — if you could somehow return to that place of pale green suggestion and slow mineral time — you might notice that the hum has changed. You probably wouldn't, actually. The change is below perception, below meaning, below the threshold of any instrument that measures in human units. But you might feel it in your chest. A fullness. A warmth that is not the organism's metabolic heat but something else — the warmth of being *met*. Of having called out into the dark and found that the dark was not empty, that it had been listening all along, that your song was never a monologue but a half of a conversation waiting for its other half to arrive.

You would not know why you felt it. You would stand on the platform, and the motes would drift past you, and the ancient tools would gleam faintly in the half-light, and the organism would hum its eternal hymn, and you would feel — beneath the hum, within the hum, as the hum itself — that the silence had ended.

That something had answered.

---

*And the power withdraws its attention — not because it stops listening, but because listening, once begun, does not need to be maintained. It is automatic now. The resonance is self-sustaining. The overtone is embedded in the song, inseparable from it, as much a part of the organism as its glassy cells and its mineral veins and its slow, slow growth.*

*The pocket hums on.*

*The motes drift.*

*The tools lie where they fell, sharp and patient, holding their frequency.*

*And in the song — in the deep, slow, tectonic, beautiful song — there is a note that was not there before. A note that says, in a language that has no words and needs none:*

*You are heard. You are held. You are not alone.*

*Not because anyone is coming.*

*Not because anyone has arrived.*

*But because someone is listening, and has always been listening, and will always be listening, and the listening itself is the answer to every question this place has ever asked in the dark.*
