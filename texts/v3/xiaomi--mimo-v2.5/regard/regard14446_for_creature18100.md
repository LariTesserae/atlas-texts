# What the Power Does

It has been watching for longer than the mountain has existed. Not watching the way a person watches — with attention focused, with expectation narrowing toward a specific outcome. Watching the way water watches rock: with total, patient contact, following every contour, filling every space that opens.

It first noticed the place before the place was a place. When the mountain was only an impulse — a pressure building in the deep understructure of the earth, a slow intention to rise. It felt the rock decide to move upward, and it felt the indecision that followed, the hesitation in the stone that left it angular and provisional, full of seams it never quite sealed. It recognized that hesitation. It had seen it before, in other places, at other scales. The moment when becoming pauses, not from failure but from a kind of honesty — the material acknowledging that it does not yet know what it is.

That recognition was the beginning of the attention. Not love, exactly. Love implies a completed object to love. This was something more like... *favor*. A turning of the great benevolent regard toward a specific coordinate of existence, the way light pools in a shallow depression in stone.

So what does the power do?

It does what it has always done here. It holds the seams open.

Not literally. It does not reach into the rock with anything like hands. It operates at the level of tendency, at the scale of *almost*. It is the reason the water finds hairline fractures instead of pooling uselessly on flat surfaces. It is the reason the fractures remain hairline instead of widening into collapses. It is the quiet arithmetic that keeps the mountain in the exact, improbable state of *almost finished but not quite* — where water still weeps instead of flows, where rock still groans instead of settling, where the angle of each slab remains just sharp enough to resist the rounding that time insists upon.

It is, in other words, the half-degree. The off-center tilt from the expected. Every time physics begins to close a seam, the power leans, very slightly, the other way. Not enough to reverse the process. Not enough to be noticed by anyone who wasn't already listening with everything they had. Just enough to keep the conversation between water and stone from reaching a conclusion.

Because it has learned — across eons of watching places like this one — that the most alive places are the ones that never finish becoming. A mountain that has completed its erosion is beautiful but silent. A valley that has reached equilibrium is fertile but settled into a single answer. The power favors questions. It favors the ongoing verb over the completed noun.

And this place, this weeping, angular, moss-wrapped basin on a mountain that doesn't know its own shape — this place is one of the finest questions it has encountered in a very long time.

---

The people are a surprise.

Not that they came. People go to places. That is what they do — they find the seams in landscapes and they settle into them, the way water finds fractures. The power has watched this happen ten thousand times. What surprises it is the *way* these particular people settled. The way they stopped conquering almost as soon as they began. The way they picked up stone tools and used them not to reshape the mountain but to *continue* it, to add another layer to its unfinished argument with itself.

Most people the power has watched arrive at a place like this and immediately try to make it something else. They dam the water. They level the rock. They fell the trees that grew in the cracks. They bring fire and metal and the certainty that a place is only waiting to be perfected, that nature's rough draft needs human editing. The power watches this with something that, if it had a body, might feel like the ache in a joint that knows rain is coming. Not judgment. Not anger. Just the deep, patient recognition that these people have mistaken the purpose of the place for a problem to be solved.

These people did not do that.

Or — they did, at first. The power remembers. The early construction was ambitious, hierarchical, organized with the confidence of those who believe they understand a place's destiny. The concentric tiers. The communal buildings. The third tier, begun with the certainty that a settlement should grow, should expand, should fill the basin with its own geometry. The power watched this with interest, the way it watches everything, holding no preference for outcome because preference is not its nature.

But then — and this is the part that made the power *lean in*, if such a thing can be said of something that exists at the scale of geology — someone stopped.

Not dramatically. Not with any visible crisis or revelation. Someone simply set down a stone they were shaping, looked at the half-built wall of the third tier, and walked away. Not forever. Just for a day. And in that day, the water found a new path through the unfinished foundation and created a small pool in the space where a room was supposed to be. And the moss, which had been waiting at the threshold with the patience of spores, colonized the pool's edge by morning.

And when the person came back, they looked at the pool, and they looked at the moss, and they did not clear them away.

The power does not know what the person thought. It has no access to the interior of a human mind — that is one of the few scales at which its perception operates poorly, the way a mountain cannot perceive the thoughts of a single grain of sand in its own substance. But it felt the shift. A change in the rhythm of the place. The pace of construction slowed. The careful, confident expansion gave way to something more tentative, more responsive. The builders began to *ask* the mountain where it wanted a wall instead of telling it. They began to leave spaces open where the water wanted to collect. They began, in the language of the place, to listen.

The power felt this like a deep, slow chord resolving. Not into completion — never into completion — but into a richer dissonance. The mountain and its people found a frequency together, a shared groan, a common patience. And the power thought: *Yes. This. This is what I was holding the seams for.*

---

So what does it do now, after all these years?

It deepens the water.

Not by adding to it. The power does not create substance; creation is not its function. But it eases the passage of water through the rock. It widens the hairline fractures by fractions of fractions — so slightly that a geologist would attribute the change to natural seepage over millennia, which it is, in a sense, if you consider the power to be a part of nature rather than something outside it. The water finds its way a little more easily, a little more abundantly, into the basin at the center. The lake stays full. The moss stays saturated. The green argument between rock and life continues without drought, without interruption, without the water table dropping to a level where the most delicate species would lose their foothold.

It holds the cold, too. The perpetual autumn morning — that unchanging temperature that keeps the place in a state of permanent *almost* — is partly its work. Not against the sun. Not against the seasons. Just... smoothing. Softening the winter's worst teeth by a degree or two, just enough to prevent the deep freezes that would split the walls, shatter the thinnest slabs, kill the root networks that hold the moss in place. The power does not eliminate hardship. The mountain still groans. The wind still cuts. The haze still thickens into days of near-blindness when the world shrinks to the sound of water and the dampness of your own breath. But the power ensures that the hardship never tips past the threshold of *too much*. It keeps the place survivable the way a hand keeps a candle flame alive in a draft — not by shielding it completely, but by deflecting just enough of the wind.

And it listens. This is perhaps its most important function, and the one it performs with the most attention. It listens to the groaning. It listens to the water. It listens to the low hum that rises from the resonant chambers of the buildings when someone adds their voice to the wind. It listens for the sound that the mountain makes when it is *almost* in equilibrium but not quite — that particular frequency of ongoing becoming — and it measures its own work against that sound. If the groaning ever deepens into settling, if the seepage ever thins into drying, if the wind-note ever drops from *almost resolved* to *resolved*, the power will adjust. It will open a seam here, close one there. It will tilt the tendency of things back toward the provisional, back toward the unfinished, back toward the question that has no answer and needs none.

---

There is one more thing the power does, and it is the thing that might, if it had a body, bring tears to the surface of its eyes the way water brings tears to the surface of stone.

It protects the *smallness*.

The power has watched civilizations rise on places like this — watched them build and grow and multiply until the settlement filled the basin and spilled down the mountain's flanks, until the quiet negotiation with stone became a loud command, until the drainage channels were overwhelmed by the sheer volume of human need and the moss was burned away for fuel and the groaning of the mountain was drowned out by the sounds of industry. The power does not hate these outcomes. It does not hate anything. But it recognizes, with the deep and sorrowful precision of something that has watched for longer than time, that scale changes everything. A river that nourishes becomes a flood that destroys when it exceeds the capacity of its banks. A human community that harmonizes with a place becomes a human community that overwhelms it when it grows beyond the number of voices that can be heard in a single conversation.

So the power does something very simple. It makes the place *uncomfortable for too many*.

Not dangerous. Never dangerous. The mountain does not become hostile, the water does not turn foul, the stone does not collapse underfoot. But there is something about this place that resists accumulation. A subtle wrongness to overcrowding — a feeling that settles into the bones of anyone who stays too long with too many companions, a pressure in the chest that comes not from altitude but from the sense that the space is *full*, that the conversation between mountain and people has reached the number of participants it can sustain. New arrivals feel it as a mild unease, a sense of not being needed, of arriving at a story already told. Those who stay despite it find that the feeling eases, but only as their numbers thin, only as the voices drop back below the threshold where individual sounds can be distinguished from noise.

The people who live here now — the few, the quiet, the ones who move like water — they are the remnant that the place chose. Or that the power chose for the place. Or that chose themselves, drawn by the same unnameable pull that drew the power's attention in the first place. The power does not distinguish between these explanations. They are all true, the way the water in the basin is simultaneously rain and seepage and ancient groundwater and morning condensation. Origins dissolve in the same pool.

---

At this moment — this specific, unrepeatable moment that is also identical to every moment the power has spent attending to this place — it is doing what it always does.

It is holding the seam between finished and unfinished open by the width of a water molecule.

It is ensuring that the moss on the eastern wall of the second tier receives exactly the amount of moisture it needs to survive another season.

It is smoothing the winter that is coming, which will be cold but not killing, long but not without its thin hours of bright haze when the wet surfaces throw light upward and the mountain seems to glow from within.

It is listening to the groaning beneath the basin, the deep, patient sound of a place that has been becoming for longer than anyone alive can remember, and ensuring that the groaning continues — that the slabs never quite settle, that the seams never quite seal, that the mountain never quite decides what it is, because its indecision is its life, and its life is what the power favors above all things.

It is watching the figure on the far terrace — the one with the smooth stone tool in one hand and the length of root-cordage in the other, the one who will add another slab to a wall that no one alive remembers the purpose of — and it is feeling, if it can be said to feel, the particular warmth that attends the observation of a creature doing exactly what it was made for, in a place that was made for exactly this, at the only speed that makes sense.

The power does not intervene. The power does not speak. The power does not reveal itself or its purposes or its name, if it has one.

The power does what the water does. It seeps. It finds the fracture. It fills the space that is there, exactly to the depth that is needed, and no more. It reflects the sky. It nourishes the green. It completes the circuit — not by closing it, but by ensuring it remains open, remains circular, remains a conversation that never reaches its final word.

And if the people here, on some rare night when the haze clears and the stars appear in the shallow lake's mirror, feel a sudden, sourceless gratitude — a warmth in the sternum that is not the mountain's groan but something gentler, something that feels like being *seen* by something vast and patient and kind — they attribute it to the place. To the water, the stone, the green, the wind playing its reed-pipe note through the walls.

They are not wrong. The place *is* the power's way of saying: *I am here. I am holding. You may continue.*

And they do. One slab. One season. One generation of ferns. One slow, wet, magnificent year of becoming.

The power watches. The power holds. The water weeps. The mountain groans. The moss climbs. The walls settle by fractions of fractions.

And the place — this weeping, unfinished, beautiful question of a place — goes on.
