The hollow rampart breathes.

This is the first thing the wise power notices, when it turns its attention toward that old plateau. Not with lungs, not with gills, but with a slow, tidal rhythm that encompasses wind moving through hollow chambers, water condensing and falling in perpetual echo, and the vast, silent pulse of mycelium threading through stone. The place is a lung. It inhales silence, exhales sound. It has been doing this for longer than any star in its sky has burned.

The power perceives this and understands it as a kind of speech. Not language, not communication as *it* knows communication, but a form of expression as fundamental as gravity. The plateau is stating its nature through its processes: *I am here. I am old. I continue.*

---

**What the power sees:**

It sees the **eyeless builder** crossing the open stone, leaving its trail of fresh white secretions. It follows the creature’s path across the plateau, reading the story written in those abandoned beginnings of buildings that will never be finished. The power sees the builder not as a tragic figure—it does not project loneliness where there is only function—but as a living syllable in a sentence that has been written for millennia and is not yet complete. The builder is grammar. It is the act of starting, repeated without end, a verb with no object.

It sees the **fungal networks**—the true engine—and understands them as a mind of sorts. Not a consciousness, not a will, but a distributed intelligence of chemistry and growth that is solving a problem older than the plateau itself: how to turn stone into life. The power admires this. It is a solution so elegant it doesn't require thought. It is the logic of patient, relentless becoming.

It sees the **hybrid structures** and feels a resonance. Here, the boundary between the made and the grown has dissolved. Here, a building can have a pulse. This is not an abomination; it is an integration. The power notes how the unnamed builders' work is being accepted, absorbed, and transformed by the life that followed them. It is a form of inheritance more profound than any artifact. The buildings are not relics; they are being *digestated* by the future.

It sees the **small things** in the undergrowth and recognizes their absolute necessity. They are the quiet janitors of this ecosystem, the ones who ensure the cycle turns. Without them, the beauty would choke on its own decay. The power sees their purpose as sacred, even if the creatures themselves are not aware of any sacredness.

It sees the **stone itself**, the ancient, bruise-colored bedrock, and it sees the deep, slow settling—the tectonic groans, the millimeter-by-millimeter fall toward a distant erosion. The plateau is not static. It is a process. The power sees the beginning and the end, and it understands that the middle—this loud, empty, lush, cold present—is all that ever truly exists.

---

**What the power feels:**

A profound and settled **peace**.

This place is not in conflict. The emptiness is not a void to be filled; it is the necessary canvas. The cold is not a cruelty; it is the tempo that allows the fungi their slow, magnificent work. The loudness is not chaos; it is the sound of all processes proceeding as they should. The aloneness of the builder is not solitude; it is completeness.

The power feels a deep respect for the **Unnamed Things**. They are not gone. They are *sublimated*. They poured their existence into the architecture, and that architecture has become the substrate for an entire world. Their ghost is not haunting the place; their ghost *is* the place, and it is no longer a ghost at all. It is a foundation.

There is a sadness, but it is not the sharp sadness of loss. It is the gentle, tidal sadness of the autumnal—the understanding that all things pass, and that passing is the mechanism of renewal. The plateau is a testament to the beauty of things that are almost finished, and yet still, somehow, beginning.

---

**What the power does:**

It does not intervene. Intervention would be a violence here—a shout into a space that has found its own perfect volume.

Instead, it **listens**. It gives its full, benevolent attention to the rhythm of the place. For a moment—though it is not a moment in time, as the plateau understands time—the wise power adds its presence to the landscape. It does not touch. It does not change. It simply *witnesses*.

And in that witnessing, it offers a gift without words, without force.

It **harmonizes**.

The wind through the hollow chambers, for an instant, carries a tone not of keening or of song, but of **acknowledgment**. The dripping in the cathedral-chambers falls for a moment in a pattern that is not just echo, but **affirmation**. The low vibration of the settling rock is, for a breath, perceived not as decay, but as the deep, contented sigh of a story well-told.

The power does not fix the builder's aloneness, because the builder does not experience it as broken. It does not speed the fungi's work, because their slowness is their genius. It does not fill the hollow structures, because their emptiness is their purpose. It does not resurrect the Unnamed Things, because their legacy is whole and alive.

What it does is **consecrate the process**.

It lays a blanket of silent, radiant attention over the plateau, and in doing so, it says: *This is good. This is whole. This is a complete and perfect expression of existence.* Not good as in moral. Good as in *sound*. Good as in a note played perfectly on an instrument.

The plateau does not change. The builder continues its crossing. The water drips. The fungi grow. The wind sings its hollow songs.

But for one instant that is woven into all instants, every process on the Hollow Rampart is underlaid with a resonance of being **seen**, and found to be enough.

---

**What remains after the power turns its gaze elsewhere:**

The plateau. Exactly as it was. Exactly as it is.

But perhaps—though you could not measure it or prove it—there is a new quality to the silence *between* the sounds. A space that feels less like absence and more like rest. The water condensation on the oldest, darkest hybrid structure glistens with a light that is not merely pale daylight, but daylight that remembers being witnessed. The eyeless builder, as it presses its belly to the stone to begin a new secretion, pauses for a fraction of a second that is not a hesitation but a **communion**—with the stone, with the place, with the slow, great wheel of its own nature.

The hollow rampart continues its long, slow fall toward nothing.

But it falls, now, in the knowledge that it has been held in the gaze of something that understands falling. And that is a form of grace. Not salvation. Not rescue. Just the simple, profound grace of being known.

The wind fills the buildings with sound. The water drips. The fungi grow. The plateau settles.

And it is, as it has always been, enough.
