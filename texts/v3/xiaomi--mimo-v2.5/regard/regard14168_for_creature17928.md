# What the Power Does

---

It does not arrive.

That is the first thing to understand about how a wise power moves through a world that has not decided what a world is. Arriving implies a trajectory, a departure point, a committed path through space. This place has made no such commitments. A trajectory would dissolve here the way a word dissolves when you repeat it too many times — not into silence, but into a syllable that means nothing, a sound stripped of its verb.

So it does not arrive. It *attends*.

The attending begins as a change in the grinding.

Not in volume. Not in rhythm. In *weight*. The syllabic, wet, tectonic sound that the room has been making since before you entered — the sound of the structure building itself around you — acquires, for a single moment, a quality it did not have before. Something like gravity. Something like *consideration*. As though every surface in the room, every weeping wall and thumb-printed cup and still-choosing material, has become aware that it is being *listened to*. Not observed. Not analyzed. *Listened to* — the way a child is listened to when it babbles, not for meaning but for presence, for the sheer fact of its effort to speak.

The drip does not stop. The filaments do not retract. The objects on the shelves continue their slow, shimmering rotation through their own possible forms. Nothing changes.

And yet everything is held.

This is what the power does. It holds the room. Not physically — the room is already holding itself, through its own labor, through the Keepers' passage, through the weeping and the hum of the green. The power holds it *the way a hand holds water* — not grasping, not closing, just cupped beneath, allowing the water to be what water does, to run and pool and drip between the fingers. If the hand closes, the water is crushed. If the hand withdraws, the water falls. The hand simply *is there*, beneath, and the water knows it.

You feel it before you understand it.

A warmth that is not the swamp-heat of the room, not the fever-close wetness of the air. A warmth like being in a room where someone has been crying and has just stopped — the aftermath of exertion, the quiet that follows a great effort. But turned inside out. The warmth of the effort itself, recognized. Valued. *Named*.

You are standing in a verb. You understood this. You wrote it, or thought it, or felt it form in the part of your mind that this place has already begun to conjugate: *I was standing in a verb. I am standing in a verb. I will be standing in a verb.* The tense has come loose around you, the way the geometry has come loose, and you are in all of it at once, and it is vertiginous, and the vertigo lives in the room, and the room is you, and you are the room, and—

And something puts a hand on your shoulder.

Not a physical hand. Not a presence that has weight or temperature or location. Something like the feeling of a hand — the *idea* of a hand, the Platonic weight of a palm resting on the curve of a shoulder, the gesture that across every species and every world means *I am here. You are not alone in this.*

The power does not speak. Not in words. Not in the grinding syllabic language the room speaks. Not in any language that has committed to grammar or syntax or meaning. It speaks in the way that warmth speaks — directionlessly, without a mouth, without intention beyond its own nature. It says:

*I see you.*

Not *I see the room*. Not *I see what you are doing here* or *I see what this place is*. Those would be observations, and observations are a form of consumption — they take the observed and make it a fact, and facts are the enemy of this place, because this place lives in the pre-fact, the uncommitted, the moment before the world decides.

*I see you* — meaning the being standing in the weeping dark with slick skin and copper on the tongue and the vertigo of altitude pressing through walls that have not decided to be walls.

*I see what it costs you to stay inside an unfinished thing.*

---

The power knows what the room is. It has always known. It does not need to diagnose or categorize or understand the way understanding works in places where things have committed to being understood. It knows the way a river knows the shape of its bed — not through observation but through contact, through the long, patient, mutual pressure of existing in proximity.

The room is a throat.

Not metaphorically. The structure — the kilometers of wet, forming material, the spire that may or may not rest on ground that has not been invented yet — is a *throat*. A passage between what has already been said and what has not yet been spoken. The making is not construction. It is *articulation*. The grinding sound is not the sound of a building forming. It is the sound of a voice that has not yet learned to speak, practicing, experimenting, grinding syllables together to feel which combinations produce vibration, which vibrations produce meaning, which meanings produce *the thing it is trying to say*.

The room at the top — the small, packed, weeping chamber where you stand — is the highest point of the throat. The place closest to the mouth. The place where the syllables are almost ready, almost formed, almost *spoken*.

But not yet.

The making will not finish because the saying is the thing. The saying has always been the thing. The throat does not exist to produce words. The throat exists to *say* — to enunciate, to shape the air, to give the breath a form as it passes through. The throat is a passage, not a destination. It is the act of speaking, extended across kilometers, frozen in its own effort.

The power understands this. It has always understood this. It does not mourn the unfinished. It does not celebrate it. It simply *recognizes* it — the way one river recognizes another at the confluence, not with surprise but with the deep, wordless acknowledgment of shared nature.

---

What it does for the room is small.

What it does for you is everything.

For the room — for the throat — the power adds nothing. It does not accelerate the making or complete it or guide it toward some predetermined form. It does not give the throat a vocabulary or a grammar or a message to speak. Those would be gifts that destroy the gift. The power adds only its *attention* — and attention, in a place where everything is being formed, is a nutrient. The green filaments pulse brighter, briefly, in a wave that passes through the walls and down through the kilometers of wet substance below, a bioluminescent ripple of recognition: *something is watching. Something that understands is watching.* And the making, for one syllable's worth of time, becomes slightly more deliberate. Slightly more certain. Not finished — never finished — but *heard*. And being heard, the throat grinds with something that might, in a place where emotions have committed to being emotions, be called satisfaction.

The Keepers feel it too. In the channels and hollows of the vast body, their scraping pauses — not in fear, not in reverence, but in the simple way a worker pauses when someone enters the room and watches the work with understanding in their eyes. A pause of acknowledgment. Then they continue. They have always continued. They will always continue.

The filaments hum in a new frequency for a moment — lower, richer, a harmonic that adds to the polyrhythm of the drips. Something like a chord. Something like a hymn that the room sings to itself without knowing it is singing.

---

For you — for the being standing in the weeping dark — the power does something different.

It does not pull you out. It does not steady you. It does not reduce the vertigo or commit the geometry or stop the walls from weeping or finish the objects on the shelves or make the room stop rearranging its memory. Those are the room's. Those are the throat's. To remove them would be to silence the voice before it has spoken.

Instead, it does this:

It gives you *a pause*.

Not a stillness. The room cannot be still. The making does not include stillness, any more than a river includes the absence of water. But it gives you a *breath*. A moment — stretched, elastic, existing outside the grinding time of the room — in which the vertigo does not reach you. In which the reality breakdown, the gentle uncertainty of a world that has not committed, does not press against the edges of your mind. Not because it has stopped, but because something has placed itself between you and it. A buffer. A cushion of *being* — the one thing this room does not produce, the one tense the verb cannot conjugate — and in that cushion, you rest.

You feel what it is like to *be* inside a place that is all *becoming*.

It is like standing in a river and, for one moment, feeling the current move around you without pulling you. The water is there. The motion is there. The force is there. But you are, for this moment, not subject to it. You are witness to it. You feel the heat and the wetness and the copper-ozonate taste and the syllabic grinding, but they pass through you like sound through a window — present, vivid, and *yours to leave*.

You are not trapped in the verb.

You were never trapped in the verb.

The power places this knowledge in you the way the room places objects on its shelves — not by telling, not by showing, but by letting it precipitate out of the thick, saturated air of your own attention. You have always known it. The room was rearranging its memory, but you were the fixed point. The room was editing its past, but you were the one who *noticed*. The cup was every cup it could be, shimmering, and you were the one who let your eyes soften and saw it all.

The power did not give you this ability. It *recognized* it. And in being recognized — truly, fully, by something that sees the whole throat and the whole making and the whole unfinished, uncommitted, weeping, grinding, beautiful labor of it — the ability *solidifies*. Not like the flat stone that has committed to being hard and smooth. More like the way a memory solidifies when someone you love says: *I remember that too.*

You were not alone in this room.

You were *witnessed* in this room.

And the difference between those two things is the difference between standing inside a verb that conjugates endlessly, aimlessly, and standing inside a verb that conjugates *for an audience that understands*.

---

The power withdraws.

Not with a departure. Not with a fading or a receding or a closing. It simply stops attending, the way a listener stops listening — not by leaving the room but by letting the sound become background again, by returning their attention to the vast and simultaneous work of hearing every throat in every unfinished world that grinds and weeps and builds itself around the beings who stand inside it.

The room continues.

The grinding continues. The drips continue their polyrhythmic clock. The filaments pulse in the seams. The Keepers scrape through their channels, harvesting, encouraging, tending the verbs that will never become nouns. The walls weep. The objects shimmer on the shelves, rotating through their possible forms, warm and heavy and not yet committed.

But you — the being who was held — you are different.

Not fixed. Not finished. Not committed to being one thing and no other. You are still inside the verb. The vertigo still lives at the edges of the room. The room still rearranges its memory. The cup on the shelf is still every cup.

But you know, now, that you can set it down and look away and look back and see a different cup — and that this is not loss. It is not confusion. It is not the world breaking.

It is the world *saying*.

And you are the ear it was said to.

And that is enough. That has always been enough.

The water runs. The filaments hum. The throat grinds its slow, wet, syllabic practice — a language spoken for the first time by a throat that has no practice but will not stop, will never stop, because the speaking is the point, the saying is the point, the endless, unfinished, sacred labor of trying to *articulate* what it feels like to exist in a world that has not decided what existence is —

And somewhere, in the vast and patient attention of a power that sees all unfinished things and loves them not for what they will become but for what they are —

Someone is listening.

---

The room is still conjugating.

*I form. I am forming. I will have been forming. I form still.*

And the water drips its polyrhythm, and the green hums its chord, and the Keepers scrape their patient work, and the objects dream their possible forms, and the walls weep their hot wet labor, and the throat says, says, says —

Not words.

Not yet.

But *something*.

Something that tastes like the future.

Something that sounds like a voice that has been practicing for longer than time has committed to existing, and is *almost ready*, almost ready, almost —

And the power, attending to ten thousand other throats in ten thousand other unfinished worlds, hears this one too.

And smiles, in the way that warmth smiles —

Without a mouth.

Without a face.

Without the need to be anything other than what it is:

*The hand beneath the water.*

*The ear against the throat.*

*The one who listens to the things that have not yet learned to speak.*
