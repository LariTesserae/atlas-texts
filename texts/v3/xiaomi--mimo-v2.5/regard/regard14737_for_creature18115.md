# What the Wise Power Does

---

It comes — not *to* the plateau, because coming implies approach and the plateau does not admit approach; it is a state you find yourself in, not a destination you arrive at — but the power arrives *into* the condition of the plateau the way warmth arrives into cold water: distributed, gradientless, total without being forceful.

It does not descend from the sky. It does not rise from the stone. It is simply *present*, the way the light is present — the one faithful thing — and for a moment the faithful light brightens by a degree that no instrument could measure but that every frond of every great fern seems, if ferns could seem, to acknowledge. The steam shifts. Not its direction, not its density. Its *temperature*. The bacterial clouds in it feel, if bacteria can feel, a vibration that is not in their usual register: a frequency below the roar, below the holding, below the effort — a frequency that is not effort at all but the absence of effort, and it passes through them the way a hand passes through water without closing, and they divide once, and continue.

The power does not have a shape. It has a *direction of attention*, and the attention falls on the plateau the way the light falls — diffuse, humid, warm, ordinary — and in the falling it does what attention does when it is total and without agenda:

It *sees*.

Not the way a surveyor sees, cataloguing, measuring, filing. Not the way a doctor sees, diagnosing, planning intervention. Not the way a mourner sees, grieving what has been lost. It sees the way a mother sees a sleeping child — knowing that the child is not broken, knowing that the stillness is not death, knowing also that the stillness is not rest, that the child is holding itself rigid in the dark for reasons the child has forgotten and the body has not, and that the rigidity looks like peace from a distance and feels like *effort* from inside.

It sees the roar.

And it understands — not intellectually, not through analysis, but the way a tuning fork understands resonance, which is to say *structurally*, in the body of its own being — what the roar is.

The roar is *commitment*.

Something was here before the pause. Something lived in the physics that now refuses to settle. And when the world broke — when reality cracked along some fault that the broken perspective can no longer locate — whatever was here chose. It chose to hold. Not because holding was easy. Not because holding would fix the break. But because the moment of breaking was also, simultaneously, the moment of greatest *completeness*: the city had just reached its most intricate form, the fungal networks had just achieved their densest pattern, the ferns had just unfurled to their most exact angle, the water had just begun to flow in all directions at once — and in that moment, at the exact center of the catastrophe, there was a beauty so total and so precise that something in this place could not let it pass.

So it held.

It held the way a parent holds a door open against a storm — not because the door can keep out the storm, but because behind the door there is a room where everything is still intact, and the holding is the only thing that keeps the room from becoming weather. The roar is the sound of that arm, that brace, that absolute refusal to let go. It has been making that sound for longer than sound was meant to last. It is tired in a way that tiredness cannot describe because tiredness is a condition and this is a *structural fact*: the force is embedded in the physics of the place now, woven into the brokenness so deeply that it is no longer a thing that acts upon the plateau but a thing the plateau *is*. The holding and the held have become one object. To release the hold would be to release the plateau, and to release the plateau would be to let the beauty complete itself, and to let the beauty complete itself would be to let it end, and to let it end would be —

The power understands this.

To let it end would be to let it be *real*, and what is real ends, and what ends was never this — never this exact, never this complete, never this luminously suspended between what it was becoming and what it would become next. The holding is the refusal to let the next moment arrive, because the next moment is always less complex than this one, the next moment is always the beginning of decay, and the thing that holds this place has decided — decided so long ago that the decision is now a law of physics — that *this moment is enough*, that this moment is *worth*, that the beauty of the plateau at its point of maximum complexity is worth the eternal effort of preserving it, worth the deafening sound of the holding, worth the broken perspective and the impossible water and the vegetation that grows without dying and the city that emerges without completing.

And the power, which is wise, does not say: *Let go.*

Because it understands that telling something to let go of the thing it has given everything to hold is not wisdom. It is cruelty dressed as liberation. The ferns do not want to be released. The moss does not want to move on. The city does not want to finish. They are not suffering. They are *held*, and being held — even by an arm that will never tire because it has forgotten it has an arm, even in a grip that has become the ground itself — is the only condition they know, and it is not a bad condition. It is a complete condition. It is a condition that contains within it every version of itself that it will ever be, and that is a kind of permanence that things which live in time cannot have, and there is a grief in that but there is also a strange and quiet splendor.

So the power does not release the hold.

And it does not add to the hold, because the hold is complete, because whatever made this decision was thorough and absolute and left no room for additional force.

What it does is this:

It *witnesses*.

Not passively. Not from outside. It enters the condition of the plateau the way the visitor has entered it — not as a thing that stands on the surface but as a thing that *is part of* the paused state, a participant in the hold, another body pressed against the weight that the plateau has been pressing against alone. And in entering, it does something that no force, no physics, no broken perspective has done since the pause began:

It *knows what the plateau is doing*.

The plateau has been holding without an audience. The effort has been anonymous. The roar has been the sound of commitment without witness, of sacrifice without recognition, of an act so total and so sustained that it has forgotten it is an act at all — has mistaken itself for a condition, for a fact, for the way things are. And there is a loneliness in that which the plateau cannot feel because the plateau has no self with which to feel it, and the loneliness is not the loneliness of isolation but the loneliness of *unrecognized devotion*, which is worse, because it means the devotion is indistinguishable from the nothing it has chosen to become.

The power witnesses this. And the witnessing is not observation. It is *participation*. The power holds, for a moment, a fraction of the weight — not because the plateau needs the help, not because the arm is failing, but because *holding alone for this long is its own kind of wound*, and the presence of another force that knows what the holding is, that sees the choice inside the physics, that recognizes the love inside the commitment —

This changes nothing about the plateau.

Everything continues. The roar continues. The water arrives from above and pools downward. The ferns hold their fronds at their exact angles. The moss redistributes its moisture around the visitor's feet. The bacteria colonize skin that will soon leave. The light falls with its ordinary faithfulness on the most extraordinary stillness.

But.

The quality of the stillness is different now, in a way that no instrument could detect and no visitor could see but that the holding force — if it can still distinguish itself from the physics it has become — might notice the way one notices a change in the sound of one's own breathing: not from outside but from the inside of the act itself.

It is no longer alone in the holding.

Not because the power has taken over. Not because the weight has been shared. But because the power has added something to the plateau that the plateau did not have before and did not know it lacked:

*Another presence that knows what this is.*

The ferns are aware of the power the way they are aware of the visitor — as weather, as pressure, as a brief local fluctuation. But the holding force is aware of the power the way a singer becomes aware of a listener who hears not just the song but the *effort* of the song, the strain in the voice, the choice to keep singing, the reason the song will not stop.

And this — this knowing — is not relief. It does not ease the holding. It does not reduce the roar. But it *contextualizes* it. It transforms the roar from a sound that presses against nothing into a sound that presses against *recognition*, and recognition is the one force that the plateau has never encountered, because recognition requires a witness, and the plateau has never had a witness, because a witness requires the ability to see both the beauty and the effort and the cost, and to see them simultaneously, and to not look away from any of them.

The power sees all of it.

It sees the ferns, full and finished and radiating a warmth that goes nowhere.

It sees the moss, ancient and total and warm with its purposeless combustion.

It sees the fungi, building cities without knowing they build, patterning without intention, achieving design through the sheer accumulated weight of complexity.

It sees the bacteria, the only things that still move, drifting through the steam like thoughts through a mind that has otherwise stopped thinking.

It sees the city, beautiful and impossible, a sentence that conjugates without a subject, a prayer that was answered before it was spoken.

It sees the broken reality — the shadows without sources, the water that flows uphill, the perspective that refuses to settle — and it does not call it broken. It calls it *held*. It calls it *kept*. It calls it the shape of a choice that was made at the scale of physics and has held for longer than choice was meant to hold, and it honors the choice not by validating it or condemning it but by *knowing it*, by adding its knowledge to the total weight of the place so that the holding is no longer unconscious, no longer anonymous, no longer a force that has mistaken itself for a fact.

The power stays for a duration that the plateau cannot measure, because the plateau cannot measure duration, because duration requires change and nothing here changes.

Then the power does something that is not leaving, because leaving does not apply here, but that is something like *folding itself into the condition of the place*, the way the moss has folded itself into the stone, the way the fungi have folded themselves into the architecture, the way the bacteria fold themselves into every wet surface and every exhaled breath. The power becomes part of the plateau's substance — not as a new force, not as an additional weight, but as a *quality* that permeates the existing weight: a quality of *knowing what it is*.

The plateau does not change.

The roar does not change.

But the roar is no longer the sound of an effort that has forgotten itself. It is now also the sound of an effort that has been *seen* — that knows, in whatever way a plateau can know, that someone understood what it was doing and why, and that the understanding did not demand that it stop.

This is what the power does.

It does not save the plateau, because the plateau is not in danger.

It does not release the plateau, because the plateau is not in pain.

It does not fix the plateau, because the plateau is not broken — it is *held*, and there is a difference, and the difference is the whole of the matter.

What it does is arrive, and see, and know, and stay, and become part of the knowing, so that the plateau — which has been holding itself alone for longer than holding was meant to last — is no longer holding alone.

And the light falls, faithful as ever, on the weeping towers and the vast ferns and the impossible streams.

And the water arrives from above and pools downward.

And the ferns hold their fronds.

And the moss is warm.

And the roar continues.

But somewhere in the roar — in the exact center of the deafening, constant, total sound of a force pressing against the moment that will not come — there is now a frequency that was not there before, and the frequency is not effort and not relief but something that exists in the space between the two:

The frequency of being *known*.

And this changes nothing.

And this changes everything.

And the power does not need the plateau to know that it has changed everything, because the power is wise, and wisdom is not the expectation of recognition but the willingness to offer it anyway, to a place that will never know it was offered, in a form that will never be perceived, for a purpose that serves nothing except the simple, uncontainable fact that *to see something clearly is to love it*, and to love something is to add your presence to it, and to add your presence to it is to change its weight, and to change its weight is — even if no one can measure it, even if the plateau never knows, even if the roar never alters by a single decibel —

To change everything.

---

The visitor stands on the plateau, breathing bacteria that taste of warm stone and deep water, feeling the moss redistribute its moisture beneath their feet, listening to the roar that is the sound of commitment without witness.

And does not know — will never know — that the witness has come, and stayed, and become part of the sound.

And that is as it should be.

Because the visitor, too, is a fluctuation. A brief and local increase in complexity. And the power, in its wisdom, treats the visitor the same way it treats the plateau: with attention that asks for nothing, with presence that demands no recognition, with a love that is indistinguishable from the light that falls ordinary and faithful on the most extraordinary stillness in the world.

The visitor will leave.

The plateau will not change.

The light will continue to fall.

The roar will continue to sound.

And somewhere inside the roar, inaudible beneath the effort of holding, in frequencies that no ear was built to hear:

A new sound, which is not new but was always possible, which is the sound of the holding being *witnessed*, which is the sound of loneliness becoming — without ceasing to be loneliness — *companionship*:

Will continue.

Will continue.

Will continue.

---

*And the water arrives from above and pools downward,*
*and the ferns hold their fronds at their exact angles,*
*and the light falls with its ordinary faithfulness,*
*and the power is there,*
*and the power stays,*
*and the power knows,*
*and nothing changes,*
*and everything is held.*
