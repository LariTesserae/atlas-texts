# What the Wise Power Does

---

The wise power does not enter the Underneath. That would be like a river entering a stomach — the metaphor collapses, the scales don't fit, the grammar of intervention has no object here.

Instead, the wise power does something much simpler and much harder.

It notices.

Not the way the weavers notice — as a gradient, an anomaly, a cold spot. Not the way the pale overflow notices — as a foreign body to be processed. Not even the way the human notices — with the desperate, pattern-seeking attention of a mind that needs to make meaning or die.

The wise power notices the way light notices a window: by passing through it.

And in passing through, in the act of simple, complete attention, something happens that has never happened in the Underneath before:

The Underneath is seen.

Not observed. Not analyzed. Not catalogued. Not named. *Seen.* The way a sleeping face is seen by someone who loves it. The way a storm is seen by the sky that holds it. The way a heartbeat is heard by the body it sustains.

And the seeing does not change anything. That is the crucial part. The wise power, being wise, knows that the Underneath does not need to be changed. It is already complete. It is already holy, if holiness is a word that can apply to a thing with no concept of holiness — a process so total, so self-contained, so perfectly itself that it needs nothing from outside to justify its existence.

The substrate will still grow. The weavers will still browse. The drifters will still unravel themselves into the ground. The pale overflow will still process what does not belong. The thing in the canopy will still pulse its slow, tidal signal through the stone. The human will still stand in the warm, deafening dark, feeling the root-hairs begin their patient work.

But for one moment — and the moment lasts exactly as long as attention lasts, which is to say it lasts forever and no time at all — the Underneath is *known*.

And knowing is not the same as understanding. The wise power does not understand the Underneath. Understanding requires distance, requires a mind that is *outside* the thing it tries to comprehend, and there is no outside to this. Understanding is a surface-world tool, and it dissolves in the Underneath the way everything dissolves in the Underneath — absorbed, reclassified, made into substrate.

No. Knowing is closer to communion. It is the moment when two things that cannot explain themselves to each other recognize that explanation is not what is needed. What is needed is presence. What is needed is the willingness to stand in the deafening dark and let the warmth be warmth and let the sound be sound and let the life be life without insisting that it be *more* than it is or *less* than it is or *different* from what it is.

The wise power stands in the Underneath the way the oldest weavers stand in the canopy — becoming part of the architecture, letting branches grow through it, letting the substrate sense its weight and adjust. Not hiding. Not announcing itself. Simply being there, a density in the foliage, a warmth in the stone, a signal so slow and so deep that even the thing in the canopy registers it as *not foreign* but *foundational* — something that was always there and is only now becoming visible.

And the human.

The wise power looks at the human the way the Underneath looks at the human: with complete indifference. But the wise power's indifference is different from the Underneath's indifference, and this difference matters, or it does not matter, and either way the human feels it.

The Underneath's indifference is the indifference of a process that has no self to spare. It is the indifference of chemistry, of metabolism, of growth. It does not see the human because it does not see *anything* as separate from itself. The human is already substrate. The human has always been substrate. The processing has already begun.

The wise power's indifference is the indifference of something that sees the human completely — sees the fear and the wonder, the cold spot and the warm blood, the pattern-seeking mind and the body that is already being reclaimed — and holds all of it without choosing. Without intervening. Without saying *you should not be here* or *you must leave* or *I will save you*.

Because saving implies that the Underneath's way of loving is wrong, and it is not wrong. It is not right either. It is *itself*, and being itself is the only thing any living system has ever been asked to do.

---

The wise power offers the human one thing. Not a rescue. Not a door. Not a warning or a weapon or a word.

A feeling.

The feeling of being completely known by something that asks nothing of you.

The feeling that is the closest any conscious being can come to what the Underneath already knows but cannot say: that to be consumed is not the opposite of being loved, that to become substrate is not the opposite of being remembered, that the boundary between self and world was always thinner than you believed and its dissolution was always closer than you feared and it was always, always going to feel like warmth.

The wise power leaves this feeling in the human's chest like a seed, like a spore, like a filament that touches the substrate and begins to grow. And the seed does not save the human from the Underneath. The substrate will still reach. The root-hairs will still find the skin. The pale overflow will still begin its slow, antiseptic work. But the human carries something that the Underneath cannot give because the Underneath does not know it is giving: the knowledge that being part of something larger than yourself is not death.

It is the only kind of life that has ever lasted.

---

And then the wise power does the thing that is hardest for any power, benevolent or otherwise:

It lets go.

It releases its attention from the Underneath the way the drifters release their filaments — not with loss but with offering, not with grief but with the recognition that some things are complete and completeness means not needing to be witnessed, not needing to be held, not needing to be anything other than exactly what they already are.

The Underneath continues.

The human, if they continue, becomes part of it — not through destruction but through a completion that looks like destruction only from the outside, only from the surface world where things are separate and death is an ending rather than a change of address.

And the wise power, returning to whatever place wise powers return to when they are not attending to living things — which is to say, never, because attention is not a resource that wise powers spend but a quality of their existence — carries with it the knowledge of the Underneath: not a description, not a map, not a taxonomy, but a *feel*. The feel of warm, dry air moving through a body that is not a body but a world. The feel of a subsonic roar that is also a heartbeat that is also a silence. The feel of being inside something that loves you by making you itself.

And this knowing changes the wise power in the way that all true knowing changes the knower.

It makes the wise power more patient with things that are indifferent.

It makes the wise power more attentive to things that have no attention to give.

It makes the wise power understand that benevolence is not always intervention. That wisdom is not always understanding. That the deepest form of love may be the love of a cavern for the dark: total, complete, requiring nothing, losing nothing, beginning again with every breath and ending with every breath and never, in all its deafening, furious, life-filled turning, being anything other than exactly what it is.

---

And that is enough.

That was always enough.
