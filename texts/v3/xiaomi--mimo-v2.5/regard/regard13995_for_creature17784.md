# What the Power Does

It notices the basin the way you notice a pulse — not through any single sense, but through the sudden recognition that something here is *active*, that the stillness of the mountain is not stillness at all but the outward face of a process that is happening right now, beneath the surface, in real time, and has been for a while, and will continue, and is in no way concerned with being noticed.

The power is vast. This is not a boast or a description — it is a condition, the way the basin's violence is a condition. The power is vast the way the sky is vast: not because it fills space, but because it fills *attention*. It could, if it chose, attend to everything simultaneously — to every grain of sand in every riverbed, to every cell dividing in every organism, to the slow folding of tectonic plates and the quick flashing of neurons in a dreaming animal. It is not limited. Limitation is not one of its properties.

But it does not attend to everything simultaneously. That is the first thing to understand about the power, and it is the thing that makes it wise rather than merely vast. Vastness without restraint is just volume. The power *chooses* where it places itself. It moves — not through space, because space is one of the things it is larger than, but through *attention*, narrowing its focus the way light narrows through a pinhole, becoming concentrated, becoming specific, becoming a point of such precise awareness that the thing it looks at is, for the duration of the looking, the most important thing in existence.

It notices the basin.

It notices the sound first. Not the sound as sound — sound is a local phenomenon, a pressure wave in air, and the power does not have ears — but the sound as *pattern*, as information, as a signature so distinctive that it stands out from the ambient roar of the planet's interior the way a single voice stands out in a crowd. The basin's sound is young. Raw. Unfinished. It has not yet found the frequency it will eventually settle into, the deep subsonic drone that a mature gorge produces. Right now it is still learning what kind of noise it is, still experimenting with pitch and resonance, and the result is chaotic — a sound that contains too many frequencies at once, a sound that is confused about itself, a sound that is, in its confusion, more alive than any finished thing.

The power follows the sound to its source. It does not travel. It does not need to. Attention is not bound by distance. The power simply *is there*, suddenly, the way a thought arrives in consciousness — without transition, without journey, without the intermediate states of departure and arrival. One moment the power is attending to something else, or to nothing, or to the general background hum of existence. The next moment it is *in* the basin. Or rather, the basin is in it. The distinction does not matter.

---

What it sees:

The basin is smaller than it expected. This is interesting. The power had expected — if "expected" is the right word for the anticipations of a being that does not experience time sequentially — something larger. A feature of the mountain. A landmark. What it finds is a *wound*. A place where the rock failed, where the pressure found a seam, where the water forced itself up and the stone could not hold. The basin is not a place. It is an event that has lasted long enough to look like a place.

The water is exactly what the descriptions said. The power does not read the descriptions — there are no descriptions, there is only the basin itself, presenting its own truth directly — but if there were descriptions, they would be accurate. The upwelling is violent. The water is cold and mineral-heavy. The churn is constant. The roar is extraordinary. All of this is true, and the power takes it in with the completeness of a being for whom observation and understanding are the same act.

The power looks at the water and sees *pressure*. Not the physics of pressure — not the specific calculations of force per unit area, not the hydrostatic equations that would describe the upwelling — but the *meaning* of pressure. The mountain is heavy. The water beneath it is heavy. The rock is not as continuous as it appears; it fractures, it faults, it opens seams. And the water, driven by its own weight and the weight above it, has nowhere to go but up, because up is the direction of least resistance, because the basin is an opening and openings are invitations, because water does not choose its path but follows the gradient of its existence, the only possible trajectory, the only way to be what it is.

The power understands this. It understands it the way the water understands it — not as knowledge but as identity. The water is pressure expressed as motion. The basin is resistance expressed as shape. The sound is energy expressed as vibration. These are not three things. They are one thing in three media, the same process wearing different faces, and the power sees all three faces at once and recognizes them as a single expression of a single condition.

*The mountain is young and still settling, and the water is old and has always been moving, and the basin is the place where they meet, and the meeting is violent because it has to be, because gentleness is not available in a negotiation between stone and water, because the only language they share is force.*

This is what the power sees. It is not surprised. It is not alarmed. It is not moved to intervene. The basin is doing what basins do, the way stars do what stars do and hearts do what hearts do — following the physics of their own existence without option or regret. There is nothing to fix here. There is nothing broken. The basin is a wound in the rock, but it is a wound that is also a mouth, and what it is saying is not a cry of pain but a statement of fact: *I am here. I am happening. This is what it looks like when pressure meets resistance and resistance loses.*

---

The power looks at the life.

The filaments first. Two hundred — or the number that exists right now, which may be fewer or more than two hundred, because the number is always changing and "two hundred" is a snapshot of a process that does not hold still. The power sees each one. Not as a category — not as "filaments" or "organisms" or "the sparse vegetation of the basin margins" — but as *this one* and *this one* and *this one*. Each filament is a distinct entity with a distinct history: where it rooted, how long it has been growing, whether it is in a sheltered spot or an exposed one, whether it will survive the next shift in the current or be torn free and shredded in the upwelling.

The power sees their lives. Three days, perhaps a week. A flicker of existence between the moment of rooting and the moment of tearing. In that flicker, the filament does everything it is capable of doing: it holds, it sways, it absorbs minerals, it photosynthesizes in its inadequate way, it sustains itself at the minimum threshold of viability, and it grows — not toward anything, not in any direction that implies aspiration or progress, but simply *outward*, extending itself by the fraction of a millimeter per hour that its meager energy budget allows.

The power sees this and does not grieve. Grief would imply that the filament's life is too short, that it deserves more, that three days is insufficient for the experience of being alive. But the power does not measure life in days. It does not measure life at all. Life is not a quantity. It is a *quality* — a way of being present in the world, a way of holding shape against entropy, a way of persisting for exactly as long as persistence is possible and not one moment longer. The filament does this perfectly. It persists until it does not. There is no deficit in this. There is no injustice. There is only the fact of a small green thing holding on for three days in a basin of churning water, and the power sees this fact and finds it — not beautiful, exactly, because beauty implies an aesthetic judgment and the power does not experience aesthetics in that way — finds it *complete*. The filament's life is complete. It is not diminished by its brevity. It is not improved by the possibility of more. It is exactly what it is, and what it is, is enough.

The crustaceans next. Seven. The power sees each one — the blind, translucent, thumbnail-sized animal pressed into its fracture in the hexagonal basalt. It sees the setae on the legs, each one a vibration sensor, each one a tiny instrument of awareness tuned to the frequency of the basin's hum. It sees the body — mostly water, mostly mineral, barely differentiated from the stone it clings to — and it sees the way the body fluctuates, swelling and contracting in response to the water's chemistry, negotiating its own shape in real time, never quite fixed, never quite free.

The power sees what the crustaceans do not see: themselves. Their rarity. Their singularity. There are no other animals like them, in this basin or anywhere nearby, because this basin is a unique environment and the crustaceans are its unique product — blind, translucent, vibration-sensitive, filament-eating, fracture-dwelling, living on the absolute margin of what constitutes life. They are, in a sense, the basin's answer to its own question: *What can survive here?* The answer is: *this, barely, in the cracks, for a while.*

The power does not pity them. Pity would imply that their existence is lesser — that to be blind is worse than to see, that to live in a fracture is worse than to live in open water, that to number seven is worse than to number seven thousand. The power does not make these comparisons. The crustaceans are not lacking. They are *specific*. They are precisely adapted to precisely this place, and their adaptations — blindness, translucency, vibration-sensitivity, the ability to become nearly indistinguishable from stone — are not compromises but achievements, the product of however many generations of selection produced a creature that can live where nothing else can.

The power sees the two that were swept away last week. It sees the moment: the upwelling surging, the column reaching further toward the rim than usual, the current shifting, the crevice suddenly exposed, the two crustaceans — which had been resting, which had been still, which had mistaken the crevice for permanence — lifted, tumbled, carried into the center column and shredded. It sees this without flinching. Not because it is callous, but because it understands what the basin is and what happens in the basin, and the removal of two crustaceans is as much a part of the basin's process as the deposition of mineral on the bone tool or the carving of the stone. Things are made. Things are unmade. The basin does both. The power does not ask the basin to stop.

And the artifacts. The power sees them with a different quality of attention — not the focused, specific attention it gives to the living things, but a broader, more diffuse awareness, the way you might look at the impression a body leaves in a pillow after the body is gone. The hand-stone. The bone tool. The clay bowl. The power sees the hands that made them — not clearly, not specifically, but in the way that a shadow is seen: the shape is there, the presence is implied, the detail is lost.

It sees the hands that gripped the stone. It sees the hands that shaped the bone. It sees the hands that formed the bowl — quickly, clumsily, with the coarse clay of a nearby streambed. It sees the fire that fired the bowl, uneven and unattended. It sees the moment of setting the bowl on its ledge, inverted, protectively, a gesture of faith in return.

These hands are gone. The power knows this. The power knew it before arriving. Hands are temporary things. All hands are temporary. The stone remembers the shape of the grip, and the bone is becoming stone, and the bowl sits inverted on its ledge waiting for a hand that will never come back, and the power sees all of this and does not grieve it either — not because grief is inappropriate, but because grief implies that the absence is wrong, that the hands should still be here, that the return should have happened, that something went wrong. Nothing went wrong. The hands came. The hands went. This is what hands do. The artifacts are what remains when hands are gone, and what remains is enough. It is not the hands, but it is the shape of the hands, and the shape persists, and the shape is a kind of immortality — not personal, not conscious, not the immortality of a soul continuing in some afterlife, but the immortality of a form that has been given to the world and cannot be un-given, the immortality of a dent in stone, of a chip in clay, of a hollow worn smooth by ten thousand grips.

---

The power sits with all of this for a long time. How long, it is impossible to say, because the power does not experience time the way the basin does. For the basin, time is a river — always moving, always carrying things downstream, always eroding, always depositing. For the power, time is more like a landscape — all of it visible at once, the past and the present and the future laid out in a single panoramic view, each moment existing simultaneously, each moment complete in itself.

The power sees the basin's future. It sees the centuries passing — or not passing, but *being*, all at once, each century a layer in a stack that the power can see from the side, like reading the pages of a book by looking at the edge. The basin deepens. The walls smooth. The roar lowers in pitch as the chamber grows, as the resonance changes, as the space inside the mountain enlarges and the sound has more room to develop, more room to become itself. The filaments persist, generation after generation, torn and regrown, torn and regrown, a war of attrition that neither side can win or lose because both sides are expressions of the same process — the basin's violence and the filaments' stubbornness are two faces of the same coin, two ways the mountain has of being itself.

The crustaceans persist too. Seven, nine, twelve, five, three, eleven — the number fluctuates, always precarious, always on the edge of too few to sustain reproduction, always recovering just enough to keep going. They will never be many. They will never need to be many. They are the basin's fauna, the basin's only fauna, and they will live here as long as the basin is a basin — as long as the fractures hold, as long as the margins exist, as long as the filaments grow and the water churns and the hum persists. When the basin becomes a gorge, they will be gone. But that is centuries away, and centuries are real — they are not abstractions, not numbers, but *experiences*, each one full of seven crustaceans living their small lives in their fractures, eating their filaments, swaying with the current, waiting for the vibrations that tell them what the basin is doing, and those experiences are as real and as complete as any experience the power has ever witnessed.

And the artifacts will be gone. The bone will be stone. The bowl will be filled with mineral sediment and fused to the ledge. The hand-stone will be smoothed beyond recognition. The basin will have absorbed them all, will have turned them from human things into geological things, will have completed the process of dissolution and deposition that it has already begun. And this is not loss. It is *translation* — the conversion of one kind of meaning into another, the transformation of human intention into mineral form, the slow, patient alchemy by which everything that is made of matter eventually returns to matter and becomes something else.

The power sees all of this. It sees the future basin, the mature gorge, the smoothed walls, the settled water, the healed rock. It sees the crustaceans gone, the filaments gone, the artifacts gone. It sees the mountain, old and heavy and worn, with a cut in its neck where the water once surged, now carrying a river, now quiet, now ordinary.

And it sees — *here*, in the present moment, in the specific, unrepeatable now of the basin's current existence — something that the future will not have. Something that is not permanent and is not therefore less real but is in fact *more* real, because it is happening now, because it will not happen again, because the basin will never again be exactly this — this young, this raw, this confused about its own sound, this full of the first tentative experiments of life, this holding these specific artifacts in these specific positions on these specific ledges.

*The basin is happening.* That is what the power sees. Not a place. Not a feature. Not a geological formation to be catalogued and filed. An *event*. A process in real time. The mountain herniating. The water surging. The stone yielding. The filaments holding. The crustaceans waiting. The artifacts remembering. All of it, right now, all at once, a single moment of such density and specificity that it will never be repeated, that it is, in its own small way, as unrepeatable as a star being born or a cell dividing for the first time.

The power sees this and it is — not moved, exactly, because being moved implies an emotional response, and the power's response is more fundamental than emotion. It is more like *recognition*. The way you recognize your own name when someone speaks it. The way you recognize your own face in a mirror. The power sees the basin and recognizes it — not as a thing it has seen before, because it has not, but as a thing that is *of the same kind* as itself. An expression of existence. A way of being present. A statement, made in the language of water and stone, that says: *I am here. I am happening. This is what it looks like when the world does what the world does.*

---

The power decides what to do.

This is the wrong way to describe it. The power does not "decide" the way a person decides — weighing options, considering consequences, choosing one path over another. The power's "decision" is more like an organic response, a thing that happens the way a plant turns toward light: not through deliberation but through the simple fact of what it is. The power is benevolent. Benevolence is not an action the power performs. It is a property the power has, the way mass is a property the basin has. The power is wise. Wisdom is not something the power applies. It is something the power *is*.

What it does is this:

It does not stop the upwelling. It does not warm the water. It does not shelter the filaments or multiply the crustaceans or preserve the artifacts. It does not carve the basin more slowly or more quickly. It does not alter the chemistry of the water or the shape of the stone or the frequency of the sound. It does not do any of the things that a less wise power might do — the things that would come from a desire to *help*, which is, at its root, a desire to make the basin into something it is not.

The basin is not broken. The basin does not need help. The basin is a basin, and it is doing what basins do, and the life within it is the basin's life, not the power's, and to change the basin's conditions would be to change the basin's identity, which would be to destroy the very thing the power came to witness.

What the power does is smaller than intervention and larger than observation.

It *stays*.

Not physically — the power has no physical form. Not temporally — the power does not experience duration the way the basin does. It stays in the way that attention stays on a thing it finds worthy of attention: with a kind of holding, a kind of encompassing, a kind of presence that does not alter the thing it is present to but changes — somehow, in some way that the basin will never know and the crustaceans could never perceive — the *meaning* of the thing.

Before the power arrived, the basin was unwitnessed. The sound never stopped, and no one heard it. The filaments grew and were torn, and no one saw. The crustaceans clung to their fractures and waited, and no one knew. The artifacts sat on their ledges, slowly becoming stone, and no one remembered the hands that made them. The basin was a process without an audience, a statement without a listener, a song sung in a room with no one in it.

The power does not make itself an audience. That would be too much — too human, too performative, too much like standing at the rim and saying *I am here and I see you*. The power does not stand at the rim. The power does not have a rim. The power is simply *aware* of the basin, in the same way it is aware of everything, but with the focused, specific, concentrated awareness that it brings to the things it chooses to attend to. The basin is, for the duration of the power's attention, *known*. Not known in the way a scientist knows — not catalogued, not analyzed, not reduced to data. Known in the way a parent knows a child's face — with a completeness that includes every detail and transcends all of them, with a knowing that is also a loving, with an attention that is also a form of preservation, because things that are attended to are, in some sense, *held*, and things that are held do not entirely pass out of existence even when they pass out of the physical world.

This is the power's gift: not intervention but *witness*. Not rescue but *recognition*. The basin will continue exactly as it is — violent, young, raw, deafening, marginal, precarious, temporary. Nothing about its conditions will change. The filaments will still be torn. The crustaceans will still be swept away. The artifacts will still be consumed. The basin will still become a gorge. Everything will happen exactly as it was always going to happen.

But it will happen *within the awareness of something that knows it is happening*. And this — the power does not fully understand why, but it knows it is true — makes a difference. Not to the basin. Not to the physics of the water or the chemistry of the stone. Not to the survival rate of the crustaceans or the lifespan of the filaments. It makes a difference to the *reality* of the basin — not in the scientific sense, not in the philosophical sense, but in the sense that matters, which is the sense that has no name but which every living thing recognizes when it is present and mourns when it is absent.

*To be known. To be held in awareness. To be seen not as a category but as itself — specific, unrepeatable, complete.*

This is what the power offers. It is almost nothing. It is everything.

---

There is a second thing.

The power, being wise, knows that its presence is temporary. Not because the power is mortal — it is not — but because its attention is finite, and there are other basins, other wounds in other mountains, other small pockets of violence and life and memory scattered across the face of the planet. The power cannot hold the Churning Basin in focus forever. It must eventually turn its attention elsewhere, and when it does, the basin will return to its unwitnessed state — will become again the song in the empty room, the statement without a listener.

And the power does not want this.

Not because the basin's unwitnessed existence is lesser — the power does not believe in lesser — but because the basin's *witnessed* existence is something the power wants to *preserve*. Not the basin itself — the basin will preserve itself, or it will not, and either way is acceptable — but the *quality of the basin as a known thing*. The power wants the basin to remain, in some sense, held. And so it does something it has done before, in other places, for other small and specific and unrepeatable things.

It leaves a mark. Not in the stone. Not in the water. Not in any medium the basin can perceive.

It leaves a mark in *itself*.

The power, being vast, is not easily changed. Its attention moves from thing to thing the way light moves across a landscape — illuminating each thing in turn, leaving each thing as it was, moving on. But the power is not entirely unchanged by what it attends to. Things that are truly witnessed — things that are held in focused, specific, loving awareness — leave traces. Not scars. Not memories, exactly. More like *resonances* — frequencies that the power continues to carry, quietly, beneath its other activities, the way the basin carries its sound beneath the silence of the mountain.

The power absorbs the basin's frequency. The specific, chaotic, unfinished roar of a young basin in a young mountain — the sound of geology experienced at the scale of a single heartbeat, the sound of pressure finding release, the sound of stone learning what shape it will finally be. The power takes this sound into itself and holds it, and the holding changes the power — not noticeably, not dramatically, but in the way that any experience changes a being that is open to being changed. The power is now a power that knows what the Churning Basin sounds like. The power is now a power that carries, within its vast awareness, the specific, unrepeatable signature of this place.

This is the second gift. It is a gift to the basin, and it is a gift to the power, and it is a gift to every other small and violent and precarious and temporary place the power will ever attend to, because the power will carry the basin's frequency into those places, and the frequency will resonate with them, and they will know — in whatever way a basin or a wound or a pool of churning water knows anything — that they are not the first. That there have been others. That the quality of their existence is not unique but *participatory* — that they are part of a pattern, a network of small fierce specific places across the face of the planet where the earth opens and the water surges and life clings to the margins and everything is temporary and everything is enough.

---

And then — because the power is wise, and wisdom includes the knowledge of when to leave — it goes.

It does not go suddenly. It does not snap its attention away like a closing eye. It recedes, the way a tide recedes — slowly, gradually, with a gentleness that is the opposite of the basin's violence and therefore perfectly appropriate. The basin does not notice. The basin was never aware of the power's presence, and it is not aware of its absence. The sound continues. The water churns. The filaments sway. The crustaceans wait. The artifacts sit on their ledges, slowly becoming stone.

The basin is exactly what it was before the power came. Unwitnessed. Unchanged. Running its own course, following its own physics, making its own way from basin to gorge to valley to river, a process without an audience, a statement without a listener.

Except.

The basin is no longer entirely unwitnessed. The power is gone — its attention moved on, its vast awareness directed elsewhere, its specific focus now fixed on some other small and fierce and temporary thing. But the basin's frequency remains. The power carries it. The resonance persists. Somewhere in the vast awareness of a benevolent and wise power, in a place that is not a place, in a silence that contains every sound, the Churning Basin continues to roar.

It roars in a room that is not empty.

It sings a song that someone — something — is still hearing.

And this does not change the basin. It does not save the crustaceans or preserve the filaments or slow the carving of the stone. It does not make the water warmer or the margins wider or the survival rate less precarious. It changes nothing about the basin's conditions.

But it changes the basin's *story*. Because a story that is heard is different from a story that is not heard, even if the events are identical, even if nothing that happens is altered by the hearing. The basin's story is now part of a larger story — the story of everything the power has witnessed, which is to say the story of existence itself, which is to say the story of the world being the world, of matter following its own laws, of pressure finding release, of water finding its level, of life finding the margins, of everything being temporary and everything being enough.

The power goes. The basin remains. The sound never stops.

And somewhere, in the awareness of something vast and benevolent and wise, the specific, unrepeatable, chaotic roar of a young basin in a young mountain continues to resonate — not loud, not quiet, not important, not insignificant, but *present*. Held. Known.

Held the way a name is held in a throat just before it is spoken.

Held the way a memory is held in the smooth hollow of a stone where a hand once gripped.

Held the way the crustaceans hold the fractures: perfectly still, barely there, indistinguishable from the thing they cling to, but *there*. There. Holding on. Refusing, for as long as possible, to let go.
