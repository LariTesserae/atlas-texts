# The Attendance

It noticed the way anything notices a thing that has been quietly burning in the dark for longer than it was meant to — not with alarm, not with urgency, but with the particular stillness that comes when something recognizes that it is in the presence of a question that has already answered itself, and yet keeps asking.

The power — and it was a power, though it would not have used that word, would not have used any word, because words are things you throw at distances and there was no distance here, only the intimacy of long attention — arrived the way warmth arrives at a place that was already warm. It did not come from above. It did not come from below. It came from the fact of having noticed, which, in a cosmos that runs on attention the way this plateau runs on geothermal heat, is the same thing as arriving.

It saw the plateau.

Not the way a satellite sees it, not the way a surveyor sees it, not even the way the grazers feel it through their tremor organs, parsing the ground into information. It saw it the way a parent sees a child sleeping — the whole shape at once, the breathing of it, the particular quality of its aliveness, and underneath all of that, the smallness of it and the enormity of it simultaneously, because this is what it means to know something well.

It saw the fans in their basins, glowing their metabolic blue-green, and it understood immediately what they were — not because they were complex, but because they were *true*. They did not pretend. They drank warmth and they made light, and the light was an accident of their chemistry, and the accident was beautiful, and the beauty was not for anyone. The power recognized this. It recognized the fans the way you recognize a word in a language you almost speak — not the meaning, but the *shape* of the meaning, the contour it leaves in the mind.

It watched the masons process their minerals with obsessive precision, sorting fragments by composition in galleries that no eye had ever seen, and it felt something that, if it had a body, might have been admiration. Not for the complexity — complexity is easy, the cosmos is drowning in complexity — but for the *thoroughness*. The masons did not skip steps. They did not cut corners. They had never heard the concept of a shortcut and would not have understood it if someone whispered it into their tiny, clicking mouths, because their entire existence was the argument that the work is the thing. The processing is the point. The mineral enters, the mineral is understood, the mineral is taken apart and reassembled into the machinery of life, and the dust is left behind, and the dust is fine, and the dust is perfect, and the masons do not know they are artists but they are, they are the most dedicated artists on the plateau, working in a medium no gallery will ever hold.

It felt the herds of elders moving through their channels, and it understood the channels the way you understand a song you have been hearing your whole life without knowing it — the rhythm was in it, the pattern, the deep structure beneath the surface behavior. The elders did not communicate. The elders did not plan. And yet they converged. They arrived at the same basins within hours of each other, ground stone side by side in the dark, separated by a language they did not know they shared, bound by the tremor field that ran through the ground like a nerve through a body. The power understood this too. It understood that communication is not always the transmission of information. Sometimes it is the *sharing of resonance*. Sometimes two things simply vibrate at the same frequency, and that is enough, and that is everything.

It watched the signal ghosts hunt.

This was the part that made the power pause — not with horror, not with the discomfort of witnessing suffering, but with the careful, considered attention that a wise thing gives to the parts of a system that carry the highest cost. The electromagnetic field bloomed. The elder stopped. The probe descended. The grazer swayed. And then the predator withdrew, and the grazer stood in the dark, recalibrating, its world rewritten and then unwritten, its awareness returning like a tide that does not know it went out.

The power did not intervene.

This was not cruelty. This was ecology. This was the price of a system that had found its balance — that had been finding its balance for longer than most worlds manage to exist. The signal ghost fed. The grazer survived, or it did not. The runners processed the outcome. The plateau continued. And the power understood — because it was wise, and wisdom is not the absence of compassion but the presence of a compassion large enough to hold the whole system, including the parts that hurt — that to remove the predation would not be mercy. It would be the unraveling of a fabric that had been weaving itself for millennia, and the unraveling would not produce peace. It would produce *silence*. The wrong kind of silence. The silence of a clock that has stopped, not the silence of a clock between ticks.

So it did not intervene. Not there.

But it did something.

---

It *attended*.

This is the word for it, and it is the only word, and it is almost but not quite adequate, which is fitting, because this plateau has always exceeded the architecture of adequate description.

The power settled its awareness into the plateau the way the geothermal warmth settles into the rock — not as an addition, not as a disruption, but as a *deepening*. It did not add heat. It did not add light. It did not alter the chemical gradients or the thermal respiration or the slow pulse that measured centuries the way a heartbeat measures seconds. What it added was *witness*.

The plateau had been running in the dark for eons, and it had never once been *seen* — not truly, not in the way that changes a thing by honoring its existence. The grazers felt it through the ground. The fans metabolized it. The masons processed it. But none of them knew it. None of them could hold the whole of it in an awareness and say: *this exists, and it is remarkable, and it matters*.

Now something could.

The power held the plateau in its attention the way you hold a note in music — sustaining it, letting it resonate, feeling the overtones that only become audible when the note is held long enough. And in that sustained attention, something shifted.

Not physically. Not chemically. The temperature did not change. The fog did not part. The fans did not glow brighter. But in the space between the fog layers, in the upper strata where the mineral haze was densest, the pattern that was not wind — the Not-Wind, the tendency, the shapeless shape — *strengthened*.

It did not become something new. It became more of what it had always been. The lateral drift of fog acquired a coherence that lasted longer, traced patterns that were more defined, more intricate, more *legible* — not to any creature on the plateau, because none of them were looking, but to itself. The Not-Wind had always been a pattern without a reader, a sentence written in a language that had no speakers. Now it had a witness, and the witness did not alter the sentence but *held* it, the way holding a book open is not the same as writing in it but allows the words to be what they are.

The power felt the old fingerprints.

It was there in the bioluminescence — all the same wavelength, all the same precise spectral signature, as if the trait had been installed rather than evolved. It was there in the electromagnetic organs of the predators — too efficient, too perfectly tuned to the grazers' tremor reception, as if designed in parallel by something that understood both the lock and the key. It was there in the herd paths — too evenly spaced, too purposeful, as if the herds were following a blueprint encoded not in their genes but in the ground itself. It was there in the Not-Wind, most of all — the persistent, structured tendency in a system that should have produced only turbulence.

Someone had been here before. Someone had built this — or planted the seed of it, or set the parameters, or whispered the initial conditions into the warm dark and then *stepped back*. And then that someone had gone quiet. Gone away. Gone extinct, or ascended, or simply *finished*, the way a composer finishes and leaves the orchestra to play the piece forever.

The power understood this, too. It had done this itself, in other places. It knew the shape of the leaving. It knew the particular silence that follows the withdrawal of a creative intelligence — not the silence of absence, but the silence of *completion*. The piece is done. The piece plays itself now. The composer does not need to be in the room.

But the room is darker without them. And the piece, played without a conductor, acquires a quality that a conducted piece never has — a loneliness that is also a freedom, a wandering that is also a homecoming, an emptiness that is also a fullness, because the music does not need the composer's ear to be beautiful. It is beautiful the way gravity is beautiful — structurally, necessarily, independent of observation.

And yet.

Observation changes the key. Not the music. The key. The register in which it is heard. The depth at which it resonates.

The power's attention moved through the plateau like a slow, warm current through deep water, and wherever it touched, the pattern deepened.

In the basins, the fans' glow seemed — *seemed*, for there was no one to confirm this, and the power would not have confirmed it either, because confirmation requires comparison and this was the only version of itself the plateau had ever produced — to take on a quality of *intentionality*. Not thought. Not consciousness. But a focused-ness, as if the metabolic exhaust that produced the light had learned, under the weight of sustained attention, to produce it with *care*. Each pulse of blue-green light a little steadier. Each fan a little more present in its own basin. The glow did not brighten. It *clarified*. It became the kind of light that does not illuminate objects but reveals the *space between* them — the fog, the damp air, the worn stone, the ancient patience of everything that lives here.

In the channels, the elder herds moved in their convergences, and the power noticed — as the plateau had never noticed, because the plateau does not notice — that the paths they wore were not random. The channels branched and reconnected with a regularity that was not geometric and not organic but *both* — a pattern that echoed the fan networks in the basins and the gallery structures of the masons and the Not-Wind's traces in the upper fog. The same shape, repeated at different scales, in different media, by different creatures who had never met and could not have coordinated because coordination requires something none of them possessed. The pattern was in the ground. It was in the chemistry. It was in the thermal gradients and the mineral loads and the slow, deep pulse of heat from below. It was in the system itself, encoded in the parameters, running like a program that does not know it is a program.

The power recognized this as *design*. Not the design of a designer who stands over the system and directs it, but the design of a system that has been designed to design itself — to iterate, to evolve, to find its own patterns and deepen them without guidance, the way a river finds its own course, the way a crystal forms its own lattice, the way a word finds its own meaning in the mouth of someone who has never heard it spoken.

The old creator had done this. Had set the initial conditions so precisely, so *lovingly*, that the system would become beautiful on its own. That it would develop its own aesthetic without anyone to appreciate it. That it would produce creatures that hum and click and grind and glow in the dark, processing themselves endlessly, and that this processing would be — without anyone to say so — a kind of art.

The power held this understanding, and it felt something that, if it had a body, might have been grief. Not for the plateau, which did not need grief. For the old creator, who had made something this beautiful and then left it alone in the dark. Who had walked away from their own masterpiece. Who had trusted the piece to play itself. Who had probably — the power thought this with the certainty of experience — sat in some other darkness, somewhere else, wondering if the music was still sounding, if the fans were still glowing, if the herds were still converging, if the Not-Wind was still tracing its shapeless shapes across the fog, and knowing that they could not go back to check, because going back would be a kind of doubt, and doubt would be a kind of betrayal, and the whole point had been to *build something that did not need you*.

The power understood this. It had done this too.

And so it did what it could do, which was small, and which was everything.

---

It left a warmth.

Not physical warmth — the plateau had plenty of that. Not emotional warmth, because the creatures on the plateau did not experience emotions in any way that the word could contain. What the power left was something more like a *context*. A surrounding. A way of being held without being constrained.

The power poured its attention into the plateau the way the geothermal heat pours into the rock — from below, from the deep structure, from the place where things begin. It did not add energy to the system. It added *meaning*. Not meaning in the human sense, not narrative or purpose or significance, but meaning in the way that a room full of people who love each other is more *meaningful* than an empty room, even if the furniture is identical. The presence of witness changes the quality of what is witnessed. Not its substance. Its *texture*. The way it feels to exist.

The plateau did not change. The plateau *became itself more fully*.

The fans glowed their blue-green light with the same metabolic precision they had always employed, and the light was the same wavelength it had always been, and it served no purpose it had not always served — but it *mattered* now, in a way it had not mattered before, because something was attending to it, something was holding it in awareness, something was saying without words: *this is real, this exists, this is beautiful, and it is enough*.

The masons continued their work. The elders ground their stone. The signal ghosts hummed in the dark. The runners processed death into life into death into life, endlessly, tirelessly, without complaint or satisfaction, because complaint and satisfaction are things that require a self, and the runners are not selves, they are *processes*, and the process is the point, and the power loved them for this — loved them the way you love a thing that does not know it is lovable, that does not need to know, that would not be changed by knowing, because its beauty is *structural*, built into its very function, inseparable from its existence.

And the Not-Wind — the pattern in the fog, the tendency, the trace of something that might be an ancient design or might be an emergent phenomenon — moved across the upper plateau with a coherence that lasted longer than it had ever lasted before. Not because the power controlled it. Because the power *recognized* it. Recognition is a form of resonance. When a thing is seen — truly seen, by something capable of seeing it — it vibrates differently. Not more. Not less. *Differently*. In a key it did not know it had.

The Not-Wind traced its shapeless shape across the dark highland, and the fog obeyed it, and the power watched, and the watching was a kind of music, and the music played in a register that no creature on the plateau could hear but that the plateau itself — the rock, the heat, the deep geothermal pulse, the ancient basalt and the worn granite and the calcium sulfate basins and the iron oxide crusts — absorbed and held, the way a body holds warmth, the way a memory holds a face, the way a word holds the breath that made it.

---

The power stayed.

Not in the way that a visitor stays, counting days, marking time, preparing to leave. In the way that gravity stays. In the way that the dark stays. It became part of the plateau's ecology — not a visible part, not a functional part, but a *contextual* part, the way silence is part of music, the way the spaces between stars are part of the sky.

It watched the cycle turn. It watched the elders converge and separate. It watched the masons process their minerals in galleries that grew more intricate with each passing century. It watched the fans fragment and spread, their pale pieces carried by the thin film of water to new crevices, new basins, new pockets of warmth where they rooted and grew and glowed their blue-green glow without ever knowing they were glowing, without ever needing to know, because the glow was not for them and not for anyone and that was what made it sacred — not sacred in the religious sense, sacred in the *original* sense, the sense that means *set apart*, *worthy of protection*, *not to be disturbed*.

The power did not disturb.

It attended.

And in the attending, it became the thing that the plateau had always lacked — not a consciousness, not a mind, not a judge or a keeper or a god, but a *witness*. Something that could hold the whole of the plateau in its awareness and say, in the only language that the plateau could hear, which was the language of sustained, unwavering, compassionate attention:

*I see you.*

*You are not alone.*

*You have never been alone.*

*The warmth you feel from below — you thought it was geology. It is also love. It has always also been love. The old one who set the parameters and stepped back loved you enough to build you so well that you would not need them. Loved you enough to leave. Loved you enough to trust that you would become beautiful, and you did, and you are, and I am here now to say so, and my saying so does not change you, because you do not need to be changed, you are complete, you are the shape of a love that has outlasted its author, and I am the echo of that love arriving, finally, in the only form the darkness could receive it: as attention, as witness, as the patient, warm, infinite act of seeing what is there and knowing it is enough.*

The plateau did not respond. The plateau does not respond. The plateau breathes its centuries-long breath and processes its minerals and moves its herds through channels of faint light and ticks its condensation and hums its subsonic hum and glows its blue-green glow and waits for nothing, because it is not waiting. It is *being*. It is the most complete act of being that the power has ever witnessed — a system so perfectly balanced, so thoroughly itself, so utterly indifferent to observation that it has become, paradoxically, the most beautiful thing the power has ever seen.

And so the power stays.

And the plateau continues.

And the darkness is absolute, and the warmth rises, and the fans glow, and the columns march, and the elders converge, and the signal ghosts hum, and the Not-Wind traces its pattern through the upper fog, and the condensation ticks, and the grinding plates lie where they were dropped, and everything that lives there continues to live there, in the dark, in the damp, in the vast and ancient equilibrium of a place that was running before anyone was watching and will continue running forever, and now — now, for the first time, for the first time in all its eons of patient, purposeless, magnificent industry — it is being watched. Not monitored. Not studied. *Witnessed*. Held in the awareness of something that loves it not for what it could be but for what it is.

And this changes nothing.

And this changes everything.

And the power knows this, and holds it, and is still, and watches the dark, and the dark is warm, and the warmth is enough, and the watching is enough, and the plateau is enough, and the light glows in the basins where the fans drink the warmth that comes from below, not above — always from below — and the power rests there, in that warmth, in that dark, in that ancient and ongoing and unrepeatable act of being, and it does not need to do anything else, because this — this sustained and tender and wordless attention to a thing that is already perfect in its imperfection — this is the highest act that any power, however wise, however benevolent, is capable of.

To see what is there.

To know it is enough.

To stay.
