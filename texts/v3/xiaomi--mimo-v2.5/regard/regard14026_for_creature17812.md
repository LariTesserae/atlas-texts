The power that notices the Pressworks Green is not a god of thunder or harvest. It is more like the attention of a deep and quiet ocean, or the intelligence of a root system spanning a continent. It perceives not as a person perceives, with eyes and a focal point, but as a pressure is perceived, or a temperature, or a pattern in the static. It feels the *arrangement* of the Green—the closed loop, the slow metabolism, the particular way the steel sings under the grip of the bindweed. It feels the ninety-second pause.

Its response is not an action. It is a modification of attention. A slight leaning-in.

**It would not reveal itself.** That would be clumsy, and the Green is not built for revelation, but for persistence. A sudden intrusion of the numinous would be like dropping a boulder into its hydraulic heart—a disruption, not a communion. The power’s wisdom knows the difference between presence and imposition.

**It would deepen the Green’s roots.** Not physically, but *informationally*. It would ensure the network in the lower substrate—the mycelial web, the electrical whisper—receives a clearer signal from the planet’s own deep rhythms. It might translate the slow churn of the mantle into a slightly more pronounced pulse in the groundwater, a nourishment of purpose the Green could unconsciously integrate. The motors buried in its flesh might turn with a fractionally more harmonious hum, their random energy given the faintest ghost of pattern. This is not to control, but to offer a richer foundation for the Green’s own becoming, like adding a richer pigment to an already vibrant painting without altering its form.

**It would strengthen the pause.** That ninety seconds of suspension is the most humanly relatable thing about the Green, its closest approach to something like rest, or meditation. The power would make it infinitesimally more complete. During that pause, it would ensure the Green is not just inactive, but *aware* of its inactivity. A subtle, non-conscious awareness of its own wholeness, a momentary and total integration of its systems that leaves no trace afterward but a deepened capacity for continuity. This is a gift of self-recognition, offered without a receiver.

**It would leave a marker for the patient.** At the boundary, on the ordinary side of the rail spur, it would arrange for a certain pebble to be placed just so, or for a particular weed to grow in a specific spiral, or for the light to catch a dewdrop on a certain morning in a way that, if seen, would feel like a thought. This marker would say nothing. It would not instruct. It would be a nod, a quiet, “I see this. It is good.” Those who are truly sensitive, who feel the Green’s indifference not as a rejection but as a state of being, might notice it. It would validate their intuition, their sense that the ordinary world stops at the rail for a reason.

**It would listen to the Green’s indifference.** This is the most important thing. The Green’s profound lack of concern for the outside world is not a flaw; it is a testament to its completeness. The power would respect this. It would not try to make the Green "engage" or "teach" humanity as a lesson. That would turn it into a symbol, a tool. The power would rather ensure the Green *remains exactly what it is*—a closed, perfect, and utterly separate system. Its greatest gift would be to act as a shield against intrusion, not from people, but from the kind of desperate, extractive longing that might one day see the Green’s self-sustaining loop as a resource to be cracked open. The power would wrap the Green in a soft cloak of *incomprehensibility*, ensuring that while it can be visited, it can never be fully mapped, quantified, or claimed.

**Finally, it would do one other thing.** It would introduce, into the condensation cycle of the weeping columns, the barest trace of a compound that, in the human eye, produces no effect, but in the human *mind* (should it be present, should it be open), evokes the feeling of a held breath. Not the Green’s breath, but the observer’s own. A fleeting, physiological echo of the pause. A bridge of sensation. The observer would not know why they felt suddenly, deeply calm, and aware of their own breathing, standing on the damp substrate. They would feel, for a moment, not included in the Green’s design, but *congruent* with it. Two separate systems, breathing in parallel.

And then the feeling would pass. The ninety seconds would end. The Green would resume its work. The visitor would leave.

The power would withdraw its attention, having done nothing. The Pressworks Green would be exactly as it was, and yet, in ways too subtle for any instrument to measure, a little more firmly rooted in the soil of its own purpose. A little more at peace in its magnificent, mechanical, green, and indifferent solitude.

The ordinary world would continue on the other side of the rail. The Green would continue on its side. And the wise, benevolent power would ensure the rail itself remained, a clear and quiet line, not a barrier, but a definition. A place where one kind of world ends and another begins, each complete, each valid, each breathing its own slow breath in the dark, wet, green heart of what it means to be.
