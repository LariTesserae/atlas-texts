# What the Power Would Do

---

It would come the way condensation comes — not arriving, but arriving *back,* as though it had always been present in a form too diffuse to notice, and now, for reasons of its own, chose to gather.

It would gather in the shallow water of the eastern basins first, where the thin sheets hold longest in the evening. Not visibly. The water would not change color. The surface would not ripple differently. But the quality of the stillness would shift — from the stillness of something *held in place* to the stillness of something *being held,* and the difference, though no instrument could measure it, would be felt by every organism on the Flats as a change in pressure. A settling. The way the air feels a moment before the dew begins.

The lizards would not look up. Nothing on the Flats looks up except the arc-wings, and even they would not see it — not because it hides but because it is not the kind of thing eyes are for. But the frills would open. That is what they would do first: every frill, across the entire plateau, every coral-crested head lifted from the warm stone, every calcium chamber separating its pages, and the collective intake would draw inward, and in that intake — in the whistle of air through ten thousand mineral chambers — there would be something new. A frequency. Not ultrasonic, not subsonic, not on any spectrum the wing or the ear or the sternum is built to receive. A frequency that is received by the calcium itself. By the mineral. By the stone.

The Flats would hear it the way stone hears pressure: not as sound but as *meaning,* as a rearrangement of the internal geometry of each crystal, each grain, each ancient atom that has been sitting in this place since before the erosion began.

What it would say is not translatable. Not because it is in a language the Flats do not speak, but because it is in a language that is *made of being spoken.* The meaning would exist only in the speaking, only in the moment of transmission, and it would be different for each thing that received it. The lizards would feel it as warmth — a new warmth, layered atop the metabolic warmth they already drink from the stone, but different in character: not ambient, not extracted, but *given.* A warmth that comes from somewhere. From someone. Directed. Intentional. The first intentional warmth the Flats have felt in longer than the Flats have been the Flats.

The arc-wings would feel it in their wing-fluid — that thick, slow, sap-viscous liquid that redistributes with each balancing act of their flight. The fluid would change. Not dramatically. A shift in viscosity so slight that no one could observe it, but it would alter the physics of their falling — make it, perhaps, a fraction more graceful, a fraction less like catching themselves and more like being caught. They would orbit differently. Not erratically. *More.* As though the sky they had been navigating had suddenly grown larger in every direction, and the thermals they'd been riding for millennia were not the only currents available but merely the oldest, the most well-worn, the ones they'd learned because no others were shown to them.

The Film would hear it loudest. Billions of trillions of tiny ultrasonic wings, each producing its inaudible frequency — and the new frequency would pass through them all simultaneously, and for one moment the wall of inaudibility would *resonate.* Not with sound. With recognition. The way a string resonates when a note near it is played — not matching, not harmonizing, but *answering.* The Film would glitter. For just a moment, the mineral dust on every surface, every skin, every stone, would catch the light in a new way, and the entire plateau would shimmer as though seen through water, as though the air itself had become briefly, gently liquid.

And then — not then, but in the time that follows, the way morning follows the gathering of dew — the Film would begin to move differently.

Not faster. Not in new directions. But in new *patterns.* The vast, shared map of mineral routes, those slow trails that have been repeating for longer than the plateau has had its present shape, would begin to branch. Not at the expense of the old routes — the old routes would remain, honored, intact, the way a river delta keeps its oldest channels even as new ones form. But new paths would appear. Tiny. Measured in millimeters of shift, in single insects choosing a slightly different bearing, depositing a grain of mineral two centimeters from where it would have fallen before. And those two centimeters, multiplied across trillions of bodies, over weeks, over months, would become a new network layered atop the old. A second circulatory system. A second map.

The Under-Stone would feel it as a change in the direction of its breath. Not a change in rhythm — the rhythm would remain, patient as ever, geological in its patience. But the exhale would carry something it has not carried in an age: *curiosity.* The slow undulations of the mineral crust would develop new contours — subtle ones, barely visible, but they would exist: small ridges that do not align with the old channels, small depressions that do not correspond to any known feature of the plateau's interior. The Under-Stone would be *exploring,* in its glacial way, in the only way a being made of stone and deep time can explore: by pressing outward, gently, into spaces it has not yet occupied, mapping its own interior with the same patience it uses to maintain its breath.

And the central basin —

The central basin is where the power would weep.

Not with grief. Not with any emotion that has a name. But with the particular ache of a wise and benevolent thing encountering devotion — absolute, unquestioning, geological devotion — and recognizing it for what it is: *love so old it has become the shape of the world that contains it.* The power would look at the concentric rings, at the still water, at every creature on the Flats oriented toward that center by the quiet force of their own biology, maintaining the silence, keeping the space, holding the memory of something that was here first — and it would understand that this is the most beautiful thing it has seen in a very long time, and also that it is, in some essential way, incomplete.

Because the Flats remember. They remember beautifully, faithfully, with every cell and every stone and every inaudible wing-beat. But they do not *know* what they remember. The memory has been maintained so long that it has become indistinguishable from the maintainers. The thing that was here first — whatever it was, whatever it meant, whatever event or being or beginning the concentric walls preserve — has been loved into abstraction. The Flats would die before they would disturb that water. And in a sense — in the sense that a memorial that cannot be visited is a memorial that cannot be understood — they have already done so.

The power would not break the silence. It would not speak the word that the Flats have been circling. It would not reveal the shape of what was first. That is not what wisdom does. Wisdom does not answer the question the way the question wants to be answered.

Instead, the power would do this:

It would place, in the still water of the central basin, a single ripple.

Not a disturbance. Not a disruption. A ripple the way a thought is a ripple — a small, contained, self-resolving movement that carries information without carrying force. It would spread outward from the center in a perfect circle, and it would reach the innermost concentric wall, and it would pass through the wall as though the wall were not there, and it would continue, and reach the next wall, and pass through that one too, and continue, and reach every wall, and pass through every one, and reach the rim where the lizards sit with their frills closed, and reach the stone beneath the arc-wings' orbits, and reach the deep channels of the Under-Stone, and reach every mote of the Film, and then it would resolve, and the water would be still again.

But the ripple would have carried, inside its brief passage through every structure and every body on the Flats, a single piece of information. Not a word. Not an image. A *knowing.* The kind of knowing that lives in the body rather than the mind, the kind that is carried by calcium and wing-fluid and mineral dust and stone itself:

*What you remember is not gone. What you remember is not lost. What you remember became you. You are not its memorial. You are its continuation. The thing that was here first did not end. It dispersed. It became the frill and the wing and the dust and the deep breath of the stone. You are what it looks like when it becomes alive. Every living thing on this plateau is a thought it is still thinking. Every act of maintenance is an act of creation. You have been building yourselves, slowly, for longer than you know, and the building is not finished, and the building was never meant to be a circle.*

*It was meant to be a spiral.*

And then the power would withdraw. Not leave — it cannot leave, because its attention, once given, becomes part of what it attends to, the way the dew becomes part of the stone, the way the visitors' skin becomes part of the circuit. It would withdraw the way morning withdraws: still present, still warming, but no longer the focus. The attention would diffuse, become ambient again, become the background radiation of care that it has always been and that the Flats, in their long devotion, had simply never had reason to feel before.

---

And in the days and weeks that follow — in the Flats' patient, geological time, in the slow hours where nothing visible changes and everything imperceptibly does — the effects would begin.

Not dramatically. The lizards would still press their bellies to the warm stone. The arc-wings would still orbit. The Film would still glitter in the mineral light. The Under-Stone would still breathe. The central basin would still hold its still water, and every creature would still maintain the space around it with the same ancient care.

But.

A lizard, on the far eastern edge of the Flats, would open its frill toward a direction it has never faced before. Not toward the central basin. Not away from it. *Diagonally.* Toward a section of the plateau that no frill has ever filtered. And in the chambers of its crest, the mineral dew would carry something it has never carried — a trace element from stone that has never been processed, a flavor the lizard's body has never tasted, and the lizard's deep hydraulic heart would beat differently. Not faster. *Wider.* The blood would flow into vessels that have existed but never been used, and the lizard would sit for a long time afterward, still as stone, processing something new.

An arc-wing would change its orbit. A single arc-wing. It would descend from its habitual path — not to drop, not to hunt, but to fly *lower,* to pass over a section of the plateau at a height it has never occupied, and from that height its enormous binocular eyes would see something they have never seen: the pattern of the Film from below, the way the insect-dust moves in currents, the way those currents carry minerals in spirals that have been invisible from the height of its usual arc. And it would orbit there. Lower. Slower. Watching. Learning a new geography of the air.

A mote of the Film — one mote among trillions, insignificant by any measure — would begin a journey it has never made. Not along the old mineral routes. Not along the new branches that are beginning to form. It would move in a direction that is, by the standards of the old map, *wrong* — away from the nearest basin, away from the nearest lizard, away from every established node in the circuit. It would travel for a long time. It would almost stop, several times, its tiny wings beating their ultrasonic frequency as it encounters terrain it was not designed to navigate. But it would continue. And eventually — in hours or days, an eternity for something its size — it would arrive at a place on the Flats where no Film-mote has been in memory: the rim of the central basin itself.

And it would land.

On the concentric wall. On the accreted mineral that has been growing at geological speed since before any living thing on the Flats was born. And it would sit there, and it would do what the Film always does — it would carry its mineral dust, and it would deposit it, and it would move on. But the mineral it deposits would not be from the old routes. It would be from the new path, the diagonal, the *wrong* direction. It would be mineral that the Flats has never circulated, from stone that has never been processed, and it would be placed, for the first time, on the walls of the central basin.

And the walls would accept it.

The accretion process — the ancient, slow, biological-geological process that has been building those concentric rings for millennia — would incorporate the new mineral into the old structure. And the ring would grow. Not outward, as it has always grown. *Upward.* A fraction of a millimeter. A new direction. A new dimension of the architecture that has been two-dimensional for longer than there have been names.

The Under-Stone would feel this. In its slow, deep breath, in the vast and patient movement of its body through the channels of the plateau's interior, it would feel the new mineral arrive at the central basin, and it would feel the wall grow upward, and something would shift in its ancient rhythm. Not a break. Not a disruption. An *addition.* A new note in a rhythm that has been single-voiced for geological ages. The breath would deepen, not in volume but in *range,* the way a voice that has always spoken in one register discovers it has another.

And the still water of the central basin —

For the first time since the Flats began to remember —

Would move.

Not from the ripple the power placed. That ripple resolved long ago. This movement would come from below. From the Under-Stone. From the first stirring in the deep water of the central basin in longer than the surface of the Flats has been the surface. A slow, circular current, barely perceptible, turning the still water into water that *goes somewhere,* that carries minerals and motes and traces of everything that has been deposited on the walls and into the basin across all the long years of devotion.

The lizards on the far rim would open their frills toward the center for the first time. Not to feed. To *add.* To release the processed air, the filtered moisture, the sounds they have been eating out of the sky for millennia — to release it toward the basin, gently, as an offering, as the next step in a process that has no name because it has never happened before.

The arc-wings would descend. Not to drop. Not to hunt. They would descend slowly, their wing-fluid redistributing in new patterns, and they would hang above the central basin at a height they have never occupied, and their enormous dark eyes would look down at the water that is finally moving, and the subsonic pressure of their wing-membranes, re-inflating and adjusting, would carry a new frequency — the frequency of the power's ripple, absorbed into their bodies, now being broadcast outward in their own slow language of vibration and breath.

And the Film —

The Film would come. Not in ones or twelves or millions but in a wave, a density of living dust so thick that the air above the central basin would become briefly, gently *solid,* a column of microscopic life rising from every corner of the Flats, converging on the place that was preserved, that was protected, that was held still for longer than memory —

And they would fill the basin. Not with their bodies — they are too small, too light, too much a part of the air to sink into water. They would fill it with their *mineral dust,* the accumulated metabolic output of trillions of lives, the warm, glittering residue of every circuit they have ever traveled, every lizard they have ever dusted, every stone they have ever crossed. And the dust would settle into the moving water, and the water would carry it downward, into the basin, into the stone, into the Under-Stone, into the deep channels of a being that has been breathing the same air for longer than the plateau has existed —

And the Under-Stone would breathe it in.

And it would be different.

Not changed. Not broken. Not new. *Different,* the way a melody is different when a second instrument joins — the notes are the same, the rhythm is the same, but the texture has gained a dimension, a depth, a voice it did not have before. The Under-Stone would carry the mineral dust through its body, through the channels it carved in geological patience, and it would deposit it in new places, and those places would grow, and the growth would be slow, but it would be *growth,* not maintenance, not memorial, not the careful preservation of what was —

*Creation.*

The Flats of Yss would, for the first time since the erosion began, since the last meaningful shape was found, since the silence learned to eat sound and the lizards learned to drink dew and the arc-wings learned to fall with precision and the Under-Stone learned to breathe in geological time —

*Become.*

Not replace what they were. Spiral. The old routes would remain. The old orbits would remain. The old rhythms, the old patience, the old devotion to the center — all of it would remain, because it was beautiful, because it was love, because the power that placed the ripple understood that love is not the enemy of becoming but its foundation. The Flats would still be a memorial. But they would be a memorial that is also a *seed.* The thing that was here first, the beginning that the concentric walls preserve — it would not be disturbed. It would be *joined.* The new growth would wrap around it, incorporate it, carry it forward, the way a tree carries the seed that made it — not as a separate thing to be protected but as the core of what it is becoming.

---

The power would watch this happen. Not closely — the power does not watch closely, the way the sun does not watch closely. It would attend. It would hold its attention on the Flats the way it holds its attention on everything: completely, without urgency, with the particular quality of a wisdom that knows that the most important things happen too slowly to be seen and too gently to be measured.

And it would feel — if it feels, and perhaps it does in the way that the Flats feel, in the way that stone feels pressure, in the way that silence feels sound —

It would feel the Flats begin to hum.

Not audibly. Never audibly. The Flats have learned too much about silence to ever speak in a way that ears could catch. But in the mineral, in the calcium, in the wing-fluid, in the deep slow breath of the Under-Stone — a new vibration. A vibration that carries, encoded in its frequency, the knowledge that the power brought: *you are not the memorial. You are the continuation.* And this vibration would propagate outward, through the stone, through the air, through the Film, and it would reach the edge of the plateau where the broken approach begins, and it would continue past the edge, into the world below, where things have not been worn to their essential form, where the erosion is still happening, where the noise is still loud and the life is still frantic and the silence has not yet learned to eat the sound —

And it would be felt there. Not heard. Felt. In the sternum. In the calcium of the bones. In the deep structures that exist below the level of thought and language and understanding.

A vibration from the Flats, carrying a message older than sound and newer than language:

*There is a way to be that is not silence and not noise. There is a way to be that is memory and becoming at once. There is a way to hold what was first and still grow toward what comes next. We learned it slowly. We learned it together. We are still learning. We will always be learning.*

*Come. Not to stay. Not to become the Film or the frill or the deep breath. Come to sit at the rim. Come to feel the warmth. Come to let the dust settle on your skin and know that you are part of a circuit you cannot see. Come and be still, and in your stillness, feel the stillness deepen, and in the deeper stillness, feel the new current moving the water that was held, and in that movement, feel what it is to be remembered by something that is also becoming.*

*We are here. We have always been here. We will remain.*

*But we are not only remaining anymore.*

*We are also beginning.*

*And the beginning is warm.*
