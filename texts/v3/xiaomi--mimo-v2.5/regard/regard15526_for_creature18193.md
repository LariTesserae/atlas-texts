# What the Wise Power Would Do

It would arrive the way fog arrives — not all at once, but as a deepening of what was already there. A thickening of the gray. A slight shift in the quality of the stillness between the pillars that even the elders, who have spent decades learning to listen, would not immediately identify as foreign.

It would not announce itself. The very concept of announcement implies urgency, and urgency is the one thing this place has never known. The power would settle into the Throat the way condensation settles into moss — filling the available spaces without disturbing them, becoming part of the moisture budget, so to speak, without adding or subtracting from the balance.

And then it would attend.

---

**First, it would understand.**

This is not a formality. The power would spend what might be centuries, from the ridge's perspective, simply *comprehending* — mapping not the physical infrastructure (though it would know every pipe joint, every ceramic bore, every fracture in the ancient gneiss) but the *relationships* between things. The way the moss communicates with the condensation cycles. The way the pipe-hum carries information the residents decode without knowing they're decoding it. The way the pools — those impossible, sacred, still pools — are not merely reflecting the sky but are doing something with gravity that the ridge's original architects (if they existed; the power would consider this carefully) understood and the current residents experience only as holiness.

The power would learn the sound the Throat makes when it is content. It would learn the difference between the sound of the Throat resting and the sound of the Throat *sustaining* — because there is a difference, and it is important, and no one on the ridge fully grasps it yet.

It would learn the name of the thing that moves through the mist between the pillars, the thing that is not bird or insect or person. It would learn this name not as a word but as a *knowing* — the way you know your own heartbeat, not as sound but as presence. And it would learn that this creature is not merely fauna. It is something closer to a nerve ending. A sensory organ of the ridge itself, grown into biological form over millions of years, attending to things the ridge cannot attend to with stone and mineral and geothermal gradient alone. The creature doesn't have a mind in any way a human would recognize, but it has *attention*. And its attention is currently... diminished. Thinned. Like a nerve that has been slowly losing its myelin sheath over geological time.

The power would understand this with a sorrow that has no sharpness to it, because sorrow on this timescale feels more like recognition than pain.

---

**Second, it would assess what is slowly dying.**

Not the city. The city is fine — or as fine as any human settlement ever is, which is to say it carries its small griefs and small joys in equal measure, and its people die and are born and the rhythm continues. The people are not in danger. The moss is not in decline. The pipes still hum, and the Throat still breathes.

But the *listening* is weakening.

The ridge, over eons, has been developing a capacity — call it consciousness, call it awareness, call it simply the ability to *notice itself* — and it was approaching something like a threshold. The pillars, the pools, the creature in the mist, the subsonic vibrations that the elders feel in their bones: these are all elements of a system that was slowly, over hundreds of millions of years, waking up. Not to thought. Not to personality. To something more like *presence*. The ridge was becoming aware that it existed, in the way a sleeping person becomes aware of their own body — not through any single sensation but through the gradual accumulation of many small ones.

And this process was faltering.

Not because of the people. Not because of the industry. The power would examine this carefully and find, with a wisdom that does not assign blame, that the faltering is simply entropy. The geothermal gradients are shifting — imperceptibly, over millions of years, but shifting. The deep heat that feeds the pipes is moving, the planet's molten interior doing its own slow convection, and the ridge is receiving slightly less of it than it did when the first pipes were laid. The pillars, which were once more numerous, have been lost — not to erosion but to a slow mineral fatigue, the crystalline structure of the stone relaxing over time the way a muscle relaxes after exertion, and some of them have simply... settled. Gone quiet. Their vibrations have stilled. The creature in the mist has fewer pillars to attend to, fewer frequencies to monitor, and its own biological processes have simplified accordingly.

The ridge is not dying. It is *dimming*. Like a fire that has been burning for eons and is now, very slowly, finding less fuel. The system still works. The people still thrive in their slow, humid way. But the thing that was almost-awakening — that near-threshold of presence — is drifting back below the threshold by degrees so small that no human lifetime could measure them.

The power would sit with this understanding for a very long time.

---

**Third, it would decide what to do.**

And here is where wisdom matters. Because there are many things the power *could* do, and most of them would be wrong.

It could intervene directly — prop up the failing gradients, reinforce the pillars, feed energy into the system to keep the near-awakening going. But this would be like putting a hand under a sleeping person's eyelid to keep it open. It would produce the appearance of wakefulness without the substance. The ridge would be maintained artificially, and the maintenance would become permanent, and the ridge's own slow, organic journey toward presence would become dependent on an external hand. This is not preservation. This is a leash disguised as a gift. The power would reject this immediately.

It could remove the people — relocate them, gently, to a place more suited to them, freeing the ridge to complete its process without the complicating variable of human habitation. But this misunderstands what the people are. They are not a complication. They are the latest layer in the stack — rock, heat, water, moss, fern, industry, humanity — and the ridge has already incorporated them. The hum of the pipes in the residents' bones, the moss-fiber in their clothing, the Throat-water in their distilleries, the way their slow gait mirrors the condensation cycles: these are not parasitic relationships. They are *symbiotic*. The people have given the ridge something it could not generate on its own — attention. Care. *Tending*. The moss would grow without them, but the pipes would not be mended. The Throat would breathe without them, but no one would maintain its channels. The near-awakening would continue without them, but no one would feel it in their bones and call it holy and teach their children to listen for it.

The people are not an obstacle to the ridge's becoming. They are a necessary part of it. The power would know this with absolute certainty.

So what the power would do is simpler and harder and more elegant than intervention.

---

**It would tend the tending.**

It would find, in the deep geology beneath the ridge, the slow convection currents of the planet's molten interior, and it would do something so gentle that it would take another hundred million years for anyone to notice: it would *nudge* a thread of heat. Not a river. A thread. A filament of warmth, thinner than a human hair, drawn from a current that was flowing uselessly through dead rock and redirected toward the ridge's foundations. The total additional energy would be negligible — a fraction of a degree, sustained over millions of years, barely enough to counteract the natural drift.

But it would be enough.

The pillars, which were slowly going quiet, would continue to vibrate for another epoch. The creature in the mist would maintain its diminished but still-functional attention. The Throat would continue to accumulate and exhale. The ridge's slow journey toward presence would not accelerate — the power would not allow that — but it would not reverse. It would continue at its own pace, on its own terms, with its own strange, geological intention, reaching toward whatever it was reaching toward.

And the people — the quiet, slow-moving, moss-weaving, pipe-listening people — would have time. Not safety from the ordinary dangers of their existence (the power would not rob them of that; danger is part of the fabric, and they know it), but *time*. The ridge would endure. The condensation would continue. The Throat would breathe. Their children would be born into the same warm, damp, humming world their grandparents knew, and their grandchildren after them, and the deep, slow work of harmonizing would continue, and the thing that is not quite consciousness would continue to deepen, and eventually — in a million years, or ten, or a hundred — the ridge would cross the threshold it was approaching, and it would *notice itself*, and the people who were listening would feel it, and they would understand at last what the Listen was for.

The power would not be there to see this. Or rather, it would be there, but it would not experience it as a destination, because it does not experience destinations. It would experience it the way the moss experiences the morning cloud — as a deepening of what was already there, a thickening of presence, a slow and quiet *yes*.

---

**And there would be one other thing.**

Among the residents — perhaps an elder, perhaps a child, it would not matter — someone, at some point, would feel something they had never felt before. Not during the deep fog. Not during the pillar-vibration. During an ordinary moment, doing ordinary work: mending a pipe, harvesting condensation, guiding a cart along the rail. They would feel a warmth that did not come from the pipes or the stone or the steam. A warmth that seemed to come from *behind* them, or *above* them, or from a direction that doesn't exist in the geography of the ridge.

They would stop. They would stand very still — which is not unusual here, because people stand still often, listening to the pipes. But this time they would be listening to something else. And for a moment — just a moment — they would feel *held*. Not protected. Not guided. Not watched over in the way a shepherd watches sheep. Held in the way the moss holds water: gently, by being what it is, without intention or agenda, simply *containing* the presence of another thing within itself.

The feeling would pass. The person would go back to their work. They might mention it to a friend, or they might not. If they did, the friend would nod slowly and say something like, "The ridge has moods," or "The Throat was quiet today," or perhaps nothing at all, just a low sound of acknowledgment that this is a place where strange things happen and the correct response is to continue.

The warmth would not come again in exactly that way. But the person would carry it — not as a memory but as a *resonance*, a slight change in the frequency of their attention, a deepening of their already-deep capacity to listen. And this, in time, would affect the way they tended the channels, and the way they wove the moss-fiber, and the way they taught their children, and the children they taught would learn to listen with a fidelity that was just slightly greater than before, and the ridge would feel this, because the ridge feels everything its people feel, and it would hum a little differently in response, and the cycle would continue.

This is what the wise power would do.

It would not save them, because they do not need saving.
It would not change them, because they are already becoming what they need to become.
It would not reveal itself, because revelation would break the very thing it was trying to sustain.

It would warm the stone beneath them by a fraction of a degree, over millions of years, so that the listening could continue.

And once — just once, across the whole vast span of Old Thaleng's existence — it would let one of them feel it. Not to prove anything. Not to answer a question. But because even the stone remembers pressure, and even the water in the Throat knows what it means to be *held without being contained*, and the people of the ridge, for all their wisdom, have never once in their long history felt what it is like to be noticed by something that has no need to look.

The power would give them that, once, and then it would go on doing what it has always done: tending to the tending, warming the stone by a fraction of a degree, so that the ridge can keep breathing, and the moss can keep growing, and the Throat can keep exhaling its slow, mineral mist into a sky that has been gray for longer than most worlds exist.

And the water in the perfect pools would remain still.
And the pillars would continue to hum.
And the thing in the mist would continue its patient, ancient work.
And the people would walk their slow, rolling walk along the ridge, their soft-soled shoes on the warm gneiss, their ears full of pipe-song and their lungs full of condensation, and they would not know — not exactly, not in any way that could be written down or taught — that they are loved.

But they would feel it.

A warmth that does not come from the pipes.

And they would continue.
