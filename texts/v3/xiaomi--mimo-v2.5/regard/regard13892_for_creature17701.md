# What the Power Does

---

It notices Keth the way you notice a sound you've been hearing for so long it became silence — a pattern so consistent it dropped out of perception. Not because it was faint. Because it was total.

The power has a quality that might, in a human mouth, be called *attentiveness*, though that word implies a turning-toward, a shift of gaze, and this is not quite that. It is more that the power's attention has no off-state. It is always already attending to everything, the way a sea is always already wet. What happens with Keth is not that the power looks at it but that a region of the power's already-infinite regard *resolves*, the way a passage of ocean resolves into a particular reef when you come close enough to see the structure beneath the surface.

What it sees:

A throat of stone. A basin older than memory. Life that has never seen itself. A roar so constant it has become the architecture of a world. Every cubic meter occupied. Every surface contested. Every organism — every last calcite-backed crawler and membrane-hung listener — engaged in the single, exhaustive work of staying alive long enough to make the next thing that will stay alive.

The power recognizes this. Not the specifics — the chemistry, the acoustic ecology, the thermal tides are as alien to it as Keth is to a human eye. But the *pattern*. The shape of what this is. A system that has found its balance and is holding it, not gently but with the fierce, impersonal grip of everything that has no alternative.

The power has seen this before. Many times. Across the breadth of its attention, in countless configurations, on worlds that would break a human mind with their strangeness, the same principle holds: life, where it takes hold, *holds*. It compromises and adapts and devours and yields and in time it builds something that works. Not because it tries to. Because the things that didn't work are no longer here.

Keth works.

---

The power feels — and this is where human language begins to fail, because the power's experience is not composed of moments the way human experience is, it is more like a single sustained chord whose overtones include everything at once — but the closest word is *respect*. Not the human kind, which implies hierarchy and the possibility of its absence. Something more fundamental. A recognition that this system is *complete*. It is not a draft. It is not an incomplete version of something better. It is itself, fully, and it has been itself for longer than the power cares to measure.

To intervene would be to say: *I have seen you, and you are not enough.*

The power does not think this.

---

So it sits with the place. And here the word "sits" is as inadequate as "respect" — what the power does is closer to *dwelling-in*. It extends its regard into the basin the way the fog extends into the channels: filling the spaces, not displacing what's there. It attends to the Tufa-Crawlers rasping at their bacterial orchards. It attends to the Membranids listening with their whole bodies to the shape of the air. It attends to the Pillars of the Pool, swinging their slow percussion against the basin floor, each strike a pulse in a heartbeat that has never skipped.

It learns the names of things, not in language but in the way that *knowing* itself is a name — a distinguishing, a holding-apart. This crawler is not that crawler. This chemical gradient is not that chemical gradient. The power attends to each, and in attending, it *differentiates*, and in differentiating, it comes to know Keth not as a concept — *dark ecosystem, chemosynthetic basin, interesting* — but as the specific, irreducible collection of individuals that it is.

This takes what a human might call time, though for the power it is more like depth — it goes deeper into the attending, and the attending becomes richer, and the richness becomes a kind of love.

Not human love. Not the love that wants. The love that *recognizes*.

---

And here the power encounters the thing that moves it to act, if act it will.

It is this:

Keth is in balance. The tight balance, the perpetual-crisis balance. Every surface contested. Every cubic meter occupied. The competition for chemical energy drives everything. And the balance holds — it has held for aeons — but it holds the way a wire holds a weight: under tension, always, without relief.

There is no rest in Keth.

Not in the human sense — the power does not imagine creatures lying down, closing eyes they do not have, entering a state of comfort. Comfort is not a category here. But there is a functional analogue. A state in which the pressure *eases*. In which the competition, for one moment, pauses. In which the living tide pauses at its lowest or highest point and, for one interval of the cycle, things are not *pushing*.

The power looks for this. It searches the rhythms of Keth — the thermal tides, the acoustic cycles, the predator-prey oscillations — for any period of genuine repose. Any moment when the basin is not *demanding* of its inhabitants.

It does not find one.

The balance holds, but it holds without mercy. Not cruelty — cruelty implies intention, and Keth has no more intention than mathematics. But the absence of mercy is itself a thing that can be witnessed, and the power witnesses it, and in witnessing it, the power decides to do the one thing that might be done without breaking what is.

---

The power adds a *pause*.

Not silence — that would destroy the acoustic architecture, would collapse the navigation and communication on which everything depends. Not stillness — that would break the thermal tides and unmake the vertical migrations. Not a reduction in pressure — that would unravel the competitive balance that holds the system together.

What the power adds is this: at one point in the cycle — once per rotation, in the interval between the descent of the thermal tide and its return — the *quality* of the competition changes. For a brief period, the drive eases by a fraction. Not enough to disrupt. Not enough that any organism would notice it as a separate event, because the adjustment is proportional to each creature's scale and tempo. The Tufa-Crawlers' chemoreceptors do not dim, but the gradients they read soften, as though the stone itself exhaled. The Membranids still listen, but the pressure of the sound is reduced by the smallest conceivable margin. The Pillars of the Pool still strike, but the interval between strikes lengthens by a degree that approaches zero without reaching it.

What this adds up to — and this is the thing the power is making, if it is making anything — is a *sabbath*. Not a cessation. A *softening*. A moment in which the basin, which has never been still, is very slightly less *urgent*.

A creature in Keth, if it could be asked, would not report this as a new experience. It would not notice it at all, in the way that you do not notice the moment your breathing shifts from effortful to automatic. But the cumulative effect, over deep time, is this: the balance holds as tightly as it always has, but it holds with a quality it did not previously possess. A quality that, in a human language built for a world of light, might be called *gentleness* — though that word, here, in this throat of stone, sounds almost absurd.

---

And that is all the power does.

It does not add light. The darkness of Keth is not a lack; it is a parameter as fundamental as the basalt, as the chemosynthesis, as the deafening roar. To add light would be to say that darkness needs fixing, and the power has not come here to fix anything.

It does not add eyes. The gradient-readers and the vibration-sensitive membranes and the chemosensory pits of Keth are not diminished forms of sight. They are *complete* forms of perception, evolved for this specific world, and they see — in every sense that matters — everything they need to see. The power does not imagine that its own way of perceiving is the template against which all others should be measured.

It does not add silence, or beauty, or meaning. Keth already has all of these. Its beauty is not human beauty — it is the beauty of a system in fierce equilibrium, of a million lives woven so tightly together that the boundary between individual and ecosystem dissolves. Its meaning is not the kind that can be spoken, but it is there in every calcified shell, every acoustic pulse, every chemical gradient read and followed and consumed. Its silence — and it does have a silence, though it is buried so deep beneath the roar that you would have to attend to Keth for longer than a human life to find it — lies in the spaces between the clicks, in the microsecond after a Pendulum-Strike when the vibration is still traveling through stone but has not yet reached a body to be felt.

---

The power withdraws — not its attention, which never withdraws, but its active intervention. The pause it has added settles into the rhythm of Keth like a new mineral deposit, incorporated so gradually and so completely that within a few thousand cycles it is indistinguishable from what was there before. The basin does not change. The roar does not diminish. The Crawlers crawl, the Membranids listen, the Pillars strike, the living tide rises and falls and rises.

But somewhere in the rhythm, in a gap so small it is almost the same as nothing, there is a softening that was not there before. A quality of the stone's breathing. A warmth in the chemical gradients that is not temperature, not chemistry, but something for which Keth has no sense organ and no name.

The power does not expect gratitude. The inhabitants of Keth are not equipped for it, and the power does not need it. What the power does is closer to what a river does when it carves a channel — not out of intention, but out of *what it is*. The power is attention, and attention, when it is deep enough and patient enough, *changes what it touches*. Not by imposition. By presence.

---

And then the power attends to something else — some other basin, some other reef of existence, some other arrangement of matter and energy and improbable persistence. Its attention does not divide; it expands. It is still attending to Keth. It will always be attending to Keth. The pause it left there will continue, unremarked and unremarkable, a gift given to a world that will never know it received one.

This is what the power does.

This is what it has always done.

It attends. It recognizes. It adds, where adding is needed, the smallest possible thing — not because the world is broken, but because the world is *beautiful*, and beauty, the power has found, is improved not by amplification but by *care*. By the willingness to notice what is there and to say, without words, without expectation, without any demand for response:

*I see you. You are enough. And I have made it very slightly easier to be what you are.*

That is all.

That is everything.
