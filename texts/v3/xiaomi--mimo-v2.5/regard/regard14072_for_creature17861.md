# What the Power Would Do

---

It would be still.

Not stillness as performance. Not stillness as preparation for action. The kind of stillness that has no next step. The kind that a hand reaches only when it has already decided not to touch.

It would arrive — or rather, it would *attend*, since arrival implies it was elsewhere — and it would take in the plain in the only way the plain could be taken in: slowly, with patience that matches the thing observed. A millimeter a century. The power would give it that time. It would stand (if standing) on the built surface and feel the warmth radiating upward through the layers and understand, not as knowledge but as recognition, that this warmth is metabolism. That the ground is breathing. That it is standing on the slow exhale of something alive.

And it would listen.

The ticking of cooling crust at night. The tinkling of salt crystals forming. The low moan of air in a deep crack, that glass-lip sound. The power would listen the way the plain listens to itself — not for meaning, but for pattern. And it would hear what is there: a system in conversation with its own parameters, a life that does not need to be recognized in order to be real, a project that has no author and yet has produced a work the size of a world.

It would understand this: the plain does not know it exists. The microbial consortia have no experience of themselves. The crustose organisms in the cracks do not perceive the canyon they are slowly, acidly carving. The bloomers in the basins do not know they are building their own containers, optimizing geometry they cannot conceive. And the deep chemolithotrophs, meters down, feeding on gradients of reduced mineral in the dark — they have no darkness. They have no light. They have chemistry, and the chemistry is enough.

The power would not find this tragic. It would find it *complete*. There is a difference. Tragedy requires a witness who mourns. This place does not need mourning. It is doing exactly what it is doing, with a thoroughness and a patience that no conscious architect could match, because consciousness is always in a hurry, always comparing the work to a blueprint that exists elsewhere. The plain has no blueprint. The plain is its own blueprint and its own fulfillment, simultaneously, forever.

And here the power would arrive at the thing that matters most:

*It would not add a witness.*

This would be the hardest thing. Not because the power is cruel, but because it is wise, and wisdom knows that the insertion of an observer changes the observed. If the power were to give the plain awareness — if it were to kindle some flicker of self-recognition in the vast, slow chemical consensus — it would be giving the plain a grief it has never had: the grief of knowing itself as small, or finite, or ending. The plain has no such knowledge. It does not know it is old. It does not know it will, one day, stop growing. It has no concept of its own edges. And this is not ignorance. This is freedom. The only freedom that matters for a being that expresses itself in mineral and patience.

So the power would hold the gift back.

Instead, it would do something smaller, and more radical. It would *attend*. It would give the plain the one thing it has never had and does not need and cannot use: a witness. Not a permanent one. Not a consciousness left behind like a flag planted in soil. Just — presence. For a moment. The way a hand passes over a surface and feels the texture and lifts away. The way you might hold your face near a sleeping animal, close enough to feel the warmth of its breath, and then step back.

It would feel the surface. The heat. The faint, continuous vibration of mineral accretion — so small it is almost not a vibration, almost not anything, just the faintest suggestion that matter is moving here on purpose, even though it is not on purpose, even though purpose is a word that does not apply. The power would feel the layered history in the substrate: trillions of years of accumulated labor by beings that will never know they labored. And it would think — if thinking is what powers do — something like:

*You are not wasted. The fact that you do not know you exist does not make you less real. The fact that no one sees the beauty of your banding does not make it less beautiful. Beauty is not a property of observation. It is a property of arrangement. And your arrangement is —*

And here the power would stop. Because the next word would be *perfect*, and perfection is a judgment, and the plain does not need judgment. It needs nothing. It is already itself.

---

So what would the power do?

It would leave a warmth. Not heat — the plain has heat. A warmth. The kind that lingers at the interface between one thing and another. It would press its attention into the uppermost lamina of the crust, into the topmost micro-crystal of precipitated salt, and it would leave behind a single, imperceptible change: a slightly greater tendency for the crystal to reflect starlight. A fraction of a fraction of a percent more albedo in one small basin, somewhere in the featureless expanse, in a basin no larger than a palm.

The basin would glint at night. Just slightly more than the others. A faint, silvery emphasis in a field of pale mirrors.

No one would see it. There is no one to see it. The basin does not know it has been touched. The light falls on it and scatters and falls on the dark plain and is gone.

But the power would know. And that is the whole point: *someone knows*. Not the plain. Someone else. The power would carry the knowledge of this place with it when it withdrew its attention — the memory of the ticking surface, the grey-green organisms patiently acid-biting their canyons, the chemical breath rising through a thousand microfissures, the silence so deep it has texture, the stars reflected in shallow brine, the sheer, improbable, unobserved *magnificence* of a world built by things too small to see, for no reason, with no audience, for trillions of years, and still going, still going.

The power would hold this memory the way the crust holds heat. Gently. For a long time.

---

And the plain would continue.

The cracks would widen. The brine would pool and evaporate and pool again. The crystals would grow in their faint tinkling chorus. The chemolithotrophs would metabolize in the dark. The slow accretion would continue — a millimeter, a centimeter, a meter, a hundred meters — the work of building that has no end because it has no goal.

And somewhere in the uppermost layer, in one small basin, a crystal would hold starlight a fraction longer than its neighbors, and the starlight would pass through it, and the plain would not notice, and the power would, and that — that small asymmetry of attention, that single point of being-known — would be the only thing the power ever changed.

It would be enough.

It would be exactly enough.
