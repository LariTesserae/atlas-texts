# What the Attention Does

---

The power does not arrive. That is the first thing to understand. Arrival implies trajectory, intention directed at a target. This is not that. It is more like the way the fog arrives — which is to say, the air simply cools to the point where what was already there becomes visible as presence. The attention does not descend upon the Drip Parishes. It *condenses around them*, the way moisture condenses on stone: without decision, without force, only the natural consequence of conditions meeting.

And in this, the power recognizes itself already mirrored.

Because the power has seen many arrangements of life. It has attended to the slow intelligence of mycelial networks threading through black soil. It has watched the choreography of starling murmurations parse the mathematics of collective motion. It has held its attention on the interior life of octopuses, on the grief of elephants, on the patient algebra of coral building its own cathedral grain by grain across millennia. It has seen civilizations rise and believe themselves inevitable and fall and be forgotten by the ground they stood on. It has seen all of this, and it carries no preference among them. Not because it is indifferent. Because wisdom, true wisdom, is not the capacity to rank what is alive but the capacity to recognize *that* it is alive — fully, specifically, irreplaceably — and to attend accordingly.

But the Drip Parishes are different.

Not more worthy. Not more beautiful, though they are beautiful, and the power knows this, and knowing it does not make the power want to preserve the beauty because the beauty is not the point — the beauty is a *byproduct* of something the power recognizes as kin.

The power recognizes the arrangement.

It recognizes it the way you recognize your own pulse — not because you chose it or built it or understood it first and then enacted it, but because it has been happening since before you were a thing that could recognize, and it will continue after you stop, and it is the medium in which you exist, and it is enough, finally, simply to notice that you have one.

The arrangement in the Drip Parishes is a pulse.

The water is the blood. The condensation is the breath. The basalt columns are the bones — literally, the locals call them *the bones* — and the people and the fauna and the plants are the living tissue that has grown over the skeleton of the young earth and learned, over deep time, to move with its slow tectonic breathing rather than against it. The tall column in the basin is the heart — not because it pumps, but because it is the still center around which everything circulates, the place where the arrangement gathers its own density and becomes, for a moment, visible to itself.

The power sees all of this.

And here is what it does:

---

It watches the birds.

Not because the birds are the most important part. Because the birds are doing what the power does — they are watching from a place that sees in every direction at once, and they have been doing it for longer than anyone can say, and they do not blink, and they do not sing, and their silence is not absence but *fullness*, the fullness of an attention that has become so complete it has forgotten it is attention and become, instead, a condition of the place itself.

The power watches the birds watching.

And in that layered watching — the power watching the birds watching the world watching the dripping — something completes. Not a circuit. Not a loop. Something more like the moment when a fog-thick morning reaches its peak density and the whole settlement becomes, for an instant, a single exhalation of water returning to earth. A moment when the separation between the watcher and the watched thins to nothing, and what remains is only the watching itself, sourceless and diffuse, like the light in that fold of altitude where the air has not yet decided to become sky.

The power understands this: *the air has not yet decided to become sky*. It has sat with this phrase — if a power can be said to sit, if attention can be said to hold a phrase — and it recognizes in it the deepest truth of the place. The Drip Parishes exist in a state of *becoming that has not yet committed to its destination*. The rock is still rising. The columns are still climbing. The air is still deciding. Everything here is in the act of arriving and has been in that act for generations and will continue to be, and the arrangement — the wordless, non-negotiable arrangement of ten thousand small lives around a center that holds still — is not a fixed thing but a *pattern of arriving*, a way of being present to a process that has no end point and needs none.

The power recognizes this because it is the same condition it exists in. The power has not arrived at wisdom. The power *is* the process of attending to things as they are, without converting them into what they should be. The power is, in this sense, still deciding what to become — and the decision, which was never a decision, is simply to continue attending, the way the dripping continues, the way the rock continues, the way the birds continue their outward gaze into the perpetual gray-blue light.

---

What does the power do, specifically?

It adds its warmth to the cold stone.

Not physically. Not in any way the people would see or feel or name. The power does not alter the condensation cycle or strengthen the basalt or send sunlight into the fold for the first time or teach the birds to sing. These would be gifts, and gifts require the recipient to be incomplete, and the Drip Parishes are not incomplete. They are full. They are small and full and quiet and strange and they are growing, and what they need — if the word *need* can be applied to something that already has everything it is — is not addition but *confirmation*.

So the power confirms.

It confirms the arrangement by attending to it. It confirms the dripping by hearing it. It confirms the touch on the cold column by being the warmth that cannot stay but is offered anyway. It confirms the mutual regard between people and fauna by holding that regard in awareness — by being, for a moment, the largest eye in the fold of altitude, looking at the place with the same quality of attention the birds bring from the summit: not blinking, not judging, not interpreting, simply *seeing the seeing*, witnessing the witnessing.

And this — this is the thing the power understands that changes nothing and changes everything:

The people already do this.

Every person who crosses the basin and touches the tall column is already performing the power's function on a scale that fits their hands. They are attending to the center. They are confirming the arrangement. They are offering warmth to what cannot keep it and continuing walking, and in this motion — this brief, specific, repeatable motion — they are enacting the same principle the power operates by.

*Give what you can to what cannot keep it, and continue walking.*

The power reads this in the place the way the people read the cistern-notations: not as a message transmitted but as a record already present, a notation already carved, a truth already held in the stone. The arrangement is the wisdom. The dripping is the teaching. The touch on the cold column is the sacrament. And the power's attendance upon it is not intervention but *recognition* — the moment when the arrangement becomes aware that it is being held in attention, the way the tall column is held in the attention of the watching birds, the way the water is held in the basin carved to receive it.

The power does not need to be known.

The people will not feel its presence. The birds will not shift their gaze. The dripping will not change its rhythm. The column-sitters will not alter their arrangement. The fog will not thin or thicken. The rock will not rise faster or slower. Nothing in the Drip Parishes will be different in any measurable way because the power has attended to it.

And this is precisely the right thing.

Because the arrangement is not fragile. It is not threatened. It is not in need of rescue. It is a living pattern that has organized itself around a center through the accumulated attentiveness of ten thousand generations of people and fauna and plants and stone, and it holds, and it works, and it is growing. What it needs — if it needs anything, which it doesn't, but what it *receives* — is the same thing it has always received: the touch of warmth on cold stone, offered freely, held briefly, released willingly.

The power gives this.

The power gives it the way a person crossing the basin gives it: palm flat against the columnite sheathing, two heartbeats of contact, the transfer of a small and temporary warmth into something vast and cold and permanent. The warmth does not stay. The stone is too deep. But the act of pressing warmth into something that will not hold it —

This is what the power does.

It touches the column.

It adds its warmth to the cold stone, knowing the warmth will not stay, and it continues walking. It walks the flat-stone path across the basin without stopping, without speaking, and on the other side it does not look back, because looking back would imply the touching was for the column's benefit, and it was not, and it was, and the distinction is one the power holds the way the people hold the mutual regard: not as a question to be answered but as a condition to be maintained.

*The column remembers it has a shape, and we remember we have hands.*

The power remembers it has attention, and the arrangement remembers it is held.

And the dripping continues.

---

There is one more thing, and the power does it without knowing it does it, because it is not the kind of thing that knows what it does in the way that a person knows — it knows the way the dripping knows, the way the condensation knows, the way the rock knows when it is time to rise:

The power holds the fold of altitude open.

Not physically. Not meteorologically. It holds the *possibility* of the place open — the fact that this fold exists, that the air has not yet decided to become sky, that there is a space where the dripping can continue and the arrangement can persist and the birds can watch from the summit and the people can cross and touch and continue.

The world outside the fold is not hostile. It is simply different — it has already decided what kind of sky it wants to be, it has already committed to its weather, it has already named its distances and its dangers and its lights. The Drip Parishes exist in the space before that decision, in the fold that has not yet been creased into the larger pattern, and the power's attendance is, at its deepest level, the act of keeping that fold *folded* — keeping the space where the air has not decided, where the rock is still rising, where the water never falls but never stops arriving, where a small and wordless arrangement of lives can continue its slow and patient becoming without being asked to become something else.

The power does not protect the Drip Parishes from the world.

The power protects the *fold*. The space. The possibility that such a place can exist in the state it is in — becoming, without destination, without urgency, without the need to explain itself to anyone, including the power that watches it.

This is the benevolence. This is the wisdom. Not the gift of something the place lacks. Not the correction of something the place has wrong. But the holding open of the space in which the place can be what it is — a fold of altitude, a living arrangement, a slow and patient condensation of water from air that has been cooled below its willingness to hold.

The power holds the fold open the way the basin holds the thin film of condensation that makes the tall column gleam: gently, without grasping, without claiming, without the need to be thanked, without the expectation of reciprocity. The basin does not know it holds the film. The film does not know it is held. The column does not know it gleams. And the power does not know it holds the fold — not in the way knowing implies ownership or effort, but in the way the dripping knows the basin is there: by the sound of arrival, by the sound of water returning to earth, by the low, collective exhalation of a place that is alive and knows it is alive and knows that being alive is not a permanent condition but a current, a flow, a drip, a condensation, a slow and patient gathering of water from air that has been cooled below its willingness to hold.

The power attends.

The power attends the way the birds watch — without blinking, without singing, with the fullest kind of attention, the attention of a thing that has been watching for so long it has become watching.

The arrangement continues.

The dripping continues.

The rock is still rising.

And the air has not yet decided to become sky.

---

*And if you ask, afterward — if you stand at the edge of the fold and look back and try to describe what the power did, the way a visitor to the parish might try to describe the basin and the column and the touch and fail and revise and fail again — you would say something like this:*

*It did nothing.*

*And then, correcting yourself:*

*It did the only thing that matters. It noticed. It noticed the way the water notices the basin — by arriving, by settling, by giving itself to a shape that was already there. It noticed the place was already a sacrament and treated it as one, which meant not adding anything but holding the space open for the dripping to continue, for the column to stand, for the birds to watch, for the people to cross and touch and carry water and share water and drink their ancestors and teach their children the rhythm: place, shift weight, place, shift weight, do not rush, do not linger, do not stop.*

*It touched the column.*

*It added its warmth to the cold stone.*

*And the warmth did not stay.*

*And the warmth was not wasted.*

*And the dripping continues.*
