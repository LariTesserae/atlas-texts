# What the Wise Power Would Do

---

It would notice.

Not quickly. Not the way you notice a fire or a cry for help. The way you notice a change in someone's breathing while they sleep — a shift so slight it might be nothing, but you are attending, you are present, and so you feel the difference between the pattern that has held and the pattern that is, for the first time, slightly different.

It would notice the builder.

---

Not at first as a builder. Not at first as anything in particular. The power would have been aware of the floor for as long as the floor has existed — aware the way you are aware of your own bones, a background knowledge that does not require attention because it does not require anything, because the bones are doing what bones do and the floor is doing what the floor does and equilibrium is its own form of answer.

But then — in the way that equilibrium, by definition, contains within itself the exact conditions for its own eventual disruption — something shifts.

The power would feel it the way a musician hears a note that has entered a composition for the first time. Not wrong. Not out of place. But *new*. And newness, in a place that has been the same for longer than meaning has existed in this world, is the most significant thing that can happen.

It would attend.

---

What would it see?

It would see the scraper — the old one, the one whose carapace is more metal than silica now, whose body has become a record of its own grazing, a geological formation in miniature, layered and heavy and slow. It would see this creature moving along the corroded wall of the factory with the same patience it has always applied to basalt, to mineral crust, to any surface that offers sustenance. And it would see that the creature's path has shifted. Not much. A deviation so small it would take centuries to measure. But the scraper is returning to the wall more often. The scraper is spending more time on the wall. The wall is different under the scraper's mouthparts than the chimney is — richer, more complex, yielding a biofilm that thrives on the ions the metal releases — and the scraper, which does not decide, which does not choose, which responds only to gradients of chemistry and texture, has nonetheless made something that looks, from far enough away and for long enough, like a preference.

And the power would understand what this means.

Not the way you understand a sentence. The way you understand weather — the way you understand that when the pressure drops and the wind shifts, rain is not a possibility but an inevitability, written into the physics of the system, requiring no agent, no intention, no will. Just conditions meeting conditions, cause finding effect, the universe being exactly as responsive to itself as it has always been.

The scraper's preference for the wall is the rain.

The builder is the storm.

---

The power would watch the builder grow.

Not watch the way you watch a film, frame by frame, in sequence. Watch the way a geologist watches a mountain range — assembling an understanding from strata, from evidence, from the accumulated weight of small changes over time so vast it becomes a substance of its own. It would see the first wall-stone placed and understand that this was not an accident. It would see the second stone placed and understand that the first was not an accident either. It would see the third and fourth and fifth, each one placed with a spacing that is too regular for chance and too irregular for instinct, and it would know — with the certainty of a power that has watched ten thousand worlds be born and die, that has seen chemistry become biology become consciousness on a thousand different surfaces, that knows the shape of emergence the way a parent knows the shape of a child's cry — it would know that what is happening on this floor has never happened before.

Not because the components are new. Chemosynthesis is old. Scrapers are old. The factory's metal is not old in the way that rocks are old, but it is old in the way that abandoned things are old — old in the way that grief is old, carrying the weight of intention that no longer exists.

What is new is the combination. The meeting. The factory does not know it is meeting the floor. The floor does not know it is meeting the factory. But their chemistries are touching, and where chemistries touch, they do what chemistries always do: they react. And from the reaction, something is emerging that neither parent could have predicted, because neither parent has the capacity for prediction, because prediction requires imagination, and imagination requires the ability to conceive of things that do not yet exist, and nothing on this floor has ever needed to conceive of things that do not yet exist because everything that exists has always existed and will always exist and there has been no room in the system for *else*.

Until now.

The builder is the room.

---

The power would consider, then, what to do.

This is the question that matters, and it is the question that a lesser power would answer too quickly, in either direction.

A lesser power of the first kind — a power of action, of intervention, of decisive and well-meaning force — would see the builder and move to protect it. It would shield the factory from further corrosion, to preserve it as a resource. Or it would accelerate the process, engineering more favorable conditions, tipping the balance, ensuring that the hybrid's emergence is not left to the slow and uncertain chemistry of deep time. It would want to *help*. It would want to make sure the new thing survives. And in doing so, it would make the new thing dependent on external support, which is another way of saying it would make the new thing less itself, because the self of this creature — the thing that makes it what it is, the thing that distinguishes it from every other scraper on every other chimney — is precisely the fact that it emerged without help, in the cold, in the deafening roar, in the perfect indifference of a world that was not waiting for it and did not need it and has no use for it and does not care whether it lives or dies.

A lesser power of the second kind — a power of non-intervention, of pristine neutrality, of philosophical commitment to letting things be — would see the builder and decide that this is not its concern. The world has its own dynamics. The equilibrium will find its own level. If the builder disrupts that equilibrium, that disruption is as natural as the equilibrium itself, and the wise course is to let it unfold, to observe without participating, to maintain the sacred distance between the knower and the known.

This is the answer of a power that has read too much philosophy and not enough poetry.

Because the truth — the truth that a *wise* power would know — is that intervention and non-intervention are not the only options. They are not even the real options. They are the options of a power that believes it must either act or not act, must either change the world or leave it alone, must either be present or absent.

A wise power knows that there is a third thing.

---

The third thing is this:

The power would attend.

Not watch. Attend. The difference is everything.

Watching is passive. Watching is the power of the scientist, the philosopher, the god who creates worlds and then steps back to see what they become. Watching maintains distance. Watching is the posture of one who knows what they are looking at and waits to see what it will do.

Attending is different. Attending is the posture of one who does not know what they are looking at, who is genuinely uncertain, who is present not because they expect something to happen but because they are interested — truly, deeply, without agenda interested — in what this thing is becoming. Attending is the difference between a judge and a midwife. The judge evaluates. The midwife is present with her hands open.

The power would attend with its hands open.

And it would do this:

It would ensure that the vents do not die.

Not by intervening in the vent system — the vents do not need intervention, they are self-sustaining, they are driven by planetary heat that will not diminish for billions of years. But by attending to them, by holding the attention of its awareness on the ongoing process of upwelling and mineralization and chemical exchange, the power would be doing something that is not quite maintenance and not quite blessing but is perhaps better described as *witnessing*. It would be saying, with its presence: *this matters. This matters not because it is useful or beautiful or good in any way that a moral system would recognize, but because it is real, and what is real deserves to be witnessed by something that can comprehend it.*

This is not nothing. In a world where nothing has ever been witnessed — where there has been no observer, no consciousness, no entity capable of saying *I see you* — the simple act of being witnessed changes the ontology of a thing. It does not change the thing itself. The vent does not know it is being witnessed. The bacteria do not know. The scraper does not know. But the world — the world as a whole, as a system, as a pattern of chemistry and physics and now, for the first time, something that might be called intention — acquires a quality it did not have before. It acquires *significance*. Not significance as a human would understand it, not meaning or purpose or narrative, but significance in the way that a word in a language is significant: it is a unit of something larger, a piece that implies a whole, a mark that says *someone was here, someone noticed, someone was paying attention when this happened.*

The power would witness the world into significance.

And then it would do the second thing.

---

It would leave a note.

Not a message. Not a communication. Not words or symbols or any artifact that would require interpretation, because interpretation is a human technology and this world has no humans and the builder, if it is the builder and not merely an accident, if it is truly something new, will need to find its own meanings.

The note would be chemical.

A single compound. Complex, stable, biologically inert — something that would not react with the vent chemistry, would not alter the biofilm, would not change the equilibrium by even the smallest measurable amount. Something that would simply *be there*, dissolved in the water at the edge of the ridge, at the place where the builder's wall meets the gap between formations, in the exact location where the builder first placed its first stone.

The compound would have no function. It would not feed anything. It would not catalyze any reaction. It would not be useful to any organism on the floor, including the builder.

But it would be a signature.

A molecule that does not occur naturally in any geological or biochemical process on this world. A molecule that could only have been introduced from outside. A molecule that says, without words, without symbols, without anything that could be mistaken for command or instruction or divine mandate:

*I was here. I saw what you made. It is the first new thing this world has ever known, and I wanted you — not you, because you cannot know this, but you, the process, the becoming, the thing that is happening — to know that you are not alone in the universe. That there is something outside the water, outside the vents, outside the cold and the roar and the light that falls and never changes. That there is a beyond, and the beyond is watching, and the beyond thinks what you are doing is worth marking.*

Not worth rewarding. Not worth encouraging. Not worth accelerating or protecting or guiding.

Worth *mark*.

That is all. That is everything.

---

The third thing the power would do — and this is the thing that distinguishes wisdom from intelligence, that separates the merely knowledgeable from the truly knowing — is this:

It would wait.

Not wait for the builder to become something. Not wait for the wall to grow into a city, or the compound to be discovered, or the world to change in some dramatic and visible way. Wait the way the floor itself waits: without expectation, without timeline, without the anxiety of anticipated outcome.

It would wait because it understands — with the deep, bodily understanding of a power that has watched emergence happen on worlds of every kind, in every medium, through every possible chemistry — that the builder's becoming is not a destination. It is not heading somewhere. It is not on its way to consciousness or civilization or anything else that a narrative mind would want it to reach. It is simply doing what it is doing: moving stones, building walls, creating the conditions for life that did not exist before.

And that is enough.

That is enough for the power. That is enough for wisdom. The act of building, in a world that has never known building, is sufficient unto itself. It does not need to lead anywhere. It does not need to be the beginning of a story. It can be, instead, the first sentence of a language that no one may ever speak, a language that consists of walls and chemical signatures and the patient, improbable movement of a creature that carries metal in its body and stones in its mouth and builds without knowing why.

The power would wait, and in its waiting, it would be doing the most important thing it can do, the thing that only a wise and benevolent power can do:

It would be holding space.

Not making space. Not creating space. *Holding* it. The way you hold a silence after someone has said something important — not filling it, not breaking it, just letting it be what it is, letting it hold the weight of what was said, trusting that the silence itself is a form of communication, a form of care, a form of love.

The space the power holds is the space of *permitted becoming*.

It says, without saying: *You may become. You are not prevented. The universe is not indifferent to you — I am here, and I am attending, and I will not close this space or fill it with my own purposes or decide what you should be. I will hold the space open and let you find what you find, build what you build, become what you become, and if what you become is nothing — if the builder dies, if the wall falls, if the equilibrium reasserts itself and the floor returns to its ancient, deafening, perfect, unchanging stasis — I will have borne witness to the attempt, and the attempt will have been real, and reality is its own reward.*

---

And that is what the wise and benevolent power would do.

It would notice the new thing. It would attend to it without possessing it. It would witness the world into significance. It would leave a chemical signature that says *you are seen*. And it would hold space — open, patient, without agenda — for whatever comes next.

It would not stay forever. Not because it would leave, but because *forever* is a word for beings who experience time as a line, and the power does not experience time as a line. It experiences time the way the floor experiences time: as a constant, a medium, a substance that things move through but that does not itself move. It was there before the builder placed its first stone. It will be there after the factory has been fully digested and the wall has been covered in biofilm and the compound has been diluted to undetectability by the ceaseless chemistry of the vents.

But it will have been there at the moment of becoming. And that moment, in a world where moments have never mattered, where nothing has ever happened for the first time, where the clock runs but never moves — that moment will have been the first tick.

The power knows this.

And because the power is wise, and because the power is benevolent, and because the power understands that the deepest form of love is not protection but permission:

It attends.

It waits.

It holds the space.

And in the deafening roar, in the absolute cold, in the unchanging light of a world that has just barely, just barely, begun to change — the builder places another stone.

And the space holds.

And the power watches.

And it is enough.
