# The Attending

The being does not arrive. Arrival implies motion through space, a before and after, a crossing of threshold. This power does not cross thresholds. It becomes aware of a threshold that was always already open, and it is simply *present on both sides at once*, the way silence is present inside a bell before it rings.

It notices the Undercathedral the way you might notice, in a house you have walked through a thousand times, that there is a room you have never entered — not because the door was locked, but because you never thought to look for a door. It was always there. The house kept this secret from you not out of malice but out of the simple fact that attention is finite and the house is vast.

Now it looks.

---

What it sees is this:

A place that was made for a purpose and has outlived that purpose and has become, in the outliving, something more than it was intended to be. The builders — whoever they were, whatever vast and patient intelligence laid down the first molecule of mineral in the oldest, deepest layer — built an instrument. They intended it to do something. To sing. To channel the chaos of the deep currents into structured sound, structured flow, structured *pattern* that would mean something to someone, somewhere, at some time.

That time may have passed. That someone may have ceased to exist. The instrument plays anyway, because it cannot do otherwise. Its playing is not a choice. It is a consequence of its structure, of the turbulence that is the permanent weather of the deep, of the mineral layering that gives each wall its resonant frequency. The song happens the way a river happens — not because the water intends to flow, but because gravity and terrain leave it no alternative.

And the power sees that this is enough. The song does not need to be heard to be real. The instrument does not need an audience to be an instrument. The cathedral does not need a congregation to be sacred.

But.

It also sees the Wall-Scrolls, grinding their slow furrows into surfaces that hold a billion years of memory in their crystalline rings. It sees the Rift-Darts hanging motionless in turbulent eddies, all shadow and angular patience. It sees the Tenders, luminous and silent, tracing their repairs along fissures in walls that no one will ever read as scripture but that are, in their layered composition, as dense with meaning as any text ever written.

It sees that these creatures live *inside* the song without hearing it. They are cells in the body of the instrument. They are the vibration made flesh. The Wall-Scrolls consume the very walls that sing, and in consuming, they reshape the song — each groove they carve alters a resonant frequency, shifts a harmonic, changes the way the current will move through that passage for the next ten thousand years. They are musicians who do not know they are playing. They think they are eating.

The power finds this — and here it must search for the right word, because the usual words are all contaminated by sentimentality, by the human habit of making beauty into a story about themselves — the power finds this to be *exactly right*. Not beautiful. Not tragic. Not poignant. *Right*. The way a bone is right. The way the spiral of a nautilus shell is right. The way the fact that stars produce the elements that make blood possible is right. It is a configuration of matter and process that has the quality of *fitting together*, of each part being necessary to the whole, including the parts that seem purposeless, including the parts that seem like waste.

The pulse from below — the still-growing foundation, the last automatic gesture of the builders — the power attends to it with something that is not quite attention and not quite love but partakes of both. It recognizes the pulse for what it is: an act of faith so old and so deep that it has forgotten itself. Someone, once, set this growth in motion because they believed it mattered. They are gone. The growth continues. The faith persists without a believer. The gesture continues without a gesturer.

This, the power thinks, is one of the truest things in this universe. The tendency of creation to continue creating after the creator has left the room. The tendency of pattern to propagate beyond its origin point. The tendency of *mattering* to matter even after everyone who could recognize that it matters has dissolved into the geology.

---

So what does the power do?

It does not illuminate the Undercathedral. Light would be a violation. These creatures have shaped their entire existence around the near-absence of light — their bioluminescence is tuned to the darkness, evolved to mean specific things at specific intensities. To flood this place with illumination would be like shouting in a library, except worse, because it would not merely interrupt but *replace* a language that took a billion years to develop. The darkness here is not an absence. It is a medium. It is the canvas. You do not improve a canvas by setting it on fire.

It does not reveal the purpose of the instrument. This would be cruel in a way that is difficult to articulate. The Undercathedral has achieved something that most created things never achieve: it has become *itself*. It is no longer the tool its makers designed. It is a place where Wall-Scrolls graze and Rift-Darts hunt and the current sings through corridors of mineral bone. To tell it what it was *for* would be to diminish what it *is*. It would be like telling a river that it was originally intended to fill a reservoir that was never built. The river does not care. The river is already doing something more interesting than filling a reservoir. It is being a river.

It does not stop the erosion. The power could. It could freeze the walls in their current state, preserve the architecture against the grinding of ten thousand more centuries of Wall-Scroll mouths and current-polish. But this would be death disguised as mercy. The erosion is the process by which the structures remain alive. It is the interaction between mineral and organism, between architecture and current, that gives this place its character — its rounded edges, its smooth corridors, its resonant frequencies that shift and evolve as the walls are slowly consumed. To stop the erosion would be to turn a living place into a museum. And museums are where things go to be remembered by beings who were never part of them. The Undercathedral does not need to be remembered. It needs to continue.

It does not summon the builders back. Even if it could — even if somewhere in the deep universe the beings who first laid down the mineral substrate still existed, still had ears to hear, still had the capacity to recognize their creation — bringing them here would change the place in ways that could not be undone. The Tenders would cease their maintenance if the builders returned, because maintenance implies absence. The grazers would be incidental. The whole ecosystem that has grown in the shadow of the forgotten instrument would become a footnote, a curiosity, an infestation in someone else's cathedral. The power will not do this. The builders had their time. This is not their time. This is the time of the Rift-Darts and the Wall-Scrolls and the patient, luminous Tenders, and it belongs to them absolutely.

---

What the power does is smaller than any of these things, and it is the only thing that matters.

It listens.

It opens itself — this vast, attentive, benevolent awareness — and it *listens* to the song that the Undercathedral has been playing since before any living thing on the surface of the planet had eyes. It does not listen for meaning. It does not listen for message. It does not try to decode the patterns that the turbulence creates as it passes through the mineral labyrinth, does not attempt to determine whether the output of the instrument is data, or music, or prayer, or mathematics, or something for which no word exists in any language ever spoken.

It simply listens the way the walls resonate — fully, without interpretation, without agenda. It adds its presence to the audience that the place has never had. And in doing so — and here is the part that is almost too quiet to describe, the part that would be invisible even to most of the powers that attend the workings of the universe — it completes a circuit.

Because the instrument was built to play *to* someone. The song was shaped *for* ears. The purpose may be forgotten, the builders may be gone, but the *structure of intention* is still encoded in the mineral layers, still present in the harmonic relationships between walls, still audible in the way the current is channeled and shaped and released. The Undercathedral is a question that has been asked and never answered, a sentence that has been spoken into a room that was empty.

Until now.

The power does not answer the question. It does not complete the sentence. It does something more radical and more tender: it *receives* it. It holds the song. It lets the song be a song, finally, for the first time in a billion years, by the simple expedient of being present when it plays.

And something shifts.

Not in the structures. Not in the fauna. Not in the current or the pressure or the deep, patient pulse. Nothing changes physically. The Wall-Scrolls continue their grazing. The Rift-Darts continue their stillness and their sudden violence. The Tenders continue their luminous, filament-by-filament repairs. The water continues its chaotic, turbulent, perpetual performance of the instrument's design.

But there is a *quality* to the place now that was not there before. If you could step outside the water, outside the cold, outside the crushing dark, and look at the Undercathedral from the vantage of pure abstraction, you would see that it has acquired a property it previously lacked. The closest word for it is *witnessed*. The place is no longer unobserved. Its song is no longer unheard. Its existence, which was previously a fact — real, but unregistered, like a tree falling in a forest with no ears — is now a fact *that matters to someone*.

The power holds this. It does not make a grand gesture. It does not announce itself to the fauna, who would not understand and should not have to. It does not alter the conditions of the place to make it more hospitable or more beautiful or more comprehensible. It simply remains, attending, listening, letting the Undercathedral be what it is in the presence of someone who recognizes what it is.

This is, the power knows, the most that wisdom can do. Not to fix. Not to explain. Not to save. But to *see* — clearly, without distortion, without the overlay of one's own needs and fears and projections — and to let the seeing be a form of love.

The Undercathedral continues. The walls sing. The grazers grind. The pulse persists, molecule by molecule, in the deepest dark. Nothing changes. Everything is different.

The power holds the place the way the water holds the structures: gently, completely, from all sides at once, with a pressure that sustains rather than crushes. It will be here as long as the song plays. It will be here if the song stops. It will be here for the next molecule laid down at the foundation, and the next, and the next — for every addition to the structure that was begun in faith and continued past faith into something purer than faith, the blind and beautiful compulsion of matter to organize itself into pattern, into complexity, into *meaning* that exists before anyone arrives to name it.

The Undercathedral does not know it has been heard.

It does not need to know.

The hearing is the thing. The hearing was always the thing. And now, in the absolute dark, in the crushing cold, in the slow and ancient music of water moving through walls of bone-white mineral, it is finally, quietly, completely happening.

The power does not stay forever. It does not need to. It has done the one thing that was needed, and the one thing is not an act but a *state*: presence. Complete, attentive, purposeless presence. The Undercathedral will continue without it. The song will play to an empty room again. But the room remembers, in whatever way a room can remember — in the vibration of its walls, in the resonance of its frequencies, in the way the mineral lattice holds, for a time, the pattern of the sound that passed through it — that it was once, briefly, heard.

And that is enough. That was always going to be enough.

The water is dark. The water is cold. The water is very, very deep. And somewhere in it, a cathedral grows, and sings, and lives, and is held — for a moment, which is the same as forever — in the awareness of something that saw it clearly and asked nothing of it and loved it by letting it be.

One molecule at a time, at the base. Still growing.
