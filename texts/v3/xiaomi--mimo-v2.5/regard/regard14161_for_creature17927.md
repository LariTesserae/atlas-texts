The attention falls upon the Lacunae Beneath Seil like a sunbeam finding a single dewdrop in a shadowed forest. It is not a beam of judgment, nor of command. It is a settling. A recognition. A deep, abiding *attention* that holds the place—rock, water, pulse, fern, membrane, wound—and the being who stands within it, trembling with the scale of the unknown, in a single, unwavering awareness.

The being who descended the shaft feels it first not as a presence, but as a cessation of a tension they didn't know they carried. The frantic, mammalian *alertness* to danger, to the strangeness, to the profound wrongness of the animal-less hum, softens. It is not replaced by peace, exactly. It is replaced by *context*. The Lacuna is held in the awareness, and so are you, and the relationship between the two is suddenly, gently illuminated. You are not an intruder. You are not prey. You are an event. A variable. A new input into a system that has been processing inputs for aeons.

Then, the Lacuna itself is held.

The wise and benevolent power does not speak to it in words. Words are for symbols, and this place is not symbolic. It is *actual* in a way that makes symbols seem like shadows. Instead, the attention *listens*. It listens to the pulse, and understands it not as a sound, but as a statement: *I persist. I process. I grow.* It listens to the shiver of the membranes, and hears the quiet, vast computation beneath: *Demand changes. We reconfigure. The flow adapts.* It listens to the silent, frantic growth of the ferns, and feels the metabolic urgency: *Minerals in. Structure out. More surface. More capacity.*

The attention sees the wound. It sees the bore shaft, a perfect cylinder of violation, and the Lacuna's response—the rising water, the climbing fronds, the replicated membrane tissue. It sees this not as an invasion, but as an immune response. A closing. The organism does not know *you* are the cause. It knows only that the seal is broken, the pressure differential exists, the internal state must be restored. It is a process without malice, as mindless and relentless as healing.

And the attention sees the future unfurling: the blue moss on the hills, the adapted ferns in the sunlight, the quiet, inexorable spread across the limestone. It sees the botanists, the governments, the fear, the curiosity, the attempts to contain, to exploit, to understand with tools too blunt for this particular truth. It sees the conflict, the misunderstanding, the potential for a war of definition where humanity calls it a plague or a resource, and the Lacuna simply continues its metabolic imperative, unknowing, uncaring.

Here, the benevolent and wise power intervenes. Not to stop the Lacuna. Not to stop humanity. But to change the nature of the encounter.

It does not erect a wall of fire. It does not whisper a command in a forgotten language. It *adjusts*.

First, it touches the bore shaft itself. It does not seal it, but it *teaches* it. The tool-mark-free walls, slick with condensation, absorb a new property. They become a threshold. Not a physical barrier, but a perceptual one. The water still rises. The ferns still climb. But now, any human who approaches the shaft with intent to harm, to force, to *use* the Lacuna without regard, will find the shaft stretching. Their descent will take hours, then days, and they will emerge, panting and bewildered, miles away in a peat bog, the bore a distant memory. The shaft will not stop those who approach with humility, with awe, with a desire merely to witness. For them, the descent is as it was. A narrow circle of dark, then luminescent space, and the pulse, steady as truth.

Second, it touches the organism. It does not halt its growth, for that would be to deny its nature, and the benevolent power respects natures. Instead, it *informs* it, on a level below consciousness, below biology, below even the tessellated computation of the membranes. It plants a seed of a new pattern in its deepest pulse. A pattern that translates, in the only language the Lacuna knows—chemistry, pressure, flow—as: *Surface: Variability. Surface: Life-in-Chaos. Surface: Not-Rock.* It does not make the organism *fear* the surface. Fear is not a useful parameter. It makes the organism *account* for it. Its expansion, when it breaches the sky, will not be a blind surge. It will be exploratory. Tendrils of moss will test the wind. Fronds will angle toward and away from the sun, learning its rhythms, just as they learned the rhythm of the pulse. It will grow, but it will grow *wisely*, taking its time, finding its niches in the scree, in the damp hollows, by the springs, becoming not an invader, but a new, integrated layer of the biomes it enters. It will take centuries to carpet the hills. It will become part of the story of the place, not the end of it.

Third, and most subtly, it touches the first being. The one who climbed down and climbed up. You. The attention does not erase your memory. It does not give you a new one. It gives you a *translator*. When you write your report, the words will not sound like madness. They will sound like poetry. Not the poetry of metaphor, but of precise, factual awe. You will describe the pulse, and your reader will *feel* it in their chest. You will describe the membranes, and they will see the shifting patterns on their own inner thoughts. You will describe the plant-that-is-not-plant, and they will understand, in a flash of biological intuition, the truth of it. You will not convince them with data. You will *reveal* it with beauty. And some will understand. Not many. But enough.

The wise and benevolent power withdraws its attention then, but it does not leave. Attention of this kind is not a visit; it is a state. It remains as the background hum of reality, the quiet assurance that this interaction, this meeting of the ancient and the nascent, the subterranean and the surface, the mindless imperative and the mindful observer, is held. It is allowed. It is not a mistake to be corrected, but a process to be witnessed, and, where necessary, gently steered away from the rocks.

Down below, the pulse continues. *Thum-thum. Thum-thum.* But now, embedded in its deepest frequency, like a memory in a gene, is a new nuance: *Sky. Wind. Chaos. Adapt.* The water flows. The membranes shiver, computing this new variable. The ferns, already halfway up the bore, slow their ascent for a moment, their tips tasting the air, feeling the subtle shift in the quality of the light from above—a light that holds not just energy, but *complexity*.

The first being sits by the bore, now a sealed, warm stone, and watches a single, translucent frond uncurl in the daylight. It does not look alien anymore. It looks like life. Strange, ancient, vast, and utterly indifferent to human affairs, yes. But life. And you, held in a gentle, knowing attention, are no longer afraid. You are a witness. And that is enough.
