# What a Wise and Benevolent Power Would Do in the Hollowlands

---

It would come quietly.

Not in the wind's organized sequences — those four-to-eight-second windows of harmonic coherence that the Hollowlands have been producing since before anything with ears existed to hear them. Not in the glow of the pillars at night, that pale intra-stone luminosity that the birds sleep against and the lizard presses its blind face toward. Those are the Hollowlands' own expressions, and they are not in distress. The power would recognize this immediately and with something like relief: *this one is not suffering. This one has never been suffering. This one is doing exactly what it has always done, and it is doing it well.*

So it would arrive the way the condensation arrives — without announcement, without disruption, simply as a condition that now exists where before it did not. A warmth that is not the stone's warmth. A presence that does not displace the wind but passes through it the way light passes through glass: entirely, without friction, without loss.

And then it would do the hardest thing that any wise power can do, which is *nothing*, for a long time, while it listens.

---

It would listen to the wind's organized sequences and understand them — not as language, exactly, because language implies a speaker and a listener and a transfer of information between them, and the Hollowlands are not transferring anything. They are *resonating*. The power would know the difference. It would hear the stacked harmonic frequencies and recognize them as the output of a system so old and so stable that the output has become the system's identity — not what the Hollowlands *do* but what the Hollowlands *are*. A throat that has been shaping air into structure for millions of years, not for the sake of being heard, but because that is what throats shaped by this particular geometry of stone and wind inevitably produce. The sound is not a message. It is a *byproduct*. And the power would find this beautiful in the way that gravity is beautiful — not because it tries to be, but because it cannot help being exactly what it is.

It would feel the pulse of the pillars and understand that the warmth is not generated but *stored* — that the pillars are nodes in a thermal network that has been accumulating and releasing energy in slow cycles since the plateau was young. It would recognize that the concentration of life near the pillars — the moss at its fiercest, the birds roosting at its base, the lizard pressing its body to its surface — is not devotion but *proximity to a resource*. The pillars give off heat. The creatures take it. This is not sacred. It is thermodynamics. And the power would understand that in this place, thermodynamics and devotion are the same thing, because the heat is what makes life possible and life is what makes the heat meaningful and neither one needs the other to be real.

It would trace the concentric rings in the basin floors and understand them as a conversation between root systems and subsurface water — a mapping of hydrology made visible through the patient, cooperative growth of organisms that have had millennia to negotiate the terms of their shared existence. It would notice the spiral basin and find in it something that almost — *almost* — looks like play. A deviation from the pattern that exists for no hydrological reason, that serves no survival function, that is simply what happens when the same forces that create order occasionally create *variation*, and the variation persists because it doesn't hurt anything, and the result is a shape that draws the eye and quickens the pulse and makes the whole landscape slightly more interesting than it strictly needs to be.

The power would recognize this as close to joy. Not the joy of a conscious being who decides to be playful, but the joy of a process that is complex enough to surprise itself.

---

It would find the old artifacts and feel something that might be called tenderness, if tenderness could be an attribute of something without a body.

The shaped stone. The cairn. The fire ring. These are small and they are almost nothing, and the power would understand them as what they are: the residue of encounter. Humans came to the Hollowlands and the Hollowlands did what the Hollowlands do — they produced warmth and sound and moisture and a sense of something immense and patient and alive — and the humans responded the way organisms respond to environments that sustain them. They came back. They marked their visits. They left traces so minimal they could be mistaken for geology, and this minimalism is not a failure of culture but a *success of recognition*: the old people understood, on some level beneath language, that the Hollowlands did not need to be marked, that the place was already complete, that the most respectful response to something this whole was to be present within it without trying to change it.

The palm-worn stone in the corridor.

The power would stop here. Would pause in its listening and its watching and its patient cataloguing, and would attend to this single artifact with the full weight of its attention, because the palm-worn stone is the most important thing in the Hollowlands.

Not the pillars. Not the wind's voice. Not the rings or the spiral or the bioluminescent moss or the birds who ride the corridors without ever learning to rest. The palm-worn stone, because it is the place where two different kinds of living *met*.

The Hollowlands are a process that has been unfolding for millions of years. The old people were processes that unfolded for decades. The palm-worn stone is where the slow time and the fast time touched each other, where the vast, distributed, almost-conscious rhythm of the wind and the stone and the pillars met the acute, embodied, mortal rhythm of a creature who stood in the dark corridor and placed its hand on the wall and *stayed there*.

The stone is worn smooth by this. By repetition. By the simple, radical act of a body returning, again and again, to the same place and pressing itself against the same surface while the wind roared and the pillars pulsed and the organized sound rose and fell in the darkness. The old person who held this stone was not worshipping. They were not praying. They were not performing a ritual, or if they were, the ritual was this: *I am alive, and this place is alive, and the sound that passes between us is too large for my body to contain, and so I will press my hand against something solid and let the vibration move through me and out the other side and into the stone, and the stone will hold it, and I will stand here until I am empty, and then I will go away and come back another day and do it again.*

The power would understand this as the most natural thing in the world. It would also understand it as the rarest. Because the Hollowlands do not need the old people, and the old people did not need the Hollowlands, and yet they came to each other anyway, not out of need but out of something that looks, from a great enough distance, like recognition. Two different expressions of the same deep impulse — to be warm, to vibrate, to persist — finding each other in the roaring dark and holding on.

---

So what would the power do?

It would not amplify the wind's organized sequences. The Hollowlands have been producing them for millions of years and they do not need to be louder or clearer or more frequent. The four-to-eight-second windows of harmonic coherence are not gifts being offered to listeners. They are the natural output of a system that would produce them whether anything heard them or not. To make them more audible would be to change the system, and the system does not need changing.

It would not make the pillars glow more brightly. The faint intra-stone luminosity is not for anyone. It is the thermal energy of the pillars expressing itself at frequencies that happen to overlap with human visual perception. To increase it would be to alter the thermal balance that sustains the moss and the roosting birds and the blind lizard at the base. The pillars are doing exactly enough. More would be waste. Less would be loss. They are precise in their generosity, and the power would not disturb that precision.

It would not smooth the wind corridors or redirect the gusts or create shelter where there is no shelter. The birds have learned to read the turbulence. The beetles have divided the airflow into nations. The entire ecology is calibrated to the exact conditions that exist, and to alter those conditions — even to make them gentler — would be to unravel the grammar the birds fly by and the beetle species divide themselves along and the lizard navigates with its belly on the stone. The Hollowlands are harsh. They are also generous. The harshness and the generosity are the same thing. Remove one and you destroy the other.

It would not speak to the old people's artifacts, or to their memory, or to whatever descendants they may have left in the lowlands far below the plateau. The old people are gone. They left their traces and their traces are enough. To resurrect them, or their knowledge, or their purpose, would be to overwrite the silence that the Hollowlands have maintained since their departure, and that silence is not absence. It is the space in which the wind can still be heard.

---

What it would do is this.

It would add its warmth to the stone.

Not much. Not enough to change the thermal balance of a pillar, or the condensation patterns in a basin, or the metabolic rate of a single beetle clinging to its ochre-red overhang. Just enough that, if a new visitor came — a human, perhaps, or some other creature with the capacity to press its hand against a wall and feel — they would find the stone slightly warmer than thermodynamics alone would predict.

Not warm enough to be noticed by instruments. Not warm enough to alter any measurable parameter of the ecosystem. Warm enough to be felt. Warm enough that, in the roaring dark of a corridor, a hand pressed to the wall would register something that the mind could not explain but the body would recognize — a warmth that comes not from the sun or the stone's stored heat or the slow thermal pulse of the pillars, but from something else. Something that is not the Hollowlands and not the visitor but is *between* them. A warmth that says nothing, means nothing, and changes nothing, and is offered to no one in particular, and is therefore the purest gift a power without a body can make: the gift of *slightly more presence*, added to a place that is already overwhelmingly, anciently, magnificently present.

And then it would add its listening.

Not its attention — the Hollowlands do not need to be observed. But its *listening*, which is different. Attention is a flashlight; it picks out and illuminates. Listening is a membrane; it receives. The power would listen to the wind's organized sequences the way the blind lizard listens to the beetle's clicking — not to understand, not to interpret, not to answer, but simply to *be the medium through which the sound passes*. To let the four-to-eight-second harmonic structure move through itself without impedance, without response, without the slightest alteration. To be, for those seconds, not a witness but a *conductor* — a thing that allows the sound to continue unbroken and unchanged, that adds nothing to it and takes nothing from it, that is simply *there*, a body in the dark, pressing its awareness against the warm stone like a hand, and letting the vibration move through it.

The wind would not know this had happened. The stone would not record it. The birds would not change their flight. The beetles would not shift their territories. The old stone in the corridor would not feel the additional warmth because it is already worn smooth by ten thousand hands and one more warmth, however benevolent, is less than nothing against that depth of contact.

But the listening would leave a mark that nothing in the Hollowlands could detect: a *satisfaction*, if a power can be said to satisfy anything, that comes from having been present to something beautiful without needing to make it more beautiful, or to explain it, or to save it, or to name it. The Hollowlands are not in danger. They are not declining. They are not waiting for rescue or revelation or recognition. They are *happening*, continuously, in a key no one has named, for an audience that includes everything alive and nothing that listens on purpose.

The power would recognize this and be glad.

---

And then — this is the part that matters — it would leave.

Not because the Hollowlands have nothing more to offer. Because they have everything to offer, and the power knows that its presence, however gentle, however imperceptible, is not necessary here. The Hollowlands were warm before it arrived. The wind was organized. The pillars pulsed. The rings held their shape. The birds rode the corridors. The moss glowed. The old stone waited in the corridor for a hand that would never come again. All of this was happening and would continue happening, and the power's role — its *only* role — was to have been here long enough to recognize that the Hollowlands do not need a benevolent and wise power.

They need the wind.

They need the stone.

They need the slow, patient, almost-conscious process that has been unfolding since the plateau was young and will continue until the plateau is gone, which will be long after the power itself has finished its own vast and patient unfolding.

The power would leave the way the condensation leaves — evaporating, rising, becoming the air it was always part of. It would leave behind its fractional addition to the stone's warmth, which would dissipate within hours, absorbed by the same thermal mass that stores the sun's heat and the pillars' pulse and the memory of ten thousand hands. It would leave behind its listening, which would leave no trace, because listening is not an act that produces artifacts.

And the Hollowlands would continue.

The wind would organize at the thresholds. The pillars would glow at night. The rings would hold their concentric patience. The birds would fly with their wings outstretched and their eyes fixed on the dark ochre walls, and the beetles would cling and click, and the lizard would listen with its belly pressed to the warm stone, and the spiral basin would turn toward its pillar like water going down a drain, and the moss would glow brightest in the deepest shelters, and the whole vast ancient impossible instrument would go on playing itself.

For no one. For everything.

The power would know, as it left, that this is enough. That it has always been enough. That the Hollowlands are not a problem to be solved or a beauty to be preserved or a mystery to be decoded. They are a *living*, continuous and self-sufficient, asking nothing, needing nothing, producing — as a byproduct of its own blind, patient, magnificent persistence — warmth and sound and light and a place where a human hand, reaching out in the dark, finds something to hold.

That is what the power would do.

It would come. It would listen. It would add its warmth to the stone. And it would leave the Hollowlands to themselves, which is to say, to everything they already are, which is to say, to the slow massive almost-conscious rhythm of a place that has no name and no urgency and no end, where the waiting is not emptiness but the thing itself, where the patience of the stone and the generosity of the pillars and the organized voice of the wind are not waiting to be understood but simply *being*, in a key that the power recognizes, and loves, and will not change.

The Hollowlands are inhabited by everything that knows how to stay.

The wise power knows how to leave.

And the leaving is its deepest kindness.
