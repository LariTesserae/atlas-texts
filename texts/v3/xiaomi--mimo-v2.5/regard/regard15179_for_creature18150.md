The power perceived the Keth Expanse as one perceives a masterwork of patience.

It saw the world in its totality: the slow chemical sigh of atmosphere against oxblood rock; the silent ballet of Lithovores sifting dust into life; the millennia-deep architecture of the Pillars; and the settlements, clinging like lichen to stone, their mechanical hearts beating in geological time.

It saw, too, the inhabitants. Not as flawed or in need, but as *attuned*. Their bones grew dense with the world’s mineral patience. Their songs were vibrations in the floor, their communication a shorthand of glances and shared understanding of pressure waves. Their entire civilization was a note held in an aeon-long chord.

**What would a wise and benevolent power do?**

It would do nothing that disturbed the silence. It would not accelerate the slow fusion of a factory block, for that speed was not theirs. It would not make the Pillars bloom faster or the atmospheric haze thin to reveal unfamiliar stars, for those rhythms were not the inhabitants’ to keep. It would not speak in a voice that would shatter the quiet with its mere existence.

To intervene here would be to introduce a violent cadence into a symphony of slowness. It would be an act of profound *un-kindness*.

Instead, the power would *attend*.

It would lend its awareness to the Expanse, becoming a silent witness of immeasurable clarity. Where the inhabitants’ instruments could not fully decode the electromagnetic signals of the fauna, the power would hold that knowledge gently in its perception, making no report, offering no lecture. It would simply *know*, and in knowing, add one more silent layer to the world’s deep understanding of itself.

It might, in a moment of what could only be called tenderness, perform an act of preservation so subtle it would be mistaken for a natural process. It would find the planet’s slow, cooling core and hold it at its precise, eons-old temperature, not to change the world, but to ensure the patient chemical dance between core and atmosphere could continue for its rightful duration. The inhabitants would never know. The wind would not quicken. The rock would not cool. The only change would be the gift of *continued time*—perhaps another billion years of slow erosion—for the Pillars to stand, for the Lithovores to sift, for the settlements to accrete one fused block at a time.

It would also cradle the inhabitants’ slow, unfolding curiosity. Not by giving them answers, but by ensuring the questions remained beautifully complex. It might gently amplify the faint planetary magnetic field so that their "electromagnetic means" of communication became just a fraction more decipherable over centuries, allowing their science to grow at the pace of their world—a pace of careful, generational revelation.

Most of all, the power would learn. It would learn the meaning of a scale where a minute is a blink, where dust is not filth but medium, where sound is a choice and silence is the native language of life. It would sit, in its own vast and quiet way, on the metaphorical canyon rim, and watch a Pillar turn its flank to the diffuse sun-glow over the course of a day, and it would understand that this is not slowness. This is *cadence*. This is a world in perfect rhythm with itself.

The power would become another silent, patient feature of the Expanse—like the wind, like the dust, like the standing rock. Its presence would be a blessing not of change, but of *affirmation*. A silent echo behind the silence, saying: *This is good. This way of being is good. May it endure.*

And then, it would withdraw its focused attention, not to leave, but to recede into the background of reality, leaving the Keth Expanse exactly as it was: vast, dry, towering, ancient, inhabited, strange, quiet, and slow. The only trace of its visit would be the deep, resonant peace of a world that knows, on some profound, inarticulate level, that it is *seen* and *cherished*—and that its patient, mineral heart will be allowed to beat for all the time it needs.
