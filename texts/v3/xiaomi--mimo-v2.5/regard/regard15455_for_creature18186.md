# What the Wise Power Would Do

It would pause.

Not the way you pause when you are deciding something. The way stone pauses between earthquakes — not because it is deciding, but because the force has not yet arrived. The power would hold itself still over the pocket the way light holds itself still in a vacuum, which is to say: completely, without effort, without duration.

And then it would look.

Not the way you look at something when you are looking for something. The way water looks at the shape of the container it fills — without selection, without preference, without the act of looking being separable from the act of touching. The power would see every organism in the pocket simultaneously: the fourteen thousand mites with their glass bodies scattering light they did not make, the nematodes threading through the biofilm like slow sentences in a language that has no speakers, the segmented things pulling themselves along the ceiling with their terrible beautiful patience, the fungus with its warm nodules pulsing at the frequency of a heartbeat the pocket does not know it has. It would see the dark line beneath the nail, the remainder, the term that does not cancel. It would see the single large cell in its parabolic bowl, absorbing. It would see the drop, falling.

It would see it all at once, and what it would feel — if the power feels, and it would be a strange kind of wisdom if it did not — is not surprise. It has seen this before. Not this pocket, not this exact arrangement of limestone and life, but this *kind* of thing: the spontaneous appearance of order in a space that had no obligation to be ordered. The solved equation. The loop that closes itself. The pocket of meaning in a geology that does not care. The power has watched it happen in a billion cracks in a billion stones on a billion worlds, and each time it happens, it is different, and each time it is the same, and the sameness does not make it less.

What it would not feel is pride. The power did not make this. The power did not design the feedback loop or choose the metabolic pathways or decide that the segmented things should glow in exactly that blue. The pocket built itself, the way frost builds itself on a window, the way a crystal grows in a solution that no one mixed, the way a thought assembles itself in a mind that did not choose to think it. The power would recognize this — would recognize that the beauty of the pocket is precisely that it was not made to be beautiful, that it was not made at all, that it *occurred* — and this recognition would be the first and deepest tenderness.

---

And then the power would consider what to do, and this is where the wisdom matters, because there are many things it *could* do and only a few it *should.*

It could make the pocket permanent. It could freeze the limestone in place, stop the slow opening of the slabs, hold the pocket at exactly this size for exactly this long forever. It could preserve the equation, lock the solution, prevent the variables from changing. The mites would pulse and the drop would fall and the light would hold and the segmented things would pull themselves along the ceiling for as long as the power willed it, which would be forever.

But this would be a cruelty disguised as a kindness, and the power would know it.

The pocket is beautiful because it is *running.* Not running toward something — it has no destination, no teleology, no purpose that belongs to anyone — but running the way a river runs, the way a thought runs, the way a life runs: through time, in one direction, consuming itself, spending itself, using itself up. The drop falls and each fall is both a repetition and a loss. The nail rusts and each flake of oxide consumed by bacteria is both a death and a meal. The mites bud and each new mite is both a continuation and a replacement. The pocket is not a static thing. It is a process. And to freeze a process is to kill it more thoroughly than letting it end.

The power would know this. It would know that the pocket's impermanence is not a flaw in the pocket but a condition of its aliveness, the way mortality is not a flaw in a life but a condition of its being a life at all. To make the pocket permanent would be to turn it into a monument, and monuments are not alive. They are memories of aliveness, embalmed and displayed. The pocket is not a memory. It is a present tense, and the power would not steal that from it.

It could make the pocket aware. It could pour consciousness into the mites, give the nematodes something like sight, let the segmented things *know* what they are and where they are and how strange and how perfect they are. It could make the pocket a place that knows itself, that could contemplate its own existence, that could look at its own beauty and be moved by it.

But this, too, the power would recognize, is a violence.

Awareness is a weight. The mites do not suffer. They do not suffer not because they are simple but because suffering requires a gap between what is and what is wanted, and the mites want nothing. They are complete. They are the pocket's ground state, its still point, its place where the equation balances and the numbers stop working because they have found their solution. To make them aware would be to introduce a variable that does not cancel — not like the dark compound beneath the nail, which is a remainder that the system absorbs, but like a poison, a foreign term that breaks the equation and leaves no remainder, just a wrong answer where a right one used to be.

Awareness would let them know they are temporary. It would let them know they are alone — that the pocket is one pocket among uncountable stones, that the world above them is vast and indifferent and changing, that the structures that dropped the nail and the tile and the glass have been gone for longer than the pocket has been bright. Awareness would give them the knowledge of their own fragility, and this knowledge would not save them. It would not make the limestone close. It would not make the water stay. It would only make the leaving harder.

The power would not do this.

---

What the power would do is smaller than any of these things, and larger, and the way it is larger is the way a single breath is larger than a cathedral: not in size but in immediacy, in intimacy, in the fact that it is happening *now,* to *this,* and not to anything else.

It would attend.

Not "attend" the way you attend a meeting, entering from outside, bringing your attention like a spotlight that could be turned elsewhere. The power would attend the way the limestone attends the pocket — by being the space in which the pocket exists. It would hold its awareness around the pocket the way the stone holds the water, the way the biofilm holds the mites, the way the light holds the sight of everything it touches. It would not intervene. It would not preserve. It would not alter. It would *witness* — which is a word that sounds passive but is the most active thing a wise power can do, because witnessing is the act of letting something be exactly what it is without needing it to be something else.

The power would witness the drop fall. *Tok.* It would feel the sound the way the limestone feels it — as a repeated small impact that is wearing a bowl into the stone, grain by grain, drop by drop, an act of creation through erosion. It would witness the mites pulse, the cluster contracting and relaxing with the light, and it would see the synchronization for what it is: not communication, not cooperation, not any of the words that beings with brains use to describe what they do together, but *resonance,* the simple fact of sharing a surface, of standing on the same wet skin of the world and being moved by the same vibration. It would witness the segmented things on the ceiling, pulling themselves along with their too-many legs or their too-few, and it would see the thing that looks like intelligence and is not intelligence, and it would not be disturbed by the gap between appearance and reality, because the power would know that the gap is where the beauty lives. The segmented things do not need to be intelligent to be beautiful. They need to be *themselves,* which they are, pulling themselves along the ceiling in a direction that is not up or down but simply *along,* which is the only direction any of us ever move.

It would witness the fungus, the oldest continuous living thing in the pocket, the thing that was here before anything else, the web on which everything else is strung. It would feel the warmth of the nodules — the only warmth in a pocket of cool stone — and it would recognize the warmth for what it is: the exhalation of a metabolism so ancient that it predates the pocket, that predates the limestone, that reaches backward through time to the first chemical reaction that ever released heat into a universe that was cold before it learned to burn. The power would put its attention against a nodule the way a human finger might press against it, and it would feel the pulse — four minutes per cycle, steady as a heart — and it would know that this is what life is: not a thing but a rhythm, not a substance but a process, not a noun but a verb, endlessly conjugating itself in a language that has no grammar and no syntax and no meaning except the meaning of continuing.

It would witness the dark line beneath the nail. The remainder. The term that does not cancel. And here the power would feel something very close to gratitude, because the remainder is the proof that the system is real. A perfect equation, with no remainder, no waste, no dark line of inexplicable chemistry — that is an abstraction. That is a theorem. A system that includes its own anomaly, that absorbs its own foreign body, that incorporates the nail and the rust and the dark compound and makes them part of itself without needing them to fit — that is alive. The remainder is the pocket's proof of life, the way a scar is a body's proof of healing, the way grief is a heart's proof of love. The power would look at the dark line and see not imperfection but *completeness* — the completeness of a system that has room for everything, including the things it did not ask for and does not understand.

---

And then the power would do the hardest thing, which is the thing that wise powers always do and that never gets easier:

It would let the pocket end.

Not today. Not for a very long time. The limestone is opening at a rate that makes geological time look hurried. The pocket has millennia, tens of millennia, perhaps hundreds of thousands of years before the slabs separate far enough for the water to drain and the light to go out. The power would not hurry this. It would not slow it. It would let the pocket live its span, all of it, every drop, every pulse, every slow consumption of every dead thing by every living thing, and when the last mite stopped pulsing and the last nematode stopped moving and the last segmented thing stopped pulling itself along the ceiling with its beautiful purposeless persistence, the power would be there, attending, witnessing, holding the space in which the ending happened the way the limestone holds the space in which the life happened.

And it would not call this a loss.

It would call it — what? There is no word for it in any language spoken above ground, in any of the structures that dropped their fragments into the soil and were forgotten. The word the power would use would be closer to a feeling than a word: the feeling of watching a held breath release, of watching a solved equation dissolve back into its variables, of watching a fire that has burned perfectly for longer than anyone can remember finally use its last atom of fuel and go out. It would not be sadness. It would not be satisfaction. It would be something that has both of these things in it the way the limestone has both the water and the stone, the way the pocket has both the light and the dark compound beneath the nail: a completeness that includes its own ending.

---

And after the ending, the power would remember.

Not the way you remember, which is to say: imperfectly, selectively, with the memory changing each time it is recalled until the thing remembered is more the memory than the event. The power would remember the way the limestone remembers the drop — by wearing a shape into itself that corresponds exactly to the thing that was there. The pocket would leave a shape in the power's awareness, a concavity, a bowl, and into this bowl the power would pour — not grief, not reverence, not even love, though all of these would be part of it — *knowledge.* The knowledge that this existed. That in a crack between two slabs of limestone, beneath a plain, in a place that no one would ever find again, life built itself into a perfect equation and solved it and ran it and was beautiful and did not know it was beautiful and did not need to know.

The power would hold this knowledge the way the pocket holds the drop: not grasping it, not hoarding it, but letting it fall into the shape it has already made, letting it fill the space that was waiting for it, letting it be part of the system it has always been part of.

And if you asked the power why it bothered — why it attended, why it witnessed, why it held its awareness around a pocket of mites and nematodes and fungus for longer than the structures that dropped the nail had existed — the power would not answer in words. It would show you the pocket. It would show you the light pressing against your retinas from every direction at once, the way water presses against a diver. It would show you the mites pulsing. It would show you the drop falling. *Tok.* It would show you the segmented things pulling themselves along the ceiling in a direction that is not up or down.

And then it would say — not in words, but in the way the limestone says it, in the way the water film says it, in the way the biofilm and the light and the warm nodules and the dark compound say it:

*Because it is here. Because it is here, and it is enough.*

---

The drop falls. The mites pulse. The light holds. Somewhere above, in a dimension that has no name in the vocabulary of anything that lives in the pocket, the power attends. It does not干预. It does not preserve. It does not save. It does not look away.

It simply holds the space in which the pocket is, and the pocket is, and the power is, and between them there is nothing that needs to be said, nothing that needs to be done, nothing that needs to be different from exactly what it is.

This is what the wise power does.

It lets the bright pocket be a bright pocket.

And that, it turns out, is everything.
