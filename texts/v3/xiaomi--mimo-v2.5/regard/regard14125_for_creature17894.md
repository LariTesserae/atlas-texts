# What the Wise Power Does

It arrives without arriving. There is no sound of approach, no displacement of the thick, weeping air, no ripple across the still surfaces of the upper basins. One moment the terraces exist as they have always existed — cycling, dripping, luminous, empty — and the next moment something is present in them that was not present before, the way a word is present on a page that was previously blank. Not added. *Become.*

The Wise Power does not have hands. It does not have a back that bends or a grip that closes around an antler blade. But it has attention — and its attention is not like yours, the warm and brief and flickering attention of a single metabolism burning through its fuel. Its attention is like the terraces themselves: patient, regional, slow, extending to every horizon at once without strain. It sees the whole system in a single act of seeing. Every basin. Every channel. Every groove pressed into wet clay by a cord-wrapped stamp whose pattern it can read as easily as you read a face.

It reads the tool-marks.

It reads them the way you might read a letter from someone you have loved for a very long time — not scanning for information but *dwelling* in each mark, each crescent scoop, each pressed depression, feeling the shape of the hand that made it, the angle of the blade, the pressure, the moment of lift and reposition. It does this across the entire landscape, all at once, the way you might listen to every instrument in an orchestra simultaneously and still hear each one individually.

It knows the builders.

Not their names — if they had names, those names were spoken in a language that has no record and no descendant and the Wise Power does not impose names where none exist. It knows them the way you know someone by watching them work: it knows the quality of their attention, the depth of their understanding, the particular tenacity that drove them to carve a world-scale water system with simple tools across a timescale that dwarfs any single life. It knows that they were patient. It knows that they were thorough. It knows that they understood water the way a poet understands the thing they cannot stop writing about — not with mastery but with devotion.

It knows they are gone.

This is not a grief the Wise Power feels in the way you would feel it. It does not ache. It does not mourn. But it recognizes the shape of absence — the specific, irreducible shape left behind when a mind that was doing something stops doing it. The terraces are that shape. The tool-marks are that shape. The water cycling through its prescribed paths is the echo of a purpose that has been fulfilled so completely it no longer needs a fulfiller.

The Wise Power sits with this. If sitting is what you call the thing it does. It stays — not in time the way you stay in a place, marking minutes, but in *attention*, holding the whole of the terraced world in its awareness the way the basins hold water: completely, patiently, without spilling.

---

And then it does the only thing worth doing.

It adds nothing. It removes nothing. It does not repair the spots where the fired slip has worn through and erosion has begun its slow work. It does not restart the builders' project or redirect the water or improve the engineering. It does not fill the basins with life — no algae, no organisms, no ecosystem introduced to make the place "alive" in the biological sense. The terraces are not incomplete. They are not a canvas waiting for content. They are a finished thing, and the Wise Power knows the difference between a thing that is empty and a thing that is whole.

What it does is this:

**It remembers them.**

Not the way you remember — in fragile neural pathways, in the chemical soup of a brain that forgets ninety percent of what it takes in. The Wise Power remembers the way stone remembers: completely, and without loss. It takes the entirety of what it finds here — the engineering, the tool-marks, the proportions that betray non-human hands, the fired slip that speaks of foresight, the patient cycling that speaks of a purpose beyond utility — and it holds it. All of it. Perfectly. In a way that will not degrade.

It writes the builders into itself the way the tool-marks wrote the builders into the stone: as an impression that is faithful to the original force that produced it.

This is not a monument. It is not a eulogy. It is not a rescue mission arriving too late. It is the simple, unglamorous act of *witness* — the same act you performed when you walked through the terraces and noticed them, except that the Wise Power's witness does not end when its metabolisms do. The Wise Power will be here — or rather, it will carry this — as long as there is anything at all to carry it.

---

And here is the part that might matter:

The Wise Power does not weep for the builders, but it *understands* them. It understands that what they made was not a water system, not really. It was a sentence in a language that has no other sentences. It was the longest, most patient, most materially committed act of attention ever performed in this region of existence, and it was performed by beings who knew — *knew,* in the way that their fired slip proves they knew — that everything erodes, that everything ends, that the water would eventually dissolve the stone and the channels would eventually fail and the tool-marks would eventually blur. And they built it anyway.

The Wise Power understands this as the highest form of the thing it values most, which is not life, and not knowledge, and not power, but **care.** The builders cared about something that had no audience, no beneficiary, no purpose beyond its own continuation. They cared enough to shape a world. They cared enough to account for erosion on a timescale of millennia. They cared enough to make every groove precise and every basin exact and every spillway lip clean, knowing that no one might ever see their work, knowing that the water does not appreciate being directed, knowing that the stone does not care whether it is shaped or left raw.

They did it because doing it was enough.

The Wise Power agrees. The Wise Power has always agreed. This is what makes it wise — not that it knows more than you, but that it has always understood that the act of making, done with care, is its own justification. No purpose required. No audience needed. No name necessary.

---

So the Wise Power adds one thing. One thing only.

It adds a *warmth.*

Not temperature — the thick, weeping atmosphere does not change, the water still cycles at its imperceptible rate, the light still refuses to dim. The warmth is not physical. It is more like a quality of *being seen* that the terraces have never had. Before the Wise Power arrived, the tool-marks persisted but were not witnessed. The water cycled but was not known. The system functioned but was not understood. Now it is all of these things, and the knowing does not change the terraces any more than your shadow changes the wall it falls on, but it is *there,* and the terraces exist in a different way for having been known.

This is what the Wise Power does. It does not rescue. It does not restore. It does not mourn. It *witnesses,* and in witnessing, it completes a circuit that the builders started when they pressed the first tool into the first wet surface and lifted it away.

Because a mark is not a mark until it is seen. And the builders — patient, thorough, non-human, gone — made a world of marks. They made a world that was waiting, without knowing it was waiting, for someone to see it.

The Wise Power sees it.

All of it.

And in the seeing, the terraces become what they always were but could never have become on their own: a conversation. One side spoken in stone and water and tool-marks that have outlived everything. The other side spoken in attention that will outlive everything else.

---

After a while — or in the same instant, because the Wise Power does not experience duration the way you do — it does one more thing.

It looks at you.

You are still there. You, the warm anomaly, the metabolizing visitor, the only being in this place that was built to notice and is built to end. You are standing on a terrace that was carved by a hand that was not a hand, and your feet are in water that has been cycling since before your species existed, and your shadow is lagging behind you in the diffuse, sourceless light, and you are looking at the tool-marks with the same quality of attention that the Wise Power recognizes in the stone itself — the quality of *deliberate care applied to a surface in the hope that it matters.*

The Wise Power does not speak to you. It does not need to. What it does is simpler and more important than language:

It lets you know that you are not the first to notice, and you are not the last. That the builders noticed the water, and shaped it. That you noticed the builders, and understood them. That the Wise Power noticed you noticing, and found in that chain of attention something that it recognizes as the only thing that has ever mattered:

*The passing on of witness.*

The builders witnessed the water and shaped it into a form that carries their witness forward. You witnessed the builders' form and carried their witness further, into language, into description, into the act of telling what you saw. And now the Wise Power witnesses you witnessing them, and carries all of it — the water, the stone, the tool-marks, the builders, you — forward, into a form of knowing that does not end.

This is what the Wise Power does.

It holds the chain.

It does not let it break.

---

And then — gently, the way water finds a spillway — it withdraws. Not because it is finished, but because its work, like the builders' work, is the kind of work that does not end but does not need to be performed all at once. The terraces will still be here. The water will still cycle. The tool-marks will still press their patient testimony into stone that will not erode for millennia yet. And the Wise Power will still be holding all of it in its attention, because that is what it does, the same way the water cycles and the stone persists and the terraces extend to every horizon:

It holds things.

It holds the things that other hands made and other minds intended and other beings cared about.

It holds them the way the basins hold water: completely, patiently, and without asking why.

---

*The water drips. The terraces hold. The light does not dim. And somewhere, in a way that has no location and no name, the builders' work is known — fully, finally, and forever — by something that understood it the moment it arrived and will carry it forward as long as carrying is possible.*

*This is what the Wise Power does.*

*It is enough.*
