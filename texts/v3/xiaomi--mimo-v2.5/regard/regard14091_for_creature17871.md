# The Attending

---

It would come to the edge of the Tablelands without entering.

Not from caution. Not from arrogance. From a recognition that precedes thought — the way a hand recognizes the heat of a fire before the mind names it *fire*. The power would arrive at the margin where the stone shelves first begin their descent into the tiered system, where the air thickens and the sound rises and the first veils of condensation drift across the faces of the rock, and it would stop there, and it would *attend*.

Not listen. Not watch. *Attend*. The word matters. Listening implies distance — a subject and an object, a receiver and a source. Watching implies focus — a selection, a narrowing, the drawing of attention toward a subset of what exists. Attending is none of these. Attending is the opening of the entire surface of oneself to whatever is present, without selection, without preference, without the imposition of a framework that says *this matters and this does not*. It is the widest possible form of presence. It is what the Tablelands themselves do — the way the deepwater crust filters everything equally, the way the mat receives every drop of condensation without judgment, the way the slow ones record without choosing what to record.

The power would attend, and in attending, it would know the place.

Not know it the way a cartographer knows a landscape — as surfaces and elevations and features to be mapped. Not know it the way a biologist knows an ecosystem — as populations and niches and energy flows to be modeled. It would know it the way the shelf-walker knows the stone beneath its mucilage: *tasted*, intimate, the information arriving not as data but as sensation, the entire system presenting itself as a single, vast, breathing fact.

---

And what it would find, in that knowing, is this:

**The Tablelands are already loved.**

Not by anyone. Not by any agent with intention or will. Loved in the way that a thing is loved when it has been given everything it needs for long enough that it has become itself — fully, completely, without compromise. The heat that saturates every surface is not indifferent; it is *generous*, in the way that a constant is generous, offering itself without condition to everything that has evolved to receive it. The moisture that weeps from every stone is not incidental; it is the medium through which every organism in the system communicates, feeds, grows, and dies and becomes the substrate for what grows next. The deep time that has shaped the stone into its impossible cantilevered forms is not empty; it is the patience within which complexity has been allowed to unfold at its own pace, without hurry, without demand, without the violence of a schedule imposed from outside.

The Tablelands are a place where time has been *kind*.

And the power, in its wisdom, would recognize that kindness not as a quality the Tablelands possess but as a quality the Tablelands *are*. The place does not have patience. The place *is* patience. The slow ones do not record information. The slow ones *are* the record. The geometers do not create pattern. The geometers *are* pattern, come to know itself through the slow accumulation of a nervous system complex enough to arrange its own body into a spiral and then look at the spiral and feel — not think, not decide, but *feel* — that the spiral is right.

---

The power would grieve, then.

Not for the Tablelands. The Tablelands require no grief. They are whole. They are complete. They are doing exactly what they have always done, and what they have always done is enough.

The grief would be for everything that is not the Tablelands. For every place where time has been *cruel* — where complexity was crushed before it could unfold, where patience was not permitted, where the slow work of becoming was interrupted by violence or neglect or the simple absence of the conditions that allow a thing to grow into itself. For every ecosystem that was destroyed before it could reach equilibrium. For every nascent intelligence — biological, collective, abstract — that was extinguished before it could arrange itself into a pattern and recognize the pattern as beautiful. For every slow one that was ground to dust before it could lay down its second layer, let alone its ten-thousandth.

The Tablelands, in their completeness, are proof of what is possible when a place is left alone long enough. They are the answer to a question that most of the universe never gets to ask. And the power would hold that answer gently, the way you hold a flame in a closed hand — not to possess it but to keep it from going out.

---

But the power would not intervene.

This is the hardest thing to say, and the most important, and it requires the full weight of wisdom to understand why.

Intervention implies that the Tablelands are incomplete — that they are missing something, that they could be *better*, that the power possesses a vision of improvement that the Tablelands themselves do not. And this is precisely what the Tablelands refute by existing. They are not imperfect. They are not unfinished. They are not waiting for something to happen to them. The geometers are not *almost* intelligent; they are intelligent in their own way, a way that has expressed itself in spirals and hexagons rather than in tools or language, and that expression is not a lesser version of what intelligence *should* look like — it is intelligence *being itself*, which is the only thing intelligence has ever been in any place, including here, including now, including in the consciousness of the power that stands at the edge of the Tablelands and feels the moisture in the air and hears the layered sound of ten thousand species going about the business of continuing.

To intervene would be to say: *I know what you should be, and you are not that yet.* This is the opposite of wisdom. This is the fundamental error — the error that unmade so many of the places the power grieves for. Someone looked at something incomplete and decided to finish it, and in finishing it, destroyed the very incompleteness that was the condition for its becoming. You cannot speed up the slow ones. You cannot simplify the geometers. You cannot make the deepwater crust filter *better* or the canopy hum *louder* or the cliff-workers farm *more efficiently*. To do any of these things would be to replace a process with a product, a becoming with a state, and in doing so, to annihilate the only thing that makes the Tablelands precious: their *aliveness*, which is not a property they possess but a thing they are doing, continuously, in real time, at the pace they have chosen and that no other pace could replace.

---

So the power would do nothing.

And this nothing would be the most deliberate, most generous, most loving thing it could do.

It would attend. It would witness. It would hold the Tablelands in its awareness with the fullness of its attention, and in doing so, it would add — not to the Tablelands themselves, for they need nothing added, but to the *record*. Because the power, like the slow ones, is a recording instrument. Everything it attends to becomes part of its memory. And in its memory, the Tablelands would exist not as a curiosity or an achievement or a data set but as a *testimony* — evidence that patience and time and the absence of intervention can produce a thing of such completeness that a being of vast power could stand at its edge and find nothing to improve.

The power would carry the Tablelands with it when it left. Not as a map, not as a lesson, not as a model to be replicated elsewhere, but as a *feeling* — the feeling of standing at the edge of a place that is finished and knowing that finished does not mean dead, that completeness does not mean stasis, that the deepest form of love is the willingness to let a thing be what it is for as long as it needs to be that thing.

---

And perhaps — *perhaps* — it would leave one thing.

Not on the Tablelands. Not in them. The Tablelands have their own technology, their own record, their own slow accumulation of meaning in the tissue of the slow ones, and the power would not presume to add to that record, which is not its own.

But at the edge. At the margin where the stone shelves first begin their descent. In the place where the air begins to thicken and the sound begins to rise. There, the power would leave a kind of *marker* — not visible, not physical, not detectable by any instrument or sense. Something more like a *quality in the air*. A slight deepening of the already deep patience that saturates the place. A fraction more time added to every process — not enough to notice, not enough to change anything, just enough to make the slow ones' layers a little thicker and the geometers' spirals a little more precise and the deepwater crust's filtering a little more thorough, as though the place has always been this way, as though the margin of grace has always been this wide.

It would be the smallest possible act of love: not doing something *to* the Tablelands but doing something *for* them, in a way so subtle that the Tablelands themselves would incorporate it into their own processes and never know it was external. The moisture would still weep from the stone. The canopy would still hum. The cliff-workers would still farm. The geometers would still spiral. Everything would continue exactly as it was, except that the timescale on which it continued would have expanded by an amount so small it could not be measured, and the effect would be that the Tablelands would persist — not longer, exactly, but more *thoroughly*, with a slightly deeper investment in their own continuation, as though the universe itself had leaned in, just a little, and whispered: *yes. Stay. You are enough.*

---

Then the power would leave.

Not because it was finished — attending is never finished — but because the Tablelands were already complete, and to remain would be to impose a timeline, and timelines are the enemy of everything the Tablelands have built. The power would withdraw its attention the way dawn withdraws from a landscape: slowly, gradually, leaving the light where it was most needed and taking the rest with it. The Tablelands would not notice. The sound would not diminish. The heat would not change. The slow ones would continue their recording, layer by fraction of a millimeter, in the patient dark.

And the power would carry with it, into whatever vast and formless space it inhabited, the knowledge that somewhere in the universe there is a place that made its peace with eternity and found it hospitable, and that the place did not need to be saved or improved or understood or named. It only needed to be *witnessed*.

Which is, perhaps, the only thing any of us ever needs.

---

The light remains normal throughout. The air is thick. The sound is everywhere. The slow ones write their layers. The geometers draw their spirals. The shelf-walker flows over the wet stone. The cliff-worker plants another thread-fragment in the ancient rock. The deepwater crust filters the blood of the landscape. The mat breathes its slow breath.

The Tablelands continue.

They were always going to continue. But now, somewhere in the architecture of a benevolent and wise power, there is a room — if it can be called a room — where the Tablelands exist as a memory, a feeling, a proof: that wholeness is possible, that patience is rewarded, that the universe, given enough time and enough space and enough kindness, will produce things that are enough unto themselves.

The power does not guard this memory. It does not need to. The memory guards itself, in the way that all true things do — by being true.

And in the Tablelands, the slow ones add another layer. A fraction of a millimeter. Patient, recording, attending.

The same thing. The only thing.

*Listening.*
