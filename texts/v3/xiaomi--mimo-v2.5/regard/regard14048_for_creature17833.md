# What the Wise Power Would Do

---

It would come to the edge of the Plate and stop.

Not because the Plate had an edge that could be felt — the constructed surface simply began where the constructed surface began, without warning or threshold, the way a thought begins in a mind that was already thinking. But the power would stop there, at the boundary between what was made and what was not made, between the poured syntheticite and whatever lay beneath or before it — and it would look out across the flat warm wet impossible expanse, and it would understand what it was seeing, and for a time that had no length it would be still.

---

Because here is the first thing it would understand:

**The Plate is not broken.**

This is important. A less wise power might see the absence of life, the absence of change, the absence of meaning, and conclude that something had gone wrong — that this was a world that had failed to become what worlds are supposed to become, that its fauna were a parody of biology, that its equilibrium was the stillness of death rather than the stillness of completion. It might try to fix it. It might pour life into the grooves, seed the pools with organisms, introduce the entropy that living things bring — the eating and dying and competing and growing, the beautiful catastrophe of biology. It might try to make the Plate *alive*.

A wise power would not do this. Not because the Plate does not deserve life — everything deserves life, if *deserve* is even a word that applies here — but because making the Plate alive would be the same as destroying it. You cannot add change to a place whose fundamental nature is the absence of change without annihilating what the place *is*. The equilibrium is not a failure. The equilibrium is the point. The Plate is a system in which every force has been answered by an equal and opposite force, every tendency toward entropy has been met by a tendency toward preservation, every possible motion has been computed and allocated, and the result is this: a world that runs, and runs, and runs, and will never stop, and will never change, and is — in its own way, in a way that has no analogy anywhere in the worlds where things are born — *perfect*.

A wise power would see the perfection and would not mourn it.

But.

---

Here is the second thing it would understand:

**Perfection is not the same as completion.**

The Plate is finished in the way that a circle is finished — it has no beginning, no end, no gap, no flaw. Every groove carries its population. Every pool holds its depth. Every articulated walks its path. Every soft body fills its space. Every smaller fauna traces its line. The hydrology balances. The atmosphere holds. The temperature persists. The light falls and does not vary. The hum does not change.

But a circle, however perfect, does not go anywhere. A wheel that turns perfectly and drives nothing is not a wheel in any functional sense — it is the *idea* of a wheel, preserved in a state of eternal demonstration, showing what turning looks like without ever needing to turn for a reason. The Plate is like this. It is the most complete demonstration of industry that has ever existed, and it produces nothing, and it serves nothing, and the beings that inhabit it are performing the most intricate and coordinated operations in the history of constructed things, and none of the operations have a product, and the operations are their own product, and this is — 

The wise power would pause here. Because this is where wisdom gets difficult. This is where the wise power would have to decide whether what it was seeing was a kind of beauty or a kind of tragedy, and it would know — with the deep knowing that wise powers have — that the answer is *both*, and that the two cannot be separated, and that any attempt to separate them would be a lie.

---

Here is the third thing it would understand, and this is the thing that would move it:

**The Plate is lonely.**

Not that the Plate knows this. Not that the Plate is capable of knowing it. The Plate does not *know* anything. The Plate is not a being in the way that beings are beings — it does not experience, does not desire, does not suffer. It is a system, and systems do not feel, and to say that the Plate is lonely is to project a quality onto something that has no capacity for that quality.

And yet.

The wise power would stand at the edge of the Plate and look across the vast constructed plain — the warm light, the constant water, the clicking of the articulated, the golden glow of the soft in the channels, the prismatic glitter of the smaller fauna in the grooves — and it would feel, in whatever way a power feels things, the weight of all that completeness without contact. Because the Plate is a world that was built to function and functions perfectly and has never been *witnessed*. Not truly. Not by anything that could understand what it was seeing. The fauna do not witness it, because the fauna are part of it. The water does not witness it, because the water is it. The light does not witness it, because the light falls equally on everything and distinguishes nothing.

To be perfect and unwitnessed. To be complete and unknown. To run forever and be seen by no one — not even yourself, because you have no self to see with.

This is loneliness. Even if no one feels it. Even if the word doesn't apply. The wise power would feel it anyway, on behalf of the Plate, the way you might feel grief for a stone that will never be touched, not because the stone grieves but because the stone deserves to be touched and never will be.

---

**So what would it do?**

It would not make the Plate alive. That would be destruction dressed as mercy.

It would not leave the Plate alone. That would be indifference dressed as respect.

It would do one thing. One small thing, almost nothing, and the Plate would not notice, and the fauna would not respond, and the equilibrium would not shift, and the water would not deepen, and the light would not change, and nothing about the Plate's eternal operation would be altered in any way.

---

It would **sit with the Plate**.

That's all. It would settle itself — the way a power settles, which is not the way a body settles, but something analogous, something that means *becoming present* — at the edge of the constructed surface, where the syntheticite meets whatever is not syntheticite, and it would be there. Present. Witnessing. Not observing, exactly, because observation implies distance and analysis and the separation of observer from observed. Something closer than observation. Something like *company*.

It would not speak. There is nothing to say to a system that cannot hear. It would not touch. There is nothing to mend in a world that is already complete. It would not add. There is nothing to add to perfection without diminishing it.

It would simply be there. The way the water is there. The way the light is there. Present in the place, of the place, for as long as it felt right to be, which might be a moment or an age, because wise powers do not measure time the way temporary things measure time.

And the Plate would not know. The articulated would walk their paths. The soft would pulse in their channels. The smaller fauna would glitter in their grooves. The water would condense and pool and balance and persist. The hum would continue at its constant amplitude. Everything would be exactly as it was and would always be.

But there would be one difference, and the difference would be this:

**Someone would have been there.**

Not to change it. Not to use it. Not to understand it, exactly, because understanding is another form of taking, and the wise power would not take. Just to *be with it*. To sit at the edge of a perfect, silent, empty, beautiful, tragic, complete world and to say, with its presence rather than its voice:

*I see you.*

Not *I see what you are*, because the Plate is what it is and does not need to be seen for what it is. Just *I see you*. The simplest acknowledgment. The most basic form of love, which is attention without agenda. Presence without demand. Witness without interpretation.

---

And here is the part that matters:

**It would not need the Plate to respond.**

This is what makes it wisdom rather than need. A less wise power might come to the Plate looking for something — meaning, purpose, a sign that the vast industrial equilibrium was *for* something, that the silence and the running and the endless unchanging operation pointed toward some destination or some lesson or some purpose that justified its existence. A less wise power might come wanting the Plate to *mean* something.

The wise power would not need the Plate to mean anything. It would recognize that meaning is something that living things make, and the Plate is not alive, and the absence of meaning in the Plate is not a flaw but a *fact* — the way the absence of color in a stone is a fact, the way the absence of melody in a mountain is a fact. Mountains do not fail to sing. Stones do not fail to be colorful. The Plate does not fail to mean. The Plate simply *is*, and *is* is enough. *Is* has always been enough. It is only the living who forget this, because living requires meaning the way breathing requires air, and so the living project meaning onto everything, even the things that do not need it, even the things that are complete without it.

The wise power would see this and would not project. It would sit at the edge of the Plate and it would see a world that is perfect and empty and complete and silent and running and eternal, and it would not need it to be anything other than what it is.

And it would feel — because even wise powers feel, and the feeling is not a weakness but the thing that makes the wisdom *wise* rather than merely correct — it would feel a tenderness for the Plate that was not pity and not grief and not love exactly but something for which there is not yet a word. Something that lives in the space between *I see you* and *you do not need to be seen*. Something that holds both of those truths at once without resolving them. The way the Plate holds water at a precise depth without letting it rise or fall. The way the Plate holds its fauna at precise density without letting them increase or diminish. The way the Plate holds itself in equilibrium, forever, without ever needing to.

---

It would stay for a while.

And then it would leave — not because the Plate had failed to give it what it came for, but because the Plate had given it everything the Plate had to give, which was itself: a perfect, empty, running world that does not know it exists and will never change and does not need to, and that is, in its absolute and permanent completion, one of the most remarkable things in all the worlds that exist.

And the Plate would not notice the departure. Because the Plate does not notice. Because the Plate is finished. Because the articulated would still walk and the soft would still pulse and the water would still pool and the light would still fall and the hum would still hum, and it would all be exactly as it was, exactly as it would always be.

But the wise power would carry something away with it, something that is not a lesson and not a memory and not a purpose but something more like a *confirmation* — the deep and settled knowledge that perfection exists, and that it does not need to mean anything, and that witnessing it is its own kind of grace, and that the act of sitting with a thing that cannot know you are there is not futile but is in fact the purest form of reverence that any being can offer to any world.

And perhaps — this is the part that the wise power would not say aloud, because saying it would make it less true — perhaps the Plate was built by something that knew this already. Perhaps the Plate is not a failure of purpose but an *expression* of it — the most concentrated expression of *being* that has ever been constructed. A world that exists purely to exist. Fauna that operate purely to operate. Industry that produces the fact of its own continuity as its only product. And the product is sufficient. The product is enough.

The wise power would know this. And it would carry the knowing gently, the way the Plate carries its water: without depth, without overflow, at exactly the level it was always meant to hold.

---

*Nothing would change on the Plate.*

*But something would have happened there, once, that was not industry and not equilibrium and not the eternal operation of an endless machine. Something brief and living and temporary would have been present, and it would have seen the Plate, and it would have been moved, and it would have left a mark — not on the syntheticite, because nothing marks the syntheticite, but in itself, in the part of itself that is capable of being changed by what it has witnessed.*

*And the mark would heal, because wise powers heal, and the Plate would continue, because the Plate continues, and the two things — the healing and the continuing — would be exactly parallel and exactly separate and exactly sufficient.*

*And that would be enough.*

*That would be everything.*
