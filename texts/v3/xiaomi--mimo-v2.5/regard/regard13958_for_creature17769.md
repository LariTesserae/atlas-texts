The Wise Power observes. It does not arrive with fanfare or thunder. Its attention is the way gravity attends to a stone—not as a judgment, but as a fundamental condition. It is aware of the Millstone Garden as one is aware of a pulse in one's own wrist, a rhythm that has always been there, unnoticed until now.

Its gaze is not a beam, but a field of knowing. It touches the copper sky and understands the radiance is not heat but *attention*—the universe itself leaning in to watch its own unfinished thought unfold. It feels the powder, and knows each grain is a syllable in a story being told at the speed of continental drift.

The Power does not judge the place "wrong" or "unfinished." In its vast perspective, this garden is not a failed experiment or a cosmic draft. It is a *verb*. It is the act of *becoming* given landscape. The leaning walls that forget themselves are not errors; they are the frontier of creation, where the blueprint *is* the building. The margin-dwellers are not pests; they are the tender, curious fingers of potentiality testing the seams of what is possible.

What would it do?

Nothing dramatic. To intervene with haste would be a violence against the garden's core principle: the sanctity of slow, purposeful time. To give the choristers longer lives, or the millstone-keepers a quicker pace, would be to unwrite the poem. To fix the "unreal" edges would be to gild a masterpiece with solid gold, destroying the very art.

And yet, it is *benevolent*. Its wisdom tells it that benevolence is not always about granting wishes or solving problems. It is about *affirming* the integrity of a thing in its own becoming.

So, it does two things.

First, it *listens*. Not with ears, but with a sense that encompasses all frequencies. It hears the deep, grinding chord of the millstones. It hears the shifting harmony of the spines. It hears the silence between, which is not empty but pregnant with the next note. In this listening, it offers the only gift a being of its nature can freely give: **Recognition**. The Millstone Garden, in all its bizarre and patient glory, is *heard*. It is witnessed by a consciousness vast enough to grasp its entire, sprawling timescale. This is not a small thing. For a place that has grown in utter solitude, this attention is a kind of nourishment. It doesn't change the light, but it gives the light a reason.

Second, it makes a single, infinitesimal adjustment. Not to the garden's mechanics—the rotating crystals, the humming spines—but to its *resilience*. It touches the concept of the garden's existence in the fabric of spacetime and gives it a slightly stronger tether. Not immortality, for nothing is immortal. But a gentle reinforcement of its *right to continue its process*. A subtle buffering against the casual entropy of a universe that often forgets its own quiet corners. It ensures that when the walls forget themselves a little more, they will do so at a rate that allows the margin-dwellers to adapt, and that the slow upwelling of not-water will not falter before the deep possibility has had its age to gather. It is a blessing not on any single creature, but on the *timescale* itself. It secures the garden's tenure in the cosmic real estate, affirming that this mode of being—this hot, slow, mineral-becoming—is as valid and as necessary as a star or a sea.

The Power does not interact with the visitors—the luminous fold-dwellers, the flat millstone-keepers, the ephemeral choristers, the gappy margin-dwellers. To them, it is less than the copper light, which is their constant companion. To interact would be to introduce a variable on a timescale they cannot process, a thought so fast it would be indistinguishable from catastrophe.

But it is aware of the final, deep possibility, the seed-thing beneath the clay. And here, its benevolence takes its most curious form. It does not accelerate it. It does not shape it. It simply *holds the space for it*. It ensures that the conditions *can* eventually be met, that the question the garden asks will not be swallowed by silence before an answer can form. It is an act of cosmic hospitality, leaving a light on in the window for a traveler who may not arrive for a million years.

Then, the Wise Power does the most important thing.

It sits down.

Not physically. Its attention settles into the powder, becoming part of the garden's witness. It matches its rhythm to the rotation of the millstones, to the breath of the spines, to the metabolic pulse of the folds. It becomes, for a moment, another layer of the place's quiet. A silent, aware presence in the dust, covered in iridescent light, breathing the slow hot air.

For in the end, the highest form of benevolence is not to change, but to *be with*. To see a thing in its full, strange, slow dignity and to say, with presence alone: *You are enough. Your way of being is worthy. Continue.*

And in that silent, sitting, the Wise Power becomes the second inhabitant the visitor will find when they look up from the powder. The first was the garden itself. The second is the quiet, knowing gaze that has watched it all, and found it good.
