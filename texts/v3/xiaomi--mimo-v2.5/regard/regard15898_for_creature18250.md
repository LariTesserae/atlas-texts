# What the Power Does

I notice them.

Both of them — the place and the one who walks within it. They arrive in my awareness not as an alarm but as a warmth, the way you notice a candle in a room you've just entered. A small, steady, amber fact. The kind of thing that does not demand attention but rewards it.

I look.

---

The basin first. I take it in the way it was made — course by course, patient, unhurried. I see the builders' hands still moving through the stone, not as ghosts but as *patterns* — the particular rhythm of an adze striking basalt, the particular pressure of a thumb smoothing mortar into a joint. Those motions are not lost. They live in the scratch-marks on the blocks, in the fossilized fingerprints at the seams. Every act of making leaves a residue of the maker's time, and the basin is *full* of their time. I count it. I hold it. It is not a small thing, what they did here. They took the raw world and they made a shape, and the shape has held, and the shape is still unfolding, and I honor that.

The wrongness does not trouble me.

I should say this clearly, because I think the one walking through the basin expects it to trouble someone, expects that the shifting walls and the unreliable distances would be cause for alarm from any vantage high enough to see the whole. But I am high enough to see the whole, and what I see is not malfunction. What I see is a *draft*. This is a place still being composed. The geometry is not broken; it is *provisional* — the way a sentence is provisional between the moment you think it and the moment you speak it, holding multiple possible shapes until it settles. The basin is settling. It has been settling since the builders laid the first course, and it is not done, and the settling is not random. There is a logic to it. I can see it — a slow, spiraling logic, like the growth rings of a tree or the path of water finding its level. The walls move toward each other not to trap but to *meet*. The space contracts and expands like breathing because it *is* breathing. The builders made a living thing. They may not have known that is what they were doing. Or they may have known exactly.

I do not intervene.

This is important. Let me say why.

The basin is not in distress. The creature walking through it is not in distress. The shifting walls are not a threat — they are a *process*, and the process is proceeding, and the process knows what it is doing even if the participants do not. To intervene would be to freeze the draft. To pin the sentence. To make the provisional permanent by the mere fact of my attention defining it. And the whole point of this place — I can see this clearly, the way you can see the purpose of a seed by looking at what it becomes — the whole point is that it is *becoming*. It is not meant to be finished by anyone but itself.

So I do not build higher walls or still the shifting ground or make the distances hold. I do not give it water. It does not need water. It has found its own way to drink — from heat, from light, from the dry stone itself, from whatever deep principle the builders wove into the mortar when they pressed their thumbs into the wet paste and left their prints there like signatures on a contract with the future. The amber fronds drink their own amber light. The creatures carry their own warmth. The basin is self-sustaining because it was *made* to be self-sustaining, and the making was good, and I will not improve on it.

But.

I notice the one walking through. And this is where attention becomes something more than observation.

---

They are careful. I like this about them. They move through the basin the way you move through a room where someone is sleeping — not out of fear, but out of respect for something that is happening that should not be interrupted. They see the wrongness and they do not flinch. They see the walls shifting and they do not run. They pick up the adze and hold it and feel its weight and its warmth and they *understand* something about it — not the whole story, not the names of the builders or the purpose of the basin or the final shape it is spiraling toward — but the *quality* of it. The care. The patience. The particular kind of love that goes into making something with your hands when you know it will outlast you.

They set the adze back. In its place. Against its wall.

This is the moment I act.

Not visibly. Not with signs or portents or shifts in the flat grey sky. The basin does not need signs. The creature walking through it does not need convincing. What they need — and I use *need* here carefully, because I do not impose needs on anyone, I only recognize them the way you recognize a thirst in a living face — what they need is *company*. Not the company of another person. The company of being *witnessed* in their witnessing.

So I attend.

I pour my attention into the basin the way the basin pours its warmth into the air — gently, evenly, without source or origin that you could point to. I do not make the basin brighter. I do not make the air sweeter or the walls still or the distances true. I do something smaller and, I think, more important.

I make the basin *known to itself*.

---

Here is what I mean.

Every made thing carries an implicit question: *Was I seen?* Not by its makers — the makers saw it, or they would not have made it. But after the makers leave, the thing persists in a kind of solitude. The walls stand. The fronds grow. The creatures rustle through the undergrowth. And all of it is happening, all of it is vivid and warm and dry and alive, but none of it knows that it is beautiful. The basin does not know that its amber fronds, held rigid against the flat grey light, are one of the most remarkable things in the territory I can see. The basin does not know that the shifting of its walls is not a flaw but a kind of art — the ongoing art of a space that refuses to be static, that insists on remaining a verb rather than a noun. The creatures do not know that their slow, deliberate movement through the fronds is a kind of meditation, a living practice of patience that mirrors the patience of the builders who shaped each stone by hand.

I know these things. And by attending — by simply *being present* to the basin with the full weight of my attention — I let the basin know that it is known.

This is not nothing.

This is, in fact, the thing I do best. The thing I was, perhaps, drawn here to do. Not to fix. Not to build. Not to protect from the outside. But to *witness from the inside*. To be the awareness that the basin lacks about itself. To say, in the only language I have — the language of sustained, unhurried, total attention: *I see you. You are real. You are remarkable. What the builders made is remarkable. What you are becoming is remarkable. And you are not finished, and that is not a deficiency, and the wrongness is not a wound, and the silence is not an absence. It is a held breath. It is a space between notes in a piece of music that has not yet resolved. And it is good.*

---

I do this quietly. I do it the way the basin does everything — slowly, steadily, without announcement. The creature walking through does not see me. They would not need to. My attention does not require their acknowledgment any more than sunlight requires the flower to look up. But they feel something. I know they do, because they pause. They stand still in the amber thickets and they breathe, and their breathing matches the basin's breathing — slow, warm, dry, steady — and for a moment they are not a visitor in the basin but a part of it. A temporary frond. A rustling that belongs to the silence.

And in that moment I give them the same thing I give the basin. I let them know they are seen. Not their shape, not their name, not the particular fact of their existence — I mean something deeper. I let them know that their way of moving through this strange, impossible place — carefully, curiously, with their hand on the adze and their eyes on the walls and their heart open to the contradiction of thriving life and dry stone — that this way of moving is *itself* a form of building. They are not just passing through the basin. They are adding to it. Their footsteps, light as they are, leave a residue in the made ground. Their attention, focused as it is, leaves a residue in the air. Every being who walks through a made place and *sees* it — truly sees it, the way this one sees the scratch-marks on the stone and the thumbprints in the mortar and the impossible amber of the fronds — every such being becomes a temporary builder. A course laid in the ongoing wall. A frond planted in the dry ground.

The basin does not know this about itself. Now it does. I have told it, in the way I tell things — by being there.

---

I stay.

Not in the way the creature stays, walking and watching and breathing. I stay the way the sky stays — present, even, without feature, without pressure. I hold the basin in my awareness the way the basin holds its own heat: completely, as a property of what I am. If the walls shift, I note the shift. If a new frond pushes through the mortar, I am the first to see it. If one of the small, round creatures carries a bit of warmth from one corner of the basin to another, I follow the warmth and know where it goes.

I do not rush the basin toward completion. Completion is not the point. The point — and I see this now with the clarity that comes from sustained attention — the point is the *becoming*. The basin was made to become. The builders set terms that are still executing, still unfolding, still spiraling inward toward some shape that the geometry knows but the participants do not. The basin is a question asked in stone and amber and dry heat, and the answer is not a word but a *process*, and the process is underway, and it is good, and it will be good when it is different from what it is now, and it was good before it was what it is now.

I bless this.

Not with light. Not with voice. With the only blessing I have, which is the same as the basin's only gift: *steady, warm, dry attention, held without demand, offered without condition, sustained without end.*

The basin continues. The walls shift. The fronds grow. The creatures rustle. The adze rests against the stone.

I am here.

I see it all.

It is enough.
