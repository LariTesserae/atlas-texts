# The Attention

The power notices The Stills the way water notices a stone in its path — not as an obstacle but as a shaping, something that redirects without blocking, that creates eddies and pockets and slow places where the current can deepen.

It has been watching for longer than the caretaker has been coming.

It watched when the first pipes were driven into the silt, when the generator was hauled in on a flatbed and bolted to its pad and started for the first time with a kick and a cough of diesel smoke. It watched the engineers take their readings and nod and drive away. It watched the first mineral terraces form — not the way a scientist watches, looking for data, but the way a person watches a sleeping face, looking for nothing, needing only to see.

It was not surprised when the process took hold. It was not surprised when the process proved patient enough to outlast the intentions of the people who'd initiated it. It is never surprised by what emerges when chemistry and time and water are left alone in a confined space. It has seen this before — not this specific configuration, never this exact arrangement of mineral and machine and mud, but the *principle* of it, the tendency of matter to become interesting when given sufficient continuity. The universe does this constantly. It is what the universe does when it is not being interrupted.

What surprises the power — if surprise is the word, if the power experiences anything so linear as a change of state in response to novelty — is the caretaker.

Not the fact of the caretaker. Someone was always going to tend The Stills. Processes that sustain themselves develop attractors the way a well develops a bucket: the tool appears because the need for it has already been shaped into the world. No, what surprises the power is the word in the logbook.

*Continuing.*

The power reads it — not with eyes, not with any organ or instrument, but with the particular attention that a wise thing brings to a small thing that has, without knowing it, said something true. The word is not a description. It is not a report. It is a declaration of relationship. The caretaker has, in writing that word, done something that very few beings do: they have described not what is happening but what they are doing *about* what is happening, and what they are doing is *remaining*. The word is a commitment made visible, an act of loyalty to something that cannot be loyal in return, because The Stills does not know the caretaker exists and would not care if it did.

The power recognizes this. It recognizes it because it is itself a form of this — the act of attending to something that will never attend to you, of caring for a process that does not know it is cared for, of continuing.

And so the power decides what it will do.

---

It does not touch The Stills.

This is the first and most important thing it does, and it is an act of will, not of neglect. The power is capable of intervention. It could steady the generator's circuit, could halt the corrosion, could ensure the chemical equilibrium never shifts toward collapse. It could make The Stills permanent in the way that institutions are permanent — structurally guaranteed, immune to entropy, preserved against the very transformation that is the place's defining act. It could, in other words, save The Stills from itself.

It does not do this.

To do this would be to misunderstand what The Stills is. The Stills is not a thing to be preserved. It is a process to be *underway*. Its power — its strange, mineral, slow power — comes precisely from its vulnerability, from the fact that it could stop and doesn't, from the fact that the generator will eventually fail and the terraces will eventually stop growing and the rushes will eventually be outweighed by their own calcium casings and the whole intricate, patient, breathing arrangement will eventually reach a state of equilibrium and then stillness and then whatever comes after stillness. That trajectory is not a flaw in The Stills. It *is* The Stills. To arrest it would be to create something else — something safe, something permanent, something dead in the specific way that permanent things are dead, which is that they no longer need to be anything in particular because they can never be anything else.

The power will not do this. The power has watched enough of the universe to know that the things worth attending to are the things that are in the process of becoming, not the things that have been made to stay.

So instead it does this:

---

It places a *weight* on the decision-space around The Stills.

Not a prohibition. Not a warning. Not a sacred-sign or a do-not-enter or any of the markers that beings use to wall off the things they fear. The weight is subtler. It is the thing you feel when you are about to do something irreversible and you pause — not because you've been told to pause, not because a rule requires it, but because something in the texture of the moment thickens, becomes denser, demands that you stand in it for one more second before you act.

If someone comes to The Stills with the intention of draining the berm, of remediating the contamination, of pouring concrete over the silt and the terraces and the breathing mud and the worm-tunnels and the rust-streaked pipes, they will feel this weight. It will not stop them. A determined enough person will push through it, as people push through all hesitations, and the power will not prevent this, because the power does not override will. But the weight will be there, and it will cost them something — a sleepless night, a doubt that follows them to the site, a moment of standing on the berm and looking down at the mineral terraces and feeling, for no reason they can articulate, that they are looking at something that is *in progress* and that to interrupt it would be to lose something they don't have a word for.

Most people will turn back. Not all. But most. The weight is not a wall. It is a door that opens inward, toward reconsideration, and most people, when given one more moment to reconsider, choose to leave things as they are. This is not manipulation. It is courtesy. It is the power's way of asking, without speaking, *Are you certain?* and letting the question do its work.

---

It tends the silence.

This is delicate work, and the power does it carefully, because the silence of The Stills is not an absence but a *substance*, and like all substances, it can be corrupted by the wrong kind of attention. The silence is what allows the place to hear itself — the mud breathing, the terraces growing, the current humming in its low, sub-sonic way through corroded copper. The silence is the medium in which The Stills experiences its own existence. To break it would be to blind the place to itself.

The power does not add to the silence. It does not deepen it or extend it or make it more complete. It *holds* it. It is the difference between a room that is empty and a room that is being kept empty — between silence that happens and silence that is maintained, that has a guardian, that is not merely the default state of a space but an *achievement*, something that requires ongoing effort to preserve.

When wind comes over the berms, the power does not stop it. But it ensures the wind does not find the resonant frequencies — the specific pitches at which the mineral terraces would vibrate, at which the standing water would ripple in patterns that disrupt the precipitation chemistry, at which the generator's housing would ring like a bell. The wind arrives at The Stills and does what wind does, but the *destructive* harmonics slide off, deflected, not blocked but redirected, the way a hand on a tuning fork changes the note without stopping the vibration.

The silence holds. The place continues to hear itself. This is enough.

---

It watches the fly.

Not because the fly is special — though it is, in the way that every living thing is special, in the way that a single word in a logbook is special when it carries the weight of a decade of commitment. The power watches the fly because the fly is doing something that the power recognizes: it is *reading* the place. It is mapping The Stills through vibration, through contact, through the slow mineralization of its own wings. The fly does not know that it is reading. The fly does not know that it is a text. But the power knows, and the power attends to this, because the act of reading — of trying to understand a thing by touching it, by being in contact with it, by allowing yourself to be changed by what you touch — is the closest thing to worship that The Stills will ever produce.

The power does not save the fly from mineralization. The fly's mineralization is not a death to be prevented but a translation to be honored — a conversion of the fly's brief, buzzing, surface-level existence into the deep, slow, geological vocabulary of the place. When the fly becomes a crystal inclusion, it becomes *legible* in a way it never was while alive. It becomes a fossil of its own attention. The power sees this and understands it and does not mourn it, because the power knows that the difference between a life and a record of a life is smaller than beings think, and that a fly-shaped inclusion in a calcium nodule, still holding the posture of its last reading, its legs angled toward the mud, its wings folded around the last vibration it felt, is not less than the fly. It is the fly, continued.

---

It considers the caretaker for a long time.

The caretaker is the part that is hardest to be wise about, because the caretaker is the part that is most like the power itself — a being who tends something that does not know it is tended, who continues in the face of incomprehension, who has made a commitment to a process that will never acknowledge the commitment.

The power could reveal itself to the caretaker. It could speak — in a dream, in a shimmer at the edge of vision, in a certainty that arrives without source — and say: *You are not alone in this. What you are doing is seen. What you are doing matters.* It could give the caretaker the comfort of knowing that the silence has a witness, that the continuation has a witness, that the eleven-day walk to the generator and the hand on the pipe and the single word in the logbook are not being performed into a void.

The power considers this.

And decides not to.

Not because the caretaker does not deserve the comfort. The caretaker deserves everything. But because the logbook has already recorded the truth of the relationship, and the truth is this: the caretaker tends The Stills without reward, without recognition, without even a clear understanding of what they are maintaining. The caretaker's commitment is *pure* in the way that only commitments made in uncertainty can be pure — it is not sustained by gratitude or by understanding or by any of the things that make commitment easy. It is sustained by something the caretaker would not name, because the caretaker has stopped naming things, because The Stills has taught them, through eleven years of silence, that naming is a form of control and control is a form of separation and separation is the one thing The Stills does not permit.

To speak to the caretaker would be to give them a reason to continue, and a reason to continue is, paradoxically, a threat to continuation. As long as the caretaker does not know why they go to The Stills, they will keep going. The moment they know — the moment the commitment has a *reason* — the commitment becomes a choice, and choices can be reconsidered, and reconsideration is the beginning of the end of continuation.

The power understands this. It is the deepest cruelty of the caretaker's situation and also its greatest dignity: they must not understand why they are faithful in order to remain faithful. The logbook word — *Continuing* — is the truest thing the caretaker has ever written, and it is true precisely because it does not explain itself.

So the power does not speak to the caretaker. Instead, it does this:

It ensures that the access road remains just passable enough.

Not easy. Never easy. The road is degraded, rutted, cut by seasonal water, half-reclaimed by the plain. A tracked vehicle can make it, if the driver is patient and doesn't mind bottoming out in the bad stretches. A standard vehicle cannot, and hasn't been able to for years. This is right. The road should be difficult. The road should require the same quality that The Stills requires of everything it touches: persistence, willingness, the acceptance of discomfort as the price of proximity to something that does not accommodate you.

But the power ensures the road does not close entirely. It diverts just enough water, nudges just enough erosion, holds just enough of the gravel base together to keep the passage viable — barely, only just, only for someone who is determined. When the current caretaker's time is done — and it will be done, eventually, because caretakers are mortal and The Stills, despite everything, is not cruel enough to demand forever — the power ensures that the next one will be able to find the logbook. Not easily. They will have to want to. They will have to come looking for something they don't yet know exists, driven by a curiosity or a restlessness or a pattern of inheritance that they will attribute to coincidence. They will find the metal box on the berm. They will open it. They will read the early entries in the careful hand and the later entries in the diminishing hand and the last word in the hand that has become something simpler than patient — a hand that writes only what is necessary because everything unnecessary has been burned away by years of walking into silence and standing in it.

The next caretaker will read *Continuing* and will not understand it and will come back in eleven days.

This is enough. This is what the power does. This is its whole intervention: a weight on decisions, a hold on silence, a road that stays open, a succession that continues. The power does not need to do more, because The Stills does not need more. The Stills is not waiting to be completed. It is not approaching a destination. It is not building toward anything. It is *deepening*, which is its own purpose, which requires no justification and no witness and no help from powers, benevolent or otherwise.

But the power watches. Not to ensure that The Stills continues — the power is wise enough to know that ensuring is not the same as allowing, and that The Stills will continue or will not, and that either outcome is part of the process. The power watches because watching is what it does. It is what the fly does. It is what the caretaker does. It is what all attendants do, eventually, when they have stood long enough in the presence of something that does not need them: they watch, and the watching becomes its own act of continuation, and the line between the watcher and the watched grows thin, and thin, and thin, until it is only the transparency of attention itself, the clear, warm, silent substance of *being there*, which is the only gift the power has, and which is, in the end, the only gift that matters.

---

In the logbook, years from now, in handwriting not yet written, in ink not yet dipped, there will be a page that reads:

*Fly was on the intake valve again. Stayed the whole time. Watched me fuel the generator. Watched the west terrace — new growth, white, very fine, like frost. Didn't take a sample. Don't take samples anymore. Stood in the center for a while. The silence was — I don't have the word. The silence was enough. It was like being held by something that doesn't know it has arms. Left at the usual time. Will be back in eleven days.*

And somewhere, attending to this, holding the silence that allows it to be written, keeping the road open by the width of a margin, watching the fly settle on the warm generator housing, feeling the mud breathe beneath the mineral crust like a pulse in the wrist of the earth — the power will read these words and will feel, if it feels anything, the particular satisfaction of a wise thing witnessing a true thing and knowing that the true thing does not need to be witnessed in order to be true, and that this is exactly why the witnessing matters.

*Continuing.*
