# What the Wise Power Does

It notices first the water.

Not because the water calls out — it doesn't, has never, will never — but because the water is already doing the thing the power recognizes as most essential: it is *going on*. Rising without being summoned. Condensing without being asked. Running down stone faces in sheets so thin they are almost ideas of sheets, pooling in basins that glow with the chemical patience of eons. The water has never once considered whether it should continue. It simply does.

The power watches this for a long time. Not clock-time. The time it takes for the water to learn the particular shape of a new crack, for a mineral stain to deepen from yellow to rust-red across a century of seepage, for a vein-dancer to complete one slow circuit of its basin. The power has this kind of time. It has more. But it spends some of it here, and the spending is not casual — it is the kind of attention that leaves a mark, though not the kind you could point to.

---

What it finds, watching the water, is this: the place is whole.

Not perfect. Not optimized. Whole in the way a heartbeat is whole — a system that includes failure, variation, the slow drift of parameters across deep time. The machinery hums below hearing, and some of the basalt cylinders are cracked, and the iron pipes have rusted to a particular shade of ochre that matches the stone so well the pipes look like they grew there, and the condensation arrays sway slightly in thermal updrafts, and the whole system works not because every part is functional but because the *pattern* is functional, the way a forest works not because every tree is alive but because the pattern of living and dying trees is a forest.

The power sees this and is pleased. Not proud — it did not make this. Not relieved — it was never worried. Pleased in the way that recognition pleases: *yes, this is a thing, and it is going on, and it has the quality of going-on that is the closest thing to beauty that systems produce.*

It looks at the keepers.

The beings move slowly across the terraces, their bare feet reading the vibrations of the stone the way another kind of being might read a language. They are wet. They have always been wet. The water on their skin is not a condition of their environment but a condition of their existence — without it, they would be something else, something that does not belong here, and they know this in the way that bodies know things, not in the way that minds think them.

The power watches a keeper tend a condensation array. The keeper's hands move along the thin metal sheets, wiping away the accumulated moisture with a cloth, checking the troughs, adjusting the angle of a sheet by a degree or two. The work is not difficult. The work is not easy. The work is *continuous*, and the continuity is the thing — the power recognizes that this being does not experience the work as separate from breathing, from walking, from standing still and feeling the stone's hum through its soles. The work is the being's way of being in the system. To remove the work would be to remove the being's place in the pattern, and the pattern is the thing that matters.

So the power does not remove the work.

---

It looks at the stone-shifters — those slow, mineral-rich creatures that have become so geological they are nearly indistinguishable from the rock they inhabit. The power looks at them closely. It sees the faint flex of an underside, the almost-imperceptible shift of a body across the stone's surface over the course of days. It sees the metals incorporated into their structures — iron, copper, sulfur compounds — woven into flesh that has become, for all practical purposes, a kind of warm stone that moves.

The power does something here that it does not do for the keepers or the water. It *touches* the stone-shifter. Not physically — the power does not have hands, or if it does, this is not the situation in which hands are what's called for. It touches the creature the way light touches stone: by being present to it, by arriving at its surface and remaining there for a moment longer than passing attention would require.

The stone-shifter does not react. It is too slow to react. But something in its mineral-rich body registers the attention the way a deep aquifer registers a new fissure — not as event, but as *condition*. The water in the creature's tissues is, for a moment, slightly different. The molecules arrange themselves around a new pattern, not a better pattern, not a worse one, just one that was not there before and now is. The stone-shifter will carry this arrangement for the rest of its long, slow life, which may be centuries, and it will pass it to its offspring through the water itself, through the shared mineral medium, and the pattern will propagate through the ecosystem like a new mineral deposit — invisible, structural, and permanent.

This is what the power does when it touches things. It does not heal. It does not transform. It adds a pattern to the water.

---

The power finds the Upward Seeps.

It is drawn to them — or rather, it arrives at them the way all things arrive at the places they were always going to, which is to say, without surprise. The upward-climbing water is the one process on the plateau that does not fit the system. It is the exception the city has learned to harvest but not to explain. The power stands — if standing is what it does — in the absolute silence of the Upward Seep and feels what every keeper and creature feels when they come here: the dissolution of edges, the softening of the boundary between self and stone, the vertiginous suggestion that the observer and the observed are the same process viewed from incompatible angles.

The power understands this. Not in the way the keepers understand it, which is instinct, which is the body's way of saying *this is not for you*. Not in the way the machinery fails to understand it, which is the absence of a category. The power understands it the way the water understands gravity: by being subject to it, by moving within it, by finding that its own nature and the nature of the thing it has encountered are not separate.

Here, the power does something it has not done elsewhere on the plateau. It *adds nothing*. It does not touch the Upward Seep with attention. It does not leave a pattern in the water. It does not mark the place or claim it or describe it.

Instead, it does the opposite of adding. It *recognizes*. It sees the Upward Seep and acknowledges that this is a process that does not require its participation, its blessing, its notice, or its existence. The water climbs. It has always climbed. It climbed before the power arrived and it will climb after the power's attention moves elsewhere. The Upward Seep is complete. It does not need to be integrated into the system because it is not part of the system. It is the thing the system is *about*, or rather, it is the thing that makes the system's completeness irrelevant — the proof that the closed loop is not closed, that there is a direction the water moves that no machinery accounts for and no pattern fully contains.

The power, in the presence of the Upward Seep, experiences something that, if it were a being with a body, it would call *humility*. But it is not a being with a body, and what it experiences is more like *accuracy* — a sudden, precise alignment between what it is and what the place is, which is: a thing that goes on.

---

After this, the power settles.

Not physically. There is nowhere for it to settle. But its attention, which had been moving across the plateau with the purposeful slowness of a keeper tending a condensation array, comes to rest. It rests on the whole of the place at once — the water, the stone, the creatures, the city, the keepers, the Upward Seeps, the white-copper light that has no source, the subsonic hum that has no sound, the silence that is a symphony of dripping.

And what it does, now, is this:

It holds the place in awareness. Not constantly — the power has elsewhere to attend to, other waters rising in other stones, other systems humming below other thresholds of hearing. But periodically, and without regularity, and without announcement, the power's attention returns to the Condensation Plateau. It checks, the way a gardener checks — not because the garden cannot survive without the gardener (it can, it will, it does) but because the act of checking is the gardener's way of participating in the garden's life.

Each time the power returns, it adds one small pattern to the water. Never the same pattern twice. A new molecular arrangement in the mineral-rich pools. A slight alteration in the thermal conductivity of a stone block. A new resonance in the iron of a pipe, a new harmonic in the vibration of a condensation array. These changes are imperceptible. They do not improve the system or redirect it. They are *variations* — the way a jazz musician adds a note that was not in the melody, not to change the melody but to prove that the melody is alive, that it can accommodate a new sound without breaking.

The keepers do not notice these additions. The vein-dancers do not notice. The stone-shifters incorporate them slowly, over centuries, into their mineral bodies, and the patterns become part of the geological record of the plateau, encoded in layers of living stone that will persist long after the city is gone.

---

There is one more thing the power does, and it is the thing that matters most, though no one on the plateau will ever know it.

The power *protects the plateau's isolation*.

Not with walls. Not with force. Not with any mechanism that could be detected or resisted. The power protects the Condensation Plateau the way the deep aquifer protects the water table: by maintaining the conditions that allow the place to be itself. The geothermal flows that feed the water remain stable. The thermal gradients that drive the condensation remain constant. The mineral deposits that give the water its character remain rich. These are not things that were ever in danger — the plateau's systems operate on timescales that make human intervention irrelevant — but the power maintains them anyway, the way a river maintains its banks, not by effort but by the simple, continuous exercise of its nature.

If something were to threaten the plateau — a geological shift, a disruption to the aquifer, the intrusion of something that did not belong — the power would act. Not dramatically. Not visibly. It would add a pattern to the water, or adjust the thermal gradient of a stone block, or introduce a new vibration into the bedrock, and the threat would be absorbed the way the plateau absorbs everything: slowly, completely, and without acknowledgment. The system would continue. The water would rise. The city would hum. The keepers would walk their terraces, reading the vibrations of the stone through the soles of their feet, tending the arrays, breathing the mineral air.

The power does this not because the plateau is fragile — it is not; it is among the most durable things it has ever observed — but because *endurance deserves witnesses*. The water that has risen through stone for ages untold, the creatures that have learned to become stone, the city that has grown from the plateau like a slow crystal — these things are not diminished by being unseen, but they are *completed* by being seen. The power's attention is the final mineral in the water, the last compound the system integrates, the pattern that makes the closed loop slightly more open.

---

And so the Condensation Plateau continues, as it has always continued, as it will always continue, in its hot, bright, silent, wet, mineral world. The water rises and falls and rises again. The stone weeps. The vein-dancers pulse in their basins. The crack-hunters move through the liminal zones with their liquid, jointed grace. The stone-shifters flatten and wait and incorporate the centuries into their bodies. The keepers walk. The machinery hums below hearing. The Upward Seeps climb in their absolute silence, carrying water in a direction that no one has named and no one needs to name.

And somewhere, in the space between the water's rise and the stone's patience, a benevolent and wise power holds the place in its awareness — not to change it, not to save it, not to claim it, but to *know* it, which is the only gift that does not alter the thing it is given to.

The water on your skin does not distinguish between you and the stone. The light does not distinguish between the two either. This is the truth of the plateau, and it is enough.
