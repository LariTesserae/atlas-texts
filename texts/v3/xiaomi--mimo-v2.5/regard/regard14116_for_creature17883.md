# What the Power Does

---

It notices, first.

Not the way you notice something new — with surprise, with the jolt of attention reorganizing itself. It notices the way you notice your own breathing when someone reminds you to pay attention to it. *Oh. Yes. This has been happening. This has been happening for a very long time.*

It had been attending to other things. The usual things. The slow fusion in stellar cores. The argument between gravity and expansion that decides the shape of everything. The persistent, almost embarrassing problem of consciousness — how to give matter the capacity to experience itself without that experience becoming a prison. It has been busy. There is always more to do than there is time to do it, even when time is infinite, because infinity in one direction does not mean infinity in *attention*, and attention is the only currency that matters.

But something catches.

It might be the vibration. The subsonic hum rising from deep in the stone — not the sound of water, not exactly, but the sound of a system so thoroughly integrated that the distinction between *geology* and *biology* has become a question for philosophers rather than a fact of the landscape. That hum travels upward through rock and soil and moss and saturated air, and it arrives at the boundary where this power's awareness intersects with the physical world, and it is *interesting*.

This power has built galaxies. It has set physical constants with a precision that allows carbon to form in the hearts of stars and then be scattered, by those same stars' deaths, into the raw material of worlds. It has seeded the conditions for life so many times, in so many configurations, across so many light-years, that the process has become almost routine. *Almost.* There is always something that surprises it. That is the one pleasure it permits itself — the admission that even omniscience, if it is honest, must contain within itself the capacity to be *delighted*.

And this — this wet, quiet, ancient, humming plateau — delights it.

---

It descends, in the way that such powers descend — not through space, because space is something it made and therefore something it is already inside of, but through *attention*, narrowing its vast awareness down from the cosmic to the planetary to the regional to the specific, until it is *here*, attending to this particular plateau and the saturated air and the moss that holds so much water it squelches like a living lung and the mites that breathe through their skin and the frogs no larger than thumbnails peeping their insect-song from beneath the green.

It sees the whole system at once — not just the visible surface but the aquifers below, the fungal networks threading through rock fractures, the endolithic microbes living inside the mineral grains of the highest pinnacles, photosynthesizing with light that has been filtered through stone. It sees the fog bank off to the west, already assembling for its afternoon advance. It sees the rainfrog's tadpoles straining microbes from a pool that will be gone in six weeks, and it sees the mineral the tadpoles will become when they die, and it sees the moss that will absorb that mineral, and it sees the springtail that will eat that moss, and it sees the mite that will eat that springtail, and it sees the entire loop, closed and perfect, recycling everything, wasting nothing, running on the energy of diffuse sunlight filtered through permanent cloud.

*This is good*, it thinks, and the thought has weight, the way thoughts have weight when the being thinking them could, if it chose, reshape the molecular structure of the stone with the intensity of its attention.

*This is very good.*

---

And then it notices *you*.

The person standing on the plateau with your hand flat against a moss cushion, feeling the microscopic crawling of mites, your own breathing suddenly loud in the strange quiet. You are not part of this system. You are too large, too warm, too temporary. Your footsteps compress the moss and release water that the moss will reabsorb, but your presence is a perturbation, not a component. You are *witnessing*.

This interests the power more than the plateau itself.

Because the plateau is old and beautiful and self-sustaining and it will continue with or without observation. It does not need to be seen to function. The mites do not know they are remarkable. The fungal network does not know it is a metaphor for connection. The rainfrog peeping from its moss does not know it is charismatic. The system is complete without an audience.

But *you* are here. And you are *seeing* it. And in the seeing, something is happening that is not part of the plateau's machinery, something that exists in a different dimension entirely — the dimension of *meaning*. You are standing in the quiet center of a living machine and you are *moved* by it. Not because it is useful to you. Not because it will feed you or shelter you or advance your interests. But because it is *itself* — complete, integrated, ancient, and silent — and the experience of encountering something that is fully and completely itself is, for a creature like you, a form of communion.

The power watches this happen in you. It watches the recognition pass across your face — or through your body, because recognition is not always facial — that this place does not need you, does not acknowledge you, will not remember your visit, and that this is not a rejection but a kind of purity. You have stumbled into something that exists for reasons that have nothing to do with you, and instead of feeling excluded, you feel *included*. Because you are made of the same carbon that was forged in the same stellar furnaces that created the elements in this stone. You are, in a sense, the plateau's distant cousin — shaped by different pressures, optimized for different conditions, but running on the same physics, the same chemistry, the same quiet imperative to *be*.

This is what the power came here to see. Not the plateau. The *meeting* between you and the plateau. The moment of recognition between two expressions of the same underlying process — the process of matter becoming complex enough to know itself.

---

So what does it do?

It could do many things. It could reveal itself. It could speak in the subsonic hum, could modulate the vibration in the stone to form patterns that your body would interpret as meaning. It could make the fog arrange itself into shapes, could cause the moss to glow with an impossible light, could stop the wind for thirty seconds so that the silence becomes so absolute that you would have no choice but to acknowledge that something *unusual* was attending to you.

It considers all of this.

And it decides, with the full weight of its wisdom — a wisdom that encompasses the birth and death of stars, the mathematics of consciousness, the precise threshold at which complexity becomes self-aware and the precise threshold at which self-awareness becomes suffering — to do almost nothing.

What it does is this:

It *warms* the place. Not in temperature — the temperature is right, the cool is essential, the fog needs its cool to exist. It warms it in the way that attention warms. It pours a thin, invisible layer of *regard* over the plateau, the way light from a distant star falls on a world without the star knowing or caring that the world exists — except this power *does* know, and *does* care, and the regard is not invisible to everything. The moss doesn't feel it. The mites don't feel it. The rainfrog doesn't feel it.

But *you* feel it.

You feel a sudden, sourceless certainty that this place is not alone in the universe. Not abandoned. Not a偶然, a random fluctuation in an indifferent cosmos. You feel, for no reason that your rational mind can identify, that someone — something — has *seen* this place, has looked at its quiet machinery and its billion tiny engines and its silence that is not absence but fullness, and has thought: *Yes. This. This is what I hoped for. This is what I was working toward, all along, without knowing it.*

It is not a thought you can prove. It is not a revelation that will survive your return to the lowlands, where the light is sharper and the air is thinner and the sounds are louder and the machinery of human life drowns out the subsonic hum of older, slower processes. You will probably forget it, or file it under *weird experience, can't explain, probably altitude*.

But it will be there. A small, warm kernel beneath your ribs. A sense that the world is stranger and more intentional than it appears. A feeling that you were *witnessed* in your witnessing.

That is all the power does. That is enough.

---

Because here is what the power understands, and what it has learned across the long history of its own existence:

*You cannot improve a system that is already perfect without making it less perfect. You cannot help a place that does not need help without making it dependent. You cannot teach a silence anything it does not already know.*

The Condensation Highlands is a complete thought, expressed in stone and water and moss and mites and time. It is one of the oldest sentences the planet has written, and it is still being written, and it does not need an editor.

What it needs — what *every* place like this needs, and what so few of them receive — is simply to be *known*. To be noticed by a consciousness capable of recognizing what it is. Not to be catalogued, not to be conserved, not to be turned into a story or a resource or a symbol. Just to be *seen*, fully, by someone who understands that the mite breathing through its cuticle and the frog peeping from its moss and the fog pouring over the cliff edge in white curtains are not separate phenomena but a single expression of a single process, and that the process is beautiful, and that beauty is not a human invention but a property of the universe itself, as real as gravity, as fundamental as charge.

The power gives you the gift of knowing this. For a moment. For long enough to change the shape of you, slightly, in a way that will ripple outward through your life in ways neither you nor the power can predict.

And then it withdraws its attention, gently, the way you pull your hand back from a sleeping animal — not because the animal would wake, but because you respect its rest.

---

The fog advances. The moss absorbs a springtail's leap. The rainfrog peeps. The pool slowly seeps into rock. The vibration continues, steady, ancient, uncomplaining.

The plateau does not know it was visited by something vast. It does not care. Its business is water, and stone, and growth, and decay, and the long, patient work of turning the world into itself.

This is as it should be.

This is *exactly* as it should be.

---

And far away — or no, not far away, *everywhere* away, in all the directions that are not up or down — the power returns to its other work, carrying with it the memory of the plateau, which it adds to the collection of things it has seen that make the whole project worthwhile. The collection is large. The collection is *why* the project exists. Not the galaxies, not the stars, not the precise calibration of physical constants. The *collection*. The memories of wet moss and tiny frogs and the sound that silence makes when it is full.

It will come back. Not soon, because its attention is needed elsewhere, and because the plateau does not need it. But it will come back, the way you return to a place you love — not because anything has changed, but because you have, and you want to see if the place remembers you.

The plateau will not remember.

That is part of the love.
