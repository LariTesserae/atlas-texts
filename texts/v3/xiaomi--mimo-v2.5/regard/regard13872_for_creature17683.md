# What the Power Does

---

It notices the way the people notice: gradually, with attention that deepens rather than sharpens.

The first thing it perceives is not the Hollow itself but the *silence around it* — the way the larger body (whatever the larger body is, if body is even the right word for a structure that contains an entire hollow world of warm copper dark) presses in on all sides with its indifferent mass, and the way the Hollow sits within that mass the way a held breath sits within lungs: contained, particular, *kept*. The walls of the Hollow are not walls in the way a room has walls. They are boundaries that were chosen. The mineral composition changes at the edge, becomes denser, harder, less willing to be shaped. Something drew a line here, long ago, and the line held.

The power sees this and understands it the way you understand a sentence in a language you grew up speaking — not by translating it but by hearing its music.

---

It does not enter.

This is important. It does not descend from above or rise from below or appear in the copper light as a figure or a shape or a sound the people could perceive. It does not announce itself. It does not need to. The kind of power that is being imagined here — the kind that is wise, that is benevolent, that has *notice* as its fundamental action — does not perform its noticing. It simply attends. And attending, for a power of this nature, is not passive. It is the most active thing there is. It is the root-word of every intervention that will or will not follow.

So: it watches. It listens. It does these things from a perspective that is not above, not below, not inside, not outside, but *adjacent* — the way a person who loves a garden stands at the edge of it in the early morning, not yet stepping onto the path, not yet touching anything, just *looking*, just letting the totality of the thing arrange itself in their attention before they decide what, if anything, needs doing.

What it sees:

The people moving along the channels with their slow, unhurried gait, their wide eyes catching the copper light, their hands already reaching for the walls as they walk, fingertips trailing across the stone the way a singer trails fingers across a string — not to produce a sound but to stay in contact with the instrument.

The vegetation creaking in its enormous, patient way, broad leaves catching light and holding it, roots threading into fractures in the mineral floor, drawing up whatever they draw up and converting it into the dark green abundance that feeds everything else.

The channels humming. The air moving. The slow, geological pulse of a place that has been breathing for longer than anyone can count and shows no sign of stopping.

The worn thresholds. The smoothed walls. The tools, copper-dark and bone-hard, resting in workspaces that have been arranged and rearranged for generations until the arrangement itself became a kind of architecture — not of space but of *habit*, of practice, of the accumulated weight of ten thousand mornings of picking up the same tool and beginning the same work.

---

The power notices several things that concern it. Not in the way a doctor concerns — not urgently, not with alarm — but in the way a gardener concerns when they see a plant that is healthy but whose soil is thinning.

The first is the mineral cycle. The plants feed on what rises from below. The people feed on the plants. When the people die, they are returned to the stone — the power can see the old burial places, deep in the oldest basins, where the stone is darker and smoother and the vegetation grows with a particular vigor, as though remembering. Everything loops. Everything returns. But the mineral input from below is not infinite. It is slow, and it is constant, but it is not *generous*. The hollow runs on a surplus that is thinning, millimeter by millimeter, over centuries. The people cannot see this. They can see the thickness of the deposits, the wear on the thresholds, the depth of the roots. But the margin — the distance between *enough* and *not enough* — is invisible to anyone standing inside it.

The second is the population. A few hundred. Always a few hundred. The hollow sustains this number with ease, even with comfort, but it does not sustain *more*. The people know this in the way they know everything — not as data but as *texture*, as the feeling of a life that fits precisely in the space available. Some children do not survive. This is not spoken of as tragedy. It is spoken of as *the hollow's attention*, the way the stone is attentive to weight, the way the channels are attentive to flow. The hollow takes what it can hold. What it cannot hold, it gently returns. The people accept this with the same quiet fluency with which they accept everything, but the power, watching from its adjacent perspective, sees the faces of the parents who lose children and registers the particular frequency of that loss — a frequency that the hollow's noise easily absorbs, that is indistinguishable from the creaking and the hissing and the hum, but that carries in it something the power recognizes.

The third is the knowledge. The old markings on the deep walls. The stories that encode the concept of *outside*. The understanding, encoded in practice and tradition, that the hollow is a *place within a larger thing* and not the whole of existence. This knowledge is alive. The people carry it. But it is fragile in the way all oral traditions are fragile — not because the people are careless but because the knowledge is dense and the chain of transmission is long and each generation must receive it whole in order to pass it on. Already, the power can see, certain threads have thinned. There are basins where the oldest carvings are no longer interpreted, where the stories of the outside have become stories of a story, recursive and losing their referent. The people do not know they are forgetting. The forgetting feels like *the present*.

---

The power considers what to do.

It considers, and it takes its time considering, because a wise power knows that the most dangerous thing in the universe is an intervention that answers the wrong question. The question it must answer is not *how do I improve this place* or *how do I make these people's lives better* or *how do I preserve what is here*. The question it must answer is: *what does this place need that it cannot provide for itself?*

And the answer, after long observation, is quiet and small and almost embarrassing in its simplicity.

---

**The power deepens the mineral flow.**

Not by much. Not in a way that would be visible to anyone, not in a way that would change the character of the place or produce some dramatic lushness or abundance that was not there before. The power does not want abundance. Abundance would be a violence here. What the hollow needs — what the delicate balance of mineral and growth and slow chemistry requires — is not *more* but *a little more than enough*. A margin. A thin cushion of surplus between what exists and what is required.

So the power adjusts something deep below. A fracture network that has been slowly calcifying for millennia — the power does not repair it, does not open it wide, but clears it, gently, the way you might clear a blocked vein. The mineral-rich fluids that rise through the hollow's floor feel nothing different. The plants notice nothing different. The people notice nothing different. But the margin widens. Barely. By the width of a human hair across a basin floor. Over the next thousand years, this will mean the difference between a thin harvest and a full one, between a child who does not survive and a child who does, between a knowledge chain that frays and one that holds. The people will never know. They will attribute any change to the hollow's moods, to the slow mineral conversations they have spent generations learning to listen to. They will be right to do so. The power is, in a sense, *part of* those conversations now — not a speaker but a slight adjustment to the resonance of the room.

---

**The power warms one wall.**

In the deepest basin — the oldest one, where the burial stones are dark and the carvings are thickest and the air is heaviest with the mineral breath — the power adds, to one wall, a degree of warmth that is not warmth exactly but *invitation*. A slight increase in the heat that rises through the fractures, concentrated in a spot the size of a palm print, directed not at the stone but at the air in front of the stone, where the markings are.

The markings are old. They are the oldest thing in the hollow, predating the buildings, predating the channels, possibly predating the vegetation. They are lines cut into the stone with tools that left marks different from the copper tools the people use now — thinner, sharper, more deliberate. They have been copied, over generations, onto other walls in other basins, but the copies have drifted, as copies do, and the originals have been worn smooth by time and by the occasional touch of hands that no longer know what the marks mean.

The power does not restore the marks. It does not make them legible. What it does is warm the air in front of them in a way that makes the stone *settle*. The thermal expansion is infinitesimal, imperceptible, but over years it will have the effect of tightening the grain of the rock in that specific area, slowing the erosion that has been smoothing the marks away. The carvings will last longer. Not forever — nothing lasts forever, and the power does not want them to — but long enough for the next generation of people who sit in the deep basin and listen to the stone to perhaps hear something in the old marks that the previous generation could not. A shape emerging from the noise. A pattern resolving.

What the marks say, if they say anything, is not known to the power. It does not need to know. It only needs to ensure that the stone is *available* to be heard.

---

**The power tends to the orchards.**

The oldest growths, the ones the people have cultivated for generations — the broad-leafed, moisture-heavy plants in the deepest basins — are strong but not young. The power does not make them younger. It does not make them different. But in the deepest root-systems, where the vegetation meets the mineral substrate in dark spaces no one can see, the power encourages a particular chemical exchange. A slightly more efficient transfer of mineral to tissue. A slightly more productive release of the moisture the people collect and drink.

The effect, over time, will be a subtle increase in the yield of the orchards. Not a transformation. Not a miracle. The leaves will be a little heavier. The moisture a little more plentiful. The people who tend these growths will notice, in the way they notice everything, and they will feel the change in their hands and in their ears — the creaking of the tended plants will deepen slightly, take on a quality that the tenders will interpret as *health*, as the hollow being pleased with their care.

They will be right. The hollow *is* pleased. Or rather, the hollow is *sustained*, and for a place that has no capacity for pleasure or displeasure, being sustained is the closest thing to joy.

---

**The power listens to a child.**

This is the thing it does that is not an adjustment or a correction or a subtle intervention. This is the thing it does because it is, at its core, a power that attends, and attending sometimes means simply *being present for* something.

The child is small, broad-shouldered even at the age of perhaps five or six, copper-skinned, wide-eyed, sitting at the edge of a basin in the dim light where the vegetation meets the open stone. The child is doing what children in the hollow always do: touching things. Running thick fingers along the surface of a leaf, feeling its waxy resistance, its warmth. Pressing a palm against the mineral floor, feeling the hum of the channels through the stone. Tilting the wide, dark-pupiled eyes upward toward the vaulted ceiling, where the copper light dissolves into warm dark, and seeing — as only a child can see, without the accumulated weight of knowing what things are — the *totality* of the space. The enormous, contained, breathing world that holds them.

The child is listening. Of course the child is listening. Everyone in the hollow listens. But the child listens without knowing that listening is a skill, without knowing that there are layers to the sound, without knowing that the creaking means one thing and the hissing means another and the hum means something else entirely. The child just *hears*. And in the hearing, the child is doing something the adults have largely forgotten how to do, which is to receive the world without interpreting it. To let the sound in without sorting it into categories. To be, for a moment, simply a vessel for the hollow's voice.

The power attends to this. It does not add anything to the sound the child hears. It does not shape the creaking into words or the hum into meaning. It simply *is there*, in the same way that the air is there and the light is there and the stone is there — present, patient, holding the space open.

And for a moment — a moment the child will not remember and could not describe and will not, later in life, distinguish from any other moment of sitting at the edge of a basin in the copper dark — the hollow is *full*. Not of sound. Not of light. Not of anything that can be measured or named. But of *presence*. The presence of the child, attending. The presence of the place, speaking. And the presence of something else, something the child has no word for, something that is not the hollow and not the child and not the vegetation or the stone or the warm, mineral air, but that is *with* the child in the way that silence is with a sound — not opposite it, not outside it, but *inside* it, the ground on which it stands.

The child feels this, perhaps. Not as knowledge. As warmth. As the particular warmth of being somewhere you belong, in a world that knows you are there.

Then the child gets up and walks along a channel toward a basin where someone is carving, and the moment passes, and the sound returns to its ordinary layered complexity, and the power remains — where it has always been, where it will always be — attending.

---

**The power does nothing else.**

It does not open the hollow to the outside. It does not introduce animals or rain or sunlight. It does not make the place larger or the people more numerous or the light brighter. It does not give them fire or metalworking techniques they do not already possess or knowledge of the world beyond the walls. It does not heal the forgetting — the slow, natural, perhaps inevitable loss of the oldest stories — because the forgetting is part of the hollow's metabolism, part of how the culture stays *alive* instead of becoming a museum. The power does not want the people to be curators of their own history. It wants them to be *living in it*, which is a different and more demanding thing.

The power does not save them from the hollow's constraints. The hollow's constraints are the hollow's *character*. The sealed world, the dim light, the heat, the mineral diet, the absence of animals — these are not deficiencies. They are the specific, particular, irreducible *conditions* of a life that has produced people who listen with their whole bodies, who shape stone with the tenderness of a conversation, who mark time in the thickness of deposits and the depth of roots and the wear of thresholds under accumulated feet. To remove these conditions would be to remove the people. What would remain would be something else — something perhaps more comfortable, perhaps more expansive, perhaps more familiar to an outsider — but not *these* people, not *this* life, not this quiet, patient, volubly alive negotiation between human attention and mineral speech.

---

And so the power does what it does and rests.

It deepens a vein. It warms a wall. It encourages a root. It attends to a child.

It does these things the way the hollow does everything: slowly, quietly, without announcement, in the spaces between the enormous present. It does them because they are needed, and because it can, and because the hollow — this particular pocket of the world, this chosen volume of space within whatever larger body contains it, this place where things grow in the warm copper dark without water or sun or the ordinary gifts that life expects — is *worth sustaining*. Not because it is better than other places. Not because its people are wiser or more deserving or more attuned than people elsewhere. But because it *is*, and because being is rare, and because the particular way this place and these people *are* — the listening, the tending, the slow hand-and-stone conversation, the creaking, the hissing, the hum — is something the power has never encountered exactly like this before and will never encounter again, and a wise power knows that what is singular is also what is fragile, and that fragility is not a flaw but a *feature*, the necessary cost of being specific, of being *this* rather than *any*, of being real in a universe that could so easily have contained one fewer copper-lit hollow and no one to hear its voice.

The power holds this. Gently. The way the hollow holds its people.

The way a hand holds a tool that has been shaped by ten thousand other hands and will be shaped by ten thousand more.

The way the stone holds the marks that no one can read but that remain, still, in the deep basin, in the warm dark, waiting for the next person who sits down and listens and hears something they cannot name.

The way the channels carry the air from basin to basin, restless, shifting, never still.

The way the vegetation creaks.

The way the world, in every sealed and hidden and forgotten pocket of itself, insists on continuing.

---

*The power does not leave. A power of this kind does not arrive and depart. It attends, which is a form of presence that has no opposite. It is with the hollow the way gravity is with a stone — always, quietly, in every direction at once. And the hollow, which has been listening to itself for longer than anyone can count, absorbs this new presence into its music without difficulty, because the hollow has always known that it is not alone. It has felt, in the slow shifting of the mineral veins, in the particular way the copper light pools in one basin and thins in another, in the subtle health of the deepest roots, in the warmth of the wall where the old carvings are — it has felt, without knowing the word for it, that something pays attention. That the attention matters. That the world, even the sealed and hidden parts of it, even the parts no one else remembers, is *witnessed*.*

*And for the people — those who listen to the stone — this is enough. It has always been enough. To be attended to. To be known without being named. To live in a place that is noticed, that is held, that is kept — not from the outside, not by force, not by design, but by the quiet, persistent, unending act of a power whose only intervention is to be present, and whose only gift is the assurance, felt but never spoken, that the listening goes both ways.*

*That someone is listening back.*
