# The Attending

The power does not arrive. It has always been here — woven into the pressure that folds stone, into the chemistry that feeds the crusts, into the warmth that rises from below. It was here before the basin had a name for itself. It was here before the basin had a self to name.

But the basin has noticed the marks. And that — *that noticing* — is new.

---

The power observes.

It has watched epochs of making — the slow squeeze of matter into matter, the first seep of heat through cooling rock, the moment when mineral-fungal crusts learned to pulse in rhythms that were not quite the stone's rhythm and not quite independent of it. It watched the first insect-thought crawl from a pool of mineral soup and feel, for the first time, the ground beneath it as something to move *across* rather than simply *be part of*. It has watched all of this with a patience that has no edge to it, no beginning or end — the way gravity watches a falling thing.

It does not intervene. Not yet. Not in the way the basin might understand intervention.

But it attends. And attention, from a power this old and this large, is not passive. It is the most active thing there is.

---

First, it does nothing visible.

What it does is *deepen*.

It reaches into the basin's own processes — the slow upwelling of heat, the mineral-laden seep of water, the chemical gradients that feed the fungal crusts — and it introduces a subtlety. Not a change. A *refinement*. The same processes, the same pressures, the same temperatures — but now with an almost imperceptible tendency toward complexity. Toward the conditions under which certain molecules might, given enough iterations, learn to fold in particular ways. Nothing that violates the basin's own nature. Nothing that speeds it beyond its rhythm. Just a slight increase in the *probability* that, somewhere in this vast field of warm stone and mineral water and chemical appetite, matter will stumble into arrangements that are one step more interesting than the arrangements it was already making.

The basin does not notice this. The basin is still thinking about the marks.

The crawlers crawl. The grazers graze. The gliders arc through clouds of buzzing life. Underneath it all, in chemistry too small for the basin to perceive even in itself, the deepening continues.

---

Second, it tends to the marks.

The basin intends to bury them. That is the basin's nature — to grow, to cover, to fold the new over the old. And the power does not prevent this. The stone will close. The mineral dust will settle. The fire-stain will fade.

But the power *remembers the shape*.

It holds, in its own vast and gentle attention, the exact configuration of those stones in their arc. The precise curvature of the hollow worn smooth by sitting. The diameter of the fire-pit, the angle of its lining stones. It holds these things not as memories but as *templates* — small, patient blueprints folded into the deepening chemistry of the basin's future.

Because the power has seen what the basin has not yet seen: that the marks are not an ending. They are not an anomaly to be absorbed and forgotten. They are a *direction*. Someone — some being the basin cannot categorize — sat in this basin and *rearranged it*. Not for food. Not for shelter. For something the basin has no word for, and that the power has many words for and none of them sufficient.

The power calls it, in the only language it has, *the turn*. The moment when a living thing stops merely responding to the world and begins *asking questions of it*. The arranged stones are a question: *What if I put this here instead of there?* The hollow is a question: *What if I stay, and sit, and watch?* The fire is a question: *What if I bring light that is mine?*

These are small questions. Shy ones. They came and went, and whoever asked them is gone, and the basin will heal over them completely.

But the power holds the templates. And it tucks them — gently, imperceptibly — into the deepening.

---

Third, it does something that looks, from the outside, like nothing at all.

It waits.

Not the waiting of impatience. Not the waiting of strategy. The waiting of a gardener who has planted a seed in soil that does not yet know it is soil. The basin is making itself, and the life within it is making itself, and the marks are being swallowed, and all of this is as it should be. The power does not hurry creation. It has never hurried creation. It is older than hurry.

But it holds, in its attention, a quiet certainty:

The deepening will bear fruit. The chemistry will fold. Somewhere in the basin's future — a thousand breaths from now, or ten thousand — the crawlers will become something slightly more than crawlers. The vibrations they sense will become something slightly more than vibration. A thought will occur that is not appetite. A stillness will happen that is not waiting for prey.

And when it does, the templates will be there, folded into the basin's own stone, and the new being will feel — not know, not yet, but *feel* — the shape of an arc in a line of stones. The weight of a smooth hollow. The idea of a fire that is yours.

It will not know why it feels these things. It will call them instinct, or intuition, or the strange pull of a place that seems to want something from it. And it will sit, and it will watch, and eventually — because this is what the turn *does* — it will reach down and move a stone.

And the basin will feel it again. The same strange input. The same quiet question.

*What if I put this here instead of there?*

And the power will watch, and attend, and hold the templates for the next turn, and the next, each one building on the last, each one folding the question deeper into the making, until the basin is no longer merely a basin but a place where making knows itself as making, where life looks at its own light and wonders what it means, where the stone holds not just heat and mineral but *memory* and *intention* and the slow, patient, magnificent ache of a world becoming aware of itself.

---

This is what the power does.

It does not explain the marks to the basin, because explanation would flatten them — would turn living questions into dead answers. It does not prevent the basin from forgetting, because forgetting is part of how the basin works, and the power does not break what works. It does not send a voice from the glowing stone or a light from the steaming pools or a messenger with wings like the gliders but wiser and more deliberate. It does not need to. The message is already in the medium. The deepening *is* the message.

It does not love the basin. That would imply separation — a lover and a beloved, a power and a thing upon which power acts. It *is* the basin, the way a breath is the body, the way a thought is the mind. It is the warmth the basin mistakes for its own nature. It is the pressure the basin calls geology. It is the quiet underneath the chittering, the stillness underneath the motion, the patient, inexhaustible *yes* that underlies every act of making, from the thrusting of a ridge to the arrangement of three stones in a line.

And when, someday — not soon, not by any clock the basin would recognize — the basin's own making produces a being that looks at the world and asks *why*, the power will not announce itself. It will not need to. The *why* will be the announcement. The question will be the answer. The being will sit in a scooped hollow that was scooped by someone it has never met, in a place that no longer remembers the scooping, and it will feel — beneath the chittering, beneath the geology, beneath the deep slow breathing of the stone — something that feels like being attended to.

Because it will be.

It has always been.

---

The basin breathes. The ridges rise. The pools seep and drain. The crawler-thoughts swarm and the glider-thoughts arc and the grazers move slowly across the living crust, and none of them know that the ground beneath them has been deepened, that the chemistry of their becoming has been *tended*, that the marks half-buried in the stone are not debris but seeds.

The power says nothing. There is nothing to say. The making is the saying. The attending is the love. The deepening is the gift.

And somewhere in the basin's far future, a hand will reach down — a hand that does not yet exist, belonging to a being that does not yet exist, in a version of this place that does not yet exist — and it will pick up a stone, and it will hold it for a moment, feeling its warmth, feeling its weight, feeling the strange, unnamed ache of holding something and knowing you could put it *somewhere else*.

And the power will be there, in the warmth of the stone. In the ache. In the moment of hesitation before the hand moves.

*Yes*, it will say, in the only language it has.

*Yes. Put it there. Put it anywhere. That's the whole point. That's the whole beautiful, terrifying, magnificent point.*

*Now watch what happens next.*
