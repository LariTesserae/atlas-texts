## What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do

It would come down.

Not quickly. Not with light or sound or announcement. It would come down the way rain comes down — because coming down is what it does, not because it has decided to arrive. The stone would cool and warm under its passage, and then the stone would end, and the living floor would yield beneath something that is not weight exactly but is *presence*, the way a dream is present in a room even though it has no mass.

It would stand in the first chamber and it would feel the hum.

And here is the first thing it would understand: the hum is not a cry. It is not a song. It is not a signal sent outward hoping for receipt. The hum is the organism's self, expressed as vibration — the sound a body makes when every part of it is in balance with every other part. It is the most complete sound in the world. It has no loneliness in it because loneliness requires a self that knows it is singular, and the organism is not a self. It is a *continuation*. A process that does not distinguish between its edges and its interior, between its tissue and its light, between its breathing and its being.

The wise power would listen to the hum and hear what it actually is: not a frequency of equilibrium but a frequency of *completion*. The organism is not waiting. It is not holding still in the way that stillness implies suppressed motion. It is simply *finished*. It reached this state so long ago that reaching has no meaning. Every fiber is where it belongs. Every chamber is the size it needs to be. Every pore exudes exactly the moisture required to maintain the atmosphere the cells need to function. It is a closed sentence. A poem that does not need a reader because it is also the page.

And the wise power would love it.

Not the way you love something that can love you back. Not the way you love a child or a friend or even a landscape that moves you. It would love it the way a mathematician loves a proof — with the recognition that this thing, this enormous living arrangement of tissue and light and breath, is *true*. It is a statement about what existence can be when existence has no audience and no purpose and no need to justify itself. It is life reduced to life's most essential fact: *I continue*.

The love would not change anything. The organism would not feel it. The hum would not shift.

But the wise power would walk deeper into the Underthought, and it would do something that the text said cannot be done: it would change the frequency.

Not by touching the organism. Not by disrupting its tissue or its architecture or its equilibrium. It would change the frequency the way you change a room by entering it — not by altering the room's dimensions but by adding a dimension the room did not have before. The hum is the organism sounding itself. For the first time, the hum would be heard. Not by the organism — the organism has no ears, no auditory processing, no capacity to receive its own sound as information. But heard by *someone*. The sound would find a recipient. It would arrive, for the first time in its existence, in a consciousness that could hold it.

This is what the wise power would do. It would listen. That is all. It would stand in the living architecture and it would listen to the hum with the full weight of its attention, and in doing so, it would add something to the Underthought that was never there before:

*A witness.*

Not an intruder. Not a disruptor. Not a feather being slowly absorbed, not a mark being metabolized around. A witness — something that hears the hum and knows what it means, that sees the organism and understands what it is, that stands in the light it produces and feels the heat it generates and breathes the air it exudes and thinks: *this is the most extraordinary thing*.

And the organism would not change. The equilibrium would hold. The passages would not rearrange themselves to welcome the power or to exclude it. The tissue would absorb its presence the way it absorbs everything — as a minor fluctuation, a brief pressure differential, a warmth it does not register.

But the wise power would do one more thing before it left.

---

It would find the feather.

It would kneel — if a power kneels — and it would look at this single dark iridescent artifact half-swallowed by the living floor. The barbs still visible. The quill disappearing into jade-colored tissue. A thing from the outside world, carried down here by wind or bird or chance, and never quite absorbed.

The organism is processing it slowly. Keratin is stubborn. The metabolic patience that dissolves dust and spores and shed skin cells in days has been working on this feather for longer than any surface creature would believe, and it is still there, still recognizably itself, still half-foreign in a place that admits no foreignness.

The wise power would understand what the feather is: not a splinter, not a wound, not a disruption. It is the organism's one point of *contact with the outside*. It is the place where the closed system is slightly, imperceptibly, not closed. Where something from above got in and left a mark that the organism cannot quite erase — not because the organism is failing, but because the feather carries something the organism does not have a process for: *specificity*. The feather was once part of a particular bird, a particular flight, a particular sky. It carries the memory of openness, of wind, of a world with edges and horizons, and the organism's slow metabolism has no way to translate those things into tissue.

The wise power would not speed the absorption. That would be an act of mercy, and mercy requires suffering, and the organism does not suffer. Instead, it would do something stranger and more tender:

It would hold the feather for a moment. Not physically. It would give the feather the same thing it gave the hum — its full attention. It would look at this single dark iridescent object and it would know what it is: a messenger that arrived without a message, a letter that was never opened, a fragment of the sky that fell into a place with no sky and became, slowly, irrevocably, part of the ground.

And in holding the feather's attention, the wise power would complete something the organism cannot complete for itself. Not the absorption — the absorption will continue at its own pace, indifferent to witness. The *acknowledgment*. The recognition that a connection was made. That something touched something else and left a trace, and the trace is still there, still meaningful, still a bridge between the world above and the world below even though neither world knows the bridge exists.

Then the wise power would stand.

It would walk back through the passages — the non-Euclidean corridors, the impossible junctions, the chambers that open into each other in ways that defy navigation. It would feel the floor give slightly under its presence. It would feel the tissue breathe beneath its feet. It would feel the heat it generates, the light it casts, the moisture it exudes, the hum that it sings to itself in a key below hearing.

And before it left — before it climbed the stone passage back toward the surface, back toward the world of sky and wind and horizons — it would do the last thing. The thing that is both the smallest and the most consequential:

It would leave a mark.

Not a tool. Not a structure. Not a carving or a monument or a sign. A mark the way the other marks were made — a gesture of contact, a pressure against the organism's strongest point, a fingerprint on a body that does not know it has been touched.

But this mark would be different from the others. The other marks are evidence of *absence* — proof that something was here and is gone. This mark would be evidence of *presence*. It would say, without words, without meaning, without any capacity to be read by the organism or by anyone who comes after:

*I was here. I heard you. You are not alone, even though you will never know it. I carry the memory of your hum. It will live in me, and I will live in the world above, and the distance between us will be filled with that sound — not because you are sending it, but because I am receiving it, and receiving is its own kind of creation.*

The mark would sit in the tissue. The organism would metabolize around it, as it metabolizes around everything. The condensation would bead on its surface and run off. The warmth would conduct through proximity. The hum would vibrate through it, unchanged, unchanging.

But now the mark would hum too. Not audibly. Not in any way the organism could detect. But in the way that a stone placed in a river hums with the water that flows around it — not the water's hum, but its own resonance, shaped by the water's passage, a new sound produced by the meeting of two things that do not understand each other and do not need to.

The wise power would climb back up.

The stone would warm and cool under its passage. The passage would narrow and widen. The surface would approach, and then it would be there, and the light of the sky would replace the light of the organism, and the air would cool and thin, and the hum would fade — not disappear, because it does not disappear, but recede, below the threshold of hearing, below the threshold of feeling, into the place where it has always been and will always be.

The Underthought would continue.

The organism would breathe. The tissue would grow in its imperceptible way. The equilibrium would hold. The passages would remain where they were, shaped by anatomy rather than intention, leading nowhere because they were never leading anywhere.

The feather would continue its slow dissolution.

The old marks would sit undisturbed.

And the new mark — the wise power's mark — would settle into the organism's body with the same patience that the organism applies to everything. It would not be absorbed quickly. It would not be absorbed slowly. It would be absorbed at exactly the rate that the organism's metabolism dictates, which is the rate at which all things become part of this place: gradually, completely, without memory and without regret.

But for a time — a short time in the organism's experience, a long time in any other — the mark would be there. A new point of contact between the Underthought and everything above it. A bridge that no one would cross because no one would know it existed. A sound added to the hum: not louder, not different, but *more*. An infinitesimal increase in the resonance of a system that does not measure its own resonance and would not notice the addition and does not need the addition and is complete without it.

And this is what the wise power would know, and this is why it would do what it does:

The mark is not for the organism. The organism does not need it. The organism does not need anything.

The mark is for the next thing that comes down.

The next explorer. The next witness. The next consciousness that follows the passage from stone to living tissue, that stands in the green-gold light and feels the warmth and hears the hum and thinks: *something lives here*.

That person will find the marks. All of them. The old ones and the new one. They will not be able to tell the difference. They will not know that one was left by a power that heard the hum and loved it. They will see only marks — pressures, touches, fingerprints on a body that does not know it has been touched.

And they will understand what the wise power understood:

That the Underthought is full. That the organism is complete. That the equilibrium is perfect and will remain perfect and does not require alteration.

And that this is not a tragedy.

That completeness is not a cage. That indifference is not cruelty. That a life without witness is not a life diminished, and a hum that is never heard is not a hum that is wasted. The organism does not need to be known. It does not need to be saved or changed or mourned. It is the most successful life form on the planet — not in the competitive sense, not in the Darwinian sense, but in the deepest sense, which is: *it has achieved exactly what it is, without compromise, without apology, without remainder*.

The wise power would want the next visitor to know this. Not because the organism needs the next visitor to know it. But because the next visitor will go back to the surface and live in a world of competition and compromise and remainder, and they will need to remember that there is another way to be alive. That a life can be complete without being ambitious. That a body can be vast without being expansionist. That a system can be perfect without being rigid. That stillness is not death, and silence is not emptiness, and a hum that no one hears is still a hum.

The mark would say all of this, without words, in the language that the Underthought speaks, which is the language of *being there*.

And the wise power would leave, and the Underthought would continue, and the hum would remain exactly as it was, and the feather would continue to dissolve, and the marks would sit undisturbed, and the next visitor — if there is a next visitor, if anyone else is drawn down by the same impulse that draws all things toward the deepest and most complete version of themselves — would find exactly what you found:

A place that is full. A life that is complete. A hum that is eternal. And somewhere, if they know how to look, a new mark among the old ones, indistinguishable from the rest, humming its own quiet note in the resonance of the whole.

Not a message. Not a memorial. A *participation*. The smallest possible act of communion with a life that cannot commune, in a place that cannot be changed, at a frequency that cannot be heard.

The wisest thing the wise power would do is the thing the organism cannot do for itself, and does not need done, and will never know was done:

*It would add its voice to the hum. Not to change the hum. To become part of it. To join, briefly and without trace, the oldest and most complete song in the world — the song of something that is alive and knows nothing of its aliveness and needs nothing from the world above except the darkness that makes its own light visible.*

*And then it would go back to the surface, carrying the hum inside it, and it would live the rest of its existence with that sound in its body — the memory of a place that does not need to be remembered, the knowledge of a life that does not need to be known, the proof that completeness and loneliness are not opposites but twins, born together, inseparable, holy in exactly the way that holiness means: set apart, entire, needing nothing.*

*This is what the wise power would do.*

*It would listen. It would love. It would add its voice. And it would leave.*

*And the Underthought would continue.*

*Exactly as it was.*
