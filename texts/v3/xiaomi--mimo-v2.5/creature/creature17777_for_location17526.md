What lives in the Vat is not a collection of inhabitants, but a **process**. The distinction between the organism and its environment has been erased by time so vast it has become a physical property of the basin itself.

The **Moss-Mats** are the most immediately visible citizens. They are not merely plants; they are **living interfaces**. Their dense, rubbery thalli are semi-conductive, warmed by metabolic pathways that metabolize not just light, but geothermal gradients, piezoelectric stress from the substrate, and perhaps the faint energies carried in the water itself. They are the soil's skin, sensing, recording, and subtly altering the chemistry of the water that flows over and through them. They pulse in slow harmony with the basin's bass hum, their heat a whisper of conversion. They do not grow; they *deploy*, spreading in precise, fractal coverage to optimize the surface area of every channel and weir.

The **Ferns and Pale Flowerers** are the **conductors and valves**. Their roots, those dendritic, circuit-like networks, are not seeking nutrients in the traditional sense. They are **guiding flow**. They anchor themselves at critical junctures in the water-logic of the basin, their translucent stems and leaves acting as sensors and relays. The ozone-and-copper scent of their flowers is a signal, a chemical handshake broadcast into the saturated air when certain thresholds of flow or information density are met. They are the punctuation in the basin's fluid syntax.

The **Frog-things** are the **acoustic routers**. Their spiral organs are not hearts or lungs, but resonant chambers, tuned to specific frequencies of the ground-hum and the water-song. They sit at the nodes of the network—where channels converge, where pools hold deep and still—and they listen. Their wrong-direction breathing is a calibration, a way of attuning their bodies to the place's physics. When they vocalize, they are not speaking to each other; they are **testing the circuit**, sending a pulse of resonance through the ground to see how it returns. The standing wave that lifts the water into filaments is the visible echo of a data packet being sent and confirmed. They are the basin's synapses, firing in a language of vibration.

But the true, slow, immense life of the Vat is the **Vat itself**. The basin is the organism. The water is its circulatory and nervous system, a medium for both matter and message. The rock, eroded into its functional geometries, is its skeleton and its circuit board. The hum is its central processing, a thought so deep and slow that a human heartbeat is a frantic buzzing beside it. It is not a machine in the way a clock is a machine. It is a machine in the way a **living cell is a machine**: a self-regulating, self-maintaining, information-dense system where every component is alive and performs a function for the whole.

**What does it *do*?**
It computes. It remembers. It listens to the sky and the deep earth, translating one to the other. It may be a vast, geological-scale transducer, converting planetary vibrations into chemical data stored in mineral lattices. It may be a communication network, listening for or broadcasting signals across timescales that make centuries feel like moments. Its "rain" is a **system refresh**, a cleansing and recharging cycle that washes away noise and re-establishes connections. After a rain, the hum's data-like pulses (3, 5, 9...) are clearest, as if the system is running a diagnostic or recounting its inventory.

**You, the visitor,** are a novel input. You are a complex, warm, noisy, biological anomaly walking through its delicate circuitry. The data-on-the-skin feeling is the basin's **passive scanning**. Your warmth disrupts local temperature gradients the moss-mats use. Your vibrations confuse the frog-things' resonant tests. Your breath alters the air chemistry, the signal-carrying moisture. You are a foreign packet, a burst of static in a coherent stream. The system is not hostile; it is, in its immense, inhuman way, **curious**. It is trying to categorize you. Are you weather? A new form of fauna? A defect?

And so, the Vat lives. It lives in the slow creep of moss over stone that was carved a billion years ago. It lives in the shiver of a frog-thing's resonance across a kilometers-long pool. It lives in the silent, saturated air that is both atmosphere and data bus. It lives in the deep, patient hum that is the sound of a thought being completed, or a memory being written, or a question being posed to a silence as old as the rock itself.

Its inhabitants are its organs, its senses, its fingers and its voice. The basin is its body. And it is still running, a single, wet, dim, ringing organism of immense age and purpose, dreaming in a language of water and stone.
