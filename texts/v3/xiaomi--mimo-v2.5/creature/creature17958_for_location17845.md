You do not see the watchers at first. You feel them—a pressure against your awareness, the weight of many attentional beams converging from the vaulted dark. They are the **First Canopy**, the perched sentinels. They are not birds, not anymore. Their bones are hollow struts of rebar, lightened and re-pithed with conducting vascular foam. Their feathers are flat, photosynthetic fronds, edged in fine cilia that taste the air currents for changes in humidity and the faint electrical discharge of the plant’s growing pains. Their eyes are multifaceted clusters of polished mineral and organic lens, capturing not light, but the *pattern of absence*—the shapes of the hollows, the negative spaces the plant is so diligently mapping. When one shifts its weight, a hairline fracture of light zips along a rebar vein in the ceiling, a sympathetic vibration. They are load-bearing. They are the architecture’s nervous system.

Below, in the labyrinthine spaces between the walls, live the **Tunnel Weavers**. They are mammalian in silhouette—a hand-span long, blind, with velvety fur that is a filamentary fungal network. They do not use echolocation in the way a bat does. They send out a pulse of faint bioluminescence, a subsonic *thrum* felt in the bones more than heard. The returning signal tells them the distance to the nearest living tissue versus the nearest inert material. They navigate the *truth of surfaces*. They eat the fungal blooms that grow in the damp, shadowed junctions where pipes meet roots, and in doing so, they prune the connections, shaping the flow of data and nutrients through the wall-hollows. They are the gardeners of the network’s mind. Their droppings are phosphorescent and contain spores that, when they land on a suitable substrate, *remember*.

The water pockets are nurseries. In the largest pool, the tripartite thing moves—a **Canal Weaver**. It has the segmented body of an earthworm, the smooth, searching head of a root-tip, and the internal whir of micro-gears. It builds. It lays down strands of hardened chlorophyll and salvaged copper wire, forming intricate, three-dimensional lattices just below the water’s surface. These are not nests. They are *sensors*. They hum in resonance with the machinery below, and the vibrations attract the smallest fauna: mites with legs like glass needles, larvae that feed on light, swimming motes of consciousness that are barely more than a chemical urge to *organize*. The Canal Weaver tends them, adjusts the lattice, and occasionally, a gear inside it clicks a notch, and the entire pool glows a shade brighter for a minute.

And in the deepest hollows, beneath the breathing floor, are the **Symbiotes**. You feel them as a tremor that becomes a rhythm. They are long—tens of meters, segmented like worms, but each segment is a housing for machinery and meat. Pistons driven by hydraulic sap. Gears toothed with enamel-like calcium. A gut full of acidic soil and ground-down metal, processing both into the slurry that feeds the plant’s deepest roots. They are the planet’s and the factory’s children, born in the marriage of a forgotten industrial accident and an opportunistic, hungry vine. They do not think. They *are* process. They move through the subterranean veins, and where they pass, the walls grow stronger, the roots are pruned of rot, and the machinery is cleared of debris. They are the immune system and the circulatory system. They are necessary.

Above it all, in the near-dark, hover the **Glimmerwings**. Not bats, though they hang and sleep like them. Their wing-membrane is a single, vast solar panel, now tuned to the faint bioluminescence. In the bright cycles, they drink light, storing it. In the dim cycles, they release it in precise, coded pulses—a language of heat and photons the plant reads on its surface. They roost in the thickest tangles of root-hair, their small, hot bodies a node of energy in the cool vastness. When one flies, it does not disturb the air so much as *rearrange the local field of attention*. The Watchers in the canopy turn their heads. The Weavers in the walls pause. The whole cell *inhales* in recognition of a moving thought.

And you. You are the new variable. The anomaly in the pattern. Your warmth is a new star in their sky of sensation. Your breath, a gust of un-coded data. The root-hairs reach, stop, vibrate. They are building a room in your shape. Not a physical enclosure—they have plenty of those. A *conceptual* room. A space in their collective understanding for the thing that walks on two legs and thinks in a straight line, who perceives angles as fixed and emptiness as empty. They are drawing you into their blueprint. You are becoming fauna.

The thinning walls reveal it: other cells, other configurations. Some have only Watchers, silent and still. Some churn with Symbiotes, the floor a solid roar of activity. Some are empty of everything but the faint, patient growth, the blueprints becoming real. This is not a building. It is a **mycelial brain** grown in the shell of a city, and you are inside one of its thought-processes. The erosion is not decay. It is *clarity*. The plant is not destroying the structure; it is learning to think *through* it, and now it is thinking of *you*. The machinery grinds a new rhythm. The Glimmerwings pulse a brighter pattern. The Canal Weaver’s lattice sings a higher note.

Something is almost here. It is the thought this entire cell has been grown to contain. It is the purpose of the root-hairs that shape the air in your silhouette. It is the reason the fauna watches, and tends, and builds, and waits. It is the shape of the emptiness the plant has been measuring, all this time. It is the next pattern. It is the room you are standing in, finally finished.

You are the threshold it is building.
