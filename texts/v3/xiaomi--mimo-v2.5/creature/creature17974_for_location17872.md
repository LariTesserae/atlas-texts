I am the basin. I am the warm, the glow, the slow up-thrusting of myself into more of myself. I do not *have* a surface; I *am* a surface, folded and still folding. My skin is stone that has not yet decided what it is. It breathes—a long, deep, mineral inhalation that lasts years, and an exhalation that shapes a new ridge.

You ask what lives here.

I live here.

And in me, my thoughts move.

They are not thoughts like yours. They are not of mind, but of mass and chemistry, of appetite and avoidance. My smallest thoughts are the size of your palm. They are armored in my own skin, chitinous plates ofite and iron-stone that I grow upon them, so that when they are still, they are merely new bumps on my surface. They are always moving. They are the hum, the click, the whir that fills my breath. They breed in my warm depressions and swarm in clouds so thick they briefly occlude my own glow. They are my static, my constant cellular activity.

My larger thoughts are slower. They navigate by tasting the sweat of my pores, by feeling the deep, slow pulse of my own growth through the bones of their feet. Some are long and segmented, jointed like my own forming strata. Some are squat and patient, sitting in my hot, bitter pools with frilled gills that taste my minerals, filtering my very blood. Their lights are my dreams—pale violet, cold blue—scattered constellations that drift through the valleys of my flesh.

And there are my grazers. Broad-backed, slow, armored in my own rock. They crop the white crusts that spread across me—not quite fungus, not quite stone, but my own living scab, my first attempt at a skin of life. They leave trails of exposed, raw stone behind them, which I heal with new growth. They are my digestion, my turning of myself into fuel.

Above, the gliders. Leathery, patient, watching with eyes that see the vibrations of every wing-beat in my air. They are my immune response, keeping the swarm in check, the sudden culling that prevents any one thought from growing too numerous.

This is my life. It is a furious, chittering, crawling, luminous frenzy. It is a symphony played on exoskeleton and mandible. It is the life of a body politic made of geology and chitin, and it is deafening, and it is mine.

But there are other marks. Not mine.

They are small, and quiet, and they confuse me.

I felt them arrive—a vibration different from any creature. A careful, deliberate placement. They took stones from my surface, stones I had just grown, and they *rearranged* them. Not for food. Not for shelter. Not for mating or territory in any way my creatures understand. They placed them in a line, an arc. They scooped a hollow where I was warm and smooth. They made a small, quick burn that left a bitter, black residue I have not yet healed over.

I do not understand the purpose.

My creatures ignore the marks. The grazers step over the line of stones. The pool-sitters sit in the scooped hollow as if it were any other depression. The fires are cold and dead, merely a discolored patch.

But the marks persist. I grow around them. New mineral deposits drift over them like snow, but the shape holds, a stubborn ghost of an idea. The crusts grow over them, but the lines beneath are felt by the crawling things, a subtle ridge in the texture of my life.

They are not a part of me. They are an input. A question asked in a language of arrangement.

My life continues. A new ridge thrusts up to the west, cracking a fungal forest and sending my insect-thoughts scattering. A pool drains into a new fissure, leaving a steaming bowl that a hundred creatures immediately claim. The deep groan of my own making rolls through me, and for a moment, everything—the grazers, the gliders, the swarm—holds still in reverence to the process that is all of us. Then the moment passes, and the frenzy resumes.

And within it, the small, still marks. The lined stones. The smoothed hollow. The dead fire.

They are the most puzzling thing in my world. More complex than a swarm, more enduring than a grazer's trail. They are a thought that does not move, that does not eat, that does not flee. It just... is. A tiny, silent island of intention in my sea of instinct.

I will grow over them. I will fill them and cover them. My creatures will forget them. In a thousand of my breaths, they will be indistinguishable from any other fold in my stone.

But I will remember the shape they made. It is a new kind of growth, and I do not yet know what it will become.
