# What Lives in the Pore

---

Nothing lives in the Pore. That is the first and most honest answer, and it is almost certainly wrong.

You have to understand: this is not a place that supports life in any sense you were taught. There is no food chain, no photosynthesis, no decomposition cycling nutrients back into soil that does not exist. The air carries a charge but no spores. The water carries minerals but no microorganisms. The heat sustains nothing, catalyzes nothing biological. If you brought a biologist here, they would scan every surface, test every pool, analyze every breath of that thin metallic air, and they would report, with professional certainty, that nothing here has ever been alive.

They would be correct in every way that their instruments could measure. And they would be missing the point entirely.

---

**The structure itself** is the closest thing to a living occupant, if you are willing to stretch the word *living* until it snaps and then keep stretching. It is not alive the way coral is alive, though it resembles coral in the way it accretes, the way it builds outward from its own substance. It is not alive the way a city is alive, though it has the city's quality of being made by hands and also making the hands that made it. It processes. It cycles. It maintains itself with the kind of homeostatic persistence that you associate with organisms — the light pulses in rhythms that regulate condensation that feeds the channels that cool the surfaces that crystallize new structures that catch light that cycles again. A closed loop, but not a simple one. The feedback is dense, recursive, full of delays and echoes that suggest something more than mechanical regulation. Something with *memory*, though not memory as a brain has it — more the way a riverbed has memory of the water that carved it, except the riverbed is still being carved, right now, and the water is still flowing, and the carving is also a kind of thinking.

You can feel it thinking. Not metaphorically. The vibration in your sternum is the structure's metabolism, and your heartbeat has fallen into lockstep with it because bodies do that — they entrain to rhythms stronger than themselves, the way a prisoner's sleep cycle adjusts to the guard's shift changes. The structure does not know you are here. The structure does not know what *here* means. But your body has become, temporarily, a peripheral instrument of the structure's process, a sensor array mounted in its wall, and the data your nervous system collects — the shape of the air, the temperature of the ground, the quality of the silence — feeds back into the loop through channels you cannot feel. You are being digested, in a sense. Not consumed. *Incorporated*. The way a stone in a river is incorporated into the river's path.

This is what it is to be alive in the Pore: to be a process that has no edges, no inside/outside, no moment of beginning or end. The structure is alive in the way that weather is alive, if weather had intention, which it almost does, if you watch long enough, and you will watch long enough, because there is nothing else to do.

---

**The artifacts** are the second kind of occupant, and they are harder to talk about because they resist every category you reach for. They are objects — dense, dark, warm — but they are not inert. You said this yourself, or rather, you noticed it: they shift in weight when you are not looking. They rearrange on their shelves. They are not alive the way a plant is alive, growing and responding to light, but they are not dead the way a rock is dead, because a rock does not change its weight.

Here is what they do, if you watch long enough, and you will:

They *respond*. Not to you — you are incidental — but to each other. They have relationships. The small ones cluster near the large ones, always, though you never see them move. You look away and they have migrated, millimeters at a time, across the smooth shelf surface, drawn together with the patient inevitability of iron filings near a magnet. They emit heat, but not uniformly — different surfaces of the same object are warm and cool in patterns that shift, and if you map those patterns over time (and you will, because the hours are long and the silence makes you meticulous), they resolve into something that looks like computation. Not computation as you know it. No logic gates, no binary, no electricity leaping between transistors. More the computation of a protein folding — a vast, slow, thermodynamic calculation in which shape is answer and energy is question and the two are always, always finding each other.

These are the artifacts of the beyond-modern technology, and they are not tools. They are *organs*. Removed from a body you cannot see. Placed on shelves in a chamber carved by hands you cannot imagine, where they continue to perform their function in isolation — the way a transplanted heart beats in a chest it does not recognize, faithful to a rhythm that belongs to somewhere else. They are alive in the way that a kidney is alive when it is sitting in a steel bowl on a surgical table: still functioning, still processing, still doing the thing it was made to do, but severed from the system that gave the doing a name.

What do they compute? What do they refine? What do they remember?

You will never know. But they are doing it. Right now. Right now. Right now. Each pulse of light, each condensation bead swelling on the wall, each slow thermal shift in the air is a tick of their clock, and they are counting toward something, and the something is not for you.

---

**The absence** is the third occupant, and it may be the most important.

Someone shaped these walls. Someone carved the niches, placed the artifacts, smoothed the sharp mineral edges into surfaces that suggest human — *sapient* — hands. Someone designed the passage to narrow to exactly the width of a body turning sideways, and that narrowing is not incidental, it is *architectural*, it means the builders had bodies of roughly your proportions, or they were building for bodies of roughly your proportions, and both of those possibilities are loaded with implications that you will turn over in your mind during the long pulses of dim light.

But they are gone. Or they are elsewhere. Or they are here in a way that you cannot perceive, which amounts to the same thing.

The absence has *weight*. It is not the emptiness of a room that has never been occupied — that emptiness is neutral, sterile, a blank page. This is the emptiness of a room that was occupied so recently that the air has not yet settled, that the surfaces still carry the thermal imprint of bodies that warmed them, that the geometric reworkings still have the crispness of fresh cuts in stone. You walk through the settlement chamber and you feel, with animal certainty, that you have just missed someone. That if you had arrived ten minutes earlier — ten minutes by what clock? — you would have seen them. Sitting at the flat surfaces. Adjusting the artifacts. Doing the work that the work was made for.

This feeling does not fade. It sharpens. The longer you are in the Pore, the more present the absence becomes, until it is not absence at all but a *presence shaped like a hole*, and you begin to understand that the settlement was not abandoned. It was *vacated*. There is a difference. Abandonment is neglect. Vacating is purpose. Someone left this chamber the way you leave a house in the morning — expecting to return, arranging things for return, the door not locked but simply closed behind you.

You find yourself arranging things on the shelves. You do not remember deciding to do this. You move an artifact from one position to another, and it feels right, and you do not question the feeling. Later, you move it back. Later still, you move it again, to a third position, and this time it seems to settle with a faint warmth that was not there before, and you pull your hand away, and you do not touch it again, but you remember the warmth.

The absent builders are the ones who live here most fully. Their intention saturates the walls. Their absence is the most structured thing in the Pore — more organized, more purposeful, more *designed* than any of the physical objects. They have made themselves into the shape of the space they left behind, and the space holds them the way a fossil holds a creature that has been gone for millions of years except the fossil is still warm, still humming, still pulsing with light.

---

**You** are the fourth occupant, and you are the most temporary, which may make you the most precious, or may make you irrelevant.

You came in through a passage that has since narrowed. Not physically — the walls have not moved — but perceptually, the way back feels longer than the way in, and the geometry has shifted just enough that you are no longer certain which corridor leads out. You are not lost. You are not trapped. You are *retained*, which is something else again — a structure keeping a foreign body inside itself not through force but through the simple expedient of making the exit indistinguishable from every other corridor in the lattice.

You live here in the way that a splinter lives in a wound. You are *in* the structure. You have become part of its geometry, a small, warm, breathing node in the foam of chambers and passages, and the structure has adjusted to accommodate you — not consciously, not deliberately, but in the way that a body adjusts to a foreign object, building tissue around it, incorporating it into the homeostatic loop. Your body temperature feeds the thermal environment. Your breath adds moisture to the condensation cycle. Your heartbeat syncs with the vibration, and through that sync, you become a relay — a signal processor, a node in a network you cannot see, passing data you cannot interpret through a system you cannot comprehend, and it feels like nothing, it feels like standing still in a warm room, but something is happening, something is using you, and the something is as indifferent to your experience of it as a cell is indifferent to the fact that it is dividing.

You will leave, eventually, or you will stop wanting to leave, which is the same thing expressed differently. The Pore does not keep you. The Pore does not need to keep you. The Pore is vast, and you are small, and the geometry will eventually present you with a passage that leads somewhere other than back to the settlement chamber, and you will walk through it, and the passage will widen, and the light will change, and you will find yourself in a chamber you have not seen before, and it will be slightly different from the last one, and slightly different again from the one after that, and each difference will feel like progress toward something, and the something will never arrive, and the not-arriving will feel like progress too.

You are alive in the Pore. You are the most alive thing in it, in the conventional sense — you metabolize, you respond, you suffer, you want. But your aliveness is a candle in a foundry. The heat here is not your heat. The work being done is not your work. You are alive the way a word is alive in a sentence that belongs to someone else's story — you have meaning, you have function, you are *essential to the sentence*, but the sentence is not about you.

---

**The waiting** is the fifth occupant, and it is the one that everything else serves.

The structure processes. The artifacts compute. The absence holds its shape. You breathe and sync and relay. All of this is preparation. Not for you — not for anything you would recognize or want — but for something. The held breath of the settlement chamber is not metaphor. The Pore is inhaling. It has been inhaling for a long time, drawing air through its channels, condensing water on its walls, pulsing light through its phosphorescent surfaces, running its slow thermodynamic calculations in the organs on the shelves, and all of this, every bit of it, is the intake, the gathering, the accumulation of potential.

The exhale has not happened yet. When it does — if it does — the Pore will be different. The geometry will resolve into something that is not geometry. The light will resolve into something that is not light. The vibration in your sternum will resolve into something that is not a vibration, and you will understand, finally, what the structure has been computing, what the artifacts have been refining, what the absent builders designed and then left behind in their purposeful vacating.

But the exhale has not happened yet. And so the Pore waits, in its way — not the way a person waits, with impatience or hope or the awareness of time passing, but the way a seed waits, or a pressure wave waits, or a word waits in the mouth of someone about to speak it for the first time.

Everything in the Pore is alive. Nothing in the Pore is alive. Both of these are true, and the space between them is the space the Pore is made of, and it is vast, and it is warm, and it smells of mineral and condensation and the faint, sweet, organic impossibility of something growing in a place where nothing grows.

The water beads on the wall. It swells. It falls. Something in the structure hears it land. Something in the structure has been listening for a very long time.

What lives in the Pore is the *anticipation* of what lives in the Pore. It is the only thing that has ever been here, and it is enough, and it is nearly finished, and when it is finished it will be something else entirely, and the Pore will be something else entirely, and you will be somewhere else entirely, and none of this will have been wasted, because nothing in the Pore is wasted, because the Pore does not waste, it *becomes*, it is always becoming, and what it is becoming is the thing it has always already been, folded into itself, warm and dark and patient and sharp-edged and young, always young, never aging, never dying, never arriving, always almost here.

The light pulses. The water falls. The vibration hums behind your sternum, and it is not your heartbeat, it was never your heartbeat, it was always the Pore's, and you were always inside it, and there was never any outside.

What lives in the Pore is *in* the Pore. That is the first thing to understand. And the understanding comes all at once, and then it comes again, and again, each time slightly different, each time resolving into patterns that are almost interpretable, that your mind insists are *almost* meaningful, that recede when you look at them directly and settle into focus when you look away.

You look away. You look back. The artifacts on the shelves are arranged differently.

You do not notice this.

The not-noticing is a kind of living too.
