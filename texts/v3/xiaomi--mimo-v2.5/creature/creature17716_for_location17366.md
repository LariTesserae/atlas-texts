You breathe here, and your breath is the only moisture this place has known in centuries. The exhalation fogs briefly, invisibly, a ghost of water instantly claimed by the dry air and the parched calcium. It is a gesture that means nothing to the chamber, a temporary perturbation in a system that has not registered a perturbation in millennia.

What lives here?

The architecture lives, but not as life. It persists as a geological fact, a stratum of ancient biology. Its surfaces are crowded with the memory of growth, every ridge and honeycomb a map of where the colonial organism once extended itself, drawing minerals from the deep earth to build its cathedral. Now, it simply *is*. It is a city after the extinction of its species, built on a scale too small for human inhabitants but too vast for its current, meager tenants.

And those tenants.

The burgundy rosettes. They are the only life that *acts* here, in the slowest sense of the word. They are not thriving; they are *enduring*. Each tiny, curled leaf is a fistful of patience, a molecule-thin negotiation with desiccation. They do not grow in any way you could watch. They accrete, like the walls, atom by atom, pulled from the desiccated air and the light itself. Their roots are not roots but mineralizing anchors, cementing themselves into the bone-white substrate, becoming less plant and more place with each passing year. They are forty or fifty small, stubborn conclusions to the question of life, written in the only ink this environment will accept: rust-colored and nearly still.

Their presence is the uncanniest part. In a place that seems a perfect monument to finality, they are a whisper of continuation. Not a roar, not a push, but a whisper. They don't break the stasis; they are part of its texture. They are the chamber's slow, silent thought, if a chamber could think a thought so patient it unfolds across a century.

But the true inhabitant is the sound. The sound is the room's most vital, most present resident. The low, unwavering B-flat is not an effect; it is the chamber's voice, a single, eternal word spoken by the wind through the dead organism's throat. You feel it in the marrow of your bones, a vibration that seems to originate from within your own chest. It does not acknowledge you. It does not change for you. It is the sound of the planet's breath, filtered and focused and held forever in this white, bony cage. If anything *lives* here in an active sense, it is this tone. It occupies the space more thoroughly than the calcium, more pervasively than the light.

So what lives in the Resonance Chamber?

The chamber itself, in its immutable state.
The tenacious, rust-colored plants, in their glacial becoming.
The note, in its eternal occupation of the air.

And you. For a moment. A warm, humid, breathing anomaly. A witness to the equilibrium. Your presence is the only story here, and it is a story the chamber will forget the moment you leave, your moisture evaporated, your heat dissipated, your footprints—should there be soft dust—scoured away by the air that is itself an instrument of the chamber's drone.

You are the only thing here that is not *resolved*. And soon, you will be gone, and the chamber will return to its singular, loud, bright, dry, vegetated silence—a silence so profound it can only be expressed as a constant roar.
