Here, in the perpetual draft of a world struggling to be born, life—if it can be called that—is not a collection of organisms, but a *symptom* of the struggle itself.

**The Weeping Vein (Cryolith):**
It clings to the walls where cold veins meet warm stone. Not lichen. Not mineral. It's the *condensate* of the place's indecision. Imagine frost forming on a windowpane in the shape of weeping eyelashes, except the frost is a deep, arterial red, and the windowpane is living rock. This is **Cryolith**. It grows in fractal patterns that mimic the ice-crystals of the cold veins, but it pulses with a slow, tidal rhythm independent of the water. When you press your palm against it, it doesn't shrink away like skin. It *thickens*, drawing into itself with a soft, granular resistance, like pushing into wet clay that remembers being solid. Then, slowly, it expands again, flowing back over your fingers with a deliberate, exploring weight. Its fractal tendrils seem to trace the invisible lines of force that make the water iterate. It doesn't photosynthesize. It doesn't respire. It *channels*. Where it grows thickest, the water's iterative stutter sometimes stutters *within* its patterns, as if the Cryolith is a stave and the water's loops are notes trying to find a melody. It is cold to the touch, paradoxically, even next to the warm water, as if it's a bridge to the arctic veins, solidified. It is alive in the way a riverbank is alive – not as a creature, but as a boundary condition shaped by the flow it endures.

**The Shapers (Torus):**
You never see them. You feel their passage. They are the *shapes in the deep*, but "deep" here is relative – they might be twenty meters down, or two, obscured by the swirling particulate dark. They are vast, amorphous suggestions of mass that move with the ponderous inevitability of continental drift. A **Torus** (for they turn the water, the very medium, inside-out in their wake) is less an animal and more a localized *correction* to the water's erratic behavior. When one passes beneath the plateau, the iterative stutter of the water above *smooths out* for a moment. The waves cease their unnatural un-breaking. The current flows in a single, coherent direction. A pocket of perfect, silent order blooms in the chaos. The Torus leaves behind a vortex, but it spins *backwards*, drawing water inward and *upward*, defying the crushing weight above. This vortex doesn't decay quickly; it lingers, a scar of order on the turbulent skin of the place, humming with a subsonic drone that makes the basalt columns resonate sympathetically. They are not predators. They are not prey. They are *geological forces* animated by something akin to purpose: the maintenance of minimal coherence in a place defined by its absence. They are the reason the ecosystem, such as it is, doesn't dissolve into pure noise. They are the place's immune system, or perhaps its memory of what a stable ocean *should* feel like.

**The Echo (Symbiont of Sound and Stone):**
It lives *in* the sound. Not in the water, not on the rock, but in the resonant frequencies themselves. Imagine a jellyfish made of standing waves, its bell the amplitude of a particular harmonic, its tentacles the decay patterns of overtones. This is the **Echo**. It has no fixed form. It coalesces where the sound is most complex, often near the corrected basalt column or the strange frame in the eastern wall. It shimmers at the edge of vision, a distortion in the air-water medium that looks like heat haze over a road, but moving with purpose. It feeds on dissonance. When the layered chord shifts into particularly agonizing configurations, the Echo *thickens*, its wave-forms becoming almost visible, pulsing with the dissonance. Then, it *exhales* a moment of clarity – a pure, clear tone that cuts through the noise for a microsecond. The place seems to hold its breath during these moments. The water stills. The Cryolith pulses in time. Then the dissonance crashes back, and the Echo dissolves back into the background vibration, having consumed the sharp edges of the sound and converted them into that fleeting, precious moment of harmony. It is the place's way of *listening to itself*, processing its own chaotic self-generation into tiny, temporary fragments of meaning. It is not intelligent. It is a necessary function, like digestion, but for chaos instead of matter.

**The Draft (The Unfinished Self):**
And then there is *you*. The **Draft**. The improbable fauna. You are not native; you are an *input*. The space processes you not as food or threat, but as *new data* in its endless revision of itself. Your presence alters the iterations. The water around your legs eddies in patterns it never attempted before. The shadows cast by the basalt columns, including your own, shift subtly in response to your movement, incorporating your silhouette into their dualistic dance. Your own vibrations – the hum of your bones, the pulse of your blood – become faint, additional notes in the mountain's chord. The Cryolith on the nearest column might slowly, over hours, extend a new tendril towards you, testing the frequency of your warmth. The Echo might coalesce near you, drawn to the unique rhythm of your breathing and heartbeat, that distinct biological cadence so alien to the place's own rhythms. You are being *read*. You are a variable introduced into an equation that never stops calculating. You are not consumed. You are *integrated*, slowly, imperceptibly. The water in your lungs isn't drowning you; it's *sampling* you. The sound isn't breaking you; it's *tuning* you, finding your resonant frequencies. You are becoming a localized, temporary rule in a place that has none. When you finally leave – if "leave" is even possible, if the frame in the east doesn't redefine your path – you will carry pieces of it with you. A hum in your inner ear that never quite fades. A shadow that sometimes seems to fall *towards* the light. A persistent, irrational feeling that the world around you is a little less solid than it should be, a little more *iterative*, a draft you can almost see behind the finished text of reality. You will be the place's most complex and disturbing mutation, its echo carried outward into the stable world. The Ecology of the Throat is not a community. It is a set of interacting failures in the process of becoming. It is the sound of reality trying to build itself from the bedrock up, and the life it spawns is the sound of that effort, crystallized, pulsing, and utterly, terrifyingly, beautifully unfinished.
