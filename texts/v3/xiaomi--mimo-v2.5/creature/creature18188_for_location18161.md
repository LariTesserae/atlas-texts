Beneath the pale, sound-singing walls, life exists in a state of slow, grinding negotiation with the architecture itself. It is a life without sunlight, without seasons, without any of the rhythms that govern existence in shallower waters. The rhythms here are pressure, erosion, and the irregular, haunting music of the current being sculpted by stone.

The largest inhabitants are the **Wall-Scrolls**. You see one now, spread across a vertical face like a patch of living lichen the size of a carriage. Its body is a soft, radial disc of pale tissue, its edges fused seamlessly to the mineral. From its center, a dozen or so blunt, grinding mouthparts work in a slow, asynchronous rhythm, each one scraping a furrow into the calcite. They leave behind trails of powdered white, a fine snow that drifts into the current and is immediately dispersed. To look at a Wall-Scroll is to watch erosion happen in real time, a biological engine powered by the mineral memory of the place. They are not intelligent in any sense you would recognize. Their awareness is a gradient map of mineral density and the faint thermal whispers of the water; they move toward richer deposits, away from currents too violent to feed in. Their luminescence is a constant, soft glow from their core, a blue-white that paints the immediate wall in a circle of dim light, as if they are a slow, living lamp illuminating their own meal. They are the gentle, relentless undoing of history.

Between these monumental grazers, the **Rift-Darts** hold dominion over the open water. They are not numerous. You might see two in an hour of drifting. They are a meter long, all sharp angles and rigid fins that seem to vibrate with contained energy. They do not glow. Their bodies are the color of shadow given form, a deep charcoal that absorbs the faint light of the Wall-Scrolls, making them visible only as moving absences. They are predators, but not of the grazers—those are too large, too embedded. The Rift-Darts hunt the **Sediment-Sifters**, smaller, gelatinous creatures that move through the mineral dust clouds left by the grazers, filtering the finest particles for sustenance. A hunt is a sudden, brutal thing in this slow world. A Dart will hold motionless in a turbulent eddy, a stillness that is more frightening than motion, and then uncoil into a lunge that stirs the sediment into a momentary vortex. The Sifter simply ceases to be a coherent shape, and the Dart moves on.

Life here is a closed loop of mineral consumption and predation. There is no flora. There is only the architecture-as-food. Even the smallest creatures, the **Micro-Crusts** that colonize the newest grooves carved by the grazers, feed directly on the mineral surfaces, their tiny mandibles etching patterns so fine they are part of the wall's own texture. Everything is eater and eaten, miner and mined.

But there is another presence, sensed more than seen. It is in the way certain passages feel *watched*, though by what you cannot say. It is in the occasional drift of a fine, silken thread—too regular to be a natural filament—anchored in a crevice and vanishing into the dark. These are the remnants of the **Tenders**. They are not builders; the builders are gone, or have become the geology itself. The Tenders are what came after, or perhaps what always existed alongside. They are vast, colonial networks of neuron-like filaments that spread through the oldest, deepest layers of the structures, a living internet grown within the bone-white walls. They do not hunt. They do not graze. They *maintain*. You might see a filigree of faint light trace a path along a deep fissure, sealing it with a secretion that hardens into the same calcite-ceramic, a slow act of repair. They are the curators of the ruin, ensuring the instrument remains playable. They are the ones that feel the pulse from below and, perhaps, answer it with these minute acts of preservation. They are the patient tendons of a machine that has forgotten its own purpose.

And the pulse continues. It is the heartbeat of the deep, felt in the bones of the structures. Sometimes, in the wake of a current reversal that leaves the water still for a precious few seconds, you can hear it clearly—not as a sound, but as a *pressure pattern*. A slow, rhythmic compression and release of the water itself, originating kilometers below. The Wall-Scrolls pause in their feeding during these pulses. The Rift-Darts become agitated, their movements sharp and erratic. The Tenders glow a fraction brighter within the walls. Something is still *working* down there. Something in the deepest, most ancient layer of this grown cathedral is still adding, molecule by molecule, to the foundation. It is not conscious. It is a process, a biological algorithm set in motion eons ago and not yet terminated. It is the last, enduring will of the builders, an automatic gesture of growth continued in the dark long after the mind behind it has gone.

Life here is not a miracle. It is a logical conclusion. Given enough time, pressure, and mineral chemistry, life will find the cracks in the monolith and begin to consume it. It will build its own fragile architecture of flesh atop the fading architecture of stone. It will develop its own slow music, its own brutal ballets, its own silent maintainers. It will exist in the shadow of a purpose it cannot comprehend, in a cathedral built to play a song for an audience that may have long since departed. And it will endure, scraping the walls, hunting the drift, and feeling the deep, patient pulse of a place that was never for them, but which has become, utterly and completely, their home.
