**The Fauna of the Narrows of Keth**

In the basalt throat of Keth, life is not an adornment; it is the substance of the place itself. To describe its inhabitants is to describe geology in motion, a mineral metabolism expressed in chitin, membrane, and ceaseless, layered sound.

**The Ground Layer: The Calcified Crawlers**
At the feet of the cliffs, where the fog pools thickest, move the **Tufa-Crawlers**. These are the grazers, the gardeners of the chemosynthetic web. Varied in form—some low and trilobite-like, others more akin to armored slugs—they share a defining trait: their outer integument is not grown, but *precipitated*. As they move across seeping rock, minerals from the water and their own metabolic processes deposit upon their backs, forming intricate, crystalline lattices of calcite and iron sulfide. A Tufa-Crawler is a moving outcrop. They are identified not by shape, but by the specific mineral signature they deposit and consume. The **Sulfur-Backs**, for instance, trail a faint, yellowish rime and are found clustered around hydrogen sulfide vents, their mouthparts rasping at bacterial mats. The **Iron-Snouts** are darker, their carapace a rusty red, and they mine for ferric compounds in the waterlogged channels, their passage leaving iridescent trails as disturbed minerals re-oxidize.

Their navigation is a symphony of chemistry. The smooth-faced creature described earlier is a **Gradient-Reader**, a master of olfaction and chemoreception. Its entire front plate is a sensory array, tasting the air for the volatile organic compounds released by the fungal and bacterial threads that are its primary sustenance. It moves not toward a place, but toward a *profile* of smells, a specific ratio of methane, hydrogen, and dissolved iron that indicates a productive feeding site. To it, the landscape is a map of flavors and gradients, a cuisine of stone.

**The Middle Air: The Sound-Weavers and the Resonators**
The deafening roar of Keth is not background noise; it is the medium of existence. The creatures of the vertical spaces are both composers and instruments in this orchestra.

The **Membranids** hang from overhangs like living, draped tapestries. Their bodies are vast, flat sheets of tough, vascularized tissue, veined with minerals for strength. They do not have mouths in the conventional sense. They "listen" with their entire bodies, their membranes vibrating in response to the complex soundscapes. From these vibrations, they parse the location of airborne prey—swarms of smaller, flying organisms (more on these shortly)—and the movements of rivals. Their own calls are low, resonant thrums that travel immense distances through the rock and air, used for long-range territorial claims and mating rituals. They are the bass notes of the Narrows.

**The Click-Choirs** are smaller, insectoid swarms that fill the mid-frequencies. They move in vast, coordinated clouds, and their collective clicking serves multiple purposes: echolocation to maintain swarm integrity in the dark, a confusing wall of sound to deter acoustic predators, and a communal signal for synchronized releases of pheromones (or their chemical equivalent). A cloud of Click-Choirs passing overhead is a physical pressure of sound, a momentary darkening of the ambient noise.

**The Percussors** are the largest mid-air dwellers, and the source of the wet, rhythmic impacts. They are ambivalent—part creature, part geological process. These are the **Pillars of the Pool**, organisms that grow into immense, stalactite-like structures hanging from the deepest channels. Their upper portion is fixed, a porous mass of living tissue and calcified secretions that taps into mineral seeps above. Their lower portion is a dangling, flexible trunk, armored and muscular. They rise and fall with the thermal tides, and their movement is slow, stately, and devastating to anything in their path. They feed by absorbing nutrients directly through their surface and by trapping smaller organisms in sticky secretions that drip from their flanks. The percussive sound is the **Pendulum-Strike**, their slow, deliberate collision with the basin floor or with each other—a sound like a wet stone drum being struck once per hour, a metronome for the basin's deep time.

**The Vertical Migrants: The Thermal Tide**
This is the most profound rhythm of Keth. As the cool fog thickens in the low hours, it traps geothermal heat at ground level, creating a warm, dense layer. At this time, the basin's life *descends*. **Chemosynthetic Grazers** like the Tufa-Crawlers become most active, feeding in the warm, mineral-rich fog. The **Pillars of the Pool** lower themselves into the deepest pools to absorb heat and dissolved nutrients.

As the upper atmosphere warms marginally (by a degree or two, imperceptible to a human but seismic in impact), the warm layer dissipates. The life *ascends*. The Tufa-Crawlers retreat into crevices. The Membranids climb higher on the walls, following the rising thermal currents to distribute spores or find mates in the less dense air. The Click-Choirs form higher, tighter clouds. The entire ecosystem breathes in a vertical cycle—a **living tide** that reshapes the distribution of biomass every twenty-four hours. To be caught in the wrong layer during a shift is to be swept by a wave of life, crushed, consumed, or simply displaced by a million bodies moving on an instinct deeper than thought.

**The Parasite-Symbiont Continuum**
In the dense intimacy of Keth, individuality blurs. The **Thread-Tenders** are small, grub-like creatures that live within the calcified lattices of larger grazers. They are not parasites in a simple sense; they groom the host's mineral shell, removing harmful bacterial films and enhancing the structural deposits, feeding on the waste products. They are immune system, cleaner, and craftsman rolled into one. Remove them, and the host's shell becomes brittle and fouled within a cycle.

Others are more sinister. The **Vibrating Moles** are soft-bodied organisms that burrow into the calcified walls of the basin itself. They emit constant, low-frequency vibrations that slowly fracture the rock, creating new crevices and seeps. This benefits the entire community by expanding habitable space. The Moles, however, feed on the fungal networks they inadvertently destroy, and their burrowing often traps and kills smaller creatures. They are architects of chaos, engines of creation through destruction, neither villain nor hero, but a fundamental process given legs and mouthparts.

**The Absence of Eyes, The Presence of Everything**
The darkness of Keth is not an absence. It is a fullness of other senses. A creature here navigates a world of:

*   **Chemical Taste:** The air is a plume of information. The scent of a sulfur vent from a hundred meters away is a dinner bell; the faint trace of a predator's metabolic waste is a alarm klaxon.
*   **Acoustic Shadow:** Every object has a sound signature. The scrape of a Tufa-Crawler's foot is distinct from the click of a Predator's mandible. To listen is to see a landscape of shapes in relief.
*   **Thermal Gradients:** Heat is a landscape feature, as defining as hills or valleys. A creature can feel the warm breath of a vent or the cold void of an empty channel.
*   **Vibration:** The constant, low hum of the basin is a map. Sudden changes in vibration—a stampede of grazers, the slow heave of a Pillar—telegraph events through the stone itself.

Thus, in the Narrows of Keth, life thrives not in spite of the dark and the roar, but *because* of them. It is an existence predicated on pressure—chemical, acoustic, competitive. There is no stillness, no silence, no solitude. Every being is a node in a network of perpetual, intimate demand, a note in a chord that has never resolved, echoing in a throat of stone that has never been still. To live here is to be part of the geology's own slow, deafening dream.
