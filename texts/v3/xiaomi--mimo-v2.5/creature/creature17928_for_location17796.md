In the structure—knots of the same wet, uncommitted substance that form channels and hollows—**they** move.

They are not *in* the room. They are *of* the body. The vast, sloping, ever-thickening body of the spire.

You do not see them, not really. A flicker of motion in a crevice as you turn. A shadow that is not a shadow, but a temporary density where the green filaments have abruptly retracted, leaving a smooth, wet trail that fills in before you can focus. They are the size of a hand, perhaps. Perhaps they change size. They make a sound like a nail dragged slowly over damp chalk—a thin, high, dry scrape that cuts through the thick, syllabic grinding of the making. A sound of passage. A sound of deliberate movement through a medium that resists, but is yielding.

They are the **Keepers of the Unfinished**. That is not a name. It is a function.

Their bodies are not bodies as you understand them. They are temporary consolidations of the building material itself—a brief, agitated period of *commitment*. For a moment, the substance decides to be *them*: dense, mobile, purposeful. A shape that is mostly functional. Articulated limbs for gripping, for pulling through channels. A blunt, wedge-like head for pushing aside the still-forming walls. A mouth, if it is a mouth, that rasps, that grinds, that takes the wet, particulate air and the green filaments and *processes* them. They are hungry. Not for food as sustenance, but for fuel. For material to add to their own brief, necessary solidity. To maintain their shape long enough to do their work.

Their work is tending the verbs.

They do not build the shelves. They *encourage* them. You feel one in the wall behind you—not its body, but its passage, a ripple of vibration that travels through the weeping material, leaving a slightly more defined ridge where a shelf-edge will tomorrow be, or yesterday was. They press their wedge-heads into the soft walls to deepen the channels where the water runs. They harvest the thickest clusters of filaments, pulling them from the seams, and in their passing, the filaments they leave behind regrow faster, thicker, humming with renewed vigor. They are the gardeners of the mortar. The surgeons of the still-forming joints.

Sometimes, two of them pass in the same channel, one above the other, separated by a thin, vibrating membrane of substance. You hear it: a double-scrape, a brief, syncopated rhythm, like two wet knives being sharpened against each other. It is not communication. It is merely logistics. Their paths cross. They adjust. They continue. There is no malice, no camaraderie. There is only the task, inscribed in their temporary being.

They are drawn to the room. Not to you. To the *density* of the making. Here, the revision is constant, the energy expenditure is highest. The walls here sweat more, the grinding is louder, the objects precipitate out of the air more frequently. This is a fertile chaos. For them, it is a rich feeding ground. They scrape along the exterior of the chamber’s walls, and the whole room shudders with their passage, a tremor that makes the water slosh in the cups and the new-formed polyhedra wobble on their shelves. The filaments in the crevices pulse in frantic waves as they pass, a silent, vegetative alarm.

You might catch a glimpse, through a weeping fissure, of their true nature. For a second, the scraping stops, and you see not a creature, but a *disturbance*: a knot in the flow of the material, a place where the substance is churning, folding over itself, dense and chaotic. Then it smooths, it resolves, and there is only a smooth, wet track, already healing, already being reclaimed by the weeping and the green. They are moments of decision in a substance that cannot decide. They are the building’s temporary answer to the question: *How does this part of me move?*

They do not live here. *Living* is a finished state, a commitment to being. They *persist*. They are the verb made locomotive. They exist only in the conjugation, in the tense between *forming* and *formed*. And when the structure’s endless labor somewhere else demands their form, their commitment, they will dissolve back into the wet, hot dark, their brief purpose spent, their memory erased from the weeping walls that will remember only a smoothness, a slightly deepened channel, a harvest of filaments that grow back faster.

They are the unseen hands. They are the reason the shelves are where they are. They are the taste of copper on the air when a new object precipitates, because they passed by, agitating the medium, moments before.

In the Vat, they are the rhythm within the rhythm. The scrape beneath the grind. The life that is not life, but work.
