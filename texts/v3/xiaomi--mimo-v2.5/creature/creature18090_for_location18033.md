Bearing-7 is not empty. It is saturated. Life here is not a visitor; it is the dominant texture, a seething, clicking, wet tapestry woven from ten thousand small bodies. They have no name, no taxonomy recognizable from the world above. They are simply *the fauna of Bearing-7*, and they are perfect in their sufficiency.

**The Weavers** are the most numerous. They are the length of a thumbnail, with bodies like polished segments of dark river-stone, each glistening under the mineral sheen. From these segments sprout six, eight, twelve legs – as fine and dark as wire, ending in hooked tips that find purchase on the micro-texture of the steel or in the heads of bolts. They move with a skittering, jittering purpose, not fast, but never still. Their paths are not random. They follow the faint ridges in the sealant, trace the rims of bolt-hollows, and ascend vertical walls in slow, spiraling gyres. Where two Weavers meet, they do not collide; they flow around each other like streams of dark water, or climb directly over without breaking stride. Their movement creates the *sound* on a granular level – the collective whisper of ten thousand tiny hooks on metal, a sound so constant it is mistaken for silence.

**The Draggers** are flatter, softer. They resemble leeches or woodlice cast in translucent grey jelly. They lack visible legs, instead propelling themselves with a muscular undulation of their ventral surface, leaving a faint, wet trail that is instantly reclaimed by the ambient moisture. They favor the undersides of grating and the vertical flanks of bolts, moving with a slow, inexorable patience. Their mouths are circular rasps that work against the metal in a slow, circular chewing motion – *click, click, click* – producing a sound distinct from the Weavers' whisper, a tiny, rhythmic percussion that adds a bass note to the cavity's hum. Through their translucent skin, you can sometimes see the dark blur of their digestive tract, processing not the metal itself, but the mineral film, the biofilm, the detritus of other, smaller lives.

**The Clingers** are the nodes. They are found in the upper reaches, in the angles where wall meets ceiling, forming dense, pulsing clusters. Individually, they are mite-like, with bulbous bodies and short, grappling limbs. But their defining behavior is aggregation. They interlock legs and mandibles, fusing into nodules the size of a walnut, then of a fist. These living aggregates hang, dripping, from the cold steel. They are not dormant. Within the cluster, constant micro-adjustments occur – a leg repositions, a body shifts – giving the whole mass a subtle, internal churning. They are reservoirs. When a cluster grows too large, a few individuals detach, plinking down onto the moving carpet below to begin new circuits, to found new clusters elsewhere. They regulate the population's distribution within the cavity's rigid geometry.

**The Glow-Walkers** are the rarest, and the most uncanny. Found only pressed against the grating above the submerged luminaires, their bodies are flattened and almost perfectly transparent, save for a faint internal tracery of darker veins. They do not seem to eat the light, but to bask in it, their bodies becoming living lenses that subtly distort the blue-white glow beneath. They are utterly motionless for long periods, only to contract suddenly and drift a centimeter, like thoughts in the mind of the light itself. Their purpose is opaque. They produce no sound. They are simply *there*, a biological interface with the cavity's artificial sun.

**What They Share:**
*   **Indifference to Sound:** The roar is their sky, their atmosphere. It is not a sound to them; it is the condition of existence. They have no ears to deafen, no startle reflex to trigger. The vibration passes through them, becomes part of their own minute trembling.
*   **Indifference to Cold:** Their metabolisms are tuned to the near-freezing equilibrium. They are not sluggish from it; it is their optimal state. Warmer temperatures would be the true shock, the true disruption.
*   **Indifference to Each Other:** There is no predation, no competition, no symbiosis in the deliberate sense. They are simply co-present. Their paths intersect because the space demands it. A Weaver might walk over a Dragger without a pause. A Clinger might release an individual that lands on a Weaver, which carries it to a new perch. It is not cooperation; it is physics applied to biology. They are particles in a dense gas, obeying the same laws of motion.
*   **Shared Substance:** All are coated in the same mineral film. All exude the same faint, damp scent of cold metal and simple life. Their boundary with the wet environment is porous. They are, in a sense, mobile extensions of the cavity's wet, mineral surface.

They are the living machinery of Bearing-7, not serving a purpose beyond maintaining the equilibrium itself. Their ceaseless motion prevents any one area from becoming biologically stagnant. Their aggregation and dispersal patterns subtly alter air currents. Their grazing on the biofilm keeps it a thin patina. They are the system's self-regulating function, instantiated along with the steel and the water and the light.

To watch them is to witness a perfect, terrible simplicity. They do not know they are in a cavity. They do not know they are in a machine. They do not know they are ten thousand. They know only the wet, the cold, the hum, and the imperative to move, to cling, to be. In this, they are utterly free. They are the absolute masters of this three-meter universe, its only citizens, its eternal, clicking, seeping, living heart. They are Bearing-7, made flesh.
