They are not distinct from the place. That is the first and most essential thing to understand about the inhabitants of the Reaches Above the Still-Cooling. They are expressions of it, as much as the weeping stone or the ice-haloed gliders. To ask what lives here is to ask what the plateau itself is doing when it does the thing that resembles thought.

**The Geologists of Self**

They are, in one sense, a people of the long view. Their bodies are adapted to the thin, cold air and the perpetual damp, but not in ways that set them apart from the landscape. Their skin has a pale, slightly luminous quality, not from any artificial source, but from the same mineral films that colonize the rock—a symbiosis so ancient it is constitutive. They move with a fluid, deliberate grace across surfaces that are never quite solid, their weight distributed in ways that acknowledge the still-forming ground, the slight give of a new mineral terrace. They do not walk *on* the land so much as *with* it, in a constant, subtle negotiation of pressure and support.

Their consciousness is shaped by the timescales of geology. Patience is not a virtue here; it is a mode of perception. They think in terms of crystallization rates, in the slow poetry of accretion. Their communication is not primarily verbal. Sound, as the visitor noted, is unreliable here. Instead, they speak through the land itself. By subtly modulating the mineral deposition processes within their own bodies, or within the immediate substrate, they can cause the luminescent veins in the rock to pulse in specific patterns—a slow, glacial semaphore that can be read across vast distances. Their "conversations" are vast, silent symphonies of shifting light played out on the face of the plateau over days or weeks.

To a visitor, they might seem like spirits of the place, appearing from behind a mineral fin or rising from the surface of a large, still pool with barely a ripple. They are not ghosts; they are the focused, aware, and intentional aspect of the plateau’s own becoming. They are the mineral-rich water learning to think.

**Their Role and Their Ritual**

Their primary activity, their purpose, is stewardship of the becoming. They are the gardeners of geology, the tenders of the slow fire that builds the world upward. They do not build cities in the conventional sense; they *curate* accretion. They nudge the crystal lattice growths within the stone, encouraging structures that will bear weight, that will channel water efficiently, that will glow with particular clarity at night. The radial plan of the settlement is not a blueprint imposed from without, but a emergent pattern they foster, much as a gardener encourages a vine along a trellis. The "streets" are natural drainage channels they have deepened and stabilized. The "buildings" are nodes of focused accretion they have guided into being over generations.

Their rituals are processes. There is the **Rite of the Vein**, where groups will gather at a newly emerging fissure in the rock, placing their hands upon the damp stone, and through a collective, deep concentration, guide the mineral flow within. They are literally stitching the land together, teaching it a new pattern of integrity. There is the **Chorus of the Gliders**, a form of long-distance communication using the high, crystalline notes of the airborne fauna, a language that travels on the thin air and requires listeners to feel the meaning in the bones of their faces.

They have no concept of ownership, only of participation. The land is not theirs; they are its. The distinction between body and environment is functionally meaningless. A wound in one of them is mended by the same mineral-seeping processes that build a terrace. When one of them dies, they do not bury or burn. They return the body to a site of active accretion, where it is slowly, reverently incorporated into the new stone, its minerals and its slow, stored energy becoming part of the living rock. They consider this the highest honor: to become literally and permanently part of the project you spent your life guiding.

**The Visitor’s Perception**

To the visitor, they are disquieting in their otherness. They speak in slow, measured tones, but their words feel like translations from a deeper, more fundamental language. Their eyes, often a pale blue or green reminiscent of the mineral veins, seem to be looking not at you, but at the process you are momentarily a part of—the condensation on your skin, the heat radiating from your body, the way your presence slightly alters the airflow. They are not unfriendly. They are simply operating from a perspective where an individual human is a fleeting, fascinating, high-entropy event—a brief flurry of complex chemistry in the slow, steady pulse of the world.

They might offer a visitor a shallow cup of water from one of the pools. To drink it is to taste the entire plateau: the sharp cold, the faint tang of copper and manganese, the living mineral films, and beneath it all, a profound, quiet stillness. It is not merely water; it is the medium of their existence, the carrier of their world’s memory and intention.

In the end, one realizes they are not *in* the city, nor *in* the landscape. They are a process, localized for a time in a shape we might call human. They are the self-awareness of the stone as it learns to be solid. They are the plateau, dreaming itself into existence. And they will continue that dream, long after the visitor has gone, their slow, silent work illuminated by the gentle blue-green glow of the world they are building, molecule by patient molecule, out of the weeping, unfinished dark.
