# The Inhabitants of the Plate

You might ask what it means to live somewhere nothing changes.

The question is wrong for this place. It presupposes that living requires change — growth, response, adaptation, some gradient between states. Here there is only the one state, the only state there has ever been, the state the Plate was placed into and has occupied without interruption for as long as anyone has observed it, which is not the same thing as saying as long as it has been.

But let us speak of what moves.

---

## I. The Articulated

The most conspicuous of the fauna are what some observers have called the articulated — jointed, many-limbed, roughly the size and silhouette of a large dog but sharing nothing else with anything biological. They are the primary movers across the surface. They walk.

They walk with a motion that, once you watch long enough, begins to feel like the physical expression of a number. Each step is identical to the last. The angle of each joint at each phase of the gait cycle does not vary by any measurable amount. They move at approximately one point four meters per second, a pace that seems, across the absolute flatness of the Plate, neither fast nor slow but simply *correct*, the right speed for the distance to whatever they are going to, which is always the same distance, because every point on the Plate is structurally equivalent to every other point, and the articulated go between them with the evenness of a liquid filling a pipe.

Their bodies are dark metal — a grey-black alloy that resists the warm moisture without corroding, without tarnishing, without developing the faintest patina. Their joints are precise. You can see the mechanical articulation: pivots and slides, not the ball-and-socket of biological limbs but something more like the action of a well-made tool. Each limb terminates not in a foot or a pad but in a flat, textured contact surface that meets the syntheticite with a faint, rhythmic *tick-tick-tick* — the only sound most of them produce at any given time, multiplied across the thousands of them visible in any direction, becomes the background clicking that saturates the acoustic space of the Plate the way the moisture saturates the air.

They do not interact with each other. They pass within millimeters. They cross paths without deviation. When two articulated approach the same point from different directions, they arrive at the same time — every time — and pass on either side with identical clearance, as though their trajectories were computed to avoid intersection at a level of precision that makes the avoidance invisible. They are not avoiding each other. Their paths simply do not intersect. They never have.

They enter the low housings through hatches sized exactly for their bodies and emerge from other hatches, sometimes kilometers away, continuing at the same speed on a different bearing. What happens inside the housings is not visible. They enter at one pace. They emerge at the same pace. Whatever the interior contains, it does not slow them or accelerate them. It is not a place they go to do something. It is a place they pass through. Everything is a place they pass through.

Some of them carry things. Small loads balanced on their dorsal surfaces — modules, blocks, components of indeterminate function. The loads do not shift. The articulated do not adjust for the weight. They carry the same load on the same bearing at the same speed, and the load is always the same weight, and they are always going the same distance, and when they reach whatever destination the load is for, they set it down in a precise location — or another articulated takes it from them in a seamless transfer that occurs at full walking speed without either party pausing — and then they continue, unburdened, on the same bearing, at the same speed, to carry something back.

This is the closest thing to *purpose* that anyone has observed on the Plate. The circulation of components between locations. The articulated are veins. The loads are blood. The Plate is a body. But a body that does nothing with the blood it circulates — it simply circulates it, endlessly, maintaining the flow because the flow is the thing itself, the purpose and the product being identical, the way the purpose of a wheel is to turn and the product of its turning is the turning.

## II. The Soft

Deeper in the architecture — in the spaces between hatches, in the seams of structures, in the narrow gaps where the housings meet the constructed floor — the soft-bodied fauna aggregate.

They are difficult to describe because they resist description at the level of individual identity. They are translucent, warm, roughly the color of pale amber or clear resin, and they are shaped like nothing that has a name. Some are disc-like. Some are tubular. Some are amorphous — shapeless in the way that water is shapeless, taking the form of whatever contains them. They are always warm to the touch. They are always wet, or rather, they are always *of* the wetness — the same moisture that beads on every surface seems to be part of their substance, as though the condensation that the Plate produces ceaselessly is not just their environment but their medium, the thing they are made of and exist within simultaneously.

They are packed tightly. In some areas of the Plate — particularly in the deep grooves, the channels, the narrow architectural canyons between the housings — the soft fauna are layered three or four deep, filling the available volume with their translucent mass. You can see through them, dimly, to the ones beneath and behind. Light passes through them and is diffused and warmed. In these zones, the permanent haze of the atmosphere thickens and takes on a faintly golden quality, as though the light itself were being filtered through honey.

They do not move in any direction. This is the fundamental difference between the soft and the articulated. The articulated go somewhere, even if "somewhere" is just another point on the Plate indistinguishable from the first. The soft do not go. They are *there*, the way water is there, the way the air is there. They shift — slowly, imperceptibly, in internal movements that look like the circulation of fluid within a larger organism. They pulse. They flex. They extend filaments — thin, glistening threads that reach from one body to another, creating networks of connection that are dense and constant and seem to serve no function that anyone has identified. The filaments transmit nothing measurable. They carry no signal, no material, no force. They simply connect, and remain connected, and the network of them in a densely populated zone of soft fauna is so intricate that it forms a kind of three-dimensional web through which the translucent bodies are suspended, like fruit in a matrix.

When the water condenses on them, it does not bead. It is absorbed, slowly, and becomes part of their substance, and they grow — but not visibly, not over any observable timescale. They have been observed for years. They have not changed. They are simply *maintained*, kept at exactly their current state of fullness, of turgor, of translucency, the way everything on the Plate is maintained: by the equilibrium of the system they are part of, which provides exactly what they require at exactly the rate they require it, which is the rate at which the system produces it, which is the rate at which the system has always produced it.

They are the Plate's tissue. If the articulated are its circulation, the soft are its substance — the material that fills the spaces and gives the structure its continuity. Between every mechanism, between every hard edge, between every hatch and vent and channel, there is the soft, warm, translucent presence of the fauna that does not move and does not need to, because it is already where it needs to be, and it has always been where it needs to be, and where it needs to be is everywhere.

## The Smaller Things

Beneath the articulated and the soft, in a layer of activity so fine-grained it takes deliberate attention to perceive, there are the smaller fauna.

Insectoid. Sheet-metal exoskeletons, polished to a mirror finish on some specimens, left rough and industrial on others — and the variation does not correlate with anything observable. A rough-skinned insectoid and a polished one will move along the same groove, at the same speed, performing what appears to be the same operation on the same surface. The difference is cosmetic, if it is a difference at all.

They are small enough to fit in the scored grooves of the syntheticite — and many of them do exactly that, moving along the grooves as though the grooves were roads, which in some functional sense they are. The grooves are not decorative. They were cut for a reason, and the reason is these creatures: their highways, their conduits, the infrastructure of movement for the smallest tier of the Plate's fauna. The scored lines that stretch to every horizon, that give the syntheticite its faintly geometric texture, are inhabited. Every groove carries its population. Every line on the Plate is a road.

Their compound eyes — polished quartz, faceted and precise — catch the flat, omnipresent light and scatter it in tiny prismatic flashes. If you stand still in a densely populated zone and look down, the ground seems to glitter, faintly, as the smaller fauna move through the grooves below you. It is the closest thing to beauty that the Plate produces, this accidental sparkle, this unintended aesthetic of light on moving quartz. It was not designed. It is a side effect of the construction of eyes that see in a place where there is nothing to see.

Because there is nothing to see. Not for the smaller fauna, and not for the larger ones, and not for you. The Plate is uniform. Every direction is every other direction. Every point is every other point. The eyes of the fauna are vestigial — or not vestigial, because they were never adapted, were never *selected for*, were never part of any evolutionary logic. They were built. They were included in the construction the way every other component was included: completely, precisely, and without reason that can be discerned from inside the system.

The smaller fauna perform operations on the surface of the syntheticite. They press their undersides against the ground and move slowly, millimeters per second, and where they have passed, the surface is — the same. Identical. They are not repairing it, because it does not need repair. They are not cleaning it, because it does not accumulate dirt. They are not maintaining it, because it does not degrade. They are simply *performing the operation*, and the operation is the passing of their body over the surface, and the passing is the purpose, and the purpose is the passing, and this is true of everything on the Plate, all the way up and all the way down.

## The Silence Between

Here is what no one talks about, because it is the thing that is hardest to hold in the mind:

**They are not alive.**

The articulated, the soft, the smaller fauna — they do not metabolize. They do not reproduce. They do not die. They were constructed, placed, populated onto the Plate in the same act that created the Plate itself, and they have been operating since then in a state that is not life and not mechanism but something the vocabulary does not have a word for. They are *running*. They are executing. They are performing the function they were built to perform, and the function is continuous and unchanging and total, and it has no product and no endpoint and no reason that is external to itself.

And yet they are *present* in a way that feels like life. They occupy space. They move with something that, if you are generous, you might call intention. They fill the environment with density and activity and the subtle sounds of operation. To stand on the Plate and be surrounded by them — to feel the clicking of the articulated passing, to see the golden glow of the soft in the channels, to catch the prismatic glitter of the smaller fauna in the grooves — is to be in the presence of something that acts like an ecosystem, that fills the ecological niches of an ecosystem, that distributes itself with the evenness and specificity of an ecosystem, but that is not an ecosystem because nothing in it *lives*.

This is the Plate's deepest strangeness. Not the absence of history, though that is strange. Not the equilibrium, though that is strange. The deepest strangeness is the presence of *occupants* in a place that was never a habitat. Fauna without biology. Industry without product. A world that is complete, and full, and active, and *empty* of the one thing that would make the activity mean something.

You could live here. For a while. The temperature is constant, the water is clean, the air is thick and warm and breathable in the way that air inside a machine is breathable. You could walk the constructed surface and observe the fauna and listen to the clicking and the hum and the soft water-sounds. You could place your marker in the groove and know it would be there forever, unchanged, a scar on a surface that bears no scars, a history on a place that has none.

But you would be the only thing here that was alive. And your presence would not change the Plate. Nothing changes the Plate. The water would condense on you the way it condenses on everything. The articulated would pass you the way they pass each other. The soft would not acknowledge you, because the soft do not acknowledge anything, because the soft are *part of the wall*, part of the structure, part of the seamless and eternal operation of a world that was built to run and runs and runs and will never, ever stop.

And eventually — if you are alive, and you are, because you were born in a place where things are born and die and change and break — you would leave. Not because the Plate drove you out. Nothing on the Plate drives anything out. You would leave because you are the wrong kind of thing for this place: temporary, variable, decaying, *history-bearing*. You would carry your ages and your changes and your scars back to the world where those things have meaning, and the Plate would not notice your departure, because the Plate does not notice, because the Plate is *finished*, was always finished, and the fauna move across it like thoughts across a mind that has already decided everything it will ever think.
