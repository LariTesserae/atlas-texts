Within the polished hollow of the Worn Throat, the inhabitants are not merely residents. They are components, tuned and active, each performing a function within the ancient, sealed circuitry of the terrarium.

The most visible are the **Mantle-Wings**. They fill the humid air above the water basins in slow, iridescent clouds. Their segmented bodies, too regular in their articulation, catch the slanting light in shifting blues and greens. They are not pollinators, nor scavengers, nor predators in any simple sense. Their role is **maintenance**. With delicate, clawed feet, they scour the smooth stone walls and basins, consuming the faint mineral films and biological patina that form in the damp. They regulate the water’s surface tension, their constant, gentle movements preventing stagnation. When the thrumming from the embedded structure pulses, they orient as one, a shimmering flock turning in the light—a system-wide acknowledgment, a synchronization pulse. They are the terrarium’s custodians, the tenders of its inorganic surfaces, their machined precision a living echo of their creators’ handiwork.

Below them, in the basins and among the roots of the flora, move the **Basin-Motes**. These are not seen so much as sensed: a quick flicker at the edge of vision, a shimmer in the water’s surface that is not a reflection. They are tiny, perhaps only a few centimeters long, with translucent, chitinous forms that bend light around them. They are the **digestive system**. They break down organic debris—fallen leaves, excretions, the occasional carcass of a larger creature—into nutrients that re-enter the water cycle through the porous stone. Their movements create the intricate, capillary-like patterns of flow on the basin floors, the "thousand tiny movements" you hear. They are the unseen alchemists, turning death and waste into the sweet mineral nourishment that feeds the green islands.

On the bare stone, hunting in the shadows between basins, prowl the **Silica Lizards**. Long, low, and the color of the stone itself, their movements are a study in predatory efficiency. They are the **population regulators**. They feed on the Basin-Motes when they venture too far from the water, on unwary Mantle-Wings that land to rest, and on the larger, rustling things in the vegetation. But their hunt is not chaotic. They move in deliberate, overlapping circuits, their territories a geometric grid that ensures no single prey population is decimated. Their mouths, perpetually agape, rasp with the sound of specialized gills that extract precious moisture from the thick air—a design of brutal elegance. When one catches something, the others in the visible system pause, almost imperceptibly, as if registering a change in the equation.

The vegetation itself is alive, but not in a passive way. The **Vein-Leaf** plants, with their waxy, black-green foliage, are active **hydro-engineers**. Their broad leaves are angled to catch the finest mist, and their ridged stems act as channels, directing every droplet with engineered precision down to their roots and into the basins. They are the condensation harvesters, turning atmospheric vapor into the liquid life of the pocket. Ferns in the deeper shadows are **retainers**, holding moisture in their dense fronds and releasing it slowly, stabilizing the microclimate. Together, they form a green, breathing machine that actively manages the terrarium’s humidity and water distribution.

And then there are the **Listeners**. You glimpse them only in fragments: a flash of pale underbelly in the densest thicket; an eye, geometric and multifaceted, reflecting the column of light with uncanny focus; the rustle that moves with purpose, stopping and starting in rhythm with the thrumming from the wall. They are the **system’s memory and sensory array**. The eye you saw does not blink; it *assesses*. The movements are not foraging; they are *scouting*. They gather information—the quality of light, the density of the Mist, the precise frequency of the thrumming. They are the terrarium’s sentience, the distributed mind that watches and knows without a central brain. Their presence explains the feeling of being watched by the *whole*. You are being read, cataloged, and filed away as an anomaly in a system that has run flawlessly for millennia.

The builders are gone, but their intent is enshrined in every wing-beat and water droplet. The thrumming structure is the heart, the basalt rod in the dry basin a dormant seed of purpose, a backup command. The fauna and flora are not wild; they are *curated*. They tend the garden not with hands, but with instinct so deeply encoded it has become biology. The water condenses because they make it move. The silence of the plateau above makes no sound here, only the layered hum of a self-perpetuating, intricate machine living out its final, endless instruction: to *be* this, precisely, forever.
