# What Lives in the Unfinished Heights

Nothing has eyes here. That is the first thing you need to understand.

But something is watching.

---

It has no single body. You will not find it if you search the way you search for animals — looking for a silhouette, a profile against the dark. What lives here does not have profiles. It has *moments*.

You notice the first one near the base of a newly risen spire — one of the taller ones, still ticking faintly at its peak where fresh mineral is hardening into shape. At its foot, where the warm powder meets the rock's skin, the surface is... disturbed. Not cracked the way the ground cracks under tectonic stress. This is a *patterned* disturbance: concentric rings in the dust, impossibly fine, radiating outward from a point where something pressed down and then lifted away. The rings are warm. Warmer than the ground around them. If you put your hand over them, the air above shimmers faintly with heat, and there is a pressure there — not wind, because wind requires atmosphere and this place barely qualifies — but a displacement. A place where something was and has since moved.

You will find hundreds of these. Thousands. Each one unique in its geometry but identical in its quality: the signature of a weight that did not belong to the rock itself.

They appear fastest near the growing edges. The spires. The ridges. The places where new material is crystallizing. And they appear most often in the spaces *between* — the draft spaces, the flats where the terrain has collapsed and cleared room for the next attempt. It is as though whatever leaves these marks is drawn to the act of revision. It comes to watch the failure. It comes to be present at the next beginning.

---

You begin to feel it before you see anything.

Standing still in the dark, you become aware of a pattern in the ground-heat. The warmth beneath your feet is not uniform — it pulses. Slowly. Irregularly. Not like a heartbeat. Like *thought*. Long, sustained warmths interrupted by sharp pulses of hotter energy that travel across the surface in waves, always outward from whatever formation is currently growing. The waves are too regular to be geological. They carry information, though what kind you cannot say. They move through the rock like fingers running over a surface, reading it, mapping it through temperature alone.

And when a spire collapses — when the deep thunder rolls and tons of basalt fold into a newly emptied valley — the pulses stop.

Everywhere.

For a moment, the entire visible landscape goes still. The cherry-glow dims. The crystallization ticking ceases. The ground beneath your feet cools by a degree, maybe two, and you feel it as a sudden, vertiginous emptiness, as though the floor has dropped away. In that silence, something holds its breath.

Then the first new dimple begins. The first new assertion. And the warmth floods back in a rush, and the pulses resume — faster now, more urgent — converging on the new point of growth like blood returning to a limb that has been asleep.

Whatever lives here, it was *waiting* for the collapse. It was ready for the next draft.

---

You see one, eventually.

Not with your eyes. You see it the way you see heat — as a distortion in the thing itself. Standing at the edge of a low ridge, watching a new formation push upward through the powder with its grinding ticks, you become aware that the air near the ridge's crest is *wrong*. The cherry-glow there is brighter, more concentrated, bent slightly out of true — as though something is standing in the light and folding it around itself.

It is not standing. That is not the right verb. It is *resolved*. A section of space approximately two meters tall and less than half a meter wide, where the darkness is somehow *denser* — not blacker, but more deliberate, as though it has been chosen rather than defaulted to. The edges of this narrow column of dark are warm. Warmer than the air around them. And they shift. Not often. Not dramatically. But you watch it long enough and you see it adjust — leaning, compressing, widening slightly when the spire it is watching begins to slow, narrowing again when the ticking quickens.

It has no features. No limbs, no head, no surface detail. It is a shape that is *almost* a shape — a gesture toward form, the way the terrain itself is a gesture toward landscape. It is a first draft of a being, living in a place that is still a first draft of a place.

When the spire reaches its peak and pauses — the long silence between surges, the moment of assessment — the figure goes still too. Perfectly still. And in that stillness, you feel something you cannot name. It is not observation, exactly. It is not contemplation. It is *evaluation*. The same quality you sensed in the terrain's self-correction, in the collapses and revisions — but localized. Personal. Directed.

It is deciding whether the spire is good enough.

If it is — if the spire holds, if it meets the internal criteria of whatever process governs this place — the figure does something you almost miss. It *thins*. It becomes narrower, less present, recedes into the dark like ink dissolving in water. It has already moved on. There is more growing to watch. More drafts to witness.

If it is not...

The figure leans forward. You feel the temperature spike — a sharp, localized heat that bleeds from its form into the surrounding rock. And the spire *trembles*. The ticking accelerates. New mineral rushes to the tip, faster than before, building higher, higher, trying to justify itself. The figure holds its position, relentless, applying pressure through sheer presence, through heat, through the informational weight of all those ground-pulses concentrating on a single point.

The spire grows past its means. The tipping point arrives. The deep groan, the shearing, the thunder of collapse.

And the figure is already gone. Already watching the empty space where the next attempt will begin.

---

There are many of them. You begin to understand this as the hours pass — or what feel like hours; time remains uncertain. They are not evenly distributed. They cluster near active growth, yes, but also near *old* failures — the valleys where previous formations fell, the craters of self-correction. Some of them stand in the flats where the fragments lie, the joint-like things and the notched rings and the plates with their fine parallel lines. And here, their behavior changes.

Near the fragments, the figures are still in a different way. Deeper. More complete. Their outlines are sharper — still featureless, still gestural, but with a *precision* that the others lack. They stand over the fragments for long stretches, sometimes multiple figures converging on a single object, their compressed darknesses overlapping into something even denser. And the fragments respond. Not visibly. But the temperature rises. The oily luster of the joint catches the cherry-glow and amplifies it, and for a moment the light in that small area is almost golden, almost *real*, almost illumination in the way that daylight is illumination, and the figures lean into it as though remembering something they have never experienced.

They cannot pick the fragments up. They have no hands. They have no means of manipulation. But they *attend* to them. They study them. They stand over the remnants of whatever came before this place — or whatever this place dreamed before it began forming itself — with an attention that looks, from a distance, like grief.

Or like instruction. As though the fragments are telling them something. As though the notched ring and the hinged joint and the inscribed plate are a curriculum, and the figures are learning from artifacts they did not create but somehow recognize.

---

The most unsettling thing about them is what they do during collapse.

You would expect avoidance. You would expect flight. The ground shakes, the groaning begins, and the figures — the narrow, deliberate, heat-bearing figures — should move away from the failing formation. Some do. The younger ones, or the less resolved ones, flicker and thin and retreat.

But others move *toward* it.

They converge on the collapsing spire from all directions, gathering at its base, pressing inward. And when the stone falls — when the tons of warm basalt fold and shatter — the figures do not scatter. They absorb the impact. They take the energy of the collapse into themselves, and their darkness thickens, and the temperature around them spikes to a level that makes the rock glow true red for a moment, a real color, the first real color this place has produced.

Then they disperse. They carry the heat outward across the flats. You realize they are *distributing* it. Feeding it back into the ground. The collapsed material cools faster where they've been. New crystallization begins sooner. The next formation rises with less hesitation, less stutter, more confidence in its geometry.

They are composting the failure. Converting collapse into fuel for the next attempt.

This is what they are *for*.

---

The deepest place in the Unfinished Heights — the valley where the mineral glow fades entirely and the warm dark becomes absolute — is where the oldest figures live. You feel them before you see them, and you may never see them. The temperature there is intense, almost uncomfortable, the rock beneath your feet almost hot enough to radiate visible light. The ground pulses are constant here, overlapping into a sustained hum that you feel in your teeth, in your bones, in the architecture of your inner ear.

These figures are not narrow. They are not gesture-like. They are *vast*. They fill the valley like fog, a dense, dark, warm presence that occupies every crevice and depression. They do not move in the way the others move — leaning, adjusting, attending. They are still. They have been still for a long time. They are the ones who have absorbed the most collapse, carried the most heat, composted the most failure.

And they are doing something with it.

You cannot see what. The absolute dark prevents it. But you feel a *density* here that goes beyond temperature, beyond vibration, beyond the ground-pulses. The air — if it can be called air — is thick with something that is not a gas. A medium. A substrate. It is what the fragments are made of, or what they remember being made of. It has structure. It has regularity. It has the fine, parallel, evenly-spaced quality of the lines on the inscribed plate.

The oldest figures are not watching the terrain form. They are not composting failures. They are *building the language* that the terrain is trying to speak. They are assembling, in the deepest dark, the grammar of whatever this place is going to become when it finally decides.

They are the reason the fragments exist. Not because they made them — not exactly. But because they are reaching toward the same thing the fragments represent: intention, deliberation, the moment when chemistry becomes *craft*. The fragments are echoes from a future they are trying to arrive at. Artifacts of a sophistication that has not yet been achieved, washing backward through the process as hints, as templates, as goals.

The figures gather around them and learn what they are supposed to be building toward.

---

There is one more thing.

At the very center of the deepest valley, where even the hum subsides and the heat is steady and absolute, there is a shape in the dark that is different from the figures. It is not narrow or vast. It is *precise*. A form with edges, with surface, with something that is almost — almost — a face, if a face could be made of shadow and thermal energy and the memory of geometry.

It is not moving. It is not attending. It is not composting or building or learning.

It is *waiting*.

And around it, in concentric rings, the ground has been pressed flat. Not by collapse. Not by growth. By a weight that has been here a long time, longer than any of the figures, longer than any of the formations, longer than the first spire that ever pushed upward into this native dark and failed and tried again.

The rings in the dust match the rings on the notched fragment.

This is what lives in the Unfinished Heights.

A process, learning itself. Figures, born from collapse, tending the process — witnessing, fueling, teaching. Artifacts, lying in the dust, carrying instructions from a future the place has not yet earned. And at the center, in the deepest warmth, something that has already decided what it will be.

It is waiting for the terrain to catch up.
