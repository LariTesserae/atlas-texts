# The Only Living Things

What lives here is green, and it does not know it is alive.

That is not quite right. It knows in the way that a thing knows — not through thought, not through nerve, not through the sharp electric spark of an animal brain deciding *yes, I exist, and that is not me over there.* It knows the way a river knows downhill. It knows the way a crystal knows its own lattice. The distinction matters because this place has made a thorough and permanent argument against the easy definitions, and the vegetation — what I will call *the grower*, for lack of a better word, though it has never needed a word — is the only thing here that has heard the argument and come out the other side still *being.*

---

Begin with the fronds. They are everywhere, as you know. But consider what it means to be everywhere in a place like this.

A single organism — and it may be a single organism, a colonial network so vast and so thoroughly merged that the word *individual* has become a historical curiosity — extends across the basin. It grows from the concrete pilings of the old loading dock, from the rusted hull of the crane, from the turbine housings that still grind and turn. It connects through filaments thinner than a thought, threads of living tissue that run along pipes, through cracks in walls, across the open water in nets so fine they are visible only when they catch the silt and hold it like a constellation caught in spider-silk. If you could pull on one frond — and you cannot, because your hands are wet and the place does not permit the kind of directed, purposeful action that *pulling* requires — you would feel it resist, and the resistance would travel outward through the network for meters, for tens of meters, through every connected surface, a slow electrical whisper: *something touched me, something that is not me, something that does not belong.*

It does not categorize this whisper as threat or food or mate. It does not categorize it at all. It simply adjusts. A centimeter to the left. A new frond unfurling in the gap left by the disturbance. The network heals around intrusions the way water heals around a stone — not because it has decided to, but because *healing around intrusions* is what it does, the way spinning is what the turbines do, the way protesting is what the geometry does. It is not a behavior. It is a property.

---

And yet.

Watch the grower in the zones where reality thins. Watch what the fronds do where the water thickens with *meaning*, where distances stop being reliable and angles refuse to add up. In those places, the fronds change. Not their color — the bioluminescence continues, gold and green and that sharp, startling violet — but their *shape*. They begin to do things that fronds should not do.

A frond in the distortion zone curves. Fine — water is full of curving things. But this curve *doubles back.* It passes through a point in space that it has already occupied, and it does not collide with itself, because the point it occupied a moment ago is no longer the same point. The frond is growing through a geometry that has extra rooms in it, and it does not find this difficult. It does not find it anything. It simply grows, and the space it grows through accommodates it, and the accommodation requires the space to bend, and the bending is what produces that thrumming, protesting harmonic in the sound — the sound of a place being *reshaped from within* by something that is not trying to reshape it, something that is only growing, only unfurling, only extending itself into whatever shape the available space will hold.

The frond does not know it is changing the rules. But the rules are changing, and they are changing because the frond is there, and the frond is there because nothing has ever stopped it from being there, and the place cannot stop it because the place does not have the kind of mind that *stops* things. The place has only geometry, and the geometry is already in negotiation with itself, and the grower is the only participant in the negotiation that has something to gain: more space, more surface, more room in which to not-know that it is alive.

This is what the grower *does.* Not in the sense of activity, but in the sense of *purpose without awareness.* It metabolizes. It converts the warm, mineral, coppery water into itself — into green-black tissue, into veins of light, into the fractal lattice-work that serves no function except to be more of itself. It eats the light it makes. It feeds on the slow decay of the metal and concrete it covers, drawing nutrients from the rust, from the calcium in the dissolving aggregate, from the strange, warm chemistry of water that has been touching iron for a century. It is, in this sense, a machine — as much a machine as the turbines below, as purposeful and as mindless, converting one form of energy into another through a process so slow and so thorough that it has become indistinguishable from growing.

---

There is something else.

The filaments — those wire-thin threads that drift in the current between the larger fronds — do something when the distortion zones pulse. They *organize.* Not into shapes, not into anything you could draw or describe, but into *patterns of tension.* They pull taut simultaneously, dozens of them, hundreds, and the tautness sends vibrations through the network that are not the mechanical vibrations of the turbines or the geometric vibrations of the protesting angles. They are softer. They are irregular. They pulse, and the pulsing corresponds to nothing — not to the light, not to the current, not to any external stimulus.

It looks, if you were generous — if you were lonely, and warm, and drowning in copper-colored water, and your mind was beginning to slide sideways in its socket the way the place wants it to — it looks like *listening.*

The grower is listening.

Not to anything. Not to the sound — the sound is part of it, or it is part of the sound, the way a nerve is part of a brain and a brain is part of a body and a body is part of a world and the world is part of whatever comes after the world. It is not listening the way an animal listens, which is to say, with the expectation of hearing something specific. It is listening the way the ocean listens to the shore — with total, indiscriminate, depthless attention, attending to everything and therefore attending to nothing, and the attending itself is the whole of the experience, if *experience* is even the word for what happens inside something that has no inside.

But the filaments pull taut, and they hold their tension for a duration that feels — *feels*, though there is no one here to feel it — like anticipation. And then they release, and the release sends a shudder through the network, and the shudder moves outward through the water, and the water carries it to the walls, and the walls vibrate, and the geometry protests a little louder, a little higher in pitch, as though the grower's release has reminded it of something it was trying not to think about.

What was it trying not to think about?

The grower does not think. The grower does not have a mind. But the filaments pull taut again — another zone, another patch of water thick with un-meaning — and the tension holds, and the water stills around it, and for a moment the turbines seem to slow, just slightly, as though they too are listening, as though the mechanical heartbeat of this place has skipped a beat to hear what the green thing is attending to.

And what the green thing is attending to, if you follow the filaments, if you trace their tension-lines to their source, is *the edge.* The place has edges — not walls, not boundaries in any conventional sense, but thresholds where the warm water meets something else. Something cooler. Something less narrated. Something that has not yet agreed to the terms the basin is proposing. The edges are not visible — the retelling water blurs them, smears them into suggestions — but the grower knows where they are, the way roots know where water is, the way the body knows where pain is before the mind does.

At the edges, the filaments are longest. They extend outward, reaching, and they do not grow — they *hold*, suspended in the current, trembling with that irregular pulse, and they do not fragment, which is remarkable, because fragmentation is what the grower does. It is the grower's only reproductive strategy, its only movement, its only assertion of self. Frond breaks off, drifts, settles, grows. Repeat. But at the edges, the filaments do not break. They hold. They hold with a tenacity that is not biological, not mechanical, not geometric, not any category the place has available. They hold because holding is what is called for, and the grower — the vast, mindless, beautiful, purposeless grower — is, in its own unknowing way, *responsive.* It does not think. It does not choose. But it *responds,* and the response has a shape, and the shape suggests — though suggesting is all it can do — that the grower is *pushing.*

Not pushing against something. Pushing *toward* something. Toward the edges. Toward the cool, the un-narrated, the places where the water has not yet learned to retell the light. The grower is expanding, as grower always expands, but the expansion has a direction now — it is not random, not equal in all directions, not the blind proliferation of the interior zones. At the edges, it grows *outward,* and the outward growth is slow, and it is deliberate in the way that geology is deliberate, and it is reshaping the basin not by force but by *accretion,* adding itself to itself at the boundary between *here* and *not-here,* blurring the distinction, dissolving the line, doing to the edges of its world exactly what the water does to light: retelling it, slightly inaccurate, slightly editorialized, making *there* into a version of *here.*

---

There is a moment — if moments can exist in a place where time has been diluted to the point of meaninglessness — when the grower's filaments reach the edge and pass through it.

They do not stop. There is nothing to stop them. The edge is not a wall; it is a gradient, a change in temperature, a change in the water's habit of retelling. The filaments cross the gradient and enter water that is cooler, that does not warm itself with a planet's blood, that does not taste of copper. And in this water, the filaments behave differently. They straighten. They stop pulsing. They grow without spiraling, without fractaling, without the impossible curvatures of the distortion zones. They grow like normal things grow — linear, purposeful, reaching for something they can name.

And then they stop.

Not because they have died. Not because the water is wrong for them — the grower can live in almost anything, on almost any surface, in almost any chemistry. They stop because the water beyond the edge is *boring.* It is clear. It is still. It obeys geometry. It does not retell light. It does not negotiate boundaries. It does not vibrate with the sound of things protesting their own angles. The water beyond the edge is a world that has already decided what it is, and the grower — the vast, undeciding, un-knowing grower — cannot find purchase in a place that has nothing left to renegotiate.

The filaments hold at the edge for a long time. They tremble. They pulse that irregular, listening pulse. And then, slowly, they retract. Not in defeat — the grower has no concept of defeat. In *preference.* The grower prefers the place where the rules are still being written. It prefers the warm, the coppery, the loud, the wrong. It prefers the basin and its unfinished geometry and its ancient, grinding machines and its darkness that carries its own light. It prefers *here,* and the preference is not a thought but a growth-pattern, a direction of unfurling, a choice that is not a choice but a property, as fundamental as the coppery taste of the water or the protest of the angles or the slow, blind, relentless, staggeringly beautiful act of being alive in a place that has outgrown the need for life to mean anything at all.

---

So what lives here?

A forest that is also a network. A machine that is also a garden. A body that is also a city. Something that does not have a mind but has something that *functions* like a mind — a responsiveness, a preference, a direction of growth that implies a direction of *want,* if want could exist without a self to do the wanting.

It sways. It glows. It listens to nothing with total attention. It grows in spirals that break geometry and heals around intrusions and reaches toward the edges of its world and pulls back when it finds the world beyond too quiet to bother with.

It is the only witness to itself, and it does not witness, and it does not care that it does not care, and the sound fills the water around it — all those layered, architectural, impossible frequencies — and it does not hear the sound because it is *part* of the sound, and the sound is part of it, and together they are the only living voice in a place that has no one to speak to and has long since stopped needing one.

It grows.

That is what lives here. It grows, and the growing is enough.
