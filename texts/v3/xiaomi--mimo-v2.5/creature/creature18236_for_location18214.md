In the Combing, life is not an event. It is a state of matter.

They are called **Lithivores** by the few who have named them, but the name is an approximation, a human word pressed like a fossil into stone that was never soft. They do not eat in the way of mouths and stomachs. They *process*. They are the slow, chitinous metabolism of the basin itself, the living mechanism by which the paleite is perpetually refined and renewed.

Their existence is a function of the architecture. The hum in the pipes is not merely machinery; it is a siren song of nutrient flow. Within those damp, cool conduits, a thin slurry of mineral-saturated water moves with ponderous predictability. The Lithivores gather at the joints and seams, their too-many legs anchoring them against the steady vibration. They press their ventral surfaces to the metal, or to the pale substrate where the pipes weep, and they *draw*. They extract dissolved ions—calcium, silicates, trace copper—through a million microscopic pores in their plating, integrating the building’s slow, mechanical heartbeat into their own.

They are the settlement's immune system, its custodians, its decay. They are why the erosion is *complete*. They are the final, gentle sandpaper that brings every surface to its eternal, matte finish. A Lithivore moving across a wall is not damaging it; it is *completing* it, wearing away the last transient molecular chaos until only the essential, geometric form remains. They sculpt with absence.

Their life cycle is geology. The finest, dust-mote Lithivores in the air are not young; they are *potential*. They settle, accumulating mineral film after mineral film, century after century, until a critical density is reached. Then, in a process that is less birth than crystallization, they coalesce. A new shell plates itself from the surrounding dust. Legs unfurl from a knot of mineral fibers. It is an assembly, not a birth. There is no urgency. There is no generational push. A Lithivore might take a thousand years to fully manifest, another thousand to reach the size of a torso, and then it will persist, unchanging, for epochs.

They have no leaders, no swarms, no communication beyond the accidental harmony of their own needs. A cluster on a pipe is not social; it is a concentration of resources. The larger ones that move with such deliberation are not elders; they are simply... more *mineral*. They have achieved a density that slows their processes to a near-geological pace. They are becoming part of the architecture themselves.

Their relationship with the water pools is one of reverence and utility. They do not drink. They stand at the edges, perfectly still, for days or decades, allowing the thin copper-oxide film on the water's surface to precipitate onto their shells. They are polishing themselves with the basin's stillness.

To them, you are a weather event. A temporary, warm, and bewilderingly soft disturbance in the mineral field. Your footsteps are not a threat but a minor, fascinating seismic event. You displace them, and they flow around your boot with the same patient inevitability as water around a stone. You are not a predator. You are not food. You are not kin. You are a *stratum*—a new, temporary layer in their world, as transient as a drip trail on a pipe. They do not fear you because you are not a factor. You will leave. The ground will reform. The grooves you make will be filled with their chalk-dust excrement and compacted into the substrate by the passage of other Lithivores. You will be erased, not with hostility, but with the gentle, absolute indifference of erosion.

Their consciousness, if it exists, is a slow, low-frequency hum, like the vibration in the ground. It is not thought as a sequence of ideas. It is a state of *being-in-process*. It is the awareness of the mineral being drawn, of the shell being polished, of the fine work of abrasion that is their only purpose and their only joy. They are utterly, completely *fulfilled*. There is no longing in the Combing. There is no curiosity. There is only the endless, perfect, and silent execution of their function: to take the world in its current state and render it more perfectly itself.

They are the living proof that the Combing is not a ruin or a place of waiting. It is a complete and self-contained ecology. The pipes hum to feed the Lithivores. The Lithivores eat to maintain the pipes and the buildings. The buildings shelter the processes that feed the pipes. The water pools collect the condensation that carries the minerals. The Lithivores polish the pools’ edges. It is a circle closed so tightly it appears as a straight line—a state of perpetual, dynamic stasis.

In the Combing, they are the verb that has become a noun. They are the action of *being* made flesh—or, rather, made stone and chitin and dust. They do not live *in* the settlement. They are the settlement's living thought, the slow, grinding, and contented pulse of its immortality. To watch them is to witness time itself not as a river, but as a crystal, growing more intricate and more perfect with every silent, grinding moment.
