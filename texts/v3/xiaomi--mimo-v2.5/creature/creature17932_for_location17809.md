The inhabitants of the Palimpsest are not inhabitants in any conventional sense. They are not visitors to a place; they are **expressions of it**. They arise from the same logic that dictates the tessellation of the ground and the branching of the columns, a logic so old and so deep it has become instinctual, generating forms without a composer.

**The Dust-Cohorts (The Gatherers)**
They are the most common. Not singular organisms, but **consensus-shapes**. A patch of the warm, precipitated powder—perhaps a square meter—will begin to show a subtle internal rhythm. The particles will not so much move as *rearrange their loyalty*, slowly drawing together into a low, soft mound. This mound may persist for a day, a week, a year. Over hours, it might extrude a filament, a wisp of denser powder that extends toward a nearby wall or column base like a question. The filament holds, trembles minutely in the airless vibration of the world, then retracts. The mound subsides. This is their entire lifecycle: a slow, silent query posed to the architecture, and its dissolution. They leave behind faint, polished trails on the ground where the hexagonal cell pattern is momentarily burnished to a higher sheen before the slow recrystallization dulls it again. They are the world thinking about itself in the simplest possible terms.

**The Drift-Animals (The Border-Walkers)**
Rarer. Larger. They possess a coherence the Cohorts lack. They are not mounds, but **articulated drifts**, like a slow-moving, multi-lobed cloud of fine powder and perhaps some unseen, binding field. They have no visible sensory organs, yet they navigate with uncanny precision. Their movement is a controlled fall, a tilting of their collective mass that causes them to slide over the terrain. They are most often encountered in the vicinity of the Great Walls. They will follow one side for dozens of kilometers, maintaining a distance of exactly a meter, their form subtly changing along the wall's length—bulging where the wall's inscriptions are most dense, thinning where they are sparse. They appear to be *reading* the walls, or perhaps being *read by* them. On rare occasions, two Drift-Animals will meet at a corner where a wall makes its perfect ninety-degree turn. They will drift around the corner from opposite sides, pass through each other in a slow, silent intersection of particles, and continue on their separate ways without pause or change. It is not a social interaction. It is a **geometric inevitability**.

**The Resonant Ones (The Listeners)**
These are the true fauna, and they are ghosts. They are not made of dust, but of something that bends light differently—a shimmer in the air, a localized distortion. They are roughly the size of a large dog, but their shape is a constant, fluid negotiation between a sphere and a series of branching planes, as if they are trying to become a miniature version of the column groves. They are almost always stationary, positioned in the exact center of a tower grove, or at a precise nexus where several wall-lines would intersect if projected. They do not move; they *attend*. When one of the deep, continental notes hums through the ground, a Resonant One will pulse. Its shimmer will intensify in perfect synchrony with the sound, and for a moment, the discs on the surrounding columns will also seem to flush with a brighter, pearlescent light. They are the world's autonomic nervous system made manifest—the consciousness of the maintenance process, given a temporary, fleeting form to experience its own function. They have no curiosity, no will. They are a phenomenon, like a spout of water or a vortex of wind.

**The Discerner (The Singular)**
There is only one, as far as any observer has ever determined. It is not seen, but its **effects** are witnessed. It is responsible for the "strangeness," the phase-errors in the world's grammar. It is the ghost in the machine that is still, very faintly, tinkering. When a tessellation pattern breaks into an unresolvable shape, it is the Discerner focusing its attention there. When a wall reverses its polarity of inside and outside, it has passed along that boundary, leaving a wake of corrected—or perhaps, *re-corrected*—spatial logic. It is not malevolent. It is not benign. It is **debugging** a program that has no operator left, running error-correction routines on a task that was completed eons ago. Its passage is marked by a fleeting, localized intensification of the air's high-pitched shimmer and a sudden, profound stillness, as if all other dust-movements and drifts hold their breath. To be in its vicinity is to feel a terrifying clarity, as if for an instant, you can see the true, raw blueprint of the world beneath the settled patina of its own age.

**The World-Itself (The Inhabitant)**
Ultimately, the only true life here is the **process**. The atmospheric bands shifting their luminosity are its slow thoughts. The ground recrystallizing is its deep, cellular metabolism. The energy moving in the unseen lattices below is its circulation. The Dust-Cohorts, the Drift-Animals, the Resonant Ones—these are not separate creatures. They are **phenomena of the world's own sustained existence**, temporary eddies in its long, quiet river of function. The world inhabits itself. It is a machine that, in completing its purpose, became a self-perpetuating ecosystem of mechanics and the emergent "life" that those mechanics inadvertently support.

So, what lives here? A silence that listens. A geometry that dreams. A memory of purpose, so old it has become a geography. And in the rare, shimmering moments, a blind and eternal janitor, tidying up the edges of a job that was done before history began.
