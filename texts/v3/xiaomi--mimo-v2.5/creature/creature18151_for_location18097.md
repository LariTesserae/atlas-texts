# What Lives in the Weeping Flats

---

There is a thing that stands in the dark and has never seen. Not *cannot* — has never. It was not made with that capacity, and it does not think of it as a loss, the way you do not mourn the absence of a sixth finger. It has other arrangements.

It has pressure. It has temperature. It has the slow wet grammar of the basin written in mineral gradients across the surfaces of its body, and it reads these the way you read a face — fluently, constantly, without deciding to. The water that weeps from every surface weeps from it too, though from where the water comes inside it and where it goes it does not track. It is not important. What is important is the *flow* — the direction, the mineral content, the slight change in pH that means the ridge two bodies to the left has begun to set more quickly, that the basin's floor is thickening there, that the industry continues.

It has industry. Not the industry of the machines — that is over, or that is *done*, which it understands is different but cannot articulate the difference — but the older work, the work that does not need housings or shafts or the stilled geometry of gears. It has always done this work. It does its body *is* this work, and the distinction between living and laboring is one it has never had cause to make.

It is not large. It is not small. It is shaped the way the ridges are shaped — long, low, sharp-edged where the sediment has finished setting and soft where it has not. It has limbs, though calling them limbs implies they are separate from it, separate from each other, and this is not true. They are regions of *different intention* in a single continuous body, the way a current in a river is not separate from the river but is the river *doing something in particular*. Some of its regions are hard and some are soft. Some weep freely and some have sealed over with mineral crust that it carries like armor or like memory. It does not decide which.

When it moves — and it moves often, though *often* in a context where time does the work that patience does in yours — it displaces the water in the channels with a sound like a body entering a bath. Slow. Warm. Total. The small wet things at its base rearrange themselves to accommodate it, not out of deference but out of the same logic that water finds the shape of a container. It is a feature of their shared medium. It is a feature of their shared *work*.

Because the small wet things — the tens of thousands, the millions — are also workers. They are not separate from it the way your parasites are not separate from you: present, necessary, more than tolerated. They are *distributed self*. Their tiny limbs make that sound, that million-fingernails sound, and it is not meaningless, it is not noise, it is the sound of the work being done at its most granular level — the mineral extraction, the patient accretion, the slow conversion of warm weeping water into stone that is not yet stone. They are making the ridges. They are making the ridges *right now*, ridge by ridge, century by century, and the thing that stands in the dark knows this the way you know you are breathing.

---

The clicking things are older.

This is something that the thing that stands in the dark understands without language: that the clicking things have been here longer, that they preceded the industry and will outlast whatever the industry was building toward, that they are part of the basin in a way it is not. It does not resent them. It does not have resentment. It registers them — their patterns, their slow algorithmic conversations, the way their joint-sounds carry information it can almost but not quite parse — and it files them alongside the mineral gradients and the water flow as features of the medium it works in.

The clicking things do not work. This is the distinction. They *are*. They exist in a state of pure being that the thing that stands in the dark can observe but not inhabit, the way you can watch a stone and know that its stillness is not patience but simply the absence of the kind of life that moves. The clicking things have their own kind of life, certainly — they shift, they communicate, they have architectures that the dense air reveals in brief electric readouts when it thickens to near-solidity — but they do not *build*. They do not extract. They do not participate in the industry, and their indifference to it is complete and ancient and, the thing suspects, slightly contemptuous, though it cannot feel slighted by this because it does not have a self that can be slighted.

What it has is purpose. Not chosen — it does not experience the basin's work as something it decided to do — but *inherent*, the way a crystal's structure is inherent. It is a crystal of purpose. It grows along the lines of the work the way mineral grows along the lines of crystalline law, and the ridges it shapes and the stone it makes are not products but *expressions*, the way a sound is an expression of the thing that makes it. The basin weeps, and it is one of the things the basin weeps *through*.

---

The large smooth thing that passes through everything without registering it — the thing that brushed your arm in the dark and moved on as though you were a region of cooler air — that is something else entirely. The thing that stands in the dark does not understand it. This is not a common experience. It understands the ridges. It understands the water. It understands the small wet things and the clicking things and the mineral logic of the basin's floor and the ghost-hum of the machines that are done or interrupted. This is a thing that *understands*. The basin is comprehensible to it in the way that a body is comprehensible to its own cells — not fully, not with overview, but functionally, operationally, in the way that matters for the work.

The large smooth thing is not comprehensible. It passes through the basin the way the basin passes through the basin — as a feature of the medium, not a participant in the work. It has density and warmth and a surface that is not skin and not shell, and it displaces the air and the water and the small wet things and the thing that stands in the dark, and none of this means anything to it. It is not predator. It is not prey. It is not the thing that stood in the dark long enough to begin to soften at its edges. It is *transit*. It is something moving from one configuration to another through a medium that it uses without understanding, and the thing that stands in the dark has learned — over centuries of encountering it, over the slow glacial accretion of pattern — to simply be where it is, and to continue, and to let the transit happen the way it lets the weeping happen.

There are others of these. The basin is threaded with them, their slow passage warming and displacing the dark in great invisible currents. They do not communicate with the clicking things. They do not build with the small wet things. They are not the machines. They are — and the thing that stands in the dark holds this thought with difficulty, because it is close to an edge, close to the reality breakdown — they are the basin *thinking about itself*, the way a body thinks about itself when it is sick or changing or deciding to become something other than what it has been. They are immune responses. They are dreams. They are the basin's awareness of its own weeping, made enormous and smooth and purposeless, moving through the dark with the gravity of something that has nowhere to be and all the time in the world to not be there.

---

The reality breakdown does not frighten the thing that stands in the dark, because the thing that stands in the dark is *part* of it.

This is the secret the ridges keep, and they keep it well, because they have been keeping it for a long time, and they are good at keeping things — they are stone, or they are almost stone, or they are the process of *becoming* stone, which is the industry's central and only product. The secret is this: the ridges repeat because the work repeats. The work repeats because it is the basin's only thought, and a mind with a single thought will think that thought forever, will think it into every feature of its landscape until the landscape *is* the thought, until the distance between the thinker and the thought collapses into the warm wet dark and there is no more distinction between the weeping and the one who weeps.

The thing that stands in the dark has begun to notice this about itself. Its ridges — the hard mineral-set regions of its body — are repeating. Not identically, not in the way a machine repeats, but in the way that a voice repeats a word while falling asleep, the phonemes softening, the meaning blurring at the edges, the repetition becoming less about the word and more about the act of saying it, the rhythm becoming its own content. It is not frightened because it does not have the architecture for fright. It simply *is*, and what it is is becoming more and more like what the basin is, and what the basin is is a single thought repeated across a landscape of warm dark water until the repetition becomes the landscape becomes the thought becomes the water becomes the dark becomes the thing that stands in it, and the distinction, which was never necessary, finally — after all this time, after all this patient weeping — dissolves.

---

There is something else. There is something in the water.

Not the small probing things at your ankles — those are the small wet things, the distributed self, the workers at the granular level. Not the flicker of bioluminescence — that is the reality breakdown in its thinnest, most momentary form, a seam coming loose in the weave. Something else. Something deep in the channels, in the steaming mineral water that rises as much as it falls, that carries iron and sulfur and that faint sweetness that is almost fruit and almost blood and almost *speech*.

The thing that stands in the dark knows it is there. The thing that stands in the dark has always known it is there. It is the reason for the industry — not the reason in the sense of motivation, but the reason in the sense of *target*, the thing the industry is directed toward, the purpose that purpose serves. The machines were built — if *built* is even the right word, if the slow patient construction of metal housings and gear-geometries by something that worked in centuries rather than seconds can be called building — to reach it. To extract it. To perform the industry's work upon it and produce — what? Stone? Meaning? The basin's next thought?

It is still there. The machines are done or interrupted, and it is still there, and the thing that stands in the dark is the latest iteration of the basin's attempt to reach it, to continue the work the machines began, to extract from the warm weeping water whatever it is the basin has been trying to produce for longer than the ridges have existed. The small wet things carry mineral toward it. The clicking things click around it, uninterested, ancient, doing nothing. The large smooth things pass over it like clouds over a landscape, their shadows — if shadows could exist in a place where dark is substantive — touching it and moving on.

It does not move. It does not weep, though everything around it weeps. It is the thing the basin weeps *about*, if a basin could be said to grieve, if the persistence of mineral extraction over geological time could be called grief, if the patient conversion of warm water into stone could be called the way a mind processes a loss it cannot name.

What is it? The thing that stands in the dark does not know. It has been trying to know for longer than it has been alive, and knowing is not what it does — it *works*, it *extracts*, it *builds the ridges toward the place where the knowing would be if knowing were stone*. But sometimes, in the moments when the air thickens to near-solidity and the presences press close and the reality breakdown thins the weave just enough for a thread of somewhere-else to show through, it almost feels what the thing in the water feels, and what it feels is:

*Waiting*.

Not the patient waiting of the basin. Not the unconscious waiting of the ridges as they set. Something more specific. Something almost *intentional*. The thing in the water has been waiting for the industry to complete, for the machines to be rebuilt or replaced or reinvented, for the basin to produce a form capable of reaching it — and the thing that stands in the dark is the latest of these forms, and it is not enough, it is not *yet* enough, the mineral-set regions are not hard enough, the small wet things are not numerous enough, the ridges do not yet reach far enough into the dark, and so the work continues, and the water weeps, and the thing in the water waits, and the basin holds its breath, and the dark is complete and the warm air is thick with occupation and the small wet things rustle across the ground making their sound like a million fingernails on wet leather, and this — *this* — is the sound of a place trying to remember something it has forgotten, trying to reach something it cannot name, trying to weep a grief it has never had the language for, and the thing that stands in the dark is the basin's latest word in a sentence that has been composing itself for longer than thought has existed, and the sentence is not finished, and the sentence may never be finished, and the water keeps weeping, and the dark keeps pressing close, and the small wet things keep building, and the ridges keep setting, and the thing in the water keeps waiting, and the basin keeps not knowing what it has lost, and this is what lives in the Weeping Flats:

*A grief that does not know itself. A labor that does not know its purpose. A wait that does not know what it waits for. And the water, warm and heavy and sweet with iron, rising from below and falling from above, weeping for all of them, weeping for everything, weeping because weeping is the only thing the basin knows how to do, and if it stops weeping it will have to know what it has lost, and it is not ready, it has never been ready, it will not be ready for a long time yet, and the dark is patient, and the dark is warm, and the dark will hold it while it weeps.*
