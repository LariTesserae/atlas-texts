# The Unfinished Heights

There is no horizon. Not because the land curves away from you — it hasn't decided what shape it is yet.

The ground beneath your feet is warm. Not hot, not scorching, but *alive-warm*, like the belly of something breathing. The rock — if it is rock — has a softness at its edges, a quality of recently hardened. Step on it and you feel the faintest give, as though the surface is a skin still curing over a deeper body. It is dry in a way that has no precedent in any terrestrial landscape: not parched, not drought-stricken, but *ignorant* of water entirely, as though the concept hasn't occurred to this place. The mineral is powdery where it is granite, glassy where it is obsidian, and somewhere between the two it takes on textures that don't resolve cleanly — striations that loop, crystalline faces that meet at angles a compass would refuse. Every surface has a faint warmth radiating upward into the dark.

Because it is dark. Not the darkness of night, which carries with it the memory of a sun. This is a *native* darkness — the deep, self-authored black of a place that has never been illuminated. You can see, somehow. Not well. The warmth has a color if you stare long enough: a dim, deep-cherry luminosity that seems to bleed from the rock itself, from the spaces between mineral grains, from the hairline fractures that web across every surface like veins in something just born. It is not light, exactly. It is the *adjacency* of light. The suggestion that the rock is barely below the threshold of glowing, that if the temperature climbed another few degrees it might begin to see itself.

And above — if above is even the right direction — the terrain is impossible.

It rises. Everything rises. The land does not slope; it *launches*. Pillars and slabs and sheets of dark stone jut upward at angles that would be structural nightmares if any of this had existed long enough to have a structure. Some formations are miles high, tapering to needle-thin spires that vanish into total blackness far overhead. Others lean against each other in vast, precarious geometries — cantilevers of warm basalt spanning hundreds of meters with no apparent support, their undersides sweating that faint cherry glow. There are ridges so narrow at their crests that they seem to have been drawn with a knife. There are depressions — not craters, because nothing has impacted them — that descend into depths where even the mineral glow fades, and the warm dark becomes absolute.

And the place is *moving*.

Not fast. Not visibly, at first. But stand here long enough — a minute, two minutes — and you feel it. A vibration in the ground that is not seismic in any familiar sense. It is the vibration of *formation*. The spire to your left — the one that wasn't there when you first looked at it, you're almost certain — is growing. Not like a plant grows, not smoothly or patiently. It pushes upward in stuttering, grinding increments, stone compressing itself against stone, new mineral crystallizing at its tip with faint, almost-inaudible ticking sounds, like cooling glass. Between these ticks — long silences, stretches of absolute stillness and quiet where you can hear your own blood — the next surge arrives, and the pillar adds another meter to its height, and somewhere nearby a different formation groans, tilts, and shears. You hear the fall only distantly: a deep, muffled thunder, more felt than heard, as tons of warm rock collapse into a valley that was, moments ago, a plain.

The erosion here is not the erosion of wind or water. There is no wind. There is no water. The erosion is *self-correction* — the terrain collapsing under its own ambitions, shedding mass it cannot support, reforming. You realize, after watching several cycles, that the landscape is iterating. Each collapse clears space. Each new growth fills it. The place is sketching itself, rubbing out its mistakes, trying again. The sparse distribution of features — a lone tower here, a low ridge there, vast empty flats between — is not emptiness. It is *draft space*. Room to fail. Room to become.

Scattered across the flats, half-buried in powder and grit, you find fragments. They are difficult to categorize. One looks like a joint, or a hinge, cast in something harder than the surrounding basalt — a mineral with a faint, oily luster that catches the cherry-glow and amplifies it into something almost golden. It is not a tool. It has no handle, no edge, no purpose you can name. But it is *shaped* — deliberately, geometrically — in a way that the growing towers are not. Another fragment is a ring, or a section of one, with regular notches along its inner face. A third is a flat plate inscribed with parallel lines so fine they might be natural fracture patterns, except that they are perfectly evenly spaced, and they stop at a precise border as though drawn by someone who knew exactly where to end.

These fragments have no context. There are no ruins, no walls, no foundations. They sit in the dust like dropped letters from a language that doesn't exist yet. They are warm, like everything else. They are old — or they are new. In a place where the ground is still deciding what it is, age is an open question.

The quiet here is immense. It is not silence — silence implies that sound has been removed. This is *pre-sound*, the acoustic equivalent of the landscape's own pre-formation state. The noises that do occur are singular events: the ticking of crystallization, the subsonic groan of a slab shifting a millimeter to the left, the brief, papery whisper of mineral dust sliding down a slope that has just steepened by a degree. Each sound arrives, occupies the dark, and vanishes. Nothing echoes. The geometry is too broken, too new, for repetition. The sound goes out and dies in some unfinished crevice.

You realize you are standing inside something that is trying to be born.

Not a creature. Not a world, exactly — or not one that has committed to being a world yet. It is a *process*. A place in the act of becoming, using heat and pressure and the blind persistence of chemistry to pile matter into shapes it cannot yet justify. The turbulence is not destruction. It is the labor of assembly. Every collapse is a revision. Every spire that pierces upward into the lightless void is a thesis: *this is what I will be*. And the silence between them is the place listening to itself, deciding whether to agree.

If you stay — and the warmth invites you to stay, it is comfortable in a way that darkness should not be — you might watch the nearest flat expanse begin to dimple. A gentle depression, no wider than a room. The cherry-glow at its center brightens fractionally. Over the course of an hour, or a day — time is unmoored here — the dimple deepens, the walls steepen, and the base crystallizes into a new material: something veined with gold-bright fracture lines, humming at a frequency just below hearing. It is the first object in this new valley. The first assertion.

It will not be the last. But it will fall, too, eventually. And something else will try.
