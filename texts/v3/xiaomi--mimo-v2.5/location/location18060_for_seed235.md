# The Resin Plateau

You are standing on a table of the world that should not exist.

The altitude alone should kill you — you are so high that the curvature of the earth is a rumor you can almost verify with your eyes. The sky is not the blue of lowlands. It is a thick, humid amber, swollen with moisture that refuses to behave according to altitude. Steam rises from everything. The air itself is a bath: warm, dense, almost liquid in the throat. At this height there should be thin, freezing air and clean silence. Instead there is a cauldron.

The sound is the first thing that breaks you.

It comes from everywhere and has always been coming. A roar that is not wind, not water, not geological — or rather, it is all three at once, fused into a single continuous bellow that has the texture of a voice if a voice were a mountain range. It does not ebb. It does not pulse. It is *constant*, a wall of sound so total and so unchanging that the ear stops hearing it the way one stops feeling gravity. It is simply the medium now. You think inside it. The roar is the silence of this place.

Below you — far below, in the sense that everything here is simultaneously *below* and *beside* and *within* — a city spreads across the plateau like a living bruise.

It was not built. There are no tools here, no devices, no evidence of hands. The city was *grown*. Or rather, it emerged, the way coral emerges, or the way a stalactite knows its own length. Towers of compressed peat andite calcium rise in dense clusters, their surfaces slick with condensation, weeping mineral water in sheets that run down their faces and pool in the channels between them — channels that function as streets, that curve and intersect with an urban logic that has no architect. The structures are organic in profile but precise in repetition: arches that recur every nine body-lengths, domes that all share the same curvature, doorways — if they are doorways — that open onto interior darkness at a regularity that suggests planning without suggesting a planner. The entire city looks like something a river would build if a river thought in straight lines.

The vegetation is voracious. Lush does not capture it. The green here is a *geological force*. It is everywhere — not growing *on* the structures but growing *from* them, *as* them, the boundary between city and forest dissolved into irrelevance. Great ferns with fronds the length of houses unfurl in the perpetual steam and never die back, because nothing here ever dies and nothing here ever changes. Vines thick as your torso wrap the towers in spirals so regular they look machined, and from their surfaces hang curtains of moss that drip constantly, feeding the streams in the streets below. The ground between the structures is not ground — it is a carpet of broad-leafed growth, wet and warm to the touch, breathing out moisture that rises to join the already-saturated air. At this heat and altitude, the vegetation should be sparse, alpine, stunted. Instead it is equatorial, monstrous, indifferent to the physics that should constrain it. Every leaf is the deep green of a thing that has never known a dry day. Every stem is fat with water. The whole plateau is a jungle wearing the shape of a city, or a city that has forgotten it was ever separate from a jungle.

There are no animals. Not a single one. No birds in the steam-thick sky, no insects humming in the vast canopy, no lizards warming themselves on the wet stone. The silence of life — beneath the roar — is total. The ecology here is *complete without fauna*: plants only, fungal networks only, bacterial clouds only, a world of autotrophs and decomposers that has somehow closed its cycle without ever requiring a consumer. It is a sentence that conjugates without a subject. You notice, after a while, that the plants do not respond to your presence. They do not bend toward you or away from you. You are not food and not threat. You are weather, briefly.

And the weather is reality, here, and reality is broken.

It is not broken dramatically. There is no shattering, no obvious seam between the possible and the impossible. It is broken the way a bone is broken when it heals slightly wrong: everything still functions, but the angles are *off*. Perspective is wrong. The towers in the middle distance are both farther and nearer than the towers beside you; your eyes cannot settle on a consistent depth. Shadows fall in directions that have no corresponding light source. The steam that rises from the ground does not rise — you can see it, you can feel it, but if you watch carefully it is always *arriving* from above and pooling downward, a rain that un-happens. The streams in the streets flow in all directions simultaneously, including uphill, including into themselves. The city is the correct size for the plateau it sits on, and yet when you look at its edges — where the jungle meets the cliff and the cliff drops away into whatever this elevation calls "the world below" — the city is *also* too large for the plateau, spilling past its borders by distances that should be visible but are not, because perspective is wrong, because the edges of things have become suggestions rather than facts.

The water never stops condensing. It is always settling on surfaces, always pooling, always running, always arriving. The entire plateau weeps, constantly, at a rate that should long ago have washed it clean or carried it away. But the erosion is established — settled, ancient, *done*. The plateau has reached the shape it will hold forever. Water flows and the stone does not change. The streams have worn their channels over millennia and those channels are now exactly as deep as they will ever be, a frozen equilibrium of geological process, a snapshot of erosion that has been held in place like a breath never exhaled.

You begin to understand that this place is a pause.

Everything here is *mid-process* — the erosion mid-flow, the vegetation mid-grow, the city mid-emerge, the sound mid-roar, the reality mid-collapse — and the pause has been held for so long that it has become the permanent state. The world broke and then *stayed* broken, in exactly the configuration of its breaking, and all the forces that should have continued — erosion, growth, decay, the settling of a collapsing reality into something stable — instead froze at the moment of their greatest complexity. This is not a ruin. This is not a living place. It is a *held place*, a breath held by something that has forgotten to exhale, and the deafening sound is the effort of that holding: the sound of every process in this landscape pressing against the moment that will not come.

The light is normal. That is perhaps the strangest thing. After everything else, the light is just light — diffuse, humid, the pale gold of a sun filtered through heavy steam. It falls on the weeping towers and the vast ferns and the impossible streams with an almost tender ordinariness, as though it has not noticed what everything else is doing. It does not flicker. It does not bend. It is the one faithful thing.

You stand on the plateau, and the plateau does not change, and you cannot tell if you have been here for seconds or centuries, because time here is the same as everything else: paused, mid-breath, deafening with the effort of not becoming.
