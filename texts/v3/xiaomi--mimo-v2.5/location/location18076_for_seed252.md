# The Bright Wet Pocket

You are beneath the surface — not far, but enough that the world above has compressed into a pressure you feel in your inner ears rather than consciously register. The space is small. Calling it a cave overstates it. It is a *pocket*, a pocket within something larger, and whether that something is stone or something else you cannot tell because you have been here only long enough for your eyes to adjust to a brightness that shouldn't exist this deep.

But it does.

The light comes from the walls. Not uniformly — in patches, in patches within patches, the way mold blooms on bread, except this is light and the surface is rock and the temperature is high enough that you sweat within seconds of standing still. The rock itself is pale, almost white where the luminosity is strongest, and the brightness has a faintly wet quality to it, as though each glowing patch is slightly damp. They are. The walls weep. A thin film of condensation covers everything, gathering in droplets along sharp crystalline edges that jut from the cavity's surfaces at angles too clean to be natural erosion — though the formation is young enough that there *is* no erosion yet, and everything looks as though it was cut or extruded or deposited recently, in geological terms. Days, maybe. Or centuries. The sharpness doesn't diminish your suspicion that this space didn't exist a short time ago.

The droplets fall. This is the sound — the only persistent sound — a slow, arrhythmic tapping on the floor, which is not flat but faceted, like a geode split open and only partially emptied. The floor is composed of the same pale mineral as the walls, and where the water collects in shallow, angular basins, it takes on a faint opalescence, shifting between clear and milky as the mineral dissolves at rates that are, by any normal standard, too fast. The water is not warm. It is not cold. It is *hot*, matching the air, which itself matches the walls: a steady, enveloping heat that doesn't fluctuate. There is no breeze. The air is thick with humidity, heavy in the lungs, and every breath tastes faintly of something metallic, something almost alkaline, something your tongue wants to call *electrical* but can't quite settle on.

There is no plant life. Nothing green, nothing fungal, nothing that would register as biology to a casual observer. The surfaces are mineral — entirely mineral — except that the mineral is doing things. In the brightest patches, if you watch long enough, you can see a faint redistribution of the luminosity: the light migrates, slowly, across the surface, like shadows of clouds passing overhead except there are no clouds and no overhead. The glow *moves*. It concentrates along certain edges, dims along others, pools in the basins where water stands, and occasionally — rarely, and you might miss it — two patches of light will touch and one will extinguish and the other will brighten as though it fed.

This is what you mean by *technology*. Not circuits, not screens, not anything shaped by a hand or a purpose you can name. The mineral itself appears to be processing something. The lattice structure of the crystal — visible under the luminosity if you press your face close enough, which the heat discourages — is intricate in a way that is not decorative. The patterns repeat but not identically. They *adapt*. A fracture line runs through one face of the wall, and around that fracture the crystal has reorganized itself in a branching, dendritic arrangement that looks less like growth and more like *routing*, the way wires redirect around a break in a circuit board, except no one built this and the circuit board is made of something that doesn't appear in any mineral database you've ever encountered.

The fauna is rare, but present. Small things. You see one — a translucent, shrimp-sized organism clinging to a wet overhang, nearly invisible against the luminous rock. It has no eyes. Its body is segmented and mineral-crusted, and when you exhale near it, it releases from the surface and drifts — doesn't swim, *drifts* — on a current of warm, dense air before resettling two inches away. There may be others. You don't hear them. You don't hear anything except the droplets and, beneath that, a very faint hum. Not mechanical. More the way a cathedral hums — resonance, the space itself vibrating at a frequency just below your threshold of real hearing, felt in the teeth and in the sternum.

The pocket is not empty, but it is sparse. The luminous patches are scattered, separated by stretches of dark, dry rock that is just stone — old stone, older than the bright mineral, older than the water, the basement geology through which this pocket has somehow opened. You can touch it if you reach past the wet crystal faces. It is coarse-grained and warm but not hot, ordinary granite or gneiss, and it has no luminescence and no purpose. It is the native material, the world that was already here before the pocket appeared and filled itself with weeping light-mineral and hot condensation and whatever chemistry is running silently through the crystal lattice at a speed and sophistication you cannot measure with anything you brought.

The process is ongoing. You can see new crystal faces forming — thin, bright, translucent sheets extruding from the existing surfaces at the water line, growing in real-time, not quickly but noticeably, adding millimeters while you watch. They grow, they dim as they mature, and the luminosity relocates to the freshest formations. The water feeds this. The heat feeds this. Something beneath the floor feeds this — a warmth rising from deeper, from below even the below, that keeps the whole system active, alive, or whatever word applies to a mineral formation that computes and glows and weeps in a small hidden pocket beneath an unremarkable surface.

You could stand in this space and turn in a full circle and see all of it. It is not a room. It is not a cavern. It is a *feature* — a single, contained, self-sustaining feature embedded in ordinary rock like a jewel in a seam, like a thought in a mind that doesn't know it's thinking. The bright light hums. The water taps. The crystal grows. The small eyeless things cling and drift. No one made this. And yet something is being made.
