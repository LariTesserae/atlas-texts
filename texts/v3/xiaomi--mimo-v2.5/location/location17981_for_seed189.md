# The Breathing Pocket

You are below the surface. Not deep — perhaps a hand's breadth of earth above — but below, where the light that reaches you is already filtered, already translated, already *wrong*. It is normal enough to see by. It comes from no particular source. It simply *is*, the way hunger is: ambient, directionless, owned by the space rather than cast into it.

## The Space

The hollow is small. Measurable. You could span it with your arms if your arms could agree on which direction to reach, which they cannot, because the walls are at once inches from your face and impossibly receding — a corridor of warm air that is no wider than a clenched fist but stretches, in places, into distances that have no business existing inside a thing this size. The geometry is young. It hasn't settled. The edges are sharp, the surfaces fresh, as though the rock cracked open moments ago and is still deciding what shape to be.

And everywhere — *everywhere* — the plants.

## The Living Architecture

There is no soil here. There is no stone that is not also tissue. The entire substrate is vegetative: a dense, layered crust of root-matted growth that coats every surface, that *is* every surface. The walls are the outer skin of something alive. Press your palm against one and it yields, slightly, the way a cheek yields — warm, turgid, slick with a condensation that has no source you can name. The texture beneath your fingers is not bark, not moss, not fungal mat, though it partakes of all three. It is growth-as-material, plant-matter so thoroughly committed to being the ground that it has forgotten it was ever anything else.

Filaments hang from the ceiling — or what functions as a ceiling — in curtains so thin they are nearly invisible until the light catches them and they ignite into thread-thin filaments of photosynthetic green, each one no thicker than an eyelash, each one *humming* with internal fluid pressure. The sound they make collectively is not a hum. It is closer to a roar. A vast, wet, arterial roar, like pressing your ear to the chest of something enormous and hearing its whole circulation. This is the loudness of this place: not the crack of thunder or the rush of wind, but the intimate enormity of water moving through living tissue at speed, through ten thousand vessels all conducting their private rivers simultaneously.

The floor — if you can call it a floor — is a quilted expanse of swollen root-nodes, each one roughly the size of a man's fist, packed together with just enough gap between them for moisture to collect. The gaps are dark. The nodes are pale, almost luminous, and they *pulse*. Not rhythmically. Not with any pattern you could chart. They pulse the way a bruise pulses: irregularly, involuntarily, each one on its own private schedule of pressure and release. Step on one and it flattens, exudes a warm fluid that smells of green-cut stems and copper, then slowly, over the course of a minute, reinflates beneath your weight as though offended by your presence.

## The Emptiness

Between the living masses — between the thick walls of root-crust and the hanging filaments and the pulsing floor — there is *nothing*. Vast, ringing nothing. The air itself feels diluted, not thin exactly but *absent*, as though the space between objects has been stretched and stretched until it is mostly potential rather than substance. You can wave your hand through a gap between two root-columns and your hand moves for what feels like seconds before encountering anything again. The distances lie. The distances have always been lying. This place was not carved by water or cracked by frost. It was *imagined* open, and the imagination was generous, and the resulting void is generous in return: patient, warm, and very nearly empty.

Dust motes — or spores, or pollen — drift through these gaps with a slowness that suggests the air itself is reluctant to move them along. They catch the sourceless light and hold it. Some of them glow faintly on their own, a bioluminescent dust that accumulates in the hollows between root-nodes and makes them look like the sockets of something vast and sleeping.

## The Water

Moisture here is not weather. It is *exudation*. The plants weep. The walls bead with condensation that gathers, swells, detaches, and falls in fat drops that land on the nodes below with a sound like fingers tapping a table — but there are so many drops, from so many surfaces, that the tapping becomes a texture, a constant percussive patter that underlies the arterial roar like a secondary heartbeat. Where the drops collect, they form pools — tiny, shallow, no deeper than a thumb-width — that sit in the cups between root-masses and reflect the unchanging light with a stillness that feels deliberate, almost confrontational, as though the water is watching you with the patience of something that has never needed to move.

The pools are not water. They are too viscous, too faintly green. They cling to your skin when you lift your hand from them, and they do not evaporate. They are absorbed. Your skin drinks them, and where they were, the skin becomes briefly, inexplicably warm.

## The Sound

You have been hearing the roar. You have been hearing the patter. But beneath both — or above both, or *inside* both — there is a third sound, and it is this: the creak of growth. The slow, tectonic groan of living tissue expanding against itself, of walls swelling incrementally outward, of root-tips prying apart mineral grains one at a time with the patient violence of a seed splitting stone. It sounds, in the aggregate, like a house settling in winter. Like knuckles cracking. Like the earth remembering it is alive and flexing to prove it.

Every few minutes — though time does not track well in this place — the groaning intensifies, and the walls *shift*. You feel it: a millimeter of movement, a slight increase in pressure, a change in the air's shape. The space is not static. It is growing, slowly, in the direction of its own appetite, and you are inside that appetite, and it is not hungry for you, not yet, not specifically, but it is hungry in general, and its hunger takes up room.

## The Fauna

There are things here. Rare. Brief. You catch them in your peripheral vision: a skittering in the gaps between nodes, too fast to be insect-shaped, too slow to be anything you'd call a bug. They leave no marks. They make no sound of their own. When you look directly at them, they are already somewhere else, some crevice deeper in, some fold of root-crust that shouldn't be large enough to contain them but does. They are small. The size of thumbnails, perhaps. Perhaps smaller. Perhaps larger than they have any right to be. Their outlines suggest chitin but their movement suggests *purpose*, and those two things together in a place this strange make you uneasy in a way you cannot fully articulate.

Once — only once — you see one clearly. It is perched on a root-node, drinking from a pool. Its body is translucent. You can see the pool's green fluid inside it, moving, being *used*. It looks up at you with an eye that is not an eye but a cluster of cells that have agreed to function as one, and in that look there is something that might be recognition or might be calculation or might be nothing at all, just the mechanical attention of a nervous system responding to stimulus. It finishes drinking, and the pool is dry, and the node beneath it has gone slack and pale, and the creature is gone into a crevice that leads places you cannot follow and do not want to.

## The Remains

Someone has been here before you.

There are marks on the root-crust: shallow gouges, the kind made by a sharpened stone dragged across living tissue. The tissue has healed around them, swelling slightly at the margins, but the marks remain, and they are deliberate. They are not accidental damage. They are *notation*. Someone scratched something into the walls of this place with a hand-tool — a flake of obsidian, perhaps, or a sharpened bone — and the marks form patterns that are not language but are not random. Three parallel lines, then a gap, then a spiral, then a cluster of dots arranged in a shape that might be a handprint. The gouges are old enough to have been overgrown but not old enough to have been forgotten by the tissue that surrounds them. The walls remember being cut. The walls are still deciding whether to forgive.

No tool remains. No body. No fire-scar, no hearthstone, no pile of discarded shells from something edible. Just the marks, and the suggestion that someone stood where you are standing, in this pocket of impossible space beneath the soil, surrounded by the roar of a living architecture, and needed to leave a sign.

## The Breakdown

Here is what is wrong with this place:

The root-nodes on the far wall are growing *downward*. This is not unusual. But the root-nodes directly beneath them are growing *upward*, toward the first set, and where they almost meet — where the gap between them narrows to the width of a breath — the air shimmers, and the light bends, and for a moment you can see the space between them as it truly is, which is not empty at all but *full*, packed with something your eyes refuse to render, a density that should not exist in a place defined by its emptiness, and then the shimmer passes, and the gap is just a gap again, and the air is just air again, and the two sets of root-nodes are just growing toward each other with the blind patience of things that do not know they are impossible.

The pools in the deepest hollows do not reflect the ceiling. They reflect something else — a sky, maybe, or the memory of a sky, or the idea of a sky that this place has formed from nothing but the concept of *up*. The reflection shows clouds. It shows a horizon. It shows weather that does not exist down here, has never existed down here, but the pools insist, with the stubborn clarity of still water, that the sky is real and that it is just above, just above, just barely out of reach, and if you could dig through the root-crust and the soil and the stone above, you would find it: the sky of a world that is not the one you entered from, bright and open and utterly indifferent to the fact that it is underground.

You cannot dig through the root-crust. It is alive, and it does not want to be opened, and it is strong in the way that living things are strong — not rigidly, not immovably, but *flexibly*, with a tenacity that absorbs force and returns it gently, firmly, without malice but without compromise.

## The Warmth

The temperature is constant. Not body-warm — warmer. The warmth of a thing running a fever. The warmth of fermentation, of decomposition, of the deep chemical heat that rises from matter in the act of becoming other matter. It presses against your skin from all directions. It does not relent. It does not fluctuate. It is the warmth of a place that is metabolizing itself, slowly, patiently, turning its own mass into energy and its energy into growth and its growth into more mass, and the cycle is tight and efficient and it has been running for long enough to have saturated the air with its heat, which is not uncomfortable exactly but is *constant*, and the constancy of it is its own kind of pressure.

## What This Place Is

It is a pocket. A fold in the earth where the rules went soft and the plants took advantage. It is small enough to hold in your cupped hands and vast enough to lose yourself in, and both of these things are true at the same time, and the contradiction is not a trick — it is the place's fundamental nature, the way it *is*, the way it has always been since it cracked open young and sharp and hungry and began, immediately, to fill itself with the only thing it knew how to make: more of itself.

It is loud with living.

It is empty with space.

It is warm with purpose.

And somewhere in its walls, in the healed-over scars left by hands that used sharpened stone to mark a place they could not explain, there is a message that no one alive can read, written in a language that was never spoken, in a place that should not exist, by someone who came here, and saw what it was, and left the only mark they could: a few scratches in the skin of the living world, and the faith that someone else would come, and see, and know.

You see. You know.

The root-nodes pulse beneath your feet. The pools reflect their borrowed sky. The filaments hum their arterial hum. The creature with the not-eye watches from its crevice. The walls swell, a millimeter, two, inward, outward, breathing.

The place is warm, and young, and alive, and wrong, and it has been waiting.

For what, you do not ask. Some questions, in a place like this, are best left to the pools and their impossible reflections.
