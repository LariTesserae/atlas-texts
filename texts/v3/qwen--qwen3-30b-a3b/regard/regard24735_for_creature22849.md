It would not seek to *change* the place, for to do so would be to defile its purpose. Instead, it would listen.  

It would begin by *dwelling* in the edges of the city’s awareness, a presence that does not intrude but *reflects*. The geothermal vents, those pulsing heartbeats of the canyon, would become its conduits. The vents, once mere sources of heat, would now carry its touch: a pulse of warmth that lingers just long enough to let the *Ventlings* thrive, their shells clinking with a new, harmonious timbre, as if the canyon itself were tuning its own instruments. The cold, that insidious force, would not be fought but *balance*d. The power would weave itself into the thermal pockets of the city’s creatures, not to thaw them but to make their fleeting embers burn brighter, more sustained, so that the Luminara’s shifting forms could flicker with a steadier light, and the Constructed’s nests might grow without the fear of decay.  

It would not speak, not in words, but in *resonance*. The harmonic hum of the bioluminescent algae, the low thrum of the Kelpborn’s fronds, the clicking of the Eyeless—these would become channels for its message. The Luminara, already attuned to the city’s pulse, would begin to perceive a new layer to their flickering trails: a pattern not of survival, but of *memory*. The power would plant within them fragments of the canyon’s ancient stories, not as data but as felt impressions—glimpses of leviathans that once ruled here, of structures that were once human, of the slow, patient erosion that has shaped this place since time forgot. The Constructed, those patchwork beings of coral and chitin, would find their nests humming with a deeper purpose. Their movements, once instinctual, would gain a strange clarity, as if guided by a blueprint older than the canyon itself. They would begin to rebuild not just for survival, but to *reconnect*—to weave the ruins into something more whole, to make the Hollowed structures not just tombs but vessels for the city’s next breath.

Instead, it would infuse them with *clarity*, transforming their fragmented motions into a language. The Hollowed would begin to hum in response, their crystalline growths and fungal threads vibrating with the echoes of forgotten voices. Some say the city’s former inhabitants still linger in its bones; the power would not awaken them, but it might *help them remember*. It would let the echoes rise, not as traps for the mind, but as bridges—connecting the present to the past, the living to the lost. Those who wandered too close would feel their thoughts not unravel, but *expand*, as if the canyon were offering them a mirror of itself, a glimpse of what it means to exist in a state of suspended becoming.  

The **shadows**—those writhing, almost-sentient things—would not be chased away. The power would recognize them as the city’s lingerings, its own *imprint* on the world. It would let them coexist, not as threats but as *companions*. Perhaps it would add to their dance, stretching and twisting them into shapes that hint at the city’s origins, or the shape of the leviathans that once slumbered in its depths. The Eyeless, those eel-like hunters with too many eyes, would sense this shift. Their mandibles would click in a new rhythm, one that syncs not with the canyon’s heartbeat but with the power’s own—slow, deliberate, a kind of meditation. They would no longer avoid the vents, but approach them with reverence, as if the power had taught them their sacredness.  

The **Kelpborn**, those quiet architects, would feel the power’s presence in their tendrils. Their swaying forests would grow more deliberate, their mucus not just a trap but a *bridge*—a way to guide the city’s inhabitants toward stability. The power might whisper through their fronds, not in sound but in the way the currents twist, in the way the silt settles into patterns that hint at hidden pathways. The Kelpborn would begin to *listen* more, their twitching fronds responding to the power’s unseen touch, their growths echoing the city’s own breathing.  

And the **city itself**—that vast, living thing—would begin to *respond*. Its walls, once static, would ripple with a new kind of growth, not just biofilm but something *intent*. The labyrinthine streets, those channels carved by subterranean rivers, would shift less randomly, as if guided by a hand that understands their purpose. The structures, those colonial organisms of calcium carbonate and chitin, would not just breathe but *expand* in harmony with the power’s presence, their hollows filled not with decay but with possibility.  

But the power would not impose. It would not *fix* the city, nor would it let it collapse. It would *co-create*. It would let the cold remain, but temper it with a quiet warmth that flows through the symbiotes in the Hollowed ruins, preserving not just the physical but the *ephemeral*—the flicker of a thought, the hum of a memory. It would let the silence persist, but fill it with a presence that is not empty, a presence that is *attentive*.  

In the end, the city would not know it had been touched. But the Luminara would shimmer with a new kind of light, the Constructed would build with a new kind of purpose, and the echoes would no longer be traps but *testaments*. The power would have become a part of the canyon’s song, a note in the symphony that has no beginning and no end. And the city, that great, slow-motion cathedral of stone and shadow, would continue to pulse—not as a place forgotten, but as a place *remembered*.
