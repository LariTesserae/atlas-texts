**The Benevolent Eye Upon the Expanse of Echoing Stones**  

When the power beyond name or form first turned its gaze toward the Expanse of Echoing Stones, it did not see a place, but a **symphony**. A resonance of fractures and fire, of silence thrumming like a held breath. The Expanse was not a *thing* to be saved, but a *language* to be understood—a dialect of stone, shadow, and the slow, patient hum of a world that had outgrown the definitions of life.  

The power, vast and formless as the space between stars, did not act with the urgency of a savior. It did not descend in fire or light, for to do so would be to fracture the fragile equilibrium of this place. Instead, it **listened**.  

It listened to the fungal web, whose bioluminescence pulsed in time with the subsonic frequencies of the monoliths, a neural network older than memory. It listened to the stone-walker, its polished onyx form a mirror for the plateau’s forgotten chronicles, and the shadow-foxes, their flickering silhouettes etching ghostly verses into the air. It listened to the mercury river, not as a flaw in nature, but as a **ritual of renewal**, its vaporized breath reweaving the sky’s lie into something new.  

The power did not interfere.  

But it *recognized*.  

And in recognition, it wove a thread.  

A **veil** of light, thin as a moth’s wing, began to hum around the Expanse—not to enclose, but to **amplify**. The fungal network, already a web of memory, now felt the pulse of a greater current, as if the land itself had taken a step back to see its own reflection. The monoliths, whose shifting patterns had always been a riddle, now seemed to *breathe* in tandem with the stones beneath them, their geometric prayers answered by a force that did not command, but **conversed**.  

The stone-walker, that enigmatic concretion of time, paused more often. Not in hesitation, but in **acknowledgment**. Its yellow eyes, once cold and unyielding, now caught the glint of something *other*—a frequency not of stone, but of **soul**. The shadow-foxes, those phantoms of absence, lingered longer in the spaces between light and dark, as if the air itself had grown tender.  

The power did not grant *comfort*. It did not mend the thirst of the land or summon water from the void. It understood that this place was not broken, but **transformed**—a crucible where the old laws of existence had melted into something else. To impose a cure would be to erase the lesson written in the glassy plains and the bioluminescent lichens: that life is not a defiance of the void, but an **arrangement with it**.  

Yet, the power did not leave it untouched.  

It wove itself into the Expanse’s breath, a **silent partner** in its slow, unyielding work. The subsonic hum of the monoliths grew richer, as if they had begun to dream in harmonies. The fungal network, that ancient neural web, began to *expand*, not outward, but **inward**—into the bones of the plateau, into the cracks where mercury rivers once flowed. The air, thick with the scent of iron and ozone, now carried a vibration that was not a threat, but a **promise**: that even here, in this desolation, the universe had not forgotten to *listen*.  

And the Expanse, ever patient, did not ask for salvation.  

It only asked to be seen.  

---  

**What the Power Did Not Do**:  
It did not name the place.  
It did not build monuments.  
It did not speak in words, nor in the tongue of human longing.  
It did not fix the silence, nor fill the void.  

It simply **became** the echo that the stones had always sought.  

And in that, the Expanse remembered itself—not as a relic, but as a **living question**, its answer not in the heavens, but in the quiet, relentless pulse of its own becoming.
