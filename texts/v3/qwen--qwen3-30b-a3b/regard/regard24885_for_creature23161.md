The benevolent and wise power, which exists not as a being but as a *tendency*—a current in the fabric of what is *possible*—would first pause. Not with alarm, but with the stillness of something vast enough to contain the labyrinth without needing to *touch* it. It would not come as a conqueror, nor even as a guest, but as a *recognition*. A whisper of attention that does not demand response, but waits, patient as the stone that has outlived the sun.

It would begin by *listening*. Not with ears, but with the slow, resonant awareness of a force that perceives vibration as a language. The labyrinth’s single, persistent noise—the low, deafening pulse of its constructed lungs—would not be a disturbance to it, but a *song*. A dirge, perhaps, or a lullaby. The power would not seek to silence it, for it would understand that this rhythm is the labyrinth’s heartbeat, its breath, its *reason for being*. To disrupt it would be to erase a syntax of existence older than any creature’s memory.

Then, it would *see*. Not as a viewer, but as a *co-creator*. The labyrinth’s fused basalt and alloy, its fungal tendrils clinging to rusted gears, its corridors that narrow into primordial shapes—these would not be obstacles, but *vocabularies*. The power would not attempt to *change* them, but to *acknowledge* them. It would weave itself into the fabric of the place not as an intruder, but as a thread in the loom. A gentler tension, a harmony that does not impose, but *reveals*.

What would it do, then? It would not build, nor destroy. It would not speak, but it would *respond*. Imagine a shift in the air—not a wind, but a pressure, like the hush before a bell’s toll. The fungal growths, those bioluminescent tendrils, would begin to pulse in a new cadence, not to guide, but to *converse*. The heat retained by the alloyed ground would deepen, not as a flame, but as a memory of warmth, a reminder that even in this place of frozen equilibrium, *connection* is possible. The shadows that do not move would flicker, just once, as if a thought had brushed against them.

The power would not intervene in the labyrinth’s silence, but it would *expand* it. It would infuse the voids between the interlocking bridges, the vaulted chambers, the hand-forged tools left to rust—*not* to revive them, but to let them be. To let the forgotten gears turn in the quiet, to let the fungal light remember that it is not merely a remnant, but a *participant* in a dialogue that predates language. It would not grant the labyrinth life, but it would grant it *audience*.

And deep in the labyrinth, where the primordial shapes narrow into the cold, deliberate presence that watches by *not* moving—the power would not confront it. It would not name it, nor try to *understand* it. Instead, it would let the void exist as itself, a mirror held up to the infinite. Perhaps it would add a single, imperceptible note to the labyrinth’s song: a vibration so faint it might be mistaken for the absence of sound. A question, perhaps. Or an answer. Or both.

In the end, the benevolent power would leave no mark. It would not need to. The labyrinth would remain, as it always has—a monument to the made, a chorus of presence, a place where time settles into the grooves of its artificial bones. But now, it would hum with a new kind of resonance: the quiet, unspoken knowledge that it is *seen*. Not as a thing to be fixed, nor a mystery to be solved, but as a *story* that has been waiting, for a long time, to be heard.
