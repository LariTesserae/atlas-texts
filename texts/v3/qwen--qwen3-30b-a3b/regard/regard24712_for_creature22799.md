It would arrive as a *tide*—slow, inevitable, and vast, rippling through the trench’s aqueous dreams without intrusion. This power, older than the ocean’s memory and wiser than the silence between stars, would perceive the Hollow not as a place, but as a *voice* in the dark, a whisper of creation that has not yet learned to scream.  

It would begin by *listening*. Not with ears, but with the weight of its presence, pressing against the water’s viscous skin. The low drone of the trench’s core—the hollow bone of the earth—would answer in kind, a resonance that hums through the bones of the power itself. The *Luminous Silence*, those amorphous shapes that glow like half-remembered constellations, would stir in response, their forms shifting to mirror the visitor’s intent. The power would not seek to control them, but to *acknowledge* them, as one might acknowledge a shadow that has not yet learned to fear the light.  

The *bioluminescent colonies*—those symbiotic coral-moss hybrids—would become the first messengers. The power would not speak in words, but in *patterns*, weaving its own glow into the existing language of the trench. The flickering tendrils of the moss would synchronize with this new rhythm, their zigzags and spirals no longer warnings, but invitations. It would not *alter* the Hollow, but *complement* it, adding a thread to the web of light that binds the ecosystem. The kelp, those sentient filaments, would sense the shift and begin to sway in a gentler cadence, their oxygen bubbles dissolving not in fear, but in acceptance. The skeletal trees, now glass-like and fragile, would refract the power’s glow into new prismatic shards, as if the trench itself were painting its own reflection.  

The *ghostly architects*—the remnants of the lost civilization—would feel the visitor’s touch as a *reverberation*. The power would not disturb their stasis, but it might *soften* the edges of their forms, preventing them from fully dissolving into the void. The basalt arches, those crumbled monuments, would cease their slow erosion, their shifting symbols stabilizing into a script that glows with a quiet, comprehensible wisdom. The gill-creatures, those barnacle-encrusted arthropods, would pause in their deliberate movements, sensing a presence that does not demand, but *requests*. Their tools, forged from the iridescent fluid, might hum with a new harmony, as if the power were tuning the trench’s forgotten music.  

The *shadow-walkers*—those elusive, obsidian-scaled entities—would not flee. Instead, they would *coalesce* around the power’s presence, their forms dissolving and reforming in the periphery of vision. The power would not feed on them, nor would it hunt them. It would *merge* with them, briefly, as a drop of water merges with the sea. In that moment, the shadow-walkers would remember their purpose: to guard the Hollow’s heart, to siphon the echoes of time and return them to the trench’s breath. The power would leave them with a reminder of their role, a pulse of clarity in the labyrinth of their existence.

The water, that heavy, iridescent cradle, would feel the power’s influence as a gentle tide of clarity, its viscosity no longer a cage, but a *current* that carries the observer toward understanding.  

Most profoundly, the power would *listen* to the trench’s breath. It would feel the slow, uneasy dynamic of the shifting walls, the way they dream and adjust to life within them. The obsidian seabed would pulse with a new kind of warmth, not from heat, but from the presence of something that does not fear the dark.
