Some resemble amorphous blobs of blackened bronze, their surfaces pitted and glowing faintly, as if the metal is alive and breathing. Others are more crystalline, their bodies fracturing into prismatic shards when disturbed, only to reassemble moments later. They do not move, but they *listen*—their surfaces rippling in response to the basin’s silent hum, absorbing the mist as though it were a language.

Their movements are silent, their forms blending with the darkness until they vanish, only to reappear elsewhere, as if the basin itself is folding them into its breath. They are not predators, but they are not prey either. They exist in a state of *symbiotic detachment*, their existence tied to the valley’s unending cycle of condensation and seepage.
