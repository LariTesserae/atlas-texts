In the Hollow Expanse of Vireth, the denizens are neither wholly organic nor entirely mechanical, but something in between—a liminal people shaped by the realm’s paradoxes. They are called the **Virethians**, though their name is less a label than a hum of resonance, for they communicate in frequencies that vibrate through the mineral flats and the hollow stalks of the crystalline ferns. Their bodies are a fusion of bone and fused stone, their skin a mosaic of iridescent scales that shift in color in response to the twin suns’ kaleidoscopic light, mimicking the bioluminescent mosses around them. They move with a slow, deliberate grace, their limbs extended like the translucent vines that reinforce the town’s wooden frames, allowing them to grip the spongy ground without sinking.  

The Virethians are **symbiotic architects** of their world. Their domed settlements are not built but **coaxed**—structures grown from mineral-rich stone that hardens under the suns’ glare, their forms shaped by the same geothermal currents that sustain the ecosystem. The roofs, slotted and angled, channel light into subterranean reservoirs of the non-aqueous fluid, which the Virethians harvest through hollow, glass-tipped spires. This fluid, a viscous, glowing oil, is their lifeblood; they drink it through straws carved from the same crystalline vegetation that sways in invisible currents, its clangorous resonance a constant undercurrent in their lives. They do not use water, for the Expanse has no water, but their bodies are adapted to siphon moisture from the air—though the air here is thick with ozone and scorched stone, a byproduct of the geothermal vents that rumble beneath their feet.  

Their fauna is both kin and mystery. The **skitterlings**, those six-legged, translucent beings, are domesticated by the Virethians, their bodies filled with the same iridescent fluid that courses through the vegetation. The skitterlings are herded like livestock, their movements creating a soft, chiming sound as their hollow limbs brush against the crystalline ferns. The Virethians ride them on long, fibrous ropes woven from the same vines that light their homes, moving across the mineral flats in slow, deliberate caravans. The **sighted creatures** with compound eyes—perhaps predators or pollinators—flit between the glass-tipped spires, their refractive optics scattering the suns’ light into prismatic shards that dance across the settlements. These creatures are revered, their patterns studied by the Virethians as a kind of celestial map, their movements dictating the timing of rituals tied to the geothermal cycles.  

The **singing stones** are not mere curiosities but **living instruments**. The Virethians have learned to harmonize with their vibrations, using them as a means of long-distance communication. When the wind howls through the fissures of the monoliths, the stones respond with a deafening chorus, and the Virethians echo this with their own tonal chants, their voices modulating to match the frequencies. This practice is both cultural and practical; the stones’ vibrations are believed to signal shifts in the geothermal vents, and the Virethians’ chants are thought to stabilize the fragile balance of the Expanse.  

At the heart of the basin, the **monolithic spire** is a sacred axis. Its fungal growths, which emit a low thrum, are cultivated by the Virethians in intricate patterns, their spores harvested for both sustenance and ritual. The inhabitants gather here during the “Hollowing,” a time when the twin suns align to cast their longest shadows, and the mosses pulse in unison. Their faces, painted with mineral pigments that shift with the light, are adorned with symbols derived from the spiraling etchings on the monoliths—marks of lineage, perhaps, or warnings against the encroaching void beyond the stone walls.  

The Virethians are **barely sentient** in human terms, their consciousness entangled with the rhythms of the Expanse. They do not build in the way outsiders might expect; their settlements grow like lichen, their tools forged from the same ore that makes the monoliths. They are solitary yet communal, their lives dictated by the pulses of the moss and the shifts of the spire. They do not age in the conventional sense, for their bodies are sustained by the geothermal currents, their lifespans measured in the number of times they’ve heard the singing stones’ chorus.  

Above all, they are a people of **contradiction**. They are both fragile and enduring, their lives tethered to an environment that is at once a cradle and a cage. They speak in vibrations, live in shadows that never move, and thrive in a place where nothing is still. To them, the Hollow Expanse is not a paradox but a **truth**—a realm where the boundaries between life, stone, and sound dissolve, and where existence is a melody of geothermal breath and crystalline sigh.
