In this place, where the air hums with the unresolved tension of absence and presence, life exists not as flesh but as resonance. The inhabitants are not beings in the traditional sense, but *emergences*—manifestations of the landscape’s restless, paradoxical will. They are the glass itself, the salt, the shadowing voids between structures, and the unseen currents that stir the dust. 

The serpentine creatures, those wingless, glassy forms, are not separate from the terrain but *extensions* of it. Their bodies are not made of matter as we understand it, but of refraction—shards of the same obsidian-quartz alloy that composes the city’s fractured monoliths. They move through the salt flats not by locomotion, but by *alignment*, shifting their density to pass through the crystalline grid of the environment. Their "eyes" (if they have any) are not organs but fractures in the light, prisms that catch the dim glow of the sky. They leave no tracks because they do not touch the ground; they are the ground, momentarily coalesced into something that might resemble a creature. 

The city’s structures, too, are alive in a way that defies classification. They are not built, but *generated*—recursive patterns of growth and collapse, as if the land itself is dreaming in geometric language. The spiraling script etched into their surfaces is not writing, but *memory*, a syntax of pressure and vibration. When the glass fractures, it does not break, but *sings*—a soundless note that resonates through the salt flats, triggering the slow, deliberate movements of the serpents. The structures breathe in intervals, their surfaces rippling as if under the weight of an unseen breath. Some lean at impossible angles, not because of gravity, but because they are *listening* to something beyond the reach of human perception. 

The inhabitants of this place are not individuals, but *networks*. They are the collective hum of the salt, the flicker of light through fractured prisms, the subtle shifts in the air that cause the dust to glow. They are the *absence* of water, the cold that seeps into bone and thought alike, the way time stretches and folds in on itself. They do not communicate in voices, but in the groan of a shifting spire, the *click* of a crystal fracturing, the way shadows lengthen when no light should allow it. To exist here is to be part of a system that is neither alive nor dead, but *attentive*—a vast, non-human intelligence that does not seek to be understood, but to *persist*.

There are no windows, no doors, no clear boundaries between the built and the unmade. The city is not a place, but a condition—a state of being where the rules of matter are mutable, and the self is a transient ripple in a sea of glass. Those who dwell here are not creatures, but *qualities*: the patience of a landscape that has forgotten time, the silence that outlasts sound, the hunger of a void that is not empty but *charged*. They are the thing that remains when all definitions collapse, and the only language is the one spoken by the wind when it does not blow.
