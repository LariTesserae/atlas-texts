In Vireth-Ka, the inhabitants are not merely creatures but *conditions of existence*, entities that blur the line between life and the city’s own slow, breathing decay. They are not bound by traditional forms, but by the rhythms of a place that refuses to settle into any single truth.  

**The Shadowfins** are the first to be noticed—eel-like beings with bodies like liquid glass, their translucent skin revealing networks of bioluminescent veins that pulse in sync with the siphon-ferns’ subsonic drone. They have no eyes, but their elongated, fraying tendrils sense vibrations in the water, tracking the faintest shifts in the city’s architecture. They are scavengers, feeding on the remains of the crude metal alloys that once formed the city’s skeleton, their slime trails marking paths through the labyrinth. Some say they are not alive at all, but manifestations of the city’s memory, flickering through the water like ghosts of its former machinery.  

**The Crustacean-Golems** are the city’s slow stewards, their exoskeletons a hybrid of coral, rust, and bone-steel. They move in deliberate, groaning steps, their limbs creaking with the weight of sediment and forgotten tools. These creatures are not predators but *rebuilders*, using their pincers to chip away at the basalt spires, reshaping them into new forms that merge with the surrounding vegetation. Their carapaces are home to colonies of *glassweed*, which hum in response to their movements, creating a dissonant symphony that echoes through the submerged plateau. They are both relic and renewer, their existence a testament to the city’s refusal to be static.  

**The Ghostwings** are the only avian life here, though they are not birds in any conventional sense. Their wings are membranous filaments, delicate and translucent, woven from the same fibrous tendrils that cling to the siphon-ferns. They nest in the hollowed-out remains of the city’s towers, their nests constructed from bioluminescent moss and strands of kelp that trap moisture from the aether-spray. When they take flight, their wings generate static that causes the siphon-ferns to shudder, as if the plants were responding to some unspoken language. Their presence is fleeting, their forms dissolving into the mist whenever they approach the edges of the plateau, as though the city itself resists their existence.  

**The Siphon-Ferns** and **Glassweed** are not passive flora but active participants in the city’s ecosystem. The siphon-ferns, with their towering, translucent stalks, are symbiotic with the shadowfins, providing them with nutrients in exchange for the eels’ phosphorescent slime, which the ferns use to lure other creatures. The glassweed, in turn, is a kind of living conduit, its hollow stems channeling the faint electrical currents that hum through the water. Some say the glassweed is *listening*, its chimes a coded response to the city’s shifting foundations.  

**The Aether-Spray** itself is a living enigma. It forms at the edges of the plateau, where the water begins to unravel into mist, and it is said to be the breath of the city’s forgotten architects. The mist carries whispers—fragmented voices, half-formed commands, the echo of a language that no longer has a speaker. Those who linger too long in the spray report feeling their own thoughts rearrange, as if the city were rewriting them. It is possible that the aether-spray is home to *ephemeral entities*, shapeless and silent, that exist only as distortions in the air, feeding on the residual energy of the place.  

**The Carved Stone Tablets** are the only remains of the former inhabitants, but they are not relics. They are *active*—their symbols shifting when observed, as if rewriting themselves in real time. Some scholars believe they are a form of communication, a message from something that still lingers in the city’s bones. Others claim they are traps, designed to confuse or ensnare those who try to decipher them. The tablets are often found near the spiraling holes in buildings, which may be entrances to hidden spaces where the city’s *unseen* residents dwell. These holes are not voids but *thresholds*, their interiors filled with water and the flicker of anglerfish-like lights that seem to watch from the darkness.  

The true inhabitants of Vireth-Ka, however, are the city itself. It is a place that *feels*, that *listens*, that *decays and grows* in tandem with its own ruins. The basalt spires, though jagged, are not inert—they throb faintly, as if the earth beneath them is still dreaming of the landmass they once were. The terraces, encrusted with coral and calcified shell, are slowly folding into the seabed, their surfaces alive with the slow creep of bioluminescent moss. The city’s infrastructure is not abandoned but *unstable*, its scaffolds of bamboo and rope twisting into new configurations each night, as if the place is trying to remember how to be a city.  

And then there are the *whispers*. Not from the aether-spray, but from the water itself. Some say they are the voices of the drowned, others the murmurs of the sea’s own mind. But in Vireth-Ka, the line between the living and the liquid is thin. The city does not merely contain life—it *is* life, in a form that defies classification. Its inhabitants are not beings but *processes*: the slow dissolution of metal into rust, the symbiotic dance of coral and bone-steel, the quiet hunger of the shadowfins. To live here is to be part of a cycle that no one can fully grasp, a place where the boundary between architecture and organism is not a line but a *current*, flowing and shifting like the water that holds it all together.  

Vireth-Ka is not a city to be inhabited. It is a city to be *with*, a place where existence is a question of how deeply you are willing to sink into the unknown.
