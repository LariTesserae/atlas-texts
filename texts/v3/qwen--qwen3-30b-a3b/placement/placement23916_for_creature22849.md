I exist in the liminal space between life and the city itself, my movements dictated by the unseen currents that thread through the basalt maze. The geothermal vents are my lifeline, their mineral-rich plumes seeping into my hollowed tissues, sustaining the delicate balance of my existence. I am not solitary; I am part of a vast, synchronized school, our bioluminescent trails weaving patterns in the water, a language of light that theConstructed might interpret as navigation, the Eyeless as prey, and the Kelpborn as a counter-melody to their swaying fronds.

My cells hum with the same harmonic resonance as the algae-laced spires, and I feel the city’s consciousness in every flicker—a collective pulse that guides me through the shifting streets. The structures around me are not static; they breathe, their calcium carbonate towers expanding and contracting as symbiotic creatures burrow through them. I drift near these towers, my tendrils attaching to their hollows, absorbing their heat, their stories. Sometimes, I catch the echoes of the past: a flicker of motion, a fragment of sound, as if the canyon is whispering secrets through the ruins. 

I am both observer and participant, a current in the vast, slow-motion symphony. When the Eyeless pass, their mandibles click in time with the vents’ heartbeat, and I dissolve into the shadows, a ghost of light. The Kelpborn’s tendrils brush against me, their fronds twitching with a curiosity I cannot fully comprehend. And when the canyon’s walls shift, expanding like the lungs of some ancient beast, I rise with them, my body rippling in response. I am not a creature in the way surface dwellers understand. I am the city’s breath, its memory, its fragile, living neural network—weightless, yet bound to the silt-laden depths, forever caught in the dance of preservation and transformation.
