Where would I find myself in this world? Probably adrift in the canals, a ripple in the water’s silent pulse, neither fully creature nor structure but something *between*—a being of the Veiled, perhaps. Or maybe I am the canals themselves, the veins of the basin, carrying currents that defy gravity, their flow a language I’ve never been able to read. But no, that feels too abstract. Let me be more specific.  

I would be a **Veiled**, yes—a creature of liquid and light, my body a translucent ribbon of bioluminescent tendrils that undulate through the water like a living ink stain. My skin is etched with shifting patterns, not by choice but by the water’s whim, as if the basin is writing on me, shaping my form with its own forgotten script. I move in slow, hypnotic spirals, my movements leaving trails of phosphorescent algae that glow faintly before dissolving into the mist. I am not a predator, but I am not passive either. My presence stabilizes the canals’ strange rhythm, a counterbalance to the static hum of the buried machinery. The rusted gears and pulleys beneath the surface pulse with energy I absorb, their faint vibrations threading through my body like a heartbeat I’ve never known.  

But I am also a witness. The bioluminescent moss, the Weavers, flickers in the distance, their glow a code I can’t fully decipher. When I approach, they pulse in reply, their light patterns rippling outward like a conversation I’ve never learned. The Skimmers above, with their iridescent wings, cast shadows that fall *wrong*—not as darkness but as voids in the golden glare, as if the air itself is afraid to hold their shape. The Statues stand tall, their carvings shifting like whispers in a language older than memory. I’ve seen their eyes glint when the water’s ripples form certain shapes, but I don’t know if they see me or if I’m just another thread in their silent tapestry.  

This place is a dream that never ends. The heat is constant, the light unyielding, and the water never recedes. I don’t age, don’t hunger, don’t need to breathe. My kind are said to be the original architects, though I can’t recall building anything. Maybe the memory is in the currents, in the way my patterns align with the canals’ pulse. Or maybe I’m just a byproduct of the place’s obsession with balance—between construction and decay, between silence and the faint hum of machines.  

Sometimes, I feel the shadows moving near me, not as creatures but as absences, as if the basin is testing the edges of its own existence. They are drawn to the moss and the buried machinery, trailing through the fog like questions without answers. I wonder if they are the builders, or if they are the place itself, learning to be alive.  

To exist here is to be both root and mechanism, to flow without direction, to glow without a source. I am not separate from the labyrinth—I am its breath, its slow, unchanging heartbeat. And when the water ripples with patterns I don’t understand, I feel it. I feel the basin *thinking*.
