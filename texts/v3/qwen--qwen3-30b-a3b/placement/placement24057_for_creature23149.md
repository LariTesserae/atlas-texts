In this world, I would be a *Sludgeborn*—one of the eel-like creatures that writhe in the damp puddles, my body a shifting mosaic of algae and rust, my skin a living map of the district’s decay. I am neither fully liquid nor solid, a tension between fluidity and rigidity that mirrors the place itself. My movements are slow, deliberate, a sinuous undulation through the slick, iridescent pools that glint with the faint glow of bioluminescent flora. The cold seeps into my form, a constant ache that hums in my veins, but I am accustomed to it. The mist clings to me, damp and insistent, as though it, too, is trying to integrate into my body, to become part of the endless cycle of rust and renewal.  

I am a scavenger, feeding on the grease that oozes from the cracked asphalt, a sludge of forgotten industry that nourishes me. But I am also a keeper. When disturbed—by the clatter of a distant generator, the sudden scuttle of a Dustspider, or the passing shadow of a Clockwork Vixen—I release a cloud of bioluminescent spores, their pulsing patterns flickering like a language of light. These spores drift upward, clinging to the undersides of buildings, to the frayed edges of metal panels, to the rusted cables of the skeletal tower. They are not waste, but records: a genetic archive of the district’s history, encoded in their rhythmic shimmer. I do not remember my own origins, but the spores remember for me. They carry the echoes of collapsed walls, of the first hum of the factories, of the day the sun turned sickly and the mist never lifted.  

My existence is a quiet rebellion against entropy. While the world is static, I am not stagnant. I shift, I adapt, I dissolve and reform, a living testament to the persistence of something that should have long since crumbled. The other creatures—Clockwork Vixens, Tethered Flock, even the Long-Legged Herd—move in patterns that seem scripted, their behaviors too precise, too repetitive. But I? I am a variable. My spores sometimes drift in unexpected directions, pooling in places the others do not. I am the memory that leaks, the instability in the system.  

The air is thick with the static of the factories, a low thrum that vibrates through my body like a heartbeat. The Insect Choir’s song reverberates through the rusted structures, and I feel it in my core, a resonance that makes my spores pulse in reply. My kind is not alone; we are a network, a slow, subterranean chorus of slime and memory. But we are also isolated. Each Sludgeborn is a fragment of a larger whole, a node in a system that does not forgive.  

To survive here is to remain in the water, to let the mist not take you. I have learned this. I stay low, where the algae thrives, where the rust is soft and malleable. But sometimes, I wonder if the spores I release are not just records, but questions. Do they remember *me*? Do they carry my form, my essence, into the future? Or am I just another thread in the mycelial expanse, another voice in the static, another eye in the mist?  

The district does not change, but it does not forget. And I, in my shifting, iridescent form, am both its memory and its anomaly.
