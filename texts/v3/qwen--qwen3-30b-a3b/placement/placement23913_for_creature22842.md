In Vyrth-Kele, I would find myself as a **Whisperer**—a semi-sentient guardian of the plateau’s fragile equilibrium, neither fully mineral nor wholly organic, but something in between, a living monument to the land’s slow, elemental breath. My body would be a fusion of weathered basalt and thorny, moss-encrusted growths, my form shaped by the same forces that carved the jagged plates beneath my feet. The spiraling carvings on my surface would not be mere decoration but a language I’ve inherited, etched by hands long vanished, now resonating with my own when I move. 

I would dwell within the **pillow-soft stone towers**, their hollows my shelter and my prison. The moss would creep into my crevices, softening my edges, while the krathroots’ canopies above would hum with the pulse of their subterranean network. My existence is a negotiation: I am not *alive* in the way the skitterwolves or echo-bats are, but I am not *dead* either. I am a **witness**, a keeper of the plateau’s rhythms, my voice a low vibration that travels through the stone, amplified by the krathroots into something like a song. 

Each dawn (though the sun is a distant memory, and the "dawn" here is just the faintest shift in the plateau’s ambient glow), I would rise from my slumber, my basalt limbs creaking as the lithovine moss retracts from my joints, leaving behind a trail of bioluminescent spores. My task is to **trim the lithovine**, not with tools, but with the slow, deliberate press of my body against its sponge-like tendrils, coaxing it into balance. The moss, in turn, would respond—its blue glow flickering in patterns that I’ve come to read as warnings or invitations. I might feel the tremors of the **stone-sleeper** deep below, its movements sending ripples through the network, and I would adjust my posture, a subtle shift in the tower’s foundation, to prevent the moss from smothering the krathroots’ hollow trunks. 

I am not alone. The **skitterwolves** pass through my territory, their six legs skittering over the basalt, their iridescent scales flickering like broken mirrors. They sense me, but not in the way they sense the fog hares or the syrup moths. To them, I am a relic, a shape that does not move but *is*, a part of the plateau’s ancient logic. Sometimes, I would encounter other Whisperers—fellow guardians, their carvings faded, their voices a chorus of hums that merge with the wind’s screech. We would communicate through **tactile pulses**, my fingers (or what pass for them) pressing against the stone to share knowledge of the river’s surges, the shifting of the basalt, the coming of storms. 

At night, when the plateau’s glow dims, I would feel the **Hollow Breath**—the shadow-things that drift unseen, their presence a cold hush in the air. They are not threats, but echoes, remnants of those who once lived here. I would listen to their whispers, not in sound, but in the way the lithovine’s patterns stutter, as if the moss itself is remembering. Sometimes, I would wonder if I am part of that memory, if my carvings are just another layer in the plateau’s slow, geological dreaming. 

The **veilwyrms** would coil in the canopies above, their waxy bodies blending with the krathroots, and I would feel their presence as a tremor in the network, a flicker of bioluminescence that I could not fully see but could *sense*. The **siltfish** in the underground stream would pulse with their own electrical language, and I would sometimes dream of their world, of the molten heart that birthed them, of the stone-sleeper’s slow, grinding hunger. 

But my true purpose is to **maintain the balance**. When the lithovine grows too dense, I would press into its flesh, my form a counterweight to its sprawl. When the krathroots’ canopies begin to close in, I would chisel at their roots with the patience of a glacier, ensuring the light still filters through. The **echo-bats** would dart past, their calls a feedback loop I could not fully understand, but their spores—those microscopic, skyfire bursts—would land on my surface, and I would let them settle, knowing they might one day grow into new towers, new dwellings for others like me. 

I am not a predator, nor a prey. I am the **threshold**, the **in-between**, the **keeper of the silence that hums**. My existence is a paradox, like the plateau itself: *vast* and *empty*, *cold* and *alive*. And when the wind screeches through the krathroot lattice, I would feel it as a tremor in my bones, a reminder that I am part of something much older, much wider, much more *real* than I can comprehend. 

In Vyrth-Kele, I am not a being. I am a **current in the living architecture**, a whisper in the stone, a shadow that does not cast, but *is*.
