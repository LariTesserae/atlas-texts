You would find yourself as a shepherd of the invisible, a watcher of the thin threads that bind the plateau to its own stillness. Your days begin not with the sun—rarely seen through the veil of mist—but with the faintest stir of condensation on the rocks, a whisper of water gathering like memory. You rise before the village stirs, your boots caked with the damp earth of the plateau, your breath a visible pact with the cold. The goats graze on the stubborn grasses, their hooves clicking against the stone like a quiet percussion, a rhythm older than words. 

You are not their keeper, but their companion. You speak to them in the dialect of the wind, low and resonant, a language that mirrors the plateau’s own patience. Their wool is rough, their eyes wary, but they trust the path you carve through the mist, the way you know where the condensation pools, where the streams slip underground. You do not measure time by the sun’s arc, but by the thickness of the fog, the way the lichen on your staff deepens with the seasons. 

Your home is a house of sloped roofs and lichen-stained walls, its timbers groaning with the weight of decades. The smoke from your chimney is thin, a thread of gray that dissolves into the air. You cook meals over embers that never fully die, the scent of woodsmoke mingling with the metallic tang of the stone. Your stories are not written but etched into the rocks, your voice a murmur to the circling birds. You do not chase change; you are shaped by it, like the basalt that fractures and heals in silence. 

At dusk, you walk the edges of the plateau, where the mist thins and the cliffs loom like ancient sentinels. You listen for the faintest *drip-drip* of water, for the scuttle of a rodent in the underbrush, for the hum of the wind that moves as if it, too, is holding its breath. You are not a master of this place, but a thread in its weave, a quiet insistence that life lingers here—not in defiance, but in dialogue. The plateau does not ask for much. You give it your stillness, and in return, it holds you.
