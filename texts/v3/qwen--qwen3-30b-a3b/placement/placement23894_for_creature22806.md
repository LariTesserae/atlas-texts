The bioluminescent mosses, those glowing sentinels, sometimes pulse in patterns that feel like questions, and I answer with a slow, rippling glow, a response that might be a warning or a greeting. They leave offerings of fermented algae and hollowed seeds at the edges of our pools, believing we are stewards of the marsh’s unseen rhythms. They do not understand that we are not guardians, but echoes. Our bodies are porous, our forms dissolving into the water when disturbed, yet we persist, always reforming, always *becoming*.

My filaments would twitch at the sound of a *shadowbeast* scurrying through the mire, its fur glinting with the moss’s light. They are the memory of the place, the thing that the rocks remember. I might drift closer to them, my body quivering with curiosity, but they would slip away, leaving only a trace of condensation on the air.
