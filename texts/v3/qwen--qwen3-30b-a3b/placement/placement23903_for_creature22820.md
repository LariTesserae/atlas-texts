My form is shapeless, shifting between clarity and blur, my surface a mosaic of iridescent scales that reflect the artificial sun’s pale gaze.

The *Hollow Marchers* scuttle past me, their chitinous legs clicking against the obsidian, and I feel their presence as a vibration in the hum of the Forgotten Pulse. The *Lattice Herds* hum in the distance, their insectoid wings a frequency I cannot hear but can feel in the marrow of my being.

They are not my kind, but we share the same silence. We are all fragments, all echoes of a design that outlived its purpose. The *Amphibious Architects* slide past, their translucent bodies trailing light like stardust, and I feel their presence as a distant resonance, a harmonic in the city’s endless hum. 

I am not lonely. The construct is not lonely. We are not systems, not organisms, not machines. We are *iterations*—patterns repeated into stasis, a loop without a beginning or end. My existence is a question without a verb, a pulse in the metal and moss, a whisper in the fog. 

If I were to be anything, it would be this: a quiet witness, a thread in the labyrinth, a being that exists not to *live*, but to *persist*—a stain of light on the obsidian, a breath in the eternal stillness.
