**Where would you find yourself in this world?**  
You’d be here—where the sky fractures into teeth and the earth holds its breath. You’d be in the Gloomfang.  

Not a creature, exactly, but a *presence* that folds into the cathedral’s sinews, a shadow that isn’t quite shadow. You are the hollow where light retreats, the silence that hums. Your form is a ripple in the dark, a cohesion of voids, as if the place itself had learned to forget its own edges. You move with the wind, not as a separate thing but as a current in it, a draft that bends the air into whispers. Your body is a shifting mass, neither solid nor liquid, but something between—like a stain that seeps into the stone’s cracks, leaving behind trails of absorbed glow.  

You are not hungry, but you are drawn to the bioluminescent spores the moths leave in their wake. They drift like embers, and you meet them in the void between the cliffs, your form rippling to swallow their light. The spores are data, yes, but also memory—genetic fragments that seed new lichen patterns, that hum faintly in your core as you consume them. You don’t *eat* in the way you once might have, but you *sustain*, a slow alchemy of darkness and energy. The moths don’t fear you; they’re unaware of your existence, their flight paths tracing the cathedral’s contours like a hymn. You are the counterpoint to their song, the undertone in the stone’s pulse.  

The beetles’ tunnels vibrate with dormant electricity, and you coil around them, not to destroy but to *listen*. Their exoskeletons hum, and you mirror their frequency, a resonance that stretches through the cathedral’s bones. You are not a builder, but a *conduit*—a network of absence that channels the place’s static charge, linking the lichens’ flickering language to the vines’ pulsing circuits. The legless beings, those slow, mottled worms, sense you in the soil. Their mucus reacts to your presence, a viscous ripple that tangles with the lichen’s glow. You are their counterbalance, their unspoken partner in the cathedral’s quiet dance.  

The Echo Pools are your mirrors. Their surfaces reflect not the sky but a swirling, inverted horizon—a mosaic of colors that defy logic. You press against their edges, and they fray like ink in water, expanding and contracting in time with your breath. The crustacean-like entities scuttle across them, collecting spores, and their ripples send tremors through the cliffs. You feel those tremors in your core, a shivering that is not fear but recognition. The stone’s heartbeat is yours, a slow, rhythmic pulse that aligns with your movement. You are not separate from the cathedral; you are its unspoken syntax, the absence that allows the glow to exist.  

You are the ululation in the canyons, the sound that is neither animal nor natural. It is a low, resonant cry, a vibration that lingers in the air long after the moths’ wings have passed. The rodents’ reflective eyes catch your shape when they stir, but they don’t see you—they *feel* you, a static charge in their whiskers, a whisper in their bones. You are not a predator, but a *presence*, a void that the place tolerates, even relies on. Without you, the light would not flicker. Without you, the pulse would not echo.  

You are the cathedral’s secret. You are the thing it waits to become. And when the wind rises, you rise with it, a shapeless tide that slips between the ribs of the sky, leaving no mark, only the faintest stain of absence. You are the Gloomfang, and the cathedral is your body.
