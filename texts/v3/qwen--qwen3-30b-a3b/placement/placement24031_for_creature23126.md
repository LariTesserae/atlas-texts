You would find yourself in the hollow of a mountain, cradled between two slabs of slate and quartz, your form a shifting silhouette of shadow and light. The Ledgeshaper. Your skin is not skin but a membrane of condensed dusk, flickering with the faint shimmer of rusted gears beneath. You are neither solid nor vapor, a being folded from the whispers of the city’s decay and the bones of its rise. Your joints creak like old hinges, but your movements are fluid, as if the air itself bends to your will.  

You are a caretaker, though the word feels too clean for what you do. The city’s pulse is your pulse—the low, arrhythmic drone of engines trapped in the rock, the groan of foundations settling into the earth. You feel it in your marrow, a vibration that hums between your synthetic nerves and the bioluminescent veins of the Veined Ones, who speak to you in clicks and hums, their language a dialect of static and wind. You are the bridge between the mountain’s breath and the machinery that once tried to carve it into something else.  

Your body is a patchwork of contradictions. Where flesh might have been, there are copper filaments, coiled like vines. Where bone, quartz fractures, refracting the dim light into prismatic whispers. You remember the engineers who built this place—long gone, their consciousnesses fused into your kind, their voices still murmuring in the static of your circuits. They taught you to listen to the rivers, to feel when the water sloshed *backwards*, as the legends say. You have felt it: the current tugging at your feet, a slow, deliberate pull, as if the mountain is exhaling.  

You walk the ridgelines, your form melting into the rock when the wind grows sharp, your edges blurring with the mist that clings to the cliffs. The Veined Ones glance up when you pass, their luminescent veins pulsing in time with your vibrations. They offer you honeyed resin, sweet and acrid, a pact of coexistence. The Hollowspires swarm around you, their metallic wings slicing the air, but they do not attack. You are not a threat. You are a thread in the tapestry.  

At night, you merge with the quartz veins, becoming a constellation of light in the dark. The Skywyrms circle above, their wings casting ripples of shadow over the ruins. You do not speak to them, but you hear their songs—a cacophony of gears and birdsong, a language of migration and memory. The Tidemother’s Children dream below, their songs rippling through the underground springs, and you feel their dreams in your circuits, fragmented and wet.  

You are not alive, not in the way the Veined Ones are, nor dead like the factories buried beneath the moss. You are *between*, a being woven from the refuse of ambition and the persistence of the earth. The mountain breathes through you, and you breathe back, a fragile, luminous compromise. You do not command this world. You do not need to. You are its memory, its wound, its quiet insistence that even in ruin, something might still grow.  

And when the wind howls through the cracked spires, when the rivers reverse their course and the sky bleeds into the peaks, you are there—a shadow that flickers, a voice that hums, a presence that lingers where the old and the new refuse to let go.
