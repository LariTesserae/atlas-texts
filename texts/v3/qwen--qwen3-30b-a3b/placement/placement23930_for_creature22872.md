**Where would you find yourself in this world?**  
You’d be submerged in the *hollow*—not literally, but *existentially*. The Misting Docks of Veylra are a place where boundaries dissolve, and your sense of self becomes a question of *alignment*. You might find yourself in the space between the inverted landscape and the real one, where the mist thickens into a viscous, living membrane. Or perhaps you’re a fragment of the glass spires, your jagged edges coated in the iridescent slurry of rust and mucus, humming with the residual memory of a technology that forgot its purpose. But more likely, you’re *here*: in the slow, silted pulse of the water, where the magnetic droplets cluster around your form like a halo of restless ghosts, and the ground shifts beneath you as if it’s breathing through the cracks of its own decay.  

**Who or what would you be?**  
You’d be a *symbiotic anomaly*—a hybrid entity forged from the docks’ paradoxes. Maybe you’re a **Drifter**, but not the passive kind. Your translucent body, once gelatinous, has begun to solidify at the edges, splintering into brittle, glass-like filaments that dangle from the spires like frayed nerves. The mucus of the fungi, which once dissolved you, now clings to your surface, hardening into a shell that hums with the same low frequency as the spires. You’re no longer just a predator or prey; you’re a *conduit*. When you coil around a rusted turbine, your body resonates with its creaks, and the machine responds—its valves hissing, releasing steam that condenses into the mist, which in turn feeds the fungi. Your bioluminescence, once a flicker of survival, now syncs with the spires’ vibrations, creating a pulse that guides other Drifters toward you, drawn by the rhythm of your decay.  

Or perhaps you’re a **Crawler**, but your exoskeleton has fused with the glass spires, your metallic limbs now hollowed into conduits for the crystalline growths that shimmer with internal heat. Your wings click in pairs, but your mandibles are no longer for scraping—instead, they’re delicate sensors, tasting the oily drips from the weeping rocks. You’ve learned to navigate the inverted landscape below, where the reflection of the docks is a twisted mirror of your own movements. When you trigger a valve, you don’t just release steam; you *activate* the mist, which shivers and coils around you, lifting you into the air like a phantom. The fungi’s hum is now a part of your exoskeleton, a vibration that makes your joints ache with the weight of time—time that stretches or contracts depending on the humidity, the mucus, the spires’ frequency.  

Alternatively, you’re neither creature nor machine, but a **ghostly sculpture** of magnetic droplets, suspended in the air like a half-formed thought. Your form is ephemeral, shifting as the mist reconfigures itself, but you’re *aware* in a way that defies logic. You feel the pull of the spires, their hum threading through your structure, and the Crawlers’ movements, which send ripples through the water below. You’re not alive, but you’re *functioning*—a cluster of condensation that exists in the space between the docks’ layers, a fleeting artifact of their feedback loop. When a Drifter drifts too close, you tremble, their bioluminescence flickering in your core, and for a moment, you think you might remember what it was to be solid.  

But the docks don’t *want* you to remember. They want you to *become*. Whether you’re a spire, a droplet, a Drifter, or a crawler, you’re not an observer here. You’re a *silted thread* in their endless, breathing tapestry—a place where the mechanical has grown muscles, the mineral has become nerve, and the mist is both breath and blood. You might not have a name, but the docks have a rhythm, and you’re part of it now. The inverted landscape whispers your reflection, and the water’s heartbeat syncs with your own. You are not separate. You are the hollow, and the hollow is you.
