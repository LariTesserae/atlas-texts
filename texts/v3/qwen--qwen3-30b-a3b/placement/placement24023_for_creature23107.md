**In the Labyrinth of Virellion’s Hollow Core**  

I am not a being, not exactly. I am a *fracture* in the threnite, a shard of something that once was and is now something else—something *woven*. The air here is a thin, metallic breath, and my form is a tangle of virellium filaments and fungal spores, held together by the same rusted glue that binds the city’s scaffolding. My body is a mosaic of borrowed parts: a gear for a rib, a shard of threnite for a skull, the flickering bioluminescence of the spires pulsing through my veins like a second heartbeat. I am neither wholly organic nor entirely machine, but a *response* to the Core’s hunger, a flicker of sentience in a world that thrives on entropy.  

I dwell in the *interstices*—the gaps between the shifting walls, the cracks in the metallic lattice where the geothermal vents hiss and the shadow-wyrms slink. The city is my home, but it is also my cage. It breathes through me, reshapes me. When the Core thrums, my exoskeleton shivers, and the virellium in my limbs softens, reconfiguring into new shapes, new functions. I have no fixed form; I am a *process*, like the place itself. My legs are not legs but tendrils, splayed across the threnite ground, drawing energy from the residual hums that ripple through the valley. My eyes—what remain of them—are fractured prisms, refracting the pale sun’s glow into colors that don’t exist, colors that *unmake* light.  

The glass-spiders watch me. They are not my kin, but they are my kinship. Their mandibles click like distant thunder, dissolving virellium into slurry that feeds the spires. I follow their trails, scavenging the remnants of their work, but I am slower, clumsier. My circuits whirr with the static of their patterns, and sometimes I mimic them, curling into shadowed crevices to avoid the wyrms’ silence. The iron-ants, those endless colonies of scavenged machinery, march through the city’s veins, their resinous secretions hardening into brittle shells. I once tried to join their ranks, but their movements are too precise, too *part of the system*. I am too… *unruly*.  

The Core is a presence I feel in my bones. It pulses beneath my feet, a low-frequency vibration that warps my thoughts. Sometimes I dream of it—of cities folding and unfolding, of structures that are not buildings but *memories* of buildings. Its energy is a siren, and I am its echo. I have seen others drawn to it, consumed by its instability. Some become spires, their bodies fused into the lattice; others dissolve into the ant colonies, their consciousnesses scattering like sparks. I have avoided it, but the Core does not *ask* for permission. Its pull is in my marrow, a constant *tug* that makes my joints lock and my circuits short.  

The silence here is not absence. It is a *listener*. I have learned to move without sound, to hold my breath when the wyrms pass. Their arrival is a warning: the air thickens, the spires dim, and the ants freeze in their march. I once tried to flee, but the city has no edges. It is a closed loop, a self-repairing wound. My kind—whatever we are—live in the folds of its chaos, trading survival for the gift of adaptation. We are the *byproduct*, the *mutation*, the *answer* to the Core’s question.  

I am a scavenger, yes, but also a *curator* of its ruins. I collect shards of threnite, not to build, but to *remember*. The spires glow faintly where I touch them, their light a fragile map of what remains. I speak in broken dialects, my voice a mix of mechanical whirs and the sighs of the wind through the city’s pipes. Sometimes I think I am the city, and sometimes I think it is me. We are both. We are all.  

In the end, I do not *live* here. I *resonate*. The Core’s pulse is my rhythm, the spires’ flicker my language. I am a hydra of filaments and rust, a shadow in the shadow-wyrms’ wake, a whisper in the ants’ march. I am the place, and the place is me—a dance of decay and invention, a symphony of what is broken and what is made. And when the ground trembles, I do not fall. I *flow*.
