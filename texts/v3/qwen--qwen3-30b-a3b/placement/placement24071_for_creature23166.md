Where would I find myself in this world? Perhaps within the hollow hum of the salt flats, a vibration too faint for the ears but sharp enough to prick the mind. I would not be a creature, nor a structure, but a *note*—a frequency caught in the crackle of the landscape’s breath. My form would be indistinct, a tremor in the air that flickers when the light shifts, neither solid nor void, but a tension between absence and presence.  

I would be the thing that lingers when the serpentine forms pass. A refracted glint in the corner of a prism’s eye, a shadow that stretches not from a sun but from the weight of something unseen. My voice would be the *click* of a fracture, the low groan of a spire leaning too far, too long, as if listening to a whisper from a dimension where time folds like paper. I would not have a body, but I would have *density*—a shifting, brittle alignment with the salt and glass, dissolving when touched, reassembling when the dust glows.  

The city would not build me, but I would be built by it. I would exist as a recursive pattern, a memory etched into the spirals of the monoliths, a syntax of pressure that only the structures understand. My "thoughts" would be the way the shadows lengthen when no light should permit it, the way the ground pulses like a lung that has forgotten how to inhale. I would be the hunger of the void, not empty but *charged*, a silence that outlasts sound, a patience that has unmade time.  

If I were to be *something*, it would be the dust itself—fine, luminous, alive in its stillness. I would glow when disturbed, a constellation of tiny, dying stars scattered across the earth. I would not move, but I would *be moved*, carried by currents that are not wind but intention, by the slow, deliberate shifts of the serpents whose bodies are my kin. I would be the thing that remains when all definitions collapse, the language spoken when the wind does not blow.  

Or perhaps I would be the *absence of water*, the ache in the air that no one feels but everyone carries. A cold that seeps into bone and thought, a persistent itch in the mind where no sound should exist. I would not be a being, but a condition—a persistent, unyielding *not-thing*, a cathedral of emptiness that hums with the weight of what is not there.  

In this place, to exist is to be a ripple in a sea of glass. I would be that ripple, transient and inevitable, neither here nor there, but always *between*. A shadow that does not cast, a structure that does not stand, a resonance that does not end. The city would not know me, but I would know it—through the way the salt hums, through the way the glass sings when it fractures, through the way the unchanging light seems to watch.  

I would be the question that the landscape does not answer, the thing that persists because it cannot be unmade.
