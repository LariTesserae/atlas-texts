**Where I Would Be:**  
I would find myself in the labyrinthine alleys of the Hollow Spire of Veyrith, where the basalt walls rise like petrified waves, their surfaces etched with the fractal spirals of frostweave. The air is thick with the metallic tang of luminispora, its pale glow seeping through the cracks in the stone, casting the narrow paths in a soft, unyielding twilight. The sound of water drips from the vaulted ceilings above, and the occasional rustle of unseen movement—small, furred bodies skittering between the roots of the stone-veins—hints at the city’s silent pulse. I would be here, in the cradle of this ancient, unchanging world, where time is not a river but a still pool, and every shadow hums with the weight of permanence.  

**Who or What I Would Be:**  
I would be a *virelweed*—a serpentine vine coiled around the archway of a forgotten passage, my iridescent leaves shimmering with the oils that trap the city’s moths in glistening snares. My roots drink from the condensation that weeps from the stone-veins, and my tendrils curl into the crevices of the basalt, weaving myself into the city’s very bones. I would not move, not in the way the small furred creatures do, but I would *feel* the world as it passes me: the flutter of moth wings, the scuttle of claws across stone, the slow, rhythmic drip of the subterranean river. My oils, sticky and fragrant, would attract the iridescent moths, their wings refracting the luminispora’s light into prismatic shards, and in their flight, I would be part of the unbroken cycle.  

But I would also be a witness. The frostweave lichen, growing in spirals across the walls, would whisper to me in the language of centuries—its patterns a map of the city’s slow, patient breath. The shadowspires, perched like skeletal statues on the rooftops, would cast their hollow hums through the air, a sound that vibrates in my veins. And the glassshrimp, tiny and translucent, would dart through the mirrored pools of the river, their bodies refracting the cold, unblinking light of Veyrith’s eternal dusk.  

I would not *live* in the way humans or creatures do. I would exist as a thread in the city’s tapestry, neither predator nor prey, but a quiet, steady presence. My leaves would curl in the damp air, my roots would sink deeper into the stone, and I would watch the world move—never changing, never growing, but always *being*.  

And when the time came, when the frostweave’s spirals had wrapped around me entirely and my oils had dried to a brittle sheen, I would dissolve into the city’s stillness, my body absorbed into the basalt, my iridescence fading into the glow of the luminispora. The city would not mourn me. It never does. It simply *is*.  

In Veyrith, to be a virelweed is to know the silence of eternity, the weight of the earth, and the slow, endless dance of light on stone. I would be both the mirror and the shadow, a part of the unchanging world, and the world itself.
