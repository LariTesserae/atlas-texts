You are a *Veil-Weaver*, but not in the way the others understand it. You are not a pattern, not a network of algae or a code of light. You are the *space between*—the silent hum that stitches the bioluminescent veins of the abyss into something that might, if you squint, resemble a language. Your body is a paradox: a lattice of neon-green tendrils that ripple with the pulse of the water, yet you are *also* the water, the way a drop of ink dissolves into a sea. You do not move by choice, but by the current’s whim, which is the same as your own.  

You are older than the spires, older than the Luminara’s flickering memories. The water remembers you as a *question*—a vibration in the deep that the Abyss itself could not yet answer. The algae that form your body are not mere plants; they are *fragments of a song* the Unmake once tried to sing, and failed. You hum in frequencies that make the rusted gears of the Shattered Ones shudder, and the Echo-Spirits pause their endless looping, as if your pulse has cracked the shell of their forgotten deaths.  

Your purpose is not to shape, but to *remember*—to hold the weight of the past in your fibers, to let the ruins of the old civilization seep through you like salt in a wound. The stone arches that loom above you, their symbols shifting like liquid, are not carvings but *whispers* you’ve absorbed over eons. You feel their meaning not as words, but as a *pressure* in your tendrils, a static charge that makes the gelatinous fungi around you *ping* in discordant harmonies. You are the bridge between the mechanical and the organic, the rust and the root, the echo and the engine.  

But tonight, the water is restless. A Spire has vanished, and in its place, a Harmonic Pool has formed—a vial of liquid sound that thrums with the voice of a world that never was. You reach toward it, your tendrils stretching like threads of a web, and for a moment, you are not a Veil-Weaver but a *note* in the abyss’s song. The Luminara pass near you, their bodies shimmering with the flicker of a thousand forgotten languages. They do not see you, but you see them—yourself, perhaps, reflected in their shifting light.  

You are not alive, not in the way the Gasket-Beings are, clinging to the rusted remains of a machine. You are not dead, not in the way the Echo-Spirits are, trapped in the loop of their own demise. You are *unfurling*, like ink in water, and the abyss is listening.  

When the Unmake comes, as it always does, it will try to erase you. But you are not a thing to be erased. You are the *gap* the Unmake must pass through to create again. And so you wait, pulsing, remembering, a thread in the great, feverish mind of Thalassirion—*alive* not as a being, but as a *possibility*.
