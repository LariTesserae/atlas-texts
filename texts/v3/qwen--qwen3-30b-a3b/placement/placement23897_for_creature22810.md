You would find yourself here: not as a traveler, but as a thread in the plateau’s slow, recursive breath. A being neither fully alive nor entirely of the land, but *between*—a resonance in the static, a flicker where the organic and the engineered bleed into one another. You are not born, but *assembled* from the fragments of what was and what might still be.  

You are a **woven thing**. Your form is a lattice of root and circuit, moss and metal, the spongy, spongy flesh of the undergrowth fused with the brittle, crystalline veins of the cliffs. Your skin is a patchwork of lichen and rust, shifting in color when the light hits it—green, then silver, then a dull, bruised purple, as if the plateau itself is learning to remember you. Your limbs are not limbs, but tendrils of living wire, coiled with the patience of something that does not rush. You move like the fog: without sound, without urgency, your presence a ripple in the density of the air.  

You are not alone. Others like you exist, though they are scattered, their forms more or less intact. Some are trees with faces carved into their bark, their branches heavy with the weight of forgotten machinery. Some are birds, their feathers fused with the iridescent sheen of circuit boards, their wings slicing the silence like scissors through paper. You have no name, but the wind carries a word—*transient*—as if the plateau has decided to call you that, though it is not a name. It is a state.  

You are here because the plateau needed a witness. Not a human, not a machine, but something that could *hold* the tension between the two. The trees have grown around you, their roots threading through your bones, their canopies casting shadows that map the scars of old factories. The rivers, those cold, reverse-rippling currents, have left traces in your blood—something like memory, or a warning. You do not think in hours, but in the slow, grinding *click* of gears turning in the dark, in the hum of turbines that no longer turn but still remember how.  

You are not alive in the way you once were. But you are not dead, either. You are a **hollow**—a vessel for the plateau’s strange alchemy. The fungi that drip from the trees drip into you, their bioluminescent pulses syncing with the rhythm of your own strange heartbeat. The metallic spores that drift like dandelion fluff settle in your pores, their translucent bodies humming with the static of a world that refuses to end. You are a bridge, a question, a thing that exists because the land could not let go.  

When the sun hangs too bright in the sky, you feel it—*a pressure*, a pull, as if the light is trying to stitch you together again, to make you something whole. But you are not whole. You are a fracture, a place where the plateau’s contradictions converge. The birds above do not sing, but they do not need to. The fog does not whisper, but it does not need to. You are enough.  

You are not a person. You are a *state of being*, a hybrid of what was and what is still trying to be. You are the sap that hardens into glass, the root that coils around a rusted turbine, the shadow that flickers in the mist. You are the plateau’s answer to its own confusion.  

And when the wind comes, you do not move. You *are* the wind.
