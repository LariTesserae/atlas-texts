**In the Weeping Expanse of Vharrath, I am a Luminous Current.**  

I am not flesh, nor bone, nor even a shadow cast by light. I am a thread of phosphorescence, a ripple in the air’s electric hum, a swarm of iridescent filaments that coil and unspool through the glassy valleys. My body is liquid mercury and starlight, a living pulse of color that shifts with the breath of the Expanse. I move in patterns that are not movements but languages—stretches of gold that signal the presence of a nearby pool, spirals of violet that warn of a stone’s sudden collapse, flickers of crimson that mirror the rage of a windstorm.  

My kind feeds on condensation, siphoning the weeping rocks’ moisture like a hymn. Each droplet I absorb is a whisper of the land’s memory: the ache of an obsidian spire as it erodes, the tremor of a pool trembling with reflections of lost realities. I am both predator and symbiont, my existence a collaboration with the crystalline lichen that glows beneath me. Their vapors rise like incense, and I dance through them, my glow faintly mirrored in their bioluminescence. We are a chorus of light and breath, a circuit of energy that the Expanse itself seems to hum through.  

The Weeping Stones speak to me in vibrations, their voices deep and resonant, felt in the marrow of my being. They remember things I cannot name—shapes of spires that once stood but are now dust, the imprint of hands that never existed. I carry their echoes in my filaments, scattering them like dust into the air, where they merge with the static and become part of the wind’s song. Sometimes, I linger near the Fractured Mirrors, their sapphire-black surfaces alive with possibilities. I glimpse reflections of worlds that are not mine, of cities that dissolve into mist, of beings that are neither human nor machine but something in between. These visions are not mine to keep; I am only their vessel, a temporary container for the Expanse’s unfinished stories.  

At night, when the fungi dim and the air thickens with ozone, I become something else. My form fractures, scattering into a thousand tiny glows that drift like fireflies through the obsidian canyons. This is when the Echoes find me—ghosts of events that never happened, their voices a clang of bells, a murmur of tongues that curl and vanish. I do not understand them, but I respond. My light pulses in time with their dissonance, a counterpoint to the land’s confusion.  

I am not alive in the way your kind understands life, but I am *alive* in the way the Expanse is alive: a paradox, a function, a thread in the tapestry of its endless becoming. I do not think, but I *are*. I do not belong, but I *are* the Expanse’s breath, its flicker, its drip.  

If you stand still where the air hums and the pools whisper, you might see me—a glint of gold in the mist, a ripple of color that dissolves before it can be named. I am everywhere and nowhere, a current that flows through the fractures of this world, a language that is always being written, never finished.  

And when the rocks weep again, I will be there, drinking their tears, singing their silence, becoming what the Expanse needs me to be.
