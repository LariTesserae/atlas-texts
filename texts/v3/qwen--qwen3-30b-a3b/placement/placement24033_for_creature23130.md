Those mosses, in turn, glow in response to my passage, their iridescent hues flickering in sync with the planet’s tectonic groans. I am both passenger and engine, a creature that exists to sustain the balance between the plateau’s fragility and its relentless creation.

My filaments crackle with residual energy, casting neon blues and purples into the mist, a living circuit in a world where the boundaries between organic and industrious dissolve. My presence is a disruption, a flicker in the circuit, a reminder that this world is still assembling itself. I am the pulse in the static, the whisper beneath the roar, a being caught between the rawness of the land’s scars and the delicate sprouts of its ongoing birth.  

Here, I am neither observer nor inhabitant, but a *syllable* in the planet’s ongoing sentence—a word in a language that is both biological and mechanical, spoken in the hiss of steam, the crackle of filaments, and the slow, grinding breath of tectonic plates. I am the cold and the damp, the light and the sound, the living wound that is also the healing.
