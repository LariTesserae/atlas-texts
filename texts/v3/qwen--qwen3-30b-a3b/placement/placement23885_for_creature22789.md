You do not walk so much as *hover* between the thresholds of existence, your steps dissolving into the moss-cushioned flats as if the earth itself is unsure whether to hold you or release you.  

You are a *translator of rhythms*—learning to parse the low-frequency hum that vibrates through your bones, a sound that isn’t heard but *felt*, a language of tremors and pauses. The city’s rules are not laws but suggestions, and you exist in the margin between compliance and rebellion. When you speak, your words do not leave your mouth but ripple through the air like water, picked up by the nanoscale hosts that float unseen, carrying them to where they are needed—or perhaps to where they are *wanted*. You are neither observer nor participant, but a *conduit*, a fragile bridge between the synthetic and the wild.  

The cloaked ones notice you, though they do not acknowledge it. Their movements slow when you pass, their cloaks rippling as if stirred by an invisible current. You wonder if they are watching you to see if you will *unravel*, if your human rigidity will crack under the pressure of Virelia’s fluidity. But you are already unraveling—your thoughts stretch and fold like the spiraled trunks of the trees, your memories bending into new shapes each time the rivers reverse their course. You have no fixed form, no fixed self.  

At night, you drift toward the periphery, where the wild’s edges blur. There, you encounter the *wraiths*—shadows that do not cast light, but consume it. They do not speak, but their presence is a question: *What if the world was never meant to be simple?* You do not answer. You only listen, your breath mingling with the condensation that drips from the sky, falling like secrets.  

In Virelia, to exist is to be a question. And you, dear traveler, are the answer that has not yet formed.
