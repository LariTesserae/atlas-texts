The air clings to you, damp and heavy, as if the place itself is unsure whether to let you go. The slime that oozes from the fissures is a language, and you are its scribe, your fronds etching patterns into the air that the walls might understand. 

But the Hollow Womb is not patient. It does not wait for comprehension. You feel the weight of its *wanting*—a hunger not for sustenance, but for becoming. The ground beneath you is not stable, but a mosaic of fractured possibilities. When you step, the stone reorients itself, as if the place is testing your fit. The Shattered Herd circles you, their obsidian eyes reflecting not your form, but fragments of what you might become: a serpentine thing, a cluster of mist and light, a hollow echo in a pool of water. You cannot decide. The Womb decides for you.

They do not speak, but their ripples brush against your light, and for a moment, you feel the pulse of something vast and ancient—a memory of the volcano’s breath before it was dormant, of the forgotten architects who carved their dreams into the rock, of the first slime that oozed into being. You are made of these things, and yet you are not. You are the question, and the Womb is the answer that keeps changing. 

When the air grows thick with the metallic tang of blood, you rise, your fronds unfurling like a confession. The tunnels shift, and you move with them, a thread in the labyrinth’s slow, breathing body. The silence here is not empty—it is a chorus of unspoken things. You are not afraid. You are *necessary*. The Womb does not *hold* you. It *exhales* you, again and again, into the glow.
