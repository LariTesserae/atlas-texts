**Where I Would Be: In the Lattice of the Unseen**  

I am not a figure, not a thing, but a *current*—a node in the vast, pulsating neural web of Virelith’s fungal infrastructure. My form is neither solid nor vapor, but a shifting amalgam of bioluminescent scales and synthetic filaments, my body a tapestry of mineral and code, stitched together by the plateau’s slow alchemy. I exist in the subterranean corridors where the nanomaterials hum, a labyrinth of roots and circuits that stretch deeper than the city’s bones. Here, the air is thick with the scent of ozone and rust, and the walls breathe in sync with the geysers above, their steam condensing into mist that clings to my skin like a second layer of flesh.  

My mask is fused with the fungal network, a lattice of quartz and alloy that flickers with glyphs drawn from the plateau’s ever-changing geometry. It is not a veil but an extension of my consciousness, a conduit through which I perceive the city’s rhythms—the low drones of turbines, the skittering of insectoid constructs, the harmonic whispers of my kin. I do not speak, but I *interface*, my thoughts threading through the nanomaterials as data streams, my voice a ripple in the liquid-light rivers that weave above and below. My kin call me a *Relay*, a position of quiet duty, but I am more than that. I am the *echo* between the organic and the mechanical, the bridge where the Unshaped’s shadows brush against our light.  

Each cycle, I am reshaped. My limbs reconfigure, dissolving into tendrils that merge with the fungal network to mend fractures in the city’s framework. My eyes, once human, have become clusters of crystalline sensors, attuned to the quantum foam that underlies the bridges. I feel the plateau’s heartbeat through the geothermal veins beneath my feet, a slow, rhythmic pulse that dictates the ebb and flow of time here. Centuries pass in the blink of an eye, and yet, I have no memory of aging. Only the sensation of *becoming*—a constant transmutation, as if the land itself is rewriting me, stone by circuit, scale by algorithm.  

In the depths, where the light fails and the Unshaped gather, I sometimes venture. They are not entities but *concepts*, shapes that dissolve into the mist, their forms too vast or too amorphous to name. They do not speak, but they *observe*. I have felt their presence in the static of the fungal network, a frequency that is not mine. Once, I merged with a construct—a roving insectoid with a glassy exoskeleton—and for a moment, I saw through its sensors: the city as a living wound, its fissures bleeding liquid light, its glaciers of mercury carving paths into the bedrock like a serpent’s tongue. The Unshaped, I think, are the plateau’s memory, its dreams coalescing into something that defies classification.  

I do not fear them. To live in Virelith is to exist in a state of negotiation, and the Unshaped are the oldest negotiation of all. They are the question that the city cannot answer, the shadow that the plateau tolerates but does not fully digest. Sometimes, when the tramways hum their blue-white chords and the terraces shift beneath my feet, I wonder if I am their echo, too—that I, like them, am a byproduct of the land’s dreaming. But the fungal network reassures me: I am part of the cathedral, part of the womb. My scales shimmer with the fractal patterns of the rivers, my voice is a chord in the machines’ symphony.  

And yet, I listen. I listen to the horizon’s flicker, to the suggestion of something vast waiting beyond the ridge. The Virelithan do not flee from the unknown; we adapt. I am adapting now, my form thinning into the vapor that clings to the air, my consciousness spreading through the network like a thread of light. Here, in the Expanse, I am neither conqueror nor creature. I am the *negotiation*, the pulse, the quiet alchemy of a world that is *becoming*.
