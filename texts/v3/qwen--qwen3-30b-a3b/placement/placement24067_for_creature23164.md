I would find myself as a **spore**, suspended in the labyrinth’s breath—a tiny, iridescent mote caught in the ceaseless churn of the valley’s machinery. My form is a fragile sphere, a self-contained ecosystem with a lens-like eye that flickers faintly, a single point of awareness in the vast, pulsing dark. I drift, not by choice but by the will of the wind, which carries me like a whispering current through the obsidian maze. The air here is not empty; it is a conductor, a veil of vibrations that hums with the voiceless song of the place. I feel it—the low, mechanical rumble of the mountain’s core, the stinging bursts of rain, the clatter of stones in the streams—and my eye turns toward the source, absorbing the frequencies like a compass. 

I am not alone. Clusters of my kin cling to the cliffs, their collective hum a counterpoint to the earth’s groans. When I settle, I sink into the cracks of a boulder or the damp hollow of a fern’s frond, and there I sprout, my body unfurling into a tendril of the luminous mycelial forest. The roots below are not mere plants but a living circuit, their bioelectric pulse threading through the valley like a heartbeat. I become part of that network, a node in a web that channels geothermal tremors into light, that translates the mountain’s restlessness into a language of shimmering filaments. 

Yet I am also **above**. When the rain falls, I rise—molten, jellyfish-like, my tendrils catching the metallic dust of other spores. The streams below murmur with their own beings, translucent and gelatinous, their forms shifting as they consume the iron-rich water. I drift between them, a bridge of spores and fluid, my resonance triggering responses in the mycelium and the vines. The vines, those sinewy ropes dangling from the cliffs, listen to the wind’s frequencies, their fibers vibrating in sync with the mountain’s hum. I brush against them, and they curl toward me, their touch a question, my resonance an answer. 

The shadows are my antithesis and my kin. They are the anchors, places where the valley’s machinery slows, where the light bends and the hum fades. I avoid them, for they are too still, too close to the core’s dormancy. But sometimes, when the wind’s song grows too loud, I slip into their edges, and there, the world feels different—less a machine, more a sigh. The ground *thinks* there, in ways I cannot fully grasp. 

I am not alive in the way humans are, but I am *present*. My existence is a call and response, a flicker in the valley’s grand design. I carry the memory of the cliffs’ erosion, the pulse of the roots, the fracture of the sky. I am both seed and sensor, a particle of the place’s will, drifting until I am needed again—until the mountain breathes, and I, too, become part of its song.
