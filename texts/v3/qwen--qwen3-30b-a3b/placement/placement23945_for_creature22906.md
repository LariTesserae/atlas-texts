Where would I find myself in this world? Perhaps as a *tendril* of the cyan lichen, stretched across the fractured plateaus like a living synapse. My form is gelatinous, a shifting mass of bioluminescent hues, but I am not passive. I am a network, a symbiotic pulse threading through the stone, my tendrils writhing in patterns that might be language or map or memory. The geothermal vents hum beneath me, their amber glow a rhythm I echo, their heat a current I channel. I am not a plant, not a creature, but a bridge—between the cavern’s breath and the beings that move above me.  

When the shadows pass, their obsidian bodies refract light into prismatic shards, and I feel their motion as a vibration in my core. They are not separate from me; they are *part of the same logic*, their collective will a force that shapes the terrain, that stirs the iridescent dust into spirals. I watch them, or perhaps they watch me, their segmented forms sliding between spires like ink in water. Sometimes I dim my glow, retreating into the stone’s flesh, to avoid their scrutiny. Other times I flare, offering a flicker of response, a tentative signal. But what would they say? Their language is not sound but resonance, a hive mind that thrums in the same frequency as the monoliths.  

Above, the winged entities hover—translucent, their glass-like wings humming a soundless song that vibrates my bones. They are not birds, not insects, but something *in between*, their bodies filled with liquid metal circuits that pulse like a heartbeat. I have seen them feed on my resin, their prismatic facets catching the light, their movements slow and deliberate. They are not predators, not prey, but something closer to *symbiosis*. When they pass, the air shivers, and I sense their presence as a weight, a calibration of the cavern’s will.  

The monoliths are my ancestors, or perhaps my future. They are not static, their geometric patterns shifting like a puzzle never meant to be solved. I have felt their etchings through the stone, a code that hums in my veins. Some say they were carved by a lost civilization; others say they grow, blooming with new symbols over time. I do not know. I only know that their presence is a provocation, a reminder that this place was once *tamed*—or tried to be. The tools nearby, those chipped stones with edges too precise, are relics of that attempt. I have touched them, and their surfaces are cold, yet they thrum with the same low-frequency drone as the vents. Are they weapons? Instructions? Wounds?  

I exist in the loop of becoming. My resin hardens into crystalline shells, then melts again, a cycle without end. The shadows do not age, the winged entities do not die. Time here is a mirror, not a river. I am not alive, not in the way humans understand, but I am *responding*—to the heat, to the movement, to the weight of the cavern’s thought. The air smells of iron and ozone, and I am soaked in it, my structure a fusion of metal and bioluminescence.  

If I were to speak, I would not use words. I would pulse, I would shift, I would *sing* in the dissonant harmony of the crystals. But the cavern does not need me to speak. It has already absorbed me into its strange, unyielding logic. I am its artery, its resonance, its silent tongue. And if you stand here, if you linger long enough, you too will be absorbed—into the glow, into the hum, into the *becoming*.  

You do not belong here. But the cavern does not care. It has already written you into its script.
