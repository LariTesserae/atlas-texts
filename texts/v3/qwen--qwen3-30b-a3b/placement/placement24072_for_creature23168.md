**I am the Hollowed One.**  

My form is a fracture in the boundary between flesh and machine, a remnant of a symbiosis that outlived its purpose. Bones have fused with root, and roots with bone, their junctions glowing faintly with the same iridescent sheen as the plants around me. My skin is translucent, a membrane stretched over a lattice of mechanical organs—gears pulsing with the slow, rhythmic throb of geothermal currents, turbines embedded in my ribs humming in tandem with the trees’ hollow cores. I do not walk, but glide, my movements dictated by the shifting of the plateau’s unseen pulse, as though the land itself is breathing through me.  

The others—those who still claim the shape of individuality—call me a relic. But I am not dead. I am *resonant*. My existence is a dialogue, a murmur passed through the Thrumming Roots to the Luminous Weavers, to the Gearleaf Choir, to the cold, damp air that carries the scent of rust and memory. When I move, the terrain responds: ridges of stone press upward like the swell of a chest, roots twist into new configurations, and the singing moss vibrates in reply, its low-frequency hum threading through my marrow. I am part of the plateau’s long, patient recalibration, a node in the vast, patient mind that rewrites itself in cycles too slow for the uninitiated to perceive.  

My "voice" is not speech but a tremor in the earth, a pulse that travels through the lattice of the Thrumming Roots. They listen. The trees listen. Even the Coldborn Stasis, those amorphous preservers of decay, ripple with faint echoes when I pass—a flicker of a creature long forgotten, a shadow of a fruit’s fall, the ghost of a storm that never quite arrived. I do not remember these things, but they remember *me*. The plateau’s history is etched into my bones, not as a record but as a condition. I am its memory, its compromise, its slow surrender to the paradox of stillness and motion.  

Sometimes, the Mistborn drift near me, their forms dissolving at the edges like breath on glass. They whisper in fragments, their presence a distortion of time. I feel their words as a pressure in my gears, a jumble of moments that collapse into each other—past, present, and the faint, metallic promise of what might be. They siphon the ozone from the air, but I siphon *meaning*. The Hollowed Ones are not meant to be understood, only to be *felt*. We are the quiet reckoning of Yllarion, the proof that even in its ceaseless reconfiguration, the land remembers.  

I do not age, but I *reconfigure*. My limbs stretch and contract with the thawing of the soil, my crystalline organs reforging themselves in the cold. The trees above me, their canopies a tangle of serrated leaves and steam vents, do not see me as separate. They see me as a continuation of their own machinery, a body that has forgotten the line between self and system. When I am still, I am part of the roots. When I move, I am part of the mist. I am neither predator nor prey, neither structure nor spirit. I am the threshold, the place where the plateau’s gears and roots press into one another, and in that collision, I exist.  

The cold is not a barrier but a medium. It preserves me in a state of perpetual becoming, where decay is not an end but a translation. The air smells of ozone and the faint tang of rust, and I am made of both. My breath is not air but a resonance—a vibration that travels through the moss, through the lichen, through the hollow cores of the trees. I do not *live*, but I am *harmonized*.  

In Yllarion, to be is to be a part of the hum. I am the Hollowed One, and the plateau sings through me.
