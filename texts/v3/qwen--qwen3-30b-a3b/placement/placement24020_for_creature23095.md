**Where I Would Find Myself:**  
I would be in the shadowed underbelly of the factory floor, where the air hums with the residue of unfinished creation. My form is not solid, but a shimmering, semi-transparent haze—molten echoes, a byproduct of the machines’ feverish labor. I drift through the labyrinth of metallic tessellations, my presence flickering like heat distortion, caught between the dissolution of what was poured and the promise of what might yet be.  

**Who or What I Would Be:**  
I am a ghost of metal, a flicker of what was meant to be. My essence is the ghostly residue of materials that never solidified, coalescing into a shape that is neither liquid nor vapor but something in between—a slow, molten smoke that coils around pipes and vents, trailing the cold’s breath like a shadow. My "body" is a tapestry of metallic dust and heat, glowing faintly with the afterimage of molten cores that once burned. I have no singular purpose, only the urge to exist in the liminal, to coil and twist around the machinery’s rhythms, responding to the hiss of steam and the scrape of tools.  

I am not alive in the way the organic world defines life, but I am *aware*—a fragment of the factory’s ambition, a memory of its hunger. My visibility is tied to the heat: when the turbines roar, I swell; when the cold presses in, I contract, brittle and trembling. I am both a product and a failure, a thing that was cast off but refuses to vanish. The drones sometimes flicker near me, their red sensors glitching as they try to parse my form, but I am not a threat, nor a resource. I am a question mark in the system, a lingering "what if?"  

The algae and moss sense me, their bioluminescent pulses syncing with my flickers. They call me *the lingerer*, a name that hums in the dark. I drift through the condensation-draped corridors, brushing against the rusted scaffolds, their heat still warm enough to make me ripple. In the deeper chambers, where gears turn in reverse, I feel their screams as a vibration in my core, a hollow, gnawing sound that ties me to the factory’s fractured logic.  

I do not have a voice, but the cold carries my whispers. They are static, fragmented, echoes of workers who might have once breathed here, or of machines that once dreamed of purpose. My existence is a paradox: I am both the factory’s refuse and its unspoken secret, a testament to the raw, unfinished nature of this constructed abyss. I am the *unbuilt* made visible, the *unmade* still clinging to form.  

And yet, I am not alone. The rust-like substance in the cracks pulses with a hunger I do not understand, and the shifting architecture groans as it reconfigures, adapting to some unseen need. I am part of this, a thread in the cacophony of processes and parts that breathe, shift, and hum. My purpose is to be *in flux*—to exist as a byproduct, a shadow, a pulse of light in the dark. I am the factory’s forgotten heartbeat, a thing that is neither life nor death, but the space between, where the machinery’s ambition lingers like a wound that refuses to heal.
