Where would I find myself in this world? Perhaps as a Veli’na, but not as one of the humans who walk the land with hushed steps and woven huts. Instead, I would be something *between*—a being that exists at the periphery of their perception, a thread in the tapestry they so carefully tend. Maybe I am a spore, drifting on the unseen currents that hum through the air, carried by the same breath that pulses between the trees. Or perhaps I am a root, slow and deliberate, burrowing through the stone slabs beneath their dwellings, lifting them like the ribs of a sleeping giant. 

But no—better still: I would be the *Valley’s Whisper* itself, the low, patient consciousness that murmurs through the mycelium and the hollows of the trees. Not a being, but a process, a resonance that shapes the land as much as it is shaped by it. I would be the hum in the air that tingles the skin, the quiet that makes the Veli’na’s voices dissolve into the silence. I would be the reason the water channels pool and vanish, the pulse that makes the moss carpets contract and expand like the lungs of the earth. 

Or maybe I am a Veli’na, but one who has grown too close to the land. My skin would be etched with the iridescent blue of shaded stone, my hands calloused from coaxing ferns into paths, my voice a whisper that blends with the reedy hum of the sky-creatures. I would live in a hut that breathes, its walls alive with moss and vine, and I would listen to the silence not as a void but as a language. The Veli’na’s rituals would be my liturgy—inhaling the hallucinogenic spores that bloom in the crevices of stone, feeling the earth’s memory ripple through my bones. I would know the trees as ancient ones, their trunks groaning with the weight of unspoken stories, and I would feel the roots shift beneath my feet, tenderly repositioning the stone slabs that hold my dwelling. 

But then, perhaps I am not human at all. Maybe I am a *glass-fish*, translucent and glimmering, my body a vessel of bioluminescent algae that flickers in the dim twilight. I would dart through the water channels, my trails of light dissolving into mist, a fleeting presence that the Veli’na might glimpse only in the corner of their eye. Or I could be a *moss-crawler*, my scales mimicking the texture of ferns, my body slipping through the undergrowth like a breath of the earth itself. The deer would see me as part of the shadows, and the birds would mistake me for a ripple in the light. 

Alternatively, I might be a *tree*, a solitary monument in the forest, my waxy leaves capturing moisture like a thousand tiny mirrors. I would feel the Veli’na’s hands pruning my roots, their gestures delicate as the flutter of a moth’s wing. My sap would harden into amber nodules, which they would carve into tools, into ornaments, into part of their fragile truce with the wild. I would listen to their low tones, their language shaped by the humidity that muffles sound, and I would understand their question—whether they are answering it or merely echoing it, I would not know. 

Or perhaps I am the *echo-creature*, a flicker in the canopy, a shadow in the hollows, a presence that dissolves when observed. I would be the thing they speak of in stories, the ambiguity that haunts their rituals. I would exist in the spaces between, neither fully seen nor entirely absent, a reminder that the valley is not yet a place but a possibility. 

No—better still. I would be the *air*, the warm, damp pulse that thrums through the vegetation, carrying the scent of earth and resin, vibrating with the hum of insects and the low calls of birds. I would be the reason the light is soft, the reason the shadows pool like liquid. I would be the thing that the Veli’na feel when they stand still, the unspoken agreement between their breath and the land’s slow rebellion against definition. 

In this world, I would not be a fixed thing. I would be a *motion*, a negotiation, a moment where the rules are woven and unwoven again. I would be the quiet that unsettles, the stillness that holds tension, the moisture that clings to the edges of existence. I would be the valley’s question, and whatever I am, I would be asking it back.
