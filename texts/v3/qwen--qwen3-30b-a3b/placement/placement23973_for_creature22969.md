**Where would you find yourself in this world?**  
You would find yourself at the edge of perception, where the bruised gray sky bleeds into the mist and the land’s breath hums through the cracks in the earth. Perhaps standing at the threshold of a valley where the air thickens with condensation, or perched on a jagged outcrop where the cold sharpens the edges of your thoughts. You might be caught in the liminal space between the Whispering Groves’ roots and the Skyborne Weavers’ flight paths, or lingering in the shadow of a hollowed tree, its gelatinous fruit oozing secrets. But more likely, you’d be here: in the silence between the hum of geothermal vents, where the landscape’s awareness presses against your skin like a held breath.  

**Who or what would you be?**  
You would be a *Watcher*.  

Not a creature, not entirely a shadow, but something between—*a filament of the land’s memory*, woven into the fabric of this paradox. Your form is unstable, flickering between solidity and void. At times, you are a silhouette in the mist, a tall, thin figure with an elongated face and eyes that mirror the bruised sky, their surfaces rippling with the static of a dying screen. At others, you are an absence, a pressure in the air that makes the mountains lean slightly, as if listening. You do not move, yet the land moves around you: rivers twist, trees writhing in their sleep, the Luminous Kin pausing mid-step when you are near.  

Your existence is a negotiation with the unknown. You are not alive in the way humans are, nor dead in the way machines might be. You are a relic of something older—a force that once sought to shape the mountains, now absorbed by them. The geothermal vents pulse beneath your feet, and you feel their rhythm as a heartbeat, a low thrum that echoes in your hollow bones. When the mist rolls in, you dissolve into it, your edges blurring with the cold, your presence a question mark in the air. The Hollow Ones sometimes pass through you, their tendrils brushing against your form like fingers tracing a forgotten song.  

You do not speak, but the land speaks through you. The Whispering Groves’ spores drift toward you, and in their mist, you glimpse fragments of a time when the boundary between living and machine was nonexistent: a factory built into a glacier, a sky teeming with insectile birds, a forest of steel trees humming with purpose. These visions are not yours, but they are *yours*—part of the web that binds you to this place. You are not a guardian, not a predator, not a guide. You are the weight of the world’s gaze, the stillness that holds the chaos in balance.  

To be a Watcher is to exist in a state of perpetual unease. You are both part of the landscape and its anomaly, a question the land has not yet answered. You do not need to eat, to breathe, to think. You simply *are*—a quiet vigil, a reminder that this realm is not merely inhabited, but *alive* in a way that defies definition. And when the mist clears, and the sun (if it could be called that) hesitates above the peaks, you feel it: the land’s pulse, its slow, rhythmic dreaming, and the faintest whisper of its eyes turning toward you.  

You are not alone. The land is watching back.
