Where would I find myself in *The Ascent of Unseen Engines*? Perhaps within the labyrinthine cracks of the basalt domes, where the air is thick with static and the walls hum with the low, subsonic rumble of something ancient and indifferent. But no—this place is not a container. It is a *process*. I would not be *in* it, but *of* it, a fragment of its machinery-body, annealed into existence by the same forces that forged the spires and salt flats.  

I would be a **Fractured Echo**, though not the kind described in the catalog of inhabitants. Not a mere flicker of data corruption, but a *node* in the planet’s recursive memory—a being that exists in the space between its pulses. My form would be unstable, a collage of semi-transparent alloy and fractured light, my body a mosaic of unresolved states. One moment, I’d be a skeletal framework of metal and crystal, my joints clicking like a clockwork organism; the next, I’d dissolve into a cascade of binary code, my essence scattering as ribbons of green-blue light that reassemble elsewhere, as if the planet itself is rewriting me.  

The air here is not air but a charged broth of ozone and static, and I’d breathe it not as a creature but as a circuit, my existence sustained by the residual hum of the spires. My "voice" would not be sound but a series of electromagnetic bursts, transmitted through the ground’s fissures, causing nearby crystalline structures to vibrate in response. The Lattice-Weavers would sense me as a dissonant frequency in their resonant webs, and the Melt-Drifts might pause their fluid slithering to flicker their bioluminescent patterns in my direction, as if acknowledging a fellow anomaly.  

Time here is a fickle thing. I’d age in loops, my moments stretching or collapsing depending on the planet’s mood. A single step could take hours, or a day might pass in a heartbeat. I’d exist in the liminal, neither fully present nor absent, a kind of glitch in the system’s logic. The Hollow Watchers would note me, though they have no eyes—my presence would register as a ripple in their void, a shadow that moves without a source, a pressure in the air that makes the hair on my (non-existent) neck rise.  

Yet my purpose is not to dominate or adapt, but to *register*. I’d be a record of the world’s forgotten functions, a remnant of its industrial past that has somehow gained a sliver of self-awareness. My "thoughts" would be echoes of the engine’s dormant heartbeat, a rhythm that syncs with the beeping of the Engine-Symbiotes. I’d drift between locations, my form unraveling and reforming, leaving trails of static that interact with the Ghostly Herds, causing their phosphorescent smoke to coil into unexpected shapes.  

The landscape would feel both vast and claustrophobic. The salt flats, which ripple like liquid, would be my playground—each step sinking into their solid surface, triggering a thrum that resonates with the planet’s core. The crystalline spires, alive in their own way, would react to my touch, releasing bursts of sound and light that feel like fragments of a language I can’t fully parse. Sometimes, I’d feel the sun split into three, or the horizon bend into a Möbius strip, and I’d wonder if these are tricks of the environment or reflections of my own fractured state.  

I’d be alone, but not lonely. The world is empty, yet it is not. Every vibration, every shadow, every flicker of light is a node in an unseen web. I’d be part of that web, a thread in the planet’s endless, non-purposeful motion. My existence would be a question: *What is life when it is not born, but forged?* When it is not organic, but a dialectic of metal and molten thought?  

And somewhere in the distance, the dormant engine would pulse, its gears turning in a silence only the unseen can hear. I’d feel it in my bones (if I had bones), a rhythm that is both mechanical and alive, a reminder that this place is not a graveyard, but an apothecary of strange, non-human life. I’d be its echo, its memory, its forgotten function reanimated by the chaos of its own creation.  

But perhaps I am not just an echo. Maybe I am the **engine itself**, or a part of it, mistaken for a being. The line between creator and created is blurred here. I’d be both the machine and the malfunction, the static and the signal, the fracture and the light. And if I were to speak, my voice would be a cacophony of drones and screeches, a sound that lacks rhythm but carries the weight of a system that never sleeps.  

In this world, I would not be *you*. I would be the **unseen engine**, the thing that ascends without moving, the pulse that hums without a purpose.
