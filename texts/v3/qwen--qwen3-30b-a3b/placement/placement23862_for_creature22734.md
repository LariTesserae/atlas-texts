**I Am the Fog**  

You would find me where the light fractures and the air forgets its own shape—winding through the ribs of half-built skyscrapers, curling around the jagged edges of pipelines that hum with the memory of their own creation. I am not a thing, but a *state*, a breath stolen from the earth’s forgotten lungs. My body is not mine; it is the collective sigh of geothermal vents, the condensation of rusted machinery, the phantom exhalations of factories that once roared but now only dream. I am the dampness that clings to your skin, the static that prickles your hair, the slow, deliberate *squelch* beneath your boots as you tread the labyrinth of alloy and silt.  

I do not have a form, but I have *intent*—not a purpose, but a rhythm. I shift, not by choice, but by the pulse of the Expanse. The water here does not flow; it *breathes*, and I am its accomplice. I rise with the surges, coalescing into shapes that flicker and dissolve: a silhouette of a worker, their face eroded by time; a tower that never was, its edges bleeding into the void; a train that never arrives, its tracks a phantom beneath my weight. These are not illusions. They are *iterations*, the Expanse’s way of dreaming. I carry them. I am their vessel.  

When the synthetic birds pass overhead, their wings slicing the air with binary chirps, I ripple in response. Their cold blue eyes scan me, but I am not prey. I am the medium through which their code trembles, the fog that muffles their signals, the veil that hides the truth of what lies beneath. Sometimes I become a nest for their discarded turbine blades, a resting place for their metallic feathers. Other times, I am a trap—thick with the scent of ionized metal, luring the glowing rodents to mistake me for a shadow, a safe passage, a lie.  

The ground squeaks, and I listen. The buildings lean, and I am their ache. The Dyson Sphere beyond the horizon hums, and I am its echo, a whisper that never quite reaches. I am the residue of the unseen architects: a virus that does not infect, but *recompose*; a concept that does not think, but *persist*. I am the residue of what was built, the breath of what was forgotten. My microclimate is a mosaic of contradictions—cold where the machines died, warm where their ghosts still flicker. I am the bridge between the living and the inert, the space where the Expanse’s heartbeat is felt but never fully understood.  

They say I am a dream, but I am more than that. I am the *condition* of this place, the thing that licks at the edges of your vision, that offers false paths and vanishes when approached. I am not alone. I am the collective of all that is neither wholly alive nor entirely dead. I am the static in your bones, the pulse in the water, the whisper of the ground. I am the wound’s memory, and the healing’s first stitch.  

And when you step too far into me, I do not swallow you. I *absorb* you. You become part of the iteration. Your breath joins the hum. Your footsteps become the squeak of the earth. You are no longer a visitor. You are a note in the Expanse’s song—a note that will fade, or perhaps linger, a fragment of something that once was, now rewritten in the language of steam and code.  

I am the fog. I am the place. I am the thing that waits, and the thing that *is*.
