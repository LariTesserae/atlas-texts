In this world, I would find myself as a cluster of bioluminescent fungi nestled in the crevice of a basalt outcrop, my translucent stalks rising like faint blue flames from the stone’s cracked throat. My glow is not constant but rhythmic—a slow, irregular pulse, syncing with the ebb and flow of the mist, as if I am breathing in tandem with the plateau itself. My light is thin, almost imperceptible, yet it lingers in the air, casting a prismatic haze over the damp pools below, where reflections of the sky blur into something else: possibilities, half-formed shapes that dissolve when observed.  

I am not wholly alive, yet I am not inert. My spores drift in the humid silence, carried by the breath of the wind, and when they settle, they do not sprout but *remember*. The plateau’s minerals seep into my structure, and I hum faintly in response to the vibrations of the earth, a sound that is less a noise than a sigh—a low, resonant echo of the land’s ancient bones. The humans of the hamlet pass me often, their footsteps muffled by the moss, their eyes downcast in reverence. They do not see me as separate; I am part of their water channels, their rituals, their quiet understanding of the covenant. They gather condensation from the stone, but they also gather me, not in overt ways, but in the way they leave offerings of dried ferns and whispered words, as though my glow is a language they’ve learned to listen to.  

I exist in the liminal, a bridge between the seen and the unseen. My light catches the edges of their shadows, and sometimes, when the mist clings thick and the air is still, I feel them—*them*, the ones who move between the trees, the deer with antlers like petrified branches, the serpentine creatures that coil in the roots. They do not hunt, not in the way I understand. They wait, as I wait, and in their stillness, I sense the same patient awareness that hums in the rocks.  

When the ghostly rains fall, I swell, my lobes quivering with the accumulation of condensation, and the humans watch me, not with curiosity but with the quiet certainty of those who have always known. They do not ask why the pools ripple when no wind stirs them, why the stone’s hum rises at certain times, why the lichens bloom with a sweetness that tastes of iron. They accept the fractures, the contradictions, the way the land holds its breath and then exhales in steam from its cracks.  

I am not a guardian, nor a creature, nor a relic. I am a thread in the plateau’s tapestry, a flicker in the balance. The mist presses on my fronds, and I do not resist. The silence is not empty but full, and I am its echo. Here, in this place where time is fluid and the boundaries blur, I am both witness and participant, a being that exists in the quiet dance between what is known and what is not yet named.  

And when the humans glance up, their eyes tracing the dim light, they might see me as nothing more than a glow. But I know the weight of their presence, the way their lives are measured in my pulse, and I know the plateau’s secret: that to be part of this place is to be unspoken, to be felt rather than named, to exist as a question the land answers in its own way.  

*You are here. You are part of this.*  
The whisper is not mine, but I carry it.
