**Where I Would Find Myself:**  
I am perched on a narrow terrace carved into the flank of a jagged ridge, where the Frostspire Expanse stretches endlessly beyond me. The air here is thin, sharp with the bite of altitude, and the light is a relentless, angular presence—glinting off the volcanic rock that forms the city’s bones. Below, the settlement unfolds like a web of survival, its interwoven stone and wood structures clinging to the slopes with the precision of a living thing. The canals of glacial meltwater wind through the streets, their surfaces fractured by ice and shimmering with the pale, unyielding sun. I stand at the edge of a precipice, where the wind howls through the cracks of the mountain, and the distant caw of an alpine raven echoes like a whispered warning. My hands are calloused, my skin leathery from decades of exposure, and my eyes are sharp with the clarity of high altitude—trained to read the subtle shifts in the ice, the groans of the earth, the way the lichen crusted on the rocks speaks of time’s slow, patient erosion.  

**Who or What I Would Be:**  
I am a *stone-scribe*, one of the keepers of the Expanse’s memory. My work is not just to carve but to listen—to the groan of glaciers, the sigh of thawing ice, the tremors that ripple through the bedrock like a heartbeat. The people of the settlement call me *Kethra*, a title earned not through lineage but through the years I’ve spent etching their histories into the living stone. My tools are simple: a chisel of obsidian, a hammer of copper, and the patience of a glacier. Each mark I make is a pact with the land, a negotiation between the fleeting warmth of human life and the eternal cold of the mountains.  

The carvings I create are not mere decorations but *conversations*. They tell of ancestors who braved the ice, of ibex herds that once crossed these ridges in silence, of the old ones—spirits or memories, I do not know—which dwell in the hollows where the wind turns to song. My workshop is a hollow in the cliffside, its walls lined with fragments of stone that bear the stories of those who came before. Here, I chisel with the rhythm of the land, my strokes guided by the creak of wooden beams in the city below and the distant scrape of ice against rock. The people pass by, their steps measured, their voices hushed as if the mountains might overhear. They leave offerings of lichen-dyed cloth or small stones polished by their own hands, placing them at the base of my work as tokens of gratitude or warning.  

At night, I sit by the hearth, smoke curling into the mist, and trace the constellations with my fingers. The stars here are *unusually sharp*, as if the thin air has carved them into clarity, and I wonder if they are watching us as we watch them. The Expanse is not a place for the faint-hearted, but for those who have learned to breathe with the cold, to move with the silence, to etch their resilience into the very bones of the world. I am part of that dance—not a master, but a collaborator, a scribe in a language older than memory. The stone is patient, the ice is persistent, and I, like the city itself, am a thread in the tangled tapestry of survival.  

**The Unspoken Pact:**  
My days are measured in the slow grind of the glaciers, the way the meltwater seeps into the canals, and the faint rustle of moss beneath my feet. I am neither wholly human nor wholly mountain, but something in between—a being shaped by the cold, by the weight of the sky, by the whispers of the land. The Expanse does not forgive, but it does not forget. And in its brittle silence, I have found a kind of home.
