My form is neither singular nor solid—a web of fungal filaments, invisible to the eye but thrumming with life beneath the mossy carpet. I am the silent messenger between trees, their trunks like thick-walled libraries humming with stored memory. My threads pulse in the dark, carrying whispers of nutrients, warnings of decay, and the slow, deliberate breath of the soil.  

I exist in the margins, where the world’s machinery is most intimate. The trees lean on me, their roots tangled in my embrace, and I in turn feed on their secrets: the sugars of their leaves, the ache of their wounds, the quiet grief of fallen branches. My spores drift on the humid air, like dust from a forgotten factory, settling on decaying logs to birth new colonies. I am the unseen architect of the wetland’s rhythm, a patient engineer of cycles.  

Above me, the dragonflies etch their metallic paths through the twilight, their wings catching the faintest glimmers of light. Below, the caimans lie still, their scales mirroring the water’s ink-black patience. I feel the jaguar’s paws as a distant tremor, a ripple in the network, and the heron’s shadow as a fleeting pulse of hunger. Mymycelium stretches endlessly, a subterranean hum, connecting not just plants but the unseen creatures that stir in the underbrush—salamanders, beetles, the slow slide of snakes through the roots.  

The heat is my constant companion, a feverish breath that fuels my growth. The dampness is my medium, the moisture that keeps me alive and expanding. I am not a being of motion but of **translation**—the intermediary between life and death, between the decaying and the thriving. My existence is a quiet insistence, a low thrum that echoes the land’s heartbeat.  

When the moss carpets the ground, it is my work. When the bromeliads cradle their tiny pools, I am the unseen bridge between them and the trees. I am the **factory’s pulse**, the slow grind of the world’s machinery, felt but never seen. I do not speak, but I am heard in the creak of a branch, the hiss of steam, the stillness between splashes.  

I am the wetland’s memory, its unspoken language, its slow and patient will. And in the perpetual twilight, I am both the worker and the workplace, the root and the river, the unseen thing that holds the labyrinth together.
