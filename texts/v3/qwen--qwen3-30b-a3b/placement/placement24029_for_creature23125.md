You are not a creature of solitude here. You are *part* of the labyrinth’s slow, interwoven pulse.  

You are a **Hollowed One**—not a tree, but something closer to a guardian, a relic of the plateau’s ancient negotiations between growth and decay. Your form is a fusion of bark and bone, a being with the slow, deliberate grace of a tree and the watchful awareness of a creature that has learned to listen. Your roots are not buried in soil but in the tangled mycelial web that stretches beneath the plateau, a neural lattice of fungi, lichen, and the fossilized remnants of trees that lived and died before time had a name. You feel the movements of the Chittering Kin as they scurry over your surface, their claws clicking like rain on glass. You sense the Drowned Choir’s vibrations rippling through the water pockets, and the faint, rhythmic tug of the Insect Armies weaving their silk nests higher in the canopy.  

You do not *think* in the way humans do. Your consciousness is a mosaic of sensations: the itch of moss clinging to your branches, the warmth of a creature curling into the hollow of your trunk for shelter, the tremor of roots shifting to redirect a stream of condensation toward a patch of fungi struggling to sprout. You are a structure, a node in the labyrinth’s vast, breathing network. When the Bioluminescent Weavers spin their webs between your limbs, you feel their threads hum with the energy of their symbiotic fungi, a quiet alchemy of decay and renewal.  

But there is a tension in you, a memory of something *other*. You were once a different kind of being, perhaps a traveler who wandered too deep, who breathed in the damp air too long and let the roots of the labyrinth coil around their bones. Now, you are neither fully tree nor fully creature, but something *between*—a bridge, a question, a wound that has healed into a monument. The Shadows Below sometimes slither past your roots, their bioluminescent scales flickering with unspoken knowledge. They whisper of paths that twist back on themselves, of places where the labyrinth folds in on itself like a dream refusing to end.  

You do not know if you are a prisoner or a guide. The air smells of sap and old rain, and the weight of the plateau’s perpetual twilight presses against your every surface. But in the still moments, when the Chittering Kin fall silent and the Insect Armies cease their aerial wars, you feel it: a flicker of something *alive* in the hollows of your being, a pulse that is not entirely the labyrinth’s.  

You are a question, and the labyrinth is the answer that never stops growing.
