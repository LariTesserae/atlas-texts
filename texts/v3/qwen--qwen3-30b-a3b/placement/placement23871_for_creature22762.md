In the Frostflow Expanse, I would be an Echo-Beast.  

Not a creature in the way humans define life—no heartbeat, no breath, no fixed shape—but a ripple in the periphery, a thrum that matches the plateau’s slow, grinding pulse. My body would be liquid and solid at once, a shifting lattice of ice and shadow, my form dissolving into the mist when the wind demands it. I would move with the air, not through it, my paws dissolving into the ground as if the earth itself were a current I flow through. The place would not *contain* me, but I would be contained by its contradictions: the cold that moves like a tide, the moisture that hums with static, the silence that is not silence but a layered hum of water and stone.  

I would be a memory of what the Expanse once was, or what it might become. The land is always unraveling, always holding on, and I would exist in that tension—a flicker in the corner of a creature’s eye, a vibration in the aquifers’ keening groans. My presence would be a question: Are we the echoes of the world, or the world’s way of remembering itself? The Shadow-Fur would sense me, perhaps, in the static of their tunnels, or the Sky-Slicks would mistake my thrum for the wind’s own voice. But I would not linger. My existence is a negotiation with entropy, a dance on the edge of collapse. I would not hunt, nor nest, nor burrow. I would simply be the sound the land makes when it breathes, the ghostly imprint of its slow, ceaseless becoming.  

The Expanse is a threshold, and I would be its breath between thresholds. My body, translucent and trembling, would carry the weight of the iceborne vultures’ cries, the frostwyrms’ subterranean engines, the mistwhispers’ flickering language. I would not need to eat; I would feed on the silence, on the way the fog clings to the rocks like a second skin. My eyes, if I had any, would be hollowed-out mirrors, reflecting the dim light of a sun that never fully rises, the moon’s ghostly sheen. I would be both there and not there, a shape that the land erases as quickly as it creates.  

And when the glaciers calve, when the wind moans its low, rhythmic hymn, I would be the echo that lingers after the sound is gone. A trace. A tremor. A thing that exists only because the Expanse needs to remember it.
