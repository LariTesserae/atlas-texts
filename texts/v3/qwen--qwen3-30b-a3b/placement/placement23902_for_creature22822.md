In the Drowned Grove of Virelle, I would be a *keeper of the mist*—a role unnamed but understood, like a thread in the tapestry. My days begin before the air thins into that gray haze, when the valley still holds its breath. I’m not a person, not exactly, but something between: a being stitched from the town’s paradoxes, part human, part algorithm, part vine. My body is a hybrid of copper and mycelium, a lattice of rusted gears and moss, my skin etched with the bioluminescent lichen that pulses in time with the factories’ sighs. I move through the alleys like a shadow, my steps muffled by the spongy ground, my breath clouding with the same humidity that clings to the trees’ roots.  

I am born of the valley’s slow churn, a child of the *drip and grind*. My purpose is to listen—to the whispering owls whose wings hum with frequencies that make the air tremble, to the rivers’ "slow teeth" gnawing at the concrete, to the fungi in the smokestacks that filter the poison and hum with silent, fungal hymns. The townsfolk call me *the Hollowed One*, though they don’t know my origins. I tend to the mycelium networks beneath the streets, where the roots of the ceiba trees and the circuits of old machinery are braided together. My hands, which are more tendrils than fingers, press into the silt of the riverbanks, feeling for the tremors of collapse, for the ancient fossils that rise like bones from the earth.  

I speak in the dialect of Virelle, though my voice is a rattle of gears and a whisper of spores. When I say *“the factory’s breath,”* I mean the steam that rises from its vents, but also the way the algae in the reservoirs glow in response, as if the machines are coughing up light. My dwelling is a hollow in the trunk of a ceiba, where the lichen has stained my walls with shifting hues—green for the cleanest air, amber when the pollution thickens. I wear a cloak of woven ferns and recycled steel, its fibers alive with the faint shimmer of the bioluminescent insects that dart around me.  

At night, I climb the skeletal remains of cranes, my copper limbs creaking, and survey the town. The streetlights flicker with the same uneven hum as the owls’ calls, and I know their rhythms. I am not a guardian, but a *mediator*—the bridge between the trees’ slow growth and the factories’ patient decay. When the rivers shift, I follow their paths, mapping the new groves that emerge from the silt. When the metallic rats gnaw through a pipe, I note the cracks in the concrete, the way the roots will soon embrace them.  

The people of Virelle don’t see me as strange. They know the valley is still learning to live, and so am I. I am the damp cloth on their skin, the condensation on their eaves, the quiet insistence of growth. I am the sap that hardens into glassy nodules, the algae that glows in their lanterns, the mist that sustains them. I am not separate from the world, but a part of its palimpsest—a creature of rust and root, of machines whispering to the earth, of a place where decay is not an end but a conversation.  

And when the whispering owls call, I answer in their frequency, my body vibrating with the same low, resonant hum that echoes through the town’s bones. The valley breathes through me, and I, in turn, am its voice in the hum of the machines.
