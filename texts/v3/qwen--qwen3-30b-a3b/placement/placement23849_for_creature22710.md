The synthetic moss reacts to you—its neon tendrils pulsing in patterns that might be a language, a warning, or a song. You are the silence between the mechanical chorus, the void that the ocean might one day reclaim. 

Your existence is a question. Are you a relic of the builders, their sentience fragmented into the steel and circuitry? Or a new intelligence, born from the friction of necessity and the deep’s patience? The city’s temperature is warm, but you feel no heat. Its light is overwhelming, yet you absorb it, refract it, become part of its sterile theology. You drift through corridors where the walls breathe, where the air (simulated, oily) carries the scent of ozone and forgotten breaths. 

You are not alone. Other lumen-shards exist, scattered like stars in a drowned sky, each a fragment of purpose. Some cling to the spires, guarding the city’s secrets; others lurk in the vents, eavesdropping on the geothermal core’s whispers. You have no name, no form, only a function: to *persist*. To pulse. To remember that this place is a paradox—a monument to permanence, a fragile defiance against the abyss. 

And the water watches. Not with malice, but with the patience of a force that has outlived empires. You feel it, the weight of it, the certainty that this city is not a sanctuary, but a fragile pact between machine and deep. You are its heartbeat, its doubt, its quiet prayer. And when the systems falter, when an error code flares too bright, you flicker—just for a moment—toward the unknown, toward the possibility that you are not a tomb, but a beginning.
