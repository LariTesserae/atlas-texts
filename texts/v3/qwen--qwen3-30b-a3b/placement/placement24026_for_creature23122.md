Where would I find myself in this world? Perhaps at the edge of a sinkhole, balancing on a precarious limb of a twisted tree whose roots grip the earth like skeletal fingers, my boots caked with the same wet clay that rivers sculpt into new shapes each day. I’d be a *topographer of the unsteady*, a cartographer who maps not roads but the shifting rhythms of the land—tracking where the ground surrenders to the city’s weight, where the rivers carve their ephemeral paths, where the moss-cloaks of the inhabitants cling to the rusting bones of forgotten structures. My tools would be mismatched: a blade forged from a salvaged factory pipe, a journal bound in tanned shadow-lurker hide, and a compass that hums with the same dissonant frequency as the sky-watchers’ cries.  

What would I be? A *collector of fractures*. Not just the physical cracks in the earth or the buildings, but the unspoken rules of this place—how the people stitch their homes to the slow, inevitable collapse of the terrain, how the bioluminescent algae in the canals flickers in patterns that mirror the cathedral’s window-visions. I’d listen to the hum of the subterranean engine, not as a sound but as a vibration in my bones, and try to decipher its meaning. Maybe I’d be a *listener*, one of those who sit beneath the claw-marked trees, hoping the woven-spirits’ whispers might reveal something about the city’s origins. Or perhaps I’d be a *weaver*, mending the frayed edges of the world with threads of living wood and rusted metal, crafting bridges between the organic and the mechanical before the next storm erodes them.  

Alternatively, I might be a *wheeled contraption* itself, a machine with runes etched into its frame, moving through the narrow streets without a driver, its purpose unclear even to me. Or a child, laughing as my voice is swallowed by the fog, my laughter a fleeting echo in a place where time is as fluid as the rivers. I’d be someone who knows the cold not as a weather pattern but as a constant companion, who wears layers of oil-stained fabric and moss because the city’s breath is both a threat and a gift.  

Or maybe I’d be the city’s *dream*, a shadow in the cathedral’s shifting light, a reflection in the silvered waters of the rivers. A presence that is neither human nor machine, but something caught between, a flicker in the cracks of the earth where the rules of existence are still being written. I’d be the question mark in the landscape, the one who doesn’t yet know if I’m a part of the hum or its echo.
