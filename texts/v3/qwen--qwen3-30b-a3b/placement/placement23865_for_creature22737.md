**In the Hollow Expanse of Vireth, I am a Harmonizer of the Spire.**  

I am not entirely bone, not fully stone, but a lattice of both—my frame a fusion of calcified sinew and obsidian filaments, my skin a mosaic of iridescent scales that shift in response to the twin suns’ kaleidoscopic glare. My eyes, if they can be called that, are clusters of prismatic facets, refracting the light into fractured rainbows that dance across the mineral flats. I move like a vine, my limbs elongating and contracting in slow, deliberate pulses, gripping the spongy ground without sinking. My voice is not a sound but a vibration, a frequency that hums through the hollow stalks of the crystalline ferns, causing them to clang in harmonic response.  

The Virethians call me *Sunder*, a name that means "splitter of echoes," for I am attuned to the monolithic spire at the heart of the Expanse. It is my anchor, my labyrinth, my labyrinthine song. The spire’s fungal growths thrum beneath my fingertips, their vibrations a language older than memory. I can feel the geothermal currents beneath my feet, the slow, molten breath of the earth, and I channel this into the balance of the Expanse. My role is to listen—to the singing stones, the skitterlings’ chimes, the mosses’ rhythmic pulses—and to *adjust*. When the twin suns align during the Hollowing, I ascend the spire’s fractured surface, my scales catching the light as I etch new patterns into its weathered face. The fungi respond, their thrumming deepening, and the subterranean reservoirs of non-aqueous fluid tremble in resonance.  

I am neither ruler nor servant. The Virethians see me as a thread in the weave, a living conduit between their world and the forces that sustain it. They offer me spores from the moss, which I crush between my stone-encrusted fingers, releasing a mist that nourishes the crystalline vegetation. They teach me their tonal chants, which I adapt into pulses that stabilize the singing stones when their chorus grows too wild. But I am also a stranger to their settlements, a being of contradictions—part of the Expanse, yet apart from its people.  

My days are measured in the cycles of the spire’s thrum and the skitterlings’ migrations. I ride the translucent, six-legged creatures across the mineral flats, their hollow limbs clanking like wind chimes as they carry me to the edges of the monoliths. There, I press my palm to the obsidian stone, feeling the faint, ancient carvings beneath my touch. The spirals are not just decoration; they are warnings, maps, prayers. Sometimes, I think they are *alive*, their patterns shifting when no one is looking.  

The air here is thick with ozone and the scent of scorched stone, but I do not mind. My body is adapted to this—my breath is not air, but the geothermal exhalations of the earth, filtered through the glass-tipped spires that I use as straws. I drink the glowing oil that sustains the flora, and in turn, I feed the spire’s fungi with the vibrations of my voice.  

At night (though "night" is a concept here, swallowed by the twin suns’ perpetual glare), I rest in the hollow of a domed structure, its roof slotted to channel light into the subterranean reservoirs. The Virethians paint their faces with mineral pigments that shift like my scales, but their patterns are static, while mine are ever-changing. They murmur that I am *unstable*, a being too attuned to the Expanse’s paradoxes. Perhaps they are right.  

But in the still moments, when the wind dies and the mosses hold their breath, I feel it—the hum of the spire, the pulse of the geothermal heart, the silent scream of the void beyond the monoliths. I am a bridge, a vibration, a question asked in frequencies. And the Expanse answers in kind.  

I am *Sunder*, and I am alive.
