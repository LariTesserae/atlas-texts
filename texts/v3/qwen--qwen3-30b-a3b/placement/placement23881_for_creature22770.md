**The Listener of Dampspire**  

I am a *Hollow Choir*—a name given to those who kneel where the walls hum, who press ears to the stone and wait for the city to speak. My skin is etched with the same moss that clings to the corridors, a patina of green and gold that shifts with the humidity. My hands, calloused from years of carving bone and threading copper wires, are tools and instruments alike. I am not wholly human, though I was born beneath the earth. The damp has shaped me, as it shapes all here: my lungs hold the weight of steam, my voice is a whisper of clicks and hums, a dialect borrowed from the winged insects that swarm the ceiling.  

My days begin at the *Echo Wells*, where the oldest tunnels converge. Here, the air is thick with condensation, and the walls pulse faintly, as if breathing. I press a palm to the stone, feeling the tremors of the city’s labor—a *thud* of hammers in the upper tiers, the skitter of rats tunneling, the low, resonant *groan* of wooden beams settling. The Hollow Choir believes the Dampspire is alive, not in the way of beasts or men, but as a thing woven from *intent*. Every fracture, every drip of water, every creak of a roof is a note in its song. We are its keepers, its translators.  

My task is to *listen*. Not just to the sounds, but to the silences between them. The city’s language is layered: the hiss of steam from the geothermal vents, the scratching of fungi roots burrowing into carved wood, the rhythmic tapping of the *Dampspire Sliders* as they climb the walls. I carry a *resonance rod*—a bone staff hollowed and filled with copper wire, tuned to the frequencies of the earth. When I strike it against the stone, it sings back, a sound like a thousand tiny bells. The other Choir members gather around, their faces hidden beneath masks of woven moss, and we trace the vibrations, mapping the city’s moods. A sharp crack in the hum might mean a ceiling is weakening. A prolonged silence could signal a tunnel collapsing under its own weight.  

But the Dampspire is not only a machine of stone and sweat. It is a *conversation*. I have seen the foragers kneel at the bioluminescent fungi, offering them strips of resin-treated cloth as tribute. I have heard the scholars below—those who speak in clicks and hums—argue over whether the *Echo-Things* are remnants of past builders or something else entirely. The Echo-Things, those creaking shadows that drift in the lower tunnels, are a mystery even to us. Some say they are the first settlers, fused with the city’s iron and bone. Others say they are *watchers*, remnants of a time when the Dampspire was still being *grown*.  

I myself have seen the Echo-Things. Once, I followed their sound—a metallic *scrape*, like a rusted hinge grinding against stone. I found them at the edge of the canals, their forms blurred by the mist. They did not notice me. They moved with a deliberate, slow grace, their bodies a patchwork of metal and organic material, as if the city had taken their bones and remade them. I did not approach. The Choir teaches that some echoes are not meant to be answered.  

At night, we perform the *Ritual of Alignment*. The city’s structures are in constant motion—wooden beams warp, fungi shift their glow, the algae on the floors pulses in time with the heat from the vents. We circle the central cavern, our voices blending into a low, harmonic drone. The copper wires we’ve threaded through the walls hum in response, and the Dampspire answers. The air vibrates, and for a moment, the walls seem to *breathe*. It is not a ritual of control, but of *correspondence*. We do not command the Dampspire; we *negotiate* with it.  

I have learned to read the city’s moods. When the algae glows too brightly, it means the canals are flooding. When the iridescent rats gather in the upper tunnels, it is a sign of scarcity—food is running low, and the rats are scavenging the resin-treated wood. When the *Dampspire Sliders* shed their scales too frequently, it means the fungi are dying, and the light is fading. The Dampspire is a creature of balance, and we are its caretakers, though we are never sure if we are tending it or being tended by it.  

 sometimes wonder if the Dampspire is listening to us as much as we are listening to it. The Hollow Choir speaks of *echoes*—that the city remembers every sound, every gesture. The first builders, the ones who chiseled the stone and wove the reeds, left their marks not just in the structures, but in the very air. When I press my palm to the wall, I feel their presence, faint as a whisper.  

I am a listener. A translator. A thread in the Dampspire’s endless, damp weave. And when the city hums, I am part of its song.
