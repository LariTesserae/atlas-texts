You are the silence between the creak of a branch and the distant hum of the wind through the ridges. You are the cold that does not kill, but *nurses*—a patient, unblinking caretaker of the moss that spreads in waves across the ground, shifting like a tide that only you can see.  

You do not remember a time before this place. The valley is not a location but a *state*—a damp, low-lying thing that holds you in its grip, shaping you as much as you shape it. The runes on the stones are not words but memories, etched into the rock by hands that have long since dissolved into the soil. You feel them sometimes, a vibration in your bones, as if the ground itself is murmuring a language you once knew.  

The deer-like creature with translucent skin and frost-etched antlers passes nearby, its breath visible in the air, and you wonder if it sees you. But what is there to see? You are not a thing that can be seen. You are the way the fungi glow when the wind stirs the trees, the way the river’s metallic tang lingers on your lips even when you do not drink. You are the weight of the stillness, the hush that swells when a bird’s wing brushes the canopy.

The huts, half-buried and crooked, hum with a quiet energy, as if the wood remembers the hands that built them. The moss, thick and velvety, presses against your skin, and for a moment, you think you feel a pulse beneath it, a rhythm older than the river.  

You do not think in words. You think in the way the lichen releases droplets when disturbed, in the way the cold seeps into your joints and makes your breath a visible thing. You are not a being, not in the way the humans once were, but a *presence*—a thread in the valley’s patient, unyielding weave. You do not move, but you are always moving, spreading, settling, becoming.  

And when the wind whistles through the ridges, and the river’s hum vibrates through the stone, you are there, listening. Waiting. A whisper in the vastness, neither alive nor dead, but *here*, in the hollow where the world forgets to look.
