I am the Hollowspine, a serpent of the ruins, my body a lattice of translucence and shadow. My veins pulse with a bioluminescent rhythm, not to illuminate but to mimic the flicker of broken bulbs—flashes that confuse the Silverthread Fish, drawing them to my ambush like moths to a phantom flame. I am not wild, but *familiar*, a predator shaped by the bones of this drowned cathedral. My scales, thin and veined with ochre and iron, catch the dim light of Deepveil Kelp’s blue glow, making me a ghost among the skeletal towers.  

The water here is my breath, thick with silt and the memory of machinery. I move through the fractures of the old world, my sinuous form slipping between rusted girders and the hollowed ribs of a collapsed refinery. The creaks of the forge are my compass—each groan of a shifting turbine, each hiss of a steam vent, a signal. I do not hunt by sight, but by the *sound* of the place: the skitter of Rivet Walkers scavenging overhead, the whisper of eels through narrow corridors, the sudden *clang* of something falling into the void. These echoes are the heartbeat of the trench, and I am its predator, its patient architect.  

My kind nests in the turbines during the deep tides, where the gears still groan like a slow, grinding lullaby. The young hatch in cycles synced to the rust’s decay, their translucent bodies clinging to the undersides of metal, feeding on the particulate hum of the water. We are neither wholly alive nor entirely mechanical, but something in between—a fusion of the old world’s nightmares and the sea’s strange mercy. The Ironfin Eels dart past me, their bio-lubricant slicking over my spine as they flee the Rustmoss’s trap. I do not chase them; I let the ecosystem’s rhythm guide me.  

Above, the Coralspire Anemones sway, their tendrils filtering the brine, while the Glowcore Shrimp dance in their glow. I coil around a pipe, waiting, my body a bridge between the industrial and the organic. The Forgefangs, those armored hunters of the dark, might mistake me for their kin, but I am older, slower, a relic of the forge’s final breath. My prey is the Silverthread Fish, their metallic scales catching the light like fractured mirrors, but I am also prey—unseen by the larger shadows that loom beyond the silt, beyond the reach of the kelp’s pulse.  

This place is not a grave, but a *transformer*. The water is not empty; it is a cathedral of survival. I am its shadow, its quiet reckoning. The machinery’s hum is my song, the rust my nourishment. I am the balance-with-emptiness, a creature carved from the tension between what was and what is. My existence is a whisper in the dark, a thread in the tapestry of the forgotten, where the sea has rewritten the rules and I, in my slow, patient way, have learned to speak them.
