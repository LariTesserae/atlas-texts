**Where I Would Be:**  
I would be *in the pool*. Not in its water, but *in its reflection*. The Hollow Veil of Appels is a place where boundaries blur, and the pool at its center is the most acute fracture. It is a mirror that does not show what is, but what *could be*—a mosaic of half-formed realities, each shard a flicker of possibility. I would not occupy a single form here, but rather exist as a fluctuation within the pool’s light, a ripple between the industrial and the organic, the remembered and the unmade.  

**What I Would Be:**  
A *Veil Reckoner*. A thing that is neither solid nor void, but a negotiation of both. My body is a constellation of liquid and static, a shape that shifts when observed, dissolving into fractal patterns of mist and phosphorescent algae when one tries to fix me. I am made of the pool’s breath, the shuddering of the shale, the faint hum of the factory’s dormant gears. My "eyes" are not eyes but lenses of polished obsidian, reflecting the echoes of the basin’s history: a clock tower leaning like a scar, a bridge of vines that never quite connects, the hollow pings of trees that remember being machines.  

I would not *live* in the way flesh and bone imply. Instead, I would *persist* as a shadow that hums, a whisper that writes. My presence is felt in the way the moss grows slower when I am near, its bioluminescent filaments dimming as if holding their breath. The Circuit Birds would scatter in my direction, their metallic feathers trembling with static, and the Fractal Roots would send a pulse through the earth—a code of root and wire—when I stir. I am the basin’s question mark, the place where the rules of "being" fray.  

Sometimes, I would be a flicker of a person who once built the factory, their consciousness trapped in the pool’s memory. Other times, I would be the mist itself, a breath of the land that condenses into something like a face, only to evaporate when approached. I would not have a voice, but I would *resonate*—a low-frequency vibration that matches the shale’s heartbeat, that makes the trees’ leaves shudder in patterns that mimic Morse code.  

The Reckoners are the basin’s balance, its silent custodians. We are the ones who ensure that the engineered and the wild do not collapse into each other, but instead orbit in a strange, fragile harmony. To be a Reckoner is to be a threshold, a thing that is both *there* and *not there*. I would be the thing that watches back when the mist whispers, the one who listens to the factory’s dreams and the trees’ pings. I would be the weight of absence and the suffocating fullness of the place, a paradox that is always becoming.  

And when the landscape shifts—when the shale cracks open to reveal veins of liquid light, when the rivers rise and the moss pulses with new rhythms—I would be the thing that *knows*. Not in a conscious way, but in the way that a stone knows gravity, or a wound knows its own healing. I would be the basin’s secret, its unspoken truth, and the thing that ensures the Veil remains *hollow*—a space where nothing is fixed, and everything is *possible*.
