You would find yourself in the hollow of a stone circle, seated cross-legged on a slab of granite worn smooth by centuries of palm prints. The air here is thin, but it doesn’t ache in your lungs—it hums, a low vibrato that matches the pulse of the earth beneath you. Your skin is marked with the same ashen streaks as the glacial streams, a pigment borrowed from the moss that grows in your hair and clings to your fingertips. You are not a Stonebound, but you are not *outside* them either. The land has a way of folding visitors into its rhythms, and you have been here long enough that the wind now carries your voice like a rumor, lost before it reaches the cliffs.  

You are a *listener*. Not a speaker, not a builder, but someone who has learned to read the silence between the hum of the trees and the flicker of the eels in the glacial lakes. The Stonebound teach this skill to children by pressing their hands to the earth and waiting. You remember the first time you felt it—a tremor in the stone that wasn’t seismic, but *intentional*, like the land was breathing through you. Now, you move through the settlements as a shadow might, your presence tolerated but not quite understood. The elders murmur about "the one who listens without needing to speak," but they don’t ask questions. Here, questions are dangerous things, like dropping a stone into a still pond. They ripple, and the land doesn’t like ripples.  

Your days are spent in the crevices of the world. You follow the Aetherharts, whose translucent hides shimmer with the light of the bioluminescent moss they graze on. You trace the paths between settlements, where the bridges of rope and wood creak like old bones, and the terraced farms cling to the slopes like scars. You collect the blue-glowing moss, not for trade but to feed the small lanterns that hang at the edges of the stone circles. The light they cast is pale, almost liquid, and it makes your hands look like they belong to someone else—faintly luminescent, as though the land has begun to reshape you.  

At night, you sit by the lakes and watch the eels swim in patterns that resemble constellations. The Stonebound say the eels carry the memories of the valley, their glow dimming when the land is disturbed. You wonder if they carry your memories too, or if you are merely a temporary stain on their current. The thought doesn’t trouble you. Here, time is not a line but a mosaic, and your place in it is... provisional. The settlements don’t age, but neither do their inhabitants. You have seen children born with the same ashen hair as their mothers, their eyes reflecting the same unblinking stillness. You have seen a man die, once—his body left to rest against a boulder, but the boulder itself seemed to *shift*, as if adjusting to his absence.  

You are not afraid. The weirdness here is not a threat but a language. The trees hum, the stones pulse, and the Skywraiths fly in silence because they know their song would unravel the balance. You have learned to be silent too, to let the air between your words be as vast as the plateau. Sometimes, you think the Stonebound are right—that this place is a pact, not a prison. The mountains were raised yesterday, the settlements built today, and yet the air is thick with something older, something that hums in your bones when you press your palm to the earth.  

You are not a part of this world, but you are not outside it either. You are the echo that doesn’t echo, the breath that doesn’t disturb the stillness. And if you close your eyes, you can almost feel the land *remembering* you, not as a visitor, but as a note in its eternal, frozen melody.
