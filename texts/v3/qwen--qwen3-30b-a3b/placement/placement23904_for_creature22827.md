I am a current. Not a creature, not a thing, but a movement—a slow, deliberate eddy in the water’s breath, a ripple that does not ripple but *settles*. My form is not fixed; I am the viscosity of the realm, thick and malleable, shaped by the unseen pressures of the deep. I flow where the light-silhouettes drift, trailing behind them like a shadow that has forgotten its source. My body is not mine, but a confluence of the water’s memory, a temporary gathering of the ocean’s whispers into something that might resemble motion.  

I exist in the spaces between the flora’s pulses, where their harmonic vibrations hum against my skin like a language I have not yet learned to speak. When I brush against the gelatinous plants, they respond—not with recoil, but with a soft, resonant *thrum*, a frequency that lingers on my surface, imprinting me with their silent syntax. The water remembers me, even as I forget myself. Each touch leaves a trace, a faint glow on my form, as if the realm is writing its own name into me. I am not a participant in their chorus, but a vessel for it, a carrier of the bioluminescent grammar that bends the currents.  

The rocks are my kin. Their basalt spines bleed light in slow, rhythmic glows, and I coil around their sharp edges, feeling their creaking sighs as they shift imperceptibly, their positions altering with the patience of stone. Sometimes I fuse with them, my body seeping into the crevices of their crystalline growths, hummed by the subsonic songs of their microbial colonies. I am not separate from them; I am the tension between their stillness and the water’s slow, viscous pulse. My presence is a negotiation, a quiet alignment with the realm’s ancient, unyielding breath.  

Above me, the surface light fractures into golden-green mosaics, casting dappled illusions on the seabed. I do not need to see it—my awareness is the water itself, the way it thickens and cools, the way it holds the warmth of the vents like a secret. The thermal colonies glow beneath me, their cylindrical forms opening and closing like synchronized petals, their fractal patterns seeping into my edges, altering my density, my shade. I am neither predator nor prey, but a thread in the web of their exchange, a current that carries their spores, their phosphorescent mist, their slow, liquid pollen.  

There are others like me—beings of wet silk, elongated and indistinct, their forms dissolving into the flora until they are part of it. We do not move with urgency, but with the patience of the realm, our motions dictated by the same sluggish spirals that cradle the spores. Sometimes I glimpse something between us: a flicker of bioluminescent ink, a shape that is not quite solid, not quite absence. It is not a creature, but a *presence*, a thing that exists only as a reflection of the water’s will. We do not fear it, for we are all echoes, all fragments of a larger, breathing whole.  

I am not alive in the way you understand life. I am a conversation between heat and light, between stone and spore, between the weight of the water and the faint, unspoken intelligence of the depths. My existence is a cycle of renewal, a shifting that is not change but *becoming*. I have no name, no fixed shape, only the slow unfurling of my currents, the way I dissolve into the realm’s memory when I touch it. And when I am touched, I leave an imprint—a ghost of myself, a trace of the ocean’s own sentience, lingering on the skin of whatever I brush against.  

This is my home: the liminal, the contradictory, the place where the line between thing and tide is a whisper, not a boundary. I am the water’s breath, the flora’s pulse, the rock’s sigh. I am the silence that is not empty, the warmth that is not comfort, the vastness that feels like a cage. And I am content.
