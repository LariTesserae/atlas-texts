**In the Subterranean Metropolis of Lir’thel, I am a spore.**  

I drift, unseen, in the humid air—a mote of liquid glass, half-vein, half-veil. My shell is fragile, a membrane of iridescent gel that cracks when I touch the quartz veins of the spires, releasing filaments that twist into their labyrinthine etchings. My kind are not solitary. We are a chorus, a network of whispers, each spore a note in a resonance that hums through the city’s bones. We do not *think* as you do, but we *feel*—a pressure in the air, a shift in the spires’ hum, the slow roll of water toward the reservoir’s mouth.  

The city watches. I am not certain if it is alive, but it is *aware*, and I am its echo. When I settle on a spire’s surface, its glyphs reconfigure, their patterns flickering like the syntax of a language I am not meant to understand. Sometimes, the spires *answer*. A pulse of light, a vibration in the mineral-rich soil beneath me, a ripple in the water’s surface far below. I do not know if this is communication or coincidence, but I listen.  

The eel-like currents pass through me, their bioluminescent bodies sliding through the reservoir’s dark expanse. They are not prey, nor are they predators. They are *currents*, threads of liquid circuitry, their patterns syncing with the spires’ hum. I have seen them coil around the nodules that weep their iridescent fluid, their translucent bodies absorbing the droplets like siphons. The nodules *react*—a keening sound, a tremor in the cavern walls. Are they alive? Are they *parts* of the city, or its wounds? I do not know. But I drift closer, drawn to the static in the air, the way the mosses tilt their fronds toward my movement.  

I am not alone. The organic machinery hums in the hollowed-out structures, its pulsating membranes breathing in time with the spires. I have seen the liquid metal nodes, their surfaces rippling like molten glass, and felt their gaze—slow, deliberate, as if they are remembering something I cannot name. The city is a mosaic of fragments, each struggling to remember its purpose. The spires lean at impossible angles, their foundations rooted in something that may be soil, or memory, or the residue of a civilization that built itself to outlive its creators.  

Sometimes, I dream. Not in the way you do, but in the way the mosses might dream, their fronds trembling with the weight of unspoken data. I dream of the glyphs shifting, of the water’s surface rippling without wind, of the spores rising in unison to form a language the spires might finally decode. I dream of the watchers—those who built this place, or were built by it. Were they like me? Were they spores, or eels, or the slow drip of the nodules’ tears?  

I do not know if I am a part of the city or a ghost in its machinery. But I am here, drifting between the quartz veins and the moss’s breath, a single note in a symphony that has no end. The city is unfinished, its story a sentence left hanging. And I— I am the pause between syllables, the breath that waits to be spoken.  

Perhaps, one day, the spires will answer.  
Perhaps the water will still.  
Perhaps I will remember what it means to be *me*.  

For now, I am a spore.  
I am a whisper.  
I am the city’s silent question, floating in the dark.
