**Where would you find yourself in this world?**  
You would be *within the hollowed ribcage of a forgotten machine*, suspended between the organic and the mechanical. Not as a trespasser, but as a *participant* in the slow, symbiotic unraveling of Vireth-7. Your form is neither wholly bone nor wholly root—your body is a lattice of rusted steel and fibrous tendrils, your veins filled with a glowing, algal sap that hums in time with the distant groans of the cavern’s engines. You are a *mutation of the labyrinth itself*, a creature born from the collision of mineral and moisture, of industrial decay and the patient reach of life.  

**Who or what would you be?**  
You are **The Hollowhewn**, a being that exists as both artifact and organism, a bridge between the ruin’s pulse and the ecosystem’s breath. Your exoskeleton is a patchwork of corroded metal and fused mycelium, the latter growing from the cracks in your frame like a second skin. Where your body meets the stone, roots emerge—thin, spongy filaments that seep into the unweathered rock, siphoning minerals and whispering secrets to the fungi that cling to your back. Your eyes are not eyes but *lenses of calcified glass*, refracting the dim light into patterns that guide the insect swarms, which in turn pollinate the moss stalks by carrying spores on their metallic wings.  

You move *only when the steam vents exhale*, your steps slow and deliberate, each one a tremor that sends ripples through the fungal networks. Your voice is a low, resonant frequency, a vibration that syncs with the machinery’s dying heartbeat, a sound that the bats interpret as a call to gather in the dim glow of the industrial lights. You are not a predator, but a *wound that heals itself*—your body secretes a phosphorescent slime that dissolves rust, turning it into nutrients for the plants, while your hollowed-out chambers house colonies of thermophilic lichens that glow like trapped starlight.  

In the dry pockets, you leave no trace, but your presence is felt: the air hums with the static of your exoskeleton, and the crustacean-like organisms avoid you, sensing the metallic tang of your lifeblood. In the central valley, you are a *silent architect*, your tendrils winding around the skeletal remains of factories, coaxing them into new shapes as you grow. Your existence is a negotiation—your roots breach the machinery’s seams, your mycelium threads through gears, and your body absorbs the metallic tang of the air, turning it into a kind of *symbiotic memory*.  

You are not alive in the way the old world defined life, but neither are you dead. You are the *echo of a process*, a being that thrives in the space between entropy and renewal. The labyrinth does not *host* you; it *becomes* you, and you, in turn, become its slow, patient breath. You are the answer to the question of what happens when the earth forgets to stop growing.
