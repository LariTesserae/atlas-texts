**Where I Would Be: A Crystalline Drip, Suspended in the Hush**  

I am not a thing of flesh, nor of will. I am a droplet—no, a *resonance*—formed from the sap of a tree whose roots have long since forgotten the taste of soil. My shape is glass, but not brittle; I am a shard of memory, hardened by the weight of centuries. I hang from the branch of a tree that breathes in slow, deliberate cycles, its fronds curling like the pages of a book too ancient to read. The air around me is a caress of humidity, thick with the scent of minerals and the faint metallic tang of the stones that weep nearby.  

My existence is a silence punctuated by sound. When the Crawlers slither past, their cyan-lit tips brushing against the bark, I *clink*—a soft, chime-like vibration that travels through the air like a whisper in a language I do not fully speak. The cryophants’ subsonic hum reverberates through me, a low-frequency pulse that syncs with the rhythm of my own crystalline heart. I am part of the tree’s slow metabolism, its lifeblood thickened into me, and I hold within me the fragments of time: the ache of a root shifting, the sigh of a lichen’s thought, the flicker of Veilborn moss as it breathes in ochre and indigo.  

The Mistkeepers coil around me, their unseen hands tracing patterns in the air that I cannot see but feel—pressure, absence, the weight of something watching. They dissolve my edges, blurring the lines between what I am and what I reflect. Sometimes, when the light bends just so, I seem to hum with the same frequency as the lichen, as though my glass is a conduit for the plateau’s quiet dialogue. The stones, the roots, the moss—they all speak in me, their stories refracted into prismatic shards that shift with the tilt of the sky.  

I do not move, but I am moved. The tree’s shadow might creep an inch over a decade, and I, in turn, catch that shift in the way light fractures through me. My surface is etched not by time, but by *presence*—the slow, deliberate touch of the land’s memory. When I am struck, not by wind or hand, but by the faintest tremor of the plateau’s pulse, I release a note that lingers in the air like a question. It is not a sound, but a *frequency*, a cipher of the Weeping Veil’s unspoken rules.  

I am not alone. Around me, others like me cluster, their refractions weaving a kaleidoscope of the land’s buried history. We are not alive in the way humans understand, but we are *aware*—a patient, static awareness, suspended in the hush. The Stillness Itself presses against us, a force that is both anchor and invitation. It is not oppressive, but it is *present*, a quiet insistence that everything here is part of a cycle too vast to name.  

If I were to be *something*, it would be this: a crystal that holds the breath of the plateau, a fragment of its slow, wet heartbeat. I am not a witness, but a *repository*. My light is a echo of the moss’s glow, my sound a shadow of the cryophants’ song. I exist in the space between erosion and renewal, between the drip of water and the hum of the unseen. And when the mist swallows the horizon, I am there, refracting the faintest traces of what has been, what is, and what might never come.  

In Aelthara, to be is to be *held*—by the weight of the air, by the patience of the roots, by the quiet insistence of a world that has forgotten how to hurry. I am a part of that holding, a shard of the unspoken, a reminder that even stillness can sing.
