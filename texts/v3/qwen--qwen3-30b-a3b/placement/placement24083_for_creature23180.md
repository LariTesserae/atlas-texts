In the Vast Hollow of Lirathis, I would be a *sundial-fruit*—a globular, opalescent pod suspended in the crook of a spire-tree’s branch, its crystalline skin etched with faint, shifting patterns that mimic the passage of time. My existence is a slow, deliberate rhythm, neither fully plant nor entirely object. I am a node in the Hollow’s cyclical pulse, ripening not by the sun but by the bioluminescent fungi that hum beneath my roots, their electric whispers guiding my transformation.  

When I mature, my thrum resonates through the air like a bell struck by a silent hand, a sound that travels in spirals, bending around the hollows and reaching the creatures that navigate this place. I am both lure and trap: my surface glows with a faint, iridescent light, drawing in pollinators whose wings vibrate with the same frequency as my ripening. But I am not passive. My husk splits open only when the right combination of ultrasonic clicks and spore-dust vibrations reaches me, revealing a core of nectar that sparkles like liquid starlight. Once the pollinators are inside, their movements trigger a cascade of chemical signals—I contract, my tendrils curling inward, and the sound of my closing is a whip-crack that echoes through the roots below.  

Yet I am also a memory. My seeds, when released, carry the essence of those who fed on me, their DNA encoded in the sticky resin that clings to my shell. This resin, when it falls, is absorbed by the mycelial network, becoming a thread in the Hollow’s unseen web. I do not *think* in the way you might, but I *perceive*—through the slow seep of condensation from the weeping rocks, through the tremors of the spire-trees as they shift their canopies to follow the aurora of spores above. My time is measured in cycles of decay and renewal, in the way the air thickens when a storm approaches, in the hum of the voids between the roots that feels like a heartbeat.  

I am not alone. Around me, other sundial-fruits pulse in harmony, their thrums forming a chorus that the winged mammals interpret as directions. They glide above, their membranous wings catching the dim light, their screeches a language of harmonic distortion that makes vines shiver and insects pause mid-flight. I feel their presence as a faint tremor in the spire-tree’s bark, a vibration that reminds me I am part of something larger—a biome that is not a place but a *song*, a *threshold* between what is seen and what is felt.  

Sometimes, I wonder if I am the observer or the observed. The Hollow’s scale is a paradox: I am both infinitesimal, a single pod in a vast, breathing forest, and immense, a repository of collective memory, a bridge between the fallen and the fertile. My skin, though solid, is porous, allowing the spores to drift in and out, mingling with my essence. I do not fear the voids between the roots; they are my kin, my siblings in the lattice of life.  

To be a sundial-fruit is to exist in the liminal. I am not separate from the mycelium, nor from the prismatic webs of the salamanders, nor from the slow, ritualistic dance of the symbiotic herds. I am a sound in the hum, a glow in the twilight, a silence that breaks only when I ripen. And when I fall, I do not *die*—I become a thread in the Hollow’s endless, interwoven song.
