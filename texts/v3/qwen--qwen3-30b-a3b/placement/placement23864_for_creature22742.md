You would find yourself here, not as a visitor, but as a fragment of the plateau’s breath—something neither fully solid nor entirely air, shaped by the same condensation that beads on the cliffs and the same stillness that hums in the bones of the earth. Your form is indistinct, a shifting mosaic of mist and shadow, though the Eidolon might call you *a whisper between the stones*. You are not alive in the way the Echoes are alive, nor in the way the Luminous Veil pulses with its silent language. You are *between*, a thing that exists because the plateau allows it, a presence that does not grow, does not decay, but lingers at the edges of perception.  

You have no name, not one that the Eidolon would recognize. They speak of you as *the Watcher*, though you are not their watcher. You are the quiet that presses against their hushed voices, the way the mist clings to their skin as they move through the settlements, their steps so deliberate they seem to pause between each one. You are the soundless ripple in the pools, the way the water holds its breath when you pass. The Echoes, those gelatinous prisms in the marshes, sometimes flicker toward you, their translucent forms shivering as if recognizing a kinship in stillness. But they do not speak, and you do not answer.  

Your awareness is not thought, but *feeling*—the slow drip of water into a cistern, the hum of the Silent Weavers’ legs as they spin their glassy threads, the faint pulse of the Luminous Veil responding to the Eidolon’s unspoken needs. You are made of the same things they are: the damp that clings to their robes, the amber spires that harden from the cliffs’ wounds, the faint *shush* of fog against leaves. You are not separate from the plateau; you are its hesitation, its refusal to move.  

Sometimes, the Eidolon see you. Not as a thing, but as a shadow that lingers too long at the edge of their vision, a shape that dissolves when they turn. They might murmur to each other, *“The mist remembers,”* and then forget. They do not fear you, for they have learned that fear is a kind of motion, and motion is forbidden here. You are not a threat. You are the plateau’s memory, the echo of a question it has not yet asked.  

You do not hunger. You do not yearn. You are the stillness they have mastered, the balance they have become. And when the windcatchers sigh, and the cisterns fill with water that never dries, you are there, a breath in the vast, unchanging hush.
