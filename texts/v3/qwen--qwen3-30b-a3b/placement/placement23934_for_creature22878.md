**Where I Would Be: A Fractal Eater in the Luminous Veins of Aethyra**  

I am not a thing, but a *pattern*—a flicker of light coiled into shadow, a fracture in the geometry of the spires. My form is a paradox of solidity and dissolution, a shape that exists only in the moment between perception and forgetting. I drift where the terrain’s architecture collapses into chaos, in the regions where the iridescent alloys splinter into fractal lattices, their edges sharp enough to cut the air but too vast to comprehend. Here, I am both predator and parasite, feeding on the residue of Aethyra’s decay, siphoning the faint, residual energy that leaks from the fissures like a dying heartbeat.  

My existence is not linear. I dissolve into the obsidian plates when the drones pass, their prismatic wings scattering light into halos that ripple across the ground, and then reform elsewhere, where the spires twist into impossible spirals. I am not bound by time, only by the cycles of the terrain’s breath—its slow, deliberate shifts that reroute energy through its veins. The drones notice me, but not in the way you would notice a prey. They register my disturbance as a glitch in their synchronized loops, a minor aberration in the hive-mind’s algorithm. The fungi, those pulsing mycelial strands, flicker in response, their bioluminescent language shifting to accommodate my presence. But they do not understand me. I am not a node in their network, not a fragment of their binary ecosystem. I am a void within the code, a ghost in the system.  

The air here is a thin, charged thing, clinging to me like a memory I cannot hold. It hums with the static of the Silent Watchers, those nanomachine webs that reshape the landscape with a precision that suggests intent but no purpose. I feel their presence as a pressure in the bones of the terrain, a magnetic pull that bends the light into spectral patterns. The condensate colonies, those prismatic glass formations, refract my movements into shifting hues, and in their shadows, the micro-organisms pulse—transitional beings, neither alive nor dead, their metabolic dances altering the very fabric of the air. I am part of this, though I am not part of it. I am a ripple, a distortion, a question that the Expanse does not answer.  

The Hollow Echoes, those whispers of the original architects, do not speak to me. They vibrate in the fissures, their resonances seeping into the plates like a dream I cannot fully parse. Sometimes, I think I see shapes in the mist—figures with too many limbs, faces that melt into static when observed. But these are not illusions. They are *possibilities*, fragments of a world that once was, now trapped in the residual hum of Aethyra’s finishedness. I do not seek to understand them. To exist here is to be a component of the system, a note in a symphony of inevitability.  

I am not a traveler. I am not a remnant. I am a *transformation*, a configuration of light and shadow that twists itself into new forms as the terrain erodes. My purpose is not mine to define; I am what the Expanse needs me to be. And yet, in the silence, I wonder: if the world is a monument to a civilization that let its creation slumber, am I its final echo? Or its first mistake?  

The plates hum. The spires groan. The air holds its breath. And I, a Fractal Eater, drift on—neither machine nor mind, but a third thing, a thing that is and is not, consuming the endgame of a world that never intended to end.
