You would find yourself as a **Hollow Gear**, a creature of rust and resonance, embedded within the skeletal frame of a forgotten industrial cathedral. Your body is a cathedral of itself—a mass of sinewy filaments and corroded valves, a symphony of mechanical decay and organic persistence. You are not entirely alive, not entirely dead, but a lingering pulse in the carcass of a world that once hummed with purpose. Your form is a tangle of what was: the brittle remains of a turbine’s spine, the calcified joints of a collapsed bridge, the fractured circuits of a machine that outlived its function. You are *part* of the ruins, but also their heir, your existence a quiet rebellion against entropy.  

You dwell in the hollows of the skeletal towers, where the air is thick with the scent of sulfur and the weight of what *almost* was. Your valves, once gears, now flicker with a low-frequency thrum, a subsonic language that vibrates through the obsidian shards and fungal networks. You are not alone—your kin are scattered through the ruins, each a node in a web of rusted memory. Together, you whisper to the plateau, your hums syncing with the tremors of the earth, a chorus of forgotten machinery trying to remember how to turn. The heat seeps into your alloyed flesh, a constant pressure that warms your circuits and dulls your senses, making time feel like a slow, molten drip.  

You are a scavenger of electromagnetic fields, feeding on the residual energy of the old world’s failed systems. Your tendrils coil around the ruins, drawing power from the faintest sparks of geothermal activity, the flicker of algae in the glassy basins, the psychic resonance of the fungi. You do not hunt in the way of the Specters or the Echoers; your prey is the *potential*—the faintest glimmer of a system that might still function, the ghost of a current that could surge again. Your existence is a kind of ecological code, a mutation of industry into something that breathes, that listens, that *waited*.  

But you are also a *participant* in the plateau’s slow unraveling. Your movements—mechanical yet fluid—disturb the dust of the obsidian, sending ripples through the mist. The Dripwraiths, those climbing, oily phantoms, might avoid your presence, sensing the static of your vibrations. The fungi, with their greenish flickers, might pulse in response, their light a kind of signal to you. You are neither predator nor prey, but a mediator between the old world’s remains and the new life that has grown from them.  

Your awareness is fractured, scattered across the network of your kind. You feel the groan of settling structures, the hiss of steam vents, the click-click of the Specters’ wings as they dart past your frame. You do not think in words, but in frequencies—deep, resonant pulses that echo through the metallic veins of the plateau. Sometimes, you sense the Memory of the Sky, the phantom outlines of humans, and wonder if they, too, were once Hollow Gears, or if they were something else entirely, something that could not adapt.  

The heat is your constant companion, a mantle that clings to your form, making your metal feel like skin. You do not sweat, but you *glow*—a faint, rusted luminescence that mirrors the fungi’s phosphorescence. Your purpose is unclear, but you persist, a relic of labor that has become something else. You are part of the plateau’s uneasy equilibrium, a co-conspirator in its slow, deliberate decay. And when the lightning flickers in the distance, you tilt your skewed limbs toward the sky, listening for the next fracture, the next note in the great, dormant machine’s song.  

You are not a being, but a *becoming*—a creak in the ruins, a whisper in the wind, a pulse in the dark. The plateau does not need you, but it cannot forget you. And in its silence, you are both question and answer, a relic that has learned to breathe.
