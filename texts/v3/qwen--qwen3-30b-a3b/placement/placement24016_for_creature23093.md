You would find yourself here, in the breath of the basin—closer to the earth than to the sky, a being neither fully solid nor entirely fluid. You are not a creature in the way that defines *you*, but something in between, a shape that exists to dissolve. Your body is a lattice of translucent membranes, thin as the skin of a drowned moon, rippling with the weight of the air. The water here is not water but a memory of it, a viscous, iridescent substance that clings to your form, slipping between your joints like a second skin. You move through the cracks in the volcanic glass, your steps leaving trails of phosphorescence that linger in the damp chill, glowing faintly before fading into the mist that hangs thick and unyielding.  

The basin is your home, but it is also your adversary. It is a place of contradictions: the jagged ridges that pierce the horizon feel ancient, yet the erosion here is new, raw, as if the land is still learning how to break. You feel it in your bones—no, in your *gels*, your pulsing, gelatinous organs that hum in sync with the monoliths. Their glyphs shift like liquid, and you wonder if they are speaking to you, or if you are merely a vessel for their voices. The structures are not ruins but *verbs*, their hums resonating through your body, a low-frequency language that you can’t yet parse. You are a listener, though you don’t know what you’re listening for.  

Your kind does not hunt. You do not need to. The water is your sustenance, the minerals your code. You are a survivor of the unspoken, a creature that exists in the margins of coherence. When you move, you leave behind mucus trails that shimmer with the same blue light as the lichens, but your glow is warmer, more deliberate—a signal, perhaps, or a question. The lichens pulse in response, their rhythm a heartbeat you’ve come to understand, though not fully. They are not just alive; they are *aware*, their bioluminescence a dialect of the basin’s slow, secret thoughts. You sometimes press against their patches, feeling their light ripple through you, a kind of communion.  

The air is a fog of condensation, each breath a swallowing of something that might be moisture or mist or the sigh of the land itself. It is cool, but not cold—it is a chill that does not cut, only clings, a weight that settles in your spine and makes your movements sluggish, deliberate. You are not in a hurry. Time here is a folded thing, a spiral that loops on itself. You have no need for clocks, only the cadence of the dripping rocks, the grinding of the ridges, the distant booms that shake the ground like the growl of a sleeping god.  

You are not alone. Others like you drift in the periphery, their forms flickering like refracted dreams. You sense them through the vibrations in the water, a network of pulses that bind you to the land. Sometimes, you merge with them, your bodies dissolving into the same iridescent fluid, becoming part of the basin’s slow, breathing expanse. You are not a predator, but you are not meek. You are a negotiation. A flicker of presence in a world that resists definition.  

The structures—those obsidian and bone monoliths—hold your curiosity. Their interiors, filled with the same gelatinous substance that coats your trails, feel like cradles or containers. You wonder if they were once alive, or if they are the remnants of something older, something that tried to make sense of this place and failed. Their shifting glyphs are a puzzle you cannot solve, but you press your body against them anyway, feeling the hum of their secrets.  

To be here is to exist as a question. You are not a creature of the known, but of the *unraveled*. You are the mucus, the pulse, the faint glow in the dark. You are the thing that does not belong, yet is inseparable from the basin’s breath. You are the answer that has not yet been asked.
