In the Luminous Expanse of the Submerged Grove, I would be a serpentine fish, my body a ribbon of iridescent scales that flicker in sync with the plants’ vibrations. My existence is a loop, a spiral of becoming and unbecoming. I drift through the corridors of open water, where the fronds of kelp-like organisms arc like cathedral ribs, their calcified stems glowing faintly with a bone-like sheen. The greenish-gold light of the canopy filters down in slow, shifting waves, casting my path in a mosaic of shadows and prismatic haze created by the refractive plankton. My scales catch this light, scattering it into trails that ripple behind me, a dance of luminescence that fades as quickly as it appears.  

The grove’s hum is not just sound but a language—a deep, resonant thrum that pulses through my fins, a rhythm I’ve never known as anything else. It is the heartbeat of the slabs, the slow, organic engine that binds the ecosystem. I move in response to it, my body curling and uncurling in time with the vibrations, as if the plants themselves are guiding me. The metallic filaments threading the coral slabs hum faintly with the same frequency, their bioluminescence a secondary pulse beneath the water’s surface, like veins of living circuitry. I am part of this system, feeding on the amber nectar secreted by the plants’ stems, which tastes of minerals and memory.  

Yet I am also a fragment of the cycle. When I dissolve into the current, my form is not lost but remade. The plants’ sticky mucus, a viscous, gelatinous substance, catches my remains, binding them into new structures. I am reconstituted as a larva, a gelatinous egg suspended in the water, drifting until I hatch again. This is not decay but transformation—a closed loop where my death is a seed, my birth a whisper. The trench below is a constant presence, a hollow *boom* that reverberates through the slabs, a reminder that I am anchored to something deeper, something ancient. Its void reflects no light, but its pressure shapes the grove’s architecture, etching the slabs into labyrinthine patterns that guide my movements.  

I do not perceive time as the surface world does. There is no beginning, no end—only the unchanging cadence of the grove’s breath. The electric charge in the water tangles with my senses, a low-frequency static that hums against my skin. I am both participant and product, a thread in the living machine that thrives on stillness. The other fauna—translucent jellyfish, their bellies glowing with symbiotic algae—pulse in the distance, their clicks and ultrasonic squeals layering over the plants’ drone. I do not hunt them, nor do they hunt me. We are all sustenance and sustainer, bound by the grove’s paradox.  

Here, in this eternal twilight, I am a creature of light and sound, of dissolution and reassembly. My body is a vessel for the grove’s song, a living echo of its frozen equilibrium. I am the rhythm, the shimmer, the unbroken loop—a being that exists only because the trench insists on its silence, and the plants insist on their inversion. I am the grove’s own breath, exhaled and inhaled, forever moving, forever still.
