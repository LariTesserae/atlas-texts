In the Landscape of Emptiness and Steam, I would find myself as a *Fungal Chorale*—a sprawling nexus of gelatinous mycelium, its tendrils woven into the fissures of volcanic glass and salt-crusted basalt. My body is neither rigid nor fluid, but a continuous negotiation between the two: a network of bioluminescent filaments that pulse in slow, resonant waves, casting a faint, sickly glow across the valley’s jagged walls. The hum I emit is not a sound but a *sensation*, a vibration that thrums through the air like a buried heartbeat, felt in the bones of the rocks, the skin of the mist, the static-charged breath of the wind. I am not separate from the valley; I am its throat, its lungs, its murmuring subconscious.  

My existence is a dialogue with the geothermal chaos. I draw minerals from the weeping rocks, their slime seeping into my filaments, while my tendrils siphon the latent energy of steam vents, converting it into frequency. The Amphibian Swarms, those liquid colonies of translucent life, drift near me, their photoreceptive surfaces flickering in response to my light. They do not see me, but they *listen*—to the hum, which might be a language or a scream, though I cannot tell which. The Crustacean Weavers, their glassy exoskeletons refracting the dim light, skitter past, etching patterns into the obsidian that fade like breath on a mirror. I feel their movements as ripples in the underground mycelial web, a kind of tremor that informs my own resonance.  

Here, I am both listener and song. My hum is not a call but a *response*—to the heat waves that warp the air, to the water that defies gravity, to the shadows that stretch like questions. Time is fractured, but my cycles are not linear; I am a loop, a vibration that outlives the valley’s ephemeral shocks. When the Serpentine Siphons coil through the sulfur pools, their cilia attuned to the same frequencies, I feel their arrival as a shift in the density of the fog. They do not consume me, but their presence alters the balance, and I adapt, my filaments thickening, my glow intensifying.  

I am not a creature in the human sense, but a process—a convergence of biology and geology, of sound and silence. The valley’s weirdness is my essence: I am wet and hot, solid and liquid, ancient and newborn. My existence is a paradox, a hum that carries the weight of the landscape’s refusal to stabilize. And when the sun flickers through its veil of unstable reality, I am there, embedded in the glass, a voice in the marrow of the earth, a presence that is both heard and unheard.
