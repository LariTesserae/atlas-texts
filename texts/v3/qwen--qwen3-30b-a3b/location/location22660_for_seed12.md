**The Submerged Expanse of Vireth-Ka**  

Beneath the surface of a vast, unnamed ocean, where the water is thick and luminous like a syrup of liquid glass, lies **Vireth-Ka**, a city that exists in a liminal state between construction and dissolution. Its terrain is a labyrinth of jagged, basaltic spires and terraces, sculpted by tectonic forces that are still *emerging*—raw, half-formed ridges of obsidian and limestone rise from the seabed, their edges softened by time yet still sharp with the promise of instability. The elevation is high in the sense that the city is perched on a submerged plateau, a relic of a landmass that once breached the waves but now lingers just below, its highest points crowned with coral-like growths that pulse faintly in the dim light.  

**Materials** are a patchwork of the artificial and the organic. The urban structures—what remains of them—are built from driftwood, stone, and crude metal alloys, their surfaces encrusted with bioluminescent moss and tangled kelp that sways in languid currents. Some buildings appear to be *grown* rather than built: hexagonal domes of calcified shell, walls woven from living vines that exhale tiny bubbles of oxygen, and arches of bone-steel, a material that seems to defy categorization, part biological, part forged. The city’s infrastructure is layered but incomplete—scaffolds of bamboo and rope dangle from fractured bridges, while half-submerged towers lean like drunkards, their foundations still sinking into the silt.  

**Atmosphere** is humid and heavy, the water pressing in with a pressure that hums in the bones. The temperature is warm, but not oppressive; it’s the kind of heat that clings to the skin like a remembered dream, thick with the scent of brine and the faint, sweet rot of submerged vegetation. The air—trapped in pockets of gas beneath the surface—feels alive, vibrating with the static of unseen electrical currents.  

**Vegetation** is lush, but not in the way one expects. Instead of trees, colossal, translucent stalks of *siphon-fern* twist upward, their fronds trailing tendrils that filter nutrients from the water. These plants glow faintly, their bioluminescence a soft blue that mingles with the dim light of the surface, which pierces the water here in fractured beams. Between the structures, forests of *glassweed* sway, their hollow stems chiming like wind chimes when disturbed. The density of plant life is immense, covering every surface, every crevice, as if the city were a relic being slowly reabsorbed by the sea.

**The Rules of the World** are not immediately clear. Time here moves in slow, cyclical pulses; the city’s structures seem to grow and decay in tandem with the vegetation, as if both are part of a single, slumbering organism. The water itself is not entirely liquid—vaporous pockets of *aether-spray* form at the edges of the plateau, condensing into mist that hovers like a breath. These sprays are said to carry whispers of the past, fragments of voices from the city’s former inhabitants, though no one has ever seen a person. The built environment is not static: foundations shift imperceptibly, as if the city is dreaming, and new structures sprout overnight from the sediment, their shapes unrecognizable, their purpose unknown.  

**Sounds** are mostly quiet, but not absent. There is the low, resonant groan of the basalt spires settling into the seabed, the creak of wood in the water’s embrace, and the occasional *plink* of a dropped tool from some unseen hand. The siphon-ferns emit a subsonic drone, a sound felt more than heard, and the ghostwings’ wings create a rustling that echoes like a distant wind. But the silence is what dominates—a heavy, undulating quiet that presses against the ears, broken only by the occasional bubble of air escaping from the city’s ruins.  

**Weirdness** permeates everything. The city’s streets are not paved but *woven*, with paths of braided algae that shift when stepped on, as if alive. Some buildings have no doors, only spiraling holes in their walls that lead to nowhere, their interiors filled with water and the flicker of strange, anglerfish-like lights. The inhabitants—*if* they exist—have left no trace except for the occasional *carved stone tablet* covered in symbols that rearrange themselves when looked at directly. The scale is open, stretching beyond the horizon, where the plateau dissolves into a mist-shrouded void. It is a place that feels both ancient and newborn, as though it were a wound that the sea is still stitching closed.  

In Vireth-Ka, the boundary between nature and construction is porous. The city is not a place you *inhabit* so much as a place you *coexist with*, its structures breathing, its vegetation whispering, and its silence holding the weight of something waiting to be understood.
