The temperature hovers in a pleasant warmth, as if the ocean itself has been heated by some unseen geothermal core, though the source remains obscured beneath the city’s jagged spires.  

The terrain is a paradox of youth and construction. Erosion is minimal, the structures here still sharp-edged, their surfaces unweathered by time. This is a place that has only just been built—or perhaps remade. The *built* scale is near-perfect at 2.7/3, every inch of this environment a testament to human (or alien) ambition. The scale is intimate, a pocket-world buried within the ocean’s vastness, its boundaries defined by pressure seals and the soft *hiss* of airlocks.

There is no fauna, no creatures gliding through the currents; the silence of absence is punctuated only by the rhythmic clatter of automated repair units, their metallic limbs snapping and sliding as they scan for fractures in the city’s shell.

Bioluminescent panels line the corridors, their light harsh and unyielding, casting long, angular shadows across the metallic floors. The *dynamic* scale is active, but not in the way of forests or seas. This is a place in flux, its systems constantly recalibrating. There’s no art, no ornamentation—only the stark geometry of survival.  

This is a place that should not exist, yet it does: a microcosm of human ingenuity pressed into the ocean’s throat. It is warm, bright, and loud, a cathedral of metal and light, built to defy the void. But the water is always watching, patience unyielding, and the city breathes in steady, mechanical pulses, never quite sure if it is a sanctuary—or a tomb.
