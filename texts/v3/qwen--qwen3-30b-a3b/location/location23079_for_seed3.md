The landscape is a fractured, living mosaic of steel and stone, where the bones of the earth are still raw and unrefined, as if the planet itself is in the act of assembling its own anatomy. It lies at the edge of a vast, high-altitude basin, cradled by jagged peaks that claw at the sky like the fingers of a colossal, unfinished sculpture. The elevation is extreme—soaring above the clouds, yet not quite reaching them—creating a liminal space where the air is thin and cool, carrying the sharp tang of iron and ozone from the factories that cling to the mountainsides like barnacles. The terrain is a paradox: a labyrinth of eroded cliffs and chasms, some still in the throes of collapse, their slopes fracturing under the weight of their own creation, while others are smoothed by ancient, slow-moving glaciers that hum with a low, resonant frequency.  

Water is present but elusive, a whisper of moisture in the condensation that clings to the metal scaffolds of the urban structures, or in the sudden, surging streams that burst from fissures in the rock, only to vanish into the sandstone below. There are pockets of it—lagoons in the valleys, their surfaces shimmering with an unnatural iridescence, as if the liquid itself is a byproduct of the landscape’s instability. The vegetation, though lush, is sparse in its distribution: dense groves of bioluminescent ferns and mosses cluster around these water sources, their fronds glowing faintly in the dim light, while elsewhere the ground is a tapestry of cracked, rust-colored earth and brittle, glass-like formations that spark with static when the wind passes. Trees here are not conventional; they are twisted, metallic, their trunks hollowed by the erosion process, their branches trailing cables and vines that pulse with a faint, greenish bioluminescence.  

The urban sprawl is a patchwork of survival, built into the mountains’ ribs. Streets are carved into the rock, their surfaces slick with condensation and lined with factories that exhale plumes of steam and smoke, their chimneys jagged and asymmetrical, as though constructed by hands unsure of their own purpose. The buildings are a mix of organic and mechanical—some resembling coral reefs fused with rusted pipes, others leaning at impossible angles, their foundations half-submerged in the slow, deliberate crawl of the earth’s crust. Bridges span chasms that seem to widen and narrow unpredictably, as if the landscape is breathing. The scale is vast, stretching beyond the horizon in a way that feels disorienting; the valley is not a single entity but a network of interconnected depressions, each holding a fragment of the city, its infrastructure bleeding into the wilderness.  

Erosion is not a passive force here but an active, almost deliberate one. The rock is constantly shifting, as if the mountains are alive and shedding their skin. Some areas are strewn with debris from recent collapses, while others show the scars of ancient quakes, their surfaces etched with deep, spiraling grooves that resemble both rivers and veins. The air is thick with the sound of grinding machinery, the low, rhythmic thud of heavy engines, and the occasional shriek of a collapsing cliff. Yet amid this industrial cacophony, there are also the cries of creatures—abundant, strange, and unceasing. Birds with iridescent, shifting feathers dart between the spires, their wings leaving trails of light. Large, serpentine beasts with too many joints slither through the mist, their scales reflecting the dim light in prismatic, halting pulses. In the shadows of the factories, bioluminescent insects swarm in clouds, their movements synchronized to the pulse of the machines.  

Weirdness permeates the place. The laws of physics are malleable here; water sometimes flows upward into the sky, defying gravity, while the sun—when it manages to pierce the perpetual haze—casts double shadows. Time feels stretched, the dynamic of the world slow but relentless, as if everything is part of a long, patient process of transformation. The city’s inhabitants, if there are any, are unseen, their presence marked only by the hum of distant activity and the flicker of lights in the fog. The scale of the place is both grand and intimate: you could walk for days and never reach the edge, yet every step reveals a new anomaly—a tree that blooms in reverse, a river that sings in a language no one understands, a rock that shifts when you’re not looking.  

The atmosphere is one of uneasy coexistence, where the industrial and the natural are not adversaries but collaborators in a strange, evolving dance. The dim light filters through a haze of dust and vapor, creating a gloom that is neither night nor day, but something in between—a twilight that never ends. The fauna thrives in this liminality, adapted to the cool, damp air and the constant noise, their forms a mix of the familiar and the alien. And the landscape, though still emerging, feels ancient, as though it has always been here, waiting to be shaped by the hands of the world’s unfinished design.
