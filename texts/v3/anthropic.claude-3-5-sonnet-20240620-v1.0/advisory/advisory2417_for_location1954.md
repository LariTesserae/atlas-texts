To prepare for visiting this extraordinary underwater world, an ordinary human multiverse traveler should take the following steps:

1. Breathing apparatus: Develop or acquire a specialized breathing system that can extract oxygen from the viscous, alien fluid. This system should be compact, reliable, and able to function for extended periods.

2. Pressure adaptation: Undergo a series of treatments or use advanced technology to help your body withstand the intense pressure of the deep ocean environment. This may involve gradual acclimatization or the use of a protective suit.

3. Vision enhancement: Obtain special goggles or eye implants that can amplify the low light conditions and filter the bioluminescent displays to prevent sensory overload.

4. Communication devices: Bring equipment that can transmit and receive sound waves through the dense medium, allowing you to communicate with your team and potentially with some of the more intelligent lifeforms.

5. Protective suit: Wear a flexible, durable suit that protects your skin from the alien fluid while allowing for maximum mobility. This suit should also regulate your body temperature in the warm environment.

6. Propulsion system: Equip yourself with a quiet, efficient propulsion device to navigate through the plant structures and vast open spaces.

7. Navigation tools: Bring 3D mapping technology and sonar-like devices to help you navigate the ever-changing landscape of plant structures.

8. Sample collection gear: Pack tools for safely collecting and storing samples of the local flora and fauna for later study.

9. Energy source: Ensure you have a long-lasting, waterproof power source for all your equipment.

10. Decontamination protocols: Establish strict procedures to avoid introducing harmful elements to this delicate ecosystem or bringing back potentially dangerous organisms.

11. Psychological preparation: Undergo training to cope with the alien environment, sensory overload, and potential feelings of claustrophobia or agoraphobia.

12. Language and communication study: Research any known methods of communication with the intelligent life forms in this world.

13. Buoyancy control: Master the use of a buoyancy compensation device to navigate the varying densities of the fluid environment.

14. Time dilation awareness: Be prepared for the possibility that time may flow differently in this realm and have contingencies in place.

15. Emergency protocols: Establish clear emergency procedures, including methods for rapid ascent if necessary, and treatment for potential exposure to alien microorganisms.

16. Nutrition plan: Bring a supply of nutrient-dense food that can sustain you in this high-pressure environment, as well as water purification systems.

17. Waste management: Implement a closed-system waste management solution to avoid contaminating the environment.

18. Recording equipment: Bring high-definition, pressure-resistant cameras and sensors to document your experiences and discoveries.

19. Artifact interaction protocol: Establish clear guidelines for how to approach and study any remnants of the ancient civilization without disturbing them.

20. Meditation and centering techniques: Learn methods to maintain your mental equilibrium in an environment that may challenge your sense of reality.

Remember, flexibility and respect for the environment are key. This world is unlike anything on Earth, and your preparations should reflect a mindset of exploration, adaptation, and conservation.
