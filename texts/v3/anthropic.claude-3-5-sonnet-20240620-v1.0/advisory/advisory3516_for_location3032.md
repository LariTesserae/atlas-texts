To prepare for visiting this extraordinary living metropolis, an ordinary human multiverse traveler should consider the following:

1. Environmental Protection:
- Wear a high-quality, sealed environmental suit. This will protect you from the intense moisture and any potentially harmful spores or pollen in the air.
- Ensure your suit has excellent filtration systems to purify the air you breathe.
- Include a cooling system in your suit to regulate body temperature in the humid, tropical-like environment.

2. Footwear:
- Opt for waterproof boots with excellent traction. The spongy, moss-like ground may be slippery and unstable.
- Consider boots with adjustable air cushions to adapt to the varying firmness of the living substrate.

3. Navigation Equipment:
- Bring a advanced GPS system, as traditional landmarks may shift or disappear as the city grows and changes.
- Include a 3D mapping device to track your movement through the multi-layered canopy.
- Carry a compass, but be prepared for potential interference from the unique electromagnetic fields of the plant life.

4. Scientific Instruments:
- Pack a multi-spectral camera to capture the bioluminescence and unique light refractions.
- Bring a portable spectrometer to analyze the composition of the plant structures and water.
- Include a high-sensitivity microphone to detect and record the subtle sounds of the environment.

5. Survival Gear:
- Carry a water purification system, despite the abundance of water. The alien nature of this ecosystem means the water may not be safe for human consumption without treatment.
- Bring nutrient-dense, long-lasting food supplies. Do not assume anything in this environment is edible for humans.
- Pack a multi-tool with plant-cutting capabilities for emergencies.

6. Communication Devices:
- Bring multiple forms of long-range communication devices. The dense plant matter may interfere with certain signals.
- Include a emergency beacon that can penetrate the thick canopy.

7. Medical Supplies:
- Carry a comprehensive medical kit including antihistamines, anti-fungal medications, and broad-spectrum antibiotics.
- Include a portable oxygen supply in case of allergic reactions or breathing difficulties.

8. Documentation Tools:
- Bring waterproof notebooks and writing tools for observations.
- Include a small, rugged tablet or computer for digital note-taking and data analysis.

9. Protective Eyewear:
- Wear adjustable-tint goggles to protect against the intense light and potential UV radiation.
- Ensure the goggles are sealed to protect your eyes from the omnipresent moisture.

10. Mental Preparation:
- Be prepared for sensory disorientation due to the lack of familiar urban or natural landmarks.
- Practice mindfulness techniques to stay calm in an environment that may trigger claustrophobia or agoraphobia.
- Be ready to challenge your understanding of the boundaries between artificial and natural environments.

11. Time Management:
- Bring multiple timekeeping devices. The perpetual brightness and lack of day-night cycle may disrupt your circadian rhythm.
- Plan for regular rest periods to avoid fatigue from the constant stimulation of this alien environment.

12. Sampling Equipment:
- If permitted, bring sterile sampling tools and preservation materials to collect specimens for later study.
- Include a portable microscope for immediate analysis of collected samples.

Remember, this is an entirely alien ecosystem. Approach everything with caution, avoid unnecessary contact with the plant life, and be prepared for unexpected phenomena. Your goal should be to observe and document while minimizing your impact on this unique environment.
