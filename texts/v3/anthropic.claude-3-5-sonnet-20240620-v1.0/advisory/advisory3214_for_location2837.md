To prepare for visiting this remarkable subterranean environment, an ordinary human multiverse traveler should consider the following preparations:

1. Physical Conditioning:
   - Engage in cardio and strength training, focusing on legs and core.
   - Practice climbing and crawling to navigate tight spaces.
   - Work on balance and stability for traversing uneven terrain.

2. Gear and Clothing:
   - Wear moisture-wicking, thermal base layers to regulate body temperature.
   - Bring a waterproof, breathable outer layer to protect from cave dampness.
   - Pack sturdy, waterproof hiking boots with excellent grip.
   - Bring knee pads and gloves for protection when crawling.
   - Carry a helmet with an attached headlamp for safety and illumination.
   - Bring spare batteries and backup lighting sources.
   - Pack a waterproof backpack for supplies.

3. Navigation and Safety:
   - Bring detailed cave maps and a compass (GPS may not work underground).
   - Carry emergency beacons or locator devices designed for subterranean use.
   - Bring a first-aid kit tailored for potential cave injuries.
   - Pack emergency rations and water purification tablets.
   - Carry a multi-tool and basic repair kit for equipment.

4. Specialized Equipment:
   - Bring ropes, harnesses, and carabiners for vertical passages.
   - Pack a waterproof camera to document the unique environment.
   - Carry water sampling equipment to test for potability and analyze microorganisms.

5. Environmental Considerations:
   - Bring biodegradable soap and waste bags to minimize environmental impact.
   - Pack protective eyewear to shield from potential debris or cave-dwelling creatures.
   - Bring a small tarp or ground cloth for resting on damp surfaces.

6. Scientific and Observational Tools:
   - Pack a thermometer and hygrometer to measure temperature and humidity.
   - Bring a geological hammer and sample bags for rock collection (if permitted).
   - Carry a magnifying glass or portable microscope for examining microorganisms.
   - Bring a field journal and waterproof pens for documentation.

7. Mental Preparation:
   - Practice meditation or mindfulness to manage potential claustrophobia.
   - Familiarize yourself with basic geology and cave formation processes.
   - Learn about cave ecosystems and the types of life you might encounter.

8. Health Precautions:
   - Get vaccinated against tetanus and rabies (due to the presence of bat-like creatures).
   - Bring any necessary personal medications in waterproof containers.
   - Consider bringing dust masks to protect against fungal spores.

9. Time Management:
   - Plan for extended periods underground without natural light cues.
   - Bring a reliable, waterproof watch or time-keeping device.

10. Communication:
    - Bring long-range, waterproof radios for communication within your group.
    - Learn basic cave sign language for situations where verbal communication is difficult.

11. Respect for the Environment:
    - Study "Leave No Trace" principles and apply them to the cave environment.
    - Learn about the fragility of cave ecosystems and how to minimize your impact.

Remember, the goal is to explore and observe while preserving this unique environment. Move carefully, speak softly, and be prepared for the unexpected in this alien yet fascinating subterranean world.
