To prepare for visiting the Verdant Abyss, an ordinary human multiverse traveler should consider the following preparations:

1. Lighting: 
- Bring multiple high-powered, waterproof headlamps and flashlights with extra batteries.
- Consider bioluminescent light sticks as backup.
- A UV light might reveal hidden patterns in the fungal growth.

2. Clothing:
- Wear moisture-wicking, quick-drying layers.
- Bring waterproof outer layers (jacket and pants).
- Sturdy, waterproof hiking boots with excellent grip for slippery surfaces.
- Gloves to protect hands when climbing or touching potentially hazardous plants.

3. Navigation:
- Traditional compasses may not work underground; bring a cave-specific navigation system.
- Marking tape or biodegradable trail markers to avoid getting lost.
- A detailed map of known areas, if available.

4. Safety Equipment:
- Climbing harness and ropes for traversing difficult areas.
- Helmet to protect against falling rocks or low-hanging stalactites.
- First aid kit including treatments for cuts, sprains, and fungal infections.
- Emergency shelter and thermal blanket.
- Water purification tablets or filter, as cave water may contain harmful microorganisms.

5. Food and Water:
- High-energy, non-perishable food for several days more than planned stay.
- Multiple ways to carry and purify water.

6. Communication:
- Long-range, waterproof radios (though they may not work deep in the caves).
- Consider leaving a communication schedule with someone outside.

7. Scientific Equipment:
- Camera with underwater housing for high-humidity environment.
- Sample collection containers for studying unique flora.
- pH testing strips for water analysis.
- Thermometer and hygrometer to measure temperature and humidity.

8. Respiratory Protection:
- Dust masks or respirators to protect against potential spores or fungi.

9. Specialized Gear:
- Collapsible walking stick for balance and probing terrain.
- Waterproof notebook and pencils for documenting observations.
- Small tarp for creating a dry rest area.

10. Mental Preparation:
- Research and practice basic caving techniques.
- Prepare for sensory deprivation and disorientation in darkness.
- Learn meditation or calming techniques to manage potential claustrophobia.

11. Cultural Sensitivity:
- Bring small, biodegradable offerings for the primitive inhabitants.
- Learn basic cave preservation ethics to minimize impact.

12. Time Perception Tools:
- Reliable, waterproof watch to maintain sense of time.
- Journal to record experiences and track the passage of time.

13. Fungal Protection:
- Anti-fungal cream and powder to prevent infections.
- Sealed, waterproof bags to protect gear from spores.

14. Adaptive Technology:
- Consider bringing simple tools or materials that could be useful for interacting with primitive technology (e.g., strong cord, small pulleys).

Remember, the environment is unique and potentially hazardous. Move slowly, stay alert, and be prepared to turn back if conditions become unsafe. Respect the delicate ecosystem and any potential inhabitants you might encounter.
