To prepare for visiting this extraordinary underwater realm, an ordinary human multiverse traveler should take the following precautions and preparations:

1. Pressure Protection:
The extreme depth and pressure require a specialized diving suit or submersible vehicle. This should be able to withstand pressures hundreds or thousands of times greater than surface atmospheric pressure. The suit or vehicle must be thoroughly tested and certified for such extreme conditions.

2. Breathing Apparatus:
A closed-circuit rebreather system is essential, as there is no surface to return to for air. This system should be capable of recycling breathable air for extended periods, potentially days or weeks. Multiple redundant systems are crucial.

3. Temperature Regulation:
While the cold is described as not biting, prolonged exposure could still be dangerous. The suit should have excellent thermal insulation and an active heating system to maintain body temperature in the near-freezing water.

4. Illumination:
Given the near-absolute darkness, powerful yet energy-efficient lighting systems are necessary. These should include options for different spectrums of light to potentially interact with or observe the bioluminescent algae without disrupting them.

5. Propulsion:
The dense, gelatinous nature of the water will make movement difficult. A propulsion system designed for high-viscosity fluids is needed, possibly using magnetohydrodynamic principles to move efficiently.

6. Communication:
Standard radio waves may not propagate well in this environment. Consider acoustic communication systems or possibly quantum entanglement-based communication for contact with the surface or other team members.

7. Sensors and Scientific Equipment:
Specialized sensors to analyze the unique chemical composition of the water, detect subtle currents, and measure the strange acoustics of the environment. Equipment to take samples of the algae and the structure materials without contaminating them will be crucial.

8. Time Dilation Preparation:
The description of the environment suggests a place where time moves differently. The traveler should be mentally prepared for potential time dilation effects and have equipment to accurately measure the passage of time relative to the surface.

9. Power Source:
A long-lasting power source is crucial. Consider radioisotope thermoelectric generators or advanced fuel cells that can operate for weeks or months without recharging.

10. Psychological Preparation:
The alien nature of this environment, combined with isolation and potential time dilation effects, could be psychologically challenging. The traveler should undergo extensive psychological training and possibly bring AI companions for mental stimulation.

11. Decontamination Procedures:
Given the primordial nature of this environment, strict protocols to prevent contamination in both directions (from the traveler to the environment and vice versa) are essential.

12. Emergency Ascent System:
Despite the depth, an emergency system to return to the surface quickly if needed is crucial. This could involve a rapid-ascent vehicle or a teleportation system if available in the traveler's multiverse.

13. Data Recording:
Advanced holographic recording devices to capture the intricate patterns and structures in 3D for later study.

14. Translation Tools:
If the patterns on the structures are indeed a form of writing, advanced AI-driven translation tools might be helpful in attempting to decipher them.

15. Microgravity Adaptation:
The buoyancy in this dense water might create effects similar to microgravity. Training similar to that of astronauts could be beneficial.

16. Temporal Shielding:
If available in the traveler's multiverse, some form of temporal shielding to protect against potential time-warping effects of this ancient environment.

Remember, this is an environment utterly alien to human experience. The traveler should be prepared for the unexpected and ready to abort the mission if unforeseen dangers arise. The key is to approach with caution, respect for the environment, and a mindset of scientific curiosity tempered with an awareness of one's limitations in such an extreme setting.
