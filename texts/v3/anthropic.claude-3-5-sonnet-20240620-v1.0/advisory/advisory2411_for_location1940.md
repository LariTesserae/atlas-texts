To prepare for visiting Lithopolis, an ordinary human multiverse traveler should consider the following:

1. Physical Preparation:
- Undergo extensive physical conditioning to adapt to the cool, damp environment and confined spaces.
- Practice moving in tight, enclosed areas to prevent claustrophobia.
- Train in rock climbing and spelunking to navigate the potentially challenging terrain.

2. Gear and Equipment:
- Bring thermal underwear and moisture-wicking clothing to stay warm and dry.
- Pack a high-quality headlamp with extra batteries for personal illumination.
- Carry a water filtration system to safely consume the bioluminescent liquid if necessary.
- Bring a small, collapsible tent or bivouac sack for rest periods.
- Pack lightweight, nutritionally dense food that doesn't require cooking.
- Bring a multi-tool for various purposes.
- Carry a first-aid kit specialized for subterranean environments.

3. Communication and Navigation:
- Learn basic vibration-based communication methods to interact with the Lithos.
- Bring a seismograph or vibration-sensitive device to detect and interpret local communications.
- Carry a 3D mapping device to create a real-time map of the ever-changing city layout.

4. Scientific Equipment:
- Pack a portable spectrometer to analyze the composition of flowstone and other materials.
- Bring a high-resolution camera with low-light capabilities to document the environment and its inhabitants.
- Carry small sample collection containers for gathering specimens (if permitted).

5. Temporal Anomaly Preparation:
- Wear a reliable timekeeping device that can withstand temporal distortions.
- Bring a journal to record personal experiences and observations, especially in areas with time anomalies.
- Consider carrying a small, shielded container to protect certain items from temporal effects.

6. Cultural Preparation:
- Study any available information on Lithos culture, customs, and taboos.
- Learn to move slowly and deliberately to match the Lithos' geological timescale.
- Practice patience and silent observation to better integrate with the environment.

7. Mental Preparation:
- Undergo meditation training to cope with the isolation and unique sensory experiences.
- Prepare for potential hallucinations or altered perceptions due to the unusual environment.
- Be ready to challenge your understanding of life, time, and physical laws.

8. Safety Precautions:
- Carry a personal locator beacon in case of emergencies.
- Bring a small supply of oxygen in case of poor air quality in certain areas.
- Pack a radiation detector to monitor for any potentially harmful emissions.

9. Ethical Considerations:
- Prepare a set of ethical guidelines for interacting with this unique ecosystem and civilization.
- Be ready to abort the mission if your presence seems to negatively impact the environment or its inhabitants.

Remember, flexibility and adaptability are key when visiting such an alien environment. Be prepared to abandon plans and equipment if the situation demands it, and always prioritize respect for the local ecosystem and civilization over personal goals or curiosity.
