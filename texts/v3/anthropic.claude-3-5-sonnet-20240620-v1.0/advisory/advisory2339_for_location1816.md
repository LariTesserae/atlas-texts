To prepare for visiting this extraordinary underwater realm, an ordinary human multiverse traveler should take the following precautions and preparations:

1. Pressure-resistant suit: Develop or acquire a highly advanced deep-sea diving suit capable of withstanding extreme pressures. This suit should be flexible enough to allow movement while protecting against the crushing force of the deep ocean.

2. Life support system: Ensure the suit has a robust life support system with ample oxygen supply, CO2 scrubbers, and temperature regulation. The system should be able to function for extended periods without resurfacing.

3. Communication equipment: Install a sophisticated communication system that can transmit through the dense, viscous fluid. This may require developing new technologies that use sound waves or other forms of energy that can penetrate the unique medium.

4. Bioluminescent-sensitive vision enhancements: Equip the suit with advanced visual sensors that can detect and amplify the full spectrum of bioluminescent light. Include a heads-up display that can provide real-time analysis of observed phenomena.

5. Propulsion system: Incorporate a quiet, efficient propulsion system that can navigate through the thick fluid without disturbing the local ecosystem or alerting potentially dangerous lifeforms.

6. Shielding against extreme temperatures: Ensure the suit can protect against both the ambient warmth of the ocean and the super-heated emissions from thermal vents.

7. Chemical protection: Design the suit to resist potential corrosive or toxic elements in the alien ocean's chemical composition.

8. Adaptive camouflage: Consider including a system that can mimic the bioluminescent patterns of local lifeforms to avoid unwanted attention.

9. Sample collection tools: Equip the suit with instruments for safely collecting fluid, mineral, and biological samples for later analysis.

10. Energy harvesting capabilities: Include systems that can harness energy from the thermal vents or other sources to extend mission duration.

11. Advanced sonar and echolocation: Develop systems to navigate and map the environment using sound, given the limited visibility.

12. Psychological preparation: Undergo extensive training to cope with the sensory overload, disorientation, and potential claustrophobia of this alien environment.

13. Decontamination protocols: Establish rigorous procedures for cleaning the suit and equipment before and after the mission to prevent cross-contamination between universes.

14. Emergency ascent system: Include a rapid ascent mechanism in case of suit failure or other emergencies.

15. Diplomatic preparation: If the probes encountered are part of an advanced civilization, prepare for potential first contact scenarios.

16. Time dilation considerations: Account for potential differences in the passage of time between this universe and the traveler's home reality.

17. Interdimensional beacon: Carry a device capable of signaling across universes to ensure retrieval is possible.

18. Cultural and ethical briefing: Undergo training on respectful exploration and non-interference with the ecosystem and any potential intelligent life.

This preparation should provide a solid foundation for exploring this fascinating aquatic realm while prioritizing safety, scientific inquiry, and respect for the alien environment.
