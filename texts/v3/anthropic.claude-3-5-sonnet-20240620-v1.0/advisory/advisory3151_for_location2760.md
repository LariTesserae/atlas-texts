To prepare for visiting this remarkable underground mechanical city, an ordinary human multiverse traveler should take the following steps:

1. Hearing Protection: The constant cacophony of industrial noise could be damaging to human ears. Bring high-quality earplugs or noise-canceling headphones to protect your hearing while still allowing you to experience the unique soundscape.

2. Respiratory Equipment: The air is likely to be filled with fine particulates from the constant mechanical activity. Pack a good quality respirator or gas mask with appropriate filters to protect your lungs from metal dust, oil vapors, and other potential airborne contaminants.

3. Heat-Resistant Clothing: Given the warm, thick air and the abundance of operating machinery, temperatures are likely to be high. Wear lightweight, breathable, heat-resistant clothing. Consider bringing cooling vests or personal fans.

4. Protective Eyewear: Sparks from exposed circuitry and potential oil or steam sprays necessitate eye protection. Bring sealed goggles that won't fog up in the warm, humid environment.

5. Sturdy Footwear: The narrow streets and potentially slick surfaces (from oil and condensation) require shoes with excellent grip and ankle support. Steel-toed boots might be advisable for protection from falling objects or accidental contact with moving machinery.

6. Light Source: While the city is illuminated, having a personal light source is crucial. Bring a powerful, durable flashlight with extra batteries. A headlamp would be particularly useful for hands-free operation.

7. Navigation Tools: The labyrinthine layout of the city could be disorienting. Bring a compass (though be aware it may be affected by the massive amounts of metal), and perhaps some method of marking your path, like bright string or chalk.

8. Water and Nutrition: Given the apparent lack of natural water sources, bring ample water supplies. Also, pack dense, non-perishable food items, as finding edible substances for humans may be challenging.

9. First Aid Kit: Include treatments for burns, cuts, and eye irritation, as these might be common in such an environment. Also, include any personal medications you might need.

10. Communication Device: Standard communication devices may not work underground. Consider bringing a low-frequency radio or other specialized communication equipment.

11. Protective Gloves: The abundance of machinery and potentially hot surfaces necessitates hand protection. Bring heat-resistant gloves that still allow for dexterity.

12. Cooling System: The constant heat might be overwhelming. A personal cooling system, like a cooling scarf or vest, could be invaluable.

13. Anti-Static Equipment: The amount of machinery might create significant static electricity. Bring anti-static wristbands or sprays to protect yourself and any electronic equipment you carry.

14. Translation Device: If you plan to interact with the artificial inhabitants, some form of universal translator might be necessary to understand their communication.

15. Samples Collection Kit: For research purposes, bring sterile containers and tools to collect samples of the bioluminescent fungi or other unique materials.

16. Time-Keeping Device: Given the timeless nature of the city, a reliable watch or clock will help you keep track of your visit duration.

17. Protective Suit: Consider a full-body protective suit that's resistant to heat, electricity, and corrosive substances, just in case of unexpected hazards.

Remember, the goal is to observe and experience this unique environment while minimizing your impact and ensuring your safety. Be respectful of the city's workings and inhabitants, and be prepared for the possibility that this mechanical ecosystem might react unpredictably to organic presence.
