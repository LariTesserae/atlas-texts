To prepare for visiting the City of Nascent Pools, a human multiverse traveler should consider the following:

1. Waterproof gear: Pack waterproof clothing, including boots, pants, and jackets. The constant moisture and mist will quickly soak through regular fabrics.

2. Amphibious footwear: Bring shoes that can transition easily between water and land, such as sturdy water shoes or quick-drying hiking sandals with good traction.

3. Moisture-resistant equipment: Use waterproof cases for any electronics or sensitive items. Consider bringing a waterproof notebook and writing implements.

4. Lightweight, quick-drying clothes: Pack clothes made from materials that dry quickly and resist mildew, as the humid environment will make it difficult for regular fabrics to dry.

5. Sun protection: Despite the mist, UV rays can still penetrate. Bring waterproof sunscreen, a wide-brimmed hat, and UV-protective sunglasses.

6. Portable water filtration system: While water is abundant, it may not be safe to drink without treatment. A reliable water filter or purification tablets are essential.

7. Anti-fungal treatments: The damp environment may promote fungal growth on skin and gear. Pack anti-fungal powder or cream.

8. Insect repellent: The watery environment likely supports various insects. Bring effective, waterproof insect repellent.

9. Trekking poles or a walking stick: These will help with balance when navigating the uneven, slippery terrain.

10. Dry bags: Pack several dry bags of various sizes to keep spare clothes, food, and equipment dry.

11. Bioluminescent-friendly lighting: Bring red-light headlamps or flashlights to preserve night vision and avoid disrupting the natural bioluminescence.

12. Waterproof camera: To capture the unique landscape and bioluminescent displays.

13. Portable shelter: A lightweight, waterproof tent or bivy sack in case you need to rest away from the stone structures.

14. Thermal underlayer: Despite the humid environment, it may get cool, especially at night. Pack a moisture-wicking thermal layer.

15. First-aid kit: Include treatments for water-related injuries like trench foot, as well as standard supplies.

16. Language aids: Research local communication methods and bring translation tools if necessary.

17. Protective eyewear: Consider bringing goggles to protect eyes from splashing water and mist.

18. Appropriate currency or trade goods: Research the local economy and bring suitable means of exchange.

19. Respirator or mask: The misty air might contain spores or other particulates; a good quality respirator could be helpful.

20. Cultural guide: Study local customs and taboos to avoid unintentionally offending the inhabitants.

21. Compact, waterproof food supplies: Bring lightweight, non-perishable food that won't be affected by the damp conditions.

22. Water-resistant backpack: Choose a backpack designed for wet conditions to carry your supplies.

23. Portable water transportation: A collapsible bucket or water bag might be useful for gathering and moving water.

24. Waterproof map and compass: GPS might not work, so have backup navigation tools.

25. Patience and adaptability: Be prepared for constant change and the need to adjust plans frequently.

Remember, respect for the environment and local customs is paramount. Be prepared to move slowly and carefully, and always prioritize safety in this unique and potentially hazardous environment.
