The inhabitants of the City of Nascent Pools are a unique species that has evolved in perfect harmony with their ever-shifting, amphibious environment. These beings, known as the Fluidfolk, are as much a part of the landscape as the pools and stone structures themselves.

Physically, the Fluidfolk are a marvel of adaptation. Their bodies are semi-translucent, with a shimmering, gelatinous quality that allows them to move effortlessly between water and land. Their skin is covered in a thin, mucus-like layer that keeps them constantly moist and protected from the harsh minerals in the water. This layer also hosts colonies of symbiotic algae and bacteria, giving each individual a unique pattern of bioluminescent markings that pulse gently with their breath and moods.

The Fluidfolk have no bones in the traditional sense. Instead, their bodies are supported by a complex hydrostatic skeleton, allowing them to alter their shape and density at will. They can flatten themselves to slip through narrow crevices in the porous stone or expand to float lazily in the deeper pools. Their limbs are elongated and webbed, equally adept at swimming and climbing the rough coral-like structures.

Their faces are alien yet oddly beautiful, with large, lidless eyes adapted for seeing clearly both above and below water. These eyes are compound, like those of insects, giving them an almost 360-degree field of vision. In place of a nose and mouth, they have a series of sensitive tendrils surrounding a central feeding orifice. These tendrils serve multiple purposes: filtration of water and air, detection of chemical signals, and complex communication through a combination of movement and bioluminescent pulses.

The Fluidfolk do not speak as we understand it. Instead, they communicate through a complex language of gestures, color changes, and vibrations in the water. Their entire bodies serve as instruments of expression, with subtle ripples and waves conveying nuanced meanings. They can also produce haunting, whale-like songs by forcing water through specialized chambers in their bodies, using these sounds for long-distance communication and to shape the growth of their stone dwellings.

Reproduction for the Fluidfolk is a communal affair, tied closely to the cycles of their environment. During certain lunar phases, when the mineral content of the pools reaches a specific concentration, groups of Fluidfolk gather in the largest lagoons. Here, they release genetic material into the water, which combines and develops into thousands of tiny, tadpole-like offspring. These young are cared for collectively by the community, gradually developing their adult forms as they learn to shape the stone and navigate the ever-changing landscape.

The Fluidfolk possess a deep, almost mystical connection to their environment. They can sense minute changes in water chemistry and predict geological shifts long before they occur. This ability guides their cultivation of the coral-like structures and their management of the water flows. They do not see themselves as separate from their surroundings but as an integral part of the city's ongoing evolution.

Their society is as fluid as their bodies, with social roles and hierarchies shifting as naturally as the pools they inhabit. Leadership is based on an individual's ability to read and respond to the environment, with the most skilled becoming guides and shapers of the community's future.

The Fluidfolk have a rich culture centered around the cycles of their world. They create intricate, temporary art by manipulating currents and sediments in the pools, and their music—a blend of their eerie songs and the natural sounds of their home—is hauntingly beautiful. They mark time not by the sun or seasons, but by the ebb and flow of the waters and the growth patterns of their stone dwellings.

To outsiders, the Fluidfolk might seem alien and inscrutable. But in truth, they embody a profound harmony with their world, their very existence a testament to the adaptability and resilience of life. In the City of Nascent Pools, they are both caretakers and creations of their ever-changing home.
