The inhabitants of Echovia, known as the Echovians, are a unique and highly adapted subterranean species that has evolved to thrive in this dark, watery environment. They are humanoid in general form, but with several distinct characteristics that set them apart from surface dwellers.

Echovians stand about 5 feet tall on average, with a slender, almost willowy build that allows them to navigate the narrow passages and rope bridges of their city with ease. Their skin is pale and slightly translucent, with a faint bioluminescent quality that gives them a soft, ghostly glow in the darkness. This bioluminescence can be controlled to some degree, allowing for basic communication through patterns of light.

Their most striking feature is their eyes - large, lidless orbs that dominate much of their face. These eyes are incredibly sensitive to light, capable of discerning the faintest glimmers in the darkness. In brighter areas, a second, inner eyelid closes to protect their vision. Their pupils are vertical slits, reminiscent of cave-dwelling amphibians.

Echovians have no hair, but their heads are crowned with sensitive, feather-like appendages that can detect air currents, changes in pressure, and even subtle vibrations in the cave walls. These "sensory fronds" play a crucial role in their navigation and communication.

Their hands and feet are slightly webbed, with long, nimble fingers adapted for climbing and manipulating the various materials found in their environment. Their toes can grip like fingers, making them excellent climbers.

Echovians have a unique respiratory system that allows them to extract oxygen from both air and water. Gill-like structures are hidden behind their ears, while their lungs are highly efficient at processing the damp, sometimes stagnant air of the caves.

Their diet consists mainly of fungi, cave fish, and a variety of subterranean plants that they cultivate in specially illuminated chambers. They have developed techniques to extract nutrients from the mineral-rich waters that flow through their city.

Echovian society is highly communal, with decisions made through consensus. They have a deep spiritual connection to their environment, viewing the cave system as a living entity of which they are but a small part. Their culture revolves around the cycles of water flow and the subtle changes in air currents, which they believe carry messages from the heart of the earth.

The Echovians have developed a complex language that combines vocal sounds, light patterns, and air vibrations produced by their unique musical instruments. This multi-sensory communication allows them to convey complex ideas even in complete darkness.

Despite their isolation, Echovians are curious about the outside world. A small group of explorers, known as the Surface Seekers, occasionally venture out of the caves to gather information and resources. These journeys are considered sacred and dangerous, with the travelers requiring extensive preparation to protect their sensitive eyes and skin.

The oldest and wisest Echovians are said to have developed a form of echolocation, allowing them to "see" the shape of distant caverns through sound alone. These elders are highly respected and often serve as guides for expeditions into the deeper, unexplored regions of the cave system.

Echovians reproduce slowly, with pregnancies lasting nearly two years. Children are raised communally, spending their early years in specially designed nursery chambers where they can safely develop their unique sensory abilities.

As a species, Echovians are deeply attuned to the rhythms and movements of their subterranean world. They can sense impending geological events long before they occur, allowing the community to adapt and move as needed. This connection to their environment has allowed them to survive and thrive for countless generations in the depths of Echovia.
