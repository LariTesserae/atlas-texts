The inhabitants of Lithopolis, known as the Lithos, are truly unique beings that blur the line between organic life and mineral formation. These entities are living sculptures of flowstone, their bodies in a constant state of slow, fluid transformation.

The Lithos have no fixed form, instead existing as amorphous, semi-translucent beings composed of the same glass-like substance that makes up their city. Their bodies shimmer with an inner light, pulsing in sync with the bioluminescent channels that run through Lithopolis. This light seems to serve as a form of non-verbal communication, with subtle changes in hue and intensity conveying complex ideas and emotions.

Despite their fluid nature, the Lithos can form temporary appendages as needed - pseudopods for manipulation, sensory stalks for perception, and even wing-like structures for gliding through larger chambers. Their malleable bodies allow them to squeeze through the narrowest of passages, giving them unparalleled mobility within their labyrinthine home.

The Lithos do not have conventional senses as we understand them. Instead, they perceive their world through vibrations in the flowstone, feeling the subtle movements and changes in their environment. This gives them an almost prescient awareness of their surroundings and of each other.

Reproduction for the Lithos is a slow, gradual process. Two or more individuals may merge their bodies temporarily, exchanging genetic material in the form of mineral compounds. Over time, a new Lithos will slowly form, budding off from its parent(s) when it has accumulated enough mass and complexity.

The lifespan of a Lithos is measured in geological time. They grow and change over millennia, accumulating knowledge and experiences that are encoded into their very structure. The oldest Lithos are revered as living archives, their bodies etched with the history of their civilization.

The Lithos possess a collective consciousness of sorts, able to share thoughts and memories through direct contact with the flowstone walls of their city. This creates a kind of group mind that allows for rapid dissemination of information and coordinated action on a city-wide scale.

Despite their alien nature, the Lithos display clear signs of intelligence and culture. They create intricate sculptures and patterns in the flowstone, manipulate the temporal anomalies in their city for what appears to be artistic or spiritual purposes, and engage in complex social hierarchies based on age, knowledge, and ability to shape their environment.

The relationship between the Lithos and the ghostly entities that occasionally appear in their city is complex. Some Lithos seem to be able to communicate with these beings, acting as intermediaries or shamans. These interactions often result in new discoveries or changes in Lithopolis's structure.

In essence, the Lithos are a species perfectly adapted to their strange, subterranean world - part of the living architecture of Lithopolis, shapers of their environment, and keepers of ancient, otherworldly knowledge. Their existence challenges our very definitions of life, consciousness, and civilization.
