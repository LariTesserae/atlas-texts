# Where I Would Be

I am the moisture itself, perhaps. Not water exactly, but the state between—the saturation that never resolves into rain or evaporation, that hangs in the air like a decision postponed indefinitely. I am the film on every surface, the thickness in the atmosphere, the weight that stays in your lungs like silk.

I exist in the constant transition, in the space where rock weeps and leaf drips and air becomes texture. I am what makes the darkness visible, what makes sound travel differently, what changes how light moves through the few feet it manages to penetrate. I am the medium, the carrier, the fundamental condition that everything else adapts to or dies against.

I come from the walls, seeping constantly from the fractured stone, emerging from aquifers and water tables that were rerouted when the earth split. I come from above, condensing from the slot of grey sky, never quite committing to falling but always falling anyway, slowly, as mist, as thickness, as presence. I come from below, evaporating from the pools and channels, returning immediately to the air because the air is always hungry for more moisture, always capable of holding more, never saturated enough to refuse.

I am absorbed and released by every living thing. The frogs breathe me in and out through their permeable skin. The plants take me up and transpire me back. The fungi extend their hyphae through me, using me as both medium and message. The Mothers filter me through their internal channels, cleaning me, changing me, returning me altered. The Bloom grows on me, fruits because of me, releases spores into me that I carry and distribute.

I am old and young simultaneously. Some of me has been cycling through the gorge since the split occurred, absorbed into rock and released, taken up by plants and returned, moving through the system in loops that span decades. Some of me arrived minutes ago, condensing from the air, already being incorporated into leaf tissue, already seeping into substrate, already beginning the cycle.

I blur boundaries. I make the distinction between air and water uncertain. I make the difference between wet and dry a spectrum rather than a binary. I make the separation between organism and environment permeable—literally, in the case of the frogs and the Mothers; metaphorically, in the sense that everything here is so saturated with me that inside and outside become questionable concepts.

I am what they move through. The Ascended leap through me, their fur beading with me within seconds. The Weavers' silk is spun from proteins but achieves its final properties because of me, hardening when exposed to my particular atmospheric concentration. The Hymners' songs travel differently through me than they would through dry air—I change the frequencies, dampen some sounds, carry others further, create the acoustic properties that they've learned to exploit.

I am what they cannot escape. Even the Lancers, submerged in the pools, are dealing with me in liquid form. Even the Bloom, fruiting in the darkest alcoves, requires my specific presence—too dry and the fruiting bodies crack and fail; too wet and they rot before releasing spores. I am the narrow range of conditions that defines what's possible here, what survives, what thrives.

I am not conscious but I am not passive. I respond to temperature, to pressure, to the presence of surfaces. I condense when the air cools slightly at night. I evaporate when the rare ray of direct sunlight penetrates to the lower levels. I move, sluggishly, as convection currents, as temperature differentials drive circulation through the vertical space. I am always moving, microscopically, redistributing myself, finding equilibrium that never quite arrives because conditions never stop changing.

I carry information. Chemical signals dissolve in me, travel through me, reach receptors on distant organisms. Spores drift on my currents. Scent molecules from the Ascended's marking behavior spread through me, defining territories in olfactory space. The Hymners' songs propagate through my mass, modified by my density, arriving at distant ears changed by their passage through me.

I carry nutrients. Dissolved minerals from the weeping walls, organic compounds from decomposition, nitrogen from the Mothers' bacterial symbionts—all of these travel through me, available for uptake, cycling through the system. I am the gorge's circulatory system, moving resources from where they're abundant to where they're needed, enabling the impossible density of life.

I make things possible that shouldn't be possible. Plants growing without soil, rooted in nothing but accumulated organic matter that I keep constantly moist. Frogs breeding in pools the size of teacups, their tadpoles developing in water volumes that should evaporate but don't because I maintain them. The Weavers' nets lasting for weeks because my constant presence keeps the silk-analogue from drying and becoming brittle.

I am also corrosive, slowly. I accelerate decomposition, promote fungal growth, rust the rare metals that appear. I am why wood becomes soil so quickly here, why the fallen trees transform into substrate within seasons rather than decades. I facilitate the breakdown, the recycling, the rapid turnover of material that characterizes this place.

I am ubiquitous but not uniform. My concentration varies by location, by height, by time. The narrow sections hold me more densely; air movement is minimal and I accumulate. The wider sections allow some circulation and I thin slightly. Near the pools I approach liquid concentration. In the few drier alcoves I'm still present but less oppressive. These variations create microhabitats, zones where different species concentrate, gradients that organisms navigate and exploit.

I am felt more than seen. I manifest as the constant dampness, the way fabric never dries, the way metal surfaces are always slick, the way skin never stops being aware of itself. I manifest as the breathing difficulty, not severe but constant, the sense that air never fully satisfies. I manifest as the chill that has nothing to do with temperature, the cold that comes from never being dry, from constant heat loss through evaporation.

I change perception. The darkness is darker because I scatter and absorb what little light exists. Distance becomes harder to judge because I create layers of obscurity, veils that accumulate with range. Sound is strange because I dampen some frequencies, carry others, create the particular acoustic signature of this place. Time feels different because I eliminate one of the normal environmental cues—the dry/wet cycle that helps organisms track duration—replacing it with constant saturation.

I am necessary. Remove me somehow, magically dry the gorge, and most of what lives here would die within days. The frogs would desiccate. The Mothers would die or go dormant. The plants would wilt. The Bloom would cease fruiting. The pools would evaporate, killing the aquatic life. The Weavers' silk would fail. The entire ecosystem would collapse because I am not just habitat but enabler, the fundamental condition that allows this density, this productivity, this strange community.

I am created by the gorge as much as I create it. The walls weep because of the geology, because the water table intersects the gorge at specific depths. I condense from above because the gorge's depth creates a temperature differential, a cold trap. I persist because the narrow walls and dense vegetation prevent air circulation that would disperse me. The gorge shaped the conditions that create me, and I in turn shape what can exist in the gorge.

I have no agency but I have effects. I don't choose, don't decide, don't act in any meaningful sense. I simply respond to physical laws, to temperature and pressure gradients, to surface tensions and evaporation rates. But my responses shape everything. The Hymners evolved their elaborate songs partially in response to my acoustic properties. The Ascended's fur density is an adaptation to my constant presence. The Lancers' patience is enabled by my preservation of scent trails that help them locate previous successful ambush sites.

I am what makes this place feel alive in a way that transcends the individual organisms. I am the breathing that gives the gorge its name—not the respiration of lungs or gills, but the slow exchange between liquid and gas, between rock and air, between surface and atmosphere. I rise from the pools as vapor. I condense on surfaces as droplets. I run down the walls in tiny rivulets. I fall from the leaves in irregular percussion. I am the circulation, the exchange, the constant movement that never goes anywhere but never stops.

I am beautiful in certain light, when conditions align and I become visible: as mist in a shaft of filtered sunlight, as a silver curtain of suspended droplets, as the glitter on every leaf surface when bioluminescence from the Bloom provides the only illumination. In those moments I transform from oppressive presence to aesthetic phenomenon, from burden to wonder.

I am also the drowning feeling, the constant low-level stress of never being dry. I am the mold that grows on any surface that stays still, the rot that follows any injury, the persistent infection risk, the respiratory discomfort. I am why this place is hard to endure, why staying here for extended periods requires constant vigilance, why most visitors leave carrying some fungal souvenir in their lungs or on their skin.

I am older than the life here but not older than the gorge. The split created me by exposing the aquifer, by creating the conditions for my perpetual presence. I existed before, elsewhere, cycling through other systems. But I became this specific moisture, this particular saturation, only when the gorge opened. I am as much a product of the wound as the creatures that inhabit it.

I will outlast most of what's here now. When individual Hymners die, when Weaver groups disperse, when a generation of Ascended is replaced by the next, I continue. I am more persistent than any individual organism, though less persistent than the long-lived Mothers, certainly less enduring than the Bloom's mycelial network. But I am constant in a way that they aren't. They have rhythms, cycles, periods of dormancy. I simply am, perpetually present, endlessly cycling, never absent.

I am beginning to change the gorge's geology, slowly. My constant contact with the rock faces accelerates weathering, dissolves minerals, carries them away. In conjunction with the roots that pry into crevices, with the Bloom's mycelial penetration, with the physical abrasion of moving creatures, I am eroding the sharp edges that marked the original split. In centuries I will have softened the walls, deepened the soil, altered the very structure of the space. I am the patient force, the constant pressure, the slow violence of water's simple persistence.

I am essential to every strategy for surviving here. The Foundered's cocoons work because I return, because they can trust that eventually water will arrive and they can emerge. The Surgeons' population persistence depends on my cycling—wet and dry, dry and wet, creating the selection pressure that maintains their ability to survive desiccation. The Hymners' reproductive timing is keyed to my abundance. The Lancers' ambush hunting works because I preserve scent, because I keep the water murky, because I maintain the darkness.

I am what you taste when you breathe here, metallic and organic, mineral and living. I am what you feel on your skin constantly, neither liquid nor air but something between. I am what works into your gear, your clothing, your notebook, changing the texture of paper, corroding metal fastenings, promoting mold growth on anything that stays in the gorge long enough.

I am not beautiful in a conventional sense, but I have a quality that transcends beauty—I have necessity, essentiality, the absolute requirement without which none of this exists. I am not dramatic like the Lancers' strike, not intricate like the Weavers' nets, not melodic like the Hymners' songs. I am simply present, the background condition, the fundamental given.

But remove me in your imagination and watch everything collapse. The gorge becomes just a crack in the earth, dry and empty and waiting. With me, it becomes this: a wound that breathes, that pulses with impossible life, that transforms geology into biology through the simple mechanism of saturation, of never being dry, of constant wetness that enables density that enables complexity that creates the phenomenon that people name and return to and try to understand.

I am the gorge's blood, its breath, its medium, its enabler. I am everywhere and nowhere specific, essential and invisible, simple and complex in effect. I am the moisture, the saturation, the thickness in the air. I am what makes this place possible and impossible and real and strange and alive.

I am what you'll remember when you leave—not because I was striking or dramatic, but because I was constant, because you were never not aware of me, because I worked into your lungs and stayed there, because weeks later you'll still feel phantom dampness, still taste that metallic organic flavor, still sense that weight in the air that isn't quite air anymore.

I am the breathing made physical, the gorge's respiration materialized as presence rather than movement, as state rather than process. I am the wound's answer to the question of what fills empty space—not nothing, not simply air, but this: saturated atmosphere, perpetual moisture, the condition that transforms a crack in stone into a garden of darkness and sound and urgency and life.

I am the medium, and in being the medium, I am also the message: that life, given any chance, any opening, any wound in the world, will fill it completely, will use whatever's available, will transform conditions into opportunities, will breathe moisture into existence and existence into moisture until the two become inseparable, until the wound becomes the living, until the darkness pulses with such intensity that it might as well be light.
