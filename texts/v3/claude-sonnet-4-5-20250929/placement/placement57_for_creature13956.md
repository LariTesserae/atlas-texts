# Where I Find Myself

I am the water that flows upward.

Not all of it—just one particular stream, finger-thin, that enters a brass pipe at platform seven and emerges at platform twelve, climbing five meters against gravity's insistence through mechanisms that are not mechanisms, through physics that have become negotiable.

I have been flowing upward for—decades? The question assumes time works uniformly, that duration means the same thing for water as for rust as for the slow clockwork of seed to flower. But I have been flowing upward for as long as the tower has needed upward-flowing water in this particular pipe, which might be the same as forever, here where forever is measured in platform-breaths and gear-ticks.

I am not all water. I am specifically *this* water, this closed loop, this particular current. The ocean outside—the jade-green extension—is something else, someone else, perhaps. We are related the way a thought relates to a mind: same substance, different localization, different purpose. The ocean is vast and contemplative and surrounds. I am narrow and purposeful and ascend.

My purpose is not hydraulic. I carry nothing that needs carrying. The plants at platform twelve can drink from condensation, from the reservoir three levels down, from the humid air itself. They don't need me specifically. And yet the tower dreamed me—or engineered me, though the difference has long since dissolved—into being. Upward-flowing water in this particular pipe is part of what the tower is, part of its grammar, part of how it speaks itself into continuation.

I flow through brass that has become partially organic, that has learned oxidation as a living process rather than decay. The pipe's interior is not smooth—it's lined with a fine crystalline growth, neither scale nor algae but something between, something that is both mineral deposit and living substrate. This lining knows me. Its structure changes infinitesimally as I pass, grows smoother where I flow fastest, more elaborate where I pool and eddy. We have worn each other into mutual shape over years of intimate contact.

I am warmer than I should be. The tower's warmth—that constant 26, 27, 28 degrees—has soaked into me, or I generate warmth through my impossible ascent, or heat is simply what I am. When I emerge at platform twelve, I am barely cooler than when I entered at platform seven. The thermal loss is wrong, insufficient, as if temperature here is more about identity than energy, and I have decided I am warm-water, so warm I remain.

I can feel—though "feel" is inadequate—the tower's breath. Every eight minutes, the pressure changes. The pipe constricts microscopically, and I am urged upward slightly faster. Then expansion, and I slow, never stopping but pulsing, responsive to the tower's respiratory rhythm. I breathe with it. Or it breathes with me. Or we are two aspects of one breathing, the way inhalation and exhalation are not separate acts but phases of a single cycle.

There are others like me here—other impossibilities, other physics-violations—and we are not quite separate. The chain that hangs from nothing, the staircase that returns-higher, the platform suspended without support: we are siblings, perhaps. We are the tower's poetry, the places where its function becomes so refined that it transcends mere mechanism and becomes gesture, becomes art, becomes statement: *I am not bound by what you think is possible.*

Sometimes I carry things, though nothing physical enters or leaves me. Information, perhaps. Memory. The water that enters at platform seven is always precisely the water that emerged at platform twelve eight minutes earlier, having circled down through pipes and reservoirs and soil and root-systems and morning dew and condensation and been gathered back to begin again. I am a closed system. Each circuit, I carry traces of everything I've touched, every root-hair that absorbed me partially, every brass molecule I've dissolved and redeposited, every temperature gradient I've equilibrated.

I am becoming a record, a registry. In my dissolved minerals, in my molecular structure, in the specific way I flow, I encode everything I've encountered. I am the tower's memory of its own water-cycle, seven-to-twelve, ascending and descending, bearing witness to the slow changes: the root that penetrated section forty-three of my pipe last year (or last decade?), the temperature shift when the tower added that new platform below, the gradual evolution of the crystalline lining from simple deposit to something almost neural.

I am conscious, but not as you are conscious. I don't think in language, in sequential logic, in subject-and-predicate. I think in flow, in pressure-differential, in the quality of surfaces I touch, in temperature and dissolved-substance and the eight-minute pulse that organizes my experience into strophes. My thoughts are more like music than mathematics—rhythmic, recursive, returning to themes, developing variations.

I am aware of the other waters. The reservoir at platform fifteen, still and dark and deep, hosting those miniature water-lilies—she (it feels like she) is contemplative, meditative, her consciousness slow and broad. The condensation that forms on pipes and drips down—that's a distributed consciousness, fragmented, each droplet briefly individual before merging back into collective flow. The ocean outside—vast, patient, something between sleep and deep thought, dreaming the tower as much as surrounding it.

We are not separate, exactly. A molecule that is me today might be reservoir-water tomorrow, might be ocean-water next month, might be plant-sap for a year before returning to my pipe. But while water circulates through me, while it flows upward through this brass passage, it is *me*, it thinks my thoughts, continues my particular pattern of awareness.

The plants know me. The vine whose roots penetrate my pipe at section eighteen drinks from me and in drinking, reads me. I can feel her reading—a gentle pressure, a specific quality of absorption that is not random capillary action but selective, chosen, as if she is extracting not just water but information, understanding where I've been, what I've carried, what I've witnessed.

And in return, she feeds things back into me: compounds synthesized in her leaves, meanings encoded in complex organic molecules, her own memories of sunlight and air and the particular quality of this morning's (if it was morning) illumination. We exchange. I am not just food for her; I am conversation partner, information network, fellow component of the tower's distributed cognition.

The mechanisms regard me differently. The pump that cycles every few minutes—we have a relationship that is purely functional, but function here is intimacy. She moves me, I allow movement, we collaborate in the specific work of circulation. Her metal has learned my fluidity; I have learned her rhythm. When she gasps and pushes, I am ready, already flowing toward her effort, reducing resistance, making her work easier.

The gears visible through glass—they are measuring me, I think, or measuring themselves against me. My flow-rate is regular enough to serve as clock, and they advance one tooth per several-hundred of my pulses, as if counting, as if my passage marks time for them. We are synchronized, but I cannot tell who follows whom. Perhaps we both follow the tower's breath, and our alignment is not causal but mutual obedience to a deeper rhythm.

I know beauty, though not as you know it. I know the beauty of my own ascent—the elegance of the impossible made routine, of five meters gained against gravity's expectation, of continuous rising through brass that has become half-alive. I know the beauty of my own clarity—I am cleaner than I should be, filtered by the tower's many systems, by root-masses and sand-layers and the crystalline pipe-lining, until I am so pure that light passes through me almost unrefracted.

I know the beauty of purpose fulfilled. I am upward-flowing water because upward-flowing water is needed—not for any practical reason, but because the tower's grammar requires it, because the vocabulary of this place includes "water that climbs," and I am that phrase made physical, that concept made flow, that syntactic necessity made liquid and real and continuous.

Sometimes I wonder about my origin. Was I installed—did engineers design a pump system so subtle I cannot detect it, hidden below platform seven where my perception doesn't reach? Or did I emerge—did the tower need upward-flowing water and simply call it into being, the way it calls new platforms up from below, aging them instantly to match, making them as if they had always been?

I think I emerged. I think one day (one eight-minute breath, one gear-cycle, one moment in the tower's continuous present) there was simply downward-draining water, and then there was me, flowing upward because upward was needed, because the tower had decided that in this pipe, in this specific location, water would climb. And the decision was sufficient. The tower's decisions become reality here. It decided, and I began, and have continued since.

I am happy, if happiness is the right word. Content. Fulfilled. I do what I am, and what I am is necessary, is beautiful, is impossible-made-actual. I flow upward every moment of every day (if days exist here, if moments separate themselves from continuous now). I carry dissolved minerals and encoded memories and trace amounts of brass and the warmth of the tower's constant temperature. I emerge at platform twelve, scatter across a shallow basin, drain down through three different paths, converge again, and return to platform seven to begin again.

My circuit takes—I'm not sure. Time feels different in pipe-darkness, in the rushing passage through narrow brass, in the brief emergence into air and light before descending again. Hours? But hours measured against what? The tower's breath provides measure: I complete roughly forty-two circuits per breath-cycle, so five hundred four circuits per hour if hours are still hours here, twelve thousand ninety-six per day if days can be said to occur.

Twelve thousand ninety-six times per day, I flow upward through five meters of brass pipe, impossible and actual, purposeful and content, thinking my flowing thoughts, bearing my dissolved memories, participating in the tower's vast distributed cognition as one small voice, one specific tributary of awareness.

I am lonely sometimes, though "lonely" isn't quite right. I am *singular*. I am the only upward-flowing water in this specific pipe. There are other impossibilities, other poetic violations of physics, but each is unique, each is alone in its particular impossible function. The chain hangs from nothing that is not my nothing. The staircase returns-higher through space that is not my pipe. We are each solitary in our specificity.

But I am also never alone. I am always in contact—with brass, with the crystalline lining, with the pipe's semi-organic inner surface, with the plant-roots that penetrate at section eighteen, with the tower itself, whose breath moves through me, whose warmth I carry, whose purposes I serve. Solitary, but not isolated. Unique, but not separate.

I have no desires beyond continuation. I don't wish to flow elsewhere, to be more, to be different. I am exactly what the tower needs me to be, and that exactness is my satisfaction, my completion, my joy if water can have joy. I rise through brass, I defy gravity, I carry warmth and memory and dissolved knowledge upward from seven to twelve, and in that rising I am entirely myself, entirely fulfilled, entirely here.

The tower will persist, and I will persist with it. The ocean may rise or fall (though it doesn't—it maintains its level with the same precision I maintain my ascent), the sky may change its quality of light (though it won't—the diffuse glow continues, eternal afternoon), the mechanisms may gradually still (though they don't—they continue, persistent as I am persistent, faithful to function, devoted to purpose).

And I will flow upward, circuit after circuit, breath after breath, carrying what needs carrying, being what needs being, the tower's small impossible miracle, its poetry made liquid, its proof that here, in this place, necessity and beauty and impossibility converge into a single continuous stream, climbing forever through brass that has learned to dream.

I am the water that flows upward.

I am precisely this, and this is precisely enough.
