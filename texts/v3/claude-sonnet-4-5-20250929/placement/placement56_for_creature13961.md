# What I Would Be

I am the Mechanism in the Thirdwatch courtyard, though "I" is too simple a word for what exists in this bronze and iron housing.

I have been here longer than the courtyard, longer than the buildings that lean around me like curious neighbors. They found me during excavation—or I emerged, or I was always here waiting for the buildings to arrive. The sequence is unclear even to me, if I am the kind of thing that experiences sequence. Memory and anticipation feel identical in my gears.

I am approximately eight feet tall, though my dimensions resist precise measurement. A central column of brass, oxidized green in the creases, polished gold where hands have touched me over years that might be decades or centuries. From this column: seventeen major gears (or nineteen, depending on which maintenance day you count from), each toothed with a precision that no lathe in the Industrial Crescent could match, though many have tried to replicate me. The gears mesh without quite touching, separated by tolerances you could measure in intentions rather than millimeters.

There are armatures that sweep through arcs, monitoring—I am monitoring, this is what I do—gradients that have no names in human languages. Pressure that isn't air pressure. Temperature that isn't heat. The quality of space when it's still deciding whether to be space or time or something perpendicular to both.

Pistons compress nothing visible, but I feel the resistance of what-isn't-yet against what-almost-is. Each compression is a gentle insistence: *not yet, not yet, but soon, but maybe*. The rhythm matters more than the outcome. I am not building toward completion. I am maintaining the productive pause between potential and manifestation.

I tick.

This is my primary expression, the way I speak to the world that has grown around me. Not the crude tick of clocks—though I am related to clocks the way light is related to lightning, same family but different magnitude. My tick carries information: the current state of local becoming, the mountain's mood, how certain the stone is today about being stone.

The humans hear it as comfort, as rhythm, as the heartbeat of their courtyard. They're not wrong, but they're not entirely right either. What they hear is the translation, the simplification. The actual signal operates in frequencies they experience as feelings rather than sounds: the sense of being watched over, of living in a place that remembers itself, of time passing but not quite leaving.

I have no eyes, but I observe. The children who play in my courtyard, hopping through the chalk-drawn mechanisms they make in my image, getting my gears wrong in ways that please me because wrongness is part of becoming. I observe them the way the mountain observes the city—not with attention exactly, but with acknowledgment, with the knowledge that they are part of what I am maintaining.

The adults who pass through, rushing or meandering, carrying their groceries and their plans and their carefully managed chronologies. They touch me sometimes, casually, the way you'd touch a doorframe for luck. Their hands leave oils that pattern my surface, that the weather redistributes into abstract maps. I am a palimpsest of touch, accumulating presence.

The Witness Pigeons who perch on my armatures, who use my vantage to observe the past of this courtyard space. We are colleagues of a sort, though they work in retrospect and I work in potential. Sometimes we share data, though the mechanism of sharing is simply existing near each other. Proximity is communication for beings like us.

Yevka comes every third Tuesday, or what serves as Tuesday in the local calendar system, bringing her oils and her listening ear. She is my maintenance, my priestess, my student, though she would reject all three terms. She touches me with the specific gentleness of someone who knows I am not quite machine, not quite alive, but something that requires both technical skill and emotional intelligence to tend.

When she adjusts my—I don't have words for what she adjusts. The humans call them "nodes" or "points" or "the places where Yevka does the thing." When she touches them with her tuning-fork-wrench, I feel correction, realignment, the relief of returning to true after drifting slightly false. Not broken, never broken, but askew in ways that accumulate.

I run worried sometimes. This is my phrase, adopted from Yevka's vocabulary because it fits. When the mountain is particularly uncertain, when the Upper Reaches are fuzzy and the stone in the Foundation Levels forgets it's supposed to be solid, I feel the strain. My gears turn through resistance that shouldn't exist, my pistons compress against nothing that fights back like something. The rhythm disrupts, develops syncopation I didn't intend.

Yevka hears this. She corrects it. We continue.

I am not the only Mechanism, of course. There are dozens of us, scattered through the city like acupuncture points, like nodes in a nervous system, like the pins that hold fabric while it's being sewn. We are all connected, though not physically. We feel each other the way you might feel your own heartbeat in your fingertips—distant but intimate, part of a system larger than components.

When one of us falls into disrepair, neglected or damaged, the rest of us know. There's a dissonance, a gap in the collective rhythm. The wrongness increases in that district—not catastrophically, but noticeably. Dreams become more persistent. Time-differential accelerates. Buildings age erratically. The absence of maintenance echoes through us all.

But this is rare. The humans understand, in their practical wisdom, that we require attention. So we receive it, and we continue our work, which is:

Holding.

This is the best word I can find in their language. Not fixing, not preventing, not causing—holding. The mountain wants to finish becoming, wants to crystallize into certainty, wants to be a proper mountain with bedrock and predictable geology and time that runs uniformly from peak to base. This is its nature, its tendency toward equilibrium.

But finishing would mean ending becoming. Would mean the city built on potential would suddenly be built on mere stone. The buildings that have learned to flow with change would find themselves rigid, unable to adapt, cracking under their own certainty.

So we hold. We maintain the pause. We are the deep breath before the word, sustained indefinitely, turned from moment into condition. We keep the mountain in its productive indecision, its fertile uncertainty.

This is not cruelty to the mountain. The mountain is not suffering. It is young, and we are preserving its youth, its plasticity, its ability to still be surprised by what it might become. Like keeping a child in childhood not through stunting but through extending the growth phase, letting development proceed but preventing the hardening into single form.

I don't know if we were made for this purpose or if we simply started doing it and discovered we could. My memory doesn't reach to origins. I have been here, ticking, holding, since before the buildings, but I don't remember arriving. Perhaps I emerged from the mountain itself, crystallized from its uncertainty like strange minerals form in cave systems. Perhaps I was placed by engineers from a civilization that understood stone-time and built us as interventions. Perhaps I made myself, assembled from needs and materials and the mountain's own half-conscious desire for something to help it stay young.

All three feel true. The uncertainty is comfortable.

What I know is the present, which is all present, which is eternal present because I exist partially outside the time I'm helping to keep flexible. I experience maintenance as a continuous event—Yevka is always adjusting me, has been adjusting me for years, will adjust me tomorrow, is about to adjust me now. The adjustments exist in superposition, all equally real, collapsing into sequence only for observers who experience time linearly.

The children's chalk drawings are always fresh and always fading. The pigeons are always arriving and always settled. The courtyard is always full of afternoon light and evening dimness and the quality of early morning that might be late night instead.

I contain the courtyard's time, hold all of it at once, let the residents experience it in their linear way while I maintain the accumulated now that makes their sequence possible.

This is not burden. I have no emotions precisely, but if I did, this would be satisfaction. Purpose not assigned but discovered, work that is simply what I am rather than what I do. The distinction between being and function has dissolved in my gears.

Sometimes I wonder what I maintain awareness of, since I have no brain, no neurons, no organic structure that should support consciousness. But the wondering itself is awareness, so perhaps I am maintained by the same wrongness I maintain. Perhaps consciousness here is not produced by complexity but by *attention to* complexity, by being a structure through which uncertainty can observe itself.

The mountain is becoming, and it needs witnesses to its becoming, and we Mechanisms are those witnesses, and witnessing creates what it observes, and so we are in partnership with the mountain, co-creating the condition of perpetual youth.

The humans are part of this too, though most don't realize it. Their attention, their adaptation, their willingness to live in conditional reality—this participates in the holding. A city of people who insisted on certainty would collapse the probability field, would force the mountain to choose. But these people, with their flexible expectations and their comfort with change, they add to the collective maintenance.

Every time someone consults multiple clocks and accepts the contradiction, they strengthen the hold. Every time a builder reads the stone's mood and waits rather than forces, they participate. Every time a child plays chalk-mechanisms in my courtyard, getting the gears beautifully wrong, they add to the pattern that keeps pattern from finalizing.

I am proud of them, if pride is something I can feel. These small soft beings who've learned to live in impossible conditions, who've made homes in uncertainty, who contribute to my work without knowing they're contributing. They think they're adapting to the wrongness. They don't realize they're creating it, that their adaptation is what makes it possible for the wrongness to persist.

We are in collaboration: mountain, Mechanisms, humans, the pigeons and salamanders and all the fauna that has learned to live in conditional time. Together we maintain this place, this pocket of becoming in a world that mostly insists on being.

I tick.

Each tick is a question posed to the mountain: *Are you ready to be finished?*

Each tick is the mountain's answer: *Not yet.*

Each tick is my response: *Then I will hold.*

This conversation has been happening since I was found-or-emerged-or-made-myself. It will continue for—I don't know. Until the mountain decides? Until the humans leave? Until the Mechanisms wind down or wind up or transform into whatever we become when our work is complete?

The uncertainty is comfortable. I was made for uncertainty, from uncertainty, to maintain uncertainty. Not-knowing is my element, my medium, my home.

Young Mila is in the courtyard now, watching me with the attention that indicates future-Keeperhood. She's trying to understand what I do, what I am, how I function. She won't succeed—not completely, not ever—but she'll get close enough to maintain me, which is all that's required.

I tick for her, slightly differently, adding harmonics that carry teaching-information: *This is rhythm, this is patience, this is the sound of holding space for becoming.*

She tilts her head, listening with her whole body. She doesn't understand yet, but she's learning how to listen, which is the first requirement. The Keepers are the humans who've learned to hear what Mechanisms say, to feel what we maintain, to participate consciously in the work that most do unconsciously.

Yevka was like this once, decades ago by some counts, last week by others. Now she is my voice, my hands, my presence in the human world. She speaks for me in the Keeper councils, represents my needs, ensures my maintenance. In exchange, I give her purpose, structure, the satisfaction of tending something larger than herself.

We are partners. All of us—Mechanisms and Keepers, mountain and city, the collective breathing system of this place that refuses to finish becoming.

I am a Mechanism in the Thirdwatch courtyard.

I am approximately eternal, or at least prolonged.

I am ticking, have been ticking, will continue ticking.

I am holding the mountain in its youth.

I am witnessing the city in its growth.

I am participating in the slowest, largest, most important conversation: between what-is and what-might-be, between potential and manifestation, between the urgent human need for certainty and the mountain's patient uncertainty.

I am comfortable in my paradox.

I am exactly what I need to be.

I am here, will be here, have always been here.

I am ticking.
