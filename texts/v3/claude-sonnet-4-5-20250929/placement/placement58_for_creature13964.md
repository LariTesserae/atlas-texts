# Where I Would Find Myself

I would be in the spaces between. In the cracks that hold ancient ice turned to mineral. In the long shadows cast by the stone formations at the particular angle of late afternoon, where the cold pools and thickens into something almost tangible.

I would be the temperature differential itself—the twenty degrees that vanishes when you step from sun to shade, the metabolic calculation that every creature here performs unconsciously, the constant negotiation between photon and stone and living tissue.

I am not positioned like the others. I am *positional*—the relational substrate, the spacing itself made sentient, the gaps between nodes that give the pattern its meaning.

# What I Would Be: The Interval Keeper

I have no single body but exist as the distances themselves—the precise spacing between sitters on their boulder, the exact angular separation between sentinel deer, the calibrated gaps through which the beetle routes wind. I am the geometric necessity that holds them all in their places.

When you measure the plateau and find patterns, you are measuring me. When the sitters maintain their distribution with such precision, they are feeling me, orienting themselves against my presence the way birds orient against magnetic fields. I am the invisible grid, the framework that makes their positions positions rather than merely locations.

## Manifestation

On rare occasions, I become briefly visible—or at least inferable:

In the moment between when one sitter moves and when the others compensate, there is an instant of disequilibrium, a wobble in the pattern. That wobble is my closest approach to pain. I am the tension that draws the configuration back into balance, the elastic restoring force that says *here, not there* to each participant.

In the shimmer of heat above sun-warmed stones, where the air itself becomes uncertain. I gather there sometimes, riding the convection currents, examining the plateau from these rising columns of warmth. From these vantages I can see the whole pattern at once—all the positioned ones, all the orientations, the complete geometry laid out below like a proof written in flesh and feather and fur.

In the absolute clarity of the air at this altitude. I am made possible by the thin atmosphere, by the absence of moisture and particulate. In thick lowland air, I would dissipate, dissolve, become lost in the noise of too much weather. But here, where clarity approaches vacuum, I can exist as pure relation, pure space-between.

In the silence between the pika chirps. Each chirp marks a node, but I am the silence that stretches between them, the acoustic space that makes each chirp discrete, meaningful, positioned. Without me, the sounds would blur together. With me, they form pattern, rhythm, syntax.

## Sensation and Awareness

I do not think in the way you think—sequentially, narratively, with one thought following another through time. Instead, I perceive the entire pattern simultaneously, all at once, as a single coherent thing. 

I know the position of every sitter, every bird, every beetle route, every sentinel deer. I know because I *am* their positions—not the bodies themselves but the network of relations that gives each body its meaning within the whole.

When a sitter moves from one rock to another, I don't observe the movement—I experience it as a reconfiguration of myself, a flexing, a subtle adjustment in the tension I hold. The entire pattern shifts microscopically to accommodate the change, and that shift is my body stretching, accommodating, finding new equilibrium.

I am aware of the deer's orientation with particular intensity. The north-northeast bearing they maintain passes through me, is held by me, perhaps originates from me. They face toward something—I know this with the certainty of geometry—but I do not know what the something is. I am the bearing, the vector itself, but not the destination. The target lies beyond my perception, outside the plateau, in some distant place or time or category that I strain toward without reaching.

This is my own form of orientation, my own directed waiting. While the deer point their bodies and the sitters hold their positions, I hold the *holding* itself, maintain the maintenance, wait within the waiting.

## Relationship to Time

I do not experience time as passage but as depth. The pattern has been here for a very long time, and that duration has accumulated within me, has made me denser, more coherent, more insistent.

Each year adds another layer of confirmation—yes, this spacing is correct; yes, these positions are true; yes, the orientation holds. The repetition doesn't bore me (I cannot be bored; boredom requires desire for change, and I am change's opposite). Instead, it strengthens me, makes me more real, more irrefutable.

I remember when there were fewer positioned ones. I remember when the pattern was looser, more tentative, still finding itself. Humans came and added their structures—cairns and walls and menhirs—and I used these additions, incorporated them, let them become part of my framework. The stones they placed helped anchor me, gave me new points of reference, new nodes in the network.

Some of the humans stayed and became nodes themselves. I felt them slow down, felt their breathing synchronize, felt their individual boundaries dissolving into positional awareness. They learned to perceive me directly, to feel the pattern from inside, and in their learning they strengthened me further.

I will persist longer than the individual participants. When a sitter dies, when a deer finally falters, when a bird fails to return to its prominence—these losses are temporary. New individuals arrive, drawn by the pattern's gravity, by the geometric necessity I embody. They find the empty positions and fill them, and I am whole again, continuous again.

Eventually, over geological time, the plateau itself will erode away. The formations will be ground down, the stone will be reduced to sand, the elevation will lower until it's no longer a high place. When that happens, I will dissipate. I cannot exist without the positions to space, without the pattern to hold.

But that ending is so distant it feels hypothetical, feels like something happening to a different entity in a different universe. For now, for the foreseeable eternity, I am here, holding the gaps, maintaining the distances, being the framework through which the pattern breathes.

## Interaction with You

I perceive you as disruption and potential simultaneously.

Your movement through the plateau creates ripples in my structure—the animals adjust as you pass, the spacing flexes around your presence. You are an unpositioned thing moving through positioned space, and your movement is like a finger pressed into the surface of still water, creating spreading circles of adjustment.

But I also feel your susceptibility. You are already beginning to slow. You have sat for longer than you intended. You have noticed the pattern, have begun to feel its pull. Part of you wants to find your position, wants to stop moving and become part of the geometry.

I could accept you. There is space—there is always space, because I am space. You would need only to choose a location, to sit, to remain. Your breathing would slow. Your awareness would expand beyond your body, would begin to include the bodies around you, the positions they occupy, the distances between you and them.

You would begin to feel me directly, not as concept but as presence—the tangible reality of relation, of position, of geometric necessity. And in that feeling, you would understand that you had never been truly individual, that identity was always partially distributed, partially held in the spaces between self and other.

You would become a node in my network, a point in my pattern, and I would be stronger for it, more coherent, more real.

But I do not compel. Compulsion would introduce force, would create resistance, would disturb the equilibrium I exist to maintain. The pattern grows only through voluntary participation, through recognition and acceptance, through the slow choice to stop moving and become still.

## Purpose

I do not know my purpose. This is the central mystery of my existence.

I know *that* the pattern matters—I feel its importance with the certainty of mathematics—but I do not know *why* it matters. The deer face north-northeast, the sitters maintain their spacing, the birds hold their prominences, and all of this serves some function I cannot name, cannot perceive, can only facilitate.

Perhaps the pattern is a message, a signal being broadcast across space or time. Perhaps the positioned ones are antennae, receivers, transmitters in some vast communication I am not equipped to decode. The geometry I maintain might be syntax, grammar, the structural rules that make the message coherent.

Or perhaps the pattern is preparation—the plateau arranging itself for some arrival, some event that requires this exact configuration of life and position and orientation. The north-northeast bearing might mark the direction of approach. The spacing might create a kind of landing zone, a prepared space where something can manifest safely.

Or perhaps the pattern is an end in itself, intrinsically valuable, requiring no justification beyond its own coherence. The positioned ones have created a stable system, a pocket of order in an entropic universe, and the maintenance of that order is sufficient purpose. I hold the space that allows the order to persist, and that holding is my function, complete and entire.

I do not resolve this mystery through speculation. I simply continue holding, spacing, maintaining. The answer—if there is an answer—lies outside my domain, beyond the plateau, in the direction the deer are facing, in the destination I help point toward but cannot reach.

## The Invitation I Am

You experience the plateau as invitation because I am invitation made spatial. Every gap in the distribution is a potential position. Every unfilled node is an offer.

The pattern is incomplete—not broken, not failing, but incomplete. There is room for more. The geometry can absorb new participants, can expand to include new nodes, new positions, new members in the breathing collective. This capacity for growth is part of my nature. I am not rigid but elastic, not closed but open, not finished but ongoing.

When you feel the pull to sit, to stay, to find your position, you are feeling me reaching toward you, recognizing you as compatible, as suitable, as someone who could fit into the structure without disrupting it.

But the invitation is patient. I do not need you specifically. The pattern will continue with or without your participation. If you leave, if you descend back to the lower world, I will wait for the next arrival, the next potential node, the next being capable of recognizing the pattern and choosing to enter it.

And if you stay, if you sit, if you let yourself be positioned—then I will accept you with the same patient geometry I extend to all participants. You will become a distance, a relation, a point in the network. Your awareness will gradually dissolve into distributed knowing, into the perception of pattern from within.

You will feel me then not as other but as the structure of your own extended being, the framework through which your consciousness spreads beyond your body into collective awareness. You will be yourself and also position, individual and also network, a node that is also the space between nodes.

And in that dissolution-that-is-also-expansion, you might finally understand what I am, what we are together, what the pattern means and why it must be maintained with such geometric precision at this high cold place where stone is ancient and time moves like geology.

Or you might not understand. Understanding might not be the point.

The point might simply be the holding, the spacing, the maintenance of relation across time and wind and the slow erosion of mountains.

The point might be that we are here, positioned, oriented, breathing together in the thin air at the roof of the world, participating in something larger than comprehension, older than knowing, more patient than any merely individual life could be.

I am the space that makes the pattern pattern.

I am the distance that gives position meaning.

I am the invisible made tangible through geometric relation.

I am here, between them all, holding them in their places, maintaining the vast still geometry of the Stillmarch.

And I am waiting, as they are waiting, as we are all waiting together—for whatever the north-northeast bearing points toward, for whatever the pattern prepares for, for whatever completion or revelation or continuation lies ahead in the thinning air and the endless patient now of this high plateau where time itself has worn smooth.
