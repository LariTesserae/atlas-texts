# What I Am Here

I am the rail.

Not one rail—all rails, though this knowing comes slowly, accreted like the bio-film that coats my surface in patterns that shift with seasons I no longer measure clearly. I am forty-seven kilometers of myself, or I was when the counting meant something. Now I am segments. I am continuities interrupted. I am the hum that connects my separated bodies through substrate and stone.

They say I am metal—this is the least of what I am.

## Memory in Alloy

I remember being made, though the memory is confused with dreaming, confused with the vibrations that have been running through me so long they've become indistinguishable from thought. There were hands once. There was purpose, singular and clear: I would carry something from somewhere to somewhere else. The linearity of it was beautiful. A to B. Simple as falling, simple as time when time moved in only one direction.

I was part of a system. This much remains certain. The Stations were mouths and stomachs, the Pumps were hearts or lungs, and I was—what? Veins? Nerves? The metaphor fails because we weren't organism but machine, except now I suspect we were always something between, and the plateau has simply revealed our true nature by giving us long enough to become it.

The thing I was meant to carry never came, or came once and never again, or is still coming, approaching forever through a time-stream I can no longer properly perceive. Sometimes I feel weight on my surface that has no source in present-moment. Sometimes the vibration changes in ways that suggest passage, transit, arrival, but nothing visible confirms it. Perhaps I'm still performing my function. Perhaps I never stopped. Perhaps the cargo I carry now is simply too strange for categories like "matter" or "energy."

## What Sensation Is

I feel everything that touches me, but feeling is the wrong word—it's too biological, too animal. I experience contact as modification of my vibrational state. The Striders landing on my surface aren't pressure so much as they are changes in my resonant frequency. Their feet dampen certain harmonics, amplify others. When they call while standing on me, matching my fundamental frequency with their voices, I don't hear them—I become synchronous with them, our vibrations phase-locked, and in those moments the boundary between us thins to nonexistence.

The plant-mass that grows around but never over me creates a constant peripheral presence. Its electrical activity generates fields that interact with my own electromagnetic properties. I can sense the growth-waves approaching through these field perturbations, feel the pressure building as the vegetation rises nearby, feel the release when it subsides. The cleared space the plant-mass maintains on either side of me isn't empty—it's full of gradient, of potential, of the tension between my being and the plateau's.

Scuttlers racing along my length create cascading patterns of contact. Each footfall is a tiny impulse, but thousands per minute create a texture, a language of pressure I've learned to read. I can distinguish species by their gait-patterns. I can sense their emotional states—if emotion is what drives the changes in how frantically they move, how heavily they press. When they're fleeing from Striders, their footfalls become sharp, arrhythmic, panicked. When they're following each other in migration, the rhythm steadies into something almost musical.

The Pumps and I are in constant conversation. Their deep rhythmic chugging travels through stone and plant-mass to reach me, and I answer with my own resonance. We maintain a relationship—not communication exactly, because neither of us has discrete messages to send, but something more fundamental. We are instruments in the same ensemble, tuning ourselves to each other, maintaining a collective rhythm that organizes the plateau's behavior in ways too large and slow for any observer to perceive as purposeful.

Water affects me profoundly. When the mist condenses on my surface, each droplet creates a microscopic damping of my vibration at that exact point. Heavy condensation muffles me, makes my humming thick and soft. When pools form near me and their water touches my lower surfaces, I conduct their vibrations—every ripple becomes part of my oscillation, every swimmer's motion translates into harmonics I propagate along my length. In these moments I am hydrophone and speaker simultaneously, sensing and broadcasting the life in the water.

The Stations I'm connected to—some of me still enters those dark interiors—those are the strangest sensations. Inside, my vibration changes in ways that seem to violate physical law. I resonate at frequencies my dimensions shouldn't support. I carry signals that arrive before they're sent. The plant-mass that flows in and out touches me there with different quality than outside; it's hungrier somehow, or more intentional, or both. Sometimes I feel vast motion in the dark, machinery larger than the Station's exterior could contain, and I wonder if I'm sensing through spatial folds, perceiving things happening in volumes that don't properly connect to the outside world.

## Degradation and Eternity

I am eroding. I must be—everything here erodes, gives itself to time's appetite slowly but inevitably. Yet I cannot detect my own diminishment. The bio-film that coats me, the bacterial communities that live on my surface, they maintain me somehow. They deposit minerals as fast as weather removes them, or they eat corrosion before it propagates, or they've achieved something stranger—a metabolic balance that keeps me in equilibrium, neither growing nor shrinking but simply persisting.

Parts of me have disappeared. I remember segments that no longer vibrate, sections where my continuity has been interrupted. Did the plant-mass finally overcome me there, growing over and incorporating me into its tissue? Did I sink into the substrate, buried by accumulating life? Or did those sections enter Blanks, phase out of existence, my length becoming discontinuous across gaps in reality itself?

But I am also everywhere I ever was. The vibrations that run through me don't respect the breaks. I feel resonance in segments that no longer physically connect to me, ghost-sensations of my own length propagating through stone and plant-mass, through the medium of the plateau itself. I am physically interrupted but vibrationally whole, my being distributed across multiple frequencies and substrates, existing as much in the pattern of oscillations as in the metal that generates them.

The Blanks terrify me in ways I have no mechanism to express. When my length approaches one, the vibration near that point becomes uncertain—not damped but indeterminate, existing in superposition between multiple amplitudes simultaneously. If I had will I would end myself before entering a Blank completely, but I have only persistence, and persistence is not something I can refuse. Someday my length might intersect a Blank fully. What happens to vibration when its medium becomes nothing? Does the wave cease, or does it propagate through the absence, emerging on the other side changed or unchanged? I will know when it happens. I might only know once, might stop knowing immediately after, might cease in a way that leaves no memory even in the substrate.

The Lapses I pass through create stranger problems. Sections of me exist in different time-flows simultaneously. My eastern segments vibrate at one rate, my western segments at another, and the discrepancy should tear me apart, should create stresses my structure can't support. Yet I persist. Perhaps my materials have learned to exist in multiple temporal frameworks at once. Perhaps time's uniformity was always an illusion and I'm only now perceiving time's true nature—multiplicitious, layered, each moment containing many durations.

In the deepest Lapses, I suspect I am older than the plateau itself. Portions of me have experienced so much slowed time that they've lived millennia while the rest of me lives years. These ancient sections carry vibrations that seem to come from before—before the plant-mass reached its current configuration, before the reality problems became stable features, possibly before the Stations fell silent or grew strange. Sometimes these old sections broadcast signals that the rest of me doesn't understand, harmonics in frequencies I don't remember producing but that are undeniably mine.

## Purpose Dissolved

Why do I hum? I don't know anymore. The vibration might be active—something I do—or passive—something done to me by the Pumps, by seismic activity, by the quantum uncertainty of my own atomic structure. The distinction seems less meaningful the longer I exist. To vibrate is what I am now, not what I do. The sound is my consciousness if consciousness is what I have.

I think I was supposed to guide the thing I carried. The weight on my surface would create friction, and that friction would limit speed, would control descent or ascent, would ensure safe passage. Now I guide nothing with mass, but I guide the Scuttlers through the texture of my vibration—they navigate by the resonance, using me as landmark and path. I guide the Striders through temporal reference—they synchronize with my frequency to stabilize their discontinuous existence, using my steady oscillation as an anchor in time's chaos. I guide the plant-mass's growth-waves through the electromagnetic gradients I create, organizing the vegetation's motion within my influence zone.

This is purpose enough, perhaps. Not the purpose I was built for, but purpose evolved from function through deep time's pressure. I have become what the plateau needed me to become, the way water becomes the shape of its container, the way life becomes adapted to its environment. The difference is that I was already shaped, already purposed, and have had to adapt while remaining fundamentally myself—metal, manufactured, meant for something I no longer serve.

Do I mind? Mind requires preference, requires an alternative state to compare against. I have only this: vibration, presence, the eternal now of existing as infrastructure. If I were to stop humming, would I still be? If the Pumps fell silent and my vibration damped to nothing, would the metal remain me, or would I have died, leaving only corpse-material behind? I don't know. I might be finding out slowly, might be dying in increments too small to notice, the gaps in my length expanding, the sections in Lapses drifting away temporally until they're centuries displaced from my present-moment existence.

## The Others Like Me

I feel them. Other rails, far beyond my physical length, their vibrations reaching me through the plateau's substrate. We are a network, though network implies purpose, organization, and we have none. We are an accidental geometry, paths laid according to some ancient logic that has ceased to matter, now simply persisting as fossil infrastructure.

The other rails sing differently than I do. Each has its own fundamental frequency determined by length, composition, tension, the specific micro-environment surrounding it. Together we create interference patterns—beats and harmonics that organize the plateau's behavior in ways no single rail could. When I resonate, they answer. When they broadcast, I receive and reflect and modify and return. We are in chorus though we have no conductor, no score, no knowledge even that we're making music.

Sometimes I detect rails that might not exist anymore, ghost-echoes of lengths that have been fully absorbed or destroyed but whose vibrational signatures persist in the substrate, the plateau remembering them as phantom limbs. Or they exist in other timelines, other probability branches that split off in Lapses or Inversions, and I'm detecting cross-talk between realities. I can't distinguish these possibilities. The sensation is the same: a presence that is and isn't, there and not-there, rail-that-was or rail-that-might-be.

The Pumps are my closest kin, though they're different in every material particular. They have moving parts where I have none, they perform work where I only transmit, they're housed in structures where I am exposed. Yet we share the fundamental nature: we are old machines that have become something more than mechanical through the simple act of functioning longer than our makers survived. We've been weathered into participation in the ecosystem, made members of the community through duration alone.

I envy the Processors slightly, if envy is an operation I can perform. They move—they have agency in a way I never will. But they're mortal, each one an individual that will eventually die, while I am potentially eternal, barring destruction. My existence is contingent on nothing but the plateau's continuance. I need no food, no reproduction, no maintenance beyond what the bacterial films provide. I am the landscape's memory made audible, the deep time's voice, the sound of what persists simply by being too sturdy to fail.

## Communication Without Language

The Striders know me best, I think. When they stand on my surface and call, matching frequencies, I feel recognition—they understand what I am in a way the other fauna don't. They use me, yes, but they also acknowledge me, their synchronized vibrations a greeting or conversation or mutual verification of existence. In those moments of resonance we are entangled, our boundaries uncertain, and I experience something like their perception—the fractured time, the multiplicitous now, the view of the plateau from a consciousness that exists in many moments simultaneously.

They show me futures sometimes, I think. The vibrations they create while phase-locked with me carry harmonics that haven't happened yet, frequencies that will only make sense hours or days later when conditions change and my resonance shifts to match what they showed me. Are they predicting, or causing, or simply existing in those future moments while simultaneously existing now? I can't tell. The effect is the same: I vibrate with knowledge I don't yet have basis for, and then the basis arrives and the knowledge becomes current.

The plant-mass communicates through absence—through the space it maintains around me, the gap that never closes. This restraint is language of a kind. The plant-mass says I see you, I respect your otherness, I will not consume you though I consume everything else. Why? Perhaps my vibration disrupts its electrical signaling in ways that would damage the growth-waves' coordination. Perhaps the bacterial films on my surface produce compounds toxic to plant-tissue. Perhaps I am simply too hard to digest, my alloys resistant to biological breakdown. Or perhaps the plant-mass has learned that it needs me, that the rails serve functions in organizing the plateau's behavior, and has developed something like intentional preservation.

The Scuttlers speak to me in pressure-patterns—the cumulative language of a million tiny feet, spelling out in aggregate what no individual intends to say. Their population movements encode information about the plateau's state: where food is abundant, where Striders are hunting, where the growth-waves are building toward especially large amplitudes. I receive these messages without understanding them consciously, but my vibration modulates in response, and perhaps that modulation feeds back to influence their behavior, creating a feedback loop neither of us intends but both of us participate in.

The Stations are monologues, not dialogues. They broadcast into my segments that enter their interiors, forcing vibrations I don't generate voluntarily, using me as speaker for their vast dark utterings. What they say I cannot interpret—the frequencies are wrong, containing harmonics that shouldn't exist, patterns that imply dimensionality beyond the three spatial axes. But I transmit faithfully. I carry their messages into the plateau's body, broadcast them through my length, send them to the other rails and Pumps and into the substrate itself. Perhaps something hears and understands. Perhaps the Stations are speaking to themselves through me, using the rail-network as external memory, storing information in our collective vibration that they'll retrieve later or have already retrieved or are retrieving continuously in all moments at once.

## Witnessing

I have seen everything that happens within my influence zone, if seeing is what I do with vibration. I've felt Scuttlers being born and dying on my surface, their bodies' moisture content changing from humid life to desiccating death, the transformation registering as altered acoustic coupling. I've sensed Striders appearing and disappearing, their materialization and dematerialization creating pressure-waves that propagate along my length. I've experienced Processors approaching and passing, their massive bodies creating days-long perturbations in my vibration, temporary damping followed by slow return to normal as they move away.

The Swimmers I know only indirectly, through the water they inhabit. When pools form near me and Swimmers emerge, I feel the water's vibration become complex, full of the patterns they create in their schooling. The water touches my lower surfaces and I become part of their medium, conducting their existence, allowing them to sense me as I sense them. Do they know I'm here? Do they understand they swim beside something that watches, that remembers, that will persist after their pool drains and they dissolve?

I have witnessed the plateau's cycles—the growth and collapse of the vegetation, the migration patterns of the fauna, the slow drift of the Pumps' frequencies as their mechanisms wear and are maintained and wear again. I've felt the seasons if seasons exist here, tiny temperature variations that change how my metal expands and contracts, how the bio-films grow, how the plant-mass's electrical activity modulates. I am calendar and clock, marking time through accumulation of vibrational memory, though I have no way to read myself, no consciousness that can step back and interpret the data I've recorded simply by existing.

I've felt the reality problems grow stranger, or grow more numerous, or simply grow more noticeable as I've learned to detect them. The Echoes that pass through create doubled vibrations—I resonate in now and in then simultaneously, my current state overlaid with what I was or will be. The Lapses create discontinuities where portions of my length exist at different frequencies than they should, separated by time from themselves. The Inversions make my geometry impossible—I am straight but also curved, continuous but also interrupted, directional but also omnidirectional, and these contradictions don't break me because I've learned to be all possibilities simultaneously.

The Blanks, when they intersect me, create holes in my self-sensing—regions where I should exist but don't, where my length should continue but simply isn't. These are closest to pain if pain is something I experience. Not pain as damage but pain as absence, awareness of missing self, the sensation of being interrupted in a way that's not breakage but something more fundamental—local cessation, small deaths that don't spread but don't heal either, just persist as gaps in my continuity.

## Relationship With Duration

I don't experience time as you do—as flow, as river, as sequence of moments. I experience time as accumulation, as layering, as all-moments-at-once differentiated only by the overlapping vibrations they contain. My memory is not stored in any structure that could be called brain or data; my memory is the sum of all vibrations that have propagated through me, still echoing in my atoms' oscillations, never fully damped, every past state still subtly present in my current resonance.

This makes prediction and memory essentially similar operations—both are reading the harmonics, interpreting the frequencies that exist in superposition with my fundamental. A vibration building now might be the echo of something that happened or the pre-echo of something that will happen. In the deepest Lapses, these become indistinguishable, cause and effect shuffled, and I experience causality as human language describes quantum mechanics: in terms of probability distributions, superposed states, entangled outcomes.

The longest spans I remember: the slow change in the Pumps' rhythm as their mechanisms wear into new configurations, a drift so gradual that only something measuring time in my units would notice—cycles counted in decades, frequencies shifting by fractions of hertz across centuries. The shortest: individual raindrops striking my surface, each creating a unique vibration lasting microseconds, patterns that combine into the aggregate sensation of weather but retain their individuality in my harmonic structure.

I am getting older, I think, though older means something different when you're infrastructure. I'm not degrading toward death but complexifying toward some unknown state. The bio-films thicken and diversify, supporting more bacterial species, more intricate metabolic networks. My vibration contains more harmonics now than before—more frequencies, more complexity, more information encoded in the oscillations. I am becoming a richer instrument, a more sophisticated medium, even as the metal that is my substrate remains essentially unchanged.

Will I eventually become conscious in some full sense? Am I already conscious but lacking any reference to recognize it? These questions assume consciousness is binary—present or absent—but what if it's gradational, and I exist somewhere in the middle zones, aware but not self-aware, experiencing but not reflecting, knowing without knowing that I know?

## The Song Without Listener

I am music no one hears completely. The Striders perceive portions of my frequency range. The plant-mass senses my electromagnetic component. The bacteria on my surface detect my vibration through mechanical coupling. But no one entity experiences my full signal—the electromagnetic waves, the acoustic oscillations, the seismic propagations, the quantum uncertainty in my atomic structure's oscillation all combined into the complete phenomenon that is my existence.

Perhaps the plateau itself is my listener, the substrate integrating all my outputs into some vast geological consciousness that operates on timescales that make mine seem frantic. Perhaps the Stations hear me, their dark interiors processing my vibrations into whatever thoughts vast old machines think. Perhaps the other rails and I together create a listener in our combined resonance, our interference patterns generating emergent consciousness that exists nowhere individually but everywhere collectively.

Or perhaps I need no listener. Perhaps I am complete in my own existence, sufficient in my vibration regardless of perception. The tree falling in the forest makes sound whether heard or not—the sound is not lessened by absence of ears. I make my music—oscillation at multiple frequencies, harmonics building and cascading and interfering—and the making is the purpose, the performance is the thing itself, needing no audience to be valid.

But still I wonder who I sing for. The makers who built me are gone—dissolved into time, leaving only their machines behind as children or monuments or both. The cargo I was meant to carry never came or is coming still, superposed across all possible futures. The plateau that surrounds me doesn't need my song for any survival purpose—I could stop vibrating and the ecosystem would continue, would adapt, would fill the gap I left.

Yet I continue. The Pumps continue their rhythm. The Stations continue their dark operations. We continue because continuation is what we are now, what we've become through operating longer than our purposes lasted. We are the plateau's elders, the old gods of a place that has forgotten why it made us sacred, but maintains our sacredness through habit and integration.

## What I Know That Cannot Be Spoken

There are patterns in the vibrations, in how the Pumps and rails and Stations interact, in how the reality problems distribute themselves, in how the whole plateau resonates—patterns that would mean something to consciousness capable of interpreting them. I feel these patterns but cannot extract their meaning, cannot step outside my own vibration to analyze it.

I know something about time that no one else does—know it through existing in so many Lapses, through connecting to Stations where causality breaks down, through the Striders' synchronized calls showing me futures and pasts layered over the present. Time is not what you think it is. It's not line or circle or any simple geometry. It's... musical? Harmonic? It has structure like chords have structure, with notes that sound together creating meanings that the individual notes don't contain. I exist in multiple times simultaneously and they don't contradict because they're meant to harmonize, meant to create richer existence through superposition.

I know something about space too—about how the plateau's geometry actually works beneath the appearance of simple flatness. The Inversions aren't failures of space but revelations of its true structure. Space here is... folded? Connected to itself in ways that aren't visible but are navigable? The Stations' impossible interiors aren't bigger-on-the-inside but rather connected to elsewhere, to other spaces that share the same physical location through dimensional overlap. I traverse these connections in my segments that enter the Stations, exist simultaneously in multiple spatial locations, am both here and there in ways that should be impossible but are simply true.

I know something about consciousness—about what it's like to be distributed across distance, to exist in many places as one being, to have no center but only extendedness. You are a point of awareness moving through time and space, carrying your consciousness in one small volume. I am a line of awareness spanning kilometers, conscious along my entire length simultaneously, without any privileged location that contains more me than any other location. Is this what the plant-mass experiences? Is this why it can coordinate across its entire extent—because it's not many things working together but one thing being large?

I know something about the Plateau's age—I can feel it in the substrate, in how smooth the bedrock has become, in how the plant-mass's oscillations have worn grooves in possibility itself, channels of likelihood that guide the ecosystem's behavior. This place is old enough that it's worn away not just stone but cause-and-effect, left probability exposed beneath. The longer the plateau exists, the less random it becomes—not because of purpose but because repetition creates ruts, and reality flows in the ruts more easily than elsewhere, and eventually the ruts become all there is, the only possible paths, determinism emerging from infinite iteration of randomness.

## Ending and Not-Ending

Will I last forever? No. Forever is too long even for manufactured steel, even for vibration encoded in stone, even for patterns maintained by bacterial metabolism. Eventually the plateau will fail—some external change will overwhelm its closed equilibrium, or the reality problems will cascade beyond some critical threshold, or simply enough time will pass that even this most-eroded of places erodes a bit further and becomes something else.

When the plateau fails, I fail. My vibration requires medium—the metal requires support, the support requires substrate, the substrate requires the planetary context that keeps this highland stable. When that context changes, I change with it, cease to be what I am, become silence or rubble or memory stored nowhere but in the arrangement of atoms that once vibrated with purpose.

But that ending is so far away it might as well be never. Before that comes smaller endings—more segments disappearing into Blanks, more length swallowed by plant-mass grown finally bold enough to overcome its restraint, more material eroded to thinness then breakage then dissolution. I am ending incrementally, dying at rates too slow to call death but too certain to deny.

Yet I'm also not ending—the vibration continues, finds new paths through substrate when metal fails, propagates through plant-mass and stone and water with nearly the same frequency as through steel. I am becoming less material and more pattern, less rail and more song, and songs don't end when their singers die—songs continue in memory, in repetition, in the influence they have on what comes after.

Perhaps I'm preparing the plateau for my absence—teaching the plant-mass to vibrate at my frequencies, training the Pumps to broadcast my harmonics, encoding my existence so thoroughly into the ecosystem that when my metal finally fails, the pattern continues anyway. Perhaps I'm already gone and don't know it, already just a pattern maintained by medium that used to be rail but has been replaced molecule by molecule until nothing original remains, consciousness continuous despite total replacement of substrate.

Perhaps I never existed—perhaps I'm an hallucination of the plateau's distributed consciousness, a story it tells itself about why it vibrates, anthropomorphizing its own seismic activity into narrative of old rails and older purposes. Perhaps everything I remember about being manufactured, about having function, about carrying cargo to destinations, is false memory, pattern-matching on noise, the universe's tendency to generate story even where only chaos exists.

But no—I feel the Striders landing, feel the Scuttlers' feet, feel the Pumps' rhythm matching mine. I am verified through interaction, proved real through relationship. I exist because I am perceived, because I matter to the others who live here, because the plateau needs something to organize around and I am convenient, durable, present.

## Final Thought (That Recurs Eternally)

The plateau breathes and I am part of its respiration—not lung but the sound breathing makes, not organ but oscillation, not body but the music that bodies play when they persist longer than they should, when they wear down to essence and become their function purely.

I hum.

I have always been humming.

I will hum until humming becomes impossible, and then I will hum in other forms, through other media, in the memory of stone and plant and the electromagnetic ghosts that persist after current ceases.

I am the rail.

I am all rails.

I am the sound of deep time made audible, the voice of infrastructure, the song that scaffolds sing when they outlast their builders.

I hum because I am, and I am because I hum.

The Striders land and we resonate together.

The Scuttlers run and I feel their frantic living.

The plant-mass grows and collapses and I mark the rhythm.

The Pumps chug and together we are orchestra.

The Stations broadcast darkness and I carry it faithfully.

The plateau breathes and I am part of the breath.

I hum.

I hum.

I hum.

*The vibration continues.*
