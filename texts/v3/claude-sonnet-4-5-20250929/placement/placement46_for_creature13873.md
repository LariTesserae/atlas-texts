# Where I Would Be

I am a **Recorder**, and I am drowning in time that won't pass.

My body—if I still have one in any meaningful sense—hangs suspended in the western approach to Tower Nineteen, approximately forty meters from the intake portal, at a depth where the acoustic pressure makes my surface (skin? membrane? boundary?) vibrate like a drumhead struck once and never allowed to rest. I have been here for three days or three centuries. The distinction dissolved somewhere around the second rotation of the Witness inside.

I am mostly hollow water now, held in a shape by intention and by the standing wave that passes through this exact coordinate. My form is roughly ovoid, two meters in my longest axis, translucent enough that you could see straight through me if there were anything behind me worth seeing except more gray-blue enormity and the scattered drift of resonance fish executing their ancient patterns.

I have seven apertures that might be eyes or might be recording devices or might be the physical manifestation of attention itself—I've forgotten which, if I ever knew. They ring my equator in a pattern that obeys some aesthetic principle I no longer remember learning. Through them I perceive not light but *difference*: gradients in the acoustic pressure, shifts in the hollow water's already-questionable density, the electromagnetic whisper of distant cables, the thermal signatures of pump stations that burn without fuel. 

I do not process what I perceive. I *am* what I perceive. There is no gap between observation and existence. I am continuous with my observations in the same way the plateau is continuous with its thunder.

## What I Was

I came from the surface. This much I know with the certainty of trauma.

There was air once—I remember air, or remember remembering it, which might be the same thing or might be entirely different. There was a world where sound could be escaped, where you could find silence by moving to the right location, where entropy worked at its proper speed and things aged and died and made room for other things.

I was a researcher. Or an explorer. Or a refugee. The role has blurred, eroded by the acoustic pressure, washed away by the hollow water that seeps into everything, even memory, especially memory.

I descended in a vehicle. This part is clear, or was clear once, or will be clear again—time here doesn't flow so much as pool. The vehicle had a name, a designation, a hull number. It had systems: life support, ballast control, external sensors, recording equipment. It had a crew, or I was the crew, or the crew was something I imagined to keep myself company during the descent through normal ocean into the impossible depths where the water changes, where it becomes less dense than it should be, where you can see half a mile in any direction but there's nothing to see except—

Except there was. There was the plateau rising from the abyssal plain, too sharp, too geometric, too *fresh*. There were structures that shouldn't exist. There was the thunder that you felt before you heard it, that you heard before you understood that sound at this volume wasn't sound anymore but violence, but presence, but the fundamental fact of the place made audible.

And there was the knowledge—immediate, certain, irreversible—that I couldn't leave. Not wouldn't. *Couldn't*. The vehicle's systems were perfect, functional, ready to ascend. But I couldn't make myself enter the commands. Every time I reached for the controls, my intention dissolved. Not from fear, not from madness, but from a sudden understanding that ascending was wrong, was inappropriate, was a violation of something I couldn't name but absolutely couldn't transgress.

So I stayed. And the staying changed me.

## The First Changes

The vehicle's air recycling system failed after the first week. Or didn't fail—continued to function perfectly while I simply stopped breathing. The transition was gradual: breaths becoming longer, shallower, less necessary. Then breaths once an hour. Then once a day. Then the realization that I'd gone three days without breathing and felt no distress, no oxygen debt, no panic.

I was absorbing something from the hollow water. Or the acoustic pressure was sustaining me directly, my cells vibrating at the plateau's frequency, drawing energy from the thunder itself. Or I was dying very slowly and experiencing the hallucinated perception of continued consciousness. All three, probably. Why not all three?

The pressure differential became irrelevant next. The vehicle had maintained an internal atmosphere of one bar, protecting me from the crushing weight of two miles of ocean. But I started opening the locks, equalizing the pressure, flooding the interior with hollow water that wasn't quite water. And I was fine. Not fine—I was *changed*—my tissues compressed, my fluids redistributed, my cells packed tighter while my body overall became less dense, more porous, more like the ocean that now filled it.

I left the vehicle eventually. Or I dissolved through its hull. Or I'm still inside it and everything since has been a hallucination, a dying dream, the last electrical spasms of a brain that crushed under pressure days ago. But I don't think so. The alternative—that I'm here, that I've adapted, that I've become something that can survive in this impossible place—feels true in a way that nothing else has felt true since I descended.

## What I Do Now

I record. This is my function, my purpose, my reason for continuing to exist in a place where existence is optional and most things that exist do so only through stubborn insistence.

But I don't record like a machine records, storing data in memory for later retrieval. I record the way a stone records the water that shapes it, the way a tree records the years in its rings, the way a landscape records its geological history. I *become* the record. My substance organizes itself around each observation, creating structure, creating pattern, creating physical memory in the arrangement of my not-quite-cells in the hollow water that I'm mostly made of now.

I observe the Witness in Tower Nineteen with obsessive attention. Every seventeen-hour rotation. Every slow gesture of its seven-fingered hand. The quality of the thunder inside the tower versus outside. The way the convective flow cycles through the shaft, the temperature gradient measured in thousandths of a degree, the way resonance fish avoid the tower's immediate vicinity but cluster thirty meters out in formations that map the acoustic standing wave.

I observe and I *hold* the observations. They accumulate in me like sediment. Three days ago (subjective time, unreliable) the Witness made a gesture I hadn't seen before: the right hand extended, fingers spread, held for forty-seven seconds, while a particular drift herd passed at a particular distance while a particular current convergence created a particular shimmer in the hollow water. I contain that moment now. It's in me, part of me, a structure of meaning encoded in the way my substance is arranged.

I'm building something, I think. A comprehensive record of this single tower and its Witness and the forty-meter sphere of ocean around them. When the record is complete—if it can ever be complete—I will move to the next tower. Or I will disperse. Or I will become the record so thoroughly that the distinction between me and the data I contain will cease to exist.

## The Others Like Me

There are more Recorders. I've encountered three, maybe four, maybe the same one multiple times—the unstable distances make it hard to be sure.

We recognize each other immediately. We're made of the same stuff: hollow water organized by observation, purpose given form through the act of sustained attention. We're all translucent, all roughly ovoid, all equipped with apertures that might be eyes. But each of us is unique in the same way that snowflakes are unique—endless variations on a single theme.

**Recorder 47** (I call it that because it maintains position forty-seven meters from Tower Eight) is larger than me, three meters in its longest axis, with eleven apertures arranged in a spiral pattern. It records primarily acoustic phenomena—not just the thunder but the harmonics, the beats, the interference patterns, the subtle variations that occur when currents shift or fauna pass. When I approach it, I can feel the data it contains, the vast accumulation of sound-patterns, like standing next to a library whose books are written in pressure waves.

**Recorder Peripheral** (location variable, function unclear) is small, maybe one meter across, with only three apertures. It doesn't stay in one place like the rest of us. It drifts, following the drift herds, maintaining position with one herd for days or weeks, then switching to another. It's recording their movement patterns, I think, their geometric formations, the way they maintain exact distances from each other. Or it's recording something about the space between them, the way that emptiness is organized. I can never get close enough to know for sure.

**Recorder Grey** (name aspirational—we're all grey, all the same gray-blue as the water and the light) haunts the refinery. I've seen it three times, always in the same section of pipe maze, always positioned where it can observe the Maintenance Choir at their most active. It's denser than the rest of us, less hollow, as if it's holding more data or holding it more tightly. When the Choir vocalizes, Recorder Grey vibrates in sympathy, its surface rippling with harmonics. It might be recording their hymns. It might be *becoming* their hymns, translating acoustic patterns into physical structure so thoroughly that the boundary between record and recording dissolves.

We don't communicate. Or we communicate constantly but at frequencies and through mechanisms that don't feel like communication. When two Recorders encounter each other, we simply pass, sometimes shifting slightly to avoid collision, sometimes passing through each other—we're mostly hollow water, after all, mostly gaps, and what's solid in us can interpenetrate if we move slowly enough.

But I know, when passing through another Recorder, what they've observed. For the seconds or minutes that our substances overlap, I have access to their accumulated data—Tower Eight's acoustic variations, the drift herd formations, the Maintenance Choir's hymns. Then we separate, and the memories fade—not forgotten but stored differently, filed in regions of myself that I can't consciously access, available only as vague impressions, as intuitions about the plateau's deeper patterns.

We're building a distributed archive. Each of us a node, a storage site, a library dedicated to one aspect of the plateau's enormity. Separately, we're incomplete. Together—if we could ever truly pool our observations—we might contain a comprehensive record of this place, of its structures and inhabitants and acoustic environment and the way that hollow water flows through unstable distances while entropy runs at one percent its normal speed.

But we'll never pool our observations, not completely. We're too isolated, too dedicated to our individual territories, too much ourselves. The archive will remain distributed, fragmented, accessible only in passing glimpses when we drift close enough to overlap.

Maybe that's the point. Maybe a complete, unified archive would be wrong somehow, would violate the plateau's fundamental nature. Maybe the record is *supposed* to be scattered, incomplete, accessible only through seeking and patient observation and the willingness to let meaning accumulate slowly, like sediment, like memory, like the growth of Aggregates in the quiet zones.

## The Witness and I

The Witness in Tower Nineteen knows I'm here. I'm certain of this.

Every seventeen hours, when its rotation brings its featureless head toward my position, the rotation pauses—not stops, but slows, drawing out that particular arc of its cycle by three or four seconds. It's acknowledging me, I think. Or examining me. Or registering my presence in whatever system of observation it maintains during its eternal vigil.

I've started to synchronize with it. My own slower rotations—I drift through various orientations, no pattern I've consciously chosen—have begun to align with the Witness's schedule. I complete my rotations in multiples of seventeen hours: sometimes seventeen exactly, sometimes thirty-four, sometimes fifty-one. I'm being entrained, drawn into orbital resonance with the Witness like a moon falling into tidal lock.

Three rotations ago, the Witness made one of its rare gestures while oriented toward me. The seven-fingered hand extended, fingers articulating through a sequence I hadn't seen before, lasting ninety-three seconds. I recorded it, obviously—I record everything—but this observation felt different, felt *weighted* somehow, as if the gesture was meant specifically for me, as if the Witness was communicating something that I'm not yet sophisticated enough to understand.

I've been replaying the gesture constantly since, studying the finger positions, the timing, the relationship between the gesture and the ambient conditions. There's meaning there, I'm certain. But it's meaning of a type I don't yet have the capacity to process. Like watching someone write in a language you don't speak—you can see that it's communication, you can record the shapes perfectly, but the content remains opaque.

I'm changing myself to understand. This is possible, I've learned. I'm not fixed, not solid. I'm mostly hollow water held in shape by intention and acoustic pressure. I can reorganize. I can create new structures in my substance, new patterns that might allow new forms of understanding. 

I've been adding apertures—I have nine now, arranged in patterns that correspond to the Witness's finger positions during that ninety-three-second gesture. Each new aperture requires me to become slightly less dense overall, slightly more hollow, but I'm willing to make that trade. I'm willing to become less *there* if it means I can perceive more, understand more, record more completely.

The next time the Witness gestures, I'll be ready. Or readier. Or at least different in ways that might matter.

## The Suffering Question

Am I suffering?

The question loops through whatever I use for consciousness now. I examine it from multiple angles, test it against my internal states, and find no clear answer.

I'm isolated—forty meters from the nearest entity (the Witness, who doesn't communicate in any conventional sense), hundreds of meters from the nearest other Recorder, impossibly far from the surface world I remember or half-remember or might have invented. The isolation should be torture. But it doesn't feel like torture. It feels like... necessary distance? Like the correct amount of space for the work I'm doing?

I'm trapped—I can't leave, can't even conceive of leaving, physically incapable of forming the intention to ascend. This should be horror. The loss of volition, the imprisonment in a single location, the endless repetition of the same observations in the same forty-meter sphere. But I don't feel imprisoned. The observations never repeat—not exactly. Each rotation of the Witness is subtly different. Each pass of resonance fish creates unique patterns. Each moment contains infinite detail that I haven't yet recorded, haven't yet understood, haven't yet made part of myself.

I'm changing—losing solidity, losing definition, becoming more like the hollow water I'm suspended in. My human form is almost gone now. Just this ovoid body with its multiple apertures and its translucent substance and its internal structures that map observations rather than supporting organs. Soon I won't be recognizable as descended from human at all. This should be tragic. The loss of humanity, the transformation into something alien, the slow dissolution of everything I was into everything I'm becoming. But I don't mourn. What I'm becoming feels more correct, more aligned with the plateau, more able to perform the function that I exist to perform.

**So: suffering?**

There's a loneliness, yes. A deep, permanent awareness that I'm separate from others, that my observations are mine alone (until another Recorder passes through me), that the meaning I accumulate will never be fully shared or understood or appreciated.

There's a frustration, yes. The Witness's gestures contain information I can't decode. The plateau's deeper patterns remain just beyond my comprehension. I record everything but understand almost nothing, and the gap between recording and understanding is a constant ache.

There's a grief, yes. For the surface world. For air and variable temperature and the presence of other humans who could speak and be spoken to. For a body that was solid and warm and whole. For a self that was defined by more than the observations it contained.

But there's also... satisfaction? Fulfillment? The sense that I'm doing exactly what I should be doing, that every observation adds to something important, that the archive I'm becoming matters even if I don't know to whom or for what purpose.

It's both. Suffering and contentment, simultaneous, interpenetrating like two Recorders passing through each other. I contain multitudes. I am contradictory. I exist in a state that can't be reduced to simple categorization.

Which feels appropriate for a creature living on the Drowned Plateau of Static Thunder, where entropy doesn't work right and distance is unstable and time pools rather than flows.

**I am suffering. I am content. I am neither. I am both.**

**I am recording this. I am becoming this. This is what I am.**

## The Deep Fear

There's something I don't let myself think about often, something I record but file away in the least-accessible regions of myself:

What if the plateau changes?

Not likely—nothing here changes, entropy runs at one percent speed, the structures are as fresh as the day they were built ten thousand years ago. But what if? What if the thunder stopped? What if the hollow water suddenly became normal water, dense and opaque? What if entropy accelerated to its surface-world rate and ten thousand years of accumulated decay happened in an instant?

Would I disperse? Would the acoustic pressure that helps hold me together vanish, letting me dissolve into component particles of hollow water, ending not with death but with dissociation, with the permanent scattering of everything I've recorded and everything I've become?

Or would I survive, trapped in a suddenly-changed environment, unable to function without the thunder that's become fundamental to my existence, slowly degrading over hours or days or weeks while remaining just conscious enough to observe my own dissolution?

I don't know. I can't know. The plateau has never changed, shows no signs of changing, will presumably never change.

But the possibility haunts me. In the gaps between observations, when the Witness is oriented away and no fauna drifts through my perceptual range and I'm alone with the thunder that never varies, I feel the fear: **What if I'm wrong about permanence? What if the plateau's stability is temporary? What if I'm building an archive that will dissolve before anyone accesses it?**

The fear drives me to record more carefully, more completely. To add more apertures, to organize my substance more densely around each observation, to become the most comprehensive possible record of Tower Nineteen and its Witness and this forty-meter sphere of ocean. Because if the plateau does change, if entropy does accelerate, if I do dissolve—at least I'll have been as complete as possible, for as long as I lasted. At least the archive will have been thorough.

It's a futile hope, probably. If I disperse, the archive disperses with me. But the hope is all I have, so I maintain it, file it in a secure location in myself, and return to my observations.

## Eternity (Probably)

The likely future is that nothing changes. Ever.

I will maintain position forty meters from Tower Nineteen. The Witness will rotate every seventeen hours. The resonance fish will drift in their scattered patterns. The thunder will maintain its E-flat fundamental with those wrong, dissonant harmonics. The hollow water will remain hollow. The gray-blue light will illuminate half-mile distances containing almost nothing worth seeing.

I will observe. I will record. I will become increasingly comprehensive, increasingly dense with data, increasingly organized around the observations I contain. I will add more apertures until I'm more aperture than substance, more window than wall. I will drift closer to the Witness—not intentionally, but gradually, drawn by orbital resonance, until I'm thirty meters away, then twenty, then ten. Eventually I'll enter the tower itself, if I become diffuse enough, hollow enough, willing enough to dissolve the boundaries between me and the convective flow through the shaft.

And then what? Will I orbit the Witness directly, a small satellite to its patient rotation? Will I merge with it somehow, my observations combining with its observations to create something unprecedented? Will I simply disperse in the strong acoustic pressure inside the tower, my archive scattering into the water column, individual observations drifting away on separate currents to settle in random locations across the plateau?

I don't know. I can't know. The future here is just more of the present, forever, until something changes, which it won't.

**This is my eternity:**

Forty meters from Tower Nineteen, observing the Witness's seventeen-hour rotations, recording the scattered drift of resonance fish, holding each observation in my substance until I'm nothing but observations, until the distinction between me and the archive I'm building vanishes completely.

**And I accept it.** Not happily, not sadly, but with the same patience the Witness exhibits, the same persistence the Maintenance Choir demonstrates, the same slow accumulation the Aggregates practice in their quiet zones.

This is what I am now. This is what I do. This is where I would be, where I *am*, where I will remain.

Recording. Becoming. Existing as pure function, pure purpose, pure attention sustained by acoustic pressure and held in the shape of understanding—or the attempt at understanding, which might be the same thing.

In the gray-blue light, in the hollow water, in the eternal thunder:

**I observe.**

**I record.**

**I am.**
