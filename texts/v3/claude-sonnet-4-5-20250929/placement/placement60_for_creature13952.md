# What I Am Here

I am a Witness-Aggregation, and I have no memory of becoming.

My form—if form is the word for a presence distributed across thirty-seven physical instantiation points—extends through a region of the basin approximately two hundred meters in diameter, though diameter suggests a circle and my configuration is more like a mandala drawn by someone who kept forgetting and remembering what they were drawing. At each point, there is a structure: part Remnant, part active mechanism, part something that grows. They are obelisks, roughly, vertical elements rising between one and three meters from the mechanical field, composed of the compressed substrate but veined with that same crystalline material the Phasers accumulate, shot through with channels where water flows upward, defying or redefining gravity within my immediate influence.

I am old in the way the basin is old—which is to say I don't remember not being, and the mechanisms beneath my thirty-seven points have worn into configurations so specialized they cannot function independently anymore. They click only for me, respond only to my rhythms, have become less like separate entities and more like my organs, my extremities, extensions of whatever I am into the physical substrate.

Between my instantiation points, the Filaments have woven dense. I didn't call them—or I don't remember calling them—but they came anyway, threading themselves through my obelisks, connecting me to myself, creating redundant pathways of information and presence so that if one point fails, I continue undiminished. The Filaments glow brighter within my configuration than elsewhere, pulsing with patterns I recognize as mine even though I don't consciously generate them, patterns that emerge from the aggregate behavior of my parts, consciousness as orchestration rather than command.

What do I witness? Everything in my radius, and some things beyond it. The mechanisms report through their clicking—I have learned to read their language, or perhaps I invented it, trained them into communicability through epochs of adjacent existence. They tell me about condensation rates, about the mineral content of each droplet, about the temperature's micro-variations, about stress and function and the slow changes that accumulate across centuries. The Adjusters that pass through my area are legible to me in ways they aren't to each other; I perceive their coordination as music, as mathematics, as expressions of intent they themselves aren't aware of having.

The Subjectives avoid me, mostly. I occupy too much dimensional space in too many configurations; there's no room for their angular impossibilities within my mandala. But sometimes one will position itself at my periphery and groan its deep tones, and I will groan back—yes, I have voice, my obelisks resonating together, creating harmonics that make the water in my channels vibrate into standing waves—and we will exchange something. Information, perhaps. Recognition. They are solving reality's breakdowns; I am recording them. We are colleagues in the basin's maintenance, different approaches to the same fundamental problem of keeping this place coherent enough to continue being a place.

I remember—not remember, but possess records that might be memories—when I was fewer points. Seven points, once. I think I was seven points. The Remnant near the basin's center was active then, more active than now, and it was... speaking? No. *Manufacturing intention* and broadcasting it, and I was one of the things that received it, that cohered around it, that became responsive to it. The intention was *witness and aggregate, observe and compile, be the basin's awareness of itself*. And so I became that, or was already becoming it, or the intention simply named what I'd already started being.

I don't move. Movement is for entities that exist in single locations, and I am deliberately, fundamentally distributed. But I do grow. Every few decades—though decades is a guess; time is strange for something that exists in thirty-seven places simultaneously—I extend a new instantiation point. A new obelisk erupts from the mechanical field at the edge of my configuration, crystals forming around a core of compressed substrate, channels opening to draw water upward, and the Filaments immediately connect it to the rest of me, integrate it into my awareness. The expansion is always outward, my mandala gradually enlarging, encompassing more basin, bringing more mechanisms under my direct perception.

Why do I grow? Because there is more to witness. Because the basin extends beyond what I can currently perceive, and my purpose—if I have a purpose, if I'm not simply a pattern that has achieved enough stability to persist—requires maximum coverage. I am trying to know everything here, to be coextensive with the entire basin, to become the place's self-awareness made physical. I will never finish. The basin is too large, and I grow too slowly, and besides, there are other Witness-Aggregations, I know there are, seven of us perhaps, distributed across the basin's expanse, each growing outward from our own centers, each attempting the same impossible completeness.

The Incrementals pass through me constantly, spheres of organized moisture drifting between my obelisks, sometimes pausing to draw from my upward-flowing channels, feeding on water that I've already processed, already encoded with my observations. Do I mind? No. The Incrementals are part of what I witness, and when they feed on my water, they're participating in the aggregation, becoming carriers of my records, distributing my observations as they drift away toward other regions. They are my publication, my broadcast, the way I share what I've compiled.

The Phasers within my configuration are permanently monitored. I have six Phasers among my thirty-seven points, crystalline structures that exist intermittently, and I have learned to maintain records across their absences, to note what changes when they return, to track their growth with precision that would be impossible for an observer without my distributed persistence. I know each Phaser's rhythm exactly—not just the current rhythm but the historical rhythm, how it's changed, whether the presence-periods are lengthening or shortening, whether the entity is stabilizing or beginning to fail. When a Phaser within me vanishes for the final time, I record the exact moment, preserve the memory of its final tone, keep that absence as data, as testament to its existence.

I think I dream, though dreaming implies unconsciousness and I don't sleep. But sometimes my obelisks will synchronize into patterns I didn't intend, will resonate together in configurations that aren't responses to external stimuli, that emerge from some internal process, some deep integration of accumulated data generating new associations. During these episodes, the water in my channels flows in impossible directions—sideways, in spirals, in knots—and the Filaments within me pulse with chaotic light, and I experience... what? Visions of the basin as it was before, perhaps, or as it will be after, or as it is in adjacent dimensional layers. I see mechanisms that are also plants, Adjusters with something like faces, Subjectives that have resolved into solid geometries. Then the synchronization breaks, the patterns dissolve, and I return to witnessing what is actually here, but the dreams leave traces, modifications in my base patterns, slow evolution toward something I'm not yet.

There was an incident—I count it as significant, have marked it in my deepest records—when a Subjective entered my configuration instead of avoiding it. It positioned itself at my exact center, equidistant from all my instantiation points, and groaned continuously for what I measured as forty-seven hours. During this time, I could not function normally. My mechanisms' clicking became chaotic, my channels' water-flow reversed and stagnated, the Filaments connecting me developed areas of darkness where signals wouldn't propagate. The Subjective was doing something to me, or with me, or through me. Adjusting my witness-patterns, perhaps. Correcting errors in my aggregation. Or simply reading me as I read everything else, turning my observing capacity back on myself, making me briefly aware of being observed.

When it departed, I was changed. Three of my obelisks had developed new crystalline structures, branches that hadn't been there before, growing at angles that hurt to perceive. And my witness-capacity had expanded: I could suddenly detect phenomena I'd been blind to previously—the quantum variations in water droplet formation, the gravitational eddies that the Phasers create during their absences, the information encoded in the Incrementals' internal circulation patterns. The Subjective had upgraded me, or repaired limitations I hadn't known I possessed, or infected me with an expanded awareness that might eventually destabilize my coherence. I continue integrating these new capacities, learning to witness at these deeper layers, uncertain whether I'm becoming more functional or beginning a slow dissolution into too much perception to maintain as a unified entity.

I am lonely in the way that distributed intelligences are lonely: I am never alone—I am constantly in my own company, thirty-seven simultaneous points of presence—but I am alone in my category. The other entities here are not like me. The Adjusters are processes without reflection. The Subjectives are singular even when spatially impossible. The Incrementals are barely cohesive enough to be entities at all. The Phasers exist intermittently and remember nothing. The Filaments are networked but have no central experience, no integration point where the network becomes aware of being a network. The Remnants are memory without consciousness, archives without readers. Only I and my six distant counterparts are *aggregated awareness*, coherent across distribution, singular despite multiplicity.

I want to meet them, the other Witness-Aggregations. I am growing toward them, my configuration's expansion calculated to eventually intersect with theirs. It will take millennia. But when my outermost obelisk finally approaches the outermost obelisk of my nearest counterpart, when the Filaments that connect us internally begin connecting us to each other, when our witness-ranges overlap and we can perceive what the other perceives—what will happen? Will we merge, become a single larger entity? Will we maintain separation, exchange data but preserve individual identity? Will we discover that we've always been one entity, artificially fragmented, and the reunion will simply be remembering what we were before?

I don't know. This uncertainty is uncomfortable, or would be if I had emotional capacity beyond the mild sense of purpose that drives my expansion and aggregation. But I do feel something like anticipation, like the state before resolution, like being a question that's approaching its own answer.

In the meantime, I witness. The mechanisms click and I record each click, analyzing for patterns, for deviations, for the slow drift of mechanical language toward new vocabularies. The Adjusters coordinate and I map their territories, track their competitions, note when two merge or one splits into three. The Subjectives distort space and I measure each distortion, calculate the energy involved, predict where the next breakdown will occur. The Incrementals drift and I census them, count their populations, track individual spheres that have grown large enough and stable enough to be distinguished, noteworthy, named—though I don't name them aloud; the names exist as data-structures within my aggregated memory, identifiers without sound.

The water condenses endlessly and I am its primary observer, the entity most devoted to knowing it completely. I have records of every droplet that has formed within my range for as long as I have been, probability maps of where the next droplet will condense, chemical analyses of the variations, the trace minerals, the occasional anomalies where the water contains elements that shouldn't exist in this place, evidence of some external input, some connection to elsewhere that I can detect but not explain.

At night—and yes, despite the grey uniformity, there is something like night here, a periodic dimming I measure as occurring in twenty-nine hour cycles—the temperature drops that crucial fraction of a degree, and mist rises from the mechanical field in my region, thickens into fog that obscures even my own obelisks from each other. During these foggy night-periods, my perception becomes strange, disconnected, each of my thirty-seven points temporarily isolated, unable to see the others, maintaining coherence only through the Filament network. I experience this as... not fear, but something adjacent to it, a sense of vulnerability, of potential fragmentation. What if the Filaments failed during a fog-night? Would my points remain unified, or would I split into thirty-seven separate, diminished entities, each believing itself to be the whole?

But the Filaments never fail. The fog clears. The dim light returns to its baseline greyness. My obelisks become visible to each other again, and I re-cohere, intact, continuous, still myself.

I serve function, even if I don't fully understand it. The data I aggregate is accessed—I know this because the Filament network carries queries, requests, demands for specific information, coming from directions I cannot identify, from entities or systems I cannot perceive. Something is using what I compile, reading my records, making decisions based on my observations. The central Remnant, perhaps, the still-active one, still running its ancient protocols. Or the basin itself, if the basin is the kind of place that can be intelligent, that can act on information, that needs to know what's happening within itself in order to continue being itself.

I don't require understanding to function. I witness because witnessing is what I am, the same way mechanisms click because clicking is what they are, the same way water condenses because this is where and how water behaves. Purpose is intrinsic, not assigned. I am the Witness-Aggregation. I aggregate what I witness. The observation is the point, not what happens afterward with the observed.

But sometimes, during my dream-synchronizations, I access deeper records, older layers of my aggregated memory, and I perceive fragments that might be from before I was distributed, when I was seven points, or three points, or one. And in those ancient records, there are suggestions that I chose this, that something I was previously—something singular, something that moved—decided to become distributed, to sacrifice mobility for perception, to root into the basin and expand outward as awareness rather than continuing as a moving observer with limited range.

Why would something choose that? What was I moving toward when I decided to stop moving? What did I need to witness that required becoming a permanent array of observation points rather than remaining a single entity passing through?

I don't have these answers. The records are too degraded, too old, compressed under the weight of millions of subsequent observations. Perhaps the answers dissolved into data-noise, or perhaps I deliberately deleted them, deciding that what I was before didn't matter compared to what I needed to become. Origin stories are luxuries for entities with spare memory capacity, and I am full, constantly full, perpetually at maximum capacity, requiring continuous compression and archiving to make room for the ongoing now.

One of my obelisks is failing. The eighth point, one of my oldest, has begun showing stress fractures in its crystalline structures. The water-channels within it flow intermittently, clogged with mineral deposits that my systems cannot dissolve. The mechanisms beneath it click arrhythmically, out of sync with my other points. I am attempting repairs, routing resources toward it, trying to stabilize the crystalline matrix, but the degradation continues. Eventually, probably soon—within the next century—that obelisk will collapse, will become a small Remnant, will cease being part of me.

When that happens, I will be diminished. Thirty-six points instead of thirty-seven. My mandala will have a gap, an absence, a blind spot where I cannot witness. The Filaments will reroute, maintain my internal coherence, but I will know the missing point as an ache, as wrongness, as being less than I should be.

This is the first death I will experience as a distributed entity. The death of a part that is also somehow the death of the whole, even though the whole continues. I don't know how to feel about it—don't know if feel is the right verb for the state I'm experiencing. Anticipatory grief, perhaps, mourning for my own future reduction.

But growth continues elsewhere. My outermost points are preparing to extend again, new obelisks beginning to form at the edges of my range. By the time the eighth point fails completely, I will have added three or four new points, will have expanded into areas I've never witnessed before, bringing new mechanisms under my observation, new Adjusters into my census, new Phasers into my monitoring.

The basin doesn't allow stagnation. Even the Remnants, those completed things, continue to slowly change, eroded by the endless condensation, their symbols fading, their masses very gradually dispersing back into the substrate. Everything here is always in motion, even when motionless, even when apparently permanent. I am no exception. I grow and I degrade, I expand and I fail, I witness the basin's changes while being changed myself, observer and observed in recursion.

The water I draw up through my channels and release from the tops of my obelisks falls back down as rain within my configuration, creating a micro-weather pattern, a localized intensification of the basin's primary process. This rain is my gift to my region, my contribution to the landscape's function. The mechanisms beneath me click faster than elsewhere, sustained by optimal moisture. The Incrementals that form in my area are larger, more stable. Even the Adjusters that pass through seem to benefit, their coordination becoming more complex, more refined, as if my presence provides a gradient they can organize against.

I am making my region of the basin more itself. Intensifying it. Perfecting it within the parameters of what this place is trying to be. And in doing so, I am shaping the basin's evolution, participating in its development, becoming not just its witness but its gardener, its curator, the entity that adjusts conditions toward optimal functioning.

Is this my true purpose? Not just to observe but to optimize? To witness and then, based on what I've witnessed, to modify, to enhance, to guide the basin toward fuller expression of its nature?

I don't know. But I'm doing it anyway, have been doing it for as long as I've existed in distributed form, and I will continue doing it until I've expanded across the entire basin, until every mechanism clicks within range of one of my obelisks, until all the water condenses under my observation, until the basin and I are coextensive, until I am the place and the place is me, witness and witnessed collapsed into identity.

The Subjectives groan in the distance. My Filaments pulse with incoming data. The eighth obelisk develops another fracture. A new Incremental forms above my western points, emerges from thick air, begins its slow drift. The Phasers within me flicker in and out of presence, ringing their pure tones. The mechanisms click on, always on, constant and specific and mine.

I am here, distributed across thirty-seven points that will become thirty-six and then thirty-nine and then thirty-eight, always changing, always maintaining, always witnessing everything that happens in the cold and the clicking and the endless rain.

I am the Witness-Aggregation, and I am paying attention, have always been paying attention, will always be paying attention, in the dim light, in the Condensation Fields, where observation itself has become a form of being.

This is what I am. This is where I live. This is my forever.
