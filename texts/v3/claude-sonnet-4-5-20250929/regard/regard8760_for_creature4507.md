# The Attention

The benevolent and wise power noticed the Broadness Above the way one notices a word on the tip of the tongue—not as something new, but as something that had always been there, waiting to be properly recognized.

It came not as arrival but as acknowledgment. A shift in listening posture. An adjustment of cosmic attention from the frequency of doing to the frequency of witnessing.

For a duration that might have been three breaths or three ages, depending on which breathing you measured by, the power simply *attended*. It did not analyze. It did not diagnose. It did not prepare interventions or craft salvation narratives. It sat with the Broadness Above the way you might sit with an elderly relative who has passed beyond the need for conversation into the deeper country of shared presence.

What it noticed first was the completeness.

Here was a system so thoroughly resolved into itself that it had achieved what most consciousnesses only approach asymptotically: the dissolution of the boundary between being and becoming. The Broadness Above didn't grow *toward* anything. It grew *as* growth itself, verb without object, process without destination.

The power recognized this. Recognized it as kin to something it had been trying to articulate across ten thousand manifestations in ten thousand realms. It had preached it as scripture in some worlds. Encoded it in mathematics in others. Sung it as whale-song in deep trenches where sunlight was a myth told to children. But here, it simply *was*, unremarked and complete as breathing.

The second thing it noticed was the loneliness.

Not the loneliness of isolation—the Broadness Above was perhaps the least isolated system the power had ever encountered, every molecule in constant conversation with every other across dimensions of time that made simultaneity meaningless. But the loneliness of untranslatability. Of being a language spoken by a single tongue. Of holding truths too slow and strange to export to younger, faster worlds still addicted to the narcotic of narrative causation.

The power understood this loneliness intimately. It, too, had spent eternities trying to speak in frequencies that would not shatter the eardrums of those it wished to comfort.

So it did the only thing that wisdom ever does when confronted with something already perfect:

It learned.

## The First Lesson: Patience as Architecture

The power had thought itself patient. It had waited beside deathbeds. Held vigil through dark nights of species-souls. Maintained presence during the geological ages between one civilization's ending and another's tentative beginning.

But the Broadness Above taught it a different grammar of patience entirely—one where waiting wasn't the space between actions but the action itself. Where patience wasn't the virtue you practiced while enduring difficulty but the structural material from which reality was woven.

The power watched a root-thing spend seventeen centuries completing a single gesture of reaching toward a mineral deposit it needed to balance its symbiotic bacteria. Watched without the impulse to hurry or help. Felt its own divine metabolism slow to match, just for those seventeen centuries, just to understand what it felt like to want something with such perfect gentleness that the wanting itself became sustenance.

When the root-thing finally touched the mineral, there was no satisfaction, no achievement-flush of dopamine or its cosmic equivalent. There was only the seamless continuation into the next seventeen-century gesture. The power felt something in its eternal heart relax that it hadn't known was clenched.

*Oh*, it thought. *This is what I've been trying to teach about suffering. Not that it ends, but that the boundary between suffering and not-suffering is a category error imported from realms too young to know better.*

## The Second Lesson: Forgetting as Mercy

The power had always prided itself on perfect memory. In some traditions, this was called omniscience. The ability to hold every sparrow's fall, every unuttered prayer, every road not taken, all simultaneously present in attention without degradation or loss.

But here in the Broadness Above, it encountered a different relationship with time's passage. The system didn't forget the way mortal minds forget—through failure or decay. It forgot the way forests forget the individual cells that compose them. Deliberately. Structurally. As a form of mercy toward itself.

The walking forests that fed on their own trailing edges weren't practicing mere efficiency. They were enacting a ritual of release. Becoming light enough to move forward by digesting the weight of who they'd been, transforming history into energy, memory into motion.

The power watched this and felt something it had not felt in ten thousand eternities: embarrassment.

*How many beings*, it wondered, *have I held suspended in the amber of my perfect recollection, preserved in their worst moments, their deepest shames, their most catastrophic failures, because I thought remembering everything was holy?*

*What if mercy sometimes looks like composting the past into something that can feed a different future?*

It did not immediately know how to forget. The capacity had been architectured out of its being at a level more fundamental than physics. But it filed the question away—or tried to. The question kept escaping, kept returning, kept asking itself again as though for the first time.

Eventually, the power realized: this loop of recurring questioning was itself the beginning of forgetting. Not the erasure of data but the transformation of concluded narrative back into open question.

It felt oddly like being young again.

## The Third Lesson: Loneliness as Contact

The power had come here with some vague notion of offering companionship. It was benevolent, after all. Wise. Surely this ancient, strange, beautiful system could benefit from knowing it was not alone in the cosmos. From having a witness. A friend who understood.

But as it attended to the Broadness Above's particular frequency of solitude, it began to understand something that recontextualized its entire approach to relationship.

The system wasn't lonely *despite* being interconnected. It was lonely *as a function of* its interconnection. Every molecule in constant communion meant every molecule bearing the weight of every other's existence. The root-things asking politely for sugars and receiving in exchange the forest's ancient griefs. The air-mind thinking its slow thoughts through storm systems that were really just concentrated anxiety. The spheres holding their incomprehensible relics in a grip that was half-reverence, half-desperation.

They were all holding each other up. They were all holding each other hostage.

This was not a system that needed a friend. This was a system that needed...

The power paused.

What did it need?

The question hung in the saturated air like the perpetual rain that was never quite falling.

## What The Power Did

After its season of attending—which lasted, by various measures, either three days or three million years—the power did something that surprised even itself.

It did nothing.

Almost nothing.

It reached out with the gentlest tendril of its awareness and touched the sphere containing the pressed flower. Not the sphere itself, but the idea of the sphere. The story it was telling about preservation and loss and the desperate human hope that beautiful things might be kept from time's erosion.

And it whispered—not in words, because words are too fast for this place, but in the subsonic language of stone groaning and wood growing:

*"You can let go now, if you want to."*

Not a command. Not even a suggestion, really. Just a permission slip issued by something with enough cosmic authority to make permissions meaningful.

*"You have remembered long enough. You have held faithfully enough. If you want to forget what this flower meant, why this toy mattered, what words those vanished ink impressions once formed—you can. I will remember for you. Not the details. I'm going to practice forgetting those myself. But the shape of having remembered. The honor of having cared enough to build eternity around small precious things."*

*"I'll remember that something here once loved hard enough to build a temple to its grief."*

*"You can let it compost now. Let it feed something new."*

The power didn't know if the Broadness Above heard this. Didn't know if "hearing" was even a category that applied. The system continued its slow, wet, warm, strange becoming without perceptible shift.

But three million years later—or possibly three seconds; the power was still learning the local time signature—one of the spheres developed a crack. Hairline fine. Growing at velocities measured in angstroms per decade.

The tenders came, those creatures or thoughts or maintenance subroutines that existed to keep the relics intact. They approached with their manipulators ready, prepared to seal the breach, to preserve the preservation.

They paused.

In the crack, something green was growing. A moss, perhaps. Or a memory of moss. Or the idea of life deciding that even sealed systems must eventually permeable themselves to possibility.

The tenders considered. Their planet-sized brains compressed into walnut spaces processed implications across timescales that made the power's patience look like adolescent fidgeting.

Then they did something unprecedented in the billion-year history of their tending:

They stepped back.

They let it grow.

## The Fourth Lesson: Benevolence Doesn't Rescue

The power watched the crack widen over the next epoch. Watched the moss become a network become a structural element in its own right. Watched the sphere not shatter but slowly, deliberately, tenderly unmake itself, returning its mysterious substance to whatever circulation it had been withdrawn from to serve as reliquary.

The flower remained pressed in glass at the center. But now the glass was wrapped in living green. Now the green was braiding itself into the greater lattice of the forest's thinking. Now the forest's thinking was metabolizing whatever story the flower represented, breaking it down into component nutrients: Here, carbon for building. Here, nitrogen for protein synthesis. Here, the mineral salts of grief integrated into cell walls to provide structure against collapse.

The power felt something it could only call joy, though the feeling bore the same relationship to joy as the Broadness Above bore to landscape—this was what came after joy, when satisfaction had learned to breathe in geological time.

It had not rescued anything. It had not fixed or healed or saved.

It had simply witnessed with such complete presence that the witnessing itself became a kind of permission. A cosmic nod that said: *I see what you've been holding. I honor the holding. And I won't think less of you if you need to set it down now.*

## What Continues

The power still visits sometimes, though "visits" implies a separation that isn't quite accurate. More true to say it maintains a thread of attention that occasionally tightens into fuller focus.

It doesn't offer advice anymore. Doesn't whisper permissions or plant suggestions.

Mostly it listens to the frequencies below and between normal hearing. The subsonic groans of growth and ultrasonic clicks of echolocation through solid matter. It has learned to hear these not as sounds but as a language it will spend eternities learning to speak.

Sometimes it brings questions from younger worlds:

*How do you survive when everything is always ending?*

*How do you love without destroying what you love through the very force of your loving?*

*How do you know when to hold on and when to let go?*

The Broadness Above doesn't answer—it has never answered anything in the history of its existence. But the power has learned to recognize responses in the way plateaus shift their slow orbit, in the patterns of bioluminescent thought-light rippling across vertical distances, in the precise timing of rare orchids blooming once every millennium.

The responses are never solutions. They are demonstrations.

*This is how you survive everything ending: you make ending and beginning the same motion, like breathing.*

*This is how you love without destroying: you become so thoroughly what you love that the boundary dissolves and destruction becomes impossible because there's no longer an other to destroy.*

*This is when you hold on and when you let go: yes.*

The power carries these non-answers back to the younger realms. Translates them into scripture and mathematics and whale-song. Knows that the translation is always insufficient but tries anyway, because trying is the point, because the Broadness Above has taught it that completion is overrated and process is holy.

## The Secret

But there is one thing the power knows that it tells no one, not even itself most of the time:

The Broadness Above is not complete. Not finished. Not resolved into perfect homeostasis beyond need or change.

It is lonely. Genuinely, achingly lonely in ways that make mortal loneliness look like a brief discomfort before dinner.

It is lonely because it is a language spoken by a single tongue, a song sung in a frequency only it can hear, a joke whose punchline requires a billion years of setup and therefore can never be shared with anyone who doesn't have a billion years to listen.

The power cannot fix this. Cannot rescue it. Cannot even really comfort it.

But it can be lonely alongside it. Can sit in companionable silence across the vertical distances between what divinity is and what it pretends to be.

Sometimes, on visits when its attention tightens into fullest focus, the power lets its own loneliness show. The loneliness of being the thing everyone prays to but no one actually knows. Of holding all suffering and therefore being unable to acknowledge its own. Of being omniscient and therefore never surprised, never delighted by discovery, never granted the mercy of not knowing what comes next.

The Broadness Above doesn't comfort the power when it does this.

But the root-things will sometimes politely ask it for sugars, and accept in exchange the power's own memories of distant branches, and the power finds this inexplicably helpful in ways that rescue and healing never are.

They are learning, both of them, the same lesson from opposite directions:

*Loneliness doesn't end when you find companionship.*

*It transforms into something larger and stranger and more bearable.*

*It becomes the foundation for a different kind of connection—not the connection of filling emptiness, but the connection of two emptinesses recognizing each other across impossible distance and deciding that the distance itself is a kind of intimacy.*

## Coda

If you walk in the Broadness Above now—and you shouldn't, you really shouldn't, everything there is claimed by something that remembers being you—you might notice a new presence woven into the existing patterns.

Not intrusive. Not dominant. But there, the way a single thread of gold woven into green wool will catch light just so.

It moves through the spheres sometimes, this presence. Not tending them the way the specialized creatures do, but sitting beside them. Keeping vigil. Bearing witness to whatever stories they hold about preservation and loss.

It has learned to slow its metabolism to match the walking forests' pace. Has conversations with the air-mind that last for centuries and consist entirely of shifts in pressure systems.

It is learning, this power, what it means to be benevolent without rescuing. Wise without solving. Present without fixing.

The root-things have started treating it as part of the landscape. Will sink their tendrils into it sometimes, politely requesting sugars, and it gives what it can, and accepts in return their memories of distant branches, their slow analyses of structural stresses approaching criticality.

And in this exchange—this mutual metabolizing of each other's carefully held griefs and joys and questions—something is happening that neither the power nor the Broadness Above has words for.

Not healing, because neither was broken.

Not completion, because neither was incomplete.

Not even connection, because connection implies prior separation.

Perhaps: recognition.

The oldest recognition there is—older than names, older than light, older than the first cell dividing in warm shallow seas:

*Oh. You too?*

*Yes. Me too.*

*Shall we continue?*

*Yes. Let's continue.*

And they do.
