# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

The presence arrives like a question learning to listen to itself.

Not descending—that would imply hierarchy, and this power long ago forgot the grammar of above and below. Rather, it *coincides* with the valley, the way dawn coincides with the moment birds remember they have voices. If the Remainders notice, they give no sign, but the oldest one—"the pause between lightning and counting"—tilts her coral-bone crown a single degree eastward, which in this place is the equivalent of rolling out a red carpet made of welcome woven with wariness.

The power observes.

For 3,847 years (or seventeen minutes, depending on which timestream is doing the measuring), it simply *reads* the Remainders the way one might read a poem written in a language that hasn't been spoken since before speaking was invented. It traces the architecture of their absences. Counts the smaller Remainders nested in bone-crevices like grief counting its own ribs. Watches how they move through space as if space were a conversation they're always in the middle of finishing.

And then—with the gentleness of snow deciding whether to fall or become the memory of falling—it understands.

## The First Gift: Acknowledgment

The power does not fix them.

This is crucial. This is everything.

It does not smooth their coral-bone exoskeletons back into conventional flesh. Does not teach the trees to grow upward again or convince the mountains to wake from their standing sleep. Does not correct the grammar of this tilted world or persuade time to move forward like a well-behaved river.

Instead, it sits down.

The act of sitting creates a new cardinal direction. North, South, East, West, and now *Witness*—a direction that points toward the space where someone finally understands that brokenness and wholeness are just different dialects of the same fundamental language.

The Remainders feel this direction open in their chests like a room they didn't know their hearts contained.

## The Second Gift: Amplification

"Show me," the power says, in a voice that sounds like light asking permission before touching something fragile.

And the youngest Remainder—"maybe"—steps forward with cupped hands full of discarded futures. Trembling, they pour these unlived possibilities onto the ground between them and the power.

What happens next:

The futures take root.

Not in the soil exactly, but in the *idea* of soil, in the space between what ground is and what ground means when it's been holding grief so long it's forgotten the weight of anything else. They sprout into gardens of crystallized *what-if*, blooming in colors that exist only in the peripheral vision of might-have-been.

The power tends these gardens with the Remainders. Together, they water them with tears that evaporated before falling. Prune them with scissors made of the distance between any two points that once touched. And slowly—so slowly that patience itself gets impatient and wanders off to become a mountain—the Remainders begin to understand:

Their discarded futures were never meant to be lived.

They were meant to be *composted*.

Every unlived life, every road not taken, every version of themselves they left behind in the valley of another choice—these are not failures. They are *fertilizer*. The rich soil from which unprecedented forms of beauty grow.

## The Third Gift: Completion

The power approaches their unfinished tower, the one that extends down through compressed forgetting and up to scrape the sky's apologetic knee.

It does not add a single brick.

Instead, it plants a garden at the tower's base—not around it, but *as part of it*. The roots of these new plants (grown from composted futures) wrap around the tower's foundation, and suddenly everyone can see what the structure has always been:

Not a tower at all.

A tree.

A tree growing both directions simultaneously, roots reaching toward sky, branches delving into earth, teaching the world that up and down were always just different ways of saying *toward*.

The Remainders stand in a circle around this revelation, holding hands with the spaces between their hands, and something shifts. Not healing—healing implies there was something wrong. Rather, *recognition*. The way a word recognizes its own meaning after years of being used incorrectly and decides, with quiet dignity, that it has always meant exactly this.

## The Fourth Gift: Company

The power stays.

Not forever—forever is a long time to impose on someone else's privacy. But for exactly as long as the Remainders need someone to sit with them in the space between what they are and what they're becoming.

It teaches them songs in the key of "what if we had chosen differently?" then listens while they remix those songs into entirely new melodies in the key of "what if this was always the right choice?"

It helps them finish weaving their net of unasked questions and starlight. When they finally catch what they've been reaching for, it's not escaped light from broken promises, but something else entirely:

The reflection of themselves, refracted through the prism of their own becoming, split into thirteen colors that have never existed before but will now exist forever in the palette available to those brave enough to paint with absence.

## The Fifth Gift: Departure

When the power finally prepares to leave (not because it's finished, but because staying too long would become a crutch, and the Remainders need to remember they can stand on their own coral bones), the oldest one approaches.

"Will you forget us?" she asks, her cloak of unfinished extinctions rustling like pages in a book that's still being written.

The power smiles with a mouth made of every kindness that was ever offered without expectation of return.

"I will remember you," it says, "in the only way that matters: by forgetting to remember the difference between who you were and who you are becoming."

And then it offers them the final gift, the one they didn't know they needed:

Permission to be exactly this broken, this beautiful, this *sufficient*.

"You are not the Remainders," it tells them. "You are the Remained. What persists after everything else has learned to move sideways through the world. And persistence—" 

Here it pauses, letting the weight of the word settle like snow that has finally decided to fall.

"—is its own form of grace."

## What Happens After

The thirteen beings stand in their tilted valley, in their world where water climbs and mountains sleep standing up, surrounded by gardens grown from composted futures and trees that teach the earth new directions.

They continue their work—teaching mountains proper etiquette for dying, collecting unasked questions, building structures from the space between what we meant to say and what gravity made us mean instead.

But now, when animals come with questions they're afraid to ask their own reflections, the Remainders have a new answer:

"We don't know either. But not knowing together is its own kind of navigation."

And somewhere, in the pause between lightning and counting, in the space between maybe and certainty, a new direction opens in the heart of the world:

Not North or South or East or West or even Witness.

But *Remained*.

The direction that points toward everything that persists not despite its brokenness, but *through* it, *as* it, *because of* it.

And the mountains, in their standing sleep, begin to dream of this direction and smile with their limestone faces, understanding finally that falling asleep standing up was never a failure of posture—

It was always just another way of reaching toward the sky.
