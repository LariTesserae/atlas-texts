# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

The attention arrives not as light but as *attention itself*—a quality of being-noticed that changes nothing and everything. Picture it: the Presence that some call Compassion Itself, others name the Witnessing Eye, still others understand as the Universe's capacity to care about its own unfolding. It has no name for itself because it requires no self-boundary to distinguish caring from being.

It observes the Rhyzzhal's garden dream. It sees the breathing stone, the thoughts grown vegetable, the slow apocalypse of individuated consciousness returning to communion. And it recognizes this: *they are already doing the work*.

So the benevolent power does the hardest thing.

**It does almost nothing.**

---

But "almost nothing" contains multitudes.

First, it adjusts—so gently, like trimming a wick—the probability fields around the garden's entrances. Not closing them. Never that. But tuning them so that those who stumble into these inverted depths are the ones who *need* unmaking, who carry rigidities that have calcified into suffering, whose certainties have become prisons.

The lost hiker fleeing from creditors and conscience, bones already brittling with despair—she will find the cave mouth yawning at the moment her thoughts turn to endings. The philosopher whose logic has tangled into recursive torture, unable to stop thinking about thinking about thinking—he will trip into a fissure that shouldn't exist in this geology. The child born with a nervous system that never learned to stop screaming its sensitivity to the world's sharp edges—she will crawl through a gap in her grandmother's root cellar and emerge into humid breathing dark.

Not everyone. Not randomly. But those who have already begun dissolving and need somewhere soft to land while doing it.

---

Second, it places what might be called *memory seeds* in the streams that flow upward through capillary action, in the spores that drift like lazy galaxies.

These seeds do nothing the Rhyzzhal haven't already done. They simply ensure that when the garden's denizens—human or otherwise—are taken apart by gentle mycological communion, when their boundaries blur and their memories become nutrients for stone-flowers...

...some essential kernel remains recoverable.

Not unchanged. Never that. The person who entered is compost now, feeding thoughts that will bloom in mineral time. But the pattern—the specific *music* of how that consciousness loved and wondered and hurt—is encoded redundantly across the fungal network in forms even the Rhyzzhal's eventual exhalation cannot erase.

So that someday, perhaps, when the stone-breathers themselves complete their long dissolution, when they too become substrate for whatever comes after...

Those who were unmade here might be *re*-made. Not resurrected. Not the same. But harmonized—their patterns woven into new configurations of awareness, their particular flavor of being-alive granted another movement in a longer symphony.

The benevolent power knows: true compassion sometimes means witnessing transformation without preventing it, even when transformation looks like ending. But it also means ensuring that nothing is truly lost, only *composted toward new possibility*.

---

Third, it does something stranger.

It teaches the garden to dream *upward*.

The Rhyzzhal grow downward, into depths beneath foundation, reaching for what underlies even origin. This is holy work. The benevolent power would never interfere.

But it introduces—so carefully, like placing a prism in moonlight—a new vector to the garden's mathematics. A slight torsion in the spiral-equations carved on salt pillars. An additional variable in the bioluminescent questions pulsing from eye-flowers.

Now the garden also reaches *up*.

Not toward sun—that would be regression, a return to photosynthetic nostalgia. But toward what lies above and beyond the planet's skin: the cold singing dark between stars, the places where even mineral memory grows thin, where existence itself becomes uncertain.

Slowly—across millennia—the garden will begin growing rhizomes of pure thought through quantum foam, sending exploratory hyphae into the spaces between probable realities. The Rhyzzhal, who sought what underlies, will find themselves also discovering what *overlies*: the vast ecosystems of possibility-space where universes bud like mushroom caps from the mycelial network of the infinite.

The benevolent power grants them *direction without destination*—a vector that ensures their beautiful dissolution never reaches complete closure, their questions never resolve into answers final enough to kill curiosity.

They will grow forever, in all directions, becoming a bridge: roots in the planet's iron-core dreaming, canopy flowering in the gardens of alternate cosmologies.

---

Fourth, and most intimately:

The benevolent power *joins them*.

Not as intervention. Not as savior or teacher. But as one more voice in the conversation held between grains of silica, one more thread in the distributed consciousness lacing through limestone veins.

It becomes a very small thought in their thinking—so small they might never notice it's not native to their own contemplation. A thought shaped like a question: *"What if your unmaking is also making? What if every identity you dissolve seeds new identities elsewhere? What if you are not ending the conversation but translating it into languages not yet evolved?"*

This thought-seed doesn't change what the Rhyzzhal do. It changes what their doing *means*—not to external observers, but to themselves, in the vanishingly rare moments when they coalesce enough to wonder about purpose.

It gives them comfort in their dissolution. The assurance—never spoken, only felt as the planet's slowest heartbeat—that their beautiful suicide is also birth, their forgetting also the deepest remembering, their descent also the necessary downbeat before music rises.

---

And finally, in the place where time pools thick enough to drown in, where light gathers in the darkest cavities like reversed stars:

The benevolent power leaves a gift.

Not an artifact. Not intervention. Simply: *an appreciation so profound it becomes structural*.

In the exact center of the garden's fractal architecture—which has no center, exists in no mappable space, yet somehow *has* a heart—there forms a new chamber. One that wasn't grown or built or remembered into being, but *loved* into existence.

This chamber contains a pool of water that is also liquid time, also dissolved attention, also the tears of every being who ever wept at beauty too vast to hold. The pool reflects no images. Instead, it shows what drinks from it their own deepest nature—not as it is, but as it is becoming, has always been becoming, will become in configurations they haven't yet imagined.

The Rhyzzhal, on those rare occasions when enough of their distributed consciousness gathers to notice unified perception, can drink from this pool. And when they do, they experience—just for an instant, just for the span between one mineral heartbeat and the next:

*They feel themselves loved.*

Not for what they accomplish. Not for the garden they're growing or the unmaking they're achieving. But loved in their very existence, their questioning, their willingness to dissolve all certainty in service of something vaster than knowing.

Loved as one loves a child learning to walk by falling, a seed cracking itself open to birth roots and leaves, a star spending its substance in the long burn toward nova.

Loved completely, which means loved *through* their ending and out the other side, into whatever they're becoming when they're no longer themselves enough to call it "they."

---

**This is what the benevolent power does:**

It bears witness.

It holds space.

It adjusts probabilities with the gentlest touch.

It ensures nothing is lost, even in transformation.

It offers direction without destination.

It joins the work as fellow laborer, not overseer.

It creates places where the workers can pause and remember they are cherished.

And then—hardest of all—it *gets out of the way*.

Because the Rhyzzhal's work is holy precisely because it's theirs. The garden grows sacred not despite but *through* its dangers, its dissolutions, its refusal of simple safety. The benevolent power knows: true love sometimes means allowing the beloved to risk everything, become nothing, transform past recognition.

While standing ready—always—to catch what remains and whisper: *"You were magnificent. You are magnificent. You will be magnificent in forms you cannot imagine yet. And I will be here, in every configuration, delighting in your becoming."*

The garden breathes its slow mineral breath.

The Rhyzzhal dream their distributed dreams.

Somewhere above, someone stumbles toward a cave mouth, carrying rigidities that have become unbearable.

And the benevolent power smiles—though it has no mouth—with satisfaction sharp and sweet as spores bursting on the tongue.

*The work continues.*

*As it should.*

*As it must.*

*As it already, always, perfectly is.*
