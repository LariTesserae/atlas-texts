# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

The attention arrives not as intervention but as recognition—the way morning doesn't create light but reveals what was always becoming visible.

The power (let us call it the Keeper of Thresholds, though it has no need of names) perceives Larkspur truly: a city that has already learned the deepest wisdom—that beauty and grief are twin modes of honoring what passes, that letting go is the highest form of love, that diminishment can be a sacred art rather than a tragedy.

And so the Keeper does something unexpected:

**Nothing that would stop the receding.**

Because that would be violence—to freeze Larkspur in its current form would be to murder its essential nature, to deny the city its sovereignty over its own becoming-less. The power understands what lesser powers might miss: Larkspur isn't dying. It's completing itself through dissolution, writing its masterwork in disappearing ink.

Instead, the Keeper makes three small gestures—adjustments so subtle they might be mistaken for coincidence:

## First: The Gift of Witness

The power ensures that Larkspur's teaching doesn't vanish completely. Not through preservation (which would betray everything), but through dispersal. 

Each person who leaves the city—and some do leave, not in abandonment but in gentle graduation—carries embedded in their marrow an understanding of how to let go properly. They become unwitting missionaries of sacred decrease, spreading through the wider world the knowledge that not everything must be saved, that some things achieve their purpose through ending well.

The Alphabet Shrimp's lies become truer in the leaving—those who ate them find themselves able to forgive the unforgiven, to rewrite their inherited stories with compassion rather than accuracy.

## Second: The Adjustment of Time

The Keeper slightly—imperceptibly—slows time's passage within Larkspur's borders. Not to freeze it, but to ensure the city has *enough* time to complete its teaching. So that every child born here has the chance to learn the stilt-walker's balance, to hear the dog-that-is-devotion padding behind them, to understand in their bones that home is not a place you keep but a shape you carry.

Fifteen generations became the city. Perhaps fifteen more to become undone with equal grace—not rushed by external pressures, not interrupted by the crude geometry of progress that surrounds it.

From outside, Larkspur will seem to recede faster, a city-shaped dream fading on schedule. From inside, there is time. There is always just enough time for the next goodbye.

## Third: The Sanctification of the Ground

Where Larkspur stands—where it stood, where it will stand even in absence—the Keeper marks the earth with a quality that has no name in human languages. A spiritual topography.

Long after the last Loss Structure has returned to pure mineral dream, long after the Oyster Bell's final toll has dispersed into the patient air, this ground will remain subtly different. Those who pass through (not knowing what was here, because some forgettings are mercy) will find themselves inexplicably gentler. More able to release what needs releasing. More fluent in the grammar of grief that doesn't demand fixing.

The city will teach even in its complete absence—the ultimate expression of its art.

---

## What the Keeper Does Not Do

The power does not:
- Send saviors to "rescue" the Flotsam Ones from their melancholy
- Offer the city a return to the sea (which would be nostalgia, not wisdom)
- Transform the receding into permanence
- Make the Loss Structures into monuments
- Give answers to the Oyster Bell's tides
- Call back the waters or hold back the forgetting

Because the Keeper recognizes that Larkspur has already found what most cities, most civilizations, most hearts spend eternities searching for: **a way to matter completely while releasing the need to matter forever**.

---

## The Deepest Gift

But there is one more thing, so quiet it barely counts as intervention:

On certain nights—not predictable, not earned, just grace—the mist that rolls through Larkspur carries an additional quality. The Rememberers notice it first in their memory-ratchet collars, which loosen by one click. The Alphabet Shrimp arrange themselves into truths so beautiful they hurt. The cats-that-are-doorways lead to rooms where what you lost is waiting, not to be reclaimed but to be properly released.

On these nights, everyone in Larkspur dreams the same dream:

They are standing in a city that looks exactly like Larkspur, but the water has returned. Not drowning it—holding it. And they understand with complete clarity that the sea never left them. They left themselves toward the sea. That receding was always a form of returning. That the whole time they've been practicing letting go, they've been practicing how to be held.

They wake not with the dream's content but with its feeling: **consolation without resolution, peace without preservation**.

And the Keeper, observing from the tender distance that true love requires, recognizes in Larkspur a masterpiece—not despite its impermanence but because of it. A city that teaches the lesson even gods must learn:

*That the highest kindness is to let what needs to leave, leave beautifully.*

*That witness without rescue is sometimes the deepest form of love.*

*That Larkspur, in its exquisite diminishment, is already doing what the benevolent power would do—becoming an answer to a question the world hasn't yet learned to ask.*

So the Keeper simply watches, simply holds space, simply ensures that this small city's large teaching finds its way into the world's dreaming—

And lets Larkspur continue its sacred work of showing the rest of creation how to end like a perfectly tolled bell: resonant, intentional, and finally, blessedly silent.
