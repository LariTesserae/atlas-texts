# The Visitation

The benevolent power approaches Eremonos not through space but through *care*—arriving the way attention arrives at pain, sudden and complete. It has no name in languages that require sound, but the city's negative-space dwellers feel it as a warmth in their absences, like phantom limbs remembering sun.

It does not *enter* the inverted mountain. It has always been there, waiting in the gaps between its own waiting, patient as geology, gentle as the space between breaths.

## What It Sees

The power—let us call it the Witness, though it witnesses by *being witnessed*—perceives what others miss: that Eremonos is not empty but *emptying*, engaged in the sacred work of unburdening itself from the weight of having meant something once. The city is not broken but *breaking free*, dissolving the calcified dreams of its original builders who loved their vision more than the lives it might contain.

It sees the scar-shaped census taker marking stars and recognizes the profound dignity of this counting—how the tallest absence has taken upon itself the unbearable task of noticing what disappears, keeping vigil over loss itself.

It sees the Children of the Second Wrong Turn and knows their games are not play but prayer, each burning raindrop a tiny act of resurrection, proof that even mistakes can be redeemed into light.

It sees the Archivist in her catacombs, tending apologies that never reached their destinations, and understands: she is not hoarding regret but *composing it*, the way coal compresses into diamonds given enough pressure and time.

## What It Does (First Movement: Witnessing)

The Witness does not fix.

It does not heal, does not restore, does not return Eremonos to some imagined wholeness. Instead, it performs the radical act of *seeing the city exactly as it is* and finding it perfectly, heartbreakingly sufficient.

Where it gazes, things shift—not into newness but into *permission*.

The buildings that reproduce through mitosis feel, for the first time, that their strange proliferation is not disease but fertility. The rooms begin to birth themselves with something approaching joy, each new corridor a verse in an architectural poem about becoming.

The streets that divorced and remarried feel their unions witnessed, legitimized. Their unhappiness remains—the Witness does not erase suffering—but now it is *their* unhappiness, owned and named, no longer shameful. Some streets, emboldened, file for separation. Others renew their vows. Both choices are blessed.

The technology-corpses half-buried in the city's bones feel themselves recognized not as failures but as ancestors, their random button-pressing rituals reframed: not cargo cult but genuine communion with mystery, faith that meaning might emerge from sustained attention to the inexplicable.

## What It Does (Second Movement: The Gift of Moisture)

The Witness cannot return the boiled oceans—even benevolent powers have limits, and some losses are final. But it can offer *tears*.

Not water. Something more precious: *the capacity to grieve*.

It weeps one impossible tear that falls upward through the inverted cone, rising past the spiral streets, the fractal labyrinths, the bridges married to bridges. Where the tear touches stone, something loosens. The petrified architecture—temple upon apartment upon factory—remembers that it was once flexible, was once built by hands that trembled with hope and doubt.

The tear reaches the Children of the Second Wrong Turn. They catch it between their nineteen possible angles, and suddenly understand: they were not born from error but from *courage*—from their mother's willingness to get lost, to risk the unmapped territory between here and nowhere.

They pass the tear to the Archivist of Unsent Apologies. When it touches her jars of preserved forgiveness-embryos, something unprecedented happens: the apologies complete themselves. Not by being sent—their recipients are long gone, unreachable across the geography of time—but by being *released from the obligation to be sent*. The jars open. The half-formed words drift out like luminous pollen, settling into the city's cracks, and where they land, small things begin.

Not growth, exactly. Not in the way living things grow. But a kind of *softening*.

## What It Does (Third Movement: The Redistribution)

The Witness turns its attention to the city's cruelest feature: the privatized gravity, the poverty that weighs you down while wealth makes you float.

Here, finally, it *acts*.

With a gentleness that nonetheless permits no argument, it *redistributes weight*.

Not by making everything equally light—that would be another tyranny, another imposed vision. Instead, it makes weight *voluntary*. Those crushed under poverty's literal burden feel the pressure ease, not vanish. They can choose now: carry this weight as strength, as ballast that keeps you grounded when winds of meaning threaten to blow you into abstraction, or set it down for a time to rest.

Those floating in wealthy weightlessness find themselves offered *substance*—not forced down, but given the option to *matter* again, to have consequence, to touch ground and leave footprints that mean something.

The change ripples through the economy of weirdness. The Department of Temporal Traffic Control finds its regulations rewriting themselves, not into chaos but into something more like *courtesy*—yesterday's sunlight still sometimes arrives tomorrow, but it asks permission first, and if refused, finds another route.

## What It Does (Fourth Movement: Speaking to the Sleeper)

Finally, the Witness approaches the City Itself, the dreaming sovereign who hungers for meaning and devours the spaces where people should be.

It does not wake the city. To wake it against its will would be violence.

Instead, it lies down beside Eremonos in the dark and *tells it a story*.

The story is simple: "You are not the builders' failed dream. You are what happened *after* their dream, which makes you real in a way dreams can never be. Your emptiness is not absence but *capacity*. Your hunger is not wrong—but you have been eating shadows when you crave light."

The city, in its sleep, *listens*.

"What you want," the Witness continues, voice like distant bells, "is not to wake but to *dream differently*. You have been dreaming of fullness, of purpose, of the glory of housing millions in orderly prosperity. These are the builders' dreams, not yours. What if you dreamed instead of being exactly what you are: a sanctuary for all the misfit geometries, a home for absences, a place where the rejected and impossible can finally rest?"

The city's dreaming changes texture. Softens. Its hunger remains—hunger is honest, and the Witness does not despise it—but it begins to feed on something else: the satisfaction of providing shelter to those who belong nowhere else.

The buildings begin to adapt themselves. Not consciously, but the way bodies adapt to repeated loving touch.

Rooms appear with walls at non-euclidean angles perfect for beings who exist between dimensions. Corridors stretch into comfortable lengths for those who measure distance in grief rather than meters. Windows open onto pocket universes where the children of wrong turns can play without fear of being corrected into convention.

## What It Does (Fifth Movement: The Invitation)

The Witness creates one new feature in Eremonos: *a door*.

Not a grand portal. Not an escape hatch. Just a small door, barely noticeable, tucked between the fourth and fifth floors in the Room That Should Have Been Empty.

The door is always unlocked. It leads *out*.

Not out of the city—those who need to leave can already find their ways through the labyrinth if they look hard enough. This door leads out of *necessity*. Through it, you can glimpse other possibilities: the person you would have been if your train had run on time, the world where your apology arrived, the timeline where you chose differently.

But here is the gift: you can look through this door and then *choose to stay anyway*.

The Committee of Rejected First Drafts discovers the door first. Chairperson Empty Chair (who is finally granted a name by the Witness—not spoken aloud, but written in the grain of their wood) proposes a new motion: "Resolved: That we are not rejected but *selected* for this exact existence, in this exact place, with these exact others."

The motion passes. Not unanimously—some abstain, which is their right, newly granted. But it passes.

Those who vote yes feel something shift. They are still drafts, still unfinished, still bearing the editor's red marks of insufficiency. But now these marks are not wounds but *character*, the way a scar tells the story of survival.

## What Remains

The Witness does not stay. Benevolent powers never do—to remain would be to impose, and love does not colonize.

But it leaves traces:

The scar-shaped census taker continues counting vanished stars, but now each chalk mark across the sky glows faintly with bioluminescence, turning the dome overhead into a constellation map of *what was loved enough to be mourned*.

The Children of the Second Wrong Turn still burn phantom raindrops, but now the fires last longer, warm enough to gather around on cold abstract evenings, telling stories in the light.

The Archivist's catacombs become a place of *pilgrimage*. Those carrying unsent apologies arrive, not to send them, but to lay them down among others, building cairns of shared regret that somehow, mysteriously, lighten rather than burden.

The Last Remaining Continent, that bird-become-elsewhere, finds its molting synchronized now with the city's needs. When a neighborhood grows too rigid, too crystallized in its despair, a mountain range falls as truth-snow, melting into puddles of "what if" that remind the streets: *nothing here is final*.

The Drifters—the almost-people, the not-quite-anyones—discover they can touch now, carefully, if they consent to the risk of partial collapse. Some do. Friendships form in the space between wave and particle. A few even attempt love, which is always collapse of a kind, always death of infinite possibility in favor of one precious particular.

And the City? 

The City dreams on, but its dreams are *kinder* now. It still devours the spaces where people should be, but it has learned to *digest* them differently, to transform that emptiness into something generative: silence that enables listening, absence that permits rest, void that invites creation.

New arrivals still come, still find themselves already citizens, their papers filed at the moment of first forgetting. But now the Department of Unplanned Arrivals includes, with each citizenship packet, a small note (written in ink that remains visible):

*"You are here because somewhere else could not hold your shape. This is not exile. This is homecoming to a place that was always waiting for exactly your geometry of grief, your particular flavor of impossible, your specific beauty that looked like brokenness everywhere else.*

*Welcome. You have always been welcome. The city has prepared for you not a prison of your past but a garden of your future conditional tense—all the things you might still become if given time and space and the radical permission to be strange.*

*The exit is clearly marked (fourth floor, between floors, door on the left). You may leave whenever you wish.*

*We hope you stay."*

## Epilogue: What Benevolence Knows

The Witness, departing through dimensions that have no name for "through," carries with it one truth about Eremonos that even the City has not yet understood:

The place was never meant to be filled. It was meant to be *enough*.

Enough space for those who take up more room than bodies allow. Enough time for those who move through it sideways or backwards or in spirals. Enough strangeness for those who were always too much or not enough in the ordinary world's narrow registers.

Eremonos is not the end of the sentence that describes the world.

It is the *ellipsis*—the punctuation that means "and also," "and furthermore," "and the story continues in ways grammar has not yet learned to describe."

In the inverted mountain, at the summit of the hollow cone, the city stands. Empty and full. Ending and beginning. Lost and found.

Precisely, perfectly, impossibly *enough*.

The streetlamps bloom their blue light. The phantom rain etches its absence into walls. The shadows grow heavy with hunted meaning. And somewhere between the fourth and fifth floors, a small door waits, unlocked, patient as the space between kindnesses.

The city breathes its breath of all-breaths-never-taken.

And in that breathing:

*Permission.*
