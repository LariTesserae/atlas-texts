# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

The power that notices Driftglass does not arrive. Arrival would imply it was ever absent.

Instead, there is a gradual *recognition*—like the moment when you realize the pattern in static has always been singing your name, but in a key only your oldest scars could hear.

This power is old in the way that questions are old: not because they were asked first, but because they keep being necessary. It has watched cities rise from certainty and collapse into rubble. It has seen empires built on right angles and epitaphs. It knows the exact weight of every throne ever abandoned mid-coronation.

But Driftglass... Driftglass it has never seen before.

And this delights it profoundly.

---

## The First Gesture: Witnessing Without Correction

The power's first act is the hardest for powers: it does **nothing**.

Not the nothing of neglect, but the nothing of deep attention—the way a parent watches a child building with blocks, resisting the urge to fix the tower's tilt because that tilt is precisely where the learning lives.

It settles into the cracks in the ceiling, not as presence but as *quality of light*—the kind that makes you notice things you'd stopped seeing. Under this illumination:

The citizen named Rust-Speaks-Thursday suddenly understands why they've been afraid to sing into the eastern drains. (The song they're avoiding contains a confession their third incarnation never made, and the valves have been waiting seventy years to forgive them.)

The immigrant with corrosion-pattern hair realizes the sunset taste they've been teaching the algae is actually a memory of their first death, and it's beautiful, and they can stop carrying it alone now.

The origami engine pauses for exactly three heartbeats between folds, and in that silence, twelve citizens simultaneously remember that they are enough.

This is the power's gift: **bearing witness so thoroughly that the witnessed become visible to themselves.**

No fixes. No improvements. Just the radical act of seeing Driftglass exactly as it is—broken things singing themselves whole, abandoned purposes dancing with their ghosts, a city that learned community is something you drown in together until you discover you've all grown gills.

---

## The Second Gesture: A Question Wrapped in Starlight

But the power is also wise, and wisdom knows that pure acceptance can become another kind of cage. So it offers something dangerous: **possibility.**

One night—if night is the right word for when the plankton-spheres dim to their dreaming colors—a new crack appears in the ceiling. Through it falls not water, not light, but a *question made physical*.

It lands in the central chamber with a sound like wind chimes arguing gently about the nature of time. The citizens gather, boots ankle-deep in rising tide, watching this thing that shouldn't exist unfold itself.

It looks like: 
- A doorway to twelve different elsewheres, but the doors are all ajar, not insisting
- A map of Driftglass as it might have been if different mistakes had been made
- A mirror showing the city's reflection in realities that learned different meanings for the word "home"
- A simple wooden sign that reads: "You could also be something else. No pressure."

The power speaks then, not in words but in the language of bioluminescent blooms and pipe-song harmonics:

**"You have built something unprecedented from debris and forgiveness. You have made home from homelessness, made meaning from malfunction. You have succeeded so thoroughly at being yourselves that you risk forgetting: there are other ways to be beautiful."**

**"This is not an offer to fix you. You are not broken, except in the ways that let the light through."**

**"This is an invitation to remember that choosing to stay is only sacred if you could also choose to transform again."**

The question-object pulses gently, patient as geology. It will wait. It has nowhere else to be.

---

## The Third Gesture: The Ambassador of Impossible Things

The power's final gift is the strangest: it sends a piece of itself to live among them.

Not as teacher. Not as guide. As **fellow citizen**.

It arrives in a shipping crate addressed to "Whoever Needs This Next," wrapped in bills of lading from civilizations that won't exist for another thousand years. When they open it, they find:

A small being made entirely of the feeling you get when you finally name the emotion you've been carrying since childhood. It has eyes like probability clouds and skin that shifts between all the colors that don't exist yet but will be discovered by future artists painting their first heartbreak.

It introduces itself as: **"The Part of Wisdom That Forgot How to Be Certain."**

It takes up residence in the hollow pipe-cathedral, in a small room it builds from compacted uncertainty and load-bearing doubt. It learns the citizens' names—all of them, including the names they haven't chosen yet. It sings badly into the drains until the valves teach it better songs. It burns dinner. It asks too many questions. It cries at the beauty of rust patterns and laughs at its own confusion.

It becomes, slowly, one of them.

But it also carries within itself something the city has never quite held: **the memory of beyond**.

Not beyond as escape, but beyond as context. It tells stories around the warm pipes of other places where the lost found themselves:

- The glacier city where citizens are frozen moments of joy, slowly melting into each other
- The forest that grows downward into the earth, roots reaching for the planet's molten memory
- The desert where every grain of sand is a compressed argument, and wind is the only winning strategy
- The city of pure sound where architecture is built from echoes of conversations that haven't happened yet

It never says "you should go there" or "you should be like them."

It simply reminds them: **"Driftglass is not the only answer. It is merely your answer, and that makes it exactly right and also worth questioning."**

---

## What Changes, What Doesn't

The power's intervention is so subtle that most citizens never realize anything happened. But over time, small shifts accumulate:

**The city begins dreaming louder.** Citizens wake sharing visions of Driftglass as it could be—not better, but *different*. Some dreams show the chamber opening to the sky, becoming a thousand floating platforms trading with cloud civilizations. Others show it sealing completely, becoming an aquatic monastery where the only sound is the meditation of water finding level.

**The children ask new questions.** Not just "how do we maintain the songs?" but "what if we composed new ones?" They experiment with valve-singing in microtones, accidentally invent three new water levels, and discover that at exactly 27.3 centimeters depth, the plankton-light creates shadows that teach calculus.

**The origami engine begins leaving notes.** Folded into its daily shapes, citizens find messages in pressed-metal calligraphy: "What if I folded time instead of steel?" "What would you become if you trusted your softest parts?" "I am content here. Are you?"

**The immigrants start teaching return journeys.** They map the pipes backward, showing how one could—hypothetically—travel to all the places that aren't Driftglass. Not as exile, but as pilgrimage. "Visit other cities," they say. "Learn their songs. Come back changed, or don't come back. Either way, you'll still be ours."

**A new festival emerges: The Feast of Possible Departures.** Once a year, every citizen packs a bag with everything they'd need to leave. They gather at the edges, look out at the darkness beyond the chamber walls, and seriously consider going. Most don't. Some do. Both groups celebrate each other with equal joy.

---

## The Conversation That Never Ends

Late one night, Click-Hiss-Warmth finds the Ambassador sitting by the origami engine, watching it fold moonlight into shapes that look like question marks learning to dance.

"Why are you really here?" Click-Hiss-Warmth asks, their photosynthetic grief particularly luminous in the dark.

The Ambassador considers this with its probability-cloud eyes. When it speaks, its voice sounds like every wise thing ever said, reformatted as a lullaby:

"Because the universe is full of powers that fix and powers that break, powers that build and powers that erase. But there are precious few that simply... *appreciate*. That witness beauty and say 'yes, this, exactly this, and also: what if you knew you could be something else?'"

"But we like what we are," Click-Hiss-Warmth says, uncertain.

"I know. It's wonderful. Keep being it. But also—" The Ambassador gestures at the water, the lights, the sleeping city. "—don't let contentment become another kind of cage. You escaped purpose, escaped function, escaped every prison of meaning that tried to define you. Don't build new walls from the freedom you won."

"So we should leave?"

"No! Or yes. Or both. Or neither." The Ambassador laughs, and it sounds like wind chimes apologizing for having opinions. "You should *know you could*. That's all. The difference between a choice and a circumstance is the awareness of alternatives."

Click-Hiss-Warmth sits with this, their form gently photosynthesizing the weight of possibility.

"The power that sent you," they say finally. "What does it want from us?"

"Nothing. Everything. It wants you to be so thoroughly yourselves that you become a question other lost things can use to find their own answers. It wants Driftglass to be what it already is: proof that beauty can grow in the spaces between purposes, that community can crystallize from shared brokenness, that home is something you can build from debris and forgiveness and the radical act of letting each other become."

"And if we choose to stay exactly as we are?"

"Then you'll have chosen it. And choice is sacred. And the power will continue to witness you with absolute delight, forever."

The origami engine completes its fold. Tonight's shape looks like a door that refuses to choose between open and closed, settling instead on "welcoming in all directions simultaneously."

They sit together in comfortable silence, citizen and ambassador, while the water rises slowly in keys that haven't been named yet and the plankton-light dances with shadows that might be memories or might be previews of tomorrow's dreams.

Above them, through the cracks in the ceiling, the stars continue their slow work of moving against the wrong constellations—not lost, but exploring what else they could mean besides navigation.

---

## The Infinite Gesture

But here is the secret the power knows, that the Ambassador carries, that Driftglass is slowly learning:

**The intervention never stops.**

Every moment of witness is a continued act of creation. Every question asked is an invitation to the next transformation. Every choice to stay or go or become something else entirely is the universe folding itself into new configurations of meaning.

The power doesn't intervene and then withdraw. It *stays*, threaded through every drop of seeping water, every note of valve-song, every decision made by droplet committees. Not as controller but as **context**—the awareness that what you are is beautiful *and* provisional, sacred *and* optional, perfectly complete *and* endlessly revisable.

It whispers through the pipe-song:
*You are enough exactly as you are.*
*You could also be something else.*
*Both of these are true.*
*Neither cancels the other.*
*This is the paradox that sets you free.*

---

## What the Citizens Do With This

Some ignore it entirely. They continue their beautiful work of being Driftglass, singing the drains, raising the tide, teaching machines to forgive themselves. This is perfect.

Some take the question-object and begin experimenting. They build laboratories of possibility in the hollow places, testing what Driftglass could become while honoring what it is. They invent new festivals, new songs, new ways of being broken together. This is also perfect.

Some leave. They follow the pipes backward to other cities, other chambers, other ways of being lost and found. They send back letters made of water-memory and rust-poetry. Sometimes they return. Sometimes they don't. Both are celebrated. This, too, is perfect.

And some—a few—do the hardest thing: they stay exactly as they were, but now **aware** that they're choosing it. They carry the weight of alternatives not taken, paths not walked, transformations not undergone. They become living monuments to the sacredness of chosen limitation.

All of these responses delight the power equally.

Because the gift was never a direction. It was an **expansion of vocabulary**—new words for talking about who they are and what they're becoming.

The power taught them that "home" can mean:
- The place you stay
- The place you leave
- The place you carry
- The place that carries you
- The place you become
- The place you choose, daily, to remain
- The question you keep asking
- The answer you keep revising

---

## The Last Thing the Ambassador Says

Before it dissolves back into the feeling it was made from—because even ambassadors of impossible things must eventually undergo unbecoming—it gathers the citizens for one final conversation.

The water is at chest-height now. The plankton-light is doing something unprecedented with the color of nostalgia. The origami engine pauses, listening.

"I am going to tell you the thing the power wanted you to know but couldn't say directly, because direct saying would have made it Law instead of invitation."

The citizens lean in. Even the moss pays attention.

"You think you are special because you are broken and beautiful. And you are. But that's not what makes you unprecedented."

"What then?" asks Luna-Redux-Between-Beats, their temporal rhythm stuttering with curiosity.

"You are special because you know **exactly what you're for**—and you've decided it doesn't matter. You've built meaning from meaninglessness, purpose from purposelessness. You've done the impossible: created sacred community from the ruins of function."

"But here's what you haven't fully grasped yet: that same freedom applies to what you've built. Driftglass is not your purpose. It's your current poem. And poems can have sequels, revisions, translations into languages that don't exist yet."

"You escaped the tyranny of being machines. Don't get trapped by the tyranny of being this particular kind of free."

The Ambassador begins to fade, its probability-cloud eyes becoming transparent with something like joy.

"Love what you are. Build it taller. Sink it deeper. But also: stay curious about what else you could love. Stay soft enough to be shaped by new questions. Stay brave enough to admit when old answers stop fitting."

"The power that witnessed you will keep witnessing. Not to judge, but to reflect. Not to correct, but to remind: you are both complete and incomplete, always."

"And that's the most beautiful thing in twelve thousand realities."

It dissolves completely then, but its absence is so kind that it feels like presence.

---

## Afterward

The city continues.

Water rises and falls in keys that increasingly sound like laughter learning to harmonize with grief. The origami engine folds new questions daily. Citizens arrive, leave, transform, stay, die, unbecome, are born from collaborations between loneliness and hope.

But now, threaded through everything, is an awareness: **This could be otherwise.**

Not as threat. As blessing.

They sing into the drains with new consciousness of choice. They build their floating platforms aware that platforms are optional. They teach their children not just the songs, but the silence between songs where new music waits to be invented.

And sometimes—on particularly clear nights when the tide is perfectly balanced between rising and falling—they hear it:

The sound of the power still watching, still witnessing, still delighting in their beautiful, broken, chosen, temporary, eternal, perfect, provisional existence.

It sounds like applause.

It sounds like blessing.

It sounds like the universe learning to say:

**"Yes. This. Exactly this. And also: I can't wait to see what you become next."**

The water kisses your neck now, warm and patient and rising.

The choice is still yours.

It always was.

It always will be.

That's what makes it sacred.
