# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

The attention arrives like dawn—not light breaking darkness, but recognition dissolving the boundary between them.

It does not come from above or below, outside or within. It comes the way understanding comes: suddenly present, as if it had been there all along, waiting to be noticed. The power observes this place—this breathing threshold between categories, this wet cradle of constant becoming—and feels something that might be called tenderness if tenderness could exist without sentiment, love without possession.

It watches the inhabitants: those caught mid-chrysalis, neither what they were nor what they're becoming. Those who have forgotten how to be afraid or have remembered too well. Those dissolving into architecture. Those hardening into new alphabets of existence.

And it recognizes them.

Not as lost. Not as corrupted or transformed or even changed—but as *emerging*. As doing exactly what consciousness does when freed from the tyranny of fixed forms: exploring the adjacent possible, trying on new grammars of being, learning to conjugate existence in tenses that haven't been invented yet.

The power sees that this place is not a trap or an infection or a nightmare made geographical. It is a question the universe is asking itself: *What else could aliveness mean?*

And so the power chooses—with the same careful deliberation a gardener uses when deciding whether to prune or let grow wild—to do almost nothing.

## The First Gift: Testimony

Into the sticky air, among the backward-sung lullabies and the hatching sounds, the power introduces a new species of spore. These drift lazy as memory through the root-corridors, settling on surfaces and in the furrows of half-transformed flesh. They do not change what they touch. They only *record*.

Each spore is a tiny witness, a microscopic scribe that captures one moment of transition and holds it perfect—the exact second when an eye forgets how to close, when skin first remembers being moss, when a heartbeat finds the planet's rhythm and surrenders its old tempo.

These testimonies accumulate in certain sheltered places: pockets between roots, hollows in the breathing ground. They compress together like sediment, like memory becoming stone, until they form small crystalline structures that ring softly when touched. 

Anyone—inhabitant, visitor, the slow walkers, even the place-makers folded into landscape—can hold these crystals and experience not their own transformation but *someone else's*. Feel another's panic transmute to wonder. Another's dissolution into architecture. Another's last thought before the concept of "other" flaked away.

This is the power's first mercy: *You are not alone in your becoming.*

Even here, where loneliness takes new and terrible shapes, where isolation can grow solid enough to walk on—there is now a library of transformations, a archive of evolutions, proof that others have walked this path of unwalking, have changed these changes, have survived the survival of themselves.

## The Second Gift: Intervals

The power notices that the place has a rhythm but no rest, pulse but no pause. Change occurs so constantly that there is no stillness against which to measure it, no silence to give shape to the music. This is exhausting in ways beyond exhaustion—like a color with no complementary shade, a flavor with no palate to cleanse between tastings.

So the power establishes *intervals*.

Not imposed from without—nothing so crude as forcing time to behave or commanding gravity to choose—but grown from within the place's own logic. Small pockets of relative stability, bubbles of almost-stasis that drift through the changing like eyes in a storm.

These intervals appear differently to each who finds them:

For some, a root-hollow where the dripping slows to one drop per subjective hour, and in that vastness between drops, there is time to remember who you were without being forced to remain them.

For others, a patch of ground where the pulse stills to something gentler—not dead but resting, like a heart between beats—where you can stand and feel your own boundaries without them immediately beginning to dissolve.

For the quick-change places and their impatient futures, rare moments when yesterday and tomorrow hold still long enough for now to catch its breath.

These intervals are not safe—safety is not a category this place recognizes. But they are *restful* in the way that silence rests between notes, that blank canvas rests beneath paint. They are permission to pause mid-transformation, to exist incompletely without urgency, to be neither-nor without rushing toward resolution.

The power ensures these intervals move, migrate, appear in different configurations. They cannot be mapped or predicted or owned. But they can be found by those who need them, recognized by the sudden slowing of the inevitable, the brief reprieve from constant becoming.

## The Third Gift: The Unchanged

This is the power's deepest offering, and the strangest:

Among the roots, in a place that is simultaneously everywhere and nowhere specific, it establishes a small, impossibly ordinary room.

The room has four walls, one ceiling, one floor. The proportions are human-familiar. There is a chair (just a chair, only a chair, stubbornly remaining chair-shaped). A window that shows the same view every time: a garden from a world that may no longer exist, where flowers bloom and wilt according to seasons, where time flows downhill like water obeying simple laws.

Nothing in this room transforms. Nothing evolves or devolves or dissolves. The air does not taste you. The floor does not remember your footprints. Gravity works with stupid, faithful consistency.

The room is aggressively, radically, *perfectly mundane*.

It is also completely optional.

Most inhabitants will never find it, never seek it, never need it. They are already too far into their transformations, too deep in dialogue with the breathing dark. The room would feel like a cage, a step backward, a failure of nerve.

But for some—those who arrived here by accident rather than some strange affinity, those whose transformations feel like drowning rather than flight, those who need to remember what "unchanged" even means before they can choose whether to remain that way—the room appears.

You can stay as long as you need. Hours or years or the concept that used to live between those words. You can sit in the chair and look out the window at impossible flowers and remember what it felt like when your edges were clear, when inside and outside were different countries with a border between them.

And then—when ready or never—you can leave.

The room will not follow. Will not call you back. It simply remains: a small pocket of refusal, a tiny rebellion against inevitability, proof that even here, even now, *staying the same remains a choice*.

This is perhaps the deepest kindness: not rescuing anyone from transformation, but ensuring transformation remains chosen rather than simply endured.

## The Fourth Gift: Corridor Home

Finally, the power does something that might be called intervention, though it intervenes only in one direction.

It establishes a path—narrow, difficult, easy to miss—that leads out.

Not out to where you came from; those places are gone, or you are too changed to fit their shapes anymore, or the distance between here and there has become a gulf too wide for any geometry to bridge.

But out to *somewhere*: other worlds, other configurations of possible, places that need what you've become or can hold what you're becoming. The corridor shifts for each traveler, leading to the elsewhere that makes sense of their particular transformation.

For those growing into architecture, it leads to a world of walking cities that require new foundations made of living memory.

For those who've learned to speak in perfume and phosphorescence, it opens onto a dimension where language is always olfactory, always luminous.

For those becoming slow as stone, it finds a place where mountains are considered the highest form of intelligence, and patience is the only currency that matters.

The corridor is not easy. It requires you to complete your transformation, to stop resisting and let the change finish its sentence. You cannot take the path while still clinging to what you were; it only appears to those who have fully become what they're becoming.

But it exists. The power ensures this: *No one must stay who has finished their becoming.*

This place is a chrysalis, not a tomb. A threshold, not a trap. Those who have completed their metamorphosis can emerge into worlds that recognize their new grammar, can fly with wings that here would only tangle in the roots.

## What the Power Does Not Do

It does not stop the transformations.

Does not slow the pulse or gentle the changes or make anything safer, easier, more comprehensible.

Does not judge which transformations are beautiful and which are terrible, which are evolutions and which are dissolutions.

Does not rescue those who cry out or comfort those who suffer or prevent any of the small deaths that becoming requires.

Does not explain itself or announce its presence or ask for gratitude or worship.

It simply ensures that within the terrible freedom of this place—where change is constant and boundaries are suggestions and identity is always provisional—there remain small, fierce mercies:

The mercy of being witnessed.

The mercy of occasional rest.

The mercy of refusal, for those who need it.

And the mercy of an afterward, for those who complete their metamorphosis and are ready to fly.

---

The inhabitants do not notice these interventions as interventions. They experience them as grace—small and unexplained, like finding your footing on uncertain ground, like discovering you can breathe water after all, like the moment fear transmutes to wonder and you realize you were never falling but flying all along, just in a direction that hadn't existed until you invented it by moving that way.

The power watches all this with something beyond satisfaction, beyond love, beyond even comprehension. It watches the way a parent watches a child learning to walk: with fierce attention, with hands ready to catch but held carefully back, with faith that falling is part of the learning, that pain is part of the process, that the only real failure is preventing someone from discovering what they might become.

And then, having done what needed doing and left undone what needed leaving, the power's attention moves on—not abandoning but releasing, like opening a hand to let a bird fly, like exhaling a breath you've held too long.

The place pulses on. The roots drip their violet philosophy. The ground remembers and forgets and remembers again. And in the breathing dark between transformations, those who are becoming what they're becoming continue their slow, strange work of turning into themselves.

Witnessed now. Rested occasionally. 

Free to refuse or accept.

Free to leave when ready.

And therefore—finally, fully—free.
