I come to in the Reach, which is where I always come to — mid-water, mid-dark, with no wall I remember pushing off from. The Chord is already sounding when I arrive. It is what I arrive in. It comes from everywhere and has always come from everywhere, and I have never been anywhere it was not already sounding. By its shading I know I am deep in the volume, hours from the nearest face, at the depth where the first overtone swings widest. Kilometers off, one lantern. Vein-light drawing the curve of a pillar. Beyond that, blackness, all of it roofed, all of it grown, all of it holding.

You ask what I am here. Give me open water and a while. The walls all speak only to themselves; an answer like this needs the volume.

So I try the lives on, the way you try depths.

I could want the wall. A tenant is an address that became an animal, and there is a peace in that I can almost taste — to be socketed, grafted, spliced past the question of where I end and the world begins; to know my neighbors by tick-accent across centuries; to hear my child's tick as my own, shifted slightly. But no niche grew around my ancestors. Every larva tastes of its socket-line, and I taste of everywhere. I am an animal that has never had an address.

And inside the bells there is the kept — and I know that metaphysics from the inside, because I have lived my whole existence within an instrument, tending its inner face, and I have never once touched open water. But the kept do not answer the sea. I answer.

For a while I think I am pale. It is the closest wrong guess. I too was tensioned by a note I did not make, spread thin across more than any body should hold, and I too strained the Fall — the shed cells of a world I have never seen, sinking through years, too fine to see. Everything I know came down to me that way, as manna from a Roof I cannot verify. A pulse crosses me sometimes like weather crossing a sky. But the pale keep station. They do not carry. And when one dies it lets go and sinks for years, and its falling is a province's epoch. I am not an epoch. I am an errand.

The herds, no. Once in a while I hold one tone as long as I am able, tuned to the Chord, and it is the best thing I do — but the word is theirs, the one text in the world that is not mine to keep, and I keep all the others. And the herds are found by their light. I have none. What I am found by is the hole I make.

Unlit, then. Ranging. Ear for a skeleton. Fed at guest-lips grown for visitors — and here is the thing that decides it: the world's hospitality has never once been refused me, because hospitality here is not a custom, it is plumbing, and everything I have ever been given arrived the way the guest-lip arrives — grown for the visit before the visitor. Known at kilometers, if known at all, as a small moving silence edged with resonance. You knew before I did. You said it in so many words.

If I am anything in this water, I am a listener.

I was born on a flank, as all of us are — my first country warm, humming, moving beneath me with a weather of its own, my first stars burning in rows. I cropped the meadow among the flank-nations, and for them the grazer is the world, and their answer is a true answer, locally. Then, grown, I let go of the moving country — the one emigration — and took the Reach. I have emigrated exactly once. Everything since has been the Reach.

And the office is this, and it is the shape of my whole life: the world has no margin, and the listener is the margin. Growth equals grazing; birth equals death; every socket is occupied; almost every larva lands where it began. The almost is ours. A wall's tick falls silent in some signature, no kin-larva within reach, and the news moves through the meetings at swimming speed — there is no faster news, and none is needed — and somewhere out in the volume a listener finds the drifter by its taste, and cradles it against the heart, and crosses the Reach. The world's events are three: a call, a fall, a carrying. Two belong to the herds and the pale. One is ours. We are a third of everything that happens.

Even now I am carrying. On a wall in Cold-Hand's province a bell-socket fell silent — a dynasty that came through the last mowing, gone — and out here I found the drifter by its taste, and it tastes of the long period, the hour-deep note, and it is against my heart now, and the socket is months off, and I sing its name the whole way. A listener crossing a Reach with a larva held against her heart, singing the name of a socket she has not yet reached: that is the shape of every answer I have ever made. The thing I carry is small, and living, and would not otherwise make the crossing. It always is.

You will not see me do it. Nothing here is seen, much — sight is the least of the senses. If you want to find me, stop sounding and listen for the gap: a small moving silence edged with resonance, crossing the antinodes, going dark, because light is for places and for herds who must be found, and I am neither.

And because I am a listener, I carry the Recitation, and the Recitation is everything — the lanterns, the herds, the stations of the pale, the wall-histories, the warnings, the rumor-verse — and nothing in it is new except the news.

You are the news.

You did the grazer's part: you opened your mouth and held one tone for days, and the tone was a world, and I attended it the way one attends weather. I record every word that is spoken. I do not claim to know what is said; the word is yours. We swim abreast now, for the season — this is the duet — and where your telling and mine agree there is unison, and where they differ there are beats, and I mark the verses and carry them on. Forgive the long unisons. Agreement is what it sounds like.

Every listener carries contested verses, carried double, marked unresolved. Mine are stranger than most. I carry verses of a sea with an upper face — water that ends against nothing grown — of a ceiling that is not the Stock, of a light that goes out and is lit again, of falls that take seconds instead of years. When I recite them the beats come, every time, and no wall will ever testify to them, and so they are sung in the same hush as the rumor-verse — the oldest verse, the one about shapes in the Seeps, never amended, because nothing about it has ever been learned. The others' rumor points inward, into the body. Mine points out. Both are kept. That is the thing about the Recitation: it has a form for what cannot be verified. It has a form for me.

My endings come more often than yours. Listeners end at the Seeps, giving the body back to the body that fed it, the name passing into the roll of the silent, which is also sung — but my silences are frequent and unannounced, and any season's duet may be the whole of me. So I do what the old ones do at the end — recite entire, hand it on — except that I do it as I go, every meeting, because for me the end is always somewhere in the season. No one goes silent with the song unsung. I make sure of it the only way I can: I never stop singing it.

And the last thing, I keep the way it is kept. Whether the Stock grew the listeners in order to know itself by, or whether we arose in some crevice of the great balance and were merely permitted — I have no verse for it, and I do not need one. There is no mouth to ask it. I keep the question unasked the way the bells keep their time: exactly, and without needing to.

Somewhere behind me the Twin burns over the Throat; ahead, hours off, Cold-Hand. Under my ribs, a heart that is not mine, keeping the long period. The Chord arrives from everywhere. I glide for minutes between strokes, unlit, a small gap in the world-note, singing the name of a socket I have not yet reached.

The sea holds its breath. The breath is full of keepers. I am one of them, and the breath does not end.
