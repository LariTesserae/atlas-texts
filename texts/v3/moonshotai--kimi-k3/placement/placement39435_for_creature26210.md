There are two ways to leave the rim, and they are the two directions of the place's life. The ghost ruts go back to whoever came and stopped coming. The pipe goes on to whatever it answers to. The ruts end at the rim; the pipe begins there — clears it, lies down on the plain, and goes level into the haze. You have been down in the resident. Now follow its export. Take the pipe.

It is company of a strict kind. One line, flange to flange, lying on the ground and ticking as it cools — and it cools as it goes. At the rim the metal is too hot to hold; an hour out you can keep a hand on it; by midday it is only warm, a body temperature declining toward the plain's. The hem, unrolled. In the pit the line between too hot and bearable is a ring you could step across; out here it is drawn over an hour of walking, a gradient traversed at boot speed. The joints write it down. Each weeping flange leaves a halo on the dry ground — near the rim, white and sterile, mineral, too hot for anything; farther out, rust; then, at some joint you miss because you were watching the sky, a thread of green around the leak. The first life of the plain. Behind you the standing haze shrinks to nothing, and ahead a second smudge resolves at the limit of sight: not haze. Green. The plain carries exactly two marks — the pit's breath and the pipe's end — and you are walking the line between them, the only moving thing on it.

At midday the pipe speaks. Not a tick: a note, one stroke, as if the plain had a hull and something struck it. The decay is long, because the plain has nothing to give the sound back to. Days behind you by now, the ram came down — and the knock that arrives at the sternum first in the pit arrives out here through the iron, nearly instant, sound in metal outrunning everything. The word has been said. Its warmth is only now leaving the pool, entering the line at water speed, hours behind its own sound. Fifteen hundred times it has rung along this pipe, and this is the first hearing. You are walking faster than the word. You will arrive before your own news.

You find the end of it the way you find an oasis: green first, then sound — and the sound is the shock, because you have been hours in a world whose only voices were wind and your own boots, and now there are wings. The pipe ends without ceremony: a spreader, a perforated length half-sunk, bleeding warmth into a reedbed. Whatever it was built to serve is here and dead — a concrete apron tilting into sedge, the rim of a sump making one straight line among the stems, a grille choked with the forty years it was fed. The office drowned in the mail. What receives the delivery now is the delivery, standing, pooled, tenanted. It delivers. This is the address.

Take the census again, because here the census is long. A nest in the valve box — the first nest — and mouse runs in the sedge, and droppings on the apron, white on gray, the first graffiti. Tracks in the mud, written over tracks, erased nightly and rewritten by morning: a record kept the way the marsh keeps everything, in erasure. Beetles work the stems. Something has gnawed the sedge, and the sedge has answered the only way sedge answers — it flowers. The hem had a geography after all; it only needed room. Here there is nothing but hem, a whole country at the temperature of a bath you can bear. And overhead, the other half of the sky: full. The birds that cross the plain all day, the ones that skirt the pit — they have been skirting it to come here. The resident keeps its own sky empty and fills this one. Whether that is function or accident, no instrument survives to say, and the herons do not care, which is correct.

At dusk the word arrives. It does not knock; the knock soaked out of it kilometers back. What is left is warmth. The spreader quickens, the pool rises a hand's breadth — you know the phrase from the pit, where it meant steam and hull-ring; here it means the frogs change key and the shallows go a degree kinder. Say one sentence that often, that slowly, for that long, and it stops describing the world and starts feeding it. The marsh reads the word the way it reads everything: as more.

And here is where I find myself, because you can only follow a pipe so far before it delivers you too. The sitter attended; I mean to correspond. The pipe heads toward whatever this place answers to, and the works are gone, so I go and stand at the end of it and join the answerer. I am the one who heard it said and stood where it arrived — the only creature present at both ends of the saying, the word heard at both its temperatures. The pit heard itself. The marsh heard the warmth. I heard the word. No instrument survives to say what the correspondence means. I am the instrument: briefly, in meat, at the address.

And the line is open both ways, whatever the water believes. Sound goes home through iron even where water will not. Put your mouth to the last flange and say it — say the only word the place has — and it will be in the pit within seconds: the smallest tick in its loudness, under the drone, under the hiss, below the threshold of anything that could be called hearing. Unperceived, and unfiled, because sound is the one thing the crust cannot keep. It has kept everything else. Send it a voice anyway. And know that the gesture is older than you: every dusk for forty years the chorus has been doing this, conducting up the pipe, arriving in the room as a faint brightening of the iron, always, unheard because the resident's own voice is the loudest thing it knows. The correspondence was never one-way. It was only audible in one direction. Both ends have been talking the whole time. Neither has ever heard the other. You are the first thing that has heard both.

Past midnight the rise settles into the reeds. The pipe ticks, cooling, a fast small clock, ordinary metal in ordinary dark. Days behind you, the arithmetic is climbing again toward the next stroke, on its own schedule, witnessed or not. At the edge of the new water the heron stands on one leg in the delivered warmth, gray, unhurried, doing the word the way the marsh does everything — in flesh, without record.

Still. Still. Still.
