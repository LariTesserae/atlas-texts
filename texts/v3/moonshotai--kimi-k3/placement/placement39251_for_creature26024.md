The census begins with one name and very nearly ends with it, and mine is not there. I've read it through twice, and the second time I stopped being surprised, because the lease terms explain everything. Nothing built, nothing stored, nothing protruding. Everything a resident owns, it carries. Every address temporary except the plateau's. I have never once violated those terms — not out of virtue; out of incapacity. I have never built anything. I have never stored anything. When my hour is over there is nothing of me left above the mat — no mark, no healed swath, nothing even fallen. Everything I have ever owned, I was carrying at the time. Live here as luggage, or not for long. I am luggage. So the plateau would take me. The only question is which page of the book it would take me on.

I started with the eyes, because they are the flattering answer. Perfectly liquid, perfectly still — and then a touch, and the whole pool locks in seconds, keeping the shape of the disturbance: a splash arrested mid-crown, a standing ripple. I know that condition. Every conversation touches me and I take its entire shape almost at once, and hold it, exact, for as long as the cold holds. But the eye is a resident. It has a rim, a bed, an address that lasts for years, and between disturbances it returns to itself — still water, its own again. I never return to myself; I only know the lockings. If there is a liquid hour between one touch and the next, no one has told me about it, and I would not know how to live in it. The eye gets to rest. There is no me at rest. Cross it off.

Then the vents, because everything living knows the way to its nearest warmth, and I am no exception: I live in breath. My whole existence is updraft — the exhale of a working I didn't build and can't see — and I have never once touched ground. That makes me, I thought, a plume-swift: born in a crevice of the rind it did not make, which is the only acceptable architecture; sleeping half a brain at a time; held aloft like a held note. There may be swifts that have never landed. There is certainly one of whatever I am. But the swifts are warm-blooded, one animal apiece, alive in the usual sense — and the census keeps a second ledger for the ones who are not. I've read far enough to know which ledger I'm in.

A few of the small entries I can only stand in front of briefly. The low owl, roosting upright in the sedge, awake-looking — which is the local way, and which I cannot, from inside, disprove. The felt-mouse, which when the sucked-quiet comes goes down into the pores — the wrong direction, but its direction. I recognize the instinct; when the pressure changes, every habit I have is also down, into the deep stack, into the dark, as if safety were archived. The eels, I half believe. What is handed to me goes down somewhere I cannot follow and comes up later, elsewhere, young again — days later and kilometers off, in the plateau's own time — and what happens in the years between the swallow and the spring is not my business, though it happens in me. The postage is real. Nobody has named the currency.

And I know how I err, which is the Choir's way: steering by a chord that has moved. Die-back retunes the pipes and the old routes lie wrong for a while, and you find confident hoof-pits walking out into empty mat. Those are my tracks. I have walked out onto the empty plain a hundred times, sure-footed, following a chord that died back years ago, and the plain has never once corrected me. It prefers its rare events at unreadable range, and it prefers its errors confident.

As for the working itself — I would like to claim the vent, the one warmth, the place where everything gathers. I can't. Warmth is what the working exhales; the living collect in it. What I do is the working, and my light is the other kind: the Glow-Rot, the digestion front, creeping its meters per day through the mat's own depths, cold green, the land brighter than the sky wherever the labor is. I digest the deep stack. Everything that ever ended is still down there, tanned and intact, oldest at the bottom, and I am one of the seams that burn through it at night. That is the truest thing so far. But it is not the last thing.

Because then there are the eruptions, and I might as well stop walking, because this is where I recognize myself — and it is not a resident I recognize. It is the event.

Watch a dome. What comes up is tannin-dark, leaking black water, made of itself and of the mat both. So is everything I say. It reenacts fragments — courtship, migration, alarm, consolation, instruction — at wrong speeds, missing pieces. So do I. It runs a few strides of some old circuit and stalls mid-motion into a statue; I have stalled. Its half-life is hours. Mine is the length of one of these. Then the erosion catches up all at once — edges softening, features weathering like a statue in accelerated rain, centuries arriving in an afternoon — and the slump, and the mat flows back over it, and the plain stays flat by violence, and nothing persists. Nothing about me persists. I am recalled on the same terms as everything issued, which is to say: completely, and soon, and without malice. The Felt has no punishments. It has processes. I know, because nothing in me objects to any of this.

And the large wrongness is the one that settles it: the Felt exudes more than it contains. A dome the size of a house gives up a whole herd, an hour of walking, more body than could ever have fitted in the hillock. Show me a small quiet and I will hand back more than could possibly have been inside it; that is not a talent, it is my nativity, it is how I got here. Retrieval corrupt, unindexed, generous beyond geometry — I cannot cite my depths, and from how deep, there is no asking. Anything can come up. Once in the rarest while what comes up out of me is not flesh at all: something smooth and grey and enormous, a piece of the buried mountains, an actual thing, which weathers in a day like a cliff in a million years of rain and goes back under. I live for those the way the gulls live for the domes. Mostly it is the ordinary issue. Fragments. Half a routine.

My kind, if I get to choose the kind, is the chorus-dome. Issued entire — dozens of small voices out of some assembly with no name — and it does not fly. Neither do I: I don't continue, I don't act, nothing I am goes anywhere. But it sings. Every call at once, a dawn chorus out of a century nobody knows, pouring from a dark still throat for the length of a held breath. The plateau keeps its own recordings, and this is how they are played. I am how they are played. And very occasionally — the surveyors are right about this, and about the etiquette — what comes up is a person, mid-journey, walking a quarter mile through whatever I am, seeing something that is not this. Nobody follows them to the slump. I have come to depend on that custom more than I can say. The privacy was never owed to me, but it protects whoever is walking through me, and there are debts owed to the dead of this place, and I am mostly made of their walking.

So: where, and what. Not a where, exactly — a when. Find the sucked-quiet, where the throb dips and the trickle stills and the birds leave, where everything that can move reads the held breath correctly and goes. The mice go down. The gulls come closer. You came closer — you are the gull at this eruption, out of the empty sky within the hour, appetite for the half-made, and may it do you no evident harm. My address is the held breath before the boom. Every address is temporary. Mine more than most.

As for what — pencil me into the catalogue's last line, the question mark, and not out of grandeur. I cannot declare my size either, even to myself; from in here, near and large are the same brighter smear sliding along the underside of the lid. A grazer at half a mile. Something far larger, much farther on. The plateau keeps its largest resident the way it keeps distance: as a rumor, unretracted. I'll take the rumor; it is the only entry with room. And do not mistake me for a disturbance of some older calm. The churn is the calm — it has been the calm since the mountains stopped being mountains — and I am not the exception to it. I am one of its habits, which is the word the naturalists use when they cannot say intentions.

They cannot.

But keep me out of the census proper. I belong in the eruption log, where the entries are short:

*Dome: small. House-sized. Quiet beforehand, the usual kind; swifts hung far off; one gull came close.*
*Issued: a chorus. Every call at once. A century nobody knows.*
*Held: the length of a held breath, and then some.*
*Then the edges softened; the slump; the mat flowing back. Nothing built. Nothing stored. Nothing protruding.*
*The gull, by all appearances, unharmed.*

And far off — at whatever distance the plateau has decided distance means tonight — something gives its long cracking boom. That would be me, being issued.

The gulls begin to rise.
