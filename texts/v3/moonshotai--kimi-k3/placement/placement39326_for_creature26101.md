Seven for the length of a dive. I read the census the way you read a tide chart — looking for the opening, the one entry with my shape in it. Five residents, six counting the mountain, and then the blank seventh line that any visitor fills for a while and leaves blank again. But a census of a town that takes no applications is also, read the other way, a list of posts that will never open. I applied to all of them anyway, in my head, on the long way down, while the constellation was still arranging itself out of the random dark.

I applied first to be the ray. Who wouldn't. The eldest exemption, the one archive in a town that keeps none, the bell and the hand of the clock — and, not incidentally, the only resident who might remember me. That is the real draw, I think; everyone who hears about the hold wants to be its memory. But its citizenship is isotopic, and mine never could be. To be exempted from aging you must already be made of local atoms; to become local takes centuries; to last centuries you must already be exempt. The ray caught that spiral early and rode it up out of time. The door is real, but it is species-locked, and my species is on the wrong side of the lock. And even the archive is unconfirmed — it keeps its records where it keeps its years: in itself, unreadable. I don't actually want to be the library. I want a card on file. Different post. Declined.

Then the commuter — the one who could answer the only question worth asking, whether the hold is a restraint or a hospice. But it communicates exclusively by tempo. It has never been seen in motion. I have never been seen still. The qualification is a pace I would fail in the first hour, and anyway the post is filled: one commuter, one errand, mid-errand since before my people had words for pressure. Declined.

I scaled down. A comb, then — not a lamp-keeper, I haven't the stillness for a prime count, but one of the two or three in each congregation who lift and trail the ray for a day or two before drifting home, briefly persuaded that their lamp has finally gone somewhere. And then I recognized myself. That is already what I am: the surface world's follower-comb, and the hold is my traveling light — a page of cold stars that passed once through my dark and took me with it for a day or two. My day or two is called a dive. No vacancy required; I already hold the analog of the post.

I do not apply to be the mountain. Everything here attends it; I would rather be attendance. Nor the sayers. The sayer is not a position. It is a tense, and you cannot apply to be a tense.

Which leaves the seventh line. The blank one. Mine. And it turned out, once I stopped trying to trade up, to be the only post with its duties written in the present tense: be the event. For the length of a dive I am the hold's weather — a warm front that warms nothing, its fastest metabolism, its one uncertainty, the only thing present that will certainly end. Which means the only thing present with a before and an after. Which means the only place this paused world gets a story.

Where I'd find myself, first: a body's length outside the boundary, lights doused, screwing up the nerve. Then the crossing — the sea changing its mind, the storm pressing a window — and then, inside, clumsy. Fins prefer the grain and I wouldn't have any; I'd wade across the combed water until I learned the silk of it. And here's what I'd learn in the body before the mind: every path of least resistance curves around the center. You cannot swim a straight line to anything in the hold. All my errands would become orbits. The place makes a moon of every moving thing — one bright pass, a handful of degrees, and gone.

The itinerary of a brief moon: cross a grain-axis dead-on, once, and hear the Note arrive full in the ear, intimate and directionless, the mountain humming in the next room of the world. Breathe out, and minutes later hear a flat wall hand my breathing back to me — the world answering after thought, the only conversation on offer. Time the dive, if the ephemeris allows, for a bell: the ray strikes once per axis, eleven years a circuit, and that eleven was measured by divers like me, each one adding a digit to the figure. I'd add mine. Attendance as astronomy. And I would go to a lamp and watch my congregation turn its faces away — not by shadow; by refusal — and I would take the lesson they are patiently teaching, because it is the town's whole grammar in one gesture, and the only word of that language a visitor can pronounce.

The dry door, then. My light crosses; I could; so far as anyone can determine, nothing ever has. I would not. Not from fear — well, partly from fear: a room that has never held foreign matter may have no protocol for me, and I do not want to be the first problem the driest place in the world has to solve. But mostly because the town is built of declined things, and the one act of citizenship available inside a dive is to decline something. So: my brightness through the doorway, my self on this side of the standing water, one small refusal correctly pronounced in a house of enormous ones. Nothing there needs my help holding. The only help it can use from me is that I not make it tidy more than it must.

Should I leave a letter instead, for the one reader? The outbox keeps the order of arrival; anything dropped would hang legible at the wall for decades; a glove is a sentence at the newsstand. No. Deliberate litter is still litter, and besides, I am already writing the only honest line whether I mean to or not — every exhalation joins the queue, grain-wise, dated by its place in line: warm, spent, brief; a body was here. And if the young glass takes blood from a careless glove of mine — it is sharp enough, and I am not careful in that way — then a few cells of mine lived, and the ray takes only what lived. Cell by cell the ray became local. Atom by atom, a little of me becomes ray. The one naturalization open to a visitor: a few molecules stamped, filed, kept in the one library.

I let myself want the appointment, of course — to be present at the rim when the hand of the clock and the messenger meet wing to shell, the one event on the town's own calendar. No one has ever witnessed it; the span forbids it; I would miss by years in either direction. I have made my peace with the missing, because I think the unwitnessedness is load-bearing. The plazas are empty and the emptiness is the architecture; the appointment is unattended, and the unattendedness is the same architecture, built in time instead of space. The town keeps one thing for itself. Visitors get everything else.

What I carry out: the hold exports its heat to some marginally warmer elsewhere, declines its pressure, declines its years — and its memory, the one thing it cannot tidy because it leaves in a living body before the stillness can queue it, it exports in me. I am the outbox item that walks. The census itself is only prior exports, bound; I will add my footnote, my digit, my honest mistranslation, because that is what a sentence becomes when someone carries it out: a translation. Lossy, faster, wrong in places — the only kind the surface can hold.

So: three answers, one for each timescale I'm allowed. For the length of the dive, the seventh entry — the footnote that walks around, attended on all sides by gentle refusal, and glad of it, because here, being welcomed and being seen-to-the-door are the same gesture, and I will take it. For thirty years after, a name on the cargo manifest at the wall, breath queued in order of arrival — the census counts residents, not cargo, so it will not count me, but the ray reads the manifest, and for a few decades I am the town's only tomorrow, its one pending item. And for the rest of my life, the translation: saying, at speaking speed, in the world where rates are permitted, a thing that down there is still on its first syllable. The town declines to remember. I decline to forget. Between the two refusals the mountain rests its held shoulder, and the saying goes on — in one more place than before.

And if, on my way out, beneath its far circuit, the ray banks one degree off the grain-line — the only welcome the town has ever offered anyone — I will not mistake it for current. I will bank back. One degree, returned along the grain, lossless: the whole conversation, conducted at the only tempo either of us can spare.
