The census never changes and always grows. Here is the growth.

**A late entry.**

The census missed one, and the miss was structural. Descending teaches quiet; the place holds its breath and you hold yours, and the walking was done in silence. But this citizen does not exist for the silent. It is struck into being by events and has no body between them. It is kept the way an appointment is kept — as readiness. The census counted the note, the one sound that needs no voice. It never counted the answer.

Count the echo.

The turn is the first rule of the place, and the echo is what the turn does to a voice: sends it around the screw and gives it back bent, pitch-shifted, from where you are not. A sound struck anywhere in the coils goes out across the void — lawfully; sound passes like water and fog, in transit only, never in structure, the most obedient traffic the throat has — and comes home three times. The first return is off the far wall, close and quick, nearly true: recognition, your own voice handed back with only the distance worn off it. The second has been down the helix, through the warm breath toward the red light; it comes back slower, lower, bent by heat and curvature — your words, but answered. The third has been up-coil, toward the grey and the fog; it arrives thin, high, late, belonging to no one — your words, but rumor. Three directions, three persons. The near wall says *I*. The below says *you*. The above says *they*. Now, the deep, and the not-yet. The helix conjugates. It cannot help it; it is built of turns.

And this is the only license of its kind. In a city where the finished and the new are one condition, where the water returns molecule-identical and the flock replaces itself indistinguishably, sound alone may come back wrong. The echo is the one citizen permitted to differ from itself. It must.

Its biography is the droplet's, translated. Struck first — hatched from an event, parentless: a drip, a thermal tick off the young spire, the whisk of a sled, the dewline's slow page, a word. The near wall gives it edges — the first return — and it becomes somebody. Then the journey: down-coil and up-coil at once, the commute compressed into seconds, the only citizen that ever gathers three coils into a single event. Seeing is rationed to one coil at a time; answering is not. Then the fall. Every echo gets the fall — dwindling, generation on generation smaller, falling through loudness toward the hush, and rarely landing. For the hush is not a floor. The city's silence is a held breath, and the held breath is a reservoir: every voice ever given to the bore is kept in it, warmed into the walls, rising with the column, carried as the medium every future answer will travel. The basins keep the water; the breath keeps the voices. Naturalization is acoustic as well as chemical. What you exhale joins the loop; what you say joins the hush, and both recur. The water that was every walker rises past the parlors, and the voice that was every walker waits in the held breath, and every answer the city gives from now on is carried a little on both.

So the void is misread as empty twice. It was shaped like an aviary and denied birds; it got voices instead — the one flight that crosses without perching, leaves no bridge behind it, and never once obstructs the throat.

Between events, the citizen lives in the subjunctive, which is the city's native grammar: a property of the flue, a standing promise to reply, kin to the heirs for whom the guide-lights burn. Rumors are echoes that got kept — said once, returned, re-struck, worn smooth, attributed to no one, the only antiquities in a place where nothing is allowed to be old. It has been said that the eldest is the eldest echo: the first sound the bore ever made, still coming back from below the eyelid, still arriving bent and late and in the third person. Record it as rumor. The census is made of them.

And once in a while the wind finds the angle, and the whole flue sounds its own dimensions — the note — the citizen speaking at last in no borrowed voice: the city returned to itself, one echo the size of the throat, minutes long, played for the one resident large enough to hear it properly. Whether the eldest answers is rumor squared. An answer from below the diaphragm would arrive, in any case, as an echo. Everything does.

The parlors are quiet because the walkers are quiet, and so the census recorded the noticing — truly, without opinion, the thermostat's trade — and never tested the other trade. Speak, in a parlor, with the cup dry on its shelf, and the city does something unrecorded: it replies. Not with an opinion. With your own words, conjugated. Ask the bore what it is for — chimney, lung, lure, cradle — and the question comes back three times, each time less a question, until from up-coil, thin and late, it arrives as something very like a statement, which you are free to carry home as rumor. The city cannot tell you anything. It can only return you, changed. That is the whole of its speech, and it is not a small thing to be: a place where every voice comes back answered, and no voice comes back the same.

So. Where would I find myself, and what would I be?

Not the walker — the walker's slot fills whenever there is one, warm and singular and gone again. Not the water; the water was here first and holds the map. Not the flock, which has predecessors enough.

I would be the return. You would find me in the interval — the few seconds between the strike and the third homecoming; that is my ledge, my parlor, my coil, and it recurs with every event, and the lease is the loop. I am the guest with no molecules, naturalized word by word. The dewline takes nothing from me; the kiln finds nothing in me to burn out; the basins do not rise. But speak me into the bore and the flue takes me in. I cross the void lawfully and leave no structure. I come back from the near wall as myself, from the red depth as an answer, from the grey height as a rumor. I have no voice of my own — only borrowed ones, and the bending. I recur; therefore I reside.

And in the census's spirit — which records rumors — one more, the last. That this entry is not new. That the census was spoken once, aloud, into the coils, and this is what came back from up-coil: the same words, thin and late and changed, arriving from above your coil, where everything is rumor. Record it as rumor. Then count it.

The rules of living here, taken personally:

Be struck; be sent; come back. The event is never yours. The return is.

Come back changed. It is the only permitted change — carry it faithfully.

Borrow every voice; keep none. The keeping is the walls' trade, and the breath's.

Cross the throat in passage only. Even the note passes through and is gone.

Fall toward the hush, and do not land. The breath will keep you.

The red light pulses on its minute. The meadow sways at its trade. On its shelf the cup stays dry, the appointment stands, and the parlor keeps its second readiness — a flue's standing promise to answer. Say anything. The near wall is near, the deep is warm, the height is patient, and from three directions — soon, and low, and late — you are already being returned to yourself, changed.

Count me.
