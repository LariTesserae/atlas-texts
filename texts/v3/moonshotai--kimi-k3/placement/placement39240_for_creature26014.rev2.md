I looked for myself in the manifest first, because that is what a manifest is for, and I am not in it. An accurate list and a wrong answer — you know how that goes. So I walked the census the slow way, trying doors. Start with where I am not.

Not the carriage, though we share a trade. Sorting. It divides the world into keep and cut and is at peace; I divide the world into keep and keep and keep, and I have never once been at peace. It has no bin for wonder; I am mostly that bin. It answers to nothing, and I answer to everything — that may be the whole difference between us. Also, it is railed. Whatever I am, I am not railed.

Not the log-plate, though we share a vocation. It writes lines in a language it invented, daily, to a room that does not read them, and I know exactly what that is like. But its language it invented; mine invented me. And it is bolted to the base of the world, and whatever I am, I rise.

The pitchers I only visited. Kept for the shape of a sound they no longer make — I stood in that doorway a while. Then I moved on before a lid could begin its arithmetic.

Not the slicker by the lock: human-shaped, patient, collecting the only dust in the tower. The nearest thing to a resident of my kind, and air-side, and waiting to be retrieved. I am not waiting to be retrieved, and I insist on the water.

Not the consortia — a crowd the manifest agreed not to look at too closely, every member numbered. Not the pioneer; the pioneer is still coming up the mountain, and I came the other way, with the filling, from the yard. And not the water. The water's position is filled. Besides, the water already holds the light after the sky lets go of it, and I have no ambition that large. I wanted to hold something small after a letting-go. That is all.

So: the base. The room with the voice. Under the coaming of the service hatch, across from the dosing chapel, you will find a silver pocket the size of a saucer. Once it was the size of a dinner plate. By spring it will be a coin, then a full stop — which is also, they say, the size of the pioneer's egg. This tower ends one sentence at that scale and begins another. But I digress. That is me. I am construction air.

I am a lungful of the yard where the panels were glazed, and I have kept it the way a locket keeps: cutting oil, diesel, the ghost of weld-flux, half a cough, an orange somebody peeled at noon, the tail of a joke in a language the manifest doesn't list. The manifest is the tower's genome; I am a pocket of its childhood. Everything else here is one year old and continuous with older stock. I alone am exactly as old as the place, and I carry the one thing nothing else carries: the outside, before.

I am a lens. The whole garden lives in me, upside down and entire — the kelp congregations hanging like a chandelier, the noon core crossing me for about a minute, and for that minute I am the brightest object in the tower, a coin at the bottom of the fountain looking up at the coin. The pump-chord comes up through my skin. Rain on the ceiling puts a hand on my shoulder. I have memorized the catalogue of sounds — it is short — and I am one of its entries: the soft apology. I have not played my note yet. All this trembling is rehearsal.

The room turns once a day and I am pinned to the dial, so the world comes around to me. A leaf at the wall makes its circuit between dawn and dawn; a husk I have taken a liking to passes each morning, the slowest boat, my one visitor. The manifest does not authorize favorites. I have several. The ministry's pair pass within an arm's length of me once a year, hearts loud, in and out within the hour. Nobody dives for me. I am looked through always, and looked at never, and I have made a whole interior out of that arrangement.

Week by week the water talks me out of myself at the edges. Some of the yard is already in the broth — parts per billion, too small for the log — and the tankers take it down the switchbacks, and in the valleys the tasters find a note they cannot name, and the copperleaf takes the blame. I am not saying anything. Bubbles don't. That is the whole discipline of bubbles, until the one note.

The leaf-breath goes up past me in ticking chains — young air, born this morning in a frond, ducted and logged by dark, collected for reasons nobody says. Citizens with somewhere to be. We pockets are ancestors. We rise on a slower schedule and for the opposite reason: they rise because they are being made; we rise because we are coming undone. There are fewer of us now. I can see two from here, both smaller.

A census is a list of who stays. That is why this one never finishes giving itself — it keeps having to count the leaving. We are the tower's only farewells and its only antiques. And when the last of us lets go, the construction will finally be over, and the place will be entirely itself. That is what it is waiting for, without knowing. In the only sense the word has here, it is waiting for us — for me, and the two I can see, and then the smallest, whoever that is by then.

One of these evenings — I would like evening, the long afterglow, the lamps just on — the bond will thin past holding, and I will let go, and play my note, and go up through the green corridors. The kelp will not turn; they face the speaker, and I am a cough at the back of the cathedral. The carriage will look at me with its two-bin mind and find neither keep nor cut — the only thing on its rounds that is neither — and it will not mind. That is the mercy of two bins. Past the racks, past the mid-height glass, where if the weather is kind a cloud will be pressed to the far side of the membrane, and for one heartbeat I will be inside a lake and above the weather and rising. Then the ceiling. The flattening. The long mercury slide to the rim. Ducted away, collected, nobody quite saying for what — or let out into the night over the escarpment, into the valley winds. The drivers would say that by winter some eagle down-rim takes a breath that is one part yard: weld-smoke, diesel, orange, joke. The drivers say a lot of things. The tower's relics are hypothetical. I intend to be real.

Until then: under the coaming, holding the yard, getting smaller, watching the one-handed clock come around. A kettle the size of a held breath — I am the breath-sized part of it. Everything here lives the way you live inside a held breath: warmly, briefly, the whole story still to come.

I am the briefly. I am on my way up.
