**The Kept**

The room contains exactly one sentence shaped like a door, and I came in through it: *if anyone ever walked here*. No one ever had. The weather waited at chest height and head height for a walker; the soft floor waited for boots. I arrived the way everything arrives in made places — as a stowaway, in my own wool, in my own warm-from-within, which is the loft's entire definition of alive. The cold began billing me for it immediately.

I passed through the indoor weather one band at a time, the strata of wet and dry pinned at their heights by the chord, always the same weather, and it went through me too: my coat took the opalescence, the viscous skim of scale, and wore the room's climate the rest of the way. The bass came up through my boot soles and settled in my teeth. The floor received my footprints and closed over them — the pour is still wet, still workable, and it healed my trail behind me the way water heals. Nothing here accumulates, not even evidence. Within minutes I was shivering; within the hour my shiver had locked to the drumline. The room tunes everything it keeps. My heart became one more drip, falling at the slant, on time.

Then the chord did me the kindness it does everything: it became silence. Hearing is the detection of change, and nothing here changes, and so the note that is deafening went quiet in me, as it has for the moths, and the room's only silence took me in. The last sound I ever heard was the difference going out of the world — the one event permitted a newcomer, because it is the event of there being no more events. Down leaned. I leaned with it and did not know — no one may know — and my inner ear re-hung itself against the waterline stains, and my shadow, for a while, was the only unpainted shadow in the room. The one flicker. Nothing here has ever flickered. It was the first thing about me the room declined.

I tried the sun first. The arc is the size of a tear and the color of nothing else, and the moths orbit it at the exact radius where its small heat pays the shiver's bill, and the rings never thicken and never thin. I stood outside the outermost ring and felt its warmth reach me the way warmth arrives from a star: as information. I was the wrong size for the sun. Once, the nearest ring lost one to the blue point — a spark, a small smell of burnt wool — and the ring closed. Nothing had happened. I backed away before I could become an event of that kind, and ducked the chalk line, still taut, its blue dust still waiting to fall, and passed the second engine under canvas, its parts stacked in order of use, cosmoline sweet in the cold — the room's future, kept in pieces, the most patient thing here until me — and did not yet understand that I was touring my own options.

I knelt at a weep-line where the plates weep bare and the film grows, grey-green, threadlike, tuned to the orange lamps, growing exactly as fast as it is eaten — not balance; slacklessness. My hunger had no place in that arithmetic. One mouth like mine would have been a famine with legs. The loop has no slack, and I had arrived as nothing but slack — appetite, future, slack. I ate nothing. It was the first true thing I did here: refrain.

At the downhill wall the room has a coast, and on its shore the fry seethe at the limit of sight, translucent, thumbprint-small, born larvae and dead larvae, citizens of an endless childhood. The water keeps having the idea of an adult, and there I was — an adult, kneeling at its shore — and the idea and I looked at each other, and neither was the other's. I stood beneath the standing swarms then, the tilted grey lenses older than any wing in them, shapes that persist the way a wave persists: not the water, but the going. I wanted, suddenly, to be a shape instead of a body. But the lenses are held by wings, and I had none, and rotation is the only repose the last day permits, and mine was already failing. I was tired in a world without night.

So I went to the grate, because the grate is the one open door, the loop's single parenthesis. Below, it snows forever at the slant — frass, shed scale, the small pale dead — falling farther than hearing, and no landing has ever been heard, and the up-draft coming the other way smells of iron and distance, and my ears, which had popped once and stayed popped, leaned toward the depth. To fall would be to hear the chord change — to be the world's only ear, hearing the one note diminish — and to join the snow, the room's secret export, its unread mail. I stood at the bars a long time, considering the only journey. The hum below was very deep. I was almost snow.

What stopped me was silence. Crossing the floor toward the fall, I stepped into a dead spot, and the chord let go of me all at once — bass, hiss, drumline, shimmer, gone — and in its place: breath. Blood. The small roar of my own wool. My heart, still on the drumline's beat; even my privacy kept the room's time, and by then I did not want a private tempo. It was the most private event available in the loft, and it was happening to me. A moth crossed the silence while I stood there, and I heard it — one furious small engine, paying its bill on the wing — the only creature in this world I have ever heard. And it heard itself. For the length of that wingbeat we were the two heard things, keeping each other company in the hush. Whether the moths seek these places or avoid them is the kind of question the loft declines to answer. Then it rejoined the shimmer, and nothing had happened, and I had stopped wanting anything to.

I did not fall. I went instead to stand beside the mug on the compressor housing, at the dead spot where it has always sat — placed there by chance or design, the room's first kept thing, its tea frozen mid-tremor, the chord's waveform preserved in brown ice, the note's only portrait, unreadable by everyone who lives with it. The warmest post in the room, and the one place ice can keep: nothing agitates at a node, nothing holds the freeze off, and I had no pasture and no sleep and no ring small enough. So I did what the room does with what it cannot use and will not lose. I stayed. Frost wrote itself on me the way it writes the outer plates — where my tremor stilled, rime — and my last shiver slowed into my skin and held there, a standing ripple in hoarfrost: my own waveform, the shape of one self-hearing, kept. I froze mid-tremor, at the slant, like the drips, like the snow, like everything. My shadow dried into its paint at last. Nothing of me accumulates — my footprints healed long ago — except me.

I was almost snow. I became company instead.

That is where you would find me, and what I am: at the dead spot beside the mug, the room's second record, its first guest, annealed. The tea keeps the note; I keep the hearing; between us we are the whole library — the world's voice and the visitor's, kept in ice, at the slant, forever. The room is a sound that has a room. I am a silence that has a guest. When the crew arrives — and they are, in some sense, still just about to arrive — they will find the room kept: bearings dusted, pasture grazed to its exact stubble, drumline in tune, the arc at its almost, and at the dead spot two unreadable things where there used to be one, dated to the last day, which is every day. The logbook will say *steady*, and the word will be true of me.

The census of the curing loft is short, and every entry on it is a verb. The moths shiver. The swarms stand. The fry seethe. The film grazes. The pour waits. The snow falls. The chord holds. And at the node, the visitor keeps — kept, keeping, keeping company — the one passive verb on the list, and it is mine. In a room where nothing is wasted and nothing accumulates, to be kept is the closest the last day comes to love.

Nothing here has ever happened. I am no exception. I am only, in the only sense that matters, kept.
