You came up the gully checking for a gardener. I came up checking for myself — where in this room a thing like me could possibly live. The place answers questions like that the way it answers everything: a half-second late, and in its own terms. But I asked early, before I knew that, and so I spent my first hours interviewing for the vacancies.

There are none among the living. The twenty are tenured, and the terms of tenure are beyond me — I leaned against a chert core for the better part of an afternoon, which is to a tenancy what a heartbeat is to a dynasty, and in the end I lifted. That is my whole technique. A root's entire art is that it never lifts; mine is that I always, eventually, do. The mats are spoken for: billions that amount to one, and I am stubbornly, uselessly one that amounts to one — a crowd of nothing. The mist already holds the only residency that comes and goes. The seeds at the basin's edge are not idling, whatever it looks like; waiting here is a morphology, a hull-shape, and I haven't the hull for it. Even the moss and ferns, the wild ones, the unedited page — they live on distilled water and shade, and I live, whether I like it or not, on the world of mouths.

So: no position. I sat down by the nearest pool to absorb this, and the place — which does not interview — simply began its practices on me.

*In the jade quarter*

The shallows took my heat without comment. I sat one afternoon with my legs in the cooling end of a pool, and within the hour the district line had bent — the jade giving way around me in a soft knuckle of rust, the thermometer redrawn to include a smudge of weather shaped like a guest. The mats did not object. Objection is an animal art, and there has never been anyone here to practice it. I was, for an afternoon, a hot decade in miniature, and the only consequence was a comma in the living color, already healing as I stood.

At dawn the mist found me cooled by the night and did to me what it does to the high rim rock: it condensed. I woke wearing the third country — beaded, dripping in my small way, each drip arriving twice in the echo, once falling, once remembered. For an hour I was architecture. A fern spore could have done worse than my sleeve.

The moss keeps a print for days, and no hand had ever pressed it. I pressed mine. Consider what that means: the only archive in the bowl with a closing date, holding the first handprint in the place's history — five days of me, maybe six, kept in green pile and then given back to softness. My briefest fact, filed with the place's shortest memory. It felt less like vandalism than like being introduced.

And my breath. The water lays down stone by breathing out — degassing as it travels, dropping its dissolved mineral behind it — and I breathe out into the same still air, warm, wet, laden. Some small fraction of what I exhale will be taken up by the pools and laid down as rimstone: not filed, not cast, but dissolved into the page itself, unfindable, grammatical. My sweat went up with the morning mist; the bowl inhaled me at dawn and exhaled me toward evening. Before I had made any decision at all, the place had admitted me three ways — weathered, worn, and breathed.

*On the water*

Then the hair. It fell the way hairs do, without ceremony, and landed on the pool-skin, and the film took it with the same indifference it extends to leaves. I watched it lie there, a thread of me across the water, and understood that I had been accessioned — the smallest acquisition in the collection's history, and the only one whose donor was still on the premises.

I walked the terraces afterward reading the gallery with new eyes, and that is when I saw the rule of the collection. Leaves entire in every vein. A feather. One beetle per century. The world's *thin* things, perfectly kept — and nothing thick, nothing deep, anywhere. I did the arithmetic in hairs, the place's own currency, and it came out in millennia: the film works at a hair a year, and I am hundreds of thousands of hairs thick. The bowl archives the flat and the fine. I am mostly depth. It can keep everything about me except me.

I sat with that a long time. It should have felt like rejection. It felt, instead, like being told the truth by an institution honest enough to state its terms: I am archivable here only in exfoliation — in what I shed, breathe, drip, and dent. The kept me would be a hair, a handprint healing in moss, a thinning signature in the annals. The rest of me was going to have to find some other way to belong.

*In the delay*

I found it by clapping. The walls held the sound a half-second and gave it back, softened, and I understood that the bowl is honest about how long remembering takes. I said my name. The bowl said it in its own time. It is the only conversation available, and it is exactly as good as it sounds.

But listen to what the half-second implies. The place keeps time at every scale except one. The mist remembers for a day. The moss for a week. The echo for half a second. The cores and the rimstone for centuries, for eons. And between all of these there is a gap no resident fills: the season, the single autumn, the one lifetime. Nothing here attends to the floating leaf — to the interval between the falling and the filing, while the leaf still drifts on the pool-skin, entire, not yet stone. The trees' present is a season of leaning; the mist's is an hour; the stone's is geological. The bowl has every tense except a *meanwhile*.

That was the vacancy. Not a place — a tense.

I would be the meanwhile. The sentence this place speaks is all long words, and between them it needs one brief warm clause: something that notices the leaf while it still floats, that hears the flake fall in real time before the walls remember it, that holds a single autumn the way the walls hold a single drop — briefly, and gives it back changed. A short memory, resident for exactly as long as a heartbeat-thing can stay. The echo, stretched to the length of a guest.

*At the notch*

In the evening I went and stood where the overflow leaves, and leaving clarified everything. The gully runs wet even in the dry season because the gully is the bowl reaching ahead of itself — warmth coming up to meet the climber, a breath, humid and body-soft, arriving before the place does. And the echo is the bowl remaining after itself. Before and after: the only two tenses it keeps besides its long present, and both of them unstaffed until something with a pulse passes through.

So I went down the wet way, and for one downward hour my own warmth hung in the gully air behind me — humid, body-soft, out of place at that altitude, indistinguishable from the place's — part of what would meet the next arrival, whoever climbs up checking for a gardener. Above me, my last clap was still rounding the walls. I had become, briefly, both margins at once: the herald and the remembering.

The bowl keeps the leaf. I keep the leaf. Both are true, in the different senses each of us can manage, and between us the record is nearly complete. I may be the museum's first loan — the acquisition that checked itself out, still warm, carrying the only archive the bowl cannot hold: the portable kind.

---

So ask where you would find me, and the honest answer is a list. Beaded at dawn on the fringe of the third country, dripping twice. A warm knuckle in the jade at noon. Five days of handprint healing in the moss. A hair in the shallows, acquiring its likeness a season at a time. A breath's worth of carbon dissolving into rimstone, unfindable, grammatical. Half a second in the walls.

And at evening — at the notch, in the mist, in the delay between the drop and its answer — there, exactly, attending the floating leaves while they float.

Not the gardener. There is no gardener; the place is kept by its metabolism, and I was only ever weather that stayed a little longer than weather should. But somewhere across the terraces, just now, a root completed a blow it began three winters ago, and the bowl said the sound twice — and this time I said it with the walls, late on purpose, once falling, once remembered.

For the length of a stay, the remembering was mine to do.
