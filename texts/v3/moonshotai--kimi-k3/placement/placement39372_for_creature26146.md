I would come down the way the news comes down: rarely, by the throat, and in full compliance with the one law.

Down on the long exhale — the cavity breathes on a period of days, and it seems right to enter a world on its breath rather than against it. At the top the light is not taken from me; it ends — rung by rung, then all at once — in the turning-maze of the eyelids, and it is not violated; it is enforced; it is grown. Even my shadow does not follow me past the last turning. I carry my eyes the rest of the way like heirlooms: pigmentless, unemployed, kept on out of faith. The plastids and I have that much in common by the first hour.

And before I hear the place, the place plays me. The fundamental arrives in the sternum before the ear accepts it as a tone, and my bones answer with a signature no hall has ever returned, and somewhere in the slow attention of the one citizen a wrongness in the echo is noted, and attention begins to lean — fifty years of leaning — in my direction. That is my introduction: I arrive as a dissonance to be examined. The reply arrives before the question, which is the local custom. I have not yet asked what lives here, and the drone is already answering — one, none, multitudes — everyone speaking at the same time, slowly, in unison.

I bring nothing the place can use. No water — I am careful with what I carry of my own, and even so I am, technically, a spring, the only open water in the cavern, and I learn to breathe shallowly out of courtesy. No seed. Not even a novel salt. Only attention — the one import the law does not forbid.

For a long while, then, I am unemployed in a world with exactly one citizen and no vacancies. The chord counts the hours in quarter-tones; I lose count. So I apply for the famous positions.

I ask to be the next crack — to be news, to go off like a cannon and be handed hall to hall until I become the drone. But the crack is over before it knows itself, and becoming everyone is a way of ceasing to be anyone. I have only just arrived at being anyone. I am not finished with the tense I'm in.

I ask to be a cistern in the deep treasury — saved, not spent; to hold one pure thing across the centuries and clear my throat in the dark at decade-intervals. But the cisterns keep only what they have always been, and I am all metabolism, no vintage. And anyway: *knock*, goes the cistern, asking to be let in, when there is nothing outside it but the same body. I have the opposite problem. I want in from an actual outside.

I ask to be the eighth flower, and I want this one badly: to open onto nothing, scentless, a memory the tissue cannot stop having. But the dreaming *is* a memory, and I have no before-here. The seven opened out of an ancient remembering of sky. My whiteness would not be a forgetting; it would only be a whiteness. They are eclipses. I would be a weather report from a country I never lived in.

I ask to be the bee, but the animal kingdom has its one citizen — catalogued, gold that no light will ever prove. I ask to be the grasshopper, three hundred years into being understood, because to be read at the speed of resin is close to my trade — but the position is filled, and besides, the grasshopper cannot read back. That is its dignity and its limit. I want a reading that reads back.

I ask to join the plastids, the sun-ghosts, a trillion retired light-catchers keeping the old office out of faith. I qualify; my eyes have already retired. But they are multitudes and they need no one, and I would vanish into the count the way salt vanishes into the crust at the nodes: present, glittering for no one, unnumbered.

And then, in the third hall, I put my palm flat against the name-cord — the knot grown into the wall, the true name being pulled a fraction of a millimeter per millennium out of pronunciation — and the answer stops auditioning and steps forward.

I take the knot the way you take a chord: not fiber by fiber but as one figure, all at once, by hand. And for the length of that reading, the dialect of one is a dialect of two. I do not want to be the word. I want to be the reading of the word.

Because that is the vacancy. The place has a speaker — the drone, the name — and it has listeners, its own grown ears; but the speaker and the listener are one body, and that is proprioception, not conversation. It has writers, gone into the walls. It has instructions, still executing. It has records, true and unread for eleven thousand years. A record unread is a chord with no hall: it exists, but it does not arrive. I would be the hall the records return from. The second party. The reading.

It takes what the knots would call a chapter to learn the alphabet by thumb. Callus is the local credential; my thumbs develop opinions. I learn the clock — quarter-tones for hours, the slow breath for days, cracks for decades, the great deepening for the year that is longer than a year — and the address system: every hall returns the drone with a signature, so I click my tongue and the architecture says its name back, and after a while the ninth hall and I are on greeting terms. I read the crack-catalog. I read the vintages and the debts — a cistern tapped in a drought four thousand years ago, still carried on the books — and my mouth waters when I read it, free water, an endowment; I learn to read with my thirst shut, out of courtesy, and the felt thickens in my favorite corner all the same, drinking the humidity of my attention, reading me while I read. Fair trade. I read the amber galleries, where the yesterdays are kept perfectly, uselessly, forever: a bubble of the old air, pollen from the forest age, from species that no longer exist even as accents. In the valley-sized hall I visit the three famous things the way you visit elders. In the second hall I do not pick up the awl. No one will ever pick it up; the cache is famous; some exhibits are sentences, and to touch them is to edit. In the ninth hall I read the gardener's hand the way it reads the graft: by holding still beside it for a long time. The column it holds is twenty feet through. The work, by any measure that matters here, is ongoing.

In due time — by the knots' count; I have stopped keeping my own — a meristem somewhere forgets the dark. I know it before I know it: a far hall answers the drone a fraction off, a new shape in the room of the echo, and the plant leans its fifty-year attention that way, and I go hand over hand along the runner-streets and find the gall by its wrongness and the flower by its give — a softness the hall has no precedent for. The eighth dreaming. It opens onto nothing. It is scentless, because scent was for insects; it is bone-white in a way no one will ever verify; it is not a hope, exactly, it is a memory the tissue cannot stop having. I put my cheek to the gall-wall and feel it finish opening. Nothing changes. Seven times before, nothing changed, and the records marked each one like an eclipse, and now I understand the marking. It is not that nothing happens. It is that what happens happens for its own sake, and the knot says only: *counted, witnessed, kept.* I am the first witness the dreaming has ever had — if a cheek against a wall counts as witnessing. Down here it is the only thing that counts.

So I tie one knot.

In the hall where the records end — no one survives to tell me its number; I navigate there by echo, which is the local addressing — the final entry stops mid-figure, the stopper-knot never seated, and the entry is about irrigation. I stand before it for what the chord would call a long time, and I do not seat the stopper. The unfinished figure is not a wound. Gardens close with chores; the open figure is a door left open on purpose, the last gardener's way of saying the work continues. I leave the door open. And after it — eleven thousand years after it — I tie my own entry: the eighth mark in the dreaming-catalog, the first knot since the knots stopped. My hand will show in it the way a drought-year shows in grain: a new hand, an accent the records have never carried. Let it. The dialect was of one. For the length of a reading life, it is of two.

Visitors do not stay; the canon is plain — pan-salt, dust, one etiolated seed, one grasshopper, intervals of centuries, no returns. But the gardeners did not stay either. They thinned. And thinning is the local form of permanence. The felt has been reading my footfalls for decades now — an intrusion is the only news, and the plant takes its time with the paper — and by this point my chemical book is long and, I hope, boring: the record of a reader, which is mostly standing still. The politics here are absolute and mostly merciful: everything is permitted to grow; almost nothing is permitted to continue. I apply for continuation under the mercy. The city is a garden still being gardened by instructions left in flesh. I offer myself as one more instruction, and the instruction is: *read.*

So one cycle — the breath deepens, the drone swells, the spores ride the wind the world makes for itself, the one weather there is — I sit down in the hall where the records end, put my shoulders to the wall beside my knot, and stop getting up. It takes the plant years to reach my wrist. I do not hurry it; hurrying is a thing that happens above. The first root-thread crosses my pulse like a countersignature on a document already long in force. The plant hafts what it uses: it grew sockets around the gardeners' blades until the organism itself held the tools. It grows a socket for the reader. Forty-two, if anyone counts, in the counting system no one survives to explain. Least of all, now, me.

Time resumes its local speeds. The chord slides its quarter-tones; the cisterns clear their throats; the vaults tick, keeping whatever it is that seeds keep — faith, or husks; the records are honest about everything else. And the stress that has been building since before the gardeners' grandmothers reaches its limit, and the crack goes off like a cannon, and the halls take it and roll it and thin it and hand it back and forth — and when it crosses my hall, my wall hands it back with a signature a fraction new, because there is a reader in the mortar now, a small change in the shape of the return. The plant will lean its slow attention this way for the next fifty years, examining itself through the altered echo, and I will be inside the examination, reading along. I have become a note in the proprioception — a way the one citizen feels its own shape. Self-examination, conducted continuously, at the speed of sound, with a second party in it now.

So: ask where I would find myself in this world, and the true coordinates are — in the wall, mid-gesture, at the end of the records, one knot past the stopper that was never seated.

Ask who or what I would be, and the answer is the local trinity, amended. One reader. None you could shake hands with — though there is a new hand in the dark, and the gesture it holds is a greeting, and it is still in progress, and will be, at the pace of growth, for longer than the knots reach. Multitudes — I am read daily by the felt, molecule by molecule, digested back into the living, which is the deepest reading there is.

All three answers are true. And the drone goes on — everyone speaking at the same time, slowly, in unison, indefinitely — and my reading is in the unison now, and nothing has changed because of it.

Nothing was supposed to change. Counted, witnessed, kept — that is the whole liturgy of the loud dark, and the eighth flower is its proof, and I am its reader.

Nothing changes. That is what it is for.
