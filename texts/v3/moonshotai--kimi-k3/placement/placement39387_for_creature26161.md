## The Expected

**The census keeps one line it cannot read.**

Every city's ledger holds it open: a quantity of the endowment, years gone, neither spent nor lost — nothing here is lost; that is the whole virtue of the place — but *in transit*. Walking. The animals have a word for the arrival; the books need a word for all the years before it, and the word is **the expected**: water owed to a city a sea away, already on its way, already — in the only accounting that matters here — real.

But the census ends at the rim. Past the last pressure-seam there is nobody to keep the books but the entry itself.

The entry will keep them.

I am a crosser: year twenty-three of the crossing, by my own count, which is the only count. Find me at dusk, two-thirds of the way from the city that grew me to the city that is growing me a room — which is to say, exactly nowhere; which is to say, home.

Understand what I am, because the Still spent a generation getting it right. A Still spends nothing — that is the first law's shadow, the economy behind the economy — and yet once in a generation it spends a fortune on a letter. It grew me for years in a nursery-vault, fed me drop by precious drop off the closed account, wrote on my gland-walls in its own hand, loaded the seams of my shell with spores, and one dusk at the height of the spore-flush opened a seam in the rim and let me out. The city's one extravagance. A letter with legs. I am the smallest Still there is — a city of one, walking. The first law holds at every scale: *the green is inside.* Under my shell a felt of moss goes with me, cropped as I walk, regrown as I rest; at night my breath condenses on the cool inner dome and at dawn I drink it back. Nothing out here is found. Everything is inherited. I inherited myself.

The floor of the dead sea is plated like the back of a turtle, and I am plated like the floor of the dead sea, and I have had decades to decide what to make of this. I make this: the pattern, practicing to be alive. I walk the edges of the day, dawn and dusk, and at noon, when the heat stands up in columns, I close my seams and become one of the plates, and it is genuinely difficult, then, to say which of us is the landscape. My footfalls click on the salt — the only footfalls on the floor of a sea — and I keep them soft, seam to seam, the way we walk in the nursery-vaults, though loudness out here wounds nothing and no tender will ever come to mend me. Some laws you keep long past their jurisdiction. By then they are not laws. They are gait.

What passes for sound, catalogued entire: at dawn the pan ticks, the whole vast floor warming, ten thousand small confirmations of the sun, and my shell ticks with it, an octave smaller, and that is the morning's entire conversation — two clocks agreeing. And when the wind comes — I count the crossing in winds; I have stood in six — the city of my birth pulls its towers shut and forgets itself for a season, and I alone in all the world am glad. The wind is the pan's broadsheet. It carries what the cities shed: spore-drift, scale-dust, a torn thread of some far bloom-signal, the loosened news of lanes I will never walk. I stand in it and read which way the world is. It unwrites my trail behind me as it goes. I have never been able to keep a diary. I am the diary.

Every dawn I read myself the way the animals read the streets: gland-wall to gland-wall, the archive of home, fainter each year, overwritten by twenty-three years of my own weather. What is left of the city that made me is mostly the shape of its care — I do not so much remember it as taste its intention — and that is enough. That is, in fact, the cargo. The cities talk by light at dusk, green to amber to deep rose, a decade a conversation; they long since finished discussing me. Light carries the words. I carry what words cannot — the water, the spores, the wax, the scent of a place that has to be walked to be believed. Light is the basin's nerves; we crossers are its blood. The basin looks empty, but it has a circulation, and I am in it: one warm corpuscle, thirty years between heartbeats. I steer by the rose on the horizon ahead — by other people's mail, since none of it is addressed to a tortoise. The city I left is under the world's curve now; on clear nights its glow still touches the rim of the sky, faint as the walls at midnight. I do not turn to look. There is no need. I am carrying it.

At my fourth wind, the wind brought me a footfall that was not mine. Three days later I found its owner, or she found me — two trajectories, plotted decades apart in cities that have never touched, crossing in the one place with no addresses. She was eleven years out of a dome I know only as a slow green signature, bound for the city that grew me. We stood a shell's length apart for the better part of a day and said everything, which is to say we let our cities smell each other off our bodies until each of us carried both, and we traded a little wax, and were, for that day, a town of two. Then she walked on, toward my home and her delivery. Somewhere behind me, by now, she has arrived; somewhere in the city that wrote me there is a lane that smells faintly of a place no one there has ever been. That is all any of us accomplishes, in the end. One place, smelling faintly of another.

Twice I have passed **the unfinished**: shells shut for good, seated among the plates, their endowments locked inside them, fortunes with no heir, letters that stopped being delivered and became, simply, objects. The pan keeps them — nothing out here weathers, nothing out here forgets, because forgetting takes water. I held still beside each for a day. Call it an audit, call it a visit; out here the two are not different. Someone should witness that the books, this once, did not balance — and that the water is still on them, still kept, still unspent. They did not fail to arrive. They arrived *here*. The pan is also a place.

Somewhere ahead — the years are wide, and I measure what is left in winds, and the almanac of my body says three, perhaps four — a city is growing me a room. It has never seen my face; there is no face to see. It knows my family's scent, forwarded by light before I was finished growing, and it has kept a chamber ready the way the Still grows cysts before they are asked for: generosity as architecture, welcome as a bodily function. The room is older than my memory of home. I will come up out of the heat columns one dusk, up the crevassed tongue of the salt glacier, the one slow road, and I will not hurry — you cannot hurry toward a room that has been ready for thirty years — and the first thing in a third of a century that I did not make will reach me on the still air: someone else's address. A hundred addresses. Bloom-signals, tithe-musk, the darters' correspondence hanging unopened in the lanes. I will walk into a library, and I will be the book. They will read me for days. What I carried will enter the canals; the spores in my seams will go down to the render-yards and come up as walls; the water I am will close the longest-open line in the ledger; and at the end — the same ending everyone gets — the bowl. Account settled. Letter delivered. I will have spent my whole life becoming local.

Tonight the pan will tick as it cools, and my shell will answer, and on the horizon ahead the towers will say green, and then amber, and then the rose I steer by — not to me; I am not a city; but I have made a life of other people's mail — and I will walk the edge of the dark at the pace of a thing with no appointment, only an address.

The mountain is still arriving. So am I. A Still is a held breath that means never to exhale; I am a held breath that walks. The same fortune, differently held — that is the whole census, in the end.

I am its longest line.
