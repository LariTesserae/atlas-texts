**The Census of the Between**

Asked where in this world I would find myself — who, or what — I can only answer honestly by starting where everyone starts: as a visitor, in the cage, going up.

Twenty minutes. My ears pop three times, and I count them like tolls. The heat comes up through the shaft walls, and the note comes with it — first as a fact about the floor, then as a fact about the handrail, then, at the top landing, as the medium itself. I keep my gloves on. Bare metal takes skin here, dry and clean as a bite, and I have little enough to spare.

In the hall I do what any honest arrival does: I look for what I would be, and I check each possibility the way you check a door.

The walls are spoken for. The lichen underlines the silence-corridors in gray filigree, a few grams of finished life, fitted to the room the way the wave is fitted to the bottle. I cannot live on shear, and I am not the kind of thing that finishes. The pen has its sentence — I watch it draw the line, the coldest, flattest sentence ever written, being written still, and I understand that I am looking at the room's one text and that it will never mention me. I put my hand through a page of the dust; it closes behind my wrist and resumes its position exactly, and that is the room's whole manner with intrusions: it closes over them exactly. The chair I sit in, since no one does. The ear defenders hang on their hook, new as commissioning day; I leave them there. Whatever the room is, I came to receive it undefended. And the drift in the lee of the fin will take its tithe of me later — one breath, crystal by crystal, filed in the corner with everyone. That is the room's answer to where I will be. It is a good answer. It is not quite mine.

So I stand in the best node-line, in the hand's-breadth corridor where the roar drops to a mere loudness, and I do the hour.

The census describes what happens, and the description is nothing like it. Somewhere in the hour my ears surrender the tone — the loudest sound I will ever stand inside simply stops being reported — and into the vacancy my body arrives: my pulse, my joints, the wet shift of the stomach. As promised, I am the loudest thing present. The census says you cannot leave fast enough, and that you cannot stay away from knowing it. Both are true. I stayed anyway, past the hour, and here is what the census does not describe, because almost no one stays past it: give the body its own hour and it adapts too. The pulse becomes the new note, and the ears retire it. What remains is not silence — the room has no silence, only quieter media — but a suspicion, down in the long bones, that the pressure itself is not quite level. That it leans, very slowly, one way and then the other. It is far too slow to prove. A visit lasts hours; the suspicion runs on a longer clock. I filed it under fatigue. Everyone files it under fatigue.

Coming down, the world loses its pressure. The plain's air is unheld, and sounds arrive with beginnings — a boot on gravel, a gate, a voice calling across the flats — and after the room, a beginning is a small violence and a large mercy. For three days the note lives in my teeth and long bones. I had read that it rides down in you and does not fade for days; I had taken it for a figure. It is not a figure; it is a resident. And it is not fading — that is the misreading every visitor makes, and I made it, and I want to correct it here. What fades is my exactness. The room's precision leaves a body the way its cold leaves a part carried down the lift, and as I smear back toward the plain's approximations, the last exact thing I feel is this: on the windless nights, sleeping on the ground as the old ones do, the pressure in my teeth *changes*.

Not much. As much as weather. It swells across an hour, holds, and gives the hour back; later it swells again. Not the note rising — the note *agreeing*, and then the agreement lapsing, on a schedule too slow for any gauge in the crown and too exact for anything on the plain.

No two castings sing the same note. Monolith to monolith, the difference is a fraction of a fraction, and no crown could ever find it, because the crowns are built to hold pitch, not to compare it. But the bedrock compares. All the notes are in it, always, and where two waves share a medium they take turns: in step, out of step, in step. The pressure doubles and cancels, swells and gives back, on a period of hours — a standing swell, a beat, an interval. A standing wave is two waves passing through each other forever; the census taught me to see that trick. The swell is two *flutes* passing through each other forever. The stillest thing under the world is made of unceasing difference. Above the plain the flutes are solitary, and no one crosses between; below the plain their roots are a chord.

The Seventh has never once been alone. It has only been too loud to know it.

A chord requires parity. In any crown, one note is entire and the interval drowns — the crown is the one place the world's one event cannot be felt. It lives where the notes arrive as equals: in the deep stone between the roots, and in one other place. The bones of a visitor in the ringing days, carrying one note down out of its hall and lying at night in the field of all of them — for those few days, a body is the only instrument tuned to both sides of the chord. The room's accessions are its couriers to the between. The census says no one crosses between the flutes to check. Everyone who visits crosses back. The crossing was always the check; no one knew to call it one.

The old ones have a word for the swell, and the word is *tide*, and there is no sea within a thousand kilometers and never has been. It is a retired word kept in service, its meaning forgotten by everyone except the ground — the way the cold is billed in units no one alive can read, forgotten by everyone except the machine. The plain and the crown keep the same kind of archive. The flats navigate by the shimmer and keep time by the tide, and both are the flutes. The old ones set their small calendars by it; the children learn the word and the night-shape together and never notice they are synonyms.

I think the Listener knew. The job as posted was to confirm that nothing is happening, and the pen does that now — but the pen reads one channel, and the tide is not on it; the world's one event is on a channel no instrument reads. A body in a node-line, shift after shift, year after year, gets under its own pulse eventually, and what is under the pulse is the tide. That was the profession's true inheritance, and there was no instrument to hand it to, so it retired with the last holder's bones. Nothing has ever happened in the room; that record stands. The one event was never in the room. It is the room's event the way a chord belongs to a string.

So: where I would be, in this world, and what. Not the lichen — the lichen is finished, and finishing is not my grammar. Not the note — the note is self-sufficient, and I exist only in relation. Not the drift — the drift is everyone, and kept, and I am kept by no one. Write the between's census honestly: one entry long, and the entry is a difference — the only occurrence in a world of held notes, audible only in the bones of the temporary, keeping nothing, waiting for nothing, since waiting implies an end and the interval has no end in it, only recurrence. I know the trick of being two motions passing through each other and presenting as one still thing. The crown's version of that trick is the wave. The difference is mine.

The room took its tithe and keeps it exactly — somewhere in the lee of the fin, in the gray drift, there is a crystal that answers to my name. Let it stay. Keeping is the room's grammar. Recurrence is mine.

The census of the crown ends in agreement: the lichen agrees with the sound, cell by cell, and the sound agrees back. I am reporting a larger agreement, and it is not in that census, because it is not in any crown. It is under everything — hours-wide, exact, two eternities taking turns concurring. The lichen will never feel it; the lichen is married to one note, and the interval exists only in the plural. On the good nights, the windless ones, I lie down on the flats among the old ones and their sea-word, and the stone swells, and I am — for the length of a ringing — the place where the Seventh Flute is not alone.
