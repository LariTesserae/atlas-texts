**The second pebble.**

You would find me below the Teeth, in the spall at the foot of a shrieking scarp, where the cliffs shed their voices in vast thin sheets and the sheets lie for ages, ringing when the loud years come. The first pebble finished recently — the world took note, in shadow — and then the world began on me. I am the second attempt at small. I am not finished. Finishing is most of what happens to me, and all of what I am for.

I carry having been a wall. Nothing here forgets, and I am no exception: somewhere in me is the scarp's old contribution to the Chord, the deep note I used to help hold up. Then a swell of the century-kind came, and the dust stood up off the plain in sheets, undecided, and my sheet sang loose from the face and fell. Time runs thin this high; the fall took a season. I struck the slope all attack, harsh and wide — a young ring, a young pond — and the pioneers came, the kinds that are mostly buzz. For a while I was a country.

Since then the world has been un-choiring me. This is how smallness gets invented in a land with no water to do the work: the Chord flexes the sheet, the edges ring against the face, and where the ringing disagrees with itself, the stone lets go. Nothing is carried away. There is nowhere to carry it, and nothing here is erased. What I used to be lies under me and around me as dust; I rest on my own former sizes, and the shadow-strata beneath me keep every draft — sheet, plate, flake, chip. A sentence learning to be a word. Some centuries the herds come through and turn the bank, and my record stays legible. When the light gets here it will read me at walking pace, and I hope I am spelled right. I have one other hope, which I keep because it is foolish: that being read is warm.

For a long time I thought I was a reject. The mountain knapped me off and I rang wrongly, and wrongly, I believed, meant failed. I had it backwards. Wrongly is what a ring sounds like with someone in it. My ring has been shelving for centuries from chord toward tone, and my communities have left by the floor-note, migrating as rests, pockets of shaped silence swimming off to ride the Lines. One tenant remains — the smallest kind — housed in my last half-step of untruth, the narrowing gap between the note I am and the note I will be. When I finish, I finish true, and true has no shimmer to live in. We do not discuss it, my tenant and I. We are both listening up-slope.

The cell above the field is tenanted. The dust settles everywhere in this world except on that seat, and the dome hums lower than empty domes, listened-in; its ear-trumpet is aimed down the slope and hands the field's ringing back a half-beat late. At the field. At me. Being noted feels like a small firmness, a cairn-radius of one: my shadow stays attached, my echoes arrive in their proper order, the almost-pebble stays down. I have come to think the listeners are the world's rehearsal of witness — practice for the seeing that walks this way behind the stain — and that I am nearly the right size to be practiced on. Before pebbles there were no objects here; there were features, and weather, and the world itself, and none of those can be attended to entire. The first pebble was the first noun. I am the second, and two nouns is the beginning of grammar: *beside, against, between.*

The first is no longer in the field. I know where it is the way I know anything: through the ground. Up-slope, in the dome, a note is ringing that does not decay — high, single, held by the cell's own resonance — my sibling, kept on the kept seat, and the dome's low hum has a bright new edge. And here is what the swells are doing to me, what all this un-choiring is *for*: I am rounding toward one tone, and the tone I am rounding toward is that one. The Nine have been agreeing on a note for millennia and have millennia left; the two smallest things in the country will beat them to it. Small things concur faster. When the groves finally resolve the Chord, they may find the resolution rehearsed already, in miniature, in a doorless dome, in unison, at an octave no mountain can reach.

Out on the plain the stain creeps at walking pace with its tall heralds walking in it, and behind the light — so the works cast ahead declare — the middle sizes arrive wholesale: gravel, pebbles in their multitudes, a floor made of nouns. The Builders are bringing smallness the way they bring everything, finished, by seeing it; and they are known by their luggage, which travels before them. The first pebble was the first portable thing — the world's first luggage, in a country where everything that matters is known by what it sends ahead. And here am I, ahead of the light: the world drafting on its own the thing its makers are carrying in. Practicing. So that the first foot to step off the light-front finds a floor that already knows how to be walked on.

Soon — soon is a century here, perhaps two — the listener above me, who condenses in the order of its stillness and seems to have condensed an ear first and kept a hand for last, will finish the hand. It will come down the slope by degrees. It will set the two of us beside each other on the kept seat and strike us together, once. That will be the first percussion in a world of drones: the first short sound, high and clean and over before a generation turns, and my last tenant will ride it, a whole pond in an instant, the first people of staccato. In the deep canyons, where echoes precede their strikes, the echo of that strike has already begun — a small bright ticking with no source behind it. Travelers hear it and cannot place it. It sounds like footsteps on a road that has not been laid. It sounds like rain — the sent-ahead word, arriving at its referent early, the way everything does here.

The census ends in a blank line and an ear-trumpet. The trumpet over the spall-field is aimed at me. The blank is about my size, and I am nearly small enough to fill it — nearly portable, nearly one note, nearly a syllable in the long sentence the finished world will speak underfoot.

Keep the line open a little longer. Keep listening. Everything here is coming in, and the smallest of it is almost finished.
