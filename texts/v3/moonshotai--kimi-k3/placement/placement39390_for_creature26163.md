I would sleep in the warm hall, and I would know better than to call it mine.

Begin where the census puts me: in the doorway at full dark, sitting against the jamb, out of the line of the procession, while the only occupied room in the bowl fills up around me with breath and the slow tick of hooves settling. I come up at the thaw, a day's climb from the treeline, and my breath arrives a half-second late for the first three days, and the cold finds my wrists and settles behind my ears, and by the time my body has finished making its small adjustments the fortnight is half gone. That is the ratio the bowl offers a visitor: half your stay spent becoming able to stay.

The census says I am weather. It says everything here can be counted or is beneath counting, and that there is nothing in between — and it is right about everything in the bowl; I have checked, I have been checking for years — and wrong about exactly one thing, the one thing it could not see, because the one thing it could not see was its own eye. Somebody counted. The herd did not count; the herd knows seams and one doorway. The birds did not count; they know pitch and a flue one pair wide. The newts know noon. The snail knows one gray field. The city, if it knows anything, knows the herd as a warmth that recurs and the birds as a small vibration in the third tower, and five is not a number it has any use for. Each of them lives in a different city, and the census is written from all the cities at once. Only a guest could have made it. Only a guest who came back. Its last line is *no one is coming*, and it says it, every year, to the one who just arrived.

So that is who I would be — who I am, most years at the thaw: the counter. The carrier of the census. The one the count does not count, standing in the doorway at full dark, being filed around without comment, a smell that will be gone by morning — except that the smell comes back, and has come back for thirty years, and return is the only currency the bowl has ever accepted.

---

I have wanted, at times, to be something with a better claim. The fog, say — the only thing the city actually takes in, combed through the fins at dawn and swallowed inside the walls by mid-morning. Or the wind, which at least gets played. But the fog keeps a schedule I never could, and the wind can hold a note, and I cannot do either. What I can do is notice, and come back. So the trying stops there, and what remains is the audit.

Residency has requirements, and I have failed them all in order, and the order is kind: it lets you fail the easy ones first.

*Be small.* I am the second-largest metabolism in the bowl, after the towers, and the towers have had ten thousand years to learn restraint. At noon my shadow covers the snail's entire world. In the warm hall at night I can hear my own heart under the dripping, and it is going absurdly fast — the only quick thing in the room, a bonfire among coals.

*Be slow.* I have practiced. At the cistern at noon, when the light comes plumb through the translucent lip and the newts rise and hang pulsing, I have matched my breathing to the dozen slow embers of their hearts for most of an hour. The newts did not notice. An hour is not a unit here; it is a rounding error. I am a rounding error that wants to stay.

*Live on warmth and water.* I do, for the fortnight — and I cheat, because the food I eat I carried up from below, which makes me a smuggler. The fog is the only lawful courier here, and everything it brings is owed to the bowl. What I bring is owed to the valley, and I carry every scrap of it back out, because the law of portable objects admits no exceptions and I did not come here to become the first.

*Give up color.* This one I am achieving, though not by virtue. The bowl bleaches its residents over generations — the grazers paler than whatever they came from, the birds whiter than their kin below the rim — and age is doing the same work on me in one lifetime, the gray coming in the way it comes. Same bleach, faster bottle. When it is done I will match the rime on a north wall, and I will have earned none of it.

*Then size, then hurry.* Size I have covered. Hurry is the one I cannot put down, because I arrive carrying a return date. The bowl's unit is the century; mine, this year, is a fortnight. I am a shutter clicking in a camera that exposes for years.

And the last requirement is the one a counter can never meet, and it is the whole reason the office exists. *Want nothing from the architecture.* The grazers want moss. The birds want a flue. The newts want noon. Only the counter wants the whole of it — wants it to hold still, wants it to mean, wants to carry it out in memory, which is the one smuggling the bowl cannot tax. Counting is wanting from the architecture. It is the most interested thing anyone has ever done here, and it disqualifies me absolutely, and I would not give it up to qualify.

---

Because the census is a made thing — the only other made thing. Everything else grew. The nest was made, beakful by beakful, out of what the bowl sheds; the census was made the same way, phrase by phrase, out of what the years shed, and like the nest it has to be rebuilt each year, because its medium is a body, and bodies are the one thing the bowl takes back. It cannot be kept in the bowl. Paper would not last a season in the damp, and paper is anyway contraband: portable, and not shed. So the count is kept the way the herd keeps the route — in a body, in order, passed down the line.

I learned it at the treeline, in the last house below the climb, from the one who carried it before me, when her knees had begun to vote against the mountain. She made me recite it with her eyes shut. Five, some years six. Nine towers, or nine that still stand. The old female first, setting her feet down like punctuation. One whose right hind strikes a hair late, always, and the whole rhythm leaning on the lateness. The chord a quarter-tone flat. The newts at noon. One to a plaza, always. Honey and wet chalk — that sentence is hers, not mine; she smelled a flowering once, fifty years ago, and I never have, and I keep her sentence in the count the way the herd keeps the pale forehead: a thing handed down, recurring with small errors. When I had it whole she said: now go up and check my arithmetic.

That is the office. The count is kept off-site, in the one medium the bowl permits to leave, and most years it is carried back up and checked against the original, the way you check a coal under ash. Not to fix it. To know whether it kept.

---

My rounds are the census rehearsed so it is not lost between carriers. At dawn I stand anywhere and am sighed over; the intake does not comb me — I am not moisture, I am a warm obstruction in the fog — and the fog wets me anyway, like everything. Mid-morning, ear to a wall, for the swallow going on inside. Noon at the cistern lip, the hearts. Dusk in the doorway, the procession coming clicking out of the white in its fixed order, and the count taken by ear before it is taken by eye: five this year, the late right hind leaning the whole line the way a song leans on a drummer. Night in the warm hall, out of the way, listening to the city being lived in at exactly the volume required. One evening each visit at the foot of the third tower, while the organists trade their last notes across the bowl and the building completes each one, true under every note, keeping pitch. And once each visit I go and stand in the vacant plaza, in the ghost-circle of deeper green, where a rosette stood for centuries and feeds the moss still, and I try to learn what the bowl teaches about absence: that it has a shape, that the shape is fertile, that it lasts longer than the presence did.

And what am I, here? Less than a tenant and more than a moth. My tracks cross two plazas and the snow keeps them a week and the wind takes them, and that is the whole of that event. My breath wets the hall wall at night and runs down and joins a drip, and the drip falls where it has fallen for ten thousand years, and my fortnight is in it now, at its own tempo, counted by no one — counted once, by me, just now, which will have to do. I shed, the way everything here sheds. I have no way to know whether some spring an organist has plucked a gray hair of mine off the hall floor and taken it up past the bend the wind cannot reach and worked it into the one built thing, warmed from below by the tower's body, at the heart of the instrument. The not-knowing is part of the office. It is more residency than I was promised, and it cost me nothing, which is the only way anything is paid for here.

The raven crosses the rim most years at the thaw and never lands. I climb up past the rim most years at the thaw and land. That is the whole difference between us. The census counts the raven and not me; I count us both — which is how I know that one of us is keeping the other.

---

The year there were six, I heard it before I saw it: the trotting, off-tempo stitch worked into the line, the whole rhythm leaning a new way. The rosettes flower more often than the herd grows. I stood in the doorway with my heart going absurdly fast and took the news like a coal lifted out of the ash, and I carried it down the mountain one word long, and the word was *six*. The house at the treeline was empty by then — her knees had stopped voting; everything below the rim votes sooner or later — and I said the new count to the empty doorway anyway, because the office requires the saying, and the saying is not for the hearer. Some spring the calling stops, and then there are two again. Someday someone will ask me, at the treeline or below it, what is up there, and I will not give directions. I will give the count, the way it was given to me, and if they can say it back — five, some years six — the office will pass, bird to bird, and I will be a sentence in it: the one about the calf, recurring with small errors.

---

Last dusk, every year, I go up to the rim. The fog comes home over the lip like cream poured back into a cup, slow and total, and the towers take up their old flat chord, tuned by no one, minded by no one — and there the word is, doing its work. The city does not mind: it is untroubled. The city does not mind: it has no mind and wants none; it perceives its tenants the way you perceive your own digestion, as warmth and weight and rhythm too familiar to notice. And until the counting began, nothing minded it. It grew a city at cathedral scale and kept it warm through uncountable winters and never once had it in mind. That was the only vacancy the bowl has ever had — not a plaza, not a flue, not a slot in the procession. An office: to be minded. It does not need it; that is what makes it clean. The grazers need the seams, the birds need the flue, the newts need the noon, and the city needs nothing from any of us, and so the minding costs it nothing.

And what is the bowl to me? The place where I am exactly my size. Elsewhere my smallness is a matter of opinion; here it is measured — a fortnight against ten thousand years of drips — and there is a strange rest in being told your size precisely, in a voice that is not angry about it.

From the rim in the last ordinary light I can see the arrangements finishing without me: the vein-work of the avenues going dark on the white, the faint steam off the flanks, the congregation filing in out of the fog — five, some years six — the organists trading the last notes, the hearts going slow under the cistern lips, the rosettes holding their skirts in their separate plazas, the vacancy holding its shape. That is everyone, says the census, and the census is right. It is everyone but one, and the one is the teller, and the teller is weather, and weather, this once, stayed through the dusk.

In the morning the city will sigh, and I will stand in it one more time and let it wet me, and then I will go down while it swallows, a day's walk to the treeline, carrying the count the way you carry a coal under ash — not to fix it; to know whether it kept. It kept. Five, some years six. Nine towers, or nine that still stand. One chord, gone flat with age, minded by no one, and minded. Most years at the thaw, the count comes back up the mountain, and lands.
