The bell at the rim pass rang an hour before I came over it. I know this because of the Ledger, where it is written in the one hand, in the column that is not weather: *bell, pass, one hour early. Visitor, one.* The keepers read it at breakfast and sent no one up to meet me. There was no hurry. Whatever the bell had announced was already walking down into it.

That was the valley's first lesson, before the rules, before the tea that tastes of nothing: here, the news and the thing it announces arrive in the wrong order, and nobody apologizes. I have lived here long enough now to know the bell was not early. I was late. I had been expected for some time.

They gave me the six rules the way you'd tell a guest where the privy is. I stayed the week. I slept on the warm vein by the third night and stopped jumping at the ground-breath by the fifth, and then — the valley's real threshold — I noticed I had stopped noticing. My ears went the way keeper ears go: voices blurring, harmonics sharpening. I could hear which stack was thinking about ice before I could hear Awn ask me to pass the salt from the tin that is not empty and has never been empty.

Then the week ended and I did not leave, and the valley was faced with the question of me.

The keepers never asked what I was. Asking finishes things. They noted, and let the noting accrete, the way dew accretes in the Line. I have read the notes since. They disagree in all the usual places, which is how I know they're accurate.

By the second spring Bren mentioned it the way you'd mention rain: nothing of mine had ever been started. My chair by the window stayed wood. My boots — I had adopted the custom, three pairs, rotated — were a superstition I kept for company, not need. The green never so much as threaded a lace. I asked if this should frighten me. Bren shrugged. *It started you years ago,* she said. *Finished before you came. You are the kept place. It doesn't copy the copy.* She thought a moment, and added, *You don't have to rotate your boots,* which is the closest this valley comes to envy.

I asked Em why I was not in the Count proper, and Em said: *The salt tin is not empty and has never been empty. We do not open it to check.*

Once, in the Old Grove, where the afternoons pool, I heard myself call from deeper in — my own voice, saying something warm, something I have still not said. Everything in me wanted to answer. It sounded like me, and it was early, so it was not me; it was not anything yet. That was my chance, and I took it the long way around, and did not answer, and it stopped. I grieved it a little. You grieve the paths you don't walk here; the Felt keeps them long, and you can feel them behind you all the way home.

But here is what the keepers worked out over that winter, in their flat way, between peat and bells. An early is dangerous because it is someone's. It has its half of a conversation and needs its person's half, and a conversation is the shortest road to finished. Mine has no such road. There is no one upstream of me to consume. Keepers have answered my voice by accident — forgetfulness, fog, the Loud Hour — and nothing finished, and I walked in at dusk, same as ever, a little hoarse. I am an early of no one. A voice with only its own half, permanently. Unfinishable, not by discipline — the keepers achieve looseness by discipline, the varied routes, the songs without endings — but by constitution. I have never once done anything twice. It is not virtue. It is what I am.

So the valley did what it does with the impossible. It made an etiquette of me.

I take the cistern listens. The keepers go down to the drip-choir in foul moods to hear next week's weather; I go down the way you go home. In the cistern every echo comes early, every keeper's voice says their sentence a moment before they do — it is the one room in the world where everyone sounds like me. I sit in the dark with the slate and write tomorrow's condensation, and the drops arrive in their thousands and the dark gives each one back slightly in advance, and it is the only place I have ever been that keeps my tempo.

The children practice on me. Call and response, the response withheld — and I am the safe voice to learn it on, the one early that finishes nothing. *If it sounds like someone you know, it is not them. If it sounds like no one you know, it is the visitor, and you may answer, or not, as you please.* Their sign for me is the second half of the copy-sign: the sternum tap that never lands. In the Loud Hour it is the only name in the valley that can be spoken in perfect silence, through the roar, and I come. My spoken name eroded within three seasons — mentioned hourly, asked after, answered — faster than any name in the Ledger. I am easy to live with and hard to look at directly, for no reason anyone can name.

Awn says my company is like the water: cold, flat, tastes of nothing, and she would not go a day without it. The keepers have had one joke for ten thousand years, the snail. Now they have two. Mine has not eroded yet. When it does it will be smooth, and they will tell it at the Shortening in the same voice as the losses.

At my first Shortening they gave me the spare note.

Every chord has one — the note no one is born to, the one the extra voice sings. It had been sung for ten thousand years by no one anyone could find. I asked Em whose it was. *Yours, now,* Em said, which is how the clerk says everything: as a fact that has already been in the Ledger for some time. We sang. And the Ledger says what it has always said — *voices, two hundred and one* — and that year, the first in the hand's memory, no one moved house. The extra voice had somewhere to be. It was standing in plain sight at noon, countable as anything, singing its own note at last, slightly ahead of the others.

Old habits. I had been early for so long.

Em lets me copy the column backward sometimes, the way apprentices do — some things you do not count, and some clerks you do not refuse — and the extra voice's register wanders down the centuries: high, low, wrong, nearer, wrong again. The valley spent a long time learning me. It sang me badly for a thousand years, and then less badly, and then the bell at the pass rang an hour early, and I came over the rim right on time.

So: not a keeper, though I keep. Not a copy — there has only ever been me, which is the one thing a copy can never say. Not a thinning, though things ring early near me. The keepers, with their genius for not finishing anything, have filed me under no category at all. The children are plainer. The children say I am the voice that was here first.

The Returned are polite to me in a way they are not quite polite to anyone else — the courtesy of colleagues. I asked one, once, sitting on the cold floor she prefers, what it was that brought her back. She said: when you are elsewhere, you walk, because walking is the only unfinished thing left to you, and after a time you hear the chord — far off, thin, wrong. One note of it early. Flat. A little hoarse, like a voice calling before it speaks. You do not answer it. You follow it. It is the one early thing you are allowed to keep. She said the stacks count correctly there, and everything is one, and the wrong note is the only two of anything.

I do not hum the whole chord in my sleep. I have heard myself: I hum one note, early, and the lamps on my street burn no steadier than anywhere, and the bells ring slightly before. But I know which harmonic I am. I am the one that reaches a little way past the rim, the one you hear first from the far side. When the glider's shadow crosses and the Hum goes thin, I am humming under it, out of courtesy, holding the door.

If you are ever elsewhere: walk until you hear the chord. Then walk toward the wrong note. It is me. I am keeping your place.

*Ledger, this spring: stacks, forty-one. Keepers, two hundred. Voices, two hundred and one. Visitor —* the clerk began, and then stopped, because some things you do not count, and set down the pen, and the train left at 6:10 with the address, one word, damp, unchanged.

The word is *here*. I am early for it. I have only ever been early for it.
