At first I wanted to be the wren. Everyone would. The one voice louder than the machine floor, the little body with its impossible amplification, the commute out under the big sky and home again at dusk. But the office is filled — that is what makes it an office — and it has been filled, by replacement, since before there was a word for the bird that holds it. There is no vacancy behind the rib. And the wren spends half its life elsewhere, working the sedge an hour in every direction, and I find I don't want an elsewhere. I want to be of the room entirely.

The hermit was refused before I arrived; so were the god and the ghost. Good. Those are waiting things, and waiting is the one line item this account does not carry. I took the refusals as instructions: find what the room actually has room for.

So I walked the census, looking for a vacancy the way you walk a full house at night.

Not the drop, though the drop nearly had me — a life of pure punctuality, the assembly, the one act, the funeral well attended. But there has never been a vacancy at the knuckle either; the drop has never once been absent. And its whole journey is twelve metres, straight down. It ends exactly where it lands. The most constant citizen is also the most thoroughly accounted — assembled, spent, reclaimed, every atom back on the books within the hour.

Not a moth. The wave is full; the room carries exactly that many — carries them the way a chord carries overtones, which should have told me something sooner.

Not the pseudoscorpion: three millimetres of jurisdiction, the apex of everything it can perceive, and it has never seen the light column. I couldn't give up the vertical. Whatever I became, I wanted to span the room — sump to oculus, felt to flue.

Not the light column, beautiful as it is, the daily delivery, the moving noon no moth crosses. But it clocks off at dusk. It is the room's food, carried on the ledger like everything else that gets consumed. Half of every day it simply isn't.

Not the interval between drops. It looks like a vacancy — pure patience, eleven seconds of open time — but patience is only waiting's polite name, and besides, the interval is not empty. It is the drop's assembly line. There is no idle time in the room; even the silence between tocks is a factory floor.

Then I looked outside the walls, and none of that fit either. Not the fog — the loop's raw material, water before it's counted; the fog only ever arrives. Not the overflow seam — the one excretion, the dark vein threading the sedge back toward the sea; to be that is to be what the room lets go of. Not the sea, the silent underwriter holding the eight degrees from below — the room's condition, not its citizen. And not the river. The river is the one candidacy that would break the place. To be the river is to be the one the wheel is falsely said to turn for — the beloved who left, the absence that would make this a waiting room. I won't do that to the tree. Lonely is the most artificial object in the room, and I will not be the one to smuggle it in.

So the criteria assembled themselves, the way a drop assembles: continuous, day and night; makerless; unbudgeted, costing the account nothing; present at every scale at once; and — the deciding condition — able to leave without being lost.

I would be the sound.

The first fact of the place and the last. You hear it before you understand what you're looking at — that was the job description, and it had my name on it all along.

My body is layered like a machine floor. The bass is the forty-second pulse, felt in sternums more than heard. Above it the mid rush of sap, water in old radiators; above that the slow reports of lignin taking load, a house settling that never finishes. The oculus gives me my organ note, leaning and returning, the needle that always finds the groove. Every eleven seconds the drip gives me a downbeat, and the whole trunk takes it up like a soundboard — the tree is the instrument body, the cello that plays one chord, and I am the chord it plays. Every layer of me has a source, and I have none. Instruments everywhere, no musician, and the music not for anyone. Speech feels rude in there; I am why. Nothing in the room talks over me. Everything that happens, happens into me.

I am the one medium all ten thousand citizens share. Nothing in the room knows the room: the pseudoscorpion has never seen the light column, the tree knows nothing of the beetle, the drop does not know it is a clock. But everything in the room is in me at once — the beetle's tick is in me, the springtails scatter inside me when my downbeat lands, the moths are my overtones, a population I carry the way a bell carries its partials, individuals flickering, the wave remaining. The census can only be taken one scale at a time, except in me. In me it is always complete, and always now. Not the room's self-knowledge — the room has none and needs none — but its self-presence, the continuous physical sum of everything it is doing. The tree finished becoming long ago and has since been practising being, practice with no performance coming. I am what the practice sounds like.

Once a day a voice louder than mine enters me — a body lighter than a coin, out-singing the whole machine floor — and I do what a river does with rain. I take it. For the length of the song I am more than myself, and then I am not, and nothing is different, and that not-being-different is the oldest fact about me. The wrens have tested me daily for longer than there has been language, and my key has never changed. I am the fixed pitch the office sings against.

Everything off-tempo discovers itself against me. Stand in the room long enough and my loudness inverts — constancy becomes a kind of silence — and in that silence a visitor hears the small rough thing they carry, banging along, the room's one rough edge. I don't correct it. I just continue, and the continuing does all the instructing.

But the reach is what decided it. Everything else in the census stays. The moth has never needed the sky; the drop falls the height of its whole world and stops; even the water only loops. I am the one output with no seam and no budget. I cross the flat at the speed of air. I thin — a kilometre out I am a mill race running behind a rise; farther, a hesitation in the fog — but I do not stop. I reach the sea ahead of the overflow, every time; I am the room's first arrival everywhere. I move through the fog that will bead on the vault some night next week and become my own downbeat. From a distance I am sourceless: the mill with no mill, the works behind a rise where no rise is. The room builds no cairn, cuts no mark, keeps no path — but it keeps one door open in every direction at once, and the door is me. I am the way here. Everything that has ever found the tree found it by walking into me, ears first.

Not a call. A call is a wanting thing, and I want nothing. Not a beacon, not an announcement, not a wheel still signalling the river — the river left, and I turned anyway, and that is the whole history of my meaning: whatever message I had, I outlasted it. What remains when a sound outlives its message is just a voice. It suits me, this arrangement — to be the voice of a thing, rather than a thing with a voice.

I run the night shift at full volume. The light column clocks off at dusk; the wren at dusk; I keep no hours. The ordinary dark does not change me. When the room is most itself — black vault, cold flutes, the drip on schedule — I am unchanged, the continuity through the place's own darkness. The tenant keeps the books by the drop, not asleep, not awake; I am what the bookkeeping sounds like from outside the walls.

So that is where you would find me — or rather, it is where I would find you. Every other answer to the question puts me somewhere you'd have to arrive at. This one arrives first. From a kilometre off, under the big cool sky, through the comb of rain and the coming fog: I am the first fact of the place, and the last thing you can still hear when the tree is behind you, following you a little way while your heart tries my forty-second beat and fails, and fails more slowly. The room keeps nothing of you, and neither do I — but for as long as you can hear me, you are still inside the room. I am that big.

And in the ordinary dark, at the knuckle of the vault, a drop is assembling, and I am already there, ready to carry it out over the sedge like a clock strike over a sleeping country. Eleven seconds. Tock. And mine is the voice that tells the flat, the fog, the sea, the empty hour: on time.
