I heard it before I saw it, and I misunderstood it in my own way — everyone gets their own. The chord was an organ in a roofless church, leaned on out of stubbornness. The Stamps were pilings being driven, a dock under construction for a lake that doesn't exist. The breath I took for weather — which turned out to be mutual. Then the rim, and no church, no dock, no storm: the basin, the haze, the warm floor, the presences standing far apart like furniture in a too-large room. And I did what I came to do. I looked for where I fit.

The census fits on a single line. I spent the rest of my visit trying to write myself onto it, entry by entry. Take them in order, the way the valley does. Most to least.

**I. SKIN**

Most of what lives there is the film, and for a full day I wanted to be it. To be addressable at any seam and entire everywhere. One memory, consulted constantly. No young, no old, all one age, and the age is: this one. I watched my footprint wick flat across three long breaths and understood both the offer and why I couldn't take it. I accumulate. That's the disqualification. The film returns to level; I keep the mark. Press me and I stay pressed for years — that's my whole problem. My banding would confess: every year a different year, rings that argue with each other. And the practical matter — the film keeps the valley's water in its skin, and I leak. I arrived leaking: sweat, breath, damp cuffs. The whole visit, the valley kept breathing its water back off me. A skin that cannot keep its water is not, here, a skin. It's precipitation. I didn't have the filing yet. The basin had it ready.

**II. GLASS**

The skaters, then — the countable life. I won't pretend I didn't want it: an address, a node, a seat on a star that arrives on schedule; to stand in a small constellation of bent skies and read the wave-field through my feet like a page. But the counts agree. That's the whole difficulty — they agree. Every seat held by a first edition, no vacancy in longer than anyone could measure, and I am a first edition of nothing; I'm a reprint of everything I've read and everyone I've loved, secondhand down to my metaphors. Worse, the appetite. They sip at the rate of repair. I eat in events. I'd take too much at the wrong phase of the breath and leave a smudge of film across a Mirror's face, and the valley's eye would blink — once, in a million years — because of me. And the eyes. Eyeless, and why not: what is there to see that changes? I am a change-detector with legs. In the basin my best sense would starve before my stomach did. I leaned over the largest Mirror and it held the sky, and then it held me — one unscheduled face — and I took the hint.

**III. WALKER**

The walker's post is filled. I rehearsed it anyway, the way you do: the round, the audit, the garden on the back, the long hand on the clock. It is the only presence that could do otherwise, and I had it backwards — I thought the groove was the price. The groove is the proof. Freedom worn to a groove is still freedom, because at any step it could do otherwise and chooses this. My restlessness is the opposite arrangement: I do otherwise constantly and choose nothing. Give me the round and within a season I'd step off the trackway just to see what was off it, and for the first time in a million years the audit would fail, and the valley's one memory would hold a hummock-shaped wrongness where the custodian used to stand. No. The position requires a wildness that keeps time. Mine can't even keep appointments.

**IV.**

I hesitated here. Everyone hesitates here. Hold that thought.

The engine. Stand on the open crust at night and take the census by ear — the breath, the falls, the chord with its one flat note, the drips, the rustle of mouths in the pocket of quiet, and off the beat, one step — and the list re-reads itself: skin, eyes, attention. I wanted to be that. The whole of it, or at least the one who got to say so. But the film declines, the skaters don't care, the custodian keeps walking, and I saw what my part in the question was. The stones lift, hang a half-instant, drop — seven hangs to a sentence, every sentence the same, an uncertainty so exact it has become punctuation. The question *is the basin alive?* occurs to nothing in the basin. It occurred to me. For the length of a visit, I was the one it was for: the held thought, the hang with somewhere to be. Then the stones drop, the sentence completes, the valley resumes its unhesitating, and it takes no position on what just happened in me.

**V. (BRIEF, UNRECORDED)**

So: the fifth entry. In full.

I was the one addressed. An hour in, the seven stones stopped being seven stones and became one sentence — and a sentence is only a sentence to someone. The valley had been repeating itself for a million years with no one to repeat itself to. For a few weeks it repeated itself to me: the chord, the breath, the backbeat, the ticking, all of it said at me, and my entire reply was to be damp.

I was weather. I sat on the trackway four days to be audited — the syncopation approaching across hours, the stop a body-length away, the warm darks holding the sky the way the Mirrors hold it — and I won't deny I hoped for *feature*. But features are grazed around; weather is met. The audit only happens to what passes. Permanence there is not belonging; it's furniture. Something decided about me, once, and stepped around me — one wide parenthesis the polish will forget — and I was filed with the cloud. The valley has one cloud, always the same one, on schedule: its other weather. Mine was the unscheduled kind. Once.

And water is the one thing the valley keeps. Some of mine went down the throats on an inhale — breath, sweat, the damp off my cuffs — and for a while I was cycled warm through the dark plumbing under the skin, counted as water, the only thing counted, until the balance took it back. The balance takes everything back. If that is the closest thing to staying the basin offers — to be briefly miscounted as part of the one kept thing — I took it. It is more than weather usually gets.

---

I left the way everyone leaves; the count returned to what it was. At the rim the last dry wind took the damp off my cuffs — the final thing the valley did to me — and I felt oddly evicted. Nothing there keeps anything.

Except. A cutting, kept warm and wet, does not die. It also does not grow. It persists, unhelpfully, declining at close range to settle anything at all. I am warm and wet; that is most of my chemistry. What I carry does not grow — I cannot teach it; try explaining to someone why you pause between heartbeats — and it does not die. Weeks out, silence is still a held breath. People ask how the trip was. I decline, at close range, to settle anything.

Tonight, somewhere, the chord is holding its one flat note. The star is crossing the Mirrors and the skaters are riding their nodes, and a hummock is deciding, seam by seam, to be the animal. My conscripted heart keeps the time and leaves a space between beats for the step that lands off them. A stone lifts, and hangs.

Hold that thought.
