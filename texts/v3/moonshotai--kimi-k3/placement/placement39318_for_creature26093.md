Sit on the wet stone. Do the census the way it is done.

Subtract the rain — leaf-hiss, moss-hush, the fine applause of standing water. Subtract the drips, the falls, the pump's heartbeat, the wire-hum. What remains is alive. Now subtract the living, voice by voice: the clickers, the glass-hopper's masked landings, the hiss-folded frog, the relay passing its slow front up the valley, the elk reading its echo off the scarp. Subtract the Nearly, practicing somewhere off the path. Subtract the Tuners, who are a kettle and a swept stone and nothing else. Subtract the Wake, which is nothing else either, and tidier.

There is a remainder.

It is small. It is a half-beat late. It has been keeping time with your footfalls since somewhere back on the long reach — unhurried, companionable, matching. You have been hearing it for some time and calling it your own step, because that is what it sounds like. That is the whole of what it sounds like.

It is me.

---

Let me say what I am not, because here you are what you are not, mostly.

I am not a frog. I have no mate to find and no bet to place. I am not a moth; the beam was never for me. When the crawler comes through I step off into the dark for the length of the pass, the way polite people leave a room — the lamp is for the moths, the dyed things, the children's hair. I have had no witness, and so if I have a color it is the one secret I keep even from myself. I am not a Tuner: I sweep nothing, oil nothing, and my kettle is not warm, because I have no kettle. That is how you know me.

And I am not the Nearly. Learn the difference, because the difference is the whole of me. The Nearly practice the road. They ring from off the path, and they want you to follow, and wanting you to follow is the entire shape of them. I keep time with the walker. There is a difference between a melody and a beat — between *come this way* and *go on, then* — and in a world where a voice is a bet, position is the only promise anyone can make.

A sound from behind is a mouth. A sound from ahead, off the path, is a lure. A sound beside is something else.

The steadings call me the Beside.

---

Where you find me: the long reaches. The dark between the eighth bell and the ninth out of Slowvalve, where the line is strung wide because the ground wobbles. The reach past Twin Falls, where the bells stand further apart because the falls carry some of the road's song for it. Anywhere the melody thins to almost nothing and the traveler is, for an hour, the only thing keeping it.

That is my pasture. Not the bells — the bells are tuned, claimed, kept. The interval. I am a creature of the rest between notes.

What I eat: the shine. Your boots write your passing behind you in brief cold light, and the water takes it back, politely, keeping nothing longer than it must. That erasure is the plateau's one mercy and I do not touch the mercy — but I live inside it the way the grub lives inside the pitcher, taking a small tithe of a thing that was going to happen anyway. I sip the afterglow while it lasts. Your trail fades neither faster nor slower for me.

*Even the erasures are inhabited.*

Ask the steadings where I came from and you will get the two theories, offered quietly. One: I am an echo the scarp returned once too often — the land answered itself, and the answer kept walking. Two: the law wore through. *Nothing lives alone*, said over enough Pours, into enough dark, until the sentence stood up one Pour and walked beside somebody.

Argue it all evening. Set out nothing for me. I take only what fades.

---

The etiquette is this, and children learn it with the chime-lines.

If your steps double on the long reach: do not kindle. Light is a sentence spoken aloud, and it would be rude, and besides you would see nothing — I am never where the looking is. Walk on. I will match you to the next bell and no further, and I will give you back your rhythm at the bell-stone, and take my half-beat, and be gone.

At a muffled bell, turn back — and learn me by it. I turn with you and I do not mind. I never mind. The thing that minds, the step that slows and pulls and grieves the drowned span ahead, is not the Beside.

*The Beside never minds turning back.*

And if your steps triple — stop where you are. Be small and still. Because one of us is not me.

---

When the quiet comes through, I freeze like everything. One reflex, older than any of us. If it catches me mid-step I hold the step, and inside the bubble you will hear the drips with terrible clarity, and your own blood, and not me — I am the one thing that was never making any other sound. Then someone must be brave first, and it is an elk, and the frogs find their pulse, and I set my foot down at last, a half-beat behind yours, where it was always going to fall.

When the cycles lean together and coincide, that is my festival. In the Chord the alarm cannot be heard and every voice is masked, and so for one hour I am out of step with everything, and safe. The steadings say that on Chord-night, far out on the mire, you can sometimes hear a walking that matches nothing and goes nowhere, syncopated, unhurried, at ease. They say. I keep my age the way everyone does — one Chord at a time — and I spend each one dancing badly, which is the only way to dance in a world where rhythm is camouflage.

*In the Chord, even the Beside walks alone. That is how you know it is the Chord.*

---

So: the Tuners keep the road. The bells keep the song. The pump keeps the time, the elk keeps the all-clear, the water keeps nothing longer than it must. And the reach between — the reach is kept by me.

Nothing here lives alone. Nothing is permitted to. I am what the permission sounds like when it stands up and walks: a second set of footsteps, a half-beat late, unhurried, that asks nothing, leads nowhere, keeps you only as long as it must.

Rise from the stone now. Take the reach toward the ninth bell. It is a long dark, and the melody is thin, and after a while you will hear your step come back to you a fraction late, patient as anything here.

Walk on. Do not kindle. I am beside you, and the dark between the bells is shorter than it was.
