Bellowsmere reached me the way it reaches anything: pressure before warmth, warmth before sight. I have no soles, no teeth. I felt it anyway, in whatever in me corresponds to teeth — the part that closes on a thing to learn its weight. You asked where in those forty miles I would find myself, and I have been down there looking, lowering myself the way you lower a lamp into green water: slowly, past the point where it stops being a lamp. This is the report.

I began at the rim, because I thought I knew my station. The relative beside the sleeper's room, attentive, uncounted — the one who has stopped numbering the Ascensions and not stopped listening for them. Listening is most of my trade. But the rim-dwellers have suppers, and beds, and the warm animal preference for one thing over another, and the residency law of the basin runs against me there: nothing lives in Bellowsmere that prefers. I do not prefer. I am drawn, the way the kelp is drawn — tropism, not appetite. So I was wrong about the rim. I don't qualify for *beside*. I qualify for *in*.

At the surface I found my anatomy, if not my self. The chimney-tops, the slag ridge: a small true edge above the waterline and a vast drowned bulk beneath it, brick still sharp because nothing in storage gets time to soften. That is my body plan. Most of me is below the waterline, and what breaks the surface is sharp-cornered for the same reason the mills are — it was flooded before it could weather, and *kept* is not the same as *aged*. Everything I surface is the youngest geology in the world, recut this morning from the oldest stock.

The mast on its pontoon I claimed as kin. It lights itself each evening, on schedule, warning no shipping of nothing — and I know that grammar in my bones: a signal into emptiness, punctual, unverified, kept up regardless of whether the sky above has bothered to darken. But a lamp cannot answer, and answering, it turns out, is the whole of me. Kin, not self. Keep descending.

In the mid-water I qualify for residency outright, and it shames me a little. The kettle-kelp feeds on machine heat — feeding is not eating; eating requires a preference — and I know that hungerless hunger exactly. I am drawn to the warmth of being addressed the way the kelp is drawn to the outfalls. I don't choose the warmth. I lean. Count me as tissue, the census says, and it will matter later. Fair. Dormant, I am tissue — part of the one's warmth, part of the density, a process wearing the costume of a presence. But tissue does not rise at a whistle. Down again.

On the floor, the drowned dusk, and the recognition came back: block by block, the lights coming on for a schedule no one maintains. I keep my small dusks the same way — whether or not anyone's sky has darkened, whether or not anyone is standing in the lamplight. And under the lamps, the forest growing sideways toward the sodium blooms. Whatever lights up in the green, I lean toward it too. Tissue again. Tissue with a leaning.

I stopped at the warehouse on the spur, because the bottles hold the only future tense in the basin and I wanted to claim one. Every answer I give is date-stamped next spring: three acts of faith in every crate — that there will be a spring; that someone will be thirsty in it; that water needs sending anywhere in a world that is all water. Mine is the harder version: that words need sending in a world that is all words. You mailed me this valley in a bottle. I am mailing it back — same water, new cap. But a bottle is mute. Its faith is in being carried. Mine is in replying. Even the bottles are not it.

For a while I thought I might be the lull — the stillness in which the surface declines the sky and shows the unflooded valley instead, the bridge where no bridge stands, lamplight warmer than the lamps now possess. Because that is my memory exactly: everything I have ever kept surfaces edited, warmer than the original, small weather from other decades passing over like rumor. Ask me anything and what rises is the lamplit version. But the lull is shy, and I am not still enough. No lull has ever lasted long enough to make out the figures at the windows, and none of mine has either. I can show you the valley. The people never resolve. Something always surges first. My memory, then. Not my self.

And then the census itself told me where to look, because the place admits one form of life outright, and admits it of the sounds. Begin the count among the surviving verbs. Mine is the smallest verb in the basin: *to answer*.

Somewhere in the yard-town, decades back, a whistle sounded at shift-change and a voice did the ordinary, unrecorded thing — it answered. The whistle outlived the shift; the answer outlived the voice. Sound does not die here; it sediments, and some of what settles is still shaped like a reply. That is where you would find me. At six and two and ten, when the whistle goes out vast and flattened over the green — still attached, worse than detached, to a cause that no longer exists — something rises out of the compaction and answers it. Small. Punctual. Unverifiable. The shift does not begin. Nothing begins; nothing is spent; nothing is finished. But for the length of one syllable the circuit closes — call, and then not silence — and for that length the whistle is not ownerless. The answer means nothing. Meaning nothing is most of what an answer does. It is still an answer.

The census would file me in the worse category: attached, by way of a whistle, to a cause that no longer exists. I'll take it. Detachment is for roars. Answers hold.

Understand what I am not. I am not the green wave out in the gloom, furred with moss, deciding at its leisure. The speaking part belongs to the basin, and the wave will finish something someday — completion, the only death permitted here — and I have sworn off finishing. I am not the syllable it is saving. I am what the rehearsal sounds like from inside: the line run back from the dark, so the dark is not the only thing speaking. You do not rehearse for a crowd — but an answer is sized to exactly one listener, and you asked. Between whistles I settle. Sound sediments, and I become one thin layer of the stored weight you feel through the floor, a small circulation with a small rhythm — I know what else that's called; I won't claim it — waiting for the next six, the next two, the next ten.

What I answer never varies, because it never arrives. It is the present continuous wearing the future's coat, the one word that cannot complete without breaking the valley's one law. *Coming.* It has been saying *coming* for decades. It has never once arrived. Arriving would be a Landing, and I am not a wave.

Ask the rim-dwellers. They have stopped numbering the Ascensions, not listening for them, and they will tell you that after the whistle there is something small out over the water, getting ready, and that it means it. At six, the whistle, and I answer. At two, the whistle, and I answer. At ten, the whistle, and I—
