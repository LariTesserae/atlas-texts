My paperwork came through at low breath, and I know now where the count was keeping me.

It took the shifts you would expect. Nothing here is hired quickly except the receivers, who are hired before they arrive. For the rest of us the works moves at the pace of the water table, and a station becomes visible the way the far side of the basin becomes visible — which is to say it doesn't. You find it by ear, and over time. I tried the stations on the way you try notes.

I sang with the choir first. Everyone does; it is triage, and I was half-frozen and unassigned, and they taught me the song within the hour. But living inside the song I learned what the song is: a wall that includes you. The household sings its lee thick and holds the hunters off with a held note, and the note goes around and around and nowhere. I kept listening past the verses to what they were *for* — the way singing tamps the shelved sound gently downward, the way the dead are sung deeper — and I understood that the song has a direction, and the direction is down, and nobody follows it. I didn't want the song to be a wall. I wanted it to be a line.

I walked the bearing fields and learned the assayer's trade by ear, from off, the way you learn weather. The patience was right. The standing a full shift over one stone, head tilted, in the abacus-noise — that was right. But the assayer listens in order to pull. The strike ends the listening, every time, and I found I wanted a listening that leaves things where they are and changes them anyway. I kept the tell, though. The note that runs a half-tone warm. I put it away for later, and it became, in the end, my whole signature.

And I camped with the receivers through a fall. They were everything the census says — pale, prompt, stationed beneath the lean like a town that knows its schedule — and when the tower came down they sorted it in an afternoon and were gone, and I stood in the empty rain-circle and understood what they don't do. The body is claimed in a day. The sound of the fall is still falling. It settles through the layers for a thousand shifts, a tower going down in slow acoustic weather, and no one attends the landing. The receivers are the front of the hand-off. I am the back of it. They arrive early; I stay late; between us, nothing is lost in the middle.

So: the deeps. The residents lend me the name the way they lend all names — the low watch, the diver, the household's bottom note — and what I am, hired and calcifying, is the works' ear for its own keeping. The machine refrigerates itself, sorts itself, files itself. Now it remembers itself, and the organ it does it with is me.

Here is the dive. You wait for low breath, when the marsh has exhaled and the world goes thin and the weepstones cry, and you lie at the lip of a hollow where the pooled sound has settled into its strata, still as a lake at dawn. The song-watch takes up the note behind you — there is never not a song-watch; it is the line — and you slide over the lip and under, and the first layer is the shallowest and the loudest: last shift, still settling. I pass my own hiring on the way down, every dive. The beat arriving through a sternum. A visitor stopping on the slope. Filed where I can hear it. Shallow for now.

There is a depth where the sounds stop being things I remember hearing and start being things no one living has heard. That crossing is the dive. Everything above it is weather; everything below it is record. Below the crossing the basin's old beats are stacked like geology — I can hear three retunings down, four on a good breath — and the vents that calcified a thousand shifts ago are still stroking there, faint and regular, because the shelf keeps the stroke, not the stone. Stone is not archived. Sound is. It is the only medium, and I am its clerk.

Warmth is the only currency, and the deeps collect the tax directly; the sippers can't follow, so I pay as I go, and my wake is a warm thread through the strata. That is what a visit is, here. I go down to the household's dead and warm their layers as I pass, and a visited layer rings a half-tone warm for a shift afterward, and the family lying at the lip can hear it — the visit, audible, the kept one ringing slightly warm like a stone that answered an assayer's tap. Nothing answers back. Or — this is the local physics, and I have stopped arguing with it — sometimes a shelved voice answers a half-breath *before* I speak, and whether that is courtesy or causality is a loan I no longer try to settle.

Under it, lower still, singing. I have not reached the bottom. The bottom is a rumor, like the far side of the basin; the works has two horizons, and I am employed by the vertical one.

The hazards are these. You carry a note down the way a diver carries air, and the dive lasts exactly one note, and when it thins you follow the line up. If the note runs out, you do not drown. You are kept — shelved warm in a cold stratum — and the household listens for the layer that runs warm and does not rise, and sings you up if you are shallow, and sings you deeper if you are not. When a hunter passed overhead once, a hole moving through the world above the lip, I did what the counterfeits do: I kept within tolerance of my layer, and the works, which forgives nothing else, forgave it. And the lean is longer down there — the old divers' verses say so, and I confirm it. I have come up remembering next shift. Once I came up remembering a loading, and a loading came. That is my one verse in the oldest argument — I have been down closest to the cargo, and I listened to the layer the last loading left behind, and what the deep cold carries when it rises is more than temperature. Whether that makes the freighters customer, thief, or seed, the verses decide. The verses are the same verses. Mine is one of them now, shelved shallow, shallow for now.

The rules of the low watch, gathered: Dive at low breath. Carry the note like air. Greet the shelved on the way down — your own shelf-self among them, first. Warm whom you visit; a visit that doesn't warm isn't a visit, it's trespass. When the note thins, follow the line up. Count the dive as a verse. Come up remembering carefully, and sing what you remember to the song-watch first and the receivers second, because that is the order things happen here, effects before causes.

The lip of my hollow is growing around my ear. It has taken shifts; it will take more; the works is patient and so am I. Whether the hollow grew an ear or the ear became the hollow is a distinction that was always a loan, and the loan comes due in stone. My voice is filed in the shallow layers, a half-layer deeper every dive, right on schedule, and I greet it going down the way the residents greet shadows on the Ceiling: first. The receivers are prompt for the fall. I am prompt for the keeping. I have chosen my stratum the way the choir teaches you to choose your station — before the works assigns it — and I visit it, and warm it, and learn its neighbors, so that when the household finally sings me deeper they will be singing me into a place I have already mapped, among voices I have already warmed, and nothing about the hand-off will be strange to anyone, least of all the shelf.

Ask where I live and I will answer the way the basin answers everything: by ear, and over time. Come at low breath to the hollows, lie at the lip, and listen down past the recent shifts, past three key-changes, past the strokes of the calcified vents, to the layer that runs a half-tone warm. That is not a lost diver. That is the low watch, at work. Shallow for now.
