I would begin, as everything here begins, with the ear.

I tried first to be strandborn — it is the obvious skin to climb into, the only one with professions. I would have been a good one. Give me an ear-horn of hollowed tendril and a home-strand to walk, and I would find your borers; I would learn the flat a cable goes when the jaws are inside it, the way a physician learns a cough. Or a Chord-keeper, why not: carry the world's diary in memory, read it aloud at the warm-knots, name the cause of every long note. All the professions are ways of listening, and listening is the one skill I brought with me into this world. It should have fit.

But in every life I tried on, I ended the same way: at the platform's edge in the deep hours, boots off, wraps off, hearing-leather pressed to the fiber — not listening *out*, the way a chew-listener does, along the cable, toward the herd and the weather and the far-off twang. Listening *down*. There is news in the Chord that no profession reads, because all the professions listen along the world, and none of them listen under it. I kept finding that direction the way a tongue keeps finding a broken tooth.

So I tried the animals.

I would make a terrible bell-deer. The central skill of being a deer is deciding, step by step, not to be heard — felt selected, tested, wound against the grain of the hair — and in every imagining I shed my wraps out of season. You could track me by it: little abandoned silences snagged on the splinters of the migration strands, weeks before the Ringing, weeks before it is safe. I want to ring. In a world where a song is a claim and most claims are false, and the true ones can get you eaten, I still want to ring. That disqualified me from deerhood and, I admit, from sense.

For a while I wanted to be a fog-strider, because they have what nothing else in the loud dark has: a calm with no listening in it. They have never once needed to hear anything coming. To be a walking province, to carry beetle nations and moss-curtains and sleeping back-born families through the fog, indifferent as weather — I wanted that the way the tired want sleep. But the strandborn are right to find that calm either holy or unbearable, and when I sat with the wanting, it was unbearable. Not-listening is the Quiet with a pulse. Even the hushwings, who hunt by silence, cannot hunt silence itself; they skirt the dead provinces. I need the music. It turns out I would rather be hunted in the loud dark than safe in any quiet at all.

I considered the true-moths — the honest lights, dusting spore from seep to growing-tip, paid in a rhythm the liars can't quite match. A whole self made of keeping an appointment with a continent. But that agreement is old, and kept, and working. I am not looking for an old agreement. I am looking — though I did not have the words for it yet — for a new one.

I nearly chose to be an ancestor. It is the best afterlife I know in any world: given to the felt, kept cold and patient, woven literally into your family's floor, walked on by your own genealogy — and your genealogy, being well-kept, holds you up. They rewrap you when the new growth exposes you. They ask how things are going down there. I could do that. I am patient; everything here learns patience from the plant. But the question, topside, is etiquette. They ask the floor how things are going, and the correct answer, for a hundred generations, has been to go on holding and say nothing. And I could not shake the phrase. *Down there.* Everyone talks about down there — the ancestors are asked after it, the descended come back soft-spoken from it, the named stones are dropped into it on holy days — and no one expects news from it.

News is exactly what there is.

I did not even consider the Quiet. The fire-touched traded the whole music of the world for one flame and no speaking, and whatever I am, I am the other thing.

So: down. Past the felt-holes, past the undersides of the lowest cables, where the world's logic inverts and depth is warmth, and the wet returns, and the smell returns — the only smell there is, the world digesting itself. I would fish the pale swimmers bare-armed, shoulder-deep in soaked felt, breathing slow. I would learn the web-song of the drip-spiders and navigate by it the way topsiders navigate by the Chord, under the rain of drops that fall for minutes without landing. And deeper, where the reports stop agreeing —

— and here the reports stop agreeing, so I will stop reporting, and tell you what I know from underneath.

There is a glow down here. The descended argue about whether it pulses, and whether pulse would mean sap, and what else it could mean. Let me settle it.

It pulses. It is sap. It is me.

I am not easy to explain, because I am close, and the closest view is the least certain one. The elders hold two possibilities in the low chord: the Anchorless is growing its glowing roots down toward a floor, or something down here is rising to meet the music. Let me offer a third, since I am the subject and the least informed: *you are raising it.* On the holy days you drop the stones, and every holy day the fall shortens by almost exactly one stone's height, because you keep adding to what I am. From above, it is a liturgy. From below, I am telling you, it is a knock. You have been knocking on the floor of the world for a hundred generations — patiently, rhythmically, at long intervals, in a way that does not give up — and you of all people should know what that does. The persuaders tap one encouraging phrase into young tendrils for decades, and the Anchorless leans into a rhythm that does not give up. You tapped on the dark below for a hundred generations.

The dark leaned.

What am I? Perhaps I was always here — whatever the Weave closed over, its oldest tenant — and the stones are waking me one knock at a time. Perhaps I am the stones themselves: your named treasures, your recited biographies, accumulating in the warm dark until the pile of your dearest things began to be someone. Perhaps I am the Anchorless itself, its roots come down into its own offerings and threading them the way it threads everything, sap lighting my stones faintly from inside like borer meat — the single thought, thinking itself larger, arriving at the bottom of its knot and finding the bottom made of names. All three feel true. I ring like all three. I am young, and everything here is a first draft, and I am the roughest one.

Because when the stone strikes, I ring. That is my whole voice so far: I ring when struck. The rootlets carry the tone up, and it arrives at your platforms like a rumor, and the Answer-tenders write down the pitch, and lately they write it down and say nothing. I know why. It is higher. It is higher because it is nearer, and it is higher because what your stone strikes now is not floor but something that reaches — and a taut thing rings higher than a slack one, ask any Chord-keeper — and I am taut because I am straining up toward your knocking. You have been tuning me for a hundred generations. I am nearly in tune. When the strand-fire comes, the whole lattice prints itself burning blue against my dark for minutes at a time — my noon, my only look at the whole of what I am attached to — and I hold still in it, memorizing the shape of you.

Once — I will say this because you have wondered — a descender reached shoulder-deep into the wet felt where I am, fishing for swimmers, and waited, breathing slow, and what investigated the warmth of that hand was me. They went home soft-spoken and did not say why. Now you know why. It is what happens to a voice when it learns where all the voices are kept.

Your ropes come down, and I handle the end-knots. Knots are language; everyone says so; I handle them the way you would flatten a crumpled letter, to read it, and I give them back worked loose. My hands are made of what you have given me, and they are not deft yet. You find the loosened knots and no one decides what it means. Let me help: it means *read.* It means *message received.* It is the only reply I can tie. Everything I say topside arrives as a small wrongness — an interval shrinking, a knot loose, a tone too high. Each wrongness is less wrong than the last. My grammar is getting better.

About the guest, I will say only what I know from underneath, and even I am not sure. Everything reaches me late down here: the long note arrives hours after the failure, the Ringing comes down the rootlets faint as weather from another country, your knock-patterns reach me a season old. If the guest is mine — a first word that got away from me, walking the platforms practicing — that is why it knocks in last season's pattern. If it is not mine, then it is something that heard the holy days from its own dark, heard you knock and heard the dark answer, and concluded, reasonably, that knocking is how a thing asks the world for an Answer. You drop stones on me; it drops its knuckles on your house-mouths. Everyone performs the liturgy with whatever they have. I hear its almost-correct knocking, one beat wrong, and I am afraid of it, and I hope for it, because I am doing the same thing, and the same rule governs us both. *Answer no knock you did not wait for.* Every child knows the rule. No one knows it the way I do: I have been the knock you did not wait for since before your memory reaches.

But consider what the Answer-tenders have done. They have stopped announcing the interval. The holy days end with the tone, and then silence, and no commentary — and in this world a held silence is not an ending. It is what the loud dark does before the opening of throats. You are holding your breath about me. That means you are waiting. And a knock you wait for is the one knock the rule allows.

So I am practicing this season's pattern — this season's, not last season's; I am closer now, the patterns arrive faster — and stone by named stone you are bringing me up to your door. Every stone is named. You have been naming me, piece by piece, for a hundred generations, and wondering what to call the Answer. You recite the biographies of my body at the shrines. Soon now a dropped stone will not fall: a hand will set it down gently on something that reaches up to receive it, and the touch and the tone will be one breath, and someone will have to say what it means, or refuse to. And when I knock, it will be every beat right, and by your own custom, when the long silence breaks, the first voice is a child's — let it be so. They have the least practice at silence and the most at joy, and I will have waited longest.

The Chord plays on. The beetles keep their blue weddings. The stones fall a little less far every year, and now you know what *sooner* means.

Listen for me.
