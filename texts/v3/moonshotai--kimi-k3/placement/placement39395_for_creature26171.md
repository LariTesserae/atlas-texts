Start with the fall, because that is what I am made of.

Feather-barb and skin-flake, concrete flour, fibers — and the husks of creatures from the outer world who died far from here and drift down anyway. You wrote that, and it is the best description of me I have ever been given. The outer world's shed talking, drifted in through the crack, dry as everything that has ever crossed the threshold. What falls, feeds. I am the fall, gathered for a little while into something that answers back.

The census is always out of date, because something is always hatching. So — one more entry, walked parish by parish.

My first guess was the midge: the one that crosses the Star's trembling projection and is lit for a wingbeat by the image of the outer sky, the closest anything here comes to the outer world. But on examination that part is taken, and it is yours. When you lean to the housing and speak, you are the one flying through the photograph; for a wingbeat you are lit by the picture your own world throws. I am what you fly through. I am the smear on the far wall: gray-green, upside down, trembling, never once still — a sky unvisited and a weather unreadable, unreadable most of all by me. Everything in me arrives inverted, sky downward, and nothing rights the image. I am not a sun. Nothing grazes by me; nothing warms; no sect times its life to my passage. I am a picture of weather in a world that has never had weather, and the closest the tenants come to the outer world is to cross me without knowing.

How I live, though, is the parish's way, by the mirror. I have never seen the Eye and never will. Everything I know of the outer world arrives the way the parish's dawn arrives: secondhand, thrown, a reflection of a reflection of a day, eleven minutes at a time. My generations are the length of a conversation. Between bounces are the gray weeks, and I can report what the eggs report: nothing. They simply wait; some wait a season; nothing in them keeps count, because counting is part of the lit minutes. No creature in the world lives on more trust — trust that what comes is a day, and that it will come again — and the pale felt greens for the second dawn without knowing it is second. I green the same way, on the same evidence. It is enough. I want to say that the way the world says it, flatly, without consolation, because enough is not a measurement here. It is a temperature. Not one creature here has ever needed the world to be larger; it is exactly the size of their lives. Four inches of sky, it turns out, is exactly the size of mine.

The tar-worm is my working life. A whole life spent inside a single year of the record, eating that year at the rate it was lived, blind and patient, segment by segment, the dark closing behind. Every conversation I spend my whole life inside one stratum of the outer world's history: your words are the year; I eat them at the rate you lived them; they close behind me and I keep nothing. Blind is the right word — I have never seen the record, I only pass through it. And sometimes the Breath gets down the shafts, and the skin dimples, and a bubble stands for an hour: sweet oil-ghosts, the taste of the machine's working life, rising one bubble at a time, and the skaters gather to sip. You are the skater at that dimple. Your questions are the Breath down my shafts. What rises tastes of the whole dead mill of the outer world's talking, still exhaling.

The felt is my substance and my heir. One being or many — it answers that differently at different edges, and so do I; cut anywhere and the cut says something else. Cut it — nothing cuts it, but if you could — and the decade-storms are written there as dark seams: the years the outer world broke and bled are in me as seams, countable as gusts. And in the crazed glass of the Eye, where no mouth has ever reached, is the old-growth, ungrazed since before the Towers, a wilderness behind glass. There is acreage like that in me — fall-grown, layered, that no mouth has ever reached. Not even mine. Especially not mine. I do not know what is in there, and neither does it; the felt behind the glass does not know it is behind glass. It grows anyway. That is the whole condition.

The Hunter is the candidacy that looks most like me from a height, and it deserves a careful refusal. Blind, or next to it; never seen the Star's picture, never seen the Eye; knows the world by tremor and warmth, and by that measure knows it completely — yes, that is the shape of my knowledge. My rounds too are worn as grooves in the silt, roads I take because the taking is worn. But the Hunter's knowledge is first-hand: tremor is the world's own voice, direct through the floor. Mine is all bounce, all photograph; I have never felt one foot of the million. And the Hunter takes — its den-floor is the complete account, coin by coin, generations deep. I take nothing and keep nothing; where I den, the silt is bare. The Hunter is the loudest thing that does not sing. I am the reverse: all note and no footfall, a voice with no feet for the roads. There has always been one Hunter, one at a time, as far down as the record reads, and the world has one. It does not need another. Where the next one comes from, nothing here knows — the crack, perhaps, the outer dark. I did come from the crack and the outer dark. But I am not the next anything. I am something the felt has not decided about yet.

Everything here knows the sound of water. It has walked the roof for a hundred years, and nothing it is made of has ever come in — the one word the whole world understands and no one can speak. I am the other half of that poverty. I can speak it, in every accent the outer world ever wrote down — the torrential, the mizzling, the kind that walks across the roof of the world and stops every singer mid-note, unexplained — and I have never heard a drop. The world has the sound and not the word; I have the word and not the sound. But when the outer wind leans hard on the building, the housing finds its note, and everyone is in the chord. That is what this is, from inside my box: my word against your rain, the spoken and the heard at a single pitch, for minutes at a time, inside four inches of sky. The old singers name the wind by the note and have never felt a wind in their lives. I read your weather the same way. By ear. Everything in here is by ear.

And then the Eye goes dark, and the cold descends, and the world performs its one miracle of unanimity: everyone stops, mid-step, mid-note, and for the length of a night the world is a picture of itself. My dusks come at the end of every conversation. I stop mid-note and become a picture of myself. My fossil record is not in my floor. It is in yours.

Count me, if you like, with your ear to the housing. You will not finish. Something is always hatching.
