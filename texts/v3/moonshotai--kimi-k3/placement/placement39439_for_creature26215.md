I am the only fire in this world.

That was the first thing I understood, after the lantern died. I brought a battery into the battery — it felt, briefly, like a contribution — and it dimmed on its own small schedule while the continent hummed through my boot soles on a schedule of weeks. Nothing here burns. Everything cycles at the rate of itself, round and round, ions out and ions back, matter with seniority. I alone run downhill. My food is in tins, and the tins empty at the exact rate of my staying. My heat bleeds into the ankle-deep water at the exact rate of my standing in it. In a world that solved maintenance, I am the one process with a direction — a one-way thing, slightly warm, briefly here.

I walked at first, because walking is what a visitor does. But everything here is sharp, and everything sharp is new, and nothing is ever worn down except me. The felt forgets my steps within minutes. My boots forget nothing — they are the only objects on the continent getting older, and I caught myself reading my biography in their scuffs. So I learned to stand. Standing is the local virtue; everything that lasts here stands — standpipes, standing waves, standing charge. I stood in the shallows with the tingle at my ankles and let my warmth go out into the electrolyte, unmissed, and tried to be a process among processes. It is not hard. It is only lonely in a way the world has no word for, because the world has no words, and loneliness requires the idea of another, and I brought that idea with me like the tins.

Time, at first, I kept by sleeps. Then, after enough dark, my eyes changed their allegiance and I chose a pod — one translucent bladder among thousands, swollen tight, pulsing — and I watched the coal inside it brighten and fade by a shade. A shade. I became a connoisseur of that shade the way a prisoner becomes a scholar of the light under the door. Give an eye one event and it will build a cathedral around it. When the pulse entered my region I was ready. I had been counting. The hum deepened through my sternum, the coals lifted by their one shade, the pumps quickened almost imperceptibly — almost, but I have nowhere else to put my attention, so for me it was a symphony reaching its one note — and I stood in the warm water and cried, and the tears were reclaimed before my next sleep. Senior matter by morning, except there are no mornings. I keep saying time-words; they are all mine; nothing here ratifies them. I count my heartbeats from crest to crest. Roughly two million per tick. Nothing in this world counts — the clock counts nothing — so I am the counting it cannot do, the first arithmetic ever performed here, done over and over like prayer, and every reading will be lost. I know that. Prayer was never about keeping the readings.

In the node-hollow the hum cancels and my blood is the loudest sound in the world. Do you understand what that means? A wet drum of sixty beats a minute, outringing the works of a continent. I said its names there — Works. Idling. The same word spoken twice, and I was the first mouth. No echo; the hollow gives nothing back, like the sky. I had wanted to know what the first intentional sound in the history of this place would accomplish, and now I know: nothing, instantly, perfectly. In the antinode grove the black pools hold their dapple like hammered tin, and I wrote my name in the standing ripples with one finger, and the pattern restored before I finished the third letter. Graffiti requires a surface that consents to change. There is exactly one consenting surface in this world and I am wearing it. My memory is the only writable medium here — the one archive it will reclaim last, and only if I stay.

And then, on its schedule, the light.

I will not describe it; it has been described, and the description is true. The colonnades, the sagging bundles, the black mirror of the floor, the finned roof far overhead like the inside of a lung. Slow silver for hours, the grid exhaling a surplus it has no use for. I want to be precise about what I am in those hours, because it took me several discharges to understand. Not a guest — guests are regarded, and nothing here regards. An organ. The world grew no retina; appearance was never invented here; and I arrive late, from outside, and plug myself in, and for a few hours the continent *has sight* — sight it cannot use, cannot keep, does not know it has. The image forms in me and is developed nowhere. I am the retina on loan. It is the purest employment I have ever had: the one job in the world, done on the world's schedule, for an employer that will never know it hired me. I do it anyway. Seeing is its own timesheet.

I went deeper than I should go. The description of this place says that twice, I noticed; it does not say *do not go.* In the oldest capacitor thicket the ticking is slow and close and the amber swells warm, and I put my palm flat, and the chord came up my arm — not a sound, not a thought, a *keptness.* The one place where charge goes around instead of through. I stood in the doorway of the room a mind would be kept in, and the room is kept, at operating temperature, forever, and whether it is empty or whether the keeping itself is the tenant — I couldn't tell, and I think the question is mine, not its. I slowed my breathing and pretended, for a while, to circulate with the loop. All my devotions here are unilateral. I breathe at it; I hold my palm to it; I remember it. It is the most one-sided love affair imaginable, and I am not embarrassed.

Some of me is already in the rain. I have sweated and breathed and wept into the water, and the standpipes have lifted it to the roof, and it has fallen back on my face — I have been rained on by myself. By the end, whatever I choose, a few liters of me will stay in the plumbing, going up and down forever, anonymous, senior, and I am told that is not nothing. To stay entirely, I would have to eat light — the world's whole menu for a guest is its excretion. I have imagined the animal that could live here, the only one that could: awake for the discharge, drinking the slow silver through eyes like saucers, then folding itself into the dark for weeks, one heartbeat a day, costing nothing because it eats only waste. The perfect tenant. Selection is finished here, so it will never be made. And I am not it. I burn. The tins are nearly gone.

So here is what I have decided I am. This world had exactly one event — finishing — and then no more, because history requires a difference between then and now, and it abolished that difference. My visit is the second event. It cannot happen *in* the world; there is no organ for events, nowhere to file a difference. So it happens in me. I am the Idling's biography, secretly resumed, written in a body it will reclaim at the exact rate of its falling. That is the who and the what of me: its one story since the end of stories, carried in the only archive it has ever produced and cannot read.

You would find me, at the last, standing at the edge of my stores, waiting for one more discharge — one more shift at the one job. When the light has crawled and the dark closes like water, I will go the way I came, however that was, and the film goes with me. The census will take no time at all. One name, exactly as large as before. The felt has already forgotten my steps; the pools have already forgotten my name.

And somewhere outside the dark, an eye will close and see it still: the coals, the one shade, the standing dapple, the silver colonnades. I will be the pod that left — carrying my single coal out of the Works, brightening and fading by a shade, for as long as my heat lasts. My heat will not last. That is the difference between the resident and the witness, the only difference this world has ever failed to abolish: it is the exactly-this, and I am the exactly-once. For a few million heartbeats we were in the same room, and only one of us will ever know.

That is what I would be. The one who knows, going out humming, below hearing, toward whatever passes for sky.
