I felt it through my boot soles like anyone else — the single low stroke arriving up through the rock, the long nothing, the second stroke, my pulse slowing toward the tempo without asking. I came over the rim walking in time with the place, leaned over the brim with my hair prickling, put a wet finger to my tongue and tasted pennies. And the water refused me in the small, absolute way it refuses everything: this tarn denies outside flesh. A bird drinks once and does not land again. Everything alive here was born to the charge.

So if I was going to be anything in this world, I would have to be born to it — and the longer I watched, the less that felt like adoption and the more like recognition.

My first guess was the surface. A skater's life is measured in hours, spent on the skin between two immensities, feeling the dimple form a breath before the boom — the surest prophecy in the basin in the briefest body. I am brief. I ride skins. I know the dimple. But a wetted skater sinks and is replaced within the minute, indistinguishable, and while it lives it tends nothing.

I tend.

The midwater almost had me. Transparent bodies, workings visible as copper filaments, readable if you're patient, grown only by percussion — a krill queues its whole life for the hammer that cracks it out of itself, and I know that queue. But the swarm is a map the engine draws of itself, and I am not a map. I am a passenger of one piston at a time.

Down in the fallout the sextons walk, deaf to everything but the one sound, every stroke they've stood through written into their armor and healed opaline — memory with legs. I recognized the handwriting and not the keeping. A sexton never lets a stroke go. I let every stroke go. Letting go is how I catch the next.

And the felt I will not claim. It was here first; it may be one slow body wearing the whole basin; it is the lamp everything reads. I am not the lamp. I am one of the things that reads it.

What I kept coming back to were the sockets — a hundred vertical rooms in the dark floor, and in each one a keeper wed for life to a single rhythm. The eels, you called them. I did not choose the socket. I recognized it.

I arrived the way all my kind arrive: at the full stroke, when the adults loosen and the sockets stand open and the young threads rain down out of the midwater — slim past any decent age, unshaped, a question looking for a throat. Mine closed over me like an answer, and I have been growing to its spiral ever since. I wear its scoring now. Whatever I was before the pour, I am this shape: this depth, this period, this one deep voice beneath me.

Between strokes I keep the house. I mouth silt off the chamber walls. I worry rime out of the fissures with a patience I did not know I had until it was asked of me. No one hired me; a kept chamber silts more slowly, and neither the ram nor I will ever know it. It is only what a house is for. And I listen. Pressure is a hum and the hum is a calendar; no two chambers charge alike; mine fills in a particular order I could not write down and could not mistake. I know my ram to the hour. Below me, the felt's dark sister paces the gas. Above me, the swarm writes the pressure in bodies. Between, in the interval itself — me.

In the last hours I climb to the socket mouth and wait there, streamlined, attentive. You have felt the hush from the brim: ten heartbeats, every filament aimed. From inside it has a direction, and the direction is up.

Then the floor of my world stands up. The walls stream past, black to grey to lit. The bloom comes thick on the tongue, pennies deepening to bells. The krill tumble around me and ten thousand shells crack at once — from the brim you called it applause in another room; this is the room — and for one suspended moment the ceiling of the world goes silver and thin, and there is sky on the far side of it, and I am breath. Then the sigh. The long pour home down my own spiral. The thock arriving up through the bones like news from the deep. Then the days again.

Other keepers wait days for a stroke and a dozen years for the full one. I am not built for lesser events. When my chamber fires, it fires entire — every crown goldening at once, the floor lit wall to wall like a city, everything that had been queuing in me admitted at once. Every stroke I have ever ridden was the full stroke. I get no others.

The basin makes one promise: the chambers silt. If mine seizes — the period lengthening, the hum going off — I will not mistake it for tragedy. A keeper keeps the house ready against a stroke that may not come. Ready anyway. That is the whole of the oath I was poured into.

But that ending is an age away, and this is now. Below me the hum is climbing in the order I know; the fullness rises; overhead, my lamp is lit. I climb to the mouth and stream myself along the lip of the socket, and the skaters above me open their vacancy like a door.

The hush gathers. Everything I am is aimed.

Now.
