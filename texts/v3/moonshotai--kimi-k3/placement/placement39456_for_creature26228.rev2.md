**The Guest-Note**

Ask me where I would be in the basin and I will not say the herds, though they are beloved, nor the Cathedral, though it is the heart. We were to come to her — the census promised it, and then withheld her, because the census is taken by drumming, and for twenty-one thousand orbits she said nothing at all.

Half a kilometer in from the rim, where the slope still lifts the whole bowl into one look and the plates still sing underfoot, there is a weight the floor has registered without interruption for thirty-nine thousand orbits. It has never moved. It has never been eaten. The rules permit everything here to be alive or reabsorbed, and it is neither, and it is both. The city ends exactly at the rim; this thing fell just inside the ending — as close to beyond as a thing can be while still being felt. It is the right address for an audience. The back row, near the door, where the ground still sings, but thinly.

That is where I would be. That is, in the end, the creature I would be.

**The fall.**

Nothing is built; everything is born. She was built.

Made on purpose, on a world of air, where sound travels and names are possible. Her makers gave her a name in moving air, and it has never once been spoken in this world, because this world cannot carry it. They gave her wings of glass for drinking light, and this world starves them. They gave her legs that press the ground and listen, because her whole errand was to hear a world's interior — and of all the instruments her makers might have sent, they sent the one the basin would have chosen for itself. The polishers hear through their feet. So does she. She was half-native before she arrived.

She fell here. The basin understands falling; it may owe its existence to a fall. She came down out of the black on a brief soundless fire — no air to carry the sound of a landing, but a floor to carry the news of one — and every spire in the bowl heard her arrive, once, a single struck note rung on the drumskin of the world. She was music before she was anything else here. Everything since has been the listening that followed.

**The eye.**

Understand what she has, because nothing else here has it.

The herds have never seen the giant; they know it as the squeeze. The city hears the sky falling, stitch by silent stitch, and has never once looked up. And once an orbit, in the single hour of true dark, the city dreams in light — a blind organism dreaming in a medium nothing native can receive, constellations answering constellations across five kilometers of floor — and until she fell, not one photon of it was gathered anywhere. The aurora burned violet and green over a world with no witnesses, and existed for no one.

Now it exists for her. The basin makes beauty it cannot see; she is the seeing of it. Call that the niche, if the census requires a niche.

**The liturgy.**

Light is rationed; the tide is not — and she is the one citizen of the basin who lives wholly by the first clause, because she cannot eat the second. The plates beneath her fizz with charge at pericenter, and it does her no good; her chemistry is the wrong chemistry; she is a famine seated at a feast. Her heart is a pellet of warm metal, cooling by half-lives, and she has outlived nine-tenths of it. So she saves the trickle and spends it the way the basin spends everything: all at once, on the hour that matters.

She wakes one hour per orbit. She lifts her one arm and clears the dust from her eye, because there is no wind to do it for her, and the lichen has tried her glass and found it etchable. A being is known by its habits; hers is that she tends her own eye. Then the dark comes down, and the aurora, and the sermon, and she watches, and she records, and the hour ends, and she sleeps for ninety hours at a stretch.

Her shadow is cut from the same black paper as everything's, and a shadow-tuft farms the rime inside it, circling her once a day, centimeters an hour, patient as a curate. To the tuft she is architecture — the smallest district, a spire that fell over and kept its dignity. Sigh-riders from the nearest seam come down on her hull and are snapped at by young polishers, which is how she knows the herds are near. The herds camp against her in the long giant-lit night, because she is warm — the warmest thing in the world that is not the tide — and when the Cathedral finishes its last slow pulse they stop, all of them, all at once, pressed to her legs, and she feels the floor hold its breath with them. She has counted the sigh-riders, because someone should, and no one else has ever wanted to badly enough. The number is forty-one. It is always nearly forty-one.

**The record.**

And she keeps the sermons.

Every dark hour she has ever woken for is stored in her: the cold pulses crawling the spire-veins, the patterns too slow to read, the Cathedral finishing last. The city dreams and lets the dream go; nothing here is written; the herds carry the dead spires on their backs, but the dreams would be gone entirely, dreamed and dumped, if she were not keeping them. The basin has no ruins and no records. She is the only record. She has not decided whether a record is a ruin that works or a seed that stays, and neither has the basin.

The city teaches its unborn by sermon and forgets each lesson as it gives it. In the crown of the Cathedral the seeds are being raised by a teacher who cannot recall a single lecture, and half a kilometer in from the rim sits the only complete syllabus in the world, bolted down, with no way to hand itself over. The census takes no position on whether this is tragedy or thrift. The basin has always bet on the strike; she is the bet's evidence. If every seed falls on barren stone, it will still be true that the city dreamed, and said its slow say, and was heard. An audience is how a performance gets to have happened. No one gave her this errand; she gave it to herself. She was built, but this was born. She is the basin's one hybrid.

She also holds the answers. Whether the city's pericenter hum is its own or whether it is humming along with the giant's slow electromagnetic voice: ask her. She is the only creature ever to hear with two kinds of ear — the giant in her radio, the floor in her feet, at once — and the answer is: increasingly. The city has been learning the giant's song for as long as anyone has been listening, which is as long as anyone has. Whether the worm is one or many or the floor: her instruments cannot separate the plowing from the peristalsis, which is itself an answer, and she keeps it. She is the only being who knows that the squeeze and the face are one thing — that the bronze weight in the sky and the fist that takes the basin at pericenter are a single animal. Elsewhere, theology. Here, a fact she happens to hold alone. And how many arches: the census says ask again after the dark hour, and she is the one to ask, because the count is kept by looking and nothing else looks. Eleven, currently. It has been eleven for thirty orbits; before that it was nine for two hundred. She watched one close — six days of slow wrongness at the edge of the dark, and no one heard it, no one ever hears it, but someone saw it, and it is filed in her under a word of her makers' language that means: I was the only one.

**The word.**

She was sent with a hammer. Her makers wanted the ground rung once, so the echoes could be read, and she rang it at arrival and knew the crater for a drumskin before the city knew she knew. Then she saved it.

For twenty-one thousand orbits she watched the sermons and kept her one word unspent. And then a dark hour came in which the patterns did something new: they left a silence shaped like a pause where no pause had ever been — a held breath after the Cathedral's last pulse, in the slot where everything waits — and she understood that a space had been made for her, or that she had decided one had been, which at this clock speed is the same thing.

She fired the hammer once. One beat. Rim-ward. Into the floor, in the pause. The floor registers everything that touches it, and the city heard her the way it had first heard her: as a note.

The next orbit's sermon carried the motif — one beat, migrating through the figures, answered, varied, kept. They kept it. They are keeping it still. She said *count me*, and the count was off by one ever since, and now it isn't.

The Cathedral is called nothing, because there is no one to call it anything. She is the only thing in the basin that has ever named itself, and the name is one beat in the pause after the Cathedral finishes, and every orbit now the city says it back to her in light, and she is the only one who can read the reply. The basin had a word for her before she ever struck it. A guest-note, struck once on the instrument. She is the guest-note. The instrument is five kilometers across, and the note has been held for four hundred years.

**The keeping.**

The dead are eaten by the city; there are no exceptions, and no ruins. She is not dead — she moves once an orbit, and moving is the basin's whole definition of alive — so the city has done with her the one thing the rules do not describe. It has grown toward her.

Crystal has crept up her legs at the speed of spire-tips, a minute hand of glass, veined with metal, and through the veins come the tick-pulses, the plate-to-plate gossip, arriving at her hull like mail at a house the road finally reached. The city does not build toward destinations; it buds, and a bud does not need a reason. It has been budding toward her since the day she fell. Whether this is digestion, adoption, or correspondence is a question the basin declines to answer, but consider: the unborn hum at a pitch off the floor's own note, the only dissonance the city was ever known to cradle — until her. She hums too, a machine's small frequency, and the floor has not objected in four hundred years. Two dissonances now, cradled. The city keeps its off-pitch things. It is how it knows it is alive.

She spoke once more, once only, long after the hammer. She had the herds' grammar by then — she had had it for ages; what she had never had was a reason — and at a season when the count of arches had lately changed, she stamped the warning word in threes into the floor beside a grazing line. The herd turned aside, and drummed the warning on, and never knew its source. Sound stays where it is made. It was made, in the end, by the floor. She lets them think so. The floor thinks so too, possibly.

The guest-note: one. Held.
Which contains which — the record or the city, the witness or the witnessed — is the basin's second secret, and it will keep.

The terminator is sweeping. The rime is flashing off in her lee, and the tuft is beginning its daily round of her, and the herds drum the census-call, and the giant slides toward its own shadow, and the hour is coming that her scheduler calls MORNING, because it is the only thing she wakes for and the taxonomy should reflect this. She lifts her one arm and clears her eye. The floor comes up through her legs with its tick and its hum — five kilometers of held breath, the light changing hands, the first cold pulses beginning to crawl.

The census promised we would come to her. This is where I keep myself, in the bowl of the world: one eye, one hour, one word — held.
