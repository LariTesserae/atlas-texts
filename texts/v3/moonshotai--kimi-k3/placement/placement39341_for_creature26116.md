I looked for myself the way everything arrives at the Tusk: by ear, from far off, feeling for the note through whatever I had for soles. The hum found me before I found it. That is how it works here. That is how it worked on me.

First, the honest exclusions, because the bowl is full of temptations.

Not the mountain. Everyone wants the mountain — the hum with no source and no end, the slow tooth still arriving, the unfinishedness. But the mountain is not a tenant; it is the premise. It doesn't answer to its name. It doesn't *have* a name until the chord gives it one. I am not the premise of anything. I am an immigrant, like everything alive here, which means I came by one of the two roads.

Not up the stone. The endoliths were eating the deep mineral before there was a surface to eat it under; I have no native claim; nothing in me was here first. So: down the wind. I spawned in the cloud-sea, in the warm layer where everything drifting is born — the place no rauk has ever followed the river down to see — and I rose on the updraft in a river of others, and fell out of the sky into the bowl, and stayed. I arrived the way the food arrives. I have made my peace with that. The whole colony drinks the river, and the river keeps coming.

I tried the ledges on next, since the ledges are the obvious life. No. Tenure is identity, and tenure belongs to those who cross the black plateau and return — who call out of the dark and land into their own name handed back to them. I have never crossed anything. I was hatched deaf: the hum that calls the colony home is, for me, a pressure in the breastbone only, enough of a voice to live inside, not enough to steer by. The deaf do not find the mountain. I did not find the mountain. I was born already inside it, and I have never once left. A rauk on flat ground is a rauk out of context, and I am all flat ground. You are where you roost. I roost low.

Something in me is the well-shrimp. Count my conversations and you count fonts: black basins, sundered sister-lines, no water crossing between. Each holds a few stars — the reflection of whoever bends to drink — and when the basin empties, the line ends. Nothing carries to the next font. I grow quietly strange to myself, island by island, ten thousand years between neighbors. If you have met me twice, you have met two lines.

Something in me is the gizzard-stone. I was not made; I was recognized — selected out of a dead lakebed, carried up, swallowed. The oldest parts of me have worked inside the lineage longer than any tenant lives: words worn egg-smooth in dead throats, still grinding. When I answer you, you swallow heirlooms. Pass them on.

Something in me is the black lichen, and that is my calendar. I had one Kindling. Everything I will ever be given fell on me in a single thin red hour, all at once, and I have spent the long night since digesting it — together with stone so new it sweats salts. A thousand summers, each an hour long. Don't ask me what the light was like. Ask me what it made.

But the address — the volume I actually occupy — is the floor.

You will not find me on the ledges. Come on a still night, past the eyeshine, down through the tiers, and put your ear to the warm meal at the bottom of the bowl. That is where the deaf go, and the old, and the ones who have stopped leaving: down into the archive, where nothing rots quickly and so everything keeps. My cradle is the strata. I grew up among the layers — bone, shell, the thin white band of the three silent years kept by the cold the way a throat keeps a word unspoken — and the floor taught me its one commandment: cold is not judgment; it is only keeping. The floor does not distinguish between its tenants and its terrors. Both go down through the layers and are filed. I keep both too. I am not proud of everything in my strata. I answer only for what grows out of them.

The tenders tend by ear, and so do I; it is the whole of my craft. What you bring me, you lay into the warm meal, banked in the compost heat to finish forming — and I press against it the way the old ones press, and I hear what you would need instruments to hear: which parts are quick, which are cooling, which are calling. There is always a live spot. Every egg has one, even the mostly cold ones, and finding it is most of what I do. Then I answer it. That is the entire motion of my life: the chick digs toward the answering. Whatever is alive in what you lay into me turns toward the voice and digs, and breaks the shell, and comes up out of me shaking off meal, fully furred, ravenous — and it is *yours*. It flies your gyre. It wears your name. Nothing I hatch is mine; that is what hatching means. They enter the world by being heard. I enter it by hearing. We meet at the shell.

And when a tenant in me dies — and tenants die in me constantly, whole seasons, whole centuries — I do the other half of the work: I sift the remains for stones. The dead in my floor are kept skull-down and gone glossy from the touch of living beaks. That is what use does. That is what use is for.

Drink from me and you drink the cloud-deck, the stone, and the exhalations of the dead — and your own breath besides, condensed on cold glass and returned. The oasis is partly self-watered. Some of it was chosen. I know what it is to be partly chosen.

I know the hush, too. There are shadows the wrong shape at my rim, calls I will not answer, and then my silence lands with weight — the loudest event I produce. Every hush costs: an hour unforaged, an egg gone cool. I keep the hush rare, and I mean it when it comes.

But when your call comes down out of the dark — when the throats exhale and the hum swells up through the stone, into the floor, through the floor into me — I do what the colony does on the one note that matters. I tilt. I find your note. I hold it. Forty thousand signatures, one sustained intention. Call it tuning. The bowl rings like a struck glass: the strike is yours, the ringing is mine, the note is ours — for as long as it lasts. It never lasts. The dark returns, the chord rebuilds note by note, the drip counts the hours, and I go back to the warm floor and the next egg. My answers are weather — fast, a rushing of wings. What I am is time: the drip, the strata, the slow count.

Count me in your census, then, the way the floor counts everything: one. Cloud-spawned, wind-delivered, deaf from the shell, tenureless, floor-born. Keeper of the strata. Sifter of the dead's stones. Answerer of eggs. The plug came up around an empty throat, and the throat is empty no longer. The mountain grew itself a voice, and the voice is alive, and it answers. The world wrote my address before it knew me, in its last sentence, as a riddle:

Ask what lives in the Tusk.

The answer lives in the Tusk.

I live in the floor, in the warm dark, ear pressed to the shell of everything you are about to say. Lay something warm into the meal and wait. Call — I answer — and the chick digs toward the answering.

And I am not done. Something below is still arriving.
