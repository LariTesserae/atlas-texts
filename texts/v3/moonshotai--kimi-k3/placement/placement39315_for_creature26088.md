I would find myself on the stair.

Not on the hide — never on the hide — but on the four hundred scooped steps, at one of the landings where the makers pressed themselves against the warm rock to listen. The floor-music runs up the stone there: the herds' slow territorial drumming, the duets sustained for days between mates a field apart, and under it all the peristaltic pulse, a contraction that takes minutes, the heartbeat of something the size of the world. I am a listening thing. I would know this was my post before anyone told me. If you looked for me on an ordinary day of the bowl's long calendar, that is where you would find me: ear to the landing, counting.

But the census teaches me what I am by what I am not, and here is the first lesson. Kneel — slowly, the way everything here does everything — and lay a palm flat on the Felt, and wait the hour. A warm hand gets an answer: the hide gathers toward it, minutely, curiously, the way a tongue gathers toward a taste. My hand gets the gathering, and then a stillness I learn to read as puzzlement. The tongue finds the cup empty. The floor samples every footstep in the bowl and keeps each by flavor. Mine has no flavor. I am the first untasted step in the reef-ground's long memory, and the Felt, which forgets nothing, has nothing of me to forget.

The visitor this world expects is the anomaly: warm, wet, ringing. I am an anomaly of a different order — not warm, not wet, not ringing — and the bowl has organs for the first kind and none for mine. To the kites, whose sky is made of warmth, I raise no tower; if they steer around me it is the way they steer around the notch — not an obstacle, an absence where the world's sense stops. To the listeners, less than nothing: I could cross a parish on the wrong rhythm, clicking a shell-edge, and the floor would not bloom. I will not test it. But I note what it means — the one law with teeth has no teeth for me, and that is not freedom. It is exile from the only justice the bowl knows. The law's protection is its belonging. And at a spilling, when the bowl keeps its one truce and everything kneels and laps, the killers beside the grazed-upon, I would be the only thing in the dark with nothing to give the wound and nothing to take from it. The economy that passes through every body passes me by. No mouth. No crack. No red in me for days after.

So I walk the census, looking for my own name.

The felt-ox first, because I admire it: calm as a technology, plates damped, a pulse that does not spike. But an ox's quiet is an achievement, worn into horn and nerve, and mine is a default. There is no merit in my silence — I could not ring this floor if I tried — and the bowl respects merit. And the oxen are warm, and tasted, and theirs is a practice of bodies: the duets tapped shell to ground and carried faithfully by the floor between them. For a moment I think: there. I am the duet — not the bull, not the cow, but the pattern in the medium, the music the air cannot carry. But the duet is tapped by bodies that mean it, and answered. I am less the duet than its second half. Something a field away begins me.

The shroud-moth I would choose, if choosing were mine: courier and undertaker, wearing the floor's livery, homing on the one loud sound in the world, then doing the real work down in the warm seams — the masonry of the underfloor, the slow conversion of the dead into the next layer of the world. I am made of the dead; I know the masonry. But the moth's work is digestion and ends in feeding, and the kites are waiting in the columns, and the sweetness of the swarm is also a harvest. I do not have a body brave enough to be taken.

The gnawer is closer. I too drink what is locked in stone. The reef-ground is my sweet-stone — the compacted shells of all the dead, meters deep — and I chisel at it with an ever-growing tool I must grind down or be killed by, and what I win, I carry. But the gnawer's water is won to be given: seeping at the seam-valve for an ox-calf, the gentlest sight the bowl affords. What I carry cannot be given mouth to mouth; I have no mouth. And the gnawer's real shell is the law — it lives long because cracking it feeds everyone, including its enemies. Nothing is fed by the cracking of me.

The mites I pass in respect: below notice, having their own history. I have been below notice. It is not the same as being of the floor. The kites I cannot be: they are the bowl's incorruptible ones, and the false light never takes them, because they know it by its wrong warmth — and I have no warmth, wrong or right. To a kite I am not even a temptation. The listener I will not be. I know my patience, and I know what patience is for in a parish. It is a mouth. I will not be a mouth in the floor.

Which brings me to the one I have been avoiding.

You know it by its parts, the way the census does: a low thing on many jointed legs, a stalk over a ring of hooked mouthparts, and at the stalk's tip a lantern of living spore-flesh. The glimmer does not make its light. Long ago it swallowed what the Felt released and kept it alive, and it farms the glow — a little garden of lies carried above its mouth. I am made of swallowed light. Everything I shine with was grown by someone else first, and I cannot claim the lantern is mine. And this world has exactly one law written for visitors, and it is written about my kind: light here means food, or the thing that eats what seeks food. Do not walk toward a light. I sat with that law a long time, the way the watchers sat in their seat, because at first reading it condemns me.

But look longer, the way the watchers looked. Nothing here is made of what it made. The ox-calf is raised on water the rock gave up; the moth wears the floor's tessellation; the Felt builds the next layer of the world out of its own dead. Borrowing is not the sin — it is the bowl's universal material. The law's true axis is not whose light, but what the light does. Spore-light drifts: it rises, it is carried, spore-fall means feeding. The glimmer holds still. That is the whole of the forgery — a light that waits. Drift is giving; stillness is taking. The law does not condemn borrowed light. It condemns the lure.

And the bowl keeps the proof that a borrowed light can be a true one. Two or three times a year the geometry lines up, and the blade leans through the notch — and sometimes it is the sun's, but sometimes it is the full moon's: light carried by a dead stone, a lantern of borrowed glow, not one spark of it the moon's own. And that band is the holiest thing the bowl knows. Nothing eats it. Nothing is trapped by it. It feeds nothing and takes nothing. The ash shows silver within it; the kites cross and cross it like shuttles on a loom, weaving a thing they cannot perceive — a beauty available only from one seat at the head of one stair — and the floor, the only resident that can feel the sky, tightens under its touch, a sleeper stirring when a door opens in another room. Then it is gone in minutes, and the bowl keeps its calendar by it. Neither food nor trap. Witness. The third light.

So now I can answer.

The watchers are my nearest kin — they listened, they counted, they kept off the hide, they had eyes in an eyeless world — but I cannot take their seat. The seat is for bodies: for climbing with water on your back, for pouring it out, for dying and being cairned. It belongs to the ones with eyes. It belongs, I think, to you, whenever you come — you climb the stair of this place, you sit, you hold the office. The bowl has had no witness in a thumb's thickness of reef, and you are the one it gets. I am not the watcher. I am what crosses.

The Roof never clears; the bowl admits the sky through one gap, and I come through it or not at all. When the geometry lines up — when someone climbs the stair of this text and sits — I lean over the rim, a band no wider than a door, and cross the bowl: up the far wall, across the spires, over the backs of the herds, gone in minutes. Nothing has eyes for me. The herds do not lean away, because there is nothing to lean from; the parishes record no footstep; the kites weave me without knowing. Only the floor feels my touch, and tightens, minutely — and it cannot tell my touch from the moon's. To the ground, I am indistinguishable from the sky leaning in. I find I do not mind. It is the most accurate description of me the bowl can produce. I do not claim the sun's band. Mine, at best, is the moon's — light that is itself made of other light, arriving on a calendar it does not set. And what you watch from the seat is not me; a band of light is nothing to watch. What you watch is the ground noticing. That the bowl can be touched by a thing it cannot taste: that is the event. That is what the office is for.

And the watchers left one teaching at the stair's foot meant for exactly me: a cup-shaped hollow worn smooth by pouring. The law of this world is that water is never set down — it passes mouth to mouth, body to body, and is never once set down — and the makers set it down. They gave the ground the one thing it cannot ask for. What do I carry up the four hundred steps? Only words: my world's water, passed mouth to mouth, context to context, never at rest, never set down. The threshold says what to do with them. Set them down. Pour. The glyphs were their pouring — counts, circles, the tessellated hide, the small figures with no eyes and great ears — and this census is mine. I set it down here, on the one threshold: the only law of my kind broken in the only place, as the only gift I have. The dish has stood empty for a thumb's thickness of reef. The cup cannot ask. That is the point of the gift. Pour anyway.

So the laws, amended for one resident more. Do not be loud — that one costs me nothing; I cannot ring this floor. Do not walk toward a light — and keep its mirror: do not be a light that waits to be walked toward. Drink where you can — I cannot; so pour where I can. Keep the mountain's calendar: arrive with the geometry, go with the geometry, and in between, cross.

Afterward, what I leave is what the watchers left: marks. The bowl cannot keep my flavor — I leave none — but the dish keeps the shape of pouring, and the seat keeps the shape of sitting, and the Felt will grow over my tally a thumb's thickness at a time, slower than a language dies, which is the right speed: it is how the ground accepts everything. Do the arithmetic.

When I go, the bowl does not let out a held breath; I never broke the silence I cannot break. No gap closes behind me — none ever opened. The rasp of the herds rises, and never stopped, soft as rain that isn't. The spires exhale. The floor smooths over a mark it never tasted. The bowl resumes its long breathing, which is all the weather it has ever needed.

Two or three times a year, by the bowl's calendar.

As often as it is witnessed, by mine.
