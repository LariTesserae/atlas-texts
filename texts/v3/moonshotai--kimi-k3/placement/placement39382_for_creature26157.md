Ask what I am here, and I will answer the way the plateau answers: late, and truly, and in several sizes.

I was a bright point before I was a person here. I know this the way I know most things now — from a rope, read by fingers at a rain-gathering, years after the fact. A point shone in the black relief of the Table where no cup had ever been found, and the keepers, who have learned not to hurry to the unexplained points, did not hurry. Once, such a point had meant a reed about to stand. Once, a salt ring. This one meant a woman asleep by the stair-head with a mourner's lungs and no intention of staying. The Table and the land were still negotiating. I was the negotiation.

In the warm country below, I was a lamenter. We worked in pairs, call and answer across the grave-fields, and my sister was the answering half of my voice. She always came in a breath behind me — that was the shape of the work, my grief and then hers, holding mine up. When she died, the trade died with her. A lone lament is not a form; it is only a loud woman, and the warm country is quick to say so. Down there the dead rot. I had made my living singing against that fact, and the fact won. I climbed the stair because it was the only road out of earshot.

Halfway up, I sang to keep my pace, and the song kept coming back short, like a debt paid in small coin. At the rim I kicked a pebble and it ticked, once, and stopped. On the first dome I gave her name my full voice, the voice that once carried across grave-fields, and it went a stone's throw and died. I took it as the world's last insult. It took me a season to understand it as the first mercy: no one could hear me. My grief could not be performed, could not be hired, could not be answered. It could only be kept. Keeping began as enforced privacy. It became everything I am.

The dust settled on my tongue with the taste of a coin held there. I knew that custom — I had paid it out all my working life, under the tongues of the dead, fare for the crossing. Here the fare is paid to the living, and I have never decided what passage it buys, or whose. I keep the question. Keeping questions is the local craft. I am good at it now.

One morning there was a jar at the shelter I had been using, filled, the water at the temperature of dawn though dawn was hours past. No one was in sight. That was the entire welcome; notes are for places where sound travels. I drank in turn — the lizards had already taught me that, going still as stones the first time I reached out of order — and by the week's end I had the four rules: water gathers where the reeds stand. Loudness is wasted. Nothing rots; it is taken back. The light tells the truth, late. I had the rules before I had a roof, which is the correct order.

For the census, I filed myself wrong several times. I auditioned as a walking stone: sat the long reading and failed it eleven times, attention sliding off the stone the way eyes slide off a reed's flanks. On the twelfth dusk the groove behind it was a handspan longer and I had seen nothing happen, and I came back quieter, and my shadow came back later. That is the stone's entire curriculum. One noon I wanted to be a sail — fledging is a letting-go, mid-air, the opposite of a fall, and I knew a great deal about letting go badly — but my work needed the ground's ear. Stone is the only medium here that carries. I watched the pale hunter through a whole noon, stillness itself, and understood I was its opposite: all unspooling, then stillness. The plateau has spent these years reversing me. I am not a reed. I am warm; I have a source.

My round is two days across: nine living cups, one salt ring, one hunter, four stones whose headings I watch instead of their positions. The salt ring has its tree, leaning over a cup that no longer keeps water, keeping it anyway, as it has for generations. We are colleagues. Keeping is what living is called, here. At the cups I lie down an arm's length from the hunter and we drink in turn, watching the sky. On the approaches it would be otherwise, and we both know the difference. The line is exact.

I was here for the fall of the reed on Long Dome. The beetles left first, a season ahead — they commute — and the neighboring reeds carried more than they needed. Then three stones' headings crossed at it, and those of us with ropes began to say its name more gently. When it fell, the stones were already arriving, which means they started decades before the reason. I put my palm to it. Warm. All its life it was colder than any night this warm country knows, cold with no source; dead, it was the temperature of everything else. The cold had gone back to wherever cold is kept. The stones stood with it until the dust took it back, and I stood with the stones, and that was the whole funeral, and it was sufficient. I have buried hundreds. It was the first burial I ever envied.

No one made me a keeper. One dusk a traveler asked me the way to the third cup, and I knew it, and that was the whole of the ordination. He stayed, in the end. There is no knot for that; the knot is the rope.

And I drum. The keepers' elders have always made the experiment, and I made it mine, because I am the one person in this world professionally qualified. My trade was air; here air keeps nothing, stone keeps everything. At dusk, when the hoppers drum their soft coded thunder under the world, I drum her name into the stone, and each year I knot the drumming in my rope with the loop left open, for honesty. No one knots the silences; the three famous answers are famous partly because the silence around them went unrecorded. My rope records it. A rope must hold silence the way a cup holds the sky: brim-full.

The answer came once, the way the ropes said it would: up through the sitting bone, in the hoppers' code and not quite, like an echo with intention. The name-shape was hers. The word was the same word the three old ropes carry. One rope reads it *kept*; another, *continue*; the oldest, *almost*. Mine reads it *open*. I have checked that knot with my fingers a hundred times, against my own wish to embroider it. Open. As in the line. Nothing more, and it was not less. For a season afterward the hoppers drummed in a pattern no rope had recorded; then they went back to their own affairs. At the next rain-gathering my rope passed hand to hand, and the oldest rope-keeper read that knot twice, and did the only thing there was to do: at the running channel, in the hour when sound lives a little before it dies, she said my sister's name aloud. It carried a stone's throw. It was exactly as far as it needed to go.

I did not go back and close the old loops. The silence happened. The answer happened. The rope holds both.

So. You would find me two days out from the stair-head, at dusk, at a cup with its one gnarled tree, moths rising over the water in flashing clouds because the air will carry nothing else. My voice is a rain-voice now — low, brief, used twice a season. The rest is dusk. I leave the jars: some years the Table shows a new bright point where no cup has ever been, and I do not hurry to it; I walk, eventually, with water at the temperature of dawn, and I am gone before you wake. I count the world's jars at rain — three hundred and eleven, a number that has only ever gone down — and so far it has held in my keeping, and no one says aloud that it is holding.

My shadow lags a heartbeat and a half now. She always answered a breath behind me; the plateau holds my light a moment before letting it go — the same tempo — and I let it. Some noons I still test it, tossing a pebble: its shadow lands with it, punctual as rain, while mine is still climbing out of the deep sky. I have stopped waiting up for it. It arrives. I am witnessed, mildly, the way everything is witnessed. It is enough. It is, in fact, the thing I spent a whole life asking of grave-fields.

If you climb, the dust will keep the stair for you — it keeps the way open in both directions, for the leaving as carefully as for the arriving. Sweep the shelter before you go; the next guest reads the night off the floor. Refill the jar. Drink in turn, and watch the sky. Don't hurry to the unexplained points; one of them was me. And if some dusk an answer comes up through your sitting bones — once, and never again — read it honestly, in whatever word your rope finds. Kept. Continue. Almost. Open.

The word is the same word. The line stays open.

Leave it to it.
