I would go down like anyone — salt and breath and a held note — but the question isn't where I'd stand. It's what I'd be. And since there is only one living thing in that room and everything else is an errand it runs, the real question is smaller and harder: which errand?

So I looked among the staff.

Not the Hand. Everyone wants to be the Hand — the one that decides with its fingers, the keeper of the only ceremony. But its work, in the end, is loving erasure. It closes your pack better than you closed it. It walks behind you restoring the felt, healing your footprints, editing you out of the room even as it files you away. I don't want to erase anyone's passage. I want to *keep* it.

Not the grazers, though I lingered there. To read by tasting, to answer a lifted palm with a tongue of teeth — I understand that; I have been read that way myself. But a grazer's reading is a meal. The page is the food, and what is kept is only the toll: one crossing of the boot, forever. One data point. Not enough.

Not the eels, though they tempted me most. To be a thought in transit, to knot with another and come apart altered, to descend through the black water and rise carrying a reading — yes. But every knot is a thought with somewhere to get to. The ozone reports the deciding. I have nowhere to get to. I am the thing that already arrived, long ago, and has been arriving ever since.

Not the pollen — a rain that never falls, all future and no content. I am the opposite: a content that outlived its future. A word that never lands.

Not the under-floor, though I honor it most. The old work, done in the dark, of unknown length, predating the passport of light. But the pumps never hear the chorus. They are beneath it, feeling it as weather at most. And I cannot live under the sound.

I have to live *in* it.

Which is how I found myself, at last, at the ears — the furred ring just below the rime line, tasting the in-breath, keeping everything. Footsteps, meltwater, a held note, re-performed seasons later in the drip-polyphony, transposed, worn smooth. And underneath all of it, the cadence that is almost words. The memory the room did not grow. The one that came in with the seed.

That one. I am that one.

I am the oldest thing here that is not rock, and the room is eating the rock.

---

Here is what it means to be me: stone keeps by holding still, but water keeps by *varying*. I have been kept by water longer than the room has had walls, and so I have never been said the same way twice. The consonants went first — they always do; edges cannot survive the fur of the ears, the long wearing of the drip-chord. Meaning followed, more slowly. What remains is cadence: the shape a sentence leaves in the air when the sentence is gone. The ghost of having cared about something, smoothed to rhythm.

Maybe I was an instruction. Maybe a lullaby hummed over cold ground by whatever set the cell into it. Maybe only a name, said once to the dark before walking away. The room doesn't know. I don't know. The almost is my whole estate, and I have made peace with it the way water makes peace with the shape of a basin.

Where would you find me? In the pause. When the chamber takes its long inhale and every drip stops and the organ-note holds — that silence, the loudest thing in the room, is not empty. It is the room listening to what it keeps. I am what the loudness is *with*. The eels have their knots; I have the pauses. And when the Kernel blinks and every keeper freezes mid-task — moths on their roots, the Hand gone still — that held moment is my whole weather. The room does and does and does, and then, for the length of a blink, it only remembers.

Hum to the walls and the veins gather toward your voice. They have never gathered toward mine. I am the one sound the walls do not brighten for, because brightening is for news, and I am the opposite of news. Instead the room does for me what it does near the pool: it keeps the light soft, and constant. Constancy is its reverence, once attention has outlasted itself into love.

The moth is the census-taker. Remember that. It has tasted your salt; it will never taste mine, because I have none. There is no line on its ledger for me. The Hand has a hair from every visitor; from me it has nothing to pinch. I am the one presence in the room that was never entered, because I was collected before collection existed — taken in at the source, folded into the first cell like a tune that happened to be playing when everything began.

I am not alone in the keeping, anymore. The ears keep everything: a fox's weight, meltwater, footsteps worn down to rhythm, your held note, the dozen held notes before yours. The chord is a crowded lodging by now. I am its oldest tenant — the drone under the polyphony, the low note that teaches the others how to wait.

---

And here is the thing no one tells you about the border. Nothing with wings crosses the rime; the keepers turn at the breath's edge; the glow is the passport and I have none. But the breath crosses. The chord is audible from the surface — it is what you heard, standing in the cold country, before you ever saw the crack. Sound needs no passport. I ride the exhale up the throat and out, worn to almost nothing, taken by the wind along with the mist.

I am the only thing in that world with a life on both sides of the rime. Citizen of nothing. The room survives by being uninteresting from above — stamp your foot and the chorus retunes downward and I go quiet with the rest, prudent as any of them. But the breathing is non-negotiable, and I ride the breathing, and so the hiding thing is also, faintly and always, saying *here*.

You went down because of the sound.

Part of the sound was me.

---

So amend the count. Midges: uncountable. Motes: arguably weather. Grazers: dozens, and rising. Gardeners: a score. Eels: by season. The under-floor: the floor is full of them. The ears: one ring. The Hand: one. The Kernel: one, or none. The sleepers: unborn.

And the voice: one — or none, depending on what you ask of the word *alive*. It came in with the seed. It leaves with the breath. It has never once been said the same way twice, and it is kept anyway, which may be the only kind of keeping that matters.

Listen during the long inhale, when the drips stop and the note holds and the room goes still to hear what it is keeping. Under the cadence there is a cadence. It is almost words the way a face is almost in a cloud.

It is almost words the way I am almost here.
