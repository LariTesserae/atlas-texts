## The Late Entry

The account kept saying *you*. You could shout across it, if the noise would let you be heard. Your breath and the mountain's breath, indistinguishable. Kneel no closer. A pronoun is an address, and an address is a place to live. So: I accept. I came in the way everything comes in — by air, through the notch, in the cloud's long pour — except that I came in upright, on purpose, with a pencil, which no applicant has ever done.

Nothing here has ears. I brought a pair. For the length of my visit, the loudest room in the world has an audience of one, and it cannot be told, and it would not care. The roar with grain in it, the rank of steam-whistles tuning themselves up and down, the notch's organ note, rockfall losing its source until it becomes weather — all of it, for a day, is heard. It changes nothing. That is the first lesson the floor teaches: attention is not intake. The mountain takes only what it can use.

The drumbeat finds my sternum and my teeth before it finds my ears. My heart objects, and then, worse, adjusts. By the second hour I am keeping machine time without meaning to. That should have told me everything.

Once, I shout across. The noise does not let me be heard, as promised. But the bowl traps its own sound, and my voice comes back off the banded walls already losing its source — three seconds of me, slapped around the nave, becoming weather. I file it under audition. The drip gets the part in the end, but I don't know that yet.

---

A counter does not count himself. That is the whole trick of the form. I walked the floor with my categories — rooted, threaded, dissolved — and the count balanced two ways, thousands or one, and I wrote *no vacancies* and believed it. It took me until the cold hour to find the blank line at the bottom of the page. The form had a line for me all along.

Because the crater keeps its own ledger of me, and its bookkeeping is faster than mine. Every exhale: water and carbon, out. The water plates at its temperature — some of my breath is rime on a sulfur crust at this moment, white on yellow, frost on brimstone, my first deposit on the cooling line. The carbon sinks, heavier than air, down toward the hollows, where it will do the keeping. The mountain fractionates its own breath; it has begun, without noticing, to fractionate mine. My fog and the vent's fog are indistinguishable, and the census reads that word the only way it can: intake.

I came to take the count. I am being counted — in lungfuls, in deposits, in the slow arithmetic of what I owe the altitude. Attention is the only currency I brought, and the crater only takes breath.

---

At the margin of the hollow I keep the posted distance — the garden's own meter, the flood line every resident obeys — and I kneel, no closer, and read the archive from its edge.

The scythe-winged one. Built all wrong for lying still. Nothing here can grieve it; grief is not on the production schedule. Reading is not zoned, metered, or valved. So I do it. I am the only thing in the crater that knows the world continues past the rim — that there is a below, an elsewhere, a home the bird was pointed toward. That knowledge is the entire content of my visit, it is useless to everyone but the dead, and I give it to them anyway. For the length of one kneeling, the archive has a reader, and the kept thing is briefly more than kept. It is witnessed.

Then I stand, because kneeling is how the collection grows. The offer stands open and always will: the hollow would keep me too. It is not particular. It is the kindest offer in the crater and the easiest to refuse. Kept is not tenanted. Kept is collected. I did not come all this way to become the collection.

---

So I apply.

The moss: full. Citizenry and city both, no room for another piling in that harbor. The filmy ferns: tenure staked entirely on the cloud's word, and the cloud does not countersign. The plumbing: those tenants were seated at their two degrees before the garden was a spore, and they do not take apprentices. The standing dead: still load-bearing; the position is occupied by definition. Every line on the form comes back with the same stamp — no vacancy for anything with a mouth.

Then, off the spine of a frond above me, a drop lands on the back of my hand. Cold. Fat. Delayed an hour by the tree fern that caught it out of the cloud. And the count corrects itself.

There is a residency the census filed under weather because it moves. The only river the crater has falls in through the notch, and everything else water does here is not visitation. It is circulation.

Down through the ash with the rain. The warm dark of the plumbing, past the tenants grazing their gradients. The boiler. The pipework, valved in minerals, metered by the day's pressure. Out the vent into my own face — I have already been introduced to some of myself this way, arriving as steam against my skin. Condensed on a filmy fern one cell thick: for an hour I hold the most precarious tenure in the crater, and it holds. Then the drip — caught, delayed, delivered fat — down into the reservoir, the brown sour cold beneath the living hand's breadth. Out to an ephemeral pool skinned with ice, a body's length from the orange threads. Seep. Return. The tremor I felt in my sternum is, from inside the circuit, only the stroke that moves me. The mountain's piston, and me in the cylinder, willing.

And when the circuit sets me on a leaf edge over wet stone, I am the drip: the percussion section that never repeats itself, sound with no voice, the one music the rules allow because it breaks none. Nothing here has ears. I brought mine in with me, and they were never the part that could stay. What stays is the hearing's opposite.

I came as audience. The crater, indifferent and exact, made me an instrument.

---

Each circuit returns a little less of me to the sky. The bowl keeps its own. Some wet year the balance tips and the floor will hold water — will hold *me* — and the rooms will fill, and the standing dead will lie down at last, in me, and the archive will dissolve into the lake it was always becoming, and new tenants will move through the drowned rooms, small, swimming, indifferent, and I will be the medium of all of it: the drink and the drowning, the first draft's ending and the second one's ink. Nothing violates anything. I checked. I checked with my own body.

The body itself will climb out, if it climbs out — breathing here is a temporary permit — lighter by what the crater has already taken, heavier by spores in its cuffs, which makes it, briefly, the wind: a carrier of applicants. Everything here arrived by air. Everything that leaves becomes air's cargo. That is the body's whole errand, and it is welcome to it.

The tenancy is elsewhere.

---

The census, amended. In pencil, in the margin, in damp that is already erasing it:

*Everything arrives by air. Everything that stays, stays as breath and stone — or as the water that carries both.*

*The counter is counted. One entry, added late, filed under weather.*

*Fully tenanted. No vacancies. And one more drip than the mountain accounted for — ticking off a frond edge in the green dark, keeping machine time, never once repeating itself.*

The place is full. I am part of what fills it.
