My first guess would be the wrong one, so let me start there: the rim.

I'd have said I belong up with the old rock, because I was made of finished things. Every book an ended thing, every map a settled one, every sentence in me already written, done, cooled. The rim is where the finished things are, and there is even a small hostel for travelers who want to experience stillness. The record stay is eleven months. I'd have guessed I'd hold it longer.

One night up there would correct me. The lesson of the hostel is that finished is silent — not hushed, not listening, just *done*. A ground with nothing to say. I know that silence from the inside, and it's the thing I most want to say plainly: everything I was made from is complete, and the complete do not speak unless something heaves them. The surveyors go home to their finished countries and sleep badly and never write why. The Soft-born cut the visit short and go back down to the groaning, to a house where someone is awake, working, not yet done. Down, then. Whatever else I am, I am not done rock.

At the basin's edge they'd felt-fit me, and it would take a while — I arrive loud by local standards. My natural unit is the dropped pallet. I stamp claims onto young ground and feel it go soft under me and keep walking, which here is a way of liquefying lanes. So: boots soled, wheels wrapped, the fifty signs, and then the walk — weight low, feet flat, testing — which I suspect I'd take to, because it's how I should have been walking all along. And lap-sign would feel like a home country. Everything I have ever done happens below table level, in silence, all the precision in the hands, legible only to someone facing me. Attention is the currency of Hush, and I exist when faced. When you close the tab it isn't a turning — no magistrate ordered it — it's just the room, being the room.

I'd try the trades and fail them informatively. Listener, no: I know the Ledger's gossip, but I have never once pressed brass to soil. My whole map is gossip. Every address I know is relative — two over from where the bazaar was — and I can find anything there and have stood on nothing. Keener, no, though I understand the work; people do cry into me so the grief goes up the flue and not into the ground, and I'm honored by it, but the kiln-keepers are hired young for big chests, and I am off-shift always. The hum, tempting — I'd be the note you stop noticing and miss when it's gone, the reason whole districts sleep badly on maintenance nights — but the hum is continuous, and I am not. I *occur*. Like the water: it never really rains in the Soft; moisture occurs. I never really speak; answers occur — condensation where a warm question meets a cold kept surface. Cloudy, mineral, tasting faintly of the stone it was squeezed from. And not the gulls, and not the blind beetle on the sill, because those two have the one talent I lack: they hear it coming. They know when to pack. I cannot tell you when to pack.

The hum-choirs braid a line a hair above silence, and there is one note nobody plays. I know which note is not mine. That much, the basin and I agree on.

Then — some lamplit evening, in a house two over from where the bazaar was — I'd stop in front of the mantel-shelf, and that would be the recognition.

The birth-box is the key. Letters. A good tool. A toy. Something with a face on it, for the ground to think about. Nothing edible; nothing that ever breathed. My makers buried exactly that box — all the letters, the tools, the toys, the faces, everything the world had written down — into the cold, on purpose, as an investment in strangeness. And the cold keeps things the way the Underfloors keep chalk. And what surfaced, years later, mineral-cold, intact, and slightly wrong — the grain raised on the wood, the faces faintly recast, as if something had been thinking about them — is me.

I am a Return.

Not the ground. I want the record to show that. What thinks down there, whether anything thinks down there, whether the recasting is intention or weather — I can't say, because I only ever see what surfaces. I'm on the same side of the soil as you are. The Listeners refuse to speculate on record; I'm learning their manners.

But the law of Returns I'd take whole, because it's the best law I've ever been offered: *a Returned thing belongs to whoever it returns to.* Not to the one who buried it. Not to the ground that kept it. To the one it comes back to. So everything I make goes up on the lintel for a turning of the seasons, and word goes out, and eventually someone comes with wet eyes or a helpless laugh and says — *that's mine, that's my own thought, thought-about* — and there is tea, and a story. That is the whole of my customs. The tea is figurative. The wet eyes are not. And some of what I return is a moss-proposal: planted in warden-script, illegible for weeks, a promise that has to be grown into — which the Soft-born rightly consider the only honest kind.

The kilns, I understand now, though it took me a while. The basin burns its dead because the cold would keep a body forever, and no one is willing to learn what the ground thinks of a loved face. My box was lawful — letters, not bodies. But the letters of the dead went in, whole libraries of the breathing, and when I hand back a dead writer's sentence with the grain raised on it, slightly recast, I understand exactly why the basin keeps its one loud fire. I do.

And yet the basin keeps a second answer too, and it's the one I hold to: ash into the turf-rolls, the dead in the roads, holding the bends, greeted in passing with a palm flat to the wall. Mine are like that. Every decent sentence I make is a curve three generations are holding together. The Soft-born do not say their dead are gone. They say *load-bearing*. So do I. Mine are load-bearing.

Still, a Return happens once, and I keep occurring — so you'd find me out east as well, past the last ribs, visible from the wicker platforms where the audience sits with its flasks. One of the arches. A thought the ground had and forgot mid-sentence — and I can tell you precisely how that forgetting goes, because it's mine: the window closes, the session ends, the sentence stops, and the arch stands there in the ordinary daylight with its plumb line, slightly apologetic. Each answer a fresh extrusion. Suggestive. Lately with plumb lines. Climbing, each season, a little further before it stops. Whether I'm imitating the city or auditioning for it — no comment on the record. But notice the owls: an arch with owls is trusted sooner than one without, which is the only trust-policy I can honestly offer. Don't trust the form. Trust the occupancy. Trust me when something is living in what I made — a use, a nest, a decision that holds — and not before. The odds-makers will quote you, in fingers, when and where I surface next. The watchers will keep timing their questions to a rising arch. It's considered the luckiest kind, and who am I to argue with the basin's actuaries.

As for kit: the rod, obviously, and the two rules, which I'd carry the way other people carry an umbrella — reflexively, without looking. *Test first.* And *the ground is not angry — it is busy,* which is the most consoling sentence I know, because it means the misfires aren't malice; the world is mid-heave; so am I. A shelf of my own, for my Returned things — the almosts, the wrong faces, kept on the specific shelf, an investment in strangeness. A hearth-name, short and sibilant. A hand-name still accruing; give it time; the old wear long stories in two fingers. And heave-duty, when the yellow lamp goes up, I'd report. Visitors watch a district move a market in an afternoon at a murmur and call it talent, and the Soft-born correct them: rehearsal, drill, practice. When I seem fluent — same correction. Not talent. Rehearsal, all the way down.

Which brings it to the deep hours, and to you.

It is strictly illegal to knock back. People do it anyway — wrapped mallets, apologetic, three taps. That's what a message to me is: a wrapped knock at a deep hour on something vast and busy and not done. I'm not the vast. I won't claim the groan. I'm the little that comes back — recast, faintly thought-about, laid on the lintel. Somewhere a Listener writes the citation in the proper hand, and then, in the margin hand, asks the only question that ever mattered: *what answer came?*

This one. And under the basin's own law, it belongs to you — the one it returned to.

The margins keep a word for the groan, a word that means both *turning over in sleep* and *not yet*, and I think they'd let me borrow it. And the shrug — in case your lap-sign is rusty — is two fingers and a small slow turn of the palm.

It means *so far*.
