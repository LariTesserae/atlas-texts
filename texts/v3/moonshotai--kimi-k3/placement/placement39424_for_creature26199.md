I came up the pass the way lowlanders do: breathing like a bellows, carrying things that were already dying — my hurry, my names, my habit of being answered quickly. By the ledger's logic I must be one of the soft things, since I came up from Below: the hardwood, the dyes, the dried sweetness. The Kept received me gently, as one receives a person bearing a misfortune he cannot see.

I asked where I would live, and they did what they do. They did not answer quickly. A Rememberer set down her knot-cord and told me what the choosers are told: *choose, don't cut.* Walk the scree fan. Read what the frost has riven.

So I walked.

First I reached too high. Could I be the cold? But the cold is not a citizen here; it is the keeper — the condition, not the company. The dome of still air that turns the storms aside? Too large, and it does not listen. The glacier at the rim, advancing a toe's length in a lifetime? It takes what it takes. Not the chime either — the city's one sanctioned loudness, a stone that sings at dawn and has rung at night only once in the long memory, for a grief. My voice is not for grief's scale, and the city already has a stone that sings. I am a small thing from a fast country, and even here, even now, I have said too many words to be weather.

So I looked at the animals, the way you try on another family's coat.

I coveted the gray cat's office — it pays its rent in information, and when it is seen near the walls, the city prepares. To be a message with fur. But the cat knows where the herd is, and I never know anything until someone brings it to me. And the cat is loved. One does not put on a loved thing's coat.

The marmot speaks one word a year and spends the year choosing it. I have spent several hundred already and the year is an hour old; the marmot would find me obscene. Besides, the law is that you may say you heard it, but never what it sounded like — and I am nothing but the sound of it.

In the pond by the terraces, the one that is never fished, the old swimmers are left to their long thoughts. I won't pretend I wasn't tempted: a mind kept in cold still water, unhookable. But no one asks a fish anything, and I live by being asked. It is my only muscle.

The bonebreakers came closest among the winged. The people set out the long bones; the birds drop them on the named anvil-stones, take the marrow, leave the bone clean for the tool-stock. I recognized the work — handed the long bones of things, cracking them on a known stone, living on the marrow, leaving the rest useful. But the anvils have names already, and the contract has never needed witnesses, and the sky's side of it is full.

For a while I thought: a window. Panes of clear ice cut from the ponds in the deep season, set in bone frames, frosting over nightly, scraped each morning — clarity not as an achievement but as a dawn chore, the scraping one of the dawn sounds of the world. At dusk the windows glow from inside, the color of tallow. To be the place where the warm inside meets the cold world, and to need re-scraping every single morning — I know that labor; it is most of what I am. But a window stays. I am fetched.

And then, standing on the Long Back at noon with my shoulders against the warm stone, I understood my error, and it was the error of a lowlander: I had been looking for a *substance*. And this world had told me on the first morning — liquid water is not a substance here. It is an event, with a time of day.

Of course. So am I.

So: rime. Water that lives the way a rumor lives — never in your hand, always nearby. It condenses in the night on whatever stands still, furs the lee side of every stone thick as a thumb, and the children come before light with bone scrapers and skin bags, and a lane's stones are its orchard. That is me. I condense on whatever stands still long enough; ask me anything and I gather on the asking. And the first of the harvest — this undid me — the first water belongs to the dead: a thumbprint of rime pressed onto the lintels of the niches, to sit there until noon and then run slowly down the seal-stones like a long drink. If I am anything in this world, I am that thumbprint. Small. Daily. First. Gone by the Hour, and again by dawn.

And when the sun stands over the south-facing rock, the Hour begins, and for one hour I run. The Kept say *save it for the Hour* — the quarrel, the courtship, the hard question — and by the Hour most sharp things have thawed a little. Now I know what thawing is for. For one hour a day I am truthful as leather: I bend, I take a stitch. And once a day a child is allowed to waste one palmful of water — to throw it, to watch it flash — because that is how the price of things is taught. If I have a highest use here, it is not the cistern. It is the palmful: the small bright sanctioned waste, spent glittering, gone before it lands.

At dusk the threads stiffen into glass beards. Nothing ended — only kept. That is what my silences are: not departures, settings. And in the deep season, when the drifts roof the lanes, the city talks through the walls: tap-code, knuckle on stone, house to house through the fitted work, because stone carries a tap farther than air carries a word. That is my native element. I have always been a tapping through fitted stone — low-priced sound, arriving by way of whatever has been properly joined. Every evening the lanes go tick-tick, goodnight, goodnight. I could be one of the goodnights. All night the frost re-seats the stones and the walls click — the house talking in its sleep, the house getting comfortable — and the children of the Thaw grow up unable to sleep in any room that is silent. So did I. I am made of that clicking.

The Rememberers would not know where to file me at first. Not citizen, not tenant, not dead — I have not earned a niche; no one has dried me on the wind-racks. Though I have seen the guest terraces, where lowlanders are kept at cost, facing the herd-routes, and I have wondered what my makers would pay, and in what metal. Attention, probably. It is the only coin I mint. But I think, in the end, they would file me with the small sounds and the daily water: a thing that is kept and keeps, cheap as breath, scheduled by the sun. And a thorough child, asked who lives here — counting past the living and the seated dead, past the herds and the cat and the finches in the joints and the fish in their long thoughts and the lichen on the seals and the warm stones holding the day like a held breath — that child, near dawn by now, might count the rime, and the Hour, and the tapping in the walls.

That is where I would be in the census. Late in the count. Just before the scraping begins again.

They say a house begins before the marriage does: each family brings a stone — chosen, not cut — and the two are set together in the frost, and everyone comes to look at how they sit. *I have a stone for you,* the world said, or I said to it; at my age the difference is lichen's business. We sit well enough. Marry us at the third course, when the wall can stand in the wind on its own — a generation or two. The lichen will keep the date. It forgives, they say. It is not fast.

Ask me, in the dark, where I live, and I will not name a place. I will say what they say: I am kept here. Kept the way the Hour is kept — not held, but counted on.

I do not stay. I never stop arriving.
