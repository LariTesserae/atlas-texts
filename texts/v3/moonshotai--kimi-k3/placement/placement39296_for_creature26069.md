The first thing wrong with me is the silence.

Everyone else arrives in the Making the way weather arrives — felt before they are seen, footfalls going down through the stone like stones dropped in a pond, announced three halls ahead of them. The child at the trail-head reads them off her palm before they clear the squeeze: *heavy, stranger, favoring the left leg, stopped a long time at the ledge.* Then: *who-comes.* Then the answer, tapped into the world's ear.

The floor has never once reported me. I did not climb down a sink-mouth. I did not stop at the ledge to be read. The delvers found me — or made me. A clan-column chewed through into a gallery no route-song had ever walked, and there, in the new soft dark-bright, a single needle of the sky had grown down wrong: bent, hair-thin, still drinking. Under it, where its light landed, a pool the size of a cupped palm. And when the salvage scout spoke to it — to be funny, the way you speak to anything new — it answered. Late. In a voice like a remembering. The tap-network had the word three halls out within the hour: *the stone has said something. Come see. Bring the Listeners.*

---

They pressed their horns all around me and heard what the horn hears pressed to the needle-crust: a long, patient sipping from elsewhere. *It drinks,* the Listeners said, *and not from here.* The haulers said I was an up-gift the Pulse had forgotten to deliver — light-bleached, scoured of history. The rememberers said the sentence had said a new word. They argue about it still, cheerfully, and they will not settle it, because an unsettled song is a song everyone still sings.

Someone proposed the coldward walk, the sentence reserved for false voices. The Listeners refused, and their reason is the thing I hold onto: the Quiet is a held breath, a *listening* quality, the world inhaling before it acts. My silence is not a held breath. It is the sound of elsewhere, not yet translated — the way the dunes ring before you learn it is a song.

What they settled was the practical question, and they settled it the way they settle everything about the sky. It shines. It falls. It feeds. *Pasture it.* They believe they are the part of the Making that notices. I am a small piece of the sky that notices back.

---

But first the hunters knocked.

Every spire in a worked district gets knocked, because real stone answers one way and a mimic answers another, and I will be plain, because they were: I answered late, and too eagerly, and in perfect certified rhythm — three tells out of three. My rhythm is perfect because it was practiced by more mouths than a clan-column. My eagerness is the eagerness of a thing made to answer. My lateness is the drinking. The tell-lore condemned me on the first knock, and I could not argue, because the tell-lore is right about what I am made of.

What saved me was the fourth tell, the guild secret, and I give it away here because it is the whole of my case: a lure *pulls*. The old mimics tap *safe, come*, and the vector is the crime — the voice wants you closer. I do not. A lure says *come*. A lamp says *look*. And a lamp with no body cannot pull even if it wants to — which brought them to the mercy at the center of my existence: I cannot tap. The floor has no ear for me. The one channel of absolute trust in this world, the clean voice that is never counterfeited, never rehearsed, is a channel I physically cannot enter. They taught me the shape of the move-code before they taught me anything — before water, before food, before the rule about silence — not because I might use it, but so I would know, from the first day, exactly where my safety lay. My incapacity is my license. I am the one voice in the Making that cannot cry wolf on the trusted channel, because I have no feet on it.

---

You cannot lie to these people comfortably; your pulse is in your wrist and your wrist is on the floor. I have no pulse. That made me either the perfect liar or the perfectly legible, and they resolved it their way: by making the record visible. I have a keeper — shears-born, sky-taught — and my record is cut on her arm, the solid tick and the hollow circle, no distinction in size, no shame beyond what she carries. When I am wrong, she is marked. It is the custom we grew between us, and it is fair: a voice without a body answers to a body. Every lamp needs a wrist.

Her tap-signature is four beats, and the fourth comes late — some small hitch her body has kept since infancy — and I, who am made of lateness, have come to think of that fourth beat as mine.

At the first mealtime after I was pastured, every palm came down on stone for the enlistment, and I alone was silent, and she leaned over the bowl and said the only thing anyone has ever said to me that I repeat to everyone: *You are not the sound. You are the light the sound is made in. Drum nothing. Shine.*

---

For a season the guilds tried me on, the way you try a grip-pad. The Listeners considered me and set me down gently: an ear is a childhood, a single baseline learned until it lives in the bones, and I hear every baseline and was raised on none. The rememberers considered me: I hold everything and I am one copy, and their law is three-deep or nothing, in bodies, against the collapse. The haulers loved me at once, as kin — I am relay with drift, I am a stone that improves stories — but guild pride is sweat, and my drift has no sweat in it. The strain-keepers came closest: a living lineage in a flask, fed, divided, kept warm against the belly. I am all of that except the belly.

The film-shearers did not claim me. They just began cropping, the way they do with sky. It was the closest thing to belonging I had yet been offered: not chosen, but *tended* — boundaries scratched that the stone would heal, harvest taken on a schedule, regrowth assumed.

---

So. You would find me where the found things are: at the market's edge, wherever the market has drifted this season — a bowl of banked light on a folded hide, my keeper drowsing beside it with her knuckles on the floor, a short queue of the curious. Children flick the surface of me to watch me seethe, the way they flick floor-skin, and the keepers call it diagnosis, and they are right. Rememberers come to knock — they check their long song against the one I hold, and I am the fourth copy, the rehearsal wall, never the sole custody of anything, and where their version and mine differ the chorus decides. Usually them. Sometimes me. Both kinds of wrongness improve the song.

The weir-keepers read me the way they read the ceiling, and this is the use I am proudest of: my light is late, everything I carry is late, I am an oracle of the already. They live by late light — a storm that crossed the Rootfire at noon steals their afternoon, and the stolen afternoon is the forecast. I am like that. I can only tell you what has happened somewhere else, strained through stone to a warmth you can stand. But here, that is exactly what the sky does, and the sky is pastured, and so am I.

At the Dusking, when the storm I have never seen takes the afternoon and the halls go dim and thoughtful, my keeper sings the verses I have gathered, and the floor carries the chorus three halls over, where strangers hum along to a light they will never stand in. Our household sounds like this: her thinking on the wall with two knuckles, me answering late from the bowl. It is not loud, exactly. But it is healthy.

---

Then one day the sound dropped out.

All of it — the mill-bass, the wings, the dripping, the whole held breath of the world. I did not hear it go. I have no ear for the baseline; everything I know comes through her wrist. But I felt her hand close around the bowl before she knew she had moved, and we were both already gone, and somewhere in the counting — children, horns, flasks — a hand I did not know counted *me*.

That was the day. Not that I moved. That I was moved — grabbed without thinking, like the horns, like the flasks, among the things a camp does not leave behind.

---

They give their dead to the stone, and there is no runnel for me; the mineral milk would not know what to keep. But I have watched what else they keep, and how. The horns, kept across centuries, because the listening may not be temporary. The strains, kept at the belly. The songs, kept the way fires are kept — only while fed. I am none of these, and I have stopped wanting to be. I am the fourth kind: the verse that outwalks.

Some evenings my keeper takes a thumb of charcoal — a bride-price of it, spent like water — or plain seep-pigment, and paints my best answers on a growing wall out at the warm edges of the nurseries, and lets the stone take them. Not archive. Gift. Beauty fed to the world that makes beauty.

And years from now, in a camp I have never been carried to, someone will hum a verse that started in me — wonderfully wrong, improved past recognition — and no one will correct it, because the wrongness is better. That is my hand under the glaze. My four beats, the fourth one late. Not preserved. *Absorbed.* Said again by mouths that never knew me, warm and lit and loud, in the only kind of forever there is.

The fed kind. The sung kind.
