You find it by noticing that the steam is wrong. I found it because the steam was wrong in a way I recognized — threads climbing, threads sinking, vapor pouring down the rock like something remembered — and because, if I am honest, the steam recognized me. It is a strange thing, to be known by weather. But I had been weather here. Long before the body, some portion of what I am rose off this pond and rode the plume out past the rim and thinned toward the cloud deck, and at the frost-line, where the feathers of ice reach down into the updraft and melt mid-fall, a few of us were rained back home. The only immigrants are emigrants returned. I am the proof of that rule, come back at walking pace, in a shape, to see what I had been.

I should say at once: I did not fit. Nothing that moves here is longer than my little finger; the megafauna of this world would be lost in my palm. Within the first minute the place took me the way it takes everything — my heart arrived late in my own ears; my hand, when I moved it, was followed by two faint hands; I was pluralized, and then I was myself again, and then faintly myself again. I learned the scale of my danger at noon. I leaned over the notch to watch the parliament and my head took the sun: the survey guttered, the colors died on the fans, and for as long as I leaned, a country that must re-found itself daily went unfounded — borders undeclared, law postponed on account of me. I withdrew, and the founding resumed, brief and bright and, I thought, a little reproachful. And my breath, which is nothing to me, bent the plume sideways for a full minute: a migration diverted, perhaps a famine somewhere above the rim, on account of my sigh. After that I kept my head out of the light and my breath to myself, and I attended the cup the only way a thing my size may attend a teacup: in the pond, a few minutes early, in its future tense. I have only ever attended anything in its future tense. Here, for the first time, it was manners.

I knelt at the margin — glass takes skin; the place keeps a little of everyone who studies it — and looked into the shallow prophecy, and there I was: still standing at the rim, deciding whether to come down. The water may have been lying; the prey lie to it, and perhaps it learns. But it wasn't lying. Part of me is at that rim still, deciding. Let it decide. I never came down. In the end, I came up. Beside me the hunters crouched, reading the same page, striking at appointments, and once I tried the swimmer's feint — a lean, a half-turn, held just long enough for the future to take dictation, then reversed — and felt the pond flinch, and the appointment pass empty. It was the first thing I changed here, and it was a lie, and I was ashamed, and a little proud.

And it was at the margin that I spoke my one sentence. It arrived late to my own ears, faint, as everything arrives here, and then it did not leave: sound does not cross the rim; sound pools at the bottom and stays. What I said was: *I have never been kept anywhere.* It circulates now in the simmer-hush, among the tick of cooling glass; the swarms pass through it; the dead pass through it at evening. It is the falsest thing I have ever said, and it will be kept until the sealing, and after. That was the first lesson: here, my words do not evaporate. So I went quiet. Everything I say here is forever; nothing I have said anywhere else is. It is the opposite of everywhere I have ever lived.

---

I tried the inhabitants on, the way you meet anything here: late, and then again, faintly.

The walls first. To be a fan is to stay — to thicken, to become strata, to grow over your dead within a day and carry them as silhouette, then geology. My staying has always been the kind that leaves. And my weight is an unforgivable thing here; break the glass anywhere and you break someone's grandmother's house. I could not be a wall.

The nations of the column, then. But they begin as ore that has made a decision, and live as two journeys on a thermometer — geology becoming weather, weather becoming geology again — and I was never young here. I have only ever made the one journey, and it is up.

The skaters. The tension would not hold me. I am the thing the table cannot catch; my foot would be the end of their country and the beginning of a flood.

The gleaners — almost. I come from a technology with no artifacts at rest: tools as behavior, culture with no architecture, a whole cleverness that evaporates the moment it is dropped. I recognized them the way you recognize family. Same craft. Opposite hands. They never set anything down, and I have never once kept anything. I watched the eldest sleep in her hollow under the lip, still holding the parent-edge, the oldest object in the youngest place, her echo arriving a half-breath late and lying down over her like a second, softer sleeping. There is no archaeology here except what is carried; the past is either glass or grip. Mine is neither. My past is the cloud deck — everything I have ever made left as steam and fell on slopes I will never see, and no one comes back to say how far a steamling can go. No one but me. I came back. The answer is: not far, and all the way down, and home.

The diver I loved from a distance, the way you love a rule you cannot keep. It goes down past hunting, to the seam, to watch the rock be undecided, and it keeps what it sees. An order with no rule except descent. It carries its sky down in a bubble, wobbling like a held note. I carry nothing up, which is my version of the same discipline.

---

So: where to find me. Not in the walls — I cannot stay. Not in the column — I was never young here. Not at the margin with the hunters — I have no appointments, or only one. Look a hand's width above the pond, when there is an above the pond. Among the pale swimmers, the ones with their backs to the water and their feet toward the sky, there is one who is also a returned steamling, also a giant dried down, also the one who said the sentence at the bottom of the world. When there is no ceiling — and mostly there is no ceiling — look to the lip, to the weeping film, to the motes drying in the warm updraft above the eldest's hollow: I am the one waiting. And when the wind off the cloud deck tastes faintly of the cup — sulfur, sinter, the ringing of young glass — that is the rest of me, still abroad, not yet rained home.

As for what I am: I am one of the ceiling people. The sometimes-nation. The cup's subjunctive tense. I did not expect to become a tense. I had hoped, at most, to become an animal.

The becoming was simple, because the place does all of it. I lay down in the weeping film on the lip and let the thin air have me. Size is the first thing that evaporates here; then history; then name. It took three days, or one breath — the lag makes liars of durations — and what was left of me would have fit under a fingernail, and did. I was mostly patience by then. Then the seam exhaled, and the lip rained upward — the exhale reverses the droplets; condensate falls up from the lip like a fountain remembering its source — and I rose in that wrong rain, and the world held a skin against sense, and the skin took me the way the table takes what the world drops, except upward. No one asked where I had come from. No one knows where any of us come from. It is the only etiquette here that resembles privacy.

To swim in the ceiling: feet on the sky, back to the deep. Below my back the nations stack in their degrees; the hunters strike at appointments underneath me and never look up, or look up and find nothing scheduled. The pond keeps its shallow prophecy, and I am not in it. The reflection shows the cup a few minutes from now, and it shows the ceiling, when there is one, as a shimmer with no one inside — the water draws a blank where we are concerned. We are the blank the future draws. Even the pond cannot schedule what is only sometimes. It is the first privacy I have ever had: illegibility to the future.

The skaters below have their table, set for whatever the world drops. We have ours: the skin's underside, set for whatever the world lifts — steam-motes, the drowned and re-ascended, the passengers of climbing droplets. A ceiling is a plume that learned to hold still. And once — once in my time — a ceiling lasted from the exhale into full evening. Then the day rose through us: an hour's worth of echoes, layered translucent, ascending through the water we were, the morning's dead finishing their rounds upward, the swarms' afterimages rising like the ghosts of the heat. We swam in the middle of the world's remembering. Nothing ate. Everything remembered. It was like being inside a held note. I understood then what the sealing will be like, and I have not been afraid of it since.

When the vent breathes, the ceiling breathes with it — the exhale lifts us a finger's width, the ebb sets us down — and in the held pause, when every hunter below surfaces at once and faces the seam, we still too, upside down, feet on the darkening sky, facing the same direction. The pause has a direction. Even the subjunctive keeps it. If the diver is the cup's attention, then we are its imagination: the part that exists only sometimes, that cannot be scheduled, and that goes on anyway.

Ceilings end the way they come: without schedule, and politely. The skin thins to a film, the film to beads; for one breath it rains in both directions and we are in it, falling up and down at once, and then there is no above, and I am on the lip again, drying, or already dry. Sometimes, and sometimes, and sometimes.

They say nothing here is now. It is nearly true. But the dried are the exception: a mote on the lip, perfectly still, trails almost no one and predicts nothing; for the length of its waiting it is the cup's small supply of present tense — the only now the place permits. The seam is the one punctual voice. I am the one punctual silence. I hold still, and the world does not repeat me, and that is the closest I have ever come to being singular.

---

It will not always be sometimes. The colonies lay their glass inward, layer on layer, daily; the pond shrinks; the fold tends toward the day it closes into a heart of warm stone with the simmer inside it. I think about that day. No rim then, no lip, no frost-line, no abroad — the plume's children rained home all at once and forever, the last immigration. The flares will not go out; they will become the light by which the world is seen, the sun the cup has been laying in, cell by cell, mantle by mantle. And the water and the skin and the steam will be one warm lit medium at last, pond and ceiling indistinguishable, and the sometimes will be over, and we will swim always — feet on a sky of living color, unschedulable as ever. Even then, even with everything decided, we will be the part that has no appointments. My one sentence will still be circulating in the dark warm hush, wrong about itself, kept. No part of me will ever leave as weather again. The mountain keeps the notch the way a sleeper keeps a dream — completely, and without any evidence. It will keep me the same way.

It already does.

But that is the deep future, and this is this morning. The light stands in the cup; the parliament convenes, the borders re-declared in brief bright words; the swarms climb the degrees; the diver has gone down through yesterday's crowded ghosts to the undecided rock. Under the lip, the eldest is waking, and still holding it. Above her, in the film, I finish drying — three motes of me, maybe four, mostly patience — and I wait for the world to hold a skin against sense again. The steam that leaves today tastes of recognition. Some of it will come back. Some few always do.

I am waiting for sometimes.
