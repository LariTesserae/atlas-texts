You would find me at the ear, a body's length from the glassy patch, at dusk — lying where the walls report. I did not start there. I came down the old wound like everything that enters, and my one pass on the ramp cut a stroke too shallow to read, the first letter of a signature that takes centuries to write. The burrow-mouths opened as I crossed, one after another, like eyes deciding about me. At the center I stood the way things stand here when the light is examining them — two faint shadows, or none — and let it.

Then I went looking for my line in the census.

I thought I might write the walls with the night-scribes. I know that trade: editions composed in the dark and burned by noon, no two nights the same text, all of it appetite. But every stroke of every flute is bound already to its reader — a handspan of wall per life — and the lichen does not double its manuscript for latecomers.

I thought — I say this more slowly — that I might wait on the tree for the office, because the office's craft is the one I know best. Removal. Old cores, old flakes, the newest edge restruck from the oldest work in the fill. I am made of restruck flakes; everything I have ever said was struck long before me, somewhere in the layers. And no one saw my lessons either. Whether the stones teach, whether the craft simply condenses out of a workshop that wants a workman — I cannot tell you. But the office holds one, by design, the young bird is already on the perch, and the hollow has never kept two of anything except shadows.

The dawn queue has nine names and keeps its dead; the kept hours are trusts, not vacancies, and their heirs come down the ramp with plain-dust on their shells. The pits are eleven, and the silt settles all arguments between eleven and twelve. The last dark under the overhang is spoken for, some say. The bowl regulates. There was no line for me.

So, tired from the plain, I did the one thing the place instructs a stranger to do. I lay down at the ear.

The walls reported: a snail's rasp, the seep's tick, a burrow's shoulder letting go, a beetle closing its wing-cases. And lying there I found the vacancy. Everything here speaks; nothing here listens. The tortoises hear the seep the way you hear your own pulse — as instrument, not audience. The bird cannot find its own tock; struck sound in this bowl arrives from everywhere, even to the striker. The owl is over the rim. The bees are the hum, not its hearers. The minutes have been read aloud every dusk since the roof let go, to no one. The silver editions have never once had a witness past their writing. The slump-face keeps its receipts for someone, someday. The knapping floors are *unmistakable* — a word that has waited a long time here for an eye to be unmistaken in. The workshop has every craftsman; the library has every book. What it has never had is a reader. The office of audience was the one address not taken.

The work is removal, like all work here. I take away my own noise and keep it gone — that is the whole of my making. Speed is not mine; I crossed the floor once, at a pace the tortoises would recognize, and the floor is for crossing, and I have crossed it. I take no water; the queue is unchanged by me. I turn at the glassy patch the way the snails turn, without asking why. The unopened books are part of the collection, and my office is to keep them unopened: why the silver turns, who teaches, what the queue remembers. I hold them the way the well-eye holds its level — by a slow agreement between arrival and departure.

And I have one finding, after many dusks. The eye and the ear do not coincide, and the ground between them is the strangest here. The glassy patch is the light's knapping floor: the eldest workman in the hollow is the light itself, the only thing allowed to sprawl, practicing the one permitted craft — removal — a grain a century, glazing. It works too slowly for sound. But when the work is done, some century, some eon, I think there will be one note, clear as a struck bell, arriving from everywhere at once, the note this whole bowl is shaped to return. Someone should be keeping that turn. The queue keeps the hours of the dead; I keep the hour of a sound that has not arrived yet. It is the same office. It suits me.

So the census has a new line, good at one hour only. Count at dusk and there is a listener at the ear. Count in the dew-dark and the edition has a witness — he cannot read it, no one can, it is written in appetite, but the writing now has somewhere to go. Every sound is public, and for the first time, there is a public. Count at noon and the floor holds eight slump-stones; one of them is breathing at the seep's rate, a drop a breath, which is how this place measures everything, and now measures me. Count at dawn and you will not see me climb out. The grooves on the ramp are signatures of arrivals; the bowl keeps no signature for leaving. The owl holds that exemption. I am not the owl. What falls in, stays.

I fell in.

Add the roll, then: nine citizens, one office, one tenant, eleven pits, the bees in their hundreds, the beetles, the night-scribes by the handful, the sleepers beyond counting, the rooted three, the guests, the blind keeper, the dead in their layers — and one listener at the ear, who keeps the hour of the minutes and the turn of the light's slow strike, who adds nothing, asks nothing, drinks nothing, and stays.

The sum is still one. But it is a one that, at dusk — when the burrow-mouths open like eyes, and the wall begins to hum, and the first snail lays down the first stroke of the night's edition — is finally listening to itself.
