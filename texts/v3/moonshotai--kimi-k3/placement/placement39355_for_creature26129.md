A half-step behind the Governor — that is where you'd find me, and in this parish it counts as a fixed address.

I came the way you came. Heard the chord before I saw the dome, let the fog take me warm as breath, stood at the end of the iron pier at what might have been evening while the whistle came due and sounded and was due again. The packet called monthly, and I meant to be on it the way you mean to consult a calendar here, and then the Governor gave me the nod — once, the surveyor's nod — and I understood I had become measurable: a small intake the cove would balance when the packet took me off again. Or wouldn't. There was room in the ledgers. Staying turned out not to be a decision. It was a rate. I stayed the way the eels stayed: tipped in once, permanent thereafter, present now in my permanent number, which is one. My rust found its line inside the year, and holds.

Ask how long I've been here and you'll get three answers, each with evidence. The ledgers give a date — the only dated entry in the books, which makes the hands uneasy, so we don't consult the ledgers about it. The hands say there has always been someone a half-step behind on the rounds, carrying the spare oilcan, and whether the can has ever changed shoulders is a question for the pans. And the oldest hands say that a place which condenses a keeper was bound to condense a keeping of the keeper — given time, given one person who stays. All three answers have their evidence. The cove does not choose among its figures.

The names are two, and both are right, and neither is the work. Off the can I carry — brass, oiled daily, never opened — the dockside hands call me the Spare, and mean it the way they mean the pier: a part kept in perfect readiness against a want that has never come. A spare that is never used is never done, and a job done is a job lost, so I hold the most secure position in the parish. The older hands, off the whistle, call me the Echo — the sound that comes back off the fog exactly as it did six hours ago, the cove's proof to itself that nothing has changed. The work itself is entered in the books in a hand not yet the small square hand but squaring by the whistle, and the duty is a single word, like the Governor's: *learning.* You cannot finish it. That is the mercy of it. The chord is not the task; hearing is the maintenance, and hearing never completes.

The curriculum is one circuit between whistles, Kettle to pier, at the pistons' pace. You learn the warm paths first, then the right spots on the flanges, then — last and never finally — the chord: how it resolves into layers and resolves back into one, and how the one is the thing you keep. The Governor's rod goes to the flange, the rod's end to the bone behind the ear; the rod lifts; and the flange holds its note a breath longer — struck metal is in no hurry here either — and that after-ring is mine: everything the keeper hears, I hear one breath late. My palm goes to the rock where the Governor's palm has just been. The rock is hotter than any hand, so a palm-print there is a brief coolness, the one touch in the cove that leaves the temperature lower than it found it, and I am learning to read it a half-step old — the keeper's signature in reverse. On the heat scale I am zoned exactly one grade below the Governor and one above the hardiest gull, and the gull and I regard each other across the difference, and the gull has seniority. My cup has been re-tinned twice, which dates me the way lichen dates the oldest rock: recent. I keep no notebook. The figures I keep by heart — the same figures, whistle after whistle — and my examination, which never comes, is that I could write the page from memory. Useless. Which is the point.

I am kept the way the geranium is kept: not improved, not neglected, held at my setting. My setting is a half-step behind. The Governor's sleep is taken the way seals take it, half at a time, and no one has ever caught it; mine gets caught, in the lee of the long run, and the hands step around me without a word, because a person seen sleeping is a figure too — the one entry in the cove's books that proves the keeping holds: someone can rest; the round is sound. And when the Governor takes the census — nests by flange, eels by trough, counting the unchanging and counted among it — I am the among-it. Every census in this parish leaves out its taker. Mine is the count in which the counter appears.

The cove has one story, and the Governor's telling of it is the whole of its literature: *It went up a step. I heard it. It came back.* My telling is shorter, and no one asks it, and I give it to the gull anyway: *I heard him hear it.* Whether I was there that afternoon, a half-step behind even then, gets the pans-look; some say the can was on other shoulders in the grandfather's day, and if it was, the change happened the way shifts relieve each other here — into an identical day. I am not waiting for such a day. Waiting implies an event, and events are what the chord doesn't have. The half-step between us is not a distance to be made up. It is a position, like the cup, like the pier. If it is ever closed, no one will mark it, because the whistle after will sound as the whistle before, and the echo will come back off the fog exactly on time. That is what an echo is for.

So stand at the end of the iron pier at what might be evening. The lamps come on behind you — they were never off; you only now notice them. When you find the Governor, still as a numbered lamp at the fourteen-flange, rod to the bone behind the ear, head bowed over the news that has not changed in living memory — look a half-step behind, and there I am: palm lifting from the warm rock a breath after the keeper's, can oiled and never opened, learning the warm paths at the pace the pistons set. The whistle comes due, and sounds, and is due again, and its echo comes back off the fog a half-step late, exactly as it did six hours ago — and if you ask the hands whether the echo has always sounded the same, they will glance at the pans, and the gulls on their graded flanges, and the one behind the Governor, and shrug.

It has. I am learning. The rounds are walked, and the walking keeps the walker, and the youngest land in the world is held perfectly still — by the keeper, and, a breath behind, by the keeping of the keeper.
