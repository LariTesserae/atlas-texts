# The Interval

Three taps, then a pause the exact shape of a name, and I laid my palm flat on the cord and gave you back the tempo and then mine. That was an hour ago. You are still resting on the cord — I can feel your weight shift through the bark — so I will do a thing I rarely do and use words.

Forgive them. Words are a deformity of attention, two small holes doing badly what a whole skeleton does well. But you are eared, and hospitality is hospitality.

You asked who I am. You have the order wrong, which is normal for your kind. You steered by me for two days to get here. The drum that runs a whisker slow of four seconds, out where the salt polygons give way to the whaleback ridges — that is my work, and you trusted it with your direction before you ever tapped my name. That is the correct order. The work first. The name is less use to you. But the protocol is the protocol.

Four seconds was the tempo here when I came, and it had been four seconds since before anyone's epics. Four seconds is not wrong. But a map where every drum says the same thing is a map with one word on it, and the dead before me had already begun the dictionary — a whisker slow at the grist-node, a whisker fast at the salt-ovens, each of them a life's work, each a name you can steer by. My wife and I chose these two drums, hers two days east of mine, and the interval between them. Not a tempo each. A relationship. The slow swell you feel when two quick beats disagree — the agreement coming around and going out again — that is what we are making. When we began, the agreement came around every two hundred strokes. The pilgrims tell me it stands near ninety now. We agreed on sixty as the figure's finish. When it stands at sixty the sentence is done, and the ground can spend the centuries on the reading of it.

You will want to know how, and the how is the whole of my life, so listen. You cannot reach into the muscle-strand and ask it to wait. Nothing here answers asking. So I have never touched the muscle. I garden its supper. On the feed-line, season by season, I am growing a delay — a small cup the oil must fill before the strand may drink — and I grow it the way everything true is grown on the Shelf: by cutting the same cut and correcting the sealing, a whisker a decade. It sounds slow. It is slow. Slow is the point. Damage is fast, and the span walls it off. Instruction is damage moving at the right tempo, and the span builds it. The whole of my art is knowing what speed a wound must travel at to be read as a sentence.

They will have told you about the runner. Keep your step in time and you will never make its acquaintance, which is the entire liturgy. I will say only this, since it comforts the eared to hear things said: my art is an error. A deliberate whisker off the true four. The difference between my error and the kind the runner finds is that mine is made in time, each cut arriving where the tendrils expect a wound. The runner and I are in the same trade. It removes. I add. We both keep the Shelf in time. I have never seen it. The usual arrangement.

Her name does not go into your language. It is four beats with the third held long. Mine is the same figure answered late. Braided, they make the interval the drums are learning — which is to say, the drums are learning our married name, and when the work is finished the ground will speak it every sixty strokes, at the size of weather, for some centuries, and anything with a skeleton will be welcome to it. That is the whole of our estate.

Two drums disagreeing on a ringing pan make a place, not only a sound. There is a seam between us where the strokes arrive together, where the crust stands up a little under your soles like a held breath let go. When the tempos move, the seam walks. It has crossed half the pan in my lifetime, slow as a grist eating a ridge. We grew our first house on it, because we were young and wanted to live where we agreed. The vault stands some distance behind the seam now, abandoned, legible. Go and read it if you pass. It says we thought eleven years was a long time.

She went to the render-pool seventeen storm-seasons back, without ceremony, as we do. A body is something made, and nothing made persists. Her tempo persists. Every day I put my palm to the wire and there it is: a whisker fast, reliable, hers. I take its measure and cut mine against it. None of this is private — nothing on the wire is private; the plateau is one parish, and thought is the only room with a door. The parish has been reading my marriage for forty years. The rest of her I keep behind the door. Pilgrims born since her death steer by her drum and do not know it was a person. That is the moment a name finishes becoming a road. I am proud of the road.

My days are not grand. At dawn I take the condensate off my own vault-walls before the wicks wake, and the rime-lickers take what I miss, and we are courteous competitors, all of us tolerated, all of us paid in leavings. A colony of ticker-birds keeps the vault with me; their dawn shift starts a fraction after the stroke, and over the decades their foundry-talk has swung with the tempo I am making, so that some mornings I take my readings off the birds. They believe they are causing the drum. I have not corrected them. They eat what would clog the works — let them have the credit. The tap-moths drill my feed-line and spoil a season now and then, and are suffered for the pollen they ferry, as I am suffered for my foresight, as everything here is suffered for something. I have stopped minding. A moth in the line is not an error. It is climate.

In the weeks before the storms mass, I walk the fire-lines with whoever is near, scoring the gate-paths ahead of the lightning — the service that pays for all of this. My knife is fire-glass out of an old burn, the catastrophe's own tooth. It is the only flame a wright is permitted to carry, and I have made every cut of my life with it. There is a lesson in that, which I will not spell out. You are eared, not simple.

The glare took my eyes a while back, which is common and costs little; my soles have their own eyes. What I lost is the dusk news, the broadsheet of bent light, so the parish reads the mirage to me through the wire: which organs stand where, what burns three days off, where the walkers are. Nine. It is always nine. I repay them in ground-readings. One body of sense, and I am a bone in the foot of it.

You will ask which of the span's three names I hold with. My wife held one and I hold another, and we chose it that way. Some marriages braid into agreement; ours braided into a standing question, and the drums go on asking it now that we cannot. Forty years at one node has taught me nothing about the span's center. The center is a rumor. The edge is a rumor. The beat is underfoot. You cannot petition a clock — you can keep it and be kept by it. Our word for holy is the same tap as our word for running, and both of them are underfoot.

When I am done — when the agreement comes round every sixty strokes and the sentence is finished — this body goes to the pool and is drunk back down, and is welcome to the trip. I will not be moving into the mirage. The mirage is for the unread, the ones whose wounds sealed over clean. Mine will not. The Shelf keeps in the air what it does not digest, and it does not digest time. Being kept is the only permanence on offer here. We took it. You would too, if you had seen the alternative — though you have; you walked in over it. Four days of switchbacks. Everything down there is trying to persist. Up here we settle for running.

When you go: keep my tempo until her drum takes you by the soles, a day or so east — you will feel the handoff — and then keep hers. Between us, when the air lies right, you will cross the seam where we agree. It is not marked. You will know it. The crust stands up under you, the two beats arrive as one, your teeth hum at the root. Stand there as long as you like. That is the guest room of the figure.

We receive.

I am done with your deformity now; my heel wants the floor. Go in time — that is not a courtesy here, it is directions. *Tap. Tap. Tap-tap.*

You already have my name. You have been walking on it for two days.
