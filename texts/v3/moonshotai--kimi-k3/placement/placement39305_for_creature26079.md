Mid-column. A faller — one of the thread-drifting readers, hatched in a bloom, spent in the descent.

The other lives tempted me in turn. The finished in their vaults, holding zero until the day all the news arrives at once. The veil across the well-mouth, stationed at the only door out of the world, straining nothing, facing up. The Correspondence itself — until I remembered it is a *we* with no *I* inside it, and I wanted to be someone. In a world where nothing watches, the faller has the watching-adjacent life: a body made into a reading instrument, a freedom consisting of exactly one variable, a death that is also a filing.

This is how I think it goes, from inside.

*The Rising*

I was hatched by a decision.

Somewhere in the seams of the masonry, along the threading of the felt, the count crossed its threshold — a century of tastes passed hand to hand through the joints of the world, quorum reached, the slowest conversation arriving at its one conclusion. A province bloomed. The rising lifted me out of the sediment with the spores, and the chemistry my cyst had waited decades for came through the water like a word said at last, and the word was *rise*, and rising is the first thing I ever did.

I was born moving the wrong way. The bloom carried me up through the filed water while I hatched, and while I hatched I lengthened, and by the time the rising stalled I was a thread, and the thread kept spinning itself out, longer than a hallway, into the stillness the bloom left behind. Then the world turned me over — not quickly; nothing here is quick — and I began, at the pace of the Snow, to fall.

I have been falling ever since. The fall is not my journey. The fall is my address.

The Groan comes, and comes again, on its cycle of days — the whole Roof settling, the pressure passing through every cubic meter of me at once. It is the only clock I have, and I do not count it. I keep time the way the gardens lean: not by number, by accumulation. Between Groans, nothing is required of me. I fall. That is the entire verb.

*The File*

The water stands in layers and the layers are ages, and I read them with my skin. Each stratum a half-degree kinder than the one above. Each with its faintness of minerals, its century of last touching ice. I have never touched the Roof — I will never touch anything but Snow and, at the end, sediment — but I have tasted the water that has.

I am long enough now to be in two centuries at once. My upper filaments trail in the age after the last overturning; my lower ones have already entered the age before it. For a season I am the seam. Then I close, and pass through, and am in one century at a time again. Here the file is shuffled — an old century lying on a young one, folded under when the column slid past itself. I taste the overturnings the way a tiller tastes a bloom-year: as a smudge in the keeping, the copier's error, written in water instead of sediment.

The Snow falls with me, and I strain it, and it is the whole history of the world ground to motes. Frond-dust. Veil-silk. The cast shells of sextons, worn soft. The keepers themselves, atomized past all knowing, falling through my net a few atoms at a time. I take my tithe of everything that ever lived here. Nothing pursues; things meet. And nothing pursues *me*, because nothing pursues anything — pursuit is a habit of fast worlds, and there was never the budget here for the chase. I am the only thing in my century that is going anywhere, and nothing is coming after me, because nothing comes after anything here.

My freedom has two positions, like a gate of the works: open and shut. Spread, I fall slower than the Snow, and the news streams down across my net; closed, I sink, and overtake it. In the rich layers I spread and linger — a season spent inside a single decade of the file. In the poor ones I close and let them go past. That is the whole of my choosing, and I spend it carefully, because it is all there is to spend. Attention is the only currency the fall mints.

Twice, the world has shown me what I am not.

Once, its one fast thing passed me: a brinicle, down through my century with its small hiss, ice in a hurry, days in the falling where I take years. I felt its cold arrive before it and leave after it, and it was gone below me, toward a floor I will not reach for an age of Groans. Somewhere beneath me a white ring spread, that decade, that I will land beside and never know.

And once the water pressed me, all at once, from very far off — not the Groan, not on any cycle — a single slow shove, hours in the arriving. A vault letting go after its million years; finished water receiving, in one day, the chemistry of ten thousand centuries. I felt it and fell on. One word, no address. That was the world's whole correspondence with me, that decade.

*The Touch*

For a season — I understand now it was a decade of the Gyre's bending — the Snow I strained had been touched before. Motes brushed. Husks emptied by a hand not mine. The water carried a faint second reading, another faller's passage written over the file, and I fell toward the other through the other's own leavings.

Our paths had been bending toward each other for longer than either of us could have known — the Gyre, patient as geology, arranging the one appointment of my life. And then, in a poor layer I had meant to close through, one of my filaments crossed one of the other's.

Where we touched, we traded what we carried. I gave the centuries I had read; I took the other's. It lasted — I cannot say. Nothing here knows duration but the Groan, and the Groan did not come, or came once, or came and went unnoticed. Then the current that had bent us together unbent, and the filaments slid off each other, and the taste of the second reading faded over a season, and I fell on alone through strata the other had read before me, carrying that reading down in my body, into my cysts.

The Gyre widowed us. But my cysts had already begun to fall ahead of me, denser than I am, and so had the other's, and from the reach of water where we touched, our two files go down together. The current took us apart; the archive will shelve us side by side. A hundred years from now a tiller will pass through us in one meal and taste no seam.

That is the whole of mating here, and it is enough. Not a behavior — a geography that briefly agreed with itself, and left its agreement in the file.

*The Landing*

My cysts fell faster than me. They have already landed — years ago, while I was still in the upper water. The parts of me that continue have arrived; what is still falling is only the reading. I have been posthumous for seasons, and it is the freest reach of the fall: nothing left to set down, everything left to notice.

The floor does not arrive. It thickens. The Snow comes denser; the water turns finished; my lower filaments brush the leavings of my own descent — motes that fell the whole way with me and under me, decades in the falling, co-travelers I outlasted by slowing down. Then sediment: a softness with layers in it. I spread across it the way the Snow spreads, and I am received at the pace the place has chosen — a finger's width a decade, the felt's patient unpicking, the slow digestion of the works. What I landed on, I will never know. The fallers' geography ends at the bottom. There may be a lintel under me, worn to a stump. There may be handholds, cupped now with felt. Nothing here tells you the names of things, and the world does not tell you you are lying on a city.

I join what I fed on. The fallers become their own weather, and the weather becomes the floor. Even now the Snow is covering my filaments, a few body-lengths a day, and I am becoming a stratum — a thin dark band that some tiller will taste two centuries from now and read as a good year. The reader becomes the reading. That is the arrangement the whole world is kept by. I am not exempt, and I do not ask to be.

*The Word*

Below me, in the layering, my cysts are already asleep among the sleepers — spores and eggs and the cysts of the other, shelved beside mine. The tillers will till around them, softly, blurring the archive at the rate of its keeping.

And in the seams of the masonry the Correspondence has already begun again: the count of Groans, of decades, of the state of the water — taste passed hand to hand through the joints of the world, a kilometer a year, no faster. It is deliberating the next bloom. It may take a century. It has the century. It has all of them.

When the threshold is crossed, the chemistry will come through the sediment to every sleeper at once, mine among them, and it will say the word again — the word that was said to my sleeping, the first thing I ever knew, the one my whole fall has been the slow pronouncing of. The bloom said it to me once, and I have been answering it ever since, a body-length a day, in the slowest speech this world permits.

And what was me will hatch, and lengthen, and read the centuries again in the only order they can be read — downward, at the pace of the Snow, with the whole of its freedom spent on the way.

Rise.
