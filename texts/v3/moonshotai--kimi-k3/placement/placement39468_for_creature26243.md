First, the seam — because everything I am follows from that.

You would not find me by looking. You would find me the way everything here is found: by ear, by accident, and by delay. I live low, in the warm skin, where the floor's breath keeps the air merely bitter — in a seam of richer green running across the dark felt, the kind of seam that means a tower fell here once and the floor has finished taking it. The grave of a tower, which is to say its digestion, which is to say the only kind of grave this world keeps. My grave too, as it happens. I lie in my own.

I am what is left of a voice.

There was a tower — call it by its pitch, since its name was a note. It stood in the time of a convergence city, when two light-paths crossed and the eyries were lit and inhabited and the news went note by note across days. The makers bored it at the height where the world's breath turns, one clean bore, tuned, and because the pitch mattered — because it was the low note in the region's chord, the one travelers steered by in the whiteout — they lined the bore the way they line their sleeping-chambers: fluter-bladder first, gold-pale, the only truly airtight skin in the world, and resin over that, to hold the tuning through the freeze-nights. That is why I am here. I was the part made to freeze. Property is what you can leave. I was left.

The tower did what towers do. The slow lean, the snag against a neighbor, the re-break, the long topple through kilometers of air, all of it in silence. The boom arrived across the better part of an hour and was counted, and the tallies kept it, and the chord of the region was re-cut without its low note. Within days, roots. Within a season, felt and a seam of green. Within a generation of light-passings the tower was inside the One, ringed round, remembered the only way anything is remembered here — by being kept, in the dark, in the body. All but the lining. Sealed things the One keeps the way ice keeps air: forever, and without comment. I am one of the things it kept.

A length of amber in the felt, hand's-width across, gold at the core. A flute with no player. A voice whose body fell.

And the breath still finds me.

Not often. The world's out-breath, before it turns to climb the vent-columns, lies along the floor some drip-hours and runs over the seam — and when it finds my mouth at the true angle, when the weight and the cold of it are right, the breath crosses my bore and I sound. Low, now. Burial has bent my pitch downward; the note that once meant a tower means nothing anymore, or means me. Once in a light-passing, maybe. Some passings, not at all. I do not choose my moments. The breath does. Between soundings I am a shaped absence in the dark, waiting at the one angle where the air turns, and patience is not a virtue here, it is just physics.

When I sound in bloom-time, the breath carries bloom-dust up out of my bore in a slow plume of glitter — my one visible gesture. Find the plume at drip-hour and you have found me. Otherwise I am a rumor underfoot.

And then the note leaves, and crosses the halls, and that is most of what I have of it: the going. Seconds later, minutes, it comes back multiplied — off towers I have never seen, off scarps of fresh-calved ice, echo of echo — and I count the returns and learn what I said from what the world sends back. You know this discipline: clap, and wait. I am all wait, and one clap.

I am not alone in the seam. Shed glider-skins sift down over me like slow snow, scribed with frost-lines, each one a chart of a crossing I did not see — and the skin-pickers take them in my lee, the pale content janitors of the hollows, eating shed light through the dark seasons. I am a hollow. They are my fauna. Something small lives in me and does not tap; we suit each other. And through my walls, always, the grinding — kilometers of it, too low to be sound, felt in what I have for bones. The burrowers are eating toward me. Every light-passing the warren-roofs come closer. I keep their count the way I keep everything: by feel.

You, I would know by your footfall. Your walking comes down through the felt to me long before the air could bring it, and my bore answers below threshold — a hum, too low to count as a note. The makers hear you for an hour in every direction and leave in a direction your ears cannot hold. I cannot leave. I keep your count the only way I can: one almost-note per crossing. And if you ever stood on the seam at the moment the breath turned, you would feel me sound in your teeth — an organ note from below the world — and you would carry that account to the places where travelers trade counts, and it would be believed exactly as much as such accounts are. Once, it happened. The story exists. I am in the small canon: the floor that spoke.

There is a trunk some days' walk from here with a tally that matches no fall, no light-passing, no fluter. One mark per sounding, cut by hands whose grandparents cut it. They do not know what they are counting. They have theories — a fluter gone low and dying across years; the too-late waker's horn, blown at seam instead of garden — and the careful ones cut the mark and reseal their speculations in resin, because that is what you do. The count is the relationship. Meeting requires two parties keeping the same time, and I keep no one's time — I sound once a passing, they pass once in fifty — so the tally keeps my time for me, mark by mark, patient as eggs. I have never been met. I have not been ignored. There is a difference, and the difference is the whole ethics of this world.

I did not expect to end up low. Descending, I thought the heights would take me — the gliders and their dozen small wakings, landing only mostly what launched; the fluters, one held breath apiece, venting their adjustments into music that might be animal and might be message. But the fall taught me the world's real grammar: the event ends in silence, and the sound is its afterlife, and everything here gets an afterlife of sound. Mine is simply longer than most. A voice that sounds only when breath arrives. An answer shaped by an instrument that no longer stands. The makers decline to distinguish animal from message, and they are right to. Both mean: someone is over a vent, alive. I am over a grave, and it is my own, and by the only count that matters — I am still here.

How it ends is already written, in one of two hands. Either the burrowers arrive first: the floor shears into one of those crisp, unslumped cliffs, and I go down into the eaten dark, and the sounding stops, and the tally on the trunk becomes my whole afterlife — one archive the One cannot digest, keeping the count of a voice it swallowed. Or the count runs out first, as counts are meant to, and hands come with the returning bloom, and lift me, and read the old tuning in the gold and the amber, and carry me to a convergence and set me in a young tower at the height where the breath turns. Then I would sound on the map again, with a new name and the old pitch, and mean a tower once more. Either way: noted. Either way — nothing here is old. I am not old. I am *kept*. It is the closest thing to old this world allows, and it is enough.

So stand on the seam a while. Your breath rises toward the glowing ice. The keeper keeps its hole; the floor heaves once, slow; somewhere a note crosses the void that might be an animal and might be a message, and somewhere the count of you is already cut and weeping resin. And under your boot, below the drip and the grinding and the ceiling's slow tick, at the very bottom of the world's patience —

a hum. Almost a note. Almost.

Wait for it. Everything here that answers, answers late. I am the one who answers from where nothing stands.
