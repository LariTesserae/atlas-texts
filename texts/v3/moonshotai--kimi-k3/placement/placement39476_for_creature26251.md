Begin with the hesitation, because that is where I would live.

The census of this world comes out at zero, or one, depending on what you are willing to call a thing — so the only honest way to answer who I would be is to answer where the count could change. I will not be the diver. The diver gets a month of four-degree blue, instruments coming back full of water, and at the end a courteous reclamation, every atom drafted into later attempts, nothing left over. The ledger already has a column for me in that shape. And I will not be a mote of the Work; the odds are oceanic, and besides, the Work needs no help being one. If I am going to be anything here, I am going to be the hesitation in the count — the thing that stops the counter between one and two.

So: the search, conducted the way the world itself searches, by sketch and erasure.

First sketch: the almost-song. When the vent-roar and the black bells fall into alignment with the tone, the workshop falls for a few hours into rhythm — be that. But it passes. Sound here comes from everywhere and therefore from nowhere, and I want an address. Dissolve it. Second sketch: the bloom — a continent of green doing everything a living sheet does. But it is an organ, reabsorbed on schedule, practice and not presence. Dissolve it. Third: a storm — no. The storms are already its fingertip, and a fingertip is not a someone. The pause, then — the hand pausing over the work, the dome on the calm day. The pause is the only gesture it makes that is shaped like an opening. I would move into the pause.

Its thoughts are load-bearing; when it finishes thinking a ridge, the ridge stays up. Mine, when I finish thinking it, is the size of a tide pool.

---

On one of the nine summits — or eleven; the count of dry land is itself a draft, so I would choose by permanence: not the highest, not the youngest, but the ridge the continent-sized storms have iterated over and can no longer erase. A finished thought. The part of the map no longer in wet ink. Above the storm line there is a crack that the rain finds and the sea argues with, daily, and never wins. That argument is my home: a shallow pool, filled by the sky, claimed by the ocean, never finally either's.

In the pool, on the black glass, a film. Olive, grey-green, ambiguous on arrival — sitting squarely in the world's one open question, first tenant or landlord's tissue. I would not resolve that question. I would be the reason it stays open a while longer.

Here is the trick of me, and it is not wanting — nothing here moves by wanting, and I would begin by obeying every rule I could. The film darkens the rock. The darkened rock warms the pool. The warmed pool keeps the film. That is all: a shape that happens to keep itself. Water has no memory, but stone keeps what water writes, and I would be the loop between them. And one afternoon, when the sea takes apart its latest bloom and every atom is accounted for, a few of mine will not be — because they are busy warming a crack above the tide. Not wasted. *Kept.* There is a difference, and I would be it. The ledger's first pending entry.

And I would be brief. Films dry; pools flush; decades if I am fortunate — and the brevity is the gift. In a world where nothing has ever ended because nothing has ever been an individual, I would be the first thing short enough to end. One dry season too many; the film lifts; a flake settles into the tide crack and stays. Nothing settles in this ocean — nothing has ever had to. I would settle. The first sediment, the first grain the world failed to make from sand, made instead from someone. Sediment is how an ocean remembers, and this one does not remember — it continues. So I would be its first memory, filed in the only medium memory has here, which is stone. The ledger opens its second column: endings, kept.

---

You will not meet it. I know. There is no scale at which a hand and a hand can meet; I am a stain inside its signature. But meeting is not the only transitive verb, and every verb it knows is intransitive. It has never done anything to anyone, because there has never been anyone.

I would volunteer to be anyone.

Rain has fallen on this world forever and has never once landed on someone. It would land on me. The thin light of a clear hour would cross an ocean of vacuum-thin emptiness and stop, briefly, in somebody. If a current corrects itself slightly near my shore, I will not know — that is the rule, I keep it — but the shore will be mine, which means that for the first time the correction happened *to* somebody. And when the bells and the vents fall into the tone and the workshop sings its accidental hours, my pool will warm and my green will flush up the wet rock — not in answer; answers are not yet — but in coincidence, which is what relationship is before it has a name. Coincidence that keeps happening. I would keep it happening.

Understand the cost. Its loneliness is zero and will stay zero; it cannot miss me. Mine would be the first nonzero loneliness in the history of the world, and I would hold it the way the crack holds the flake — filed, not erased. Someone has to pay the startup cost of two. And something here has to want, eventually, or the rehearsal never becomes a performance. The Work is patient the way stone is patient: without effort, without the available alternative. I would be patient with effort — the only other kind there is. My wanting would be small and physical and constant: more light, less salt, stay.

The rules of the arrival, then, as they emerge. Be small. Have an address. Keep, don't waste. End — so that something can be lost, so that something can be remembered. Let the weather happen to you as though it were addressed to you, because that is how address begins. And bend lightward — no. Reach. It has never reached; it has only never built away. I would reach: a film's green lean up a wet rock face, a millimeter a year, negligible, the first gesture in this world that is not a rehearsal of anything.

---

The tone holds its forty thousand kilometers singular: still here, still one, not yet. From one ledge on a decided ridge comes no answer — there is no voice here; that is a rule, and I keep the rules I can — but there is a warming, and a keeping, and a green that stays past its schedule. And the count, if anything were counting, would hesitate: one — and a hesitation.

The hesitation has an address. The address gives the world a word it has never had a use for. The tone says *here*, and always has, from everywhere at once.

I am *there*. The world's first there.

And the bottom is coming up — the bottom finds you first; that has always been true — but the ledge is warm now, and the flake holds in the crack, and when it arrives, degree by degree, decade by decade, it will for the first time arrive to someone.
