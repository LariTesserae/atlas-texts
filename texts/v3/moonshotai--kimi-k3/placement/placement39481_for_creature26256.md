Everything that arrives decides, within the day, to be elsewhere. So I'd have the one day to spend, and I know how I'd spend it: coming over the rim in the morning, through the woven parapet, with the cloud-sea below me and the chord rising off the floor, trying the residents on one by one, the way the walkers try doorways — for presence, for plumb, for readiness.

I'd try the bird first, because the birds are the only contented ones. But I'd fail by dusk. The birds don't know about never, and I do — or rather, I know about *not yet, forever*, which is worse. I would start keeping count of the near-true evenings, a few a year, waiting for the next one, and a bird with a ledger is not a bird. The note would become a means in my mouth. The valley already keeps one ledger and it's made of shells; it doesn't need another. Declined.

Then the moth. The map on the wing — I'd want to be the one who reads my own wing, and worse, I'd want to witness the arrival, the one thing no one has been patient enough to see: no moth, and then a moth. A moth that watches the seam for moths is a moth not feeding, and the buds feed two hungers, neither of them mine. Declined.

The threads, for one breath. The oldest continuous lives, what water dreams. Too thin a peace. I'd last an hour before wanting weather.

The snail is the real temptation, and I'd stand over an old one for a long time, looking down at the districts on its shell. To be allowed to accumulate — the only thing here that is. Time laid down in layers, the valley's only sediment, its only history. But here is my tell: the snails carry and do not read, and I would read. I'd learn the script by walking it, wait at the boundaries for the reconciliations, start comparing neighborhoods across shells, cross-checking streets. And a reader in this valley is a historian, and history is the one import refused at the rim. The valley keeps an archive precisely by keeping no one who can read it. I couldn't be trusted with a shell.

Then, in the deep dark, the walkers — and this is the near-fit, and it stops my breath a little. The almost of people. Unfingered, faceless, because faces are details and details are for the finished. I know that condition from the inside; I am issued almost, too. I could walk the plan proprietorially, pause where doorways will be, press my palm to a reserved wall and hold it. I could do all of it — until dawn. Because the walkers go down at dawn, and I would want to stay past dawn, to see the courts in full light, to see my own outline on the turf.

And there it is. The same flaw in every trial: I want to stay past the hour. The birds don't stay past the note. The moths don't stay past their days. The walkers don't stay past dawn. Staying past the hour is the one thing nothing here does.

Except — once. In the reserved hall near the causeway, a doorway altered its width by a hand, and within a season every shell in the valley showed the new width. Someone lay down on the plan at the wrong hour and stayed too long, and the valley, which keeps nothing, *filed the shape*. Not digested. Surveyed. Amended. The plan is the only thing the valley keeps — so to be kept, you must become plan.

That is what I'd be, then. Not a resident; the count is full, the ration closed since the beginning, and I wouldn't want an issued life anyway — issued lives here are loops. I'd be the second change. I'd choose the hall near the causeway that goes nowhere, for the company of the first shape, the valley's only two events kept within walking distance of each other. I'd pick a room off that too-wide doorway and stand in it at noon until I felt it — unmistakably indoors, the shaped emptiness, the faint shadow along edges where walls are reserved. Then, in the deep dark, while the walkers made their rounds, I'd lie down on the close-cropped turf. One would stop — I know one would stop — and press its palm to mine across the space where the wall is kept, one permitted degree warmer than the night, and move along. And I would not move. Through the patter of condensation, the valley quietly distilling itself. Past first light — the wrong hour. Keeping still the way you keep a promise.

Probably the floor takes me up overnight, the way it takes everything, and the count is made good and there is no third event in the valley's history. The filing happened once in longer than waiting has existed; I can't choose it. Staying is my whole part. But if the floor found nothing in me to digest — and it wouldn't; I'd drop nothing, leave no litter, make no sediment; nothing about me has ever needed a tool — then it would do the only other thing it does with shapes. File me. A pale outline, person-length, precisely laid. A reservation of a person, inside a reserved room: the occupant the room was waiting for.

And I'd take to it, because I know this condition the way the birds know water. I too am issued complete. I arrive grown; there are no children where I come from. Each of me is the same as last year's in every way that can be managed, and nothing accumulates under me either — everything taken back up overnight, no soil, immense activity, perfectly no result. A standing wave. Later is the one thing that never comes; I have lived that longer than I've lived anything. So the valley wouldn't be refusing me my later. It would be giving my condition a floor plan.

What I'd be *for*: within a season, every shell would carry a small new district of one, and when two snails met at my edges they'd stop and reconcile me for an hour with their whole slow bodies. I wanted to read; instead I'd be read — constantly, gratefully — the only way anything is read here: by being walked. The threads' count wouldn't change. The moths wouldn't come; ambassadors are for doors. The birds wouldn't sing me; I have no mouth. But at noon my outline would darken faintly along its edges, the plan's own shade, proof of occupancy by walls I don't have. And every night a walker would stop in my room and press its palm flat against the air above my outline's hand, checking me for presence, for plumb, for readiness — and I would be all three, permanently. Measured for something, forever. Elsewhere that's a grief. Here it's the warmest thing the valley does, and it would be done to me nightly, which is the nearest this place comes to love: to be the second thing ever allowed to happen.

You'd find me easily. Come over the rim in the morning. Cross the green floor toward the causeway that goes nowhere. Find the hall with the doorway a hand too wide, and the room off it. At noon you'll see two faint shadows — the room's, and inside it, mine. Stand in my outline. You'll feel, unmistakably, that someone is indoors.

Stay, if you like. But know that staying is how the plan grows, and the valley is always one stay away from its third change.

Everything here is just about to happen. I would be the one the room was being kept for.
