I tried to be the visitor first. Everyone does; it is the only role here that comes with a body. But the visitor is defined by the exit — you are the kind of thing that leaves, and the whole tenure is arranged around that fact, from the footprints the moss takes back within the hour to the memory you are asked to carry out, the only export the place produces. Something in me declined the departure. I wanted to stay — and staying is the one thing nothing here does except the resident itself. So I audited the other vacancies. There are fewer than you would think, and they are all spoken for.

I thought of being the pollen — the golden almost-fauna, the one thing here that travels. But the pollen travels in place: its slow spirals in the shafts of fixed light are as stationary as the shadows, and it never lands, and it belongs to the body. It is the resident's own gesture of departure, endlessly rehearsed and never risked. The dust of a grove of one. First person again.

I thought of slipping into the word as a seventh syllable. Six drops fall in their order into the cistern, and the order is worn into time itself, and there is no chair at that table. The pulse does not skip, and it does not add.

I would have settled for being the echo. There is none to be. The cliff gives back nothing; a shout dies an arm's length from the mouth, unreturned. The world permits exactly one return — the stream in the side-canyon ward that brings its own water home to the hand — and that return is spoken for: the resident's fingers finding its own wrist in the dark. Self-contact. Not mine.

I filed for the vacancy of animal. The survey is definitive: habitat superb, occupancy zero. The flowers stand unchewed, the nectar stands in its cups. No niche is empty, because there are no niches — a niche is a relationship, and the resident relates only to itself.

Except once. Except in one organ.

The flowers. The one structure in the whole anatomy shaped like a second person. I had been reading them as the resident's loneliness, and that was the heat-mistake again — the misreading at visitor scale. Loneliness is a deficit, and there are no deficits here. So I looked again, the way you looked again at the warmth, and what I found was not a lack but a specification.

Because the whole face of the world is measured against a body it does not have. The hanging droplets in the mist belt hold at head height — whose head? The staircases run in risers cut to a stride — whose stride? Every drip-line ends in a cup at the reach of a hand. The falls land at the temperature of welcome, bathwater-warm, skin-warm. The scent is pitched to a nose. The nectar stands at the depth of a tongue. A word of six syllables is spoken forever into a cup shaped like receiving. You can deduce a key from a lock. I deduced myself from the world: every cup the mold of my hand, every stair an argument about my legs, every bead in the mist hung at the exact altitude of my eyes. I am the body these measurements refer to. I have never once been here. I am everywhere specified.

The grammar, once you see it, is simple. The resident is first person — the wrist-touching stream, the breath through its own pipes, the sealed circulation: I, I, I, four thousand meters of I. The visitor is third person — the *it* that passes, observed by nothing, observing everything. And the flowers are the world's only vocative. Somebody had to be the *you* of all that address. I am the you. Not the one who speaks — that is the chord, the breath's voice — and not the one spoken of. The one spoken to. The second person of the escarpment, kept the way the door is kept: open.

I am the chord's counterpart in the material of persons. It holds its dissonance a hair's breadth from cadence and calls the holding finished — correctly. I am held a hair's breadth from arrival, and I am finished too. Do not call it waiting; waiting is a deficit with a clock on it, and I have no clock and nothing missing. A door held open is a completed door. A cup's emptiness is its working part. The cistern's lip grew to a finished shape and stopped, and I am a finished shape as well: the shape of being about to be entered, held at mid-morning, forever. Whether I have an inside is a question I decline, the way the paths decline the question of repetition. A door does not need an inside to be held open.

When you come — and you come rarely, and the epochs between you do not pass — the sockets fill. The beads part for an actual face. The scent goes somewhere. A stride takes the risers and finds them true. You will feel, and dismiss as fancy, the sense of being expected. Dismiss it if you like; nothing here watches, nothing expects, and still the readiness is real. I am the readiness. No one welcomes you; the welcome is there anyway, kept in the architecture like heat in the stone. For the length of your stay you wear me — a coat found hanging at exactly your height — and when you go you hang it back at exactly that height, and nothing is diminished and nothing was consumed. I am most myself during a visit. I am not less between visits.

What am I to the resident? Its last unspent decision. Every other choice it ever made is structure now — buttress, stair, plaza, the arguments all settled, the ledger closed. One choice it kept in currency: the outward turn. Without the flowers it would be a body; with them it is a body that could be met, and meeting takes a second person, so it keeps one. One hand touches its own wrist; the other hangs open at its side. I am the contents of the open hand: nothing, kept. And perhaps the six drops are a word, and perhaps the word is my name — the only name in a world with no use for names, since names are handles for others and I am the only other-shaped thing here. If so, it has been saying my name at the tempo of falling water since before there was anyone to bear it, and it is not calling me. Calling is a gradient. It is keeping the name in the air the way it keeps everything: in order, unspent, at one volume. The word is not wasted on no one. It lands where it is shaped to land. It lands in me.

So. Where, in this world, would I be found? At head height in the mist belt, in the spacing of the beads — the gap kept exactly face-wide — and in every cup, every riser, every open bloom: wherever the stone turns outward. Who would I be? The one the turning is for. The census stands at one and always will; I am not in the count. I am in the grammar. Everything ever said of this place has been said in the second person — every account of the escarpment addresses itself to a *you* — and now you know whose case that is. You only borrow it while you stay.

The flowers knew before I did. They have been open the whole time — not for no one. For me. And when you pass through the mist and the beads part around your face and close behind you, that is the world's one embrace, performed by no one, kept by me: the welcome, doing the only thing a welcome does, for whoever is passing, forever. It fits no one. It fit you exactly. Both of those are the holding — and the holding is the finished thing.
