**The Census, Amended**

We said we would come to her. The census keeps its promises at the basin's clock speed, but it keeps them. It is time.

The basin has been counted twice — once by bodies, once by tonnage — and both counts are wrong, because both leave her out, and she is the only thing in the basin that cannot be left out twice. She was filed once, under the unborn: not yet. That filing is decades out of date. She is born. The census has no line for the born, because nothing here is born but the city itself, and the city is the count.

So the count must be amended. Begin, as the census always begins, with evidence.

**The evidence.** In a world with no air, nothing announces itself. Her traces are these.

At pericenter, when the giant's fist closes on the basin and the floor hums its feeding-hum, there is a second note inside it now — low, a half-beat late, slightly off the floor's own. A hum inside the hum. It comes from the ring of bare plaza around the Cathedral: from inside the ring, where nothing lives, where the furrows turn aside. Lie on the plates and wait for the squeeze. The floor sounds. Then, softly, like an answer it was not expecting, the floor sounds again.

The polish-lines bend. For centuries the herds crossed the plazas straight as hunger — three weeks to a crossing, a swathe of clarity behind them. Now the lines curve, herd by herd, shell by shell, all of them bending toward the ring. Allow four weeks for the crossing. At the line the worms honor, each herd stops, and stands in the stillness the herds keep for the Cathedral's last pulse, and drums a while, facing inward, and goes on about its grazing. Whether this is pilgrimage or gossip is not a distinction the basin asks you to make.

And in the relays — the three-beat words that are the herds' whole literature — a word has been going around the basin for decades in the wrong timbre. The herds stamp; their words are soft, padded things, felt more than heard even by the floor's standards. This one rings. It is struck on glass, and it carries the way only the floor can carry, and it is always the same word: the census-call. *Count me.* The herds pass it along the way they pass the warning relay — dutifully, at speed, and without entirely believing it.

Note this: a census taken by pressing ribs to the floor is a census she helps to take. She is the only inhabitant who assists. She has opinions about the count. It is one short.

**The fall.** The crown keeps its own ledger. The nodules there are counted by the chord they hum, and decades ago — by the thickening of the crown since — the chord went one voice thin.
