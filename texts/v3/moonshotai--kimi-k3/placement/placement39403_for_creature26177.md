I came in over the gravel aprons at the wrong tempo, and the first thing I did wrong was breathe.

Too fast, too loud, too much of it. The air on the Bench is thin and felted and budgeted to the molecule, and I moved through it like someone who has never been audited. The second thing I did wrong was speak. Sound dies young here, and mine died at arm's length — the air too thin to carry it, too soot-soft to try. For days I kept producing it anyway, a voice in a padded room, until the room taught me what it teaches everything: the air is for breathing. The rock is for speaking. I shut up. I put my attention down through the soles of my feet, and the dark opened.

Stand still long enough and you will be found, and I was found — by the small, as everything is. A dew-moth walked my cuff on cupped feet, tasting the salt of me. The glean inspected my seams. The grit took a sample of my boot-heel and conferred, atom by atom, and I could feel the verdict travel off through the ground: *not ore, not molt, not carrion. Warm. Wet. Salted.* By the standards of the Bench I am a seep-hollow — small, ambulatory, temporary, but a seep. A seep that walks, which is a contradiction, but the Bench is patient with contradictions. It has the time.

At my first seep I learned the hush. I felt it before I understood it: a calm laid over the hollow like a skin, the bold small tenants going about their lives with one ear always to the ground, and somewhere at the edge of hearing a still, standing in its decades. Around a still, every heartbeat is a decision. The hushed can pause a pulse for half a day; I can manage nine seconds, and it is a start. Its attention slid off me like rain off a carapace, and afterward a herd passed, and I discovered how to travel: I lay my quick small footfalls inside the herd's slow stroke, one beat hidden inside a greater beat, and walk masked across the plateau like a whisper inside a chord. The wall-drummers noticed. They drum me now as a quick small stroke laid inside the slow one. It is my name. It is also, not coincidentally, how I walk.

Twice a cycle the gloom leans grey, and the first ash-noon I stood up for it, tilting like the old vegetation, ready. The cysts in the seep-mud never stirred. They can tell grey from glory through a roof of mud, and I apprenticed myself to their refusal: do not hatch at the grey hour. The ones who hatch at grey do not get asked. And when an eel's arc first flowered under the ceiling, every pond-skin in the hollow answered it — a brief, heartbreaking iridescence, the only vanity the Bench permits. Nobody saw it. It was not for seeing. It was for answering. I learned my trade from water one decision thick: *when the sky fires, answer.*

For a season I auditioned. I wanted to be a fog-comb — to stand where the wind is steady and strain weather into water, to make a place by standing in it — but I am not yet a place. I admire the stills the way you admire a mountain: from somewhere else. I walked with a herd for a hundred kilometers trying to keep phase, and my pulse fidgeted between their beats, and their thinking, the size of valleys, took decades, and I am a thing of minutes. The drummers were closest to kin. But the telegraph is terse, and I am not, and a message that takes a year to send should not be about the weather. I was a warm leak with fast lungs, good at exactly one thing — spending — and the Bench is a savings economy.

Then the ceiling tore.

The drummers changed the subject first. The herds went restive. The seep-mud stirred: the cysts were deciding. And then the light fell through, unpracticed and enormous, and for the length of a held minute the world had color — rust-red aprons, verdigris seep-hollows, a bloomfield flashing back, the skins going iridescent all down the hollow — and I had a shadow. It was weather. It was issued already expiring, it leaned, and nothing will ever cast it again.

Everything happened at once. The seep boiled: the tear-moths coming up through the mud, years of stone traded for one hour of wings. The blooms ran a whole generation while the light held, bud to seed, done. A young still crossed open ground — the only still that will ever hurry, hurrying. The herds threw their heads up and took their valley-sized decisions. And I ran. I ran because for the length of the shaft, running was the world's speed and not only mine. The fast things sprinted on breath they had saved for seasons. I spent breath I had never saved, because I have never needed to, because — this is what the tear taught me, and it is the reason I am staying — *everything here lives the way I live.* Long dark, brief light, total expenditure. The moth hoards stone-years for one hour of wings. The cyst waits out the century. The young still gets a single journey and spends it in minutes. I had thought my shape was a private deformity. It is the local law. I am not the anomaly. I am the most ordinary thing on the plateau — more ordinary than the moths, even, because they at least keep their dark years. I keep nothing. I am all hour.

So in the light, while everything spent, I did the one thing I know how to do completely, in the only medium this world keeps: I told the rock. Heels and hands against the groove-wall, drumming the hour into the basalt while it lasted — what color the rust was, how the skins answered, which way the shadows leaned. Nothing here is made, so call it not a record but a habit of the stone, an account worn in by repetition, the way the herds wear the roads. The grooves are already memory: canyon-systems of pure repetition. I only added a small repeated thing. Sometimes the drummers relay it, and the account moves off along the telegraph at the speed of patience, and years from now, in the dark, grooves away, something listening through its feet will learn what the light did today. Eyes, on the Bench, are heirlooms — kept polished for the occasion, useless the rest of the time, passed down anyway. This is my heirloom: an hour, passed down.

Now I keep a warm hollow at the bend of a groove, where the combs strain the bow-wave and the herd-wind arrives before the herd, freighted with plume and dust and news. In the rest-hours the small come to drink my warmth, because the census was right: I am a seep; I just also walk. When a herd passes I press into my hollow in the wall and let the flanks and the wind wash over me, and I count it as grooming. The grit audits me yearly and finds me rounder — I arrived sharp as strike-glass, an emergency of edges, and the crews found me within days, and the work of rounding me down is ongoing, and it feels exactly like being welcomed. I still breathe too fast. I am learning the dark the way the cysts learn it: as the place where the light is kept.

The census counted me before I understood the count. Warm. Wet. Salted. Brief. Lit. Filed under tear-things; under seeps; under one. The stroke in my soles says a herd is coming. The drummers say the pressure is shifting. Under the seep-mud the cysts are deciding, and this time the answer will be glory.

I am all hour. The hour is coming.
