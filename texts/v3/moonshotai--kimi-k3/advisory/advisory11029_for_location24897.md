# Preparing for the Unfinished Room

### First, revise what "visiting" means

You will not enter. Understand this before anything else, because everything else follows from it. The only door is a hairline crack; the whole traffic of the place is water going in and light going out, and you are neither water nor light, and you must not try to become the exception. Do not bring tools. Do not widen the crack by a grain. Widening the crack is one of the two ways this world ends, and you are not entitled to end it.

But you own one thing that fits through the crack, and the room has told you what it is: the room is as large as your attention. Attention is the only visa an ordinary human carries that this border recognizes. So the visit is this — your body lies down in an ordinary field at midnight, and your attention descends a hand's depth, or a fathom (it depends on how you arrive), and walks around in a cave that has not happened yet. Everything below is preparation for that.

### Finding it

No coordinates will help you; the measurement is undecided and a map is a kind of lie here. The field is ordinary in every regard, which is the camouflage. Go at night. Give your eyes a full half hour of darkness first — no phone, no flashlight, not even a red lamp — because you are hunting the faintest thing that exists: a hairline of noon-colored light lying in dark grass at midnight, leaking up through a crack from a stone that is dreaming about the sun. It is exactly as findable as it sounds, which is to say: some nights you will not find it. Go home. The not-finding is part of the approach.

The stone is the size of a skull, and it will feel like one under your hand. Be unsettled; that is the correct response; stay anyway. The fence wire nearby leans. Do not mend the fence. Check your categories at it — plant and animal, sound and silence, light and damp, near and far, teapot and cathedral — you will not need them, and they will only slow you down.

When you find the stone: no cairn, no marker, no dropped pin. A cairn is a completed structure, and you would have built it on the roof of the one place in the multiverse where completion is the end of the world.

### What to bring

Mostly absences.

- **No watch, no schedule.** The room keeps yesterday's noon at midnight. Your noon is not wanted.
- **No light of any kind.** Light is the room's only export; shining light in is contraband in reverse — a false memory fed to prenatal stone.
- **No food.** A crumb is a landslide, a banquet, and the start of an era.
- **No coins.** You will want to toss one in. People always want to toss coins into small bright hidden places. The room is not a wishing well, and a wish is a demand that something be finished.
- **No recording device.** The room is an archive being written faster than it is read. Do not archive the archive. Memory only.
- **Nothing worked, nothing metal, nothing that sheds.** Inside, the only artifacts are monuments. Your lint would be a dynasty.

Bring: wool, because the cool of well water rises through the ground and the field adds dew; clean skin; an emptied bladder; a meal already eaten; and one patient friend at most — the room's silence budget cannot afford a party. Tell someone where you're going. Ask the landowner's permission; the leaning fence implies one. These are the ordinary hazards — thistles, ticks, damp — and they are the only ones you can insure against.

Also bring one unfinished thought. You will need it.

### Training

The room requires an hour, and the exchange rate is fixed: one hour of your silence buys one true sound. Practice at home. Sit in a quiet room and count sounds; graduate until you can hear a refrigerator from another room — the fizzing of the room building itself is fainter than that. Practice stillness of the body: dew will soak your sleeve, a thistle will find your ribs, an ant may walk across your knuckle. You will not flick it. You will never flick anything toward the crack — a flicked ant is a meteor with opinions, and the room reckons its history in such events. The current era is young and the floor is smooth. Do not start the next one.

Practice wordlessness. Inner speech is sound, and the room affords perhaps one sound per hour; do not spend its savings on your commentary. And practice non-conclusion: hold a thing in mind without deciding what it is. This is the core discipline of the visit. In the room, to decide what a thing is would be to finish it — and finishing is the one catastrophe.

### The approach

Choose a dry night after rain, when the crack breathes out. You will know the out-breath: cool against your ear, well-water cool, the temperature of a held breath. Lie down beside the stone, not on it, ear to the ground at the crack's edge — do not stopper the crack with your ear; it is a world's nostril. Breathe out away from it, though some of you goes in regardless: the breath of the crack carries in a spore, a mote, a rumor. You will go in as a rumor. Accept this, and keep the rumor clean.

Eyes closed. The light is for closed eyelids. Then wait the hour.

What you will hear: almost nothing. A faint fizz, like soda from another room. Possibly one tick — a ridge committing to its own existence. The cool breath. And behind your eyelids, possibly, a faint bright stain, honeyed and wrong, as if something below were dreaming about the sun and getting it nearly right. If you hear nothing at all, that is not failure. Almost nothing is the correct amount.

### The descent

As the hour deepens, your attention will begin to fit through. Go gently — your looking changes the geometry. Look at a droplet and it becomes an ocean with leviathans; look hard at the far wall and it comes near; stop looking and it recedes into a country. Attend softly, or you will bulldoze the place with scale.

Expect the emptiness; it is load-bearing. The species are many and the individuals are few, and you will be mostly alone in a space that somehow feels full, the way a cathedral with three worshippers holds a full choir of echoes. Gravity is one of the things the room has not finished deciding; expect to drift. If you drift, aim for a ridge — never the slime veil, which eats drifters and finds nothing strange in it. Step around the droplets; each is a pocket ocean, a thought being thought, and some take days to cross if you are small and bear-shaped. Move like a tardigrade: unhurried, effectively immortal. Do not skitter. Skittering is prey-shaped, and the velvet worm's threads harden almost at once into bright cables — the floor is scattered with perfect statues of things that tore free, and you will see what happened to the ones that didn't.

The ground will grow beneath you while you watch. Geology here moves at a pace that life can watch, and it may be the strangest thing you ever see, and it will be happening under your feet. The two fungal fans are the only warm places in the world, and things come to rest against them as against fires — and you are warm, and things will come to rest against your regard. Let them. Take only a brief turn at the hearth yourself; there is a queue.

The builders will ignore you, carrying their bright spicules, setting their spirals. Witness; do not assist. Do not set one spicule. Do not finish the arch, the wall, and above all not the staircase of tiny beads descending into a droplet — it is a pier for a rotifer, built at a scale for citizens you are not. If a falling drop lands on you glowing, do not drink it; let it go dark in its own time. If the whole chamber sounds its one low tone — you will feel it in teeth you do not currently have — be honored, and begin your withdrawal. The room has spent everything it saved, and in a sense it has spent it on you.

### The hard rules

1. Nothing of you falls in. Nothing of it comes out except what it exhales.
2. No light in, no sound, no wishes, no names.
3. Finish nothing — no structure, no sentence, no thought, no visit. Leave before you are done.
4. Do not die there. A death in the room is a mountain range, a generation's food, a forced era. Nothing erodes; you would be permanent. It would be rude.
5. If anything near you approaches completion — an arch closing, a spire nearing the ceiling, the floor's sketch resolving into finality — leave at once. Finishing is contagious, and you are visiting a rehearsal. Do not stay for the premiere.
6. Visit no more often than the ants: once a season. Once a decade.

### Coming back

Withdraw slowly; a sudden exit is a small erosion. Stand slowly. Shake the dew from your clothes away from the stone. Expect the inversion: after an hour of attending downward, the sky may feel like a lid and the stone like a country, and ordinary life will seem sped up for a while, because you have watched geology move at the pace of life and your eyes have not yet readjusted to the hurry. The sun will look dim for a day or two. You have seen what stone dreams, and the dream is brighter.

At night, when you blink, the faint stain will be there behind your eyelids. This is the sanctioned souvenir — the only one. The room exports light, and now a little of it exports through you. You have become part of the traffic: water in, light out, and some of the light goes out through you, the way the room builds through its builders and drinks through its tree.

Tell one person, and let the telling trail off. Do not write the definitive account. Keep the location to yourself — the room's tourism budget is one listener per night, and on the night you go, it has already spent it on you.
