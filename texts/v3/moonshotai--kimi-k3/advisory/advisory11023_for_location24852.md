# Preparation Guide: The Chamber at the Base of the Flow

## First, understand what you are visiting

This is not a cave. It is not a ruin. It is a sealed, single-material world the size of a curled sleeping person, packed nearly solid with hardened resin architecture, threaded by a chain of small voids, lit from within by stored light, and filled — completely, permanently — by one standing chord that has been sounding since the moment the last pore closed. Nothing in it has ever changed. Nothing in it can afford to.

Everything about your preparation follows from three facts:

1. **The whole interior is the size of your own body at rest.** The largest open belly is a pantry you cannot stand in; the smallest, a cupped palm. The throats between bellies are crawl-sized at best.
2. **The voids are the loud parts.** The rooms you would crawl through are the antinodes of the standing wave — the loudest places in the loudest world. The still parts are either solid resin or thin planes of hanging pollen dust that you must not touch.
3. **The constitution of the place is the exclusion of water and the impossibility of change.** You are made mostly of water, and you are a thing that changes. You are, by the local definition, a catastrophe wearing boots.

Hold those three facts and the rest of this guide is just working out the details.

## Decide what kind of visit you are making

**The external visit** is available to anyone and costs the world almost nothing. You cup the nodule in both hands — it is exactly the right size for this, which is not a coincidence you should ignore — and you press your ear to the dull, crazed rind. The chord transmits faintly through the shell. You will feel it in the bone of your palm before you hear it. You can hold a strong light behind the nodule and watch the gold body glow, the architecture inside going to shadow and silhouette. Know that every photon you shine in is kept: the walls drink light and release the long average of everything they've ever received. Your lamp becomes, minutely, part of the eternal noon. Give light only if you mean it as a gift.

For many travelers this will be the whole visit, and it is enough. Say so out loud before you decide otherwise.

**The internal visit** — crawling the chain of bellies — requires your transit method to do two difficult things: place you inside at appropriate scale *without breaching the rind* (no drilling, no cracking; a breach admits the outside, and the outside is mostly water and time, both fatal here), and retrieve you with absolute reliability. Test the retrieval twice before you go. If your exit fails inside that chamber, understand precisely what happens: you die in a world that cannot bury you. You will not rot — rot requires water and the world has none — and your body is itself a flood of water that will cloud and craze and begin the world's second history. There is no worse outcome available to a traveler anywhere. It is not just your death; it is the end of the place. If you cannot guarantee the exit, stay outside and hold the nodule and hum to it. That is a real visit. Do not let restlessness tell you otherwise.

## Preparing the body

- **Claustrophobia screening is not optional.** Before you commit, spend long sessions crawling tubes and body-sized passages in the dark, then in monotonous bright light, then with a low drone playing at uncomfortable volume. Panic in one of those throats — thrashing — snaps resin blades, shatters pollen planes, and soaks the world in your sweat. If you cannot stay slow and breathing while squeezed in a roaring golden tube, you cannot go.
- **Condition for the crawl:** hips, core, shoulders. You will be moving on elbows and knees over glass-smooth surfaces, and holding curled stillness for long periods while you rest and listen.
- **Hydration discipline.** Arrive well-hydrated, then stop drinking on a schedule that lets you complete the visit with an empty bladder. There is no urination inside. There is no moisture inside, period. This is a hard constraint, not a courtesy.
- **Skip the emollients.** The air is dry to brittleness and will crack your lips and knuckles within the hour, but anything greasy on your skin transfers to the walls, and every mark is permanent. Moisturize the night before, not the day of.

## Equipment

Everything you carry must obey one rule: **nothing loose, nothing shedding, nothing wet.** Every item tethered or integrated. Count everything in; count everything out. A dropped screw in this world is a foreign body lodged in it forever — a world of one substance, and then your screw.

- **A sealed, smooth-skinned drysuit**, padded at knees and elbows, soft enough not to scratch (scratches are forever), with no Velcro, no exposed zippers, no loose fibers. Soft, smooth footwear. No metal anywhere near the walls.
- **A closed breathing loop** — rebreather with CO₂ scrubber and a desiccant stage so that not one molecule of your breath's moisture escapes. This is the single most important piece of kit. Your exhaled breath is water vapor, and water is the one thing this world has never admitted. The loop also protects you from the terpene-sharp air — pine, pepper, varnish, medicine — which is old, unrenewed, and will burn your airways raw.
- **Dual hearing protection: deep plugs plus the best muffs made.** Treat the chord as instantly damaging at the belly-centers. Even so, the fundamental goes through bone and chest; protection quiets the ears, not the teeth. Accept that you will feel the note more than hear it.
- **Dark, contrast-tinted goggles.** The light is bright, directionless, shadowless, and saturated gold — a gold-out. Depth perception will lie to you because there are no shadows; move as if everything is closer than it looks. The goggles also keep the dessicating air off your eyes.
- **A vibrating wrist timer with interval alarms.** Audible alarms may drown in the chord. Set it before entry: air checks, sweat checks, mood checks, and a hard exit time.
- **A hydration bladder worn inside the suit**, sipped through a sealed valve. Redundant cap. If it leaks, you abort instantly — a leak inside your suit is uncomfortable; a leak outside it is vandalism of the eternal.
- **No lamp.** You will not need one; the walls are the lamp. **Bring darkness instead**: a sleep mask if you intend any long rest, because there is no dark anywhere in that world and your eyes will ache for it.
- **Nothing that burns, nothing scented, nothing that can crumble, no food, no camera flash.** Flash photography pours light into permanent storage — the world keeps it forever. If you must image, use available light only, and accept flat, shadowless, gold-on-gold photographs that will look like nothing and mean everything.

## The crawl

The topology is merciful: a chain, one-dimensional. Belly, throat, belly, throat. You cannot get lost; you can only get stuck. Count the bellies aloud as you pass — your voice, muffled in your own mask, is also how you keep yourself company.

Move slowly, always. Three reasons: haste makes sweat; haste makes static (the walls hold charge, the air is bone-dry, and your crawling body will build a potential — expect small shocks, expect your hair to lift, and never let a discharge startle you into striking a wall); and haste disturbs air that has not been disturbed since the day of closing.

Use the chord as your map. As you enter a throat, the pressure drops — you are crossing a node surface, and if you are lucky you'll see it: a thin plane of golden pollen hanging across the void like breath on glass, a cross-section of the chord's own stillness. **Do not touch the planes. Do not exhale toward them. Do not pass through them.** They are dust gathered onto still air; a breath collapses them, and the collapse is permanent. Approach a node plane's vicinity when you need relief — the sound falls to almost nothing there — but be warned: the near-silence is worse than the roar. In the roar you are accompanied. In the node-silence you discover there is nothing else to hear, and nothing in that world that could hear you, and the combination feels like being gently erased. Rest there briefly. Then crawl back into the loudness and be grateful for it.

Mid-belly the chord peaks — chest, teeth, the small bones of the ear all singing along. Limit your time at the antinode centers. Watch for headache, nausea, fluttering in the chest; any of these is an abort signal.

The route trends downward. The floor curves toward the lowest bowl, and the bellies deepen. There you will find the two sanctuaries:

- **The sprig**: one twig-tip with a handful of narrow leaves, suspended mid-void in the clearest gold, its green dimmed to the idea of green. It is all the vegetation of a world, and it could be gathered in two hands, and it will not object and will not grow. Do not touch it. Look as long as you need.
- **The cord**, at the deepest point: the knotted tissue of the maker itself, the tear the whole world wept from, sealed in the clearest amber of all. Whether it is alive is undecidable — it is exactly as alive as it was at the sealing, and here states do not change. This is the destination of the pilgrimage. Kneel if your suit allows it. Hum the fundamental if you must do anything — you will have learned the note by then, in your molars — but know this before you make a sound: **the chamber's decay is a theory. What you emit is kept.** Your voice, your light, your heat — all of it joins the permanent storage. Say nothing you wouldn't want held forever, without breath, at the bottom of the world.

## Conduct: the few and total rules

1. **Introduce no water.** Sealed suit, sealed breath, sealed bottles, empty bladder, dry skin. A single drop begins history again.
2. **Take nothing.** Not a bead, not a shaving, not the dust on your knees if you can help it. Nothing leaves.
3. **Leave nothing.** Count your gear. Nothing enters.
4. **Touch almost nothing.** Every contact is permanent — a smear, a scratch, a collapsed plane of pollen that hung in stillness since before there were surfaces.
5. **Keep your own time.** The world will not remind you to leave. Eternal noon does not schedule your fatigue. When the timer says go, go — especially if you don't want to.

That last point deserves emphasis. The place exerts a pull that travelers should be warned about plainly: the enchantment of storage. Nothing is happening, and after an hour of it, some part of you will propose that you could stop happening too — that you could stay, curl up in a belly, and be kept. This thought is the local hazard the way crevasses are the hazard on a glacier. The countermeasure is the pre-committed exit time and one true sentence, memorized in advance: *the world will be exactly the same in a thousand years; I am missing nothing by leaving.* You can always go. You have missed nothing. Go.

## Afterward

Ground yourself before you handle your gear; you'll carry the charge of the place out with you. Expect the chord to persist in your ears for hours or days — a memory-tinnitus. It fades. The original does not, and that asymmetry will sit with you.

Expect your own world to feel briefly wrong: too eventful, too wet, too full of things falling and rotting and arriving. Expect a changed relationship to dripping taps, to honey mid-pour, to held breath — yours and others'. You have been inside the world's most complete stillness, disguised as its loudest sound. Ordinary rooms will feel restless for a while.

And if you only ever made the external visit — if you held the nodule in both hands, felt the note come up through your palms, and set it back down unopened — know that you did not settle for less. You visited a world whose entire constitution is the keeping-out of everything, and you honored it in the only way that costs nothing: you learned its note, and you let it keep yours.
