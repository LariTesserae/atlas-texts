**The Kiln**

Come at it from below and you won't see it coming, because the plateau makes its own sky. The escarpment runs nine thousand kilometers without a gap — a single fault scarp, glassy-faced, fluted like something extruded rather than broken, its columnar basalt still sharp enough to cut rope. Nothing about it is weathered. The talus at its base hasn't rounded. The veins of native copper and iron that thread the rock face lie bright and unoxidized, as if the stone were poured last week and nobody has had time to let it tarnish. You climb for days through air that should be thinning toward cold, and it never does. It gets warmer. It gets dimmer. Both of these turn out to be byproducts.

The dimness is not absence. It is consumption. Above the plateau's surface stands a canopy of leaves so matte, so totally black, that they read as cuts in the world — shaped holes where foliage should be. They drink nearly all the light that arrives, and what little they pass falls into a mid-story of condensation combs, and what escapes the combs reaches the ground as a kind of exhausted green dusk, even at noon. Above the canopy, a permanent haze of spores and exhaled vapor does the same work from the other side, so the sun here is a rumor, a brightening of the murk that moves across the day. What illumination the ground gets, it makes itself: cold blue tracery in the decomposer lace underfoot, and sudden seams of galvanic glow along the metal veins when the current that runs through the food web spikes — when something is killed, the earth itself flinches light for an instant. You learn to read the ground's flickers the way you'd read weather elsewhere.

The quiet is administered. That is the only way to put it. Palm-sized membrane-flyers — the locals of this air, if anything so transient can be called local — drift in swarms like slow smoke, and their wings are acoustic traps, and where a swarm hangs, sound simply fails. They are called hushwings, or would be, if there were anyone here to name things. They converge on the loud places before the loudness finishes: a rockfall is silenced mid-tumble, thunder is harvested from its own clouds before it can speak. The storms here are enormous and voiceless — lightning moving through the manufactured cloud deck like thought through a skull, all flash and no statement. You watch weather the way the deaf watch arguments. What survives the harvest is a small list of permitted sounds: fog-water ticking off the comb-leaves into a dew-pond, too minor to be worth collecting; the expansion-tick of warm stone at dawn; your own footsteps, which arrive a half-second late, as if the ground were deciding whether to allow them. The hushwings take the rest. Sound is a resource here. The silence you walk through is a worked-out mine.

Water keeps appointments rather than cycles. There is no rain to speak of, most days — only the fog-combs, dripping their harvest into basins, and the dew-ponds themselves, which arrive on a schedule set by nothing in the sky. A pond condenses in its basin at some unannounced hour, holds for a day or two while everything in the district crowds to it — and everything does, the shores pressed three deep with drinkers, the air above it thick with wings — and then it is recalled. It does not evaporate. It leaves, all at once, and the bed it leaves behind is not even damp, is a shaped dryness, and sometimes more than that: a pond-shaped clarity in the air that bends light the way water does, but isn't. A ghost, scheduled ahead of its own return. Occasionally the cloud deck discharges warm process-rain, faintly metallic, tasting of coins, and the whole plateau drinks in an hour, and then the damp closes back over everything like a held breath resumed.

The forests are drafts. You will stand in a stand of trees that was not there last week, and you will know this because the bark has not yet decided on a texture, because the leaves are placeholders — simple green ovals, default leaves, the shape of the idea of a leaf — filling in over days with veins and serrations and intent. Sometimes you find two versions of the same tree side by side: the old draft and its revision, the elder already going translucent at the edges, fading with the patience of something that was never promised permanence. Nothing here is finished; everything is a version with a revision pending. The vegetation layers densely, near-touching — canopy, combs, a shrub-stratum of fermentation fronds exhaling warmth, and under all of it the lace: a pale decomposer carpet that is, so far as anyone could determine, a single organism spanning the entire plateau, the floor of the world and, in some sense that is hard to shake, its management. There are no straight lines anywhere, but there is repetition — the same cove-shape recurring in the escarpment bays for ten thousand kilometers, the same tree spacing held to within a hand's width across districts that have never touched. Wildness with tolerances. A wilderness with a specification.

The fauna are abundant the way a furnace is warm: through throughput. At dawn the lace buds off the day's ephemerals — creatures at every scale from midge to the size of a elk, hatching fully formed, living their whole arc in the light-hours, mating, dying by dark in a soft settling of bodies like snow you cannot hear, the hushwings having attended. Their corpses are tomorrow's soil, which is tomorrow's fauna, and the same acre supports ten times the lives any permanent population could, by the simple trick of not keeping any. The permanent residents are stranger. Follow a slope and you can watch the whole production line run: lichen mats mining the bright basalt for metal; grazer-slugs crawling fixed lanes — lanes not worn by time but by repetition, fresh since yesterday, identical everywhere — concentrating the metal in their shells; gleaners working the lanes for shell; and at the slope's foot, the vats, barrel-sized sessile stomachs sunk in the earth, receiving the dead and the molted and exhaling a warm spore-steam that rises to feed the cloud deck. The weather is exhalation. The warmth is waste heat. The plateau is warm because it is working, and the deepest basins, where the vats cluster thickest and the lace glows steadiest, are too hot to cross — forges without fire, busy and silent and dim.

And that is the thing you eventually understand: all of it is industry, and none of it is built. There is not a single artifact on the plateau, not a wall, not a tool, not a straight edge made by intention. Pick up anything — it is alive, or it was alive, or it is scheduled. Yet the signatures of scaled production are everywhere: the standardized lanes, the repeated coves, the raw inputs being drawn in from every direction — light, sound, fog, metal, yesterday's bodies — and the product rolling out continuously. The product is the world itself, revised. A factory whose entire output is its own next shift.

The revisions come as shifts, and the shifts are when the place shows its strangest face. A swarm of hushwings converges on a district ahead of time — they are scheduled; they are staff — and the silence deepens past silence, and then the hillside, say, is unmade. Not destroyed. Unselected. It loses its properties one at a time: color first, then texture, then its hold on your attention, so that you find you cannot look at it, your gaze sliding off like a hand off glass — and when you can look again there is a draft hillside, placeholder-simple, already filling in. What is destroyed outright leaves something worse: a shaped absence, a tree-shaped nothing, a boulder-shaped nothing, and these are solid. You walk around them. The vines grow around them too, as if the unmade thing still had rights. Whole fields of them stand in the older districts — the Unmade — and the dense young forest threads between the absences with the courtesy of a crowd around graves.

At the seams, causality frays. You find the crater a day before the impact. The pond's ghost arrives before the pond. Your footsteps precede your feet, once, and you stand very still for a while after that. Errors in the new fauna are kept: a grazer with two left sides browses a lane, thriving, tolerated the way variance is tolerated in any confident process — not a failure, a flourish. And the geology stays young because it is continuously reborn; the sharp edges are not preserved against erosion, they are simply new, fruit-gloss fresh, a continent with no past tense.

The rules, so far as a visitor can hold them: nothing is finished. Sound is property. Water keeps appointments. Absences are solid. Warmth means work. And do not stay past your drafting. Because the plateau will notice you — it notices everything — and it will begin, politely, to revise. Your shadow loses detail first. Then your footprints start falling into standard spacing. Then, one dusk, as the day's ephemerals settle into the lace like unwitnessed snow and the cold blue tracery brightens and tomorrow's drafts begin rising under your feet, you find a version of yourself asleep in the lace — placeholder-simple, default features, scheduled for completion — and you understand that your footsteps have been arriving late because someone has been following you at a respectful distance, and it is you, being made.
