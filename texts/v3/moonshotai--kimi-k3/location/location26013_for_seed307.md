The ridge is the first thing: a spine of pale, frost-split stone running above the cloud deck, so high that the air tastes of almost nothing and daylight falls hard and ordinary, an honest sun in an honest sky. The clouds lie below like a slow sea. Nothing about the approach suggests a place. That is the first rule of it — the notch is hidden, folded into the ridge's lee, visible only from one brief angle a bird might hold for a wingbeat and then lose. You find it by noticing that the steam is wrong.

It rises from the fold in slow threads, and some of the threads sink. Vapor that should climb instead pours gently downward along the rock, pooling in hollows like a liquid. Follow the threads and the fold opens: a cup in the stone, no bigger than a small room, a courtyard for one. It would fit in your two arms' span of attention. It contains, by any honest accounting, a world.

The walls are glass. Not weathered glass — *young* glass, sinter and obsidian-fresh mineral laid down in thin ringing sheets, sharp enough to take skin, none of it softened, none of it rounded, because nothing here has had time to be old. Erosion has not begun; accretion is still in charge. At the back of the cup, behind the pond, a seam splits the wall, and the seam is warm to the touch, and if you put your eye to it between pulses you can see that the rock inside is not quite decided — a dull light moves in it, slow as thought. The place is not ruins. The place is a draft. The mountain is still writing this paragraph of itself.

The pond at the cup's floor is the size of a bathtub, and it simmers. Here the altitude does something kind: with air this thin, water boils cool, rolling and trembling at a heat a body could briefly survive. So the pond is allowed to be both boiling and inhabited, and it takes full advantage. The water cycle of an entire climate runs in this room, fast and closed: steam rises off the surface, climbs the warm walls, meets the thin cold air at the rim, and condenses — and then the droplets misbehave. Some fall, politely. Others climb. They bead on the glass and run upward in slow silver lines, returning to the lip, where they gather into a weeping film that slides back down. The walls sweat. The floor is saturated sponge-warm sinter. Water is never far below any surface here; it is the place's second skeleton. And sometimes — not often, not on any schedule — a second skin of water forms a hand's width above the pond, a ceiling of pond, a surface tension held against sense, and things swim in it upside down, their backs to the water below, their feet toward the sky.

There are no plants. Nothing green, nothing rooted, nothing that waits on sunlight. Every niche a plant would hold is held instead by animals, and the animals are beyond counting — this is the density of a reef compressed into a teacup. The walls are not really walls: they are colonies, generations of sessile things building chimneys and fans and flutes of accreted mineral, each tube occupied, each fan filtering the steam with feathery mantles that flush rust-red, sulfur-pale, opal-white as they signal one another in color — silently, always silently. Break the glass anywhere (you shouldn't) and you break someone's house, someone's grandmother's house, someone's geology. The rock of the cup is mostly former animal. The pond's water column is stratified by heat, and each degree has its own nation: translucent swarms hanging in stacked layers like sediment of living dust, thicker near the vent where the creatures are more mineral than flesh, quick and many-legged at the cool rim where the gleaners patrol. The surface skin is walked by skaters. The steam itself is inhabited — motes, drifting aeroplankton, whole lives spent falling upward through vapor, sipping the climbing droplets. There is no empty volume anywhere in the cup. Even the air is livestock.

Among them move the hunters, and the hunters use tools. Watch the gleaners at the young glass: they pressure-flake it, striking and pressing with forelimb and mouthpart until a shard comes away with an edge like a broken promise, and they carry the shard — always carry it, nothing is ever set down — and use it to pry the tube-dwellers from their chimneys. A diver among them performs the most elegant technology in the world: before slipping into the hottest layer, it gathers a bubble of condensate at the surface and holds it over its breathing pores, a glass lung, and descends into the simmer carrying its own atmosphere. This is the whole of their craft, and it leaves nothing behind. No midden, no wall, no mark. The floor of the cup is bare of anything made. A technology with no artifacts at rest — tools as behavior, culture with no architecture, a whole cleverness that evaporates the moment it is dropped.

Now the laws, because they are local, and you will feel them within a minute of arriving.

Nothing here is *now*. Motion leaves a wake of faint translucent echoes — a half-breath behind the event, the thing happens again, softly, ghosted, and fades like breath off glass. A single gleaner trails one or two of its recent selves. A swarm, dense as the swarms are, drags time visibly: it moves like smoke, like aurora, hundreds of bodies each followed by their own immediate pasts, the crowd thickening the delay the way a crowd thickens heat. Sound obeys the lag too, or suffers worse. What little sound exists here — the simmer-hush, the tick of cooling glass, the soft give of a crust collapsing — arrives late to your ears, arrives faint, and does not cross the rim. A shelf can shear off and splash, and from three steps outside the fold, nothing. The cup keeps its sounds the way a held breath is kept. Only one sound is punctual: the seam ticks, now and then, the drafting-point of the mountain, the one thing happening on time because it has not yet finished becoming a thing.

And the pond does not reflect the sky. It reflects the cup a few minutes from now. The sun is in it, but wrong; the swarms are in it, but elsewhere in their rounds. The hunters know this. They crouch at the margin and watch the water, and strike not at the prey but at the prey's appointment. If you were to kneel and look, you would see yourself still standing at the rim, deciding whether to come down. The present, in this place, is something that happens elsewhere. The cup runs on a delay; its reflection runs ahead; between the two, you are always either arriving or already gone.

The vent breathes. Every few minutes the seam exhales: the pond rises a finger's width, the steam thickens until the rim disappears, the swarms sink in their layers like silt stirred and resettling, the climbing droplets reverse and it rains upward-falling condensate from the lip, and the hunters all surface at once in the same held pause. Then the ebb. Then the rounds again. Over this, the ordinary day: the sun crosses the notch and for perhaps two hours the direct light stands in the cup and every colony flares its signals at once, a silent shouting in mineral color, and then the light goes and the cup lives by sky-glow and its own warmth. The heat never leaves — the place cups it like hands around an ember, even when the rim outside grows feathers of frost that reach downward into the updraft and melt mid-fall.

And the cup is growing. The colonies lay their glass inward, layer on layer, measurably, daily; the pond is smaller than it was a century ago and the walls thicker. This is not a landscape eroding toward ruin. It is a landscape secreting itself, slowly sealing — and in some deep future the fold will close entirely into a heart of warm stone with the simmering water inside it, a pocket world carried in the mountain's body like an egg, like a held note. It may be the youngest place in the world. It renews its youth every morning at the seam.

Evening comes the way it comes at altitude, fast. The light withdraws up the walls, the steam takes the last of it and glows from inside, and the lag deepens in the dimness — the day repeating itself faintly all around, an hour's worth of echoes layered translucent over the darkening glass, the swarms' afterimages rising like the ghosts of the heat. Sound pools at the bottom of the cup and stays there. Nothing carries out except the steam, climbing now in the cold, leaving the fold and thinning into the cloud deck below — the only export of the place, its one message to the world, delivered as weather.

Stand at the rim and you will hear your own heart, late, in your own ears. Below you the clouds close over. The mountain keeps the notch the way a sleeper keeps a dream — completely, and without any evidence.
