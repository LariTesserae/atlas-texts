The country above is dry and means to stay that way. Thorn scrub, dust the color of old bone, heat that lifts off the ground in visible ribbons. Rain is a rumor here — it arrives twice a year if it arrives at all, and the land has arranged itself around the absence. Nothing green should thrive in this. Nothing green does, except one leaf.

The leaf lies flat against the dust, broad as two hands spread together, glossy, impossibly green, the green of deep shade and deep water. Beside it, flush with the ground, is a mouth. A pore, no wider than a forearm's length across, its rim smooth as worn horn. Stand near it at dusk and you will feel it inhale — a long, unhurried pull that streams the dust inward and tugs at your clothes. At dawn it exhales, and the air that comes up is cool, humid, and smells of crushed stems. The whole body of the thing is below. The leaf is only its signature.

This all appeared after the last great storm, weeks ago. Nothing here has been anything for long.

---

The descent is easy, which should worry you. The throat slopes down at a shallow angle, fluted and ridged, its surface covered in fine hairs that all point down. Going in, they lie flat under your hands like stroked fur. Coming out, they resist — each hair a small barb, the whole passage a patient argument against leaving. The ones who come here bring cord for this reason, and tie it to the leaf's thick stem at the surface, and do not come at all without it.

The warmth rises as you descend. The country above is hot, but this is a different warmth — blood-warm, breath-warm, the warmth of a held hand. The sound rises too. First the breath, tidal and slow, whistling softly through the throat's flutes at the turn of its cycle. Then a pulse felt through the floor before it is heard: a double beat, a long gurgling ascent of water somewhere in the walls. Then, as the passage opens out, the ticking — thousands of tiny snaps, like knuckles cracking, like a forest fire heard from very far away and played at the wrong speed. It is the sound of water columns breaking and re-knitting in the warm vessels, and in the heat of afternoon it never stops. The room is loud the way a shell is loud.

---

The chamber is small — three body-lengths across at the widest, an intimate room, a pocket. And it is full of daylight.

This is the first wrong thing, though you may not catch it immediately, because the light is so ordinary in quality. It is white, full-spectrum, the light of open sky at midmorning. There is no opening that could admit it. The walls themselves carry it — great translucent veins run through the green tissue, fiber-bundles that drink sun from the single leaf far above and conduct it down through meters of soil, so the walls glow from within like a lamp's shade. When a cloud crosses the sun overhead, the chamber dims a few heartbeats later, the dimming arriving down the stem like news. At night the same veins carry a weak silver. Yet the light in the air behaves as though none of this were true: shadows fall downward, away from an overhead sun that is not present, and at midday a bright coin of light rests on the ceiling tissue — an image of a sun this room has never seen, projected through miles of nothing.

Your shadow takes a moment to follow you across the floor. It peels off the wall reluctantly, like something the wall was keeping. And it is: the tissue holds a faint print of where you stood, a greener green, and forgets it by tomorrow.

The floor gives underfoot like cork over water, tacky in the dry spells, gelid after rain. Between the light-veins the wall-skin is matte and freckled with pores. Great vascular columns arch from floor to ceiling like ribs — the room reads as architecture, as a nave built by something that has never seen a building, only been one. In the depth of the translucent walls you can see the plant's whole brief biography in cross-section: dark lenses that are older chambers, sealed off and swallowed by new growth; the pale lace of a tree root system it grew around and digested; a stone, mid-meal, held in amber-green. It is weeks old and it already has a past, compressed into the walls like rings in a tree that grew in a single season.

---

It is not wet here. It is damp, which is a discipline. One quadrant of the wall runs cooler than the rest, where the light-veins converge, and there the air's moisture condenses in slow beads that crawl sideways along the tissue, defying the downward pull that weakens near any surface of this room. This is the weeping wall, and it is where water is taken from. There are also pools — two, sometimes three, no bigger than a basin, sunk in the floor. Their water is slightly sweet and faintly luminous when disturbed, and it is not, strictly, water: it swarms with the plant's loose cells, each one green, each one swimming under its own power. The damp of this place is alive. The dryness above has been robbed to make it, drop by conducted drop.

Once a day, at the peak of the heat, the ceiling pores open all at once and the room makes its own weather. A brief, warm, indoor rain — drumming on the floor-tissue, hammering the pools, loud as applause. Most drops fall down. Some fall slowly, in arcs, as gravity softens. Occasionally, near the walls, one goes up.

---

The middle of the room is empty. This is the point of it.

The whole vast green body — leaf, throat, walls, vessels, roots that drink the deep rare water of the dry country — exists to enclose a void, and the void is near-perfect. Almost nothing per volume. A hollowness tended like a garden. Except: hanging in the emptiness, at heart-height and higher and lower, motionless or turning so slowly you must look away and back to be sure, are the things the room has kept. A knapped flake of chert, its edge still sharp. A moth dried to paper, wings spread. A bead of hardened golden resin. A twist of cord-fiber. A small pebble. An empty seed-husk from no plant that grows under this sky. A single long hair.

Reach toward them and your hand slows as if moving through syrup. Looking is permitted. The constellation is curated — the room selects one thing from what passes through it, and holds it in the air itself, and the arrangement is not random, though it is not yet finished either. It grows by one object a season. It is beginning to describe a shape.

---

One animal lives here. A salamander, the length of a forearm, pale, its limbs translucent, and beneath its skin a green that is not its own: algae live in its cells, and it basks in the conducted daylight on the weeping wall, grazing the felt of moss that is the plant growing its own younger stage upon itself, drinking light both directly and at one remove. Even the fauna here is part-plant. It swims at rain-time. It knows which pools merely hold water and which digest, and it harvests what the digesting pools drown — the occasional beetle or moth that falls through the throat from the world above, an event, a meal, a kept thing afterward. It clicks at the echoes. It does not appear to age.

The echoes deserve explaining. Sound is never lost in this room; it is delayed and translated. Clap once, and an hour later a small pattering begins somewhere in the ceiling — your clap, returned as weather. The room is loud because nothing is absorbed; every creak of the walls growing longer at night, every snap of a water column, every drip, is stored and given back altered, greener. The ones who visit say: never speak a name inside. The room keeps names. At night it practices them in drip-code and slow creak, getting the vowels closer each season.

Beneath all of it, the lowest sound: a hum, a bottle-note, cold. It comes from the seam.

---

Behind one of the great ribs, the wall goes thin. A lens of tissue no thicker than a meniscus, its edges sharp and fresh — scar tissue, the youngest thing in this young place. Look through it and you do not see soil. You see a green that is not the green of leaves, a light with weather in it, somewhere that is raining upward. Cold radiates from it, the only cold in the room. The seed this whole body grew from fell through that thinness from somewhere else, and the seam has been healing since, a hair's width narrower between one visit and the next, its hum rising in pitch as it closes.

---

The visitor comes after the big rains, alone, with a knapped stone blade, a gourd, a coil of cord spun from this plant's own dried light-fibers — cord that faintly glows in the dark, so that the technology of this place is made of the place. Water is drawn from the weeping wall only, never the pools. A little fiber is cut. No names are spoken. Nothing is built; nothing made stays made here. What is left behind is either taken away, grown over within days, or kept — the chert flake hanging in the void was the visitor's, once, before it broke. When it is time to leave, the visitor waits for the dawn exhale, and the breath of the room carries them up the barbed throat the way a seed is carried out of a fruit. They are expelled. They are dispersed.

---

The schedule runs on: dawn exhale, the long brightening, the afternoon chorus of snapping water and the brief loud rain, the dusk inhale that sucks at the throat like a door being pulled to, the silver nights, the crackle of growth, the returned sounds, the green animal clicking back at the dark. The chamber migrates slowly through the body; new rooms open and old ones seal into the walls as dark lenses. The seam narrows. The constellation turns.

The understanding, among those who come, is that the room is not a place in the world but a thing the world is briefly holding. When the seam closes, the chamber will finish — it will seal from the parent plant like fruit from stem, and go the way the seed came: through the thin places, to some other dry country under some other sky, where it will open as a breathing pore beneath a single impossible leaf, carrying its tended emptiness, its green animal, its kept things, and its memory of a clap that became rain.

For now it is here, and young, and loud, and almost nothing at all. Above: dust, thorn, one leaf, a cool breath on your ankle at dusk. Below: daylight where no sky is, water that swims, and a void, carefully filling.
