The heat finds you before the light does, and that is the first wrongness, because the heat does not come from the sky. It comes up through the ground, out of the water, off the leaves — from everywhere at once. Above you is a ceiling of warm vapor, low and permanent, glowing a dim sourceless gray, the color of milk in a dark room. Light arrives from all directions equally. Nothing in this valley casts a shadow, because there is no direction for a shadow to fall in. If there is a sun above the ceiling, no one has ever seen its shape. It could be noon. It could be midnight. Those words have no address here.

Then the sound arrives — or rather, you arrive inside it, because it was already total. It is loud and layered and it never swells and it never fades. Somewhere beneath everything is a low continuous note, felt more in the sternum than heard: water flashing to steam in deep cracks, young rock settling into itself, the slow business of a valley being built. Over that, the drone of insects in their uncountable millions, a middle register with the texture of cloth being torn endlessly without ever finishing. Over that, calls — wet throats, wingbeats, the slap of something heavy sliding into a pool. And threading through it all, ceaseless, the sound of dripping. Because it does not rain here. The ceiling condenses directly onto every surface. Every leaf weeps. Every stone weeps. After an hour, you weep too — the air is already saturated, so your sweat cannot leave you, and instead the atmosphere beads on your skin and runs. You have become one of the valley's surfaces. You are part of the water cycle now.

The valley itself is low — a broad basin of gentle relief, nothing rising more than a few tens of meters — and it is young in a way that is difficult to hold in the mind, because it has apparently always been young. At its heart runs a low ridge the color of a crow's wing, glassy and warm to the touch, too warm for a bare palm in places. This is where the land is happening. New stone wells up along the seam at roughly the pace of fingernails growing — soft, mineral, steaming faintly — and the jungle eats it at precisely the same pace. Walk its length and you can see the whole frontier standing still: raw black rock, then a gray-green pioneer film darkening it, then a felt of mosses whose roots etch the stone like acid, then the first broad leaves, then the full weight of the forest. The gradient is perhaps fifty meters wide and it does not move. Life advances at the speed of geology, and geology is born at the speed of appetite, and the boundary between them is as fixed as a scar that has decided never to heal and never to close.

Erosion has never happened here. Water does not carve this valley; it stands, and seeps, and rises. The water table lies exactly at the surface of the ground, as if the land were a sponge lifted just barely out of a bath. Press your foot down and the print fills immediately, and within moments the living mat has drunk the water and knitted shut over the wound. There is no soil in the geological sense — no weathered mineral, no sediment, nothing carried and dropped. What you walk on is a living felt, meters thick: roots grown on roots grown on the compacted memory of roots, springy and saturated, flexing slightly underfoot like the deck of something afloat. In places the mat is partly animal — colonial things in the warm mud, filtering, breathing, so that the ground itself seems to respire in slow tidal pulses. It is difficult, standing on it, to say with confidence where vegetation ends and fauna begins, or whether the distinction is one the valley bothers with.

The vegetation is layered past counting, because there is no empty volume for it to decline into. From the waterline to the canopy, every surface carries something: leaves so broad and dark they are nearly black, angled toward the dim gray brightness of the ceiling; vines on vines; plants rooted entirely in the bodies of other plants, which are rooted in the bodies of other plants. Fungi do the work that weather cannot — shelves and threads and pale foams that take the dead apart the instant they fall, so that nothing here is ever *merely* dead, nothing lies fallow, nothing accumulates. In the understory, some of the fungal mats give off a faint green-gold luminescence, barely more than a rumor of light, but in the dimness it reads as landscape lighting. Between the glowing floor and the gray ceiling, light comes faintly from below as well as above, and the understory has no shadow side at all.

The fauna is not a presence so much as a pressure. The air has a grain to it. Insects move through it the way plankton moves through seawater, and everything else moves through the insects. Every pool seethes — larvae, swimmers, translucent-skinned things that never surface, fish that live their whole lives in the warm dark between the roots. There is no breeding season, because there is no season of any kind; every moment is the mating season and the dying season, and eggs are being laid in every crevice at every instant, and something is hatching somewhere always, and something is being eaten somewhere always, and the rates are exact. Voices join the chorus and leave it, but the chorus itself is invariant, the way a waterfall's roar is invariant though every drop of it is new. After some hours your brain does the only thing it can and stops registering the sound, and then the loudest thing in the valley is the shape of your own thoughts.

The pools where the hot springs rise are the temperature of blood, some of them hotter, steaming into air that is already steam. Where the mineral water spills it lays down crusts of white and rust-orange sinter in low terraced lips, and at the margins of these, where the heat slackens, the green film takes hold within arm's reach of water that would scald you. Everything here lives closer to its limit than seems advisable, and has done so forever, and will do so forever, because nothing about the arrangement ever drifts.

That is the rule of the place, underneath all the others: nothing drifts. The same stone arrives forever and is forever eaten. The same water condenses, drips, soaks, transpires, and rises again, balanced to the drop. Gaps open in the canopy and close at equal rates, so the pattern of light on the forest floor is, in aggregate, eternal. Trees do not fall — they subside, digested from within, gently lowered back into the loop. There is no peat, no sediment, no fossil record, no ring in any trunk. The valley keeps no archive of itself. It has no past, not because it is new, but because it has arranged to remain new indefinitely — genesis as a steady state, the embryonic held at the moment of becoming, becoming forever and never once arriving.

And so the valley cannot be marked. Blaze a trunk and the bark knits over in days. Trample the mat and it rises behind you like water closing over a stone. There is no path here, has never been a path, though things move constantly through the growth. Nothing made has ever existed in this place — no straight line, no cut edge, no placed stone, no ash, no cord, no mark. The land has never seen a tool, and behaves as though tools were a category error, a rumor from a world with looser rules.

You never see more than a few dozen meters in any direction — the vapor and the growth close the view — and yet the basin extends far past that, a full region, a day or more across. You know its openness the way you know the ocean from a boat at night: not by sight but by sound, and by sameness. Walk for a day and the land around you is not identical to where you began, but it is indistinguishable — the same features in the same proportions, the seam and the mats and the weeping pools and the drowning forest recurring like phrases in a very long sentence with no punctuation. With no sun, no shadow, no wind-shift, no landmark that can hold a name, distance quietly stops accumulating. Travelers here circle without circling. The valley does not trap you. It simply declines to register that you have moved.

Things happen in the valley — a thousand things a second — but nothing changes. Every event is perfectly cancelled by some other event, every birth by a death, every arrival of stone by an equal appetite. Standing in the hot dim roar of it, you come to understand that you are carrying the only accumulating thing in the entire basin: your own memory, your own small archive of before. The valley drinks your sweat and adds it to the loop. It closes over your footprints. It will keep nothing of you.

It has decided — or more likely it never decided, it simply is — to remain forever almost-born. And the becoming is total, and it is very loud, and it is warm as blood, and it does not change.
