There is no way in. That is the first fact of the place, and everything else follows from it. No passage connects these caverns to any surface; there may not *be* a surface, only stone above stone above stone, warm all the way up, until the stone is crushed too dense to hold a hollow. The world is sealed the way an egg is sealed, and like an egg it contains everything it will ever have.

But suppose your senses could be lowered through the rock like a plumb line, down past the crushed zone, down into the open dark. What arrives first is not sight — there is almost nothing to see with — but warmth. The stone is warm as a wall that has stood all afternoon in sun, though no sun has ever touched it. The air is warm in the lungs like a held breath. There is no cold anywhere in this world, no draft that bites, no stone that steals heat from a bare foot. Warmth is not a feature here. Warmth is the medium.

Then the dark, which is total in a way that surface darkness never is. Then the quiet, which is even more total. Then, under both, faint as a fingertip on a table: the pulse.

The whole world flexes, gently, on a long slow period — squeezed from outside by something massive and unseen that swings around it eternally through the black. Nothing here has ever seen the thing that squeezes it. Nothing here has a concept of *outside* at all. But the pulse is real, and the warmth comes from it — the endless tender kneading of the stone keeps the world at blood-heat — and every living thing in the labyrinth knows the pulse the way a fish knows water. It is the only clock. It has never once varied.

The caverns themselves are young. This is the strange thing, the thing that takes longest to understand. The stone is fresh: glassy flow-surfaces frozen mid-ripple, colonnades of hexagonal pillars with edges that could cut, fracture-plains sharp as the hour they split. Nothing is rounded. Nothing is worn. There is no sand, no gravel, no soil, no dust upon the floors — because dust is made by weathering, and nothing here weathers. The floors are bare young rock, swept only by a wind that has nothing to carry. Footprints do not keep on such stone. Time leaves no track.

The labyrinth is vast beyond any honest reckoning. There are vaults a continent wide, whose ceilings no sense has ever found — not the drifters who ride the high currents, not the ceiling-folk who read stone through their palms, not even sound, which travels far here and returns from those heights thinned to nothing. In the greatest vaults the warm air moves in standing currents, slow rivers of wind that blow one way, always, driven by gradients that never change. A haze hangs at mid-height in the big chambers, permanent, the only dust the world owns, kept forever aloft. It is the closest thing to a sky. And along the high ridges, where the standing wind shears over sharp stone, faint coronas smolder — cold violet-grey brushes of charge, soundless, steady. It is the closest thing to daylight. It never brightens and it never dims.

Weather exists here, but only as geography. Each vault keeps its own eternal climate: this one a degree warmer, this one brimstone-rich, this one with a stronger draft along its floor. Nothing fronts, nothing storms, nothing seasons. To ask the weather is to ask where you are.

Down, the air thickens. The deep shafts descend until the warm gas is pressed into something that is no longer quite gas — a heavy sea with a true surface, sensed as pressure, never seen. The Heavy Sea is perfectly still. It has no tides, because nothing that moves it ever changes; it has no waves, because no storm has ever crossed it. The only ripples it has ever known are the ones creatures make entering it, and each ripple spends itself and the surface forgets. There are swimmers below that boundary, dense-bodied, slow, doing their quiet chemistry in the crushing warmth. There are things up in the haze that have never touched stone in their lives — buoyant, membraned drifters, jellyfish of the warm air, riding the standing winds in a ribbon that circles each great vault on a route that never varies. You could navigate by them. They are more reliable than stars, and there are no stars.

And the stone itself is alive with circuitry. The rock is veined — native copper, graphite seams, sulfide lodes — and the steady warmth drives a faint, constant potential across the formations. The world is a weak, enormous battery that never runs down, because what it spends is always returned. On the charged faces grow the crusts: felted mineral mats, somewhere between lichen and crystal, not alive in any way a surface world would recognize, accreting at one edge and dissolving at the other, holding their shapes the way a standing wave holds its shape. They smell of ozone and bitter metal. Against a palm they hum with stored potential. On slightly cooler ceilings — a half-degree cooler, no more — sulfur sublimes and deposits as pale crystalline blooms: the only flowers in this world, mineral flowers, and they never wilt, because there is no such thing as wilting.

The herds graze the crusts. They are broad as doors and low, a dozen jointed legs under shells like cooled slag, and they harvest charge through tongues braided from bright conductive fibers, licking potential off the felted stone the way cattle crop grass. They are everywhere — the vault floors are paved with them, flank pressed to warm flank, a crowding with no aggression in it. When they sleep, they sleep against bare rock and give the charge back. Grazing is charging; sleeping is grounding. The herds are the world's way of moving charge in patterns — a current that also experiences itself.

Above them, the ceiling-folk crawl inverted across the roofs, long-fingered, reading the stress-songs of the stone through their palms, tapping messages to one another *through* the rock — felt, not heard. The crack-folk live inside the walls, in the fresh fractures, sifting what the haze lets fall. Filter-towers of glassy sponge stand in the wind-paths, straining the eternal dust, and once in a great while — when the pulse swells to its rare greatest crest — the towers of an entire vault resonate, a single soft tone, and the vaults chord together across a planet. It is the world's heartbeat made audible. When it sounds, everything pauses. No one knows why anything pauses. The pausing is simply part of the pattern, the way the song is.

Here is the thing the herds know and cannot say: no one has ever died here, and no one has ever been born. The cast is fixed. Every animal in the labyrinth condensed out of the same settling chemistry that made the caverns, in the world's single forming moment, and its body has maintained itself at equilibrium ever since. Wounds close. Shed plates regrow. Nothing ages, because aging is a drift from balance and nothing here drifts. There are no corpses, so there is no carrion, so there are no scavengers, no predators — what would predation even remove? Conflict exists, but it is an economy, not a war: thieves that steal accumulated charge in a quick draining bite, rivals that duel in discharge, rearing to bleed potential at each other until one yields, dimmed, and withdraws to graze itself rich again. Status is stored as voltage. A creature fat with charge shows it in ember-patches along its shell, deep red, dim as a coal seen through ash — the warmest light in the world, and still barely light at all.

Every relationship here is eternal. Every rival you have, you have always had. The herds do not find this strange. There is no word for *young*.

The silence is the herds' own keeping, and it is structural. Sound lives far too long in this world — a cry in a great vault does not stop; it thins over hours, wandering the stone, returning as its own ghost. A shout is a days-long commitment; it will travel a continent of caverns before it fades. So the world practices quiet the way a library practices quiet: not emptiness, but courtesy under saturation. What sound exists is small and constant — the soft unending note of the standing wind, the rasp of braided tongues on crust, the hush of membrane-wings high in the haze, the tick of tap-speech coming up through the floor into the soles of the feet. The dark is full of signals. Almost none of them are for the air.

There is no fire, and there has never been. The air would not feed it, and there is nothing to spare for it; in a world without endings, combustion is an absurdity — nothing is consumable, because everything must remain. The very idea of ash does not exist here.

And yet there are makers.

They are one lineage among the fixed cast — sparse, squat, their hands like clusters of chisels, their palms reading the veins in the stone. They work dark, of course, by touch and palm-sense, and their technology is shaped by the world's two great prohibitions, firelessness and deathlessness: knapped glass from the frozen flows, native copper cold-hammered out of exposed veins, ground and polished stone, cord twisted from hair the herds shed and regrow, slates cut from molted shell. No bone — there are no bones to be had. No charcoal, no kiln, no forge.

Their works are scattered and faint, and this is the deepest strangeness: nothing here decays, so nothing is a ruin. A shelter half-built is not abandoned; it is *paused*, and it may have been paused for ten thousand years, looking exactly as it did when the hand left it. The oldest incised spiral and the one cut yesterday-equivalent are equally fresh. There is no stratigraphy. An archaeologist would go mad here: the entire archive lies in a single plane of time, all of it contemporary, none of it datable.

The makers carve because carving is the only event this world permits. Nothing else can happen — nothing washes away, nothing overgrows, no footprint keeps. To cut stone is to author the one kind of change the equilibrium allows: deliberate change. Their history exists only where they have chosen to write it, and they keep it at a fixed size. Marks appear, persist — nothing natural will ever take them — and are in time ground smooth again by the same patient hands, the stone returned to blankness. Inscription and erasure balance. The archive never grows.

Their greatest works are the counts: spiral tallies, one dot ground per crest of the pulse, the only numbers in the world. Some counts have been kept for spans that would unmake a surface mind. And when a face is full, it is polished blank, and the counting begins again at the center. Nothing is lost, the makers' behavior seems to say. *The number was the same number.* A clock that forgets its completed circles. History with a fixed size.

And there is the Stair: a long flight of cut steps descending a deep shaft toward the Heavy Sea, wrought with care, worn by none — and stopping halfway. Not ruined. Not abandoned, exactly. A work in permanent progress, or complete in its incompleteness, the last step hanging over the dark below with nothing below it and no mark of haste or grief anywhere in the stone. No one unmade it. No one continues it. It has always been thus, which is what *always* means here.

The world is young — its edges prove it, its sharp glass, its sedimentless floors — and it will be young forever, because youth here is not an age but a condition: the condition of a thing that cannot change. The labyrinth is the contraction-hollow of a cooling body, caught at the instant of its own forming, and that instant has never ended. The glass flows are the last moment of the melt, still happening. The world was born finished. It is one moment long, and the moment has lasted, by the makers' erased and unrecorded counts, longer than anyone will ever know — and it will last longer still, and nothing in it will ever have happened except what someone chose to cut into the stone.

Stand there a while, in the warm press of the herds, in the courtesy of the hush. Feel the pulse come up through the floor, the world flexing gently in the grip of something it has never seen. Far off, a quartz seam stressed by the same flexing lets go a cold brief spark — the stone thinking, if stones think. The ember-patches of the rich drift past, red as the idea of a coal. Somewhere a maker grinds one more dot into a spiral that will one day be smoothed away, and the towers in the haze hold their breath for the great crest, and the wind that never changes goes on down the vault it has always gone down, and the Heavy Sea lies waveless in the deep, and the dark, which is not an absence but the medium, keeps everything.
