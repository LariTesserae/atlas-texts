Somewhere low in a greater body of resin — down at the base of an old flow, where the last and densest seepings gathered — there is a nodule, and inside the nodule, a chamber. Nothing announces it. The rind is dull and dark, crazed with a network of fine cracks that cracked long ago and went no further, the way a held breath goes no further. Weathering came here, did its work, and finished. What remains is a surface that has settled into being exactly what it is, and beneath that surface, hidden, a world the size of a curled sleeper — packed almost solid, with only a chain of small bellies left open inside it, each one no larger than a cupped palm, a shoebox, a pantry you could not stand in.

Everything is made. There is no stone here, no soil, no grain of anything that simply existed. There is one substance in many states: glass-clear gold, foamy opaque bone-color, drawn threads, poured curtains, ropes, beads, blades. All of it exuded — built the way a shell is built, by a body, without tools, without limbs, by flow and surface tension and cure. The maker was a single resinous organism, tree-like and long gone, and its architecture is its own outpouring, hardened. The chamber is the deepest pooling of that outpouring, which is why it is the clearest, the densest, the most full.

Because the chamber is full. Flow upon flow came in and stayed. Curtains hang like poured honey that was told to stop and stopped forever. Columns swell and meet. Blades interlock. What remains open is almost incidental — a labyrinth of smooth-walled voids connected by crawl-throats, the negative space left over when everything else had finished arriving. Even the voids are furnished: suspended across each one, thin as breath on glass, hang planes of golden pollen — flat, faint, tilted membranes of dust that gathered long ago onto the still surfaces of the air and never left. There is no cubic centimeter of this place without a presence in it.

The light is bright and comes from the walls. Whatever lies beyond the nodule gives light, and the light percolates inward through the surrounding body of gold, arriving from every direction at once, directionless, shadowless, saturated. The foamy, bubble-filled amber glows brightest, scattering; the clear cores transmit. And the material holds light the way it holds everything — drinking it, releasing it slowly — so the chamber shines with the long average of all brightness that has ever arrived at the rind. It is a noon that never began and will not end, a mellow, almost excessive brilliance, gold on gold on gold, constant as a property of matter.

The sound is louder than the light is bright. A chord — a low fundamental felt in the chest and the teeth, carrying a few clean partials — fills every void completely. It does not swell. It does not fade. It has no source anymore. It began at the sealing, when the last pore closed on the resin's final exhalation, and in a chamber walled with the smoothest and hardest reflector, a sound has almost nowhere to go. Its decay is a theory. The chord is as much a fixture as the columns; it is the loudest thing in the world and the only event in it.

And the chord is the architect. While the resin was soft, the sound was already standing, and the soft stuff yielded where the air moved most and gathered where the air was still. The voids trace the loud bellies of the standing wave; the packed solid traces its silent surfaces. To crawl the chain of rooms — could anything crawl them — would be to move through the antinodes of a single note, belly to belly, throat to throat, while the pollen planes mark the node-surfaces, the thin gold cross-sections of the chord's own stillness. At those planes the sound drops to almost nothing, and the near-silence is more unnerving than the roar, because there is nothing else to hear in it. There is nothing here that can hear. No animal ever entered these flows — not a mite, not a gnat, not one leg. The world is loud and deaf, all architecture, no inhabitant.

The air in the voids is old — the resin's last breath plus a little captured atmosphere from the day of closing — and it is dry to the point of brittleness, cool as a cellar, faintly sharp with the ghost of terpene: pine, pepper, varnish, medicine. The great mass averages temperature the way it averages light, so the interior rests at the long mean of everything outside, an unending cool, an eternal autumn afternoon with no sun in it. Nothing rots. Nothing can. Rot requires water, and water is the one thing this world has never admitted. The chemistry here is hydrocarbon and held breath; no drop has touched any surface since before there were surfaces, only flows. The dryness is not a condition of the place. It is the law that permits the place. A single drop, were one ever to enter, would begin history again — clouding, crazing, softening, decay — and so the whole constitution of the world is the keeping-out of water.

Of vegetation there is little, and it is all gathered in stillness. The pollen planes. One twig-tip with a handful of narrow leaves, suspended mid-void in the clearest gold, its green dimmed to the idea of green. And at the deepest bowl, where the chamber's floor curves down to the lowest point of the low world, a knotted cord of the maker's own tissue — the source of the flow, the tear the resin wept from — sealed in the clearest amber of all. Whether it is alive is not decidable, because it is exactly as alive as it was at the moment of sealing, and here, states do not change. All the plant life of the world could be gathered in two hands, and it would not object, and it would not grow.

The rules are few and total. One material. Nothing enters; nothing leaves. Nothing changes — not the light, which is an average; not the temperature, which is an average; not the sound, which is a memory; not the dust, which is arrested mid-fall; not the sprig, which will not fall and will not rot. The world had one event — its closing — and has not had another. Erosion came to the rind and finished; flowing came to the interior and finished; the drips completed their not-falling and hang as hardened drops that will never drop. Time passes outside the rind the way weather passes a window. Inside, there is only storage: light-memory, sound-memory, charge clinging to the walls, every state persisting because persistence is the nature of the substance.

If the place has a name, it is the name of its note — one low word held without breath.

It is very bright, and very loud, and very cool, and almost nothing is here, and almost everything is full, and nothing at all is happening. The loudness is simply the shape of that nothing, kept.
