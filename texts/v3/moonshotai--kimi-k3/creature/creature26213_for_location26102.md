**Population**

Nothing lives in Bellowsmere — this is true, and it is the least informative true thing about the place. The census returns zero, and the basin is crowded. The fault is not in the counting but in the form: there is no column for what resides here. To count what lives in Bellowsmere you have to lower the definition of living the way you would lower a lamp into green water — slowly, and past the point where it stops being a lamp.

Once you do, you can see the law of the place, visible everywhere: the verbs outlive the nouns. The roar outlives the wave. The light outlives the lamplighter. The schedule outlives the shift. The word outlives the hand. Begin the count among the surviving verbs.

Not the rim-dwellers — they live beside the place the way relatives live beside a sleeper's room: attentive, uncounted, never entirely inside. They have stopped numbering the Ascensions. They have not stopped listening for them.

Not the green, though it has all the paperwork of life and none of the tenancy. Growth, migration, steering after a fashion — the raft-forests trim their bladders like a fleet, the lamp-forest leans its door-wide leaves toward the sodium blooms, the kelp feeds on machine heat. But feeding is not eating; eating requires a preference, and nothing here prefers. A herd implies something hungry behind it, and there is no hunger in Bellowsmere, only the gesture of hunger, rehearsed nightly for no one. The plants live the way fire lives in a hearth: a process wearing the costume of a presence. Count them as tissue, not population. It will matter later.

The place admits one form of life outright, and it admits it of the sounds: detached from their causes, they go on living alone. So. The Long Comber's roar, decades ownerless, still arriving from a wave that has not moved. And there are older voices than that — roars whose waves have already Landed, sounds that survived their own events and now circle the valley like a dog at a doorstep, looking for the body they belong to. Sound does not die here; it sediments. The sub-bass that arrives before the place does, that you feel in your soles and your teeth, is not a tone. It is the compacted weight of every sound Bellowsmere has ever made, still settling — a geology of the audible. The deck stores thunder. The floor stores everything else. When the choir-reeds vent their chords behind a surge, the hooting means nothing — but a chord that means nothing is still a chord. Meaning nothing is most of what music does. Nothing here is spent. Everything here is stored.

Then the habits, because a habit is a thing that goes on living where its reasons died. Each evening the drowned town keeps the drowned dusk, block by block, obedient to a schedule no one maintains, and the whistles at six and two and ten are not detached sounds — they are worse than that. They are still attached, to a cause that no longer exists. The loop below — flood turning the turbines that light the lamps that feed the leaves — has no exit and no point, but it has a direction and a rhythm, and a circulation with a rhythm has another name. The town did not drown. It changed state and kept its job.

Then the bottles, because everything else in the basin conjugates in the present continuous and the bottle caps alone have a future tense. The date-stamps read next spring: three acts of faith in every crate. That there will be a spring. That someone will be thirsty in it. That water needs sending anywhere in a world that is all water. Bottle by bottle, Bellowsmere is mailing itself to a recipient who has not existed yet. Whatever lives here, record its temperament: patient, punctual, and not despairing. It believes in next spring with the whole weight of its machinery, which is the only way left to believe anything.

Then the standing waves — the only mortal things in the basin, because they are the only things that can finish. Everything else loops or holds; an arrested wave carries its ending inside it. When one Lands, decades late, all at once, that is the only death the place permits: a completion. Nothing mourns it, because mourning requires a survivor, and the roar, which survives, does not mourn; it goes on living alone. The deck stores motion's noise; the water stores motion itself. The whole basin is charged with unfinished events, and the Landings — the events by which loud events are measured — are its scansion. A stress every few decades. A very slow meter, keeping time to something.

And in the lulls, the ghost. When the surface goes still it declines to show the sky and shows instead the unflooded valley — a bridge where no bridge stands, lamplight with a warmth the lamps no longer possess, small weather from other decades passing over like rumor. Like all memory, it is edited: warmer than the original. It is also shy. It surfaces only in stillness, and if there are figures at its windows, no lull has ever lasted long enough to make one out. To see the people of the reflection, you would have to be stiller than this water ever is.

Now read the census back and see where the error was. Voice. Pulse. Habit. Faith. Patience. Memory. Breath — rain falling, rain rising, the pillars of mist standing between deck and basin like breath made visible, an exchange with no agreed rate, and the difference is the flood: the basin keeps a doorstep a season more than it gives back. It is inhaling. It has never exhaled. These are not populations. They are faculties. The form kept returning zero because it asked how many, and the answer is one. The warmth was never a comparison — air like exhaled breath, water the temperature of a held hand, waves furred with moss, a sub-bass felt in the soles and the teeth. Of course you feel it there. You are inside the thing making it. To breathe here is to drink; it is also to be breathed. Bellowsmere is not a place with something living in it. It is the shape living takes here, undiluted by livers. Population: one.

And the one is mid-word. The rim-dwellers could have told you. The log in the empty watchtower is kept in a steady hand until the middle of a word, and nothing in the basin has ever finished anything else — the thunder unspent, the wave unbroken, the stamp a season ahead of itself, the whistle that starts no shift, the rehearsal with no opening night. A body that gathers breath for decades, a doorstep a season, ends that breath one of two ways: a sigh or a sentence. The Landings are practice. Each is the loudest thing in a loud world because each is the basin getting a syllable out — the stress arriving years late, all at once, in the middle of a word that is still being said.

So the green wave out in the gloom, furred with moss, considering at its leisure whether to come down: it is not deciding whether. It is deciding what. It holds the one speaking part in a town rehearsing to be a sea rehearsing to be weather, and the audience of no one is the correct audience — you do not rehearse for a crowd.

Whatever lives in Bellowsmere lives the way a word lives in a held breath: entirely, and not yet.
