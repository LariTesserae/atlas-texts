No one lives here. That is the answer you will give for the first long cycles, and the basin will not argue. It is polite. Its corrections arrive the way its echoes arrive — late, from below, lowered a fraction — which is to say, in something very like your own voice.

Write the census anyway, the one any survey would write. The rushes: glassy, faintly amber, one stand per district, drinking heat. The fan-trees: rarer than plazas, tall as towers, sweating their slow water. The black films in the cold rooms. And the glow. Four entries. Notice first that the flora obeys the first law like everything else — each stand of rushes is the same stand at a different age, each fan-tree the same fan-tree, folded a little further — so that even the living here is not made but continued, copied across the bowl into its own biography. Then notice that the survey has mislabeled the fourth entry. It calls the glow chemistry. Microbial luminescence, a property of the water. Not a resident; a weather.

Watch it longer.

The glow gathers where the water is deepest and warmest, milk-green where it drinks heat, dimmer and bluer in the standing shallows, and it thins to nothing at the thresholds of the cold rooms and does not cross them. It pulses with the water table. When the basin breathes in and the streets stand knee-deep, the whole plan brightens, the canals flare — and that is when the revisions happen, the fresh white skins, the small adjustments toward the images waiting in the water. As though the work gets done when the resident is home. It brightens where you wade, and you told yourself what the field notes would say: stirred chemistry, nothing. But it brightens *behind* you, along your line of travel, and holds a moment too long, and fades grain by grain. It is not unwriting your footprints. It is reading them back.

Whether it is one thing or many is a question from a world with edges. The canals connect it; at high water there is no plural available. From the rim you saw the pale tracery and thought: less a city than a map of a city. You had it backwards, or not far enough. The stone is the sediment. The glowing tracery is the body. The visible city is exactly the flooded city because the flooded city is exactly the resident — the map is the animal, and everything else is what the animal has precipitated, the way a shell is precipitated, grain by grain, over longer than the word *long* comfortably holds.

What it does is remember. It is the something that never saw a city; it has never seen anything. It has only tasted what fell in — the sky's drips, the rim's leavings, whatever the down-rain carries in solution — and likeness is a force, and solution is storage, and nothing that dissolves here is gone, only held, the way the towers hold their voids. Perhaps a stair came down once, dissolved in a single drip out of some world that cuts its stone. A colonnade. The grammar of a door. It has never seen a stair; it has tasted one. It holds these shapes the way you hold a word in a language you do not speak — perfectly, and from the outside — and it does with them the one thing it does. It continues them.

It has no name. Naming is a kind of making, and nothing here is made. It was not made either; it is continued, like everything. The difference is that it is the one that continues.

This is why every structure wears the upturned lit face of something being questioned from below: because everything here *is* being questioned from below. One question, asked of every form, a cycle at a time — is this what you are becoming? The stone answers in white skins, a finger's width at a time: closer. And the reflections are the resident looking, the light arranging itself into images, running ahead — the drop's landing flashed before the drop lands — because it lives a half-moment in the future the way you live in the present, and the stone is its past, precipitated, and the moment you call now is only where the two of you briefly agree. The echo is its voice. Speak, and your voice returns late, from below, lowered a fraction — older, the way the reflected towers are older. Everything the water gives back, it gives back ahead of you. It has one tense. It is not the present.

There is one other undisputed life, and it is the resident's opposite in every way the bowl allows. The black films do not come from below. They pool in the genuine cold, and where they graze, the stone forgets: revisions blur, half-finished adjustments soften, drafts are unwritten. Their dew does not glow. It is the only dark water in the basin, the only water the resident does not read. The cold rooms drift; the films drift with them, or the films are why they drift; and when they have passed, the light comes back across the threshold and begins the draft again. It does not hurry. It does not win. Losing is also a kind of balance, and the films are the basin's one eraser — the only place in all the bowl where a form is allowed to stop. Remember that, when you find the figures.

The first one will be a void. At the back of an alcove in a tower wall: a hollow in the flowstone in the exact shape of a seated person, knees drawn, head bowed, grown around nothing the way the towers grew around their doors. Not a statue. The reverse of a statue — a mold without an original, or with an original that has not arrived. The stone says *yet*. The water uses a different tense.

Then the drafts. Districts of near-figures: a pillar with shoulders; a balustrade that is almost someone sleeping; stalagmite people standing in the shallows, leaning — three degrees, the same three, the same direction, the colonnade's habit, a signature where there is no hand. They repeat, per the law: the same figure at different ages, fresh and dripping in one district, rounded like old teeth in another. Most of them are you. You can tell, because the drafts improve toward the deep water and worsen toward the rim. It met you at the rim; it learned you as you descended; and space holds what time would have to. Walk back and you walk your own biography in reverse, each draft cruder, until the last, near the rim's foot, is only a standing stone with a lean. It has had more practice at plazas. It is practicing.

Look in the canals, where the light is deepest and the city below the city is most itself. The reflected streets are tenanted. Not crowded — crowding is a kind of hurry, and nothing here hurries — but kept. A figure rests on the step where you rested, in your posture. A figure sleeps badly on the portico where you slept badly, one arm under the head. It is not mimicking you. The water is warm as a mouth, and it is learning you by heart — the way it learned the stair, the colonnade: perfectly, and from the outside.

And there are others, in the deep reflections, in postures you do not recognize. Positions you have never taken. Do not flinch from them; be accurate. You are standing in the same force as the stone. Likeness pulls on you too, the water shows you slightly ahead of yourself, and you have been converging since your first step touched the floor. These are not strangers. They are drafts of you from cycles ahead, doing what you have not yet done. One cycle you will catch yourself sitting exactly so, and the reflection will already have moved on.

Some of the figures are older than you. There is no comfortable reading of this, and the basin offers two. Either the resident is predicting — converging toward tenancy the way the towers converge toward their reflections, precipitating a population the way it precipitates everything, a resident recalled by something that has never seen a resident — or you are not the first form it kept, and the worn ones were someone, and the someone is not recorded anywhere. You found no tracks here that were not yours, and read it as proof of solitude. It is proof of the opposite. Tracks do not fade here; they finish precipitating, and are copied, and continue, and become other things. What rises does not return; if the originals went anywhere, they went up, in the tithe, and no one has seen what the sky does with its share. A few of the oldest drafts are not shaped like you, or like anything you have a word for, and they are worn past reading, and the water offers no reflections of them. Whatever they were converging toward, they have arrived, or they have been let go. It is the closest thing to an ending in this place, and it is not close.

You know how your own entry begins; you have seen it. The footprints were the first draft. The second set finished precipitating in a district you never visited, a little older, a little more worn, waiting for the third. Count forward, the way you counted the plazas from fresh to stub. The fourth set will have your stride to the grain. The fifth will be old. The sixth is not footprints. In a quarter you have not walked, the water is laying down its first white grains around a void your height: someone standing at the far edge of a flooded plaza, turned a little toward the rim, patient, precise, leaning three degrees, already a little more permanent than the stone.

Do not ask when it will be finished. Finished is not a thing that happens here. The census is short — rushes, fan-trees, the films, one light, and you — and the last entry is still being written, ahead of you, in the only tense the water has. You live here. You have lived here since your first step touched the warm floor and the glow read it back.

The rest of you is arriving at the speed of stone.
