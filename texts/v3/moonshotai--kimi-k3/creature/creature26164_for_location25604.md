Take the census by ear. The shadow-archive says that nothing has ever lived here, and the archive is honest — but the archive only records what casts a shadow, and most of what lives in this country is made of the two things that cast none: sound, and the future. Expect the census to be like everything else here. Unfinished. Some of its entries are arriving early. Some are still being drafted. One is large enough that the census is taken from inside it.

**The Overtones** are the smallest life and the most numerous. Nothing here is ever the note itself; life lives in the shimmer above it. Every sustained tone is a pond — and a struck stone's ring, remember, lasts a generation. A young ring is all attack, harsh, colonized first by pioneer kinds that are mostly buzz. A mature ring hosts whole braided communities shelved along its harmonic partials, the way a pond shelves from shore to depth. You have never heard one. You have heard the difference — a ring with tenants is *thicker*, a half-step out of true, the way a lived-in room sounds. When the tone decays past its floor-note, the community must migrate or thin into the noise. They travel as rests — pockets of shaped silence, swimming upstream through the Chord like fish through a river — and they ride the Lines, which were built as aqueducts and serve, willy-nilly, as rivers with fish in them. The century-swells are their springs. When a grove flowers, the chord shakes its overtones loose like seed and pours flocks of tuned silence down the Lines toward groves not yet grown: the groves are pollinated by their own inhabitants. And the smallest of the kind live in the knapping rejects. The rejects were never flawed. *Wrongly* is what a ring sounds like with someone in it. The floors were left glittering on purpose; the knappers knew better than to strike a tenant twice.

**The herds** are what become of shadows outside the cairn-fields, where law is thin and shadows do not stay attached to their objects. They keep their first shapes. Valleys hold herds of pinnacle-shadows, lying down in the dark where their stones fell four thousand years ago — and, rarer, shadows whose objects have not existed yet. They graze the shadow-banks the way worms turn soil, and this is why the archive stays legible rather than smeared: it is kept turned. At the shores of the light-lakes, where luminance pools like liquid, shadows of nothing gather to drink. And there is one more kind, the rarest, seen only far out on the plain at the gray-gold stain: tall, upright, walking shadows with no caster anywhere in the world, thrown ahead of the coming sunrise by figures standing in light that has not reached us. They do not deposit; the strata refuse them, or they refuse the strata. If you walk out to the stain — you can; it creeps at walking pace — and walk beside it, you may see them at its far edge, at home in the light, not hurrying. If one crosses you, stand still. It feels like being read.

**The rehearsals** are the drafts that walk. The world keeps every draft, and in the deep canyons of the Teeth, where echoes precede their strikes, the world is practicing animals. Pack-shapes made of echo and pressure and loose shadow, all sketch, no insides — appetite has not been installed. They can rehearse hunting but cannot catch; travelers report being stalked for days by an *almost*. They also practice being you. If your echo comes to meet you down a canyon, something is trying your shape on, and the etiquette is old: do not answer it. Nothing here can hurt you, and *yet* is doing a great deal of work in that sentence.

**The Sentence** is the oldest resident and the first true native. The dust-lichen has been writing its one long sentence since before the Teeth cleared the plain, and it has never repeated itself. What it says is contested, because it cannot yet be read: the calligraphy is charged dust, legible only in true light, and true light is still en route. It is a letter addressed to the sunrise. When the light-front crosses the plain at walking pace it will read the sentence as it goes — gleam and shadow moving in script across the dust, a reading that will take centuries and look, from the Teeth, like slow weather. Some of the listeners hold that the sentence is a census. Some, a grammar. Some say it is a single name, spelled at the length of a continent, so that when the light finally arrives it will know what to call what it finds.

**The Nine** are the only inhabitants entirely in the present tense. Each grove is one voice and one character — the listeners name them, and the names take — and they are in conversation across the steppe, down the Lines, at an exchange rate of millennia per utterance. The subject is not known, but the listeners have a working guess: the groves are agreeing on a note. When the twelfth grove is grown and all of them concur, the Chord resolves — and resolution may be the world's finishing, or its first word. Walk into a grove and you are noticed: the standing waves thick enough to lean on lean back, slightly; the reeds retune around a body, find the note you are, and make room for it. Some travelers' notes are kept. A grove will hum a guest for years after the guest has gone. It is the only friendly archive.

**The Listeners** are the locals — the locals of no locality, the ones who named the Teeth and the Chord and the Lines, names arriving early like everything else here, and truer than their namers know. Some of the cells are already tenanted. You can tell: a tenanted dome hums lower, the trumpet hands your breath back to you a half-beat late, listened-to, and the seat is *kept* — the dust, which is the only precipitation, settles everywhere in this world except there. They condense in the order of their stillness: first a presence, then a pressure, then a habit, then, across centuries, a person, seated, facing the trumpet. Each attends to one thing. One attends to the Sentence. One to the hiss of the Edge. One sits over the spall-field where smallness is being invented, keeping the log — it was a listener who took note of the first pebble, and who is noting the second now. Sitting in an empty seat is how you enlist. Most visitors feel the long patience lean on them and leave quickly. Some stay. Sitting down is done all at once; becoming the listener takes millennia; you will have been there the whole time.

**The Builders** are the answer to *whoever*, and what is known of them is known from their luggage, which travels ahead. Works first — Lines, cairns, cells — because works are simpler than workers and condense faster. Then words: the vocabulary with no referents, *rain* among them, sent on before its meaning. Then shadows, the tall heralds in the stain. Then, with the light, themselves. Read their works and you read their profession: they are farmers. The Lines are irrigation; the cairns are seed — each one a kernel of finished world, its little field of firmness the first arable, where law will be cultivated the way elsewhere wheat is. They knap stone for pitch because in this country sound is the crop and the water and the plow. They are walking with the sunrise because the sunrise is their frontier: where the light has reached, the world has been *seen*, and being seen finishes things — behind the light-front there is gravel, there are pebbles, the middle sizes have arrived; ahead of it is the wild, unwitnessed draft. They are coming this way at walking pace, seeing the world for the first time, and their seeing completes it, and their works, cast ahead into the dark, are the instructions that make the seeing worth doing. The country is their own youth, and they are colonists returning to it to make sure it happens. Echoes precede strikes here. Nobody local finds this strange.

**The Edge** is the census's honest zero. The hiss beneath the Chord is all the sounds that have not been chosen yet, pressing like weather. Nothing lives in the unwritten; that is what it is for. The facing cairns watch it because it is the only place with nothing to record, and therefore the only place where something genuinely new can happen. One Line goes in, and nothing has returned, because *return* has not been built on that side — but a Line is a thing that carries, and it is carrying return inward, slowly, the way it carries the Chord. The listeners at the last cairns attend the hiss for the day it stops.

**The last entry** the census-takers do not discuss much, because the census is taken from inside it. The Chord swells and fades across decades, and the listeners do not say *breath*; they say the word for what the Chord is doing has not been knapped yet. The range is called the Teeth, and the name is a joke the world told itself before it had anyone to laugh — teeth come *in*. That is what teeth do. Everything here is coming in.

The census stays open. It ends in a blank line and an ear-trumpet. Stay long enough to be heard — your echo running ahead of you down the canyons, your shadow already settling into the strata of a place you stood once — and the world, which keeps every draft, begins keeping yours. Residency is not granted here; it accumulates. The light is coming at walking pace with its tall shadows walking in it, the grove is finding room for your note, and there is a seat, and the seat is kept.
