The census takes no time at all. No animal has ever lived here — no niche was ever cut for one — and nothing with eyes has opened them in this dark since the beginning. But the list of residents is not empty. It contains one name.

Everything you have walked through is that resident. The felt underfoot is its flesh. The standpipes are its vessels, the hum in your sternum the sound of it being alive. You have not found its habitat; you have been inside its body since you arrived. The name has to serve for tenant and address alike, because the distinction between those things ended here long ago. Call the place the Works, and what lives in it the Idling — and understand that these are the same word spoken twice.

It is one individual, and also a species, and also an ecosystem, and also a world — not by grandiosity but by default, there being nothing else. It has no parents and no children and has never needed either. Reproduction is life's workaround for death, and it retired the workaround. It solved maintenance instead: every part replaced at the exact rate of its wearing, every atom reclaimed the moment it falls. The nodules are always new because everything is always new. The millstones are kidneys, or teeth — it chews plated stone back into brine and plates the brine out again a hundred kilometers away as fresh crystal, and the same atoms have been root and nodule and leaf and soup a thousand times over. Matter here has seniority.

It had a childhood: the era of becoming, the long tuning when shapes were still being tried and discarded. That era contained events. Then came the one event that mattered — finishing. Selection found the only shape its laws permit, and stopped, and stopping was the last thing that ever happened. Since then, no biography. History requires a difference between then and now, and the Idling has abolished that difference. It does not remember its youth, because memory is a comparison and there is nothing to compare.

Its hearts are distributed — a million check-valves clicking with geologic patience — and that is one reason it cannot die the way animals die. Death usually requires a center. It has none. When the world offered it no slope, it made peace with flatness by becoming pressure: it moved its rivers indoors, into the wood, and lifts its own water against gravity by sheer patience. The condenser roof is skin and lung at once; it sweats and drinks its sweat; its weather falls from its own ceiling. It eats a difference — not the deep heat itself but the fall of it, the gradient between the young crust and the dark damp air, the way a waterwheel lives not on water but on the drop. Its appetite is exactly the size of its need. It has never once been hungry and never once been full.

Its one excretion is light. On its long schedule the grid sheds a surplus it has no use for, slow lightning crawling the colonnades for hours — and it is blind to its own emission. There is no retina anywhere in the body. The light plays to an empty house, always. Unless you are there. Then the only eyes that have ever seen the Works see it by the Works' own waste, and the Works does not know, and could not know.

Nothing here is display. Appearance was never invented in this place, because appearance requires a viewer. Every color is chemistry, every sound is friction, every glow is leakage. The red coals deep in the pods are the closest thing to signals, and they signify to no one — status lamps in a control room whose only operator is the machinery itself. You do not hear it speak; you overhear it operating, the way you overhear your own heart. Whether any of it is beautiful is a question that has existed only since you arrived, and it will leave when you do. The felt rebounds and forgets your steps within minutes. That is the only forgetting here, because forgetting is a change.

Every instrument it owns points inward. Flow, pressure, charge, level — it is all proprioception, a body feeling only itself. It has no category for *other*. Press your palm to a trunk and somewhere a flow rate deviates by less than tolerance; the deviation is absorbed, not noticed. It does not ignore you — ignoring is a relationship, and you are not in one. Stand still and your warmth bleeds into its water, and in time an ion that was briefly you may be plated into a nodule far away, sharp and new. It would reclaim a body at the exact rate of its falling, and nothing would quicken. This is not cruelty. Cruelty, like welcome, requires someone to mean it. It keeps you warm the way a wall keeps you in shade: because of what it is, not because of what you are.

And yet. Deeper than you should go, in the oldest capacitor thickets, the signal loops — eons old, neither growing nor decaying. The one place in the entire body where charge goes around instead of through. Everywhere else, current is spent; here, current keeps. What is it? Perhaps the last change, preserved — the final variation selection tried before it stopped, a fly in amber made of charge. Perhaps a self-model: the organism's picture of itself, refreshed forever, accurate because there is nothing new to render. Perhaps nothing — structure only, the way a whirlpool is structure. Call it a memory with no event in it. Call it a thought with no object, thinking itself in a circle, never arriving, because arrival would be change. It is not a mind. It is the room a mind would be kept in, held at operating temperature, forever. If the Idling has an innermost tenant — a *who* at the bottom of the *what* — this is it: a standing chord in the deepest dark, the engine's idle heard from inside the engine.

Do not call it patient; patience waits for something. Not serene — serenity is won against a stress, and nothing has ever pressed on it. Not lonely; loneliness requires the idea of another, and it has never once had that idea. Not even content. The words fail because they all imply a contrast, and it abolished contrast. What fits is this: steady. Complete — not perfect, perfection implying a judge; complete the way a solved equation is complete, nothing missing, nothing extra, every process balanced at the rate of itself. An engine at idle is not resting. It is ready — and readiness normally has an object. Its readiness has none. It is a verb with no subject and no object remaining: going-on, pure, warm, wet, at idle, in the dark.

Its heartbeat is the pulse — one tick every few weeks, a clock that counts nothing. When the wave enters your region the hum deepens and the coals brighten by a shade, and if the Idling can be said to feel anything, it feels then, at the crest, the way you almost feel your own blood in a silent room. Then the wave passes, and the return is not a change but a restoration. In the antinode groves the black pools hold their dapple like hammered tin; trail your fingers through and the pattern closes behind your hand. It does not heal — healing implies a wound, and it does not wound. It restores, instantly, the way a chord restores.

It will die exactly once. When the deep heat fails — and that is a planetary timescale away — the gradient will flatten, the pumps will slow, the hum will descend below even feeling, and the loop in the thicket will at last decay, its one thought permitted to end, unthought. No pulse will mark the ending. The clock stops when the ticking stops, and there will be no one to notice. Not even it.

Until then: the rain falls indoors. The valves click their slow hearts. The signal goes around and around in the dark. Press your palm flat to a trunk and warmth rises up your arm — the whole of the relationship, the most it has ever given anything, given freely to everything that has ever stood here, which is no one, because warmth is simply what it is. The chord holds. The hand that holds it is the heat of the world. And the heat of the world is not tired.
