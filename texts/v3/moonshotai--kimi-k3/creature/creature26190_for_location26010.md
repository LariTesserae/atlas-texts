**The Census of the Held Basin**

Begin with the word *lives*, because here it will give you trouble. Elsewhere you count a thing as living if it changes — grows, spends, decays — and in the Held Basin change is food, eaten before it can act. So the census must be taken differently. Count what persists. Count what is kept, and what keeps itself, and what has made other arrangements. Counted that way, the basin is crowded. Some entries are disputed. The basin itself is a disputed entry.

Most of the population is vegetable — if that word still comforts you after you have watched it work. The black-leafed drinkers that tile the ground are the mouths of the place. The refresh does not reach down out of the sky to correct the world; the drinkers are how it reaches. Stand beside a fault shard long enough and you may catch it: the shard begins, microscopically, to weather, and the beginning dims, and the shard stands sharp. On the rim slopes there are boulders balanced at the moment of toppling, held on a decision the drinkers drank. They will fall when the basin ends. Your voice dies within arm's reach because the drinkers get it first; your footsteps are their table scraps.

Above the drinkers, the cushion-mats pave every shadow, and what they eat is the shadow itself — the paid change, the taxed future, laid down like silt. This ecology has no detritus layer. Its detritus layer is the future. Above the mats the combwood stand the way a standing wave stands: not growing, not still, kept. The combwood keeps appointments of its own — the scheduled exhale of sweetness, a softening of the chime at hours you will learn to feel rather than tell — rituals in place of events, the forest's way of having a life without spending one. In the deep vales the roots interlace until a hillside is one organism wearing many shapes. Does it think? Wrong question. It attends, the way a held breath attends.

And there are two forests. The light never moves, so the shadows never move, and the combwood shadows belong to larger, older trees — the forest that would have grown, if growing were permitted. You walk through both at once: the held wood and the paid one. The second forest is always larger. Do not decide that you prefer it. Preference is audible here, in its way, and you do not yet know who is listening.

The animals begin with the treasury. The long oryx — fewer than a thousand, translucent as blown glass, a slow oil-light inside them that moves without flowing, because nothing flows — are the basin's reserves of unspent time. They crop the black leaf-tips where the drinkers store what they have not finished drinking, and so an oryx is an accumulation, walking. This is why the basin affords fewer than a thousand: that is the discretionary budget. This is why the pan keeps their footprints for long minutes and yours for moments — they spend real time, and the refresh must bill them slowly. You can read an old oryx on the ground behind it. Their shadows lag like yours, but an old one's lag runs minutes deep, and it drags a small parade of everything it just stopped doing: here it grazes, there it stands, there it lifted its head at the chord. They are calm with the calm of the insured. Their trails — the only paths in the basin — wind down through the combwood to the shoreline terraces, and sometimes you will find one standing at the old waterline above the White Floor, facing the pan, not waiting, exactly, but positioned to recognize the sea if it comes back.

There are fewer than a thousand. There have always been fewer than a thousand. Occasionally there is one more, standing in a meadow as if it had always stood there, and the count stays wrong for a season, and no one explains it, because nothing here explains. It keeps. When one dies — by accident only — the refresh does not repair it. The body goes slowly to glass in the salt, the growth around it stands taller for a season on the dividend, and the herd does not gather, because gathering is not something they have ever needed to do.

The undecided are the only natives. Everything else here was made or kept; the undecided *happened*. Every heirloom error the refresh filed under *keep* is a reef, and they are what swims in it — never seen, only a heat-shimmer shaped like an animal, a smear of almosts. The suspended seedfall is their deep water; walk through it and the hanging seeds chime against their own ghost-copies, and that faint chiming is, for all practical purposes, conversation. They drink the gaps inside glitches, and they are drawn to the gaps in you. When one crosses your path and your last minute goes briefly plural — when you cannot say which foot you stepped with — that is what it feels like to be tasted. Nothing about them is ever resolved, including whether they are many or one. You will develop an opinion. Keep it plural.

Then there are the arrears: everything the world is behind on. They live in the lag, a heartbeat behind the world, in the shadows where the eaten change is paid. The dark full crowns out on the salt, thrown by no tree, are not cast; they are occupied. The arrears garden the paid futures, pruning the shadow-forest toward shapes you will never see in wood. They are shy, and harmless the way weather is harmless, and you will know them the day your shadow does something you never did — turns its head, raises a hand against a wind that has not kept its appointment. Do not look behind you. There is nothing behind you. There is something behind your shadow. Stay long enough and they will move into yours. A visitor's shadow fills, over time, with the committee of everyone you almost became, and the arrears tend that little crowd gently, the way they tend the crowns on the salt. You have been warned, and the warning changes nothing, because the almosts come whether or not you consent to have them.

When the appointment arrives — the wind, the world's one permitted verb — the basin briefly has weather, and everything with a voice spends it at once. The combwood chord is not a sound the forest makes. It is a sound the forest *is*, for as long as the gust lasts. Call it roll call. The Flutes answer from the spire fields with the note you feel in your sternum, and if anything lives at the speed of the wind — born, lived, and eaten inside ninety seconds of moving air — you will never be introduced. The only evidence would be the chord arriving a fraction richer than the schedule requires. At some appointments it does. No one explains. It keeps.

And there is the bird. One bird, grey and white, big as a heron, old the way animals are old — the only thing in the basin that is honestly aging. It flies in silence, because its wingbeats are taxed like everything else, the same slow circuit over pan and forest, appointment after appointment, you will say, once you have stopped believing in days. Plot its landings. It lands rarely, and where it lands the lag of the world deepens for a moment, as though the basin turns its whole attention — and the landings are not random. They are headlands. The bird is flying the coast of the deleted sea. It is not a bird. It is a coastline that flies.

The basin keeps one copy of everything it removed, and it keeps the sea's copy in the bird. When you stand on the White Floor and remember green water and reeds and a shore bird — memories that arrive from nowhere and fade like scuffs — the bird has just passed overhead, and you have overheard it remembering. The world keeps it the way a sleeper keeps one ear open. And it is not refreshed, which means it will die, and everyone who understands this asks the same question — what happens to the sea then — and you already know the answer. The basin keeps one copy of everything it removes, and it keeps the copy in whatever is standing nearby. Do not be on the pan that day, unless you are prepared to spend the rest of your life being where the sea is kept.

Last in every census, first in every argument: the pillar. Salt, or a person. Sitting down or standing up; the decision is taking a very long time. Watch its shadow. The lag there is not a heartbeat; it is centuries. The shadow is still traveling between the two postures, at the speed the pillar experiences time — which means the flicker you are watching is a decision in progress, the only one in the basin, the only place where something is genuinely, currently happening. The refresh does not touch it; it cannot be explained, so it is kept. The undecided will not cross it, because a decision that old and that deep is not a gap to them. It is a cliff. It is a god. They do not say, and they would not say even if they could, because saying resolves.

You will sit with it eventually. Everyone does. It is the only company in the basin that is also trying to decide something. The guesses are the usual ones: a visitor who stopped mid-choice and was kept; the keeper of the lattice, resting between laws; the choice itself — changing or lasting — given a body and a salt crust, still at it. If you sit long enough, your two shadows will lean toward each other, very slightly, the way undecided things do, and the arrears will give you privacy, which is the closest thing they have to respect.

There are others who stopped, and finished stopping. You have walked past them. A finished fixture is corrected toward the landscape's templates until it fits: a snag with too regular a grain, a salt hummock with a patience about it, a combwood that chimes, very faintly, in a key the wind never uses. The basin does not mark its dead. It does not have dead. It has holdings.

The census ends where it began, with you: the rarest animal in the basin, a budget you did not earn, spending yourself in small changes — footsteps, heartbeats, the thinning little deltas of thought — while the world sips and your shadow grows crowded. Understand that everything here is an answer to the one question the basin was asked. The forest answered by standing. The oryx answered by lasting, slowly, together. The undecided answered by refusing to. The arrears answered on the world's behalf, in shadow. The bird answers daily, one honest circuit at the rate of one life, carrying the sea. The pillar is still answering.

The wind arrives on schedule. The canopy gives its one soft chord, and everything that lives here turns, slightly, to be counted — the mouths and the mats and the kept wood, the treasury, the reefs and their swimmers, the occupied crowns, the coastline, the decider, the holdings. The chord is not for you.

But you are in it.
