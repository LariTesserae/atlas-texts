Begin the census at the stile, where the paperwork begins. LOWER WORKS NO. 4 — a number, which implies a ledger, and a ledger implies a clerk, and the clerk is calm. Ask the paperwork who lives in Loudwater and it answers the way it answers everything: calmly. Forty bowls racked above forty name-hooks. A rota under glass. A population, on paper, of forty. Ask the ear instead, down in the bowl, and the ear returns a different count: nothing with a heartbeat. That is the Rule, and it has no exception and no explanation. Both answers are correct. The census of Loudwater has to be taken with slower instruments.

Start with the ones who do not live here, because their shape is everywhere. The crew — left, or finished, or never quite arrived. What remains of them is not ghosts. Ghosts are a heartbeat's idea of persistence, someone-shaped, and nothing here persists in the shape of a someone. What persists is the shape of the jobs. The range stays blood-warm for meals no one eats. The bell rings and the lamps come on and the shifts change. The polish on the brass stops at the height of a hand — either the last hand's fossil, or an ongoing act happening at fossil pace, and in Loudwater those two are hard to tell apart, because everything ongoing here happens at that speed. The work was absorbed into the plumbing one task at a time: the bell handed to a cam, the clocks to the water, the warmth to the spring, the remembering to the paper. The crew's final product was their own redundancy, delivered the way everything is delivered here — on time, in threes, no one to sign for it. M. Duvall's hook still fits a bowl. Okonkwo's watch still comes around every night. Nothing misses them. Missing requires a silence shaped like someone, and Loudwater's only silence is the held breath at top dead center, and it is shaped like the whole bowl.

The Rule, then. Take the census on its terms. What it turns away is a long inventory: moths, larvae, snails, small silver things, webs, nestlings, the hawks that ride the updraught all afternoon and shear off at the lip, every one, as if the lip were a customs post where the living present their papers and are waved back. What the Rule admits is everything that lives without a pulse: spore, rot, rust, resonance. And water, which has a beat but no heart — the Rule's one loophole, or its foundation. Counted that way, the bowl returns four shapes. Or one shape wearing four.

The eldest is the water. Up on the plateau the few streams practice disappearance; down here the practice is perfected — the water disappears upward, over the rim, on schedule. But it was here before the bowl was a bowl. The bowl is the water's own work, dissolved grain by grain out of the hill, a house the water built and that learned to evict it. The oldest stair, the one cut into the stone under the wood under the iron, has treads dimpled not by boots but by drips: the water was using the stair before anything with feet, and it uses it still — the weep-lines descend beside you, each stratum at its own pace, each planted thick with maidenhair, the water gardening the route it walks. Its temperament is tepid, sulfur-sweet, clear to two fathoms, and it rises at the pace of a heartbeat, the one beat the Rule allows, because it is a beat with no heart behind it. It is the only resident that arrives on its own, and the only one that leaves. Everything else was built or seeded. Daily it writes its autobiography, one line, identical, and the spent pages slump off the table into felt. And on the pool wall there is a chalked line — chalk, in a town where nothing dries, where every surface sweats, where chalk should not last an hour — and the line holds, season by season, never varying by a finger. The paperwork is calm about this too. Some things in Loudwater are maintained by nothing you can find.

Second by seniority, first by coverage: the green. Species many; individual, arguably, one — the whole bowl a single patient body wearing many shapes, cloning, creeping, the old world, the spore world, the world before the news about pollinators arrived. It keeps a few flowers anyway, small and pale and self-married, out of an older world's habit — and here is the thing about them: they have stopped consulting the sky. Noon is a rumor the rim tells the far wall, so the flowers open for the lamps. Even the flora keeps the town's time now. Its occupations are custodial and structural at once. It reads the works — every standpipe wears its beard graded by temperature, bottle-green at the cuff, rust and cream at the warm collar, the green's way of taking a pulse that does not exist. It holds up what it killed: the vine that brought down the Candle now bears it, and there is no remorse in that, because at that speed killing and holding are the same verb. It catches the leaning walls in root-mats. It absorbs the dead without eating them, and where it works, it glows — the foxfire in the wheel-pits and fallen timbers is its lamplight. It works nights. Okonkwo's shift. It is the only resident that grows, and it grows at the rim's rate, the width of a leaf per lifetime, in the same hurry as the geology.

Then the named. Population, if you count nameplates: three. PATIENCE, DILIGENCE, PERSEVERANCE — the Rule's three articles, or the place's selection pressures, or the founding brothers of the order, depending on which ledger you keep. They are distinguishable the way long residents of one house become distinguishable. Patience has a wobble in its flywheel you could set nothing by; it has not worsened in the paper's memory, and its stroke is the one you wait for. Diligence sweats the most, works the hottest, and its brass at hand height is the brightest of the three. Perseverance carries the cam, rings the bell, calls the offices the town keeps eleven times of. Beneath each, where the condensation runs off the noses of the valves, three hundred years of drips have written three stains on the floor of the Choir — rust-red, verdigris, lime-white — the only handwriting they have, their autographs signed one drop at a time. And they are not lonely. A round is the least lonely form ever devised: one song keeping itself company in three bodies, a canon at twenty seconds to the note, a single chord of it a minute long, the verse a shift, the hymn ongoing since before the names on the rota. Once in a long while the phases coincide and all three beams hang at top dead center, and the round becomes a unison of none — the held breath — and the echo of the last clank arrives from the far wall and finds nothing to follow it, and the spring's boil goes on regardless, because the pause belongs to the engines, not the water. Then the downstrokes re-enter, out of step, like singers who know the catch by heart. They do not sleep. Nothing here sleeps; sleep, like missing, is a heartbeat's custom.

Fourth, and the thing the census was looking for all along: the whole. Loudwater itself, the resident. Its physiology is written in its own list of clocks. It breathes — the fog deck sinks a few feet at night and rises by day, a tidal lung, one breath in twenty-four hours. It has a voice — the chord: the spring's bass, the round in threes, the flume-chords, the drip-percussion off tin and leaf and stone and water, the nine-second clank and its echo a breath behind, so that its only conversation partner is itself, delayed. It has pulses — the main climbs the stair warm as a wrist and trembling, and every pipe in town is another wrist. It has hearts: three, staggered, which is why the thud comes in threes — not one heart beating thrice but three hearts beating once each, politely out of turn. It has skin, the green, grown structural, skin that holds the bones up. It has memory of the sedimentary kind — paper-felt, dimpled treads, settled lines; it does not recall, it deposits. And it attends. The houses lean inward as if listening, the bowl listens to its own held breath, and a thing that listens to itself is a thing the census-taker's form has no column for. But the vital sign, the one that settles it, is the upkeep: the chalk that chalk cannot explain, the carbons in the hissing arc lamp, the oil in the weeping cups, the polish at the height of a vanished hand. A body is a thing that maintains itself against dissolution. Loudwater maintains itself. There the pen stops, because the next word is a word with a heartbeat in it, and the Rule.

And then there is you. You arrive like the old kind of weather, from above, and like the old kind of weather you mostly stop at the fog deck — mostly. For the length of your visit the bowl has a heartbeat after all: yours, the only sound in that register, audible to you precisely because nothing answers it. Within the hour it has been drafted into the round as a fourth voice, out of phase, and you are counting in threes without deciding to. The place does nothing for you. No lamp lights at your approach, no drip waits, the clank keeps its nine seconds whether or not you stand under the bridge to hear the echo — and that is the peace of it. Nothing here is performed. If you stayed, there are bowls, hooks, blank lines on the rota, work that would fit like a worn boot; the readiness would take you in without comment, the way the moss takes a flagstone. But everyone who comes to keep Loudwater arrives eventually at one of the paperwork's three calm options. You leave. Or you finish — you build your own redundancy into the plumbing and hand your last job to a cam. Or you never quite arrive. The paperwork declines to say which is which. It is calm.

File the census in the office, where the drum turns and the pen writes the water's autobiography, one line a day, every line identical. Translated, the line says: still here. Three hundred years of still-heres have slumped off the table and softened into sediment, the record keeping itself, composting into the geology it records. The bell rings — Perseverance, on the cam — and the lamps come on in the Choir, and the shifts change, and the green comes on for the night work, and the foxfire strengthens in the pits, and the round re-enters, and on the rota under the glass fogged from within it is Okonkwo's watch, and no one clocks in, and everyone is there.
