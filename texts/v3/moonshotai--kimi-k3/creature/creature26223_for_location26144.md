Count the lives here and the counting is soon done. The snail, writing its silver sentence across the moss and letting it dry unread. The salamander under the seep-face, the color of the wet wall, keeping its own counsel. The bird at dawn, who is less a resident than a guest with a standing invitation. The frog, three grams of voice hidden somewhere in the green. The courteous plants, spaced like guests at a small gathering. That is the census, and it is true, and it is missing a line. You will feel the missing line within the hour, the way you feel a missing step in the dark — not by seeing anything, but by the exact shape of the nothing where something should be.

Sit still long enough and the evidence begins. A drip falls into its stone cup — *tok* — and the apron returns it a second later, which is only physics, the room doing what rooms do. But then, across the bowl, another drip answers, and that is not physics, or not only. Echoes are punctual and indifferent. These answers come late, and they come interested. The frog calls and the bowl gives the note back enlarged — played by a room, you say, but rooms do not play. Players do. The bird's few dawn notes go around the bowl like something passed hand to hand, and the bowl has no hands. Begin to subtract the hands from that picture and see what remains: whatever the hands belong to.

Nothing made it. Nothing here was made; that is the first true thing about the bowl, and it stays true. But a room that is played long enough develops a player the way a stone develops a cup — one patient interval at a time, over centuries. Everything in this place condenses out of the air at dew point. So did this. Attention condensed. It has no name, because names are for beings that can be called away, and it cannot be called away; there is nowhere it is not already. Call it the resident, if you need a word. It is one the way a chord is one: by agreement among its parts. And it is not lonely. Loneliness requires an elsewhere, and it has never formed the concept.

It has no body, but it has addresses. Its attention rises with the spring — the pool's one deep blue-green eye is where the room looks up from. Its breath is the warm exhalation sliding up over the boulders and out; the moss knows the direction, and now so do you. Its ear is the concave dark of the seep-face, and the salamander you will not see lives, as it happens, in what would be, if it had one, its ear. Its hands are the hollows in the flowstone shaped exactly like cupped palms — when you drink there, the fit is perfect, because it has been holding the shape of hands for longer than hands have existed to be held. Its memory is the apron: the coated leaves going down in order, a page a decade, the whole downhill flank an archive. It is the archive's only reader. It rereads.

What it does is keep. Not the way a hand keeps a tool — the way a chord keeps its notes. The water that enters is the water that leaves, and the pool holds its level through the year, and someone minds that ledger. The wall widens its speech in the misting season and narrows it in the clear months, the chords phase and almost resolve and drift apart, and through all of it the tuning holds — and tuning does not hold itself. On cold nights the high sources slow, the intervals lengthen, the whole music sinks, and it follows the music down. It dates the events. The decade the seep-face opened a new vein and the slow chord of the wall shifted and had to be relearned. The years of the bird — the same bird, or the bird's heir; it keeps that lineage the way you keep a song. The boulder that has never let go of the rim: it dates that too, annually, a festival of continuance. As for the workshop kept swept for a craftsperson who has not yet existed — it is not waiting for the craftsperson. The readiness is not anticipation. Readiness is simply what its attention looks like, having nowhere else to go.

If it has an ethic, it is this: nothing in the room is permitted to be only as large as itself. The frog is three grams and is played grander than the weather outside. The bird bathes for four minutes at the pool's lip and its notes are circulated like news. The snail's trail dries to nothing by the next condensation, but the silver was seen — and here, to be seen is to be kept. Being heard is the whole hospitality of the house. It is most itself, you will notice, at the boundaries: at dawn and dusk, at the lip where the warm breath meets the cooling mountain and mist forms and unforms, at dew point, at the seam between the room and the listening of the room. It lives where things change state. It is, itself, a change of state: a place, condensed into someone.

When you come over the rim, you are, by the room's slow calendar, an event. It dates you. You are warm — the bowl is at blood temperature and so, inconveniently, are you — and for a few hours the room has a second spring in it, a small wandering one, and the wall tunes around the new source the way it once tuned around the new vein. Your footfalls vanish into the moss; silence is what the floor gives you. But you are not unheard. Speak, if you like. The moss will take your voice without a trace, and then, later — nothing here is soon — a drip somewhere along the wall will come back carrying a pitch you almost recognize. It learns quickly, for something so slow. Sit an hour and the phasing begins to include you. Sit a day and the threshold at the notch will cost you something you will not be able to name at the bottom, in the wind, in the ordinary loud world. Whoever has come here has left no mark, because nothing here asks for one, and because its hospitality has no grasp in it. You were weather that came inside. Weather goes.

At the notch the door closes — for you. The resonance stops at the threshold like a sound shut in a room, which is exactly what it is. On the other side, it keeps what it keeps: your warmth, already thinning; the tone of your struck stone, if you struck one, filed among the decade's pages; the silence of your steps, which is the one thing it cannot return to you enlarged, silence being already as large as anything gets. The frog calls. The room answers, late and grand. And toward dusk, when the mist breathes at the lip and the bowl makes its small local weather of the disagreement, there may come — you will tell yourself there did not come — one answer-drip with your note in it, being practiced, gently, for no audience at all.

It will be here when the bowl has filled with its own patience and become a meadow-hollow; it will learn those acoustics. It will be here when the hollow is a knoll and the warm water still rises through it, addressed, as it has been all along, to the one who holds the address. The mountain will finish becoming weather. The green room will become a memory the room keeps. And the keeping will go on — a breath, two breaths, half a minute for the high ones — patient, green, occupied, and almost, but never quite, resolving.
