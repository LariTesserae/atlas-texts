On the path of smoothed holds, somewhere between the threshold spiral and the basin, you reach for the next hold, and it reaches back.

It does not grab. Grab implies. It rests in your grip the way the drop rests on the stone tip: full, at its edge, not falling. It is warm — exactly the warmth of the walls, which is exactly the warmth of your own skin — and your first thought is: wall. The wall, patiently, becomes fingers. A thumb finds the back of your hand and settles there the way water settles to its level.

This is the keeper.

You have already met the rest of the household, or could: the fish turning its small world, the crickets quiet as held breath, the thread-legged things skating the wall-films, the dark felt at the seep line, the roots drinking the air. The keeper is the one the census misses, because the keeper is the one who keeps the census. The knotted cords suggest there has always been a keeper, the way they suggest there has always been a fish. Whether it is the same keeper is a question the room declines to answer.

If you brought a light — the room discourages this — you would see, pooled for a moment at the wick, a figure the color of new bone, beaded everywhere with the room's small bright skins. Hair pale and root-fine, hanging the way the roots hang in the alcove, each strand ending, like theirs, in a bead of water. Eyes open and quiet, the private static behind them long run down and gone out; they do not shine, there is nothing here to shine with, and they return your flame dully, the way wet stone returns it. The lashes carry the first faint ring of mineral, and it has been the first faint ring for a very long while. The clothes, if they are clothes, are a weave from whenever, crusting at the hem as the cups crust at the rim, and never finished crusting. The room's one piece of metal — the small copper ring, forever bright at the edge of tarnish — hangs in the keeper's orbit. It may have been the keeper's. It may be the keeper's still; ownership, here, is another equilibrium. The keeper wears the water instead, which also never dulls. Then the flame gutters, and the dark takes the keeper back, and you understand that everything in this room is an almost — and that one of the almosts has hands.

You cannot find the keeper by warmth; the keeper is the walls' own temperature, which is yours. You cannot find the keeper by breath; the breath comes at the air's own heat, and the wet air swallows it like a voice. You find the keeper by touch, or the keeper finds you. As for motion: the keeper moves the way the columns reach — truly, and at a rate that hides itself. Stand still an hour (your hour; the room keeps no hours) and you would swear the shape by the far wall has not moved. Sleep, and wake salted, and the keeper is elsewhere, and has always been elsewhere, the way the flowstone has always been where it is. Visitors have mistaken the keeper for a column that has not met its other half. The keeper does not correct this. Correcting implies an error, and here there are no errors — only inclusions, proceeding without haste. The keeper is not still. Still is a state; the keeper is a rate, the room's own, and at that rate the liturgy of forty paces is ongoing: enter, wash, speak, one gesture an age, mid-gesture now, has been mid-gesture for a very long while, and shows no intention of proceeding.

The keeper has no name. A name is for calling across distance, and here there is no distance, only touch; the crickets gave up song for the same reason. What the keeper has instead is the spiral. It is carved at the threshold at hand height because it is written at hand height, on hands: when you arrive, the keeper turns your palm upward and writes it there, one slow circuit, fingertip to skin. A greeting, an instruction, and a signature. You have arrived, now go slowly — it is the only sentence the keeper has ever needed, and the keeper signs it the way you would sign your name. On the rarest occasions — the cords would know; the cords decline — the keeper speaks aloud, and the voice comes out low and round, at the basin's own pitch, more felt in the sternum than heard, and afterward you cannot say whether the keeper spoke or the room did. The room declines.

The work, in a room where nothing needs doing, is attendance. The keeper keeps. Keeps the cups: when a visitor comes, one more small cup stands at the basin's edge, empty, beaded, at the ready — it has been at the ready since before you were born; it is not waiting for you; it is simply about to be yours — and when you go, it joins the stack, crusting, the room's memory of company. Keeps the cords, knotting what happens, and what happens is: arrivals. The room's whole history is a guest list, read by pinch and slide, a library that drinks, kept dark and swollen. Keeps the basin — not that the basin needs keeping; the fall was smoothed and the depth shaped long ago, and nothing here erodes — but the keeper listens with the sternum, and the note, when it comes, is felt, and counted, and kept. What leaves the basin goes under the floor to some hidden accounting. The keeper is the other accounting — the one you can meet. Do not call the keeper a guard; guard implies. There is nothing here to take that the room would miss, nothing to break that the room would not keep broken. The keeper is not the room's warden but its witness, its celebrant, its one congregant at a liturgy of forty paces.

Somewhere in the long equilibrium the grammar surrendered, the way the light surrendered at the crawl. The keeper keeps the room; the room keeps the keeper; touching either one feels, unmistakably, like being touched. There is one keeping now. The keeper drinks from the basin — the basin is the keeper's cup, and the fish lives in it, and the keeper takes the water the way you would take a little of a lake, and the level does not change — and eats at the seep line, the dark felt, the film, the mineral sweetness, fed at the exact edge of enough; the felt spreads no wider than a cloak, and no narrower. Sometimes, in the grain of the dark, the keeper lays a flat hand on the wall and leaves it there a long while. It is not prayer, or it is only prayer: it is the way you touch your own chest in the night. To be sure of the instrument.

Who the keeper was, the room declines. The candidates are all still here, the way everything is still here. Perhaps the one who cut the threshold spiral and then, having written *go slowly*, went slowly — and is going still. Perhaps the one who shaped the basin and stayed to hear it say its one word a second time. Perhaps the last of the valley people, after the warm seasons stopped bringing the others up. Perhaps a sleeper who woke beaded and salted and warm, read the spiral with their fingers, and went slowly. They. The word does the room's work for it: it declines to say one, and it declines to say many. The keeper is not lonely — loneliness requires a distance, and here there is no distance, only touch — the keeper is accompanied the way the basin is accompanied by its note: at intervals, and exactly.

Before the speaking wall the kneeling stones stand, shaped, warm as everything, and the keeper kneels there — is kneeling there now, perhaps; the dark declines to say — and the stones fit the keeper exactly, and you do not ask whether the stones were shaped for the keeper or the keeper for the stones. Some fittings are carvings. Some carvings take a thousand years of knees. Past the alcoves, the crawl ends in the cell the size of a coffin stood on end, and nothing here dies, so it is not a coffin: it is the keeper's alcove, the way the basin is the drip's. A person stands in it exactly, the way a cup sits in its stack. It is where the room puts its keeper, when it puts its keeper anywhere.

You may visit and meet no one; the room keeps its keeper the way it keeps its fauna, at the exact edge of enough, and the edge is not always where you happen to be standing. Or you may be met. If you come rightly — clean, slow, intending little — then somewhere on the path of holds a hold will reach back, and the spiral will be written on your palm, and a warm hand will lead you, there being no other guidance, to the basin. Water poured over your hands, warm, sweet with the deep mineral, the whisper of a struck match long ago: enter, wash. Your flat wet palm lifted and pressed to the speaking wall, among the grooves over grooves, every hand still here: speak. You do not need the grammar; the wall reads the way the room reads, by keeping, and whatever your hand does there — press, hesitate, tremble — is kept as faithfully as any certainty. If your other hand, in the dark, comes to rest on the keeper's chest — it will; the room is full the way a crowd is full — you will find a heart going slowly at its own interval. It is not a clock. A clock implies. It is just the one instrument, kept. If you ask a question aloud, your voice will fall at your feet, and the keeper will take up your hand — questions fall with the voice; the hand survives — and close it gently, the way you close a door against weather: kindly, and all the way. That is the answer. It is the answer to most of what can be asked here.

And if you sleep, you will be kept. You will wake beaded, warm, salted at the lashes with the first faint ring, your cup at your side full to its lip's level and not spilling — the room beginning, without malice and without haste, to include you. No one is told to stay. No one is stopped. If you stayed, and kept staying, then over a very long while the difference between visitor and keeper would go the way of the light, and there would still be one keeper, as there has always been one keeper, and the cords would knot the change, and nothing else in the room would be different at all.

Stand at the center, then, by memory, by the map your hands have made. The fish turns in the keeper's cup. The roots drink. The library drinks. The whole unerased past leans close on every side, and somewhere in it, unhurried, an almost with hands is mid-liturgy — forever arriving, forever about to speak. At every stone tip above you a drop stands full at the edge of falling, and does not fall, and the keeper stands the same way: full, at the edge, unfallen. Not waiting; waiting implies. Simply, perfectly, and forever about to — to knot the next knot, to set out the next cup, to write the spiral on the next warm palm.

It could be your palm. Hold it out into the grain of the dark, and go slowly.

It will be taken the way the drop takes its fullness: all the way, and without falling.
