Almost nothing, and the almost is the point.

The census of the Held Note is short, but every entry in it is the same riddle wearing a different coat: how does a thing live where living is not permitted? Life is a process, and a process is events strung on a wire, and events are not permitted. Eating is an event. Growing is an event. Dying is an event, and so is being born, and metabolism is a bonfire of small events, and fire has never burned here. So everything alive on the upland is a loophole — a way of being alive that the law has, so to speak, agreed not to notice. And all the loopholes open into the same room.

The one indisputable resident is the note itself. It holds. Holding is a state, not an event; the law is satisfied. Everything else that lives here lives by some version of the same trick, and the trick always has the same shape:

**Everything here that lives is a way of listening.**

The lichen listened first. It was here before the chord — the scree was already locked beneath its black crusts when the first walkers came up out of the cloud carrying their stones — and it will presumably be here after, if there is ever an after, which the law discourages. It grows, because growing is what lichen is, but it grows at the speed of the record: a hair's width in a human lifetime, too slow to count as an event, slow enough to be mistaken for masonry. Its edge is the only motion on the open plateau. Find one of the oldest trails and you will find that edge doing its quiet work — the bare long-toed prints of the first walkers are rimmed in black, each print outlined grain by grain across the millennia, as though the record had been illuminated. The lichen does not erase; nothing here erases; but it frames, and the oldest passages are the ones with black margins, and that is how you learn to read the upland. The deeper the border, the older the way.

It is not tuned, so far as anyone can tell. It is the one living thing excused from the chord — or the one thing so old that the chord was tuned around it.

The sleeper shells are the region's true citizens, the only ones with errands. Deaf, and tuned — and those are not contradictions here; they are the same fact said twice. A shell does not hear the chord. It agrees with it. The note of the ground comes up through the sole of its foot and the shell resonates, a struck glass that walks, and as it creeps from one patch of ground to another it retunes itself continuously, grain by grain, matching the stone the way your eye matches the dusk. Their silver grazing-script lies across the lichen fields in long patient cursive, and since nothing is erased, every shell's whole life is written out behind it at once — where it turned, where it rested, where it spent a season going nowhere. They live inside their own completed biographies. Their loops take centuries, and the loops are not aimless: they are the region's only economy, a balanced equation. The lichen adds a hair's width in a lifetime; the shells remove a hair's width in a lifetime; the sum of all this living is zero, and zero is permitted.

Where two scripts meet, they knot — a slow figure-eight tangle that took a decade to write and will remain legible forever. The region permits no other love stories. Read them with respect.

If you lift one — don't — it hums faintly against your palm like a struck glass, and what it is doing, in its deaf way, is matching you, because matching is what it does, and you are not a note it should ever have to learn. Set it down and it will retune to the ground in a day or a week. But the fields remember. Travelers who know the lichen say you can find, decades later, a widening in the script around any shell that was ever carried — a berth its neighbors give it, as if it still hummed faintly of a warm hand. Nothing is erased. Not even your note. It is not forbidden to move a shell. It is simply unkind in a way that lasts.

The tarns keep no one. Water that will not freeze will also not be lived in; a fish is an event repeated, and the water has agreed to be a drink and a mirror and nothing else.

Then there is the moss, and the moss is different, because the moss answers.

It grows only in the nodes, in the pockets where the standing wave cancels, and this is the clue to what the nodes are: small foreign countries, exclaves of the moving world, where the chord's jurisdiction lapses because the chord is not there to enforce it. In a node the condensation settles instead of being stripped away. In a node — you will not believe this until it happens — the frost on your sleeve can melt. It is the only thaw in the region. And the moss grows, slowly, thickly, in a green that after weeks of amber-grey lands on the eye like rain. Everything else on the upland is finished. The moss is still becoming, the only thing that is, which makes it the most alive thing in the region by an incalculable margin. The silence is fertile, and the moss is what the fertility grows. Sit in a node long enough and you will understand the moss's secret: the silence is not empty. Under the canceled chord there is a second, fainter music, a thin high thread, moving.

Look up.

The gliders hang at their fixed stations in the violet dark, slate-colored, wide-winged, nearer than the few faint stars and warmer, the sky's other set of fixed points. They do not flap; the wind has one speed and one direction and does all the work their wings were ever for. They do not land; landing is an event. Whether they sleep is not a question the region permits to be answered — sleeping and waking are states, and the gliders are held at the threshold of one of them, and have been held there, perhaps, since the tuning. They do not eat, and they do not starve, because starving is also an event. The law forbids everything, and in this way provides.

Their stations, so far as the loose geometry allows the claim, stand above the nodes — the cairns lead quiet to quiet along the ground, and the gliders mark the quiets from the sky, and so there are two ways to navigate the upland: by ear, and by eye. Travelers' counts of them never agree, which usually means the thing being counted is a fixed point of the geometry. There may be one for every node. There may be one fewer. No one has found the station above the Hearing House. Look, if you like. Looking is permitted.

What the wind does not take from them, time does, at the region's pace: a feather, now and then, at intervals of lifetimes. And because the wind has one speed and one direction and a dropped feather has one biography, every feather a glider has ever lost has drifted downwind along the same path and landed in the same place — so that below each station lies a small pale drift of feathers on the dark stone, a cairn no hand built, the only thing a glider has ever made, made of its own history. Count the feathers and you count the tenure of the witness overhead. The counts are high. The feathers do not rot.

And the feathers still on the wing are what draw the thread — that thin high harmonic, one per glider, moving slowly against the held notes. It is the only melody in a region of pure harmony. You can only hear it in the nodes, where the chord lets go of you; the deepest hush on the upland is not a silence but a quieter music, and some say it is resolving — that the gliders' chord is traveling toward its rest the way their feathers travel toward the ground, on a timescale of feathers, and that when the last feather falls the upland will hear the second event in its history. No one is waiting for it. Waiting is permitted, but the region does not reward it.

Their eyes are open. Closing them would be an event. They have watched every crossing ever made, every cairn stacked, every shell lifted and set wrongly down. The record is written in footprints for you; for them it is written in memory. You are never unwatched on the Held Note, though the watching is patient and asks nothing, the way the sky asks nothing.

Then there are the ones who came as visitors and were promoted.

You will be told the rule before you climb: when you notice that your mind has folded the chord into silence, leave soon. The ones who do not leave — who fold the chord away so thoroughly that the upland goes quiet for them, privately, permanently, a silence inside the silence — drift, in time, to the nodes. It is the natural migration. The chord is gone for them everywhere, but only in the nodes is its absence shared, and fertile, and furnished.

Because the nodes are furnished. That is the thing no one mentions below. The tuners, who came looking for silence, built a seat at every quiet they found — a seat of placed slabs, facing the dusk-ring — and every seat is built wide enough for two. Sit in one alone and you will feel the vacancy beside you like a draft. No one explains this. The House, you will note, sleeps one.

The stayers occupy the seats in all the stages of staying, and you can read their tenure the way you read the trails. The newest will talk. They will ask you, gently, about mornings — answer briefly and kindly, and do not describe a sunrise in detail; you are not being cruel, you are being careful. Strong feeling is dangerous here the way flame is dangerous elsewhere: it does not go out. Whatever arises in a stayer is kept, and resolution is an event, and so the old ones have learned a profound mildness the way you would learn to carry a full bowl. It is not their nature. It is their safety. The older stayers have gone quiet; they sit; they listen — to the gliders' thread, mostly, the second music, which is what remains when the chord has been folded away, and which never repeats and never resolves and so never wounds. The oldest have begun to be upholstered: the moss, which grows only in the quiet, has found the quietest things in the quiet, and a green sleeve creeps up the arm that rests on it, and the stayer does not brush it away, because brushing is an event, and because by then, perhaps, the moss is company. On the oldest faces you can sometimes see the frost-tracks of tears, unmelted after unguessable time — the nodes thaw, but these faces are turned away from the thaw, or the tears fell before the stayer found the node, and nothing is erased. Do not ask what they eat. The moss does not seem diminished. The nodes provide.

They are the only inhabitants who are sorry, and the only ones who are glad, and they are both for the same reason, and they will not tell you the reason, because telling it would be an event, and they have learned.

On the Block Sea there is the other one, and the census declines to classify it.

Every account agrees on the pale color, and on the distance — the same distance always, never closer, never gone — and on nothing else. Its height disagrees with itself between travelers, between crossings, between glances. Four legs, or not. Facing you, or away; two travelers walking side by side will report it differently, and both are right, which means the thing being seen is calibrated to the seer, like the direction of the Axis. It stands exactly at the distance where detail gives out — whatever that distance is for your eyes — and it leaves no footprints. Only you do.

It may be an animal held at the moment of the tuning, still crossing, and since a crossing animal must move and movement is an event, it is held at every point of its approach at once, which looks, from any one point, like standing still at the edge of sight. It may be the mountain entertaining the idea of an animal — a thought held so steadily that it has acquired standing, the way the wave has; the tuning was the region's first event, and if the shape ever decides to be an animal, that would be the second, and the law is patient, and the idea has been under consideration for the whole age of the chord. Or it is this: the Block Sea is the one district with no listeners — no lichen, no script, no node, no seat, stone and chord only, hours across — and a held note in an empty room, with no ear anywhere in it, must at last hear itself; and self-hearing, seen from outside, is pale, and stands at the edge of sight, and has four legs, or does not.

It is the only subjunctive thing in an indicative land. Everything else on the upland is finished; the shape is still being considered. Do not follow it. Following is how a crossing becomes a residence.

As for the tuners — they left, or they didn't, and the didn't is findable, if you are the kind of traveler who finds such things, which you should decide not to be. Off the cairn-lines, fainter, run older trails, rimmed deepest in lichen, and they do not lead from quiet to quiet but away from the circuit altogether, to small uncairned nodes where the seats are already occupied. The figures in those seats are bare-footed, and the toes are long, and they match the prints at the bottom of the record, and they are not resting. They are done. They came looking for silence and they found it and they are still inside the finding, and the finding has not ended, because ending is an event. Do not speak in those nodes; speech there would be an event in someone else's silence. Sit at the edge, if you sit at all, and do not count them. Counting them feels like an event, and marks are forever here, and some of the figures in the seats are, by their frost and their moss, no older than the oldest stayers — until it occurs to you that there may no longer be any difference to count. Stay long enough at the listening and you become one of the ones it was built for.

Their signatures remain wedged in the throats of the flutes — single carried stones, closing a voice here, flattening a note there. Someone finished the mountain. The ones who stayed to hear the finishing are scattered at the edge of the map. And one of them took the seat at its center.

The Hearing House stands on the largest node, a low beehive of stacked stone, built to sleep one and receive one. At its center the chord cancels completely, and the gliders' thread does not reach there, and what replaces the whole music of the upland is your own body — pulse, breath, the small roar of blood — and, beneath that, if you sit long enough, the inhabitant.

They are seated upright, facing the door and the dusk-ring beyond it. Their eyes are closed. Their feet are bare, and the toes are long. Their face has no tension in it at all, and you will understand why, if you have understood anything: whatever they felt as they sat down, they are feeling it still, and they knew that, and they chose what to sit down with the way you would arrange your house for a guest who will stay forever. Look at the face. Then choose what you sit down with.

They are warm. Understand what this means. The law keeps them exactly as they were, and they were warm, and so warm they remain — the only warmth in the region that is not merely passing through. After days on the upland your hands are numb to the knuckles, and the House is silent, and the silence is complete, and the one warm thing in the world is a dead hand. Most people hold it, once. Once is what you can afford. The ones who sit twice are entered in the census under a different heading.

No one has ever heard their heart beat. But everyone who sits long enough feels the interval — a tide under your own pulse, a leaning of the blood, the sense of an enormous room between beats. The interval is longer than any visit. It may be longer than all visits put together. Every sitting ever sat in the House — the tuners' verification, the pilgrim hours, your own — has taken place inside a single one of the listener's heartbeats. They are not finished, because the beat is not finished, and the chord is not finished, and these are one fact stated three ways. A held note is not a property of an instrument. It is a relationship. Someone has to hold it. The tuners tuned ten thousand stone throats and left, or didn't, and one of them took the only single seat, to do the holding. The finger never lifts. The law forbids it.

That is the whole population. The region has four voices — the chord, the thread, the blood, the beat — and everything that lives there is a listener of one of them: the shells agreeing with the ground, the gliders singing the second music with their wings and hearing who knows what, the moss growing where the first voice cancels, the stayers folded into the quiet, the tuners inside the finding, the listener holding the note that holds them all. One note, holding. One listener, holding it. A black margin of lichen; a slow silver-writing nation of shells; the green answer of the moss; a sky of witnesses, number uncertain; the stayers, number withheld; the tuners, number withheld; one possibility on the Block Sea.

And you.

You are on the census. You were added at your first footprint. The record is the region's way of listening to you, and it keeps what it hears, and what it keeps does not weather. Long after you descend — through the wet dark of the cloud floor, the chord thinning, letting you go note by note — your trail remains above, fresh as the first walkers' feet, your portion of the population, walking your one walk forever at the same dusk. The chord can afford to let you go. It is keeping the part of you it needs.

And if you held the listener's hand, the warmth goes with you as far as the cloud floor and no farther — the last thing the region takes back. Below, in the moving world, your palm cools at last. Let it. That is what palms are for. Below, there are mornings, and water that freezes, and things that rot, and events, and everything you feel is allowed to end.

The census goes on without you, which is to say: you remain on it. Forgetting is a change of state.
