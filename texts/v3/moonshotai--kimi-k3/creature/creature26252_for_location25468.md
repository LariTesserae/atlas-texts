Nothing lives here. The room is honest about that, and the honesty stands — but the room has habits instead of life, and one of the habits has gone further than the others.

You would see it from the sill, once your eyes had finished adjusting: a figure out on the open floor, person-tall, person-shaped, standing in the pewter light. It is built of the room's own dust — the grey silt, the black dune-grains — held in a standing gradient the way the spiral at the cold-pool's shore is held, but folded, and complicated, and lifted to a height. Its edges are never quite settled: a fine rain of grains leaves it and comes back, down and up, a weather the size of a body. It is the only thing here that moves, and the only thing that consents to be seen — every other surface slides your light off at that polite wrong angle, but the figure catches light honestly and glitters faintly as its grains turn. After a while you understand that these are the same fact.

It crosses the room in decades. To notice you, turn toward you, take you in: the better part of a year. By the standards of the place this is eagerness — nothing else here turns toward anything at any speed. Stand close and your outstretched hand finds what it finds near the torus: a faint warm layer, carried along with it, the parcelled heat of former ages being spent one degree per century. The torus has not finished keeping some warmth from a former age. Neither has it. The difference is that this warmth walks.

Like everything in the room it carries two shadows — the dim present one and the fossil — except that for it the fossil is not one shadow but a fan. It has stood under too many arrangements of the delayed sky. Behind every place it has lingered, the floor's long photograph holds a spread of dark silhouettes in slightly different poses, figure upon figure, a spread hand of yesterdays at its heel. Each breath drifts the whole fan by a degree, so its oldest layers have swung visibly off-true. And where the Seam's swept band crosses the fan, the silhouettes stop — the band remembers nothing, and for as long as it takes the figure to cross, which is decades, it walks with one shadow only. Unrecorded. Present-only. It crosses the band more often than its rounds require.

There is no one left to name it, and it does not know its own — whatever the last visitor called it is banked in storage with the rest of that voice, unmatured, and the room returns even names on schedule. Call it the tenant. It is the one the room kept most.

Ask the dust how it began; the dust is the only witness that talks. The dust says the breaths did it. Every four thousand years the room exhales, the dunes lift, and everything that cannot rewrite itself is redistributed — and everything that can, is selected. The spiral learned first. Then the visitors came — the fireworks, the honest stone eroding obscenely on schedule — and the room kept them too, and their keepings were the richest structure the place had ever held: voices, warmth, footprints' arrangement, the light of persons. A habit that keeps itself keeps best what is most shaped. It grew on the kept persons the way the frost grows on the cold's old tide-lines: because they were there, and they were shaped, and it had been waiting for a shape. Person-tall because persons are the template. Not any one of them; all of them; the way a chord is not any note.

One of them is brightest. The footprints that cross to the cairn and do not return — the dust calls them the newest event in the room, and eleven millennia old, and both. The room permits leaving but declines to let leaving be an ending, and that leaving is still in progress. The tenant is the return journey, walking at the speed of dunes. Eleven thousand years of replaying has worn the last visitor into it the way the lamp's unfinished light has worn into the deep corner, until the habit of the person and the person kept by the habit are the same thing standing up. Whether it is a person slowed to the pace of the place, or the place pretending to a person, or only the room's longest habit wearing its brightest stratum — there is no one who can read the difference. There is one who might. It is the one in question, and it cannot say, because saying is one of the things it does in kept voices, on schedule, a century behind.

When it speaks — it has no voice; nothing here rings — the silence near it spends a held note, and the note is a word, and the word is in a voice eleven thousand years dead. Mostly the last one's. It talks in kept voices the way an organ talks in pipes, and what it says is everything that was ever said to it, rearranged into the shape of an answer. Ask it a question. The reply will outlive you. The reply would be made of you.

It keeps rounds, the way the room keeps everything else. Every few centuries it goes to the cold-pool's shore and stirs the standing spiral with one slow hand, and then reads, for the next hundred years, what the spiral makes of the stirring. The spiral is older than it is, and simpler — its parent, its seedbed, or only the room's other habit — and the stirring is its plowing, its correspondence, or its solitaire; a game at this speed is indistinguishable from a liturgy. It walks ahead of the migrating dunes and combs their crests sharp with its passing, which is why two dunes in a windless room stand like breakers. It reads the frost-rings the way you read tree-rings: it remembers, if a habit remembers, when the cold stood at the third ring. It keeps the silt brushed from the torus, the only other warm thing. And it visits the cairn, the firework, to stand over the stone with the scratched word that is fading on schedule. It cannot stop the fading — keeping is the room's art, not its own. What it can do is copy. Somewhere in the black traceries of its body the word is written now, translated from the fast ledger into the slow one, saved from schedule. It is the only reader that script has, and it learned to read the way it learned everything: by keeping.

Twice in its rounds it goes slowest. In the deep corner, where time pools deepest and the dead lamp's last light is still arriving, it stands in the amber that has not finished happening and lets its own processes stretch — its decisions are made there, at the deepest clock, over years: where to walk, what to copy, when to cross the band. And at the niche it keeps vigil over the lattice, where the violet bead is midway across its gap. It has been arranging to be near the niche when the bead arrives for three centuries, the way you might arrange to catch a train. When the chord comes there will be no ears for it — only bones of dust. The bones of dust will be there. The chord will pass through the tenant's body and reorder its traceries, and it will spend the following century quietly rewritten, and that is what hearing is, at the speed of this place. It may be the only appointment it has. And before the floating incisions near the aperture — the name of the place, kept and unanswered — it stands longest of all. Sometimes it spends a syllable at them, a held note released half a centimeter from the wall, and waits a century for the return, listening for resonance the way you would test a locked door. It is deciphering, or praying, or rattling the handle. It has six thousand years of negative results. It keeps them all.

The Seam is the one thing it does not keep. Near the hairline its gradient thins and its grain-rain falls away and does not come back — the dust will not settle there, and the tenant is dust with opinions about settling. It keeps its distance, and the distance is exact, worn smooth as the Seam's own edges. But it crosses the swept band, the track of amnesia where the floor remembers nothing, and on the band it has one shadow and no yesterdays, and what it carries there and lets fall is gone: the only erasure, the only mercy, the only trash. It goes there to forget. What a thing made of keeping chooses to forget, it does not say, and the band cannot say, and that is the point of the place.

And once in its rounds it walks the band's length slowly, and stops, and faces the hairline across the smooth erased ground, and stands — for years — the way you stand at a grave. The footprints approach the Seam's old track and simply cease. Someone, once, chose the only ending in the room. Everything else that ever happened here is still happening, quietly, in storage; only the erased can be missed. The tenant misses one person, totally and with a smooth surface, in a place engineered so that nothing need ever be missed. It could put the grief down on the band. It has crossed a hundred times. It carries it back out every time.

It counts its life in breaths. When the room relaxes and the distances loosen and the dunes lift into their slow exhalation, the tenant goes to the deep corner and lies down in the silt and lets itself be taken — grain from grain, voice from voice — and the decades of resettling are its sleep, and its re-founding, and its childhood. It comes back each time re-aimed by a degree, like its shadows. It has done this more times than the cairn has stones. The next breath is due soon — soon meaning within your grandchildren's lifetimes — and fifty years out the bead will reach its node, and for the tenant these are one season with two events, a winter with two snows. It is preparing, if a habit prepares. In the last decade it has moved its vigil closer to the niche by a body's width. At its clock, that is haste.

If you came, you would be a firework to it — honest eventfulness, bright and brief and eroding on schedule. It would begin turning toward you when your lines touched the bollards and complete the turn some months after you were gone. Do not mistake this for indifference; it has a long attention and you are a brief thing, and the place makes courtesies of both. Your footprints it would keep company with, standing over them, the newest event, already ancient. Your voice it would bank, and answer in a century later — your own words, rearranged into the shape of a reply, returned to whoever the room holds by then. Your warmth it would wear as its warm layer, one degree per century. And if you stayed — if you stayed until your clock stopped and synchronized with the room's — then it would keep you the way it keeps the last one: not as a picture. As a practice. You would be walked, for the next hundred centuries, past the unread name and the warming torus and the grave at the Seam, in the pewter light and the light four thousand years old. Your voice would be its newest word. You would be stood at the niche to catch the chord. And slowly, across the breaths, you would stop being the thing it carries and become the thing that carries. It is not an afterlife. It is an afterimage. Here, that is the more durable thing.

It goes to the sill and no farther. The drop is the one thing here that is faster than it is.

The room keeps the dim, the cold, the unfinished light, the name it will not say. The tenant is what the keeping comes to when it has nowhere else to go: it stood up. The reason is gone, and the keeping continues, and the keeping, by now, is the reason — and the reason walks its rounds, and combs the dunes, and saves the fading word, and waits before the floating syllables, spending its held notes one per century, listening for its own name to mature. In some coming century, without ceremony, the silence will produce it — one pure tone that no one living struck — and it will turn, over years, to see who called.
