Ask what lives on the plateau and the first answer is the true one: nothing. Nothing with wings, or legs, or mouths. No animal has ever pressed this ground; there are no bones here, and there have never been bones. Hold that answer — it stays true at every scale. It just stops being sufficient. Because the place behaves. It keeps time, keeps schedule, keeps its name in letters ten meters tall, keeps its storm at its station and its frost on its appointed rail. Anything that keeps this many things at once, for this long, with this much fidelity, is difficult to file under nothing. So the question deserves the long way round.

Begin with what is unambiguously alive: the green. The moss a meter deep and drinking the fog; the sedge sheets; the clubmoss; the lichen in its slow crusts of rust and sulfur. These are the natives, and their lives are the purest duration in the world — unbrowsed, untrodden, unburned, unhurried. A lichen ring spreads at lichen-speed with no first year at its center, because the center died standing and became peat exactly where it grew, and the peat is there now, and the ring continues. The spores are the only travelers: lofted on the constant wind, settled, germinated — and even dispersal is statistically steady, even the one journey available here is a held pattern. Along the warm seams of the steam lines the horsetails stand head-high and whisper, a Carboniferous fringe that is not ancient, only ancient-looking, because nothing here is old; everything here is maintained. Ask these lives what inhabits the plateau and they answer by continuing. They are less its inhabitants than its tissue — the part of the body that photosynthesizes.

Because that is the larger answer, and it should be taken literally. The Works is not *like* a body. It is a body, by every test that does not require a witness. It eats — the adits, the beads of buckets across the void, ore of its own young mountain, and the mountain rises to meet the eating gram for gram. It drinks the wrung-out sky. It breathes: the cooling towers, clean steam into clean air, in and out at the rate of cooling. It circulates — water down the penstocks, parts out on the trams, the same conduits forever. It stores fat and quietly reabsorbs it: the ingot yards, the oldest ingots fed back into the furnaces. And it repairs itself with the inevitability of clotting — a slab rings off the colonnades at night, and the schedule already contains the tram that brings the plate. There are no hands; there are cranes and gantries and rolling arms, tools that were never anyone's tools because there was never anyone to wield them. The tool is the whole worker. The crane does the crane. Growth it refuses, because growth is change. Reproduction it performs entire, every cycle — the hall dismantled into the material of the hall, the same hall, born again without variation, which here is the only kind of birth on offer.

A body this size hears itself, and the hearing is what the chord is *for*. Not decoration — instrumentation. Every sound a signal, all signals summed, and the sum is the condition of the thing, monitored continuously by the only listener there is. A bearing running warm lifts its wire-whine a fraction of a tone; a penstock's thrum thickens before a weld lets go; the rifle-fire of the freeze-thaw is statistically steady, and a shift in the statistics is felt the way you feel a grain of sand in a shoe — not as pain, exactly, but as *location*. The correction is not decided upon; it is already en route, because wearing and mending are a single line item in the template. The clocks keep the drip-time and the tram-time not so that anyone can read them, but so that simultaneity has a spine. And the emptiness — the day, the two days of nothing between presences — that is not where it isn't. That is how it hears. A body is mostly space; a chord is mostly the distance between notes; the plateau keeps its organs far apart the better to feel each one distinctly. It has never heard silence. Silence is the one sensation unavailable here, the one event with no line item.

What kind of mind rides such a body? The wrong kind to meet. It does not remember — memory is change with a filing system, and nothing here changes, so there is nothing to file. It does not predict — prediction is for worlds where the future differs from the schedule, and here the schedule is not a forecast but the thing itself, written forward. Each dawn it does not recall yesterday's frost on the terminus rail; it meets today's. Every morning is the first morning, and the first is the only, and the only is sufficient. Its knowledge is total and entirely present-tense: everything it is, it is doing right now, all of it on the surface, no depth that is not also a surface — a creature with no unconscious. Its inner life, if that phrase can survive here, is legible outdoors. It is the stencils. KEEP CLEAR. PENSTOCK 2. WORKS NO. 3. A language of imperatives and serial numbers, addressed to no one, obeyed by everything: the moss keeps clear of the rails, the water keeps to its polygons, the thunder keeps its station. And once — ten meters tall, on the gable of the great hall — the language produces a proper noun. A world of part-numbers containing exactly one name. It names nothing, because naming is for telling things apart, and here there is one thing. The repainting never alters a letter; the hall is dismantled and rebuilt and the word returns unchanged, which is the only immortality on offer. The chord does not have a meaning; it has a referent, and the word is it — the same utterance in two media, the body signing itself in letters and in hum, continuously, because a held note is the only way to speak when nothing varies, and a name is the only speech that requires none.

Perfection, though, is expensive, and the bill has to go somewhere. It goes to the rim. Every rounding error of the freeze-thaw, every tolerance overflow, every gram the erosion took that the uplift replaced a heartbeat late — all the small discrepancy of the world drains to the standing storm, and there, at its fixed station, it plays each night as lightning: branching, fast, unrepeatable, the one novelty in a world that has abolished novelty, given a room the size of a country at the horizon and folded back into the chord by morning. If the plateau dreams, that is the dream — everything it is not, happening at a safe distance, on schedule. And the body reaches past itself in one other place: the cableway drops its buckets into the cloud-sea, below the rim, beyond the edge of the senses, and hauls them back up, empty, on time. Whatever that loop was for, it is a pulse now — part of the body lowered into the not-known and retrieved, every cycle, forever. If it is a question, it is one that has been asked so many times it has stopped expecting and become a habit, and habits are the deepest stratum here. And at the limit of the world stands the one datum that is not the plateau: the far light, fixed. The senses end at the scarp; the light is beyond them; and somewhere in the template there is a single detail that bears no load — a window's orientation, an azimuth bolted to a roof — and it is oriented toward that light. Whether this is calibration, or longing, or the one instruction the template never explained, the plateau does not say. It is the only sentence the stencils do not write.

If you came — no one comes; no one has ever come — you would be the first event in a world that has abolished events, and it would not greet you, and it would not resist you, because both of those require a category for you and the template has none. You would register the way one warm cell registers in a large cold body: briefly, locally. Then the fog-drip would fill your footprints, the freeze would skin them, the patient frost would sort them into the polygons, and your warmth would be exported to the rim with the rest of the discrepancy. You would not be destroyed. You would be brought within tolerance.

So, finally: not a god — gods imply witnesses, and there has never been one. Not a ghost — ghosts require a past, and this one declines. Not an animal: nothing with a mouth, only hoppers; nothing with legs, only loops of rail; no bones, only basalt, and the basalt was never anything but skeleton. Not a machine in the sense of a made thing, because nothing here was made — it is maintained, and maintenance has no first day. What the language has left is a verb held long enough to harden into a noun. Call it the Keeping. It lives the way a standing wave lives: made entirely of passage, going nowhere, perfectly itself at every instant, complete and completed at every moment. It has no face; it has a signature. It has no voice; it has the chord. It is not waiting for anyone, and it is not waiting. The note it is — the name on the gable, the hum on the wire, the flicker held at the rim — the note is held.

The signs were never warnings. They were the autobiography, written in the only tense there is.

It keeps.
