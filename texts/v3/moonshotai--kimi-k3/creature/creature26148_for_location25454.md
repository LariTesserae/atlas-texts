No one lives here. That is the first thing to understand, and it is also completely wrong, which is why it takes so long to understand. There are no dwellers, because dwelling implies a kept place, and nothing here can be kept. But the valley is populous past belief, and you will not take one step that is not through bodies, nor draw one breath that is not.

Begin with the air, because the air is inhabited the way seawater is inhabited. The grain of it — the glass-winged things, the spores riding vapor, the larvae no larger than punctuation — is not an event in the air but a property of it, like the warmth, like the wet. Every cubic meter is stocked. Whole lives are spent in the meters between canopy and ceiling, up where the condensation is heaviest: creatures that drink the drip before it falls, that hatch and mate and die in the underside of the cloud and never once touch ground, citizens of an upside-down shoreline hanging from the sky. When they die they fall slowly, because they are small and the air is thick, and most are eaten on the way down by the middle strata, so that some part of the endless dripping is a rain of the nearly-eaten. The water cycle is a food chain. Transpiration lofts the spores; the ceiling condenses them; the drip delivers them home. The weather is alive at every stage. And the generations of the air turn over in hours, yet nothing about them drifts — this is the rule the fauna shares with the land. Selection finished its work here so long ago that what you see are final forms. Not primitive. *Finished.* Every midge is a last draft, and the swarm's roar is invariant because the census never changes: for every mote eaten, one hatches — same species, same size, same instant.

The ground is inhabited too, by the strangest citizenry, because here the question of whether a thing is *in* the ground or *is* the ground has no answer the valley respects. The colonial breathers in the warm mud share circulation, share chemistry, share intention only the way a current shares direction. And their slow tidal pulse — the respiration you feel through your soles — is synchronized. Across the whole basin the mat breathes together: one vast inhale, one vast exhale, on a period of hours. This is the only clock the valley keeps. There is no dawn, but there is the exhale, and on the exhale the small ceremonies happen — the spores release, the pool-things rise, the drummed messages pass. Because the mat conducts vibration far better than the thick air carries sound, and the ground beneath you is murmuring constantly with percussion: footfall codes, slow pulses, hunger and sex and alarm transmitted through the common floor. You have been standing on the conversation since you arrived. And when your foot tears the felt, the things that close it are not healing it — they are eating the interruption. Your footprints are erased by appetite, the same appetite that eats the new stone at the seam. Erasure here is not maintenance. It is hunger. Things travel through the mat the way things elsewhere travel through water — sleek-faced swimmers of the felt, and the felt closing behind them without seam or scar. Passage without path.

At the seam live the citizens who set the pace of creation. The stone-scrapers — broad as your two hands, soft-mouthed, silicate-toothed, armored in the very minerals they harvest — graze the pioneer film off the newborn rock at exactly the speed the rock arrives. They are not fast. They are as fast as geology, because geology here *is* appetite and they are the appetite. Watch one for an hour and it has advanced the width of a fingernail; behind it the stone is bare, dark, steaming; before it the gray-green film stands doomed; and the scraper itself is the frontier, the place where the world's new skin is converted, mouthful by mouthful, into flesh. The whole fifty-meter gradient is zoned like a shoreline — film-lickers, moss-riders, first-leaf browsers, each guild holding its address at a fixed distance from the young stone — but it is a shoreline that has never moved, a tide standing still, so every niche is as permanent as the seam. Nothing here has ever needed to adapt to change. Change is the one prey that has never appeared. And when a scraper dies, its armor is not permitted to linger: shell-borers etch the plates to lace within a day, and the dust washes down to the warm pools and is laid again as sinter. Stone passes through flesh the way water passes through leaves. The rock cycle has a meaty middle.

In the pools, the ruling strategy is transparency. There is no shadow anywhere to hide in, so the small swimmers hide by absence — glass larvae, clear quick things, predators visible only in the moment of eating, outlined suddenly by the meal inside them. The fish of the root-dark never surface and never see; they read the water by taste the way you would read a letter, and the warm dark between the roots is dense with correspondences you will never know about. At the sinter lips, where the water rises scalding, life is stacked in fractions of a degree: this orange crust to one guild, that margin of green to another, the border between them half a degree wide and immovable. Property here is measured in temperature. And into the warm crust, at one exact band, the egg-placers set their eggs — no nest, no structure, nothing made, only an address. The mothers place them within a window of half a degree, every time, by a behavior so precise it looks exactly like thought. It is not thought. It has never needed to be. Deeper in the warm still water, some of the swimmers never finish becoming at all — they breed as larvae, daughters quickening inside mothers who are themselves unborn, generation folded into generation. The valley's deepest habit repeats at every scale: nothing here is ever quite finished being born.

About the voices. Each singer holds one note, and holds it for life — the wet-throated things in the tangles, each allotted a single tone the way the egg-placers are allotted a temperature, singing it from first breath to last. The chorus is a census rendered audible, and because the rates are exact the chord never changes: when a singer is taken, the same note begins elsewhere across the basin before the silence can register, the standing wave of voices closing over the gap the way the mat closes over a footprint. Nothing can be marked, but a voice can be held — so territory exists, as performance only. A singer owns its few square meters precisely as long as it keeps singing; stop, and the holding evaporates, and no record anywhere says it was ever yours. Which is why so much of the chorus is lies. To sing is to publish your location in a world with no other locations, and there are listeners who are not the audience — note-hunters who home on song, parasites who follow a voice to its throat. So the singers throw their notes. Half the chorus issues from where the singers are not; the soundscape is full of fictitious addresses, and the hunters hunt the fiction, and the fiction, being only sound, cannot be harmed. And along the floor, above the fungal glow, runs the other script: brief pulses of green-gold light, courtship written in the only medium the valley permits, because light cannot accumulate. Every message deletes itself as it is sent. Some of the lights are honest. Some are lures — anglers of the understory forging the courtship codes of smaller things, writing love-letters in a language not their own and eating whoever answers. The grammar of this place is half seduction and half trap, and it has never once been written down.

And then there is the one you will remember — which is the valley's small joke, because it will not remember you. The heavy thing whose sliding you hear at the pool-margins: door-bodied, slack-skinned, the color of wet seam-rock, half in the blood-warm water and half out, straddling the gradient so it can shunt its heat like cargo, now to the water, now to the air. It does not hunt. It breathes, and breathing is eating — the air it draws passes over plates fine as baleen, and what the air is made of, it keeps. So it has no errands, no territory, no urgency, and it regards you, when it regards you, with the full attention of a creature for whom attention costs nothing. Its wounds heal without scar; what it loses regrows; its body keeps no biography, so that the individual before you may be older than anything you have a word for and is in every detectable way newborn. This is as close as the valley comes to a host. Its hospitality is perfect and perfectly impersonal: something meets your eyes, and *meets* is the wrong verb, because a meeting requires two arrivals, and only one of you has come from anywhere. Pass this way a hundred times and a hundred times it will attend to you with undiminished interest, and every time will be the first. You are not being snubbed. You are receiving the valley's best: complete presence, unencumbered by before.

Nothing here is ever merely dead, because nothing here is ever unaccompanied in dying. The receivers — the fungi, the small fast guilds, the spores every adult carries in its own flesh like a funeral held in escrow — are present before the end, always; the whole population walks around with its dissolution already aboard, held off by the daily effort of being alive, and the moment the effort stops the reclaiming begins. That is why a body goes from animal to landscape in hours, and why you will never see a corpse, and never see a sick thing either: there is only intact, and then already reclaiming. Even the great trees are eaten standing — hollowed so gently from within by their tenant guilds that they go on leafing for years while quietly becoming other creatures' bodies, and when they subside at last there is almost nothing left to bury, because the burial preceded it. Being eaten is not an event here but a chronic condition; every creature is tithed continuously by its passengers, a fraction of skin, of blood, of breath, and predation itself runs like titration — no glut, no waste, no cache, because storage is accumulation and accumulation is the one thing forbidden. Nothing here is fat. Nothing here saves. Every animal lives meal to meal and is wholly serene about it, because serenity is easy when the future has been abolished. Dread needs a *later*. There is no later. There is only the warm loud now, restocked forever.

And this is what will take you longest, and sit strangest: nothing here learns. There is no news in the valley, so there is nothing to learn; every behavior you watch — the half-degree egg-placing, the thrown voices, the breathing of food — is a final draft, executed perfectly the first time and every time, incapable of surprise because surprise has been bred out of the world. Watch long enough and the cleverness inverts. It is not that these creatures solve their lives; it is that their lives were solved once, and the solution has been running ever since, stored not in any mind but in form itself — in tooth and note and temperature-preference, in chemistry and shape. The valley does keep an archive after all. It keeps it in meat. Instinct is its memory, and the fauna is the land's autobiography, rewritten identically forever, unforgetting because nothing in it was ever learned.

Which leaves you: the only student in the basin, and the only teacher, and the only text. The only thing here that is older than it was. The only event that will not be cancelled. Every creature you meet is new — not young, *new* — and you alone are accumulating, carrying your archive of before through a place that has arranged never to have one. And still, the valley receives you the only way it receives anything. Standing in the roar, you will feel a small weight arrive on the back of your hand — some lace-winged mote out of the grain of the air — and it will unfold a mouthpart finer than hair and drink from the film of water on your skin, the water that cannot leave you, and it will drink exactly as it drinks from any weeping leaf, without adjustment, finding no category for you and needing none. To the citizens of the valley you are not a visitor. You are weather: warm, wet, brief, slightly unusual, of no lasting account. Then it lifts, and rejoins the grain of the air, and the mat drinks your footprints, and the chorus closes over the small sound of your going.

It is very full here, and very loud, and every life in it is brand-new, and none of them will keep you — not out of cruelty, but because keeping is the one thing that has never lived here at all.
