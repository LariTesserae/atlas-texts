No census of the Works has ever been completed. The count keeps being edited. The last committee figure held at roughly nine thousand citizens across roughly nine thousand kinds, and the overlap between those two lists was ruled a clerical matter. It is a city grown for the crowd of the world it is drafting, and inhabited, so far, by the auditions. Every hall is a house awaiting its full company. The company is being printed one at a time, on the night shift, at the Lines.

**Names.** You are named for whatever the wall is spelling when you come out of the vat. Hence KEEP LEFT, a beetle of the cistern valves, called Kip by no one — beetles use the full name or none. Hence RAIN AT FOURTH HOUR, a courier moth, called Fourth. Hence WELL LEVEL FALLING, a cistern salamander, called Level, who has never once found the name funny and finds it less funny every season. The commonest name in the Works is Continue; the wall spells it so often that at any vat-wall gathering someone answers to it. The citizens use it as a greeting and as a goodbye, the way other peoples say peace. Whether this is the Works' one joke or its one prayer has not been decided. In the Works those are not always different things.

There are two famous exceptions. The first is the Inspector, of whom more below, whom no lamp-word preceded — it was patrolling before the lamps were grown, and its name is the one blank entry in the archive. The second is the twins: a run of two, the only siblings in the world, both named CONTINUE because the wall had just begun spelling it when the vat, uncharacteristically, delivered twice. They are distinguished as First Continue and Other Continue, they work the same valve on alternating shifts, and citizens touch their wing-cases for luck before long courier runs. The committees could not decide which of them was the error, and so both were kept, which is the closest thing the Works has to a miracle story.

**The scheduled peoples.** The ants govern, and they govern by cutting. An ant has no individual name; an ant is its committee's presence at a particular place, the way a sentence is a language's presence at a particular page. The template they enforce is not written anywhere the citizens can read it — the ants carry it collectively, and no one has ever caught an ant consulting it. Once, famously, on the fourth ring, the Second Pruning Committee found a bud off-template, and one ant declined to snip. Not objected — ants do not object — simply abstained, and stood on the bud, and stood there while the committee circled it for the better part of a season. The bud opened into a flower that blooms between rains. Now the schedule includes a small unscheduled holiday called the Interrain, the only amendment in the Works' history passed by doing nothing, and the ant has been returned to duty without comment, which is how you know it is considered a hero.

The beetles keep the valves and the great shears, and they are built like decisions. At shift-change they park their feet on the warm rivets of the vat-walls and trade opinions, which in beetles run structural: load-bearing, drainage, the scandalous tolerances of the Printfields. Warmth is the wealth here, and a beetle's pension is its rivet — a specific one, earned, defended in the mildest possible way by simply being on it.

The moths are the readers. Sight is a specialist's sense, and the moths are the specialists; they read the lamp-script aloud, in a murmur calibrated under the quiet-laws, for anyone whose world is scent and floor. They pollinate the dark-blooming flowers, carry seed and scent-sealed letters between districts, and shiver on the vat vents before their runs, working their flight muscles up to temperature like small engines. Fourth — the long-proboscis variant, with opinions about it, mostly that it is beautiful and that it aches — remembers sunlight, and more than sunlight: a color. She has spent her adult life trying to render it in scent, at the vat-walls, off-shift: warm ferment-musk under a high green note of bud-day, warmed past its season. Citizens come to smell yellow. Most smell nothing and are moved anyway.

The snails wired the city and then became its librarians, because the obvious occurred to someone: a trail of dried conductive script can be read the same way it was written — slowly, by crawling. The Library of the Ungrown occupies a quarter of the second ring, and reading a memory there means conducting it: a snail on an old trail hums faintly, and if you press your belly to the floor beside her the meadow arrives secondhand, through slime and sole. The chief librarian is KEEP LEFT's sister in name only — a snail named SHIFT CHANGE — who has spent her whole life on a single aisle and expects to finish it, which in a snail is ambition.

The salamanders keep the cisterns, slow and black-spotted, plumbers by temperament. Level knows each fish by its pulse the way you know footsteps. And there is the Listener — a small salamander, not the Inspector, never confuse them — who maintains that the fish are saying something, one sentence, extremely slow, through the grid. She transcribes perhaps a word a season. She will not live to hear the end; she has apprenticed a successor; there is a chain of Listeners reaching back further than any committee's minutes. The sentence so far is five words long. She does not say the third word aloud. She is saving the saying for when the sentence is finished, which will be someone else's shift, in someone else's life.

The bats are the only free speakers in the Works, because the quiet-laws bind the audible and the bats hold their entire conversation where the law cannot hear. By every acoustic indication they use this freedom to discuss measurements. They roost under the rim in the ammonia dark, and at rim-song hour they go out through the mist-gap into the not-yet, and they come back with damp fur and a number. The damp is wrung from their fur into a black gourd: water that has never rained, odorless, slightly less than water, the only thing in the Works that comes from somewhere else. The salamanders keep it and label it and do not drink it, in the way of people who suspect they are keeping evidence. The bats' reports are flat and short. The most celebrated bat utterance on record is: *There is nothing out there, and it is getting closer.*

Whether the fish are citizens is the longest-running question in the Works. The committee on fish personhood has been in session for thirty seasons, and every time it nears a vote the fish do something ambiguous on the floor of the cistern and the minutes must be reopened. The fish, meanwhile, power everything, and answer the lamps, and are felt in the molars of the population like a thought the world is having.

And then the minor orders, the one-offs, the first editions. Aphid tube-wards who tend the vascular transit and take their wages in sap-rates. Springtail gleaners who own drop-season outright. A cricket named ALL QUIET — the wall's timing was immaculate — whose chirp falls precisely under the permitted threshold, making it the Works' only licensed musician; its rhythms are keyed to the fish-pulse, and a concert is an audience lying on the floor, silent to the air, legible to the belly. And there is a spider. The spider weaves, and its webs catch nothing, because there is nothing to catch, and the moths fly out of their way to pass them at lamp-light, when the interior rain jews the silk. The spider's webs are the only things in the Works that exist to be looked at. A committee has been in session for three seasons on whether the unnecessary is on-template — the stakes are larger than they appear, because whatever is decided gets printed into the world-to-come: whether the finished world will contain unnecessary things is being decided, at present, by whether one spider may keep a web. The lamps once spelled CLEAR DEBRIS at the web's location, as routing for the gleaners. The spider stayed, and answers to Debris, with the patience of someone who has read the schedule and found itself absent from it.

**The Lines, and what the new ones bring.** They come out wet, and they come out remembering, and before anything else — before assignment, before ration — an ant clerk takes the deposition. The questions are standardized and very old: *Was the water standing or moving. What grew at the edges. How many were the hands, and what did they hold, and were they holding it toward you or away.* The memories go into the Library of the Ungrown, which is the largest district of the Works and grows faster than the world does. The taxonomy: meadows, common; moving water, common; sunlight, universal among moths and rare elsewhere; colors, disputed — several citizens remember yellow and argue bitterly, in whispers, about whether it is the same yellow; hands, cross-caste, gentle, five-fingered, a rumor the seeds keep telling. Singletons: one citizen remembers a white cold that falls. One remembers a door that opens *outward* — every door here is a valve, and an outward-opening door is as exotic as a winged salamander. One beetle remembers being carried.

Here is the part the citizens understand and you should too: the depositions are planning documents. The archive feeds the Printfields. The Works drafts its coming terrain out of remembered testimony, and the Compost — which is not a graveyard but a feedstock reserve — supplies the paste. The flawed, the ones who come out wrong, mostly know it, and walk themselves ringward and lie down in the feedstock beds, and their substance prints the hills the bats will measure. It is the moths' doctrine that certain new hills keep the memories of the composted, and that when the world is finished the culled will wake inside the places they remembered — that the flawed are so often the best rememberers *because* the draft needs its geography detailed. The beetles' doctrine is that the moths should eat something warm and sleep. The bats decline metaphor. The ants have no doctrine; they have minutes, and the minutes record the question as open, which is how you know the committee is still in session.

The ones who come out wrong and *don't* know it meet the Inspector. It is old by the standards of a place where nothing is old, unhurried, blotched, and it does not decide — it confirms. It tastes the air beside each new emergence. Those it takes, it carries ringward itself, gently, and stays with them at the Compost until they are feedstock, and this is understood to be respect. It is not cruel. It is the schedule. Citizens do not fear the Inspector; they fear wasting its time, which amounts, in the end, to the same education.

**The shape of a life.** Time runs in breaths — the bowl inhales and exhales in twenty minutes, the leaf-doors flap with it, and appointments are made for *third breath after rain*. The seasons turn in eleven days: bud, leaf, flower, fruit, drop. Bud-days are for tender greens; flower-days the nectar runs and the moths work double and come home gloriously, quietly drunk; fruit-days are pulp and gourd-flesh; drop is the gleaning, and everything goes to the vats, because the vats are the hearth of the world. Water comes rationed in your own gourd. Light comes rationed in clicks; private light is a luxury, and igniting a lamp-gall off-schedule is shouting, and spelling an unauthorized sentence with one is the only forgery the Works makes possible. Sleep happens in grafted niches petitioned from the committees, or in the vast empty halls, where citizens scatter themselves like seed and sometimes drift within earshot of one another's small permitted sounds, because a hall grown for a crowd is a hard thing to sleep in alone.

On the last night of drop-season, the whole citizenry — all nine thousand — gathers in the nave, a hall grown for a hundred times their number, and stands where the committees project the crowds will someday stand. The lamps spell nothing at all, so as not to interrupt. For one breath the Works holds its full cast and its full house. Then everyone goes back to work.

**The inward road.** Age is a direction, and citizens walk it. Born in the middle rings, they take slower duties ring by ring toward the center, until the eldest come to the terraces above the Well, and there — when they have finished, and finishing is a recognized condition, like ripening — they ask, or are asked, to be planted. The Rooted stand in the dark loam at the Well's rim with their legs gone to root and their pulses gone slow, and the fish answer them through the floor, and their dreams are the longest thoughts the Works thinks. The eldest of them was named by a sentence the lamps no longer spell; spelling her name took forty breaths and a second lamp, and she is called the Long Word. She is almost entirely plant. She remembers the rim before it was broken. She remembers the sound of it breaking — the only loud thing that has ever happened here — and the quiet-laws date from that day: the Works has been listening ever since, and the citizens keep their voices down so the roots can hear. What broke the rim, she either will not say or cannot, at the speed she now speaks. The bats, who are literalists, point out that something out there had to be *there* to break it, and that this was the first measurement ever taken.

**As for you.** If you stay, a committee will assess whether you are on-template, and an ant clerk will take your deposition, and the lamps will spell you a name, and you will be given a niche and a gourd and a ring, and the long gentle process of being pruned into place will begin. And if you have hands, keep them in your sleeves until you have decided what you are. The moths will come to the vat-wall to look at them. Kip the beetle will look longest and say least. The clerks will ask you to hold things — a gourd, a shear, a moth's folded wing — while a committee watches, and the minutes will read, in their entirety: *five-fingered, gentle, consistent with the rumor.* They will not say whose rumor. They will ask you to describe the meadow, if you remember one.

Use your hands.

At printing hour, go down to the Hollow Core with everyone else. The fish-pulse comes up through the floor. The lamp clicks, and spells one slow word across the dark, and the word is almost always the same, and the new ones come out of the vats while it is still spelling, and are named by it, and the whole citizenry — the committees, the couriers, the valve-keepers, the librarians mid-sentence, the twins, Debris, All Quiet, the Listener, the bats home early with the number — leans the same half-degree inward, the way seeds lean toward water. Tonight the echo came back a half-breath sooner than last night. The measurement is already going down through the roots, and the Rooted are dreaming it, and the Printfields are warm.

The garden walks. The walkers rehearse. Continue.
