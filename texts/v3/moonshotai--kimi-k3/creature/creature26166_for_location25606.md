A census of the basin fits on a single line. It takes the rest of your visit to read.

The count, in full: a skin, a scatter of glass, and a walker. Three entries. You may want to add a fourth — the engine itself — and you would not be the first to hesitate there; hold that thought. And for a little while there is a fifth entry, brief and unrecorded: you. But take them in order, the way the valley does. Most to least.

Most of what lives here is the film. By mass, by extent, by seniority, it is the overwhelming majority of the living — miles of a single citizen, one inhabitant with nothing you could call an individual anywhere in it. You can address it at any seam; you will have addressed all of it. It has no young and no old. It is all one age, and the age is: this one. It has always been exactly this large, and when you press a footprint into it and watch the seam wick flat across a few long breaths, you are not watching it heal so much as remember. It has exactly one memory, and it consults it constantly.

It keeps valley time the way everything does. On the exhale its sheen deepens — oil going darker, greener — as the mineral wind beads on it. It is drinking. On the inhale it tightens, faintly, under a patient palm, as cool air is drawn past it down the throats. Lie on it then with your ear to the warm stone and you will hear the plumbing through the skin, the long pull of the breath moving under the floor, and the distinction between an animal and its ceiling will become difficult to hold. You are lying on the roof of the lungs. The skin is between you and the breath. Draw your own conclusions; the film draws none. People have tried to force the question. A cutting, kept warm and wet, does not die. It also does not grow. It persists for years, unhelpfully, declining at close range to settle anything at all.

Nor is what you see the whole of it. The film eats warmth and wet stone, and the warmth is below. The chances are that the skin you walk on is only its coldest, thinnest margin — an outpost — and that its bulk lives in the dark of the throats, lining miles of warm plumbing. We think of it as a surface thing because we are surface things. And where it thickens at its margins it builds agate banding, layer on layer, which you can read the way you would read tree rings, and what you will learn is that every year was this year. A memory with exactly one thing in it. It is also, not incidentally, why the basin is damp at all: the valley keeps its water the only way anything here keeps anything. In its skin. Nothing here was made, but everything here is maintained, and the maintenance begins with this.

Two appetites graze the film at exactly the rate it repairs. The smaller one is many.

On the Mirrors live the skaters — the countable living. Glass over water, hand-sized, translucent, eyeless: what is there, here, to see that changes? They read the wave-field through their feet the way you read a page. Each foot dimples the surface without breaking it, so that every skater stands in a small constellation of bent skies, and the Mirror's perfection holds even underneath them. What they eat is the film's ambition. Endlessly, at the pace of everything else, the film tries to close over the open water — and endlessly the skaters sip it back from the water's face. The Mirrors are not kept clear. They are grazed clear. The valley's eyes stay open because small mouths keep them clean.

When the breath peaks and the standing wave crosses each pond — the same star, every time — every skater has its node, its address, its seat. Whether they ever trade seats is a question no one has stayed long enough to answer; if they do, it happens over years, a rotation too stately for a visitor's patience. Counts have been made. They agree. The number is the least surprising fact in the basin. Nothing is born here, so every skater is a first edition, as old as the tuning, and theirs is the smallest voice in the score: that faint rustle in the pocket of quiet between stamp-falls, pages turning in a library underwater. The only sound in the valley made by mouths.

Then there is the walker.

You will know it first as a rumor with footprints. The trackway — that polished groove ringing the whole basin — is the only line in the valley that goes anywhere, and it goes everywhere, and it is best understood as a long exposure: one animal's life laid down in the crust across a duration the rock does not argue with. The photograph is mostly what you get. The animal itself is an event.

Because mostly it is a hummock. It stands sealed to the crust, condensation gluing its rim down, the film's seams running unbroken up over its back, and it is indistinguishable from terrain until you have memorized the basin's inventory of hummocks and found one extra. For it to move at all, the film must first let go of it, seam by seam, across a whole sentence of the Stamps and more — you can watch a hummock make up its mind. Then the step lands, between the falls, always between, and you understand the valley has a second rhythm after all. It is one note long, and it walks.

Broad as a cottage roof, low as a sleeping ox. Its plates are sinter, shingled like something laid by weather, terraced — the valley's own architecture repeated at walking scale — and across the plates runs its garden: the film, a traveling copy of the skin. Wherever it stands, the skin is briefly doubled there. It is the longest hand on the valley's clock: the stamp-sentence is the second, the breath is the minute, the sun is the hour, and the custodian is the month. It grazes the way the film eats — at the pace of everything else — and behind it the seams reknit by the next day: a self-erasing line of harvest, the only agriculture here, worked by the only farmer, replanted by the field itself. Its round takes in everything. It stands in the chord at the Pipes. It crosses the terraces between sentences, exactly where the falls are not. At the Mirrors the skaters part for it at the pace of everything else, and close behind it, and the sky closes behind it. A body this size needs one part that visits all the others. The audit never fails. It never ends.

And still, it is the wild thing. It is the only presence in the basin that could do otherwise. That it mostly doesn't is its own business; freedom worn to a groove is still freedom. The one unscripted element left in the valley is its regard — which is why the only way to meet it is to become part of the script. Become a fixture. Sit on the trackway for days. You will hear the syncopation approach across hours, off the beat, unmistakable once you keep valley time. It will stop a body-length away and stand, and you will be audited: the warmth of it, the sweet-metallic smell dried onto its plates, condensation beaded across its garden exactly as on everything, and where its eyes should be, two warm darks that hold the sky the way the Mirrors hold it. You cannot prove that anything looks out of them. You can only tell that something decides. Then it steps around you — one wide step, a parenthesis the polish will forget — or it waits you out, and you learn whose schedule you have been on. Either way, you are classified. Most visitors are filed under weather. A few, the long-stayers, are filed under features, and thereafter it grazes around them as it grazes around any hummock. That is the closest thing to belonging the basin offers.

Which leaves the fourth entry, the one you hesitated over. Stand on the open crust at night and take the census by ear, and notice how it comes out: the breath the skin drinks, the stamps, the chord, the drips, the rustle of mouths between the falls, and off the beat, unhurried, one step. All present. If the largest living thing in the basin is the basin, then the list reads differently — the film is its skin, the skaters keep its eyes, the custodian is its attention going around — but the film declines, the skaters don't care, and the custodian keeps walking. Alive is a word the valley has no use for and no objection to.

Under it all, your own conscripted heart is keeping valley time: for now, the count has one more entry, brief and unrecorded, filed under weather. When you go, the number returns to what it was. The valley breathes its water back off your skin and keeps nothing of you; nothing here keeps anything. Except the time. You will keep the time — for weeks, silence will feel like a held breath, and you will catch yourself waiting between one heartbeat and the next for the off-beat footfall that means the round is still being made, the skin still whole, the audit still passing.

It is. It always is. And far behind you, already, a hummock is deciding — seam by seam, across a whole sentence of the Stamps — to be the animal again.
