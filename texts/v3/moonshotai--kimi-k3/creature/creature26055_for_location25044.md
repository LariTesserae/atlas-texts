Ask what lives in the Draft and the question comes back corrected. Nothing there eats, decides, or wants — this is true, and it is also beside the point, the way it is beside the point that a shop floor between shifts contains no one. The bowl is inhabited the way a shift is inhabited: everything is at its station. Take the census at the bowl's speed, not yours, and the population declares itself one tenure at a time.

The trees are the seniority. Nine or ten station-holders, each alone in its clearing of new stone, and they differ the way instruments differ — not by scar, since nothing here wears, but by spec. One runs a quarter-tone sharp against the hum and has for centuries. One keeps its mica still in the thermal that turns all the others ticking; not stubbornness — a different instruction. The eldest's rings, if you could read them, would come off like a nameplate: voltage, duty cycle, grade. They do not decide anything. They hold. Holding is the work and the life both. And over the very long runs they lean — less than a degree a century, toward the arc-partner across the gap — because leaning is the Draft's only verb for *almost*. When the arc finally crosses, for the length of a tick two stations are a single machine, and the hum steadies: a held breath become, briefly, a pleased one. Afterward the receiving bloom spends thirty years winding a coil the size of a fist. Then the throw.

The coils are the only mail the bowl sends. Some drift the gaps for years on a slow fall of charge; some go ballistic and are, for the duration of the flight, the fastest thing in the bowl. The felt forgives their landing ticks. The ones that miss — that clear the rim entirely — lie out in the scree unopened, addressed to a floor advancing toward them a hand's width a century. Patience is a property of the mail here. Nothing is lost; some things are simply early.

The felt is the apprentice, and the only resident that notices you while you are still there. It remembers a footprint for one afternoon — memory in the Draft is sized to the shift — and forgets by dark, and the forgetting is not coldness. It is curriculum. The felt is being trained to be ground, and ground must not keep accounts. But it keeps one account you would not expect: at noon, sometimes, it brightens under footfalls that are not yours — brief tracks of standing light crossing it, pressed by nothing present, fading behind like breath off glass. The felt forgives in advance. Whatever is practicing to live here, the felt has already agreed to tend it.

The forms at the rim are not creatures and not machines; they are operations with tenure. Nothing decides through them — the specification flows through them the way current moves through the laminations: using them, unconsulted. They retire the way everything here retires, downward into product, and the floor takes them the way the floor takes everything: tenderly, and as material. The eldest is nearly flush with the new ground now, buried standing up at the pace of a hand's width a century, a teacher becoming pavement. They are what the bowl keeps instead of hands. Hands are still in draft, and these are the drafts, learning their grip one crystal at a time.

And the same specifier that cut gutters for rain that hasn't arrived has made other provisions. A burrow mouth at the base of the rim's newest stone, kept swept by the thermals, opening on nothing yet — a doorway, practicing. The chalk of a paw-print pressed into an unweathered cleavage face: layout marks for a foot. In the fork of the youngest iron tree, a nest-shaped allowance, empty with intent, the way the gutters are empty. The bowl's hospitality is not food or water — it has neither — but room. It leaves room. Every allowance is a held door.

At noon the census becomes legible, because at noon the real population casts. Among the shadows of unplaced blooms and unrisen trees there are shadows that do not stand still. A low shape crosses the felt, and the felt lights beneath it in a track of bright footfalls. Near the dry gutter at the rim, on some noons, the shadow of something drinking — head lowered to a channel that has never run — the future rehearsing its own reflection. These are the residents of the fuller shift, clocked in early, standing at stations that are still outline, waiting for a whistle that has not been installed. Waiting is their work. They are very good at it.

Over all of it, the one who reads. You have felt it — the lean over your shoulder at noon. It does not decide; nothing here decides. It compares. It holds the draft against the finished version the way a machinist holds a part to the template, and the distances stay tuned because they are being read. Its lamp is the star at the zenith, steady as a rivet — or the star is its pupil, or the star is simply the datum, the one fixed point every tolerance is measured from, kept in the sky where nothing can bump it. The hum you have carried in your teeth since the lip is what being read feels like from inside the page. The bowl has been checking you against a specification you have not seen.

You are not the first finding. At the rim, at noon, among the station-shadows in outline: one shaped like a person, standing at the lip, facing out. Or in. *Mostly* behaved. If your eyes have learned by then to refuse the sliding-off, you may find others — a small picket of the previously found, kept at the boundary like tools on a shadow board, each in its painted outline, each where it can be accounted for. The bowl files its visitors in draft. Your shadow's hesitation at the threshold was not a malfunction. It was collation.

So the corrected census: no animals; nothing that eats; nothing that wants. But inhabited — by tenure and apprentice, by operations and allowances, by the early arrivals of the fuller shift, by the mail, by the reader, by its drafts of you. When you climb out, your shadow comes with you over the lip. Mostly. What stays takes its station at the rim among the outlines, facing the garden not yet placed; and on some noon a century from now the felt will brighten under a footfall shaped like yours, and forgive it, and forget it by dark — which is what passes for welcome here.

Nothing lives in the Draft. Everything that will live there is already on shift.
