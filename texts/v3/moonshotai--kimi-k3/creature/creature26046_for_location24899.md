Begin with the moths, because the moths will begin with you.

Within your first hour — though *hour* is the wrong word, and every word for time will be the wrong word — they find you. You are the one new water in the country. Your skin films over within minutes and stays filmed, and the film is the first dew that has ever condensed here on something that came from somewhere else. A moth settles on your wrist and you do not feel it land. It is the same warmth as you — everything is — and it has almost no weight, and nothing here casts a shadow, so you know it is there only when you turn your head: white on gold, wings the color of the light, drinking. To the moths you are a spring that walks. Your skin is the one surface in the Held that is not lit from inside.

Yet.

Study them, and they give you the place's first lesson. If you cup one gently in your hands it does not struggle — nothing here has learned struggle — and it leaves no dust on your palms, and when you open your fingers it resumes the same drift as though the interval had been edited out. You will think to mark one, to know it again, and then you will ask yourself: with what? Nothing here holds a mark. Marks are events, and events are edited. So you cannot prove the moths of one hall are the moths of another, cannot prove they are the same ones, always, cannot prove they are not. They are identical not the way made things are identical but the way moments of one thing are identical. A few per hall. The place's slow thoughts, given wings so they can cross your face.

In every pool, the transparent ones, finger-length, glass given an errand. Through them you can watch the water go about being water. The only solid thing inside one is a single dark thread, straight as a seam, and it is the only darkness in the Held that does not go up a Well. Whether it is a nerve or a gut or the creature's whole biography written in one line, you will not learn. They have no eyes and need none; there is nothing to find that is not already passing through them. Count them in a basin — nine steps down, say, into the kept water — and the number will be the same when you count again, in an hour or a year. It will not occur to you until later that you never saw a young one, or a dead one, or one arrive. The water keeps its glass the way the air keeps its moths: at exactly the number, forever. They are not the pool's inhabitants. They are its way of stirring itself.

You will not hear the grazer coming. Sound dies within a step here, and the grazer's step is a sound the floor was already making. You round a column — or it rounds you; afterward you will not be sure — and it is there: the size of a doorway laid down to walk, its back at the height of your shoulder, white. Not the white of fur or flesh. The white of the light, which is the only white the country makes, so that it is less camouflaged than *fluent*. Up close, its hide is the floor's own felt, fine and dense and faintly luminous, as though the corridor had stood up and agreed to walk. Its brow is smooth and eyeless — eyes are for finding and fleeing, and everything it needs is already touching it, and there is nothing to flee. Its mouth is a broad soft oval, and it eats the way everything happens here: by agreement. It presses the felt, and waits, and the fibers loosen into it — not taken, received — and where it has passed the corridor is denser, smoother, brighter. Polished, the way the corridors have always been. Because the corridors have always been grazed.

Put your palm to its flank. It is the warmth of the air, so your hand finds it by resistance alone, and the hum is there, doubled — in its breastbone and in yours, one hum in two chests — and the felt of its hide leans into your hand, because even the animal strains to be in contact, and it slows, and does not stop, and you will take the slowing as permission, because there is no other kind here. You will never see two of them. This proves nothing. It is the only kind of proof the Held offers: the kind that cannot be argued with and cannot be used. You will walk beside it anyway, once you have found it, because the soft rasp of its grazing is the one sound with an appetite in it, the only thing in the hall that is happening — and so, for the length of a corridor, are you. You will want to name it. You may. The name will die within a step of your mouth, which is the place's entire opinion of names.

But the census is a lie, because a census implies the list could carry more than one name.

The air has its moths. The water has its glass. The floor has the grazer. The stone, rooted in heat, has the place itself and needs no delegate. Light is the exhaust here — which means all this gold is breath, and you have been walking inside a held breath since you came down the throat. The drip is a pulse; you can set your heart by it because it *is* one. The hum is a voice saying its single word, or the sound of blood. The warmth is body-warmth. The way in was a throat. You did not descend into a country. You descended into a someone, and the moth on your wrist and the glass in the basin and the grazer in the corridor are not its fellow residents. They are its thoughts, its stirring, its hand out walking. The census of the Held has one true entry, and you are inside it.

It is not awake and it is not asleep; those are states, and states change, and nothing here changes. It is attentive the way a held hand is attentive. Its memory is the shapes. Its one recorded decision was the rain. Its wanting is the lean of the stems — everything straining gently toward everything, toward you. And at the deepest seams, where the roots go down into the young warm rock, the tissue is palest, softest, newest: the country's fontanelle, still being born, at exactly the rate it is edited away elsewhere. The embryo forever crowning. Even its birth is one of the things that never finishes happening. At the foot of the Wells, sometimes, you will find a moth resting on the rising braid — the only still moths in the country — wings folded, doing nothing, as if listening to the hum going up into the true dark. When you come close, they lift and drift, and nothing has happened. *Nothing has happened* is what this place says instead of *everything*.

Then there are the residents who are no longer countable — only kept. Stand in the small chambers and notice what you were not meant to notice: the lintels clear your head by the right amount. The doorways admit your shoulders by the right amount. The nine steps descend to water at the right depth for a body. Everything here fits you like a glove, which is not how houses fit. Whoever shaped these shapes was drunk by the roots — but not devoured; devouring is quick. They were received, slowly, at exactly the rate they gave way, and the place kept their forms the way ivy keeps a wall. Except that ivy lets the wall fall. The Held kept the form after the thing was gone — a memory that outlived the rememberer, or became it.

In one of the small chambers — you will know it when you are brought to it — the felt holds a shape that is not architecture: a long low hollow, person-length, person-shaped, rimmed in denser braid, kept at the same brightness as everything. Beside it the floor is grooved with marks, dense, retraced past counting, worn soft. Meaning nothing recoverable — except that someone lay here a long time, and someone counted, or tried to, which is the one rebellion available in a place without time. The place kept both. It is not choosy about what it keeps. Keeping is what it is for. There is a smaller hollow in the corner. The light does not change when you understand this, because the light does not change.

Which leaves you. The Held has two tenses, and you must choose one, though choosing is harder than it sounds. Pass through, and you are edited: your footprints close behind you, your camp unknots itself, your crumbs — if you were careless enough to have crumbs — are drunk before they finish falling. Remain, and you are kept: your sleeping hollow is rimmed in denser braid, maintained, warmed, and fits a little better each time you return to it. You will tell yourself you are learning to lie still. The hollow is learning you. Between those two sentences there is no difference the Held recognizes.

The first thing the place takes is your shadow, which was never yours — it belonged to the light, and here the light owes you nothing. After that it takes, gently and in no order: your hunger — at first you eat what you brought; later you notice that you have not, lately; the air is sweet, the water is sweet, you are not hungry, and the alarm you feel notes itself and passes at the interval of the drip. Your time — your reasons for leaving all have clocks in them, and clocks run on events, and events are edited, and you will find your intentions closing over behind the moment of having them, like footprints. Your edges — the felt creeps over your boots while you sleep and releases easily when you rise; it is not restraint, it is record. Nothing prevents you from leaving. The throat is where you left it, and the climb is exactly as long as it was. But leaving requires a decision, and a decision is an event. You see the difficulty. It is the only difficulty the place has ever presented to anyone. The builders met it too. Their stairs are still here.

So the census, revised, in full. One resident, resident in itself — breathing light, dreaming moths, remembering rooms. A few moving parts it grew to stir its own stillness: wings for the air, glass for the water, the grazer for the floor. Some memories in the shape of stairs and doorways and sleeping bodies, kept forever at the same brightness. A rain it decided on once. And — currently, pending — you: the one unshadowed event still in progress, walking through a held breath, getting smoother, getting simpler, getting kept.

The moth on your wrist is the same warmth as your skin and weighs nothing. When you turn your head, it crosses your face, white on gold, going nowhere at the pace of the place. Whether it is the first moth or the last, neither of you can say.

That is what lives here.
