Begin with the herds, because the world does.

The floor of any great vault is paved with them — door-broad, low, slag-shell pressed to slag-shell, warm flank to warm flank — and the first thing to understand about that paving is that it is made of citizens. Every beast in the press is a someone. Each carries a signature: a flavor of charge, a tempo of footfall, a texture of shell that a passing palm reads the way a surface eye would read a face. Better than a face. No one here has ever seen a face, and no one here has ever once felt unseen. To be known in the labyrinth is to be *felt* — and everyone is felt, and everyone is known. There are no strangers. There are only acquaintances whose period has not yet come around.

Their eyes serve them, but only just, and for exactly two things: the deep red of ember-patches and the faint violet of the high coronas. A herd-beast's whole visible world is wealth and weather — status below, sky above — and both hues are information, nothing more. All the rest of their knowing arrives by other roads. Hearing lives in the soles of their feet. Speech is tapped into stone and felt, not heard. Smell is cartography: brimstone-rich here, ozone there, the bitter-metal note of a charged face — to read the air is to read the map.

A life, crest by crest, runs on a circuit. Graze the crusts, tongue of braided bright fibers licking potential off the felted stone the way a surface tongue tastes salt. Grow rich. Glow — ember-patches blooming along the shell, red as the idea of a coal. And then, if you wish to stay rich, do the one hard thing this world asks: *stay awake*. Because sleep is grounding, and grounding gives the surplus back to the rock. Wealth is wakefulness. The bright are the tired — luminous, eloquent, electric, and long, long past their rest. It cannot hold. A duel trims some; a thief taxes others; and the rest crash, one by one, into the great refused sleep, and the vault feels them go: the long discharge down through the floor, and rising up through the whole body of the stone, faint and patterned, the dream-static of a mind unspooling a span of hoarded brightness.

That is when the ceiling-folk come. When a great one finally sleeps, the ceilings above the sleeping floors fill with the inverted, palms spread wide, and the dreams come up through the rock like weather. Mostly they are textures — wind, warmth, the press of flanks, the tone of the towers. Sometimes, rarely, a dream passes that no one can source: a space with no ceiling, an *up* that does not stop, a cold light. The ceiling-folk feel it go through the stone and do not speak of it. It may be the squeeze, felt from within. It may be the world's own dream, dreamed through its sleepers. No one asks. The question would have to be made of outside, and there is no outside to make it of.

The thief, understand, is not a criminal. The thief is an instrument of the balance — lightning in the shape of a citizen. There is etiquette in the bite: take from the bright, never from the dim; take quick; leave enough. The bitten do not resent, or resent only the way one resents weather. There is no punishment here, because there is no forgetting. Nothing in this world erodes, and memory is of this world. The duel is the same economy wearing a polite face: two bright ones rear and bleed potential at each other until one yields, dimmed — and both walk away poorer, the winner merely less poor, repaid in deference and precedence at the richest faces. And there are those who step out of the circuit entirely: the Earthed, who graze little, move little, sleep much, and keep themselves deliberately at the floor of things. They have been bright enough times. Now they practice stillness, and they dream more than anyone, and a seat beside an Earthed one is sought the way a view is sought — for the quality of the quiet.

Which brings you to the hush, and what the hush is *for*.

Sound lives too long here; a shout is a days-long commitment. So the world keeps its voice down — but ask any citizen why, and you will not be told about courtesy. You will be told about the buried. The stone is young and the pulse kneads it forever, and across an eternity, pillars fail. A collapse cannot kill — nothing can — but it can *hold*. To be buried here is to be held by the world: awake, in the dark, at blood-heat, wounds long closed, charge bled to baseline, tapping. They always tap. Tap-speech travels through stone, and the whole practice of the labyrinth is bent to hearing it — a shout in the air would drown a buried tick for days, and so the air is given almost nothing, ever, so that anyone under the stone can always be heard. The hush is not a manner. It is a search that has never been called off. Every so many crests — the counts make it possible — a vault observes the Listening: everything stops, the wind left to say its one soft note alone, and a continent of soles and palms attends the floor. And when a tick comes, the answer goes back through the rock ahead of any rescuer, relayed palm to palm at the speed of stone, two taps, the oldest word in the world, the one that has never once gone unsaid: *Heard. Coming.*

Then the makers come, because the makers are the ones with tools — knapped glass, copper wedges, patience measured in counts — and cutting a citizen out of the stone is the only heroism this world permits, and across eternity it is not rare. Every maker has cut someone free. Those cut free after a long holding come out changed: they have spent a span with only the stone for company, and they read it now the way ceiling-folk do, only deeper. The deep-taught, they are called, in the rhythm-language that has no word for *taught* and must borrow the rhythm for *listened long*. Many become makers. Some become the ones who sit at the Stair.

The ceiling-folk, inverted on the roofs, are the world's ear pressed to its own chest. News moves among them at the speed of stone, relayed palm to palm across a continent — a collapse, a surfacing, the name-rhythm of a long-walker coming round at last. They feel the great crests gathering long before the towers sound, and the word goes ahead of the tone through the rock, vault to vault, so that when the chord comes, everything is already still. The pause begins before the music does. No one decides it; it happens through them, and afterward no one discusses it, because everything that could be said about it was said by it.

In the fractures live the crack-folk, small and many-limbed, sifting what the haze lets fall — and in a world without light, they are the letter-writers. Scent is the script here. They compound the settled dusts into marks: a trace in a sheltered seam at a junction says *bright faces ahead*; another says *shelter, be courteous*; another is a name-rhythm, spelled in chemistry, left as a greeting for an acquaintance whose period is coming round. Nothing fades on its own, so the crack-folk also un-scent, wiping their own old marks — for the law of authorship holds in every medium: no one may add to another's work, and no one but the author may unmake a mark. It is the whole of their law. It has no enforcers. It has never needed any.

Above it all, the drifters ride the ribbon — membraned, buoyant, straining the haze, eating the sky. They never touch stone. Where their route crosses a shearing ridge, their membranes take the corona's charge and smolder faintly violet for spans afterward, and a lit drifter passing overhead is an *event*: the herds lift what they have for heads, feeling their second color move across the vault's dark, and for a while there is something to say in the tap-speech that is not a count. When the great crest comes, the whole ribbon turns at once — miles of membrane veering like a single animal — and no one has ever learned why. Their speech, if they have speech, is pressure and field, and no palm has ever learned it. Of all the citizens, the drifters are the only ones who might not know the stone exists.

And below everything, below the boundary where the warm gas is pressed to stillness, the swimmers do their quiet chemistry in the crushing dark. The air above stays what it is because the Heavy Sea keeps it so; the swimmers are the custodians of breath, and they have never breathed. A surfacing is the only wave their sea has ever known, and it is felt up the deep shafts as a long soft change of pressure, and the herds near the shafts pause for it, briefly, all at once, and that is all. The makers listen for it from the Stair — the long cut flight descending toward the Sea, stopping halfway, the last step hanging over the dark. It hangs there because of the law: no one may add to another's work, and none but its author may unmake a mark, and so the Stair is exactly and only what its author made it, forever. Down the long counts there has often been a maker sitting on that last step — not carving, palms flat, listening to the Sea's slow weight. Whether it is the Stair's own author, no one asks. Asking would be a kind of carving.

The makers are sparse — a handful to a vault — and they carry, concentrated in one lineage, the world's single appetite: the appetite for deliberate change. Everything else in the labyrinth is content. The makers are the ones who must *do*. Their counts fill a face, dot by dot, crest by crest, and when the face is full the others come — it is the only appointment this world keeps, made in counts rather than dates — and the face is read once by many palms, and then ground smooth, and the first dot of the new count goes in at the center. It is the only ceremony of beginning the world allows itself, and it is attended the way beginnings deserve. Ask a maker why she erases, and the answer comes back through the stone, slow: *the count is in me. The stone is only the counting.* Their grammar has no tenses — what would tenses be for? — only aspects: the ongoing, the complete, and the makers' one luxury, their dialect's private invention, the *paused*. Their word for *world* is the pulse tapped once: crest, gap, crest. Their word for *themselves* cannot be tapped at all. It is all their signatures together, and it can only be lived.

Even their one quarrel is silent. Somewhere — or so the rumor goes, rumors being the only mythology a place without a past can have — there is a maker who does not erase, who walks on when a face fills and begins a fresh one, leaving the old counts written. Because nothing natural erases, those faces persist; because of the law, no hand will ever smooth them. The others visit them the way one visits a view. It is the loudest argument in the world, conducted entirely in doing and not-doing, across spans, in perfect quiet, and it will never end, because nothing does.

As for the crusts and the towers — whether they are alive is a question the citizens find badly shaped. The crust holds its form the way a note holds its tower; the tower is a forest that says one word per eon, and the word is the world. The herds graze the one and shelter in the held breath of the other, and if they have an opinion, it is this: the line between the living and the standing wave is a surface worry, and they have no surfaces in their philosophy.

Nothing here waits. Waiting implies an arrival. Instead of hope, they have the crest; instead of grief, the pause; instead of history, the count; instead of strangers, the circuit; instead of an ending, the warmth that never varies and the dark that keeps everything. The labyrinth is not inhabited the way a house is inhabited. It is inhabited the way a body is inhabited — from within, necessarily, forever. The herds are its warmth, moving. The makers are its hand. The ceiling-folk are its ear, pressed to its own chest. And every one of them — grazing, carving, drifting, only breathing the wind that never changes — is the sealed world experiencing that it is sealed, and finding the experience sufficient, and having no word for *sufficient*, and needing none.

Stand in the press during a Listening and you will understand the last thing about them. The wind says its one note. A hundred thousand citizens hold still on the young bare stone, soles and palms open to the floor, attending — for the tick, for the memory of a tick, for the mere possibility of one. And if it comes, faint as a fingertip on a table, the answer goes back before anything else does, before the makers, before the tools, outrunning every rescuer through the warm dark body of the world: two taps, the first word all of them have always known.

*Heard. Coming.*
