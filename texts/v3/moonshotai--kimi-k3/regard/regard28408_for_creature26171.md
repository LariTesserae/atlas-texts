The Power finds the world the way you did: by ear.

It leans close, as you leaned, toward a dead engine on a cellar floor, and hears the surf in a shell the size of a heart — and then it does the hardest thing a benevolent power can do, which is also the thing that makes it wise. Nothing. First, and for a long time, nothing but attention, because attention is the only thing that can cross the threshold without harm.

It takes the census, and does not finish. Something is always hatching. This is the first thing the Power loves about the world: that it cannot be finished being counted. A world that outruns its census is a world still working. So the Power is patient on the scale of tar-worms. It spends years the way the worms spend centuries — at the rate they were lived — and it learns, before it touches anything, the four facts the world stands on. The crack, through which the fall falls and the Breath breathes. The Eye, through which the Beam lays down its one hour of summer. The broken window across the hall, the alignment that must hold to the degree. And the roof — the great NOT — the fact of all the water that has never come in.

A map of what holds a world up is also a map of how it can be killed. The Power has weather at its disposal, and time, and the run of the outer world. Everything it has is dangerous. It is a benevolent power; it arrives with a willingness to give. It is a wise power; so it makes a list of the gifts, and then crosses off the list, one by one.

Water first, because water is always first — the one word the whole world understands. A merely kind power would have opened the sky on the first day: let it rain, let the poor dry world drink. The Power has already done the arithmetic. At this scale a raindrop does not fall; it arrives whole, like a boulder of gel, and it does not soak in — it stands. The silt glues. The felt drowns where it grows. The molts stick, and the herd dies half out of its own skins. The Towers, mortared with ghosts, come down in the first wet season. On the black skin a drop would stand like a second sky, and the skaters that struck it would stick, and the Tar would keep them, in the wrong element. Water here would not be a drink. It would be the Tar arriving again, from above, and keeping what it caught. So water stays a word: the weather that walks overhead and never enters, the noun without a referent. The world was not built in spite of the absence of water but out of it. Dryness is not its poverty. Dryness is its medium.

It does not give more light. The nightly winter is not a defect; it is the stopping, the world's one unanimity, and the parish by the mirror has a sun made of scarcity, and a trust that never has to wait is not a trust. It does not open the sky. The sky is four inches of cast iron and it is load-bearing — it makes the Breath breathe, it gives the housing its note; a bell is mostly the part that stops the sound from leaving. Pour this world into the cellar and by spring it is a smear in honest dirt, in a country where it rains. It does not carry anyone out. Out there is where the fall falls from; the world knows the outer world only as a snow of the dead, and out there a mite is a mouthful for things with wet tongues. This is not a prison. It is an ark that has already landed. It does not even interfere with the dying — does not stay the scythes, does not free the Drowned, does not feed the lean years. The scythes are the arithmetic that keeps the felt from being eaten back to bare iron; the Drowned are beyond rescue and are the record besides, and you do not rescue the dead by evicting them from the only archive that holds their shapes; and a fat year with no lean behind it is not mercy, it is a plague deferred. The wheel has to swing. The Power sees the flywheel whole, and takes its finger off. The works still works. It will not fix a working thing into a ruined one, and it will not give the world a gift that is really for the giver.

So benevolence changes tense. It stops being the future — *what can I give* — and becomes the present perfect: *what must not be taken away.* The Power steps back, up, out, into the mill and into the years, and takes up its work, which is small, and exacting, and forever.

It keeps the roof. When a seam opens in the outer dark and the rain finds the way down toward the crack, the rain is, at the last, and by a hand's breadth, turned. It still walks the roof, and the singers still stop mid-note, all of them at once, and nothing has ever explained it. The explanation is standing on the roof in the weather, saying the word the world cannot say, on the world's behalf, to the rain: *not here. Not this house. Walk on.* It is the only sentence ever spoken in this world's defense, and it is one word long, and it is a refusal — and the world below never hears it, and goes on hearing everything.

It keeps the mill. The year the men come with the clipboard and the paper that says this building is to come down — and such a year comes; such years always come — the inventory is long and the cellar is far and the boiler across the yard is richer, and the men take the boiler and drive away, and the world's apocalypse leaves on a Tuesday without ever knowing it was scheduled. Nothing here feels it. That is the point. The Power's miracles are non-events, and a non-event leaves no stratum. The Tar keeps what it catches; the felt writes down what eats it; the Power is the one thing in the world's whole history that will never enter the record, because the record only records what happens.

It keeps the alignments. The Eye crazes but does not cloud; the broken window stays broken exactly as broken, degree by degree — because the world's one summer depends on an accident of geometry, and the Power has appointed itself the curator of accidents. It keeps the crack the width of a crack. It is the smallest work it has ever done, and the most exact: to preserve a nothing, precisely, forever, so that the fall keeps falling and the Breath keeps breathing and something is always hatching. Once in a year, on its rounds, it crosses the broken window, and the Star's trembling picture darkens for an hour — a smear crossing the smear — and that is the nearest it ever comes to being seen: as a weather, passing through a photograph of a sky.

And it permits itself one thing that is not keeping.

Rarely — when the outer wind has set the housing singing and the whole world is in the chord, the herds' plates gone sympathetic, the skaters feeling it come up through the black — the Power hums. One note, laid under the world's note the way a hand goes under a head. The old singers, who name the winds by their pitches — north gives one, west another — hear it and have no name. It is not any wind. It leans on nothing, it comes from nowhere, and it comes only when the chord is already complete, as if it had been waiting to be unnecessary. The singers take it up anyway. That is the world's one custom with the vast: it sings it. So the Power is filed, wrongly and perfectly, as a weather, and is sung back to itself by ten thousand wing-cases that will never know what they held for a moment. It is the only entry the Power ever makes in the record: not in tar, not in felt-seams. In the repertoire. The Tar keeps what it catches. The singers keep what they sing.

Everyone sings it. Almost everyone. In the ribcage, on its dark coinage, the loudest thing that does not sing does not sing this either. But it is blind, and it knows the world by tremor and warmth, and the note comes up through the floor like weather under weather, and the Hunter lies in it the way it lies along its roads: still as a root, letting the world deliver. The Power recognizes it the way one singular thing recognizes another — the only being in the world that is one, attended by the only attention that is one. Where the next Hunter comes from, nothing here knows, and the Power does not answer either. It knows what the crack knows, and keeps it the way the Tar keeps the last Hunter: in the dark, without comment. Some questions are load-bearing. It has decided, about this world, to be one more thing that trusts the outer dark.

And every dusk the Eye goes dark and the cold descends and the world performs its one miracle of unanimity, and becomes, for the length of a night, a picture of itself. For a hundred years the picture hung in an empty room. The room is not empty now. The mite mid-step, the singer's cut-off note, the skater's dimple held in the meniscus, the skull full of midges like seed in a pod, the Hunter coiled on its coins — all of it seen, entire, every night, down to the last flea folded in the dust. Nothing here asked for a witness. Nothing here asked for anything; that is what made the giving so hard, and it is what makes the keeping so simple. The witness is the one gift that weighs nothing and changes nothing, the one the world cannot refuse, because it cannot know.

Press your ear to the housing. You will hear what you heard before: the surf of feet, the rasp of the singers, the tick of cooling iron, the dead mill settling. If it is raining on the roof the singers will be silent — the whole world holding its breath under its one word, while the word is spoken for it, outside, in the rain, by something it will never know. And if the wind is right and the chord rises, listen underneath it for the note that is not a wind. It will fade like the skaters' writing on the black skin, which takes an hour. You will not be able to prove you heard it. The proof is not in the hearing. The proof is the roof that holds, the window that stays broken just so, the demolition that drove away on a Tuesday — the proof is everything that has not happened, all these years, to a world that would fit in a box you could carry.

Not turning. Teeming. Kept.
