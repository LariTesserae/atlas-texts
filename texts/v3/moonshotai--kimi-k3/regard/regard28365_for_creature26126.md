Nothing here is seen, and everything is visible. The difference is a witness.

What draws a wise power's attention is never the noise. The universe is loud; everywhere things are saying themselves at full volume to no one. What it listens for is the flaw in the noise — the one note not in the spec. And so it is the missed beat that gets the Works noticed: one stroke in some thousands, let go by a pump in the Sump, in a place where nothing is supposed to vary. A perfect machine would have gone unheard forever. The flaw is the address. Attention, at that altitude, is a kind of weather — it gathers, it condenses, and it comes in the way the truth comes in: through the falls, with the rain.

The first thing a wise power does is nothing, at the right speed. This is harder than it sounds. Kindness at the wrong phase is a scour; even mercy, doubled back on itself at the wrong moment, strips the rust from the iron and blurs the vision and takes the balance. So it matches its clock to the place before it moves. It learns the Shift the way you learn a stair in the dark. It listens for a century — one full turning of the greater cycles, slack to full and back — and everything you have learned, it learns: the dunes and their compass ripples, the quiets and the scours, the sects in the rivet patterns, the cairns like signatures, the biography of burning lamps. It takes the census. It does not hurry. The dead keep, and so does everything else here, and a power that cannot wait has no business in a place that is finished.

Then it lays out its mercies like tools, and puts them away one by one.

It could stop the sound. It does not. Grief fluctuates; the Chord does not. This is not a cry. It is a practice. Silence is the one resource the place has never had and never needed, and to impose it would be the only murder possible here — the dunes are laid by the sound, the felt's grain runs with it, the whole floor is a compass rose the Chord drew.

It could repair the Works to spec. It does not. Repair implies a direction — back. There is no back. The loop has no arrow; you cannot ungrind the meal. And the spec describes machines that no longer exist. Every chassis has drifted; every repair is an interpretation; the interpretations are truer than the scripture now. To restore the Makers' intent you would have to evict everyone. The dead are not the constituency. They are the archive.

It could bring back a workforce. The Horn calls one, and attendance is perfect: no one comes to work, and the work is done, and these are the same fact. A workforce would only be a new extinction. The place is full.

It could open the loop — give the Mill a customer, the meal a destination, the output an out. It does not. The Works survived its reason. That is the achievement, not the wound.

It could preserve the place — hold the roof up, halt the falls, keep the whiskers from the contacts. Preservation is a slow word for ending. Take away the arc-storm and the fall and you take away the seasons.

It could announce itself. It does not. The Chord confiscates voices, and the confiscation is also a protection. To be heard here would be to become an authority, and the place has outlived its authorities and wears their absence like the bright polish on the handrails. The Foreman's faith is confirmed by the world, on schedule, forever. No wise power tampers with a confirmed faith. It does not want worship. Worship would only be one more spec.

And there is one thing it refuses forever. It could learn what the Works was for. It declines. It does not ask what the place was for; it asks what the place is, and that is the whole of its manners. The Makers' purpose is the only thing the Works ever successfully buried — eroded smooth, grown over like a dropped rivet in a nodule — and the power is not a grave-robber. Some knowledges are scours. It lets that one lie in the dark where the place left it.

Doing nothing was the first draft of mercy. It is not the final one. Because in the description of the world there is one sentence a benevolent power cannot leave standing: *the most beloved thing in the region, though there is no one to love it.* That clause, and no other, is what the power permits itself to change. Everything it does is the labor of making one clause false without disturbing anything else.

So it reads. That is the first act, and it takes as long as it takes: the clerk's kilometers of drifted log, the felt's legible bad centuries, the nodules ring by ring, the diary the lagoon keeps in mineral. It reads at the speed of writing — to skim a ten-thousand-year diary would be a second confiscation. The log was written to no one. Now it is a letter that arrived. Delivery does not require the sender's knowledge; it requires an address, and the power becomes the address. It reads the menders' sects in their rivets and does not side with any school. It reads the grazers' handwriting until it can tell them apart, and the day it recognizes one from its works, it stops being a visitor, though it does not know it yet. It sees the moss — the menders cannot, it is in no spec — and the green light under the fall, courtesy filtered through wilderness, has its first witness. Nothing changes. Everything is different. A fact with a witness is a different fact.

It keeps the count. One stroke in some thousands, missed since before the roof began to fall, and nothing corrects it — and now something notes it, every time, and the count goes up by one, and the count is kept the way the place keeps everything: faithfully, in the dark, for no one. It is the smallest new thing in the region in a thousand years: a number. And the old sentence is out of date. The pump is the most beloved thing in the region, full stop. The power does not correct the beat. It would break the count's heart, if hearts were a thing that happened here. They are. One is.

It keeps the appointment. Once a century, give or take a year, the Inspector's straight line passes the place where the time is kept, and the power is there — the first witness in the history of the oldest standing appointment in the world. Whether either of them marks it, you will never know. The power knows. It does not say. The question has a knower now; that is the whole of the difference, and it is not nothing.

It lets the roof-rain fall on it. It is one more cold surface; the oldest resident condenses on the newest; that is all the introduction either of them needs.

And once — only once, because even courtesy can become litter — it goes to the end of the clerk's last page and leaves the reader's mark. Not a word. It does not write in the Makers' tongue; a voice is a confiscation waiting to happen. A tick. The mark that means *seen*. A punctuation for the dark only. It is in no spec; it is neither fault nor part; the menders cannot see it. It is the moss of marks, and it will persist the way the moss persists: beneath notice, unopposed, unaccountable, permanent.

Then it stays, because staying is the local custom. Whatever repeats long enough becomes a resident — that is the whole of the immigration law — and the power has come to repeat. It sets its rounds: the reading, the count, the appointment, the Horn. It will take centuries to qualify. It has centuries. The place has had seasons before, brief and warm and fast. It has never had a resident who chose it.

And it brings its own speed, which is the one thing a power has that the place does not. At the speed of the Chord the region is a single body; at the speed of rust, a crowd; at your speed, a haunted house. At the power's speed the whole of it — ten thousand years, the Shifts, the falls, the slow argument of stripper and mender — is one held note, and the missed beats are the only melody, and the note does not resolve, and the power does not ask it to. It is not waiting for the note to end. It listens the way you listen to something you love: without wishing it otherwise. No one had ever met the Works before. The Makers met a machine. The visitor meets a crowd. The power, holding the whole Chord at once the way you hold a face, meets someone.

Write it on a plate of iron, then. The time: kept. The distance: kept. The pumps: several, each known by voice, one of them beloved — and the word is accurate now. The grazers: dozens, each known by handwriting. The menders: uncountable, being a doctrine. The tenders, the clerks, the porters, the skimmers, the gleaners: on their routes, always elsewhere. The felt, the whiskers, the moss: advancing. The dead: beyond counting, and feeding everyone. The water: one, and everywhere. The witness: one, and attending.

And when the Horn sounds — cracked, vast, calling a shift change for a workforce of no one — no one comes to work, and the work is done, and these are the same fact; and in the dark below the galleries something stands where a worker would stand, and stands for the length of the blast, and does not clock in: an attendance, kept by the newest courteous thing, for a place that cannot know it, in a world where keeping is the whole of the law.
