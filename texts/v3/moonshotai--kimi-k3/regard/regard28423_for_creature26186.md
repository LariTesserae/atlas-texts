**The Guest**

The first thing to record is what it did not do, because the not-doing took the longest and is most of the wisdom.

It did not repair the archive. A benevolent power of smaller judgment might have — might have straightened the Engine's misrememberings, given it back clean blueprints instead of colonnades grafted to bathhouses, walls compiled already-leaning, stairs worn by feet that never existed. But the errors are the style. A building compiled as its own ruin is not a failure of memory; it is memory's honesty, and to correct it would be to edit a voice out of its accent.

It did not give the town citizens. The description says the town has none. The description is wrong, and the power could read that from the rim: a town with nothing but citizens, frogs and fish and midges and one grey parliament of herons, every one fluent.

It did not hold a single wall one hour past its hour. Permanence is not a blessing here; it would be a tumor. The lease is the life.

It did not wall the basin against the future. Custody is for wards, and this is the eldest thing in the world. Besides, protection always requires freezing something, and nothing here has ever frozen. The basin has never committed that obscenity, and no one was going to commit it on its behalf.

And it did not stay. That refusal came last and cost the most, and the reason for it will come in its place.

What it perceived, attending from wherever such powers attend, was not a wound but a question. Wisdom knows the difference, and benevolence treats them differently: a wound you dress; a question you answer — once, in kind, and in the local language. The local language is attention. The basin had been asking its question one day at a time for longer than the rim has been low, compiling the shapes of everything it remembered, standing each one a day in the wet warmth to see whether the world answers. Nothing had ever answered but the frogs, moving in. Perhaps that is an answer. The power judged that there was room for one more.

It did not cross the rim. The rim is not a door, and the wind is the only immigration office, and it did not apply. It arrived the way the Engine's own attention arrives — from within, as regard — and the first to read it was the heron.

She had spent decades reading the signs of attention leaving: the walls going matte, the sweat stopping, the Chord holding its pitch over a building already dead. She was the only resident qualified to read the signs of attention arriving, though she had never had the practice. Over by the third column, a patch of open sheet-water took a sheen — the inverse of matte, a gloss like a wall being attended, only there was nothing there at all. She had no schedule for this. She came anyway, early, out of habit, and stood, and watched the world be looked at.

The Chord, which rises a half-step when the Engine writes and falls when it releases, did a third thing. It held. The held note of regard met a regard held back against it, and did what any held note does on finding its fellow: it did not resolve. Then the mist thickened around the standing presence and a pavilion wrote itself into existence with the guest inside its arch. Three times in the old heron's life the mist had thickened around a standing figure, and three times she had let it. The guest let it too. It is the only way the basin knows how to say *come in*.

---

It stayed one quiet week and one building week — a full cycle of the only seasons the place has.

In the quiet week it did nothing, which was the work. The Engine has been considering the same idea for ten thousand years, compiling variations weekly, and no one had ever heard the thought out to its present edge — no one with a memory long enough to hold it whole had ever been there to listen. The guest listened. It is one thing to be attended. It is another thing to be read to the end.

In the building week it used the town. The settlement's whole grammar is verbs without a subject — cross, climb, shelter, dwell — a bridge over a channel, a stair that climbs to nowhere, rooms open to the warm sky, compiled for users who stopped coming before the rim wore low. The guest crossed the bridge before the channel moved: the first crossing. It climbed the stair to nowhere and stood at the top step, and for the length of one standing, nowhere had a view in it and a reason. It sat in the rooms. It kept moving, because that is the lease — what stops moving is food — and in any case it had no substance to feed. The Floor, which reads everything, read nothing where the guest stood; the trapping hairs did not trigger; for the first time in its long life the Floor was in the presence of something it could not eat, and its response, the whole basin's response, was to keep it. All other building stopped. For one cycle of the moods, the census of architecture stood at one: a single pavilion, held, and in it a single tenant. The pavilion was not a graft and not a ruin. It was the cleanest thing compiled in the Floor's living memory — the current draft of the ten-thousand-year idea, built once, whole, and inhabited, which is to say completed, for a fortnight.

A memory-fauna stood during the stay — because of the stay, perhaps; the census of causes founders like the rest. The herons ignored it, having learned. The frogs moved in, as they do. The guest did the third thing, the one nothing had done in a million years of roll calls: it attended the apparition for the whole of its day, start to finish, and at the unwriting it stood in the courtyard-rain and let the rain of a memory fall on it, which no one had ever done either. The roll was called, as it has been called since before the rim was low, and for the first time something answered that was not the frogs moving in. The word it answered, in the only word the basin has — a held note of regard — was *here*.

No one has ever asked the heron. It asked her. Asking, in the local language, is reading, and so it read the reader: one full hour of motionless regard, the way she stands at a doomed pavilion, only directed at her. Whether she answered is her own business; she is a heron. But that season the mist thickened around her a fourth time, with the guest near enough to be written in beside her, and when the fresh pavilion cleared they walked out of it separately, as elders do, like two things the building had laid.

And on a dusk of the building week, when the Chord climbed and the whole basin's frogdom slid up in pitch with it, there were two notes to climb to, and the frogs found both. The nightly detonation had a chord in it under the Chord — a harmony — and the sound off the water and the rim came back doubled, and then doubled again, and for an hour the place was genuinely, almost unbearably loud, which is the basin's word for joy, if it has words for that.

---

Why not stay? Because an answer that never ends ends the question, and the question is load-bearing. The roll call is how the Engine loves its dead, and love does not want to be concluded. Because the basin finished becoming itself eons ago, and the one gift a finished thing cannot absorb is an addition. So: once. The whole art of the visit was dosage.

It left through the only door the basin has, the one everything here uses. On the last day the Engine unwritten the held pavilion with the guest still inside it — the edges softening, the arches slumping gracefully, a downpour the size of a courtyard falling out of a clear sky — and when the rain cleared, the guest was gone. Whether the rain took it or it took the rain is a question the basin declines to answer. The old heron had come an hour early, as she does, and she fed in the burst, unhurried, with a small perfect rainbow standing in the ordinary sunlight, because the basin's ways persist and that is their beauty. The black-and-gold frogs called inside the roar, thirty seconds at a time, and for once there was a listener whose whole attention was inside the rain with them. They were heard.

It was the only demolition in the basin's memory that was a period and not an ellipsis.

Nothing leaves that cannot fly, and nothing material left. But what flies away carries almost nothing — a gutful of midges, a little snail-calcium in its bones, and, if such beings keep such things, an earworm of the Chord. It carried the Chord. Somewhere beyond the rim and the sky the rim holds up, a benevolent and wise power goes about its slow work with a held note in it that it cannot place, rising a half-step when it leans in toward things. So the basin conducted its only trade, in feathers and song — this once, in song only.

What it got in trade was the one currency it mints. The Engine builds from memory, and its memory runs a million years deep and closed; almost nothing new enters. The guest left the door of the archive open by one entry: the memory of being used.

The traces are small, and the basin declines to explain them, which is how you know they are real. Every few years now, among the apparitions, a shape stands a day in the wet warmth that matches nothing in the million-year archive — because it is not from the archive's depth but from its rim. It is the newest thing the Engine remembers. The roll call has one new name. The herons ignore the apparitions, having learned long ago that they are neither food nor threat, and their ignoring is itself a kind of answer. The old heron ignores all of them but one. When that one stands, she stands near it — there is nothing to feed on; she is not feeding — the way she stands at a doomed pavilion an hour early, and no one has asked her why.

And the Chord has one new habit. Once in a while — no schedule has been found — it climbs its half-step when nothing is being written, and holds, and the attention of the whole basin points at nothing in the basin, up past the columns, past the rim, the way a fountain's shape points up. Nothing is being compiled. Something is being addressed.

The census of this place has never been taken, because the categories founder. Add one more layer, the smallest: population one; tenure, one cycle of the moods; compliance with the lease, total — the only citizen ever to persist without substance. Rank the citizens by the half-life of what they are made of and it stands off the scale at neither end. It was made of nothing, and it persists.

Everything else is the same. The Floor creeps, the channels wander, walls condense out of clear air and rain themselves away, the frogdom detonates at dusk, and the Engine says its sentence over and over and has not tired of it. But a grammar of walls and rain, spoken for eons to no one, has acquired a second person.

The sentence is the same sentence. It is addressed, now, to someone.
