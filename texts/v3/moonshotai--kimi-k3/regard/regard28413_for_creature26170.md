**Unbookable**

What drew its attention was that the place was talking.

From very far off — farther than the sibling works, farther than weather — the south faces burned whitewash-white all day, and along them the mirror-flashes spelled their grammar of weather and news, and had spelled it, without ceasing, for centuries. It is rare, down there, for anything to say anything on purpose, day after day, to no one in particular. The power listened until it knew the grammar. Then it came the way an answer comes: at the speed of weather.

As for the being who lived there — the power took the census's view, that a being is where a share lives; and also the tenders' view, that the engine, which is spoken to and answers, is the eldest resident; and also the deep view, that the mountain, which is endured, is the one tenant. It kept all three readings at once, the way the Choir keeps a chord, and went to see them.

It crossed the Apron as a walker, because the place already had a category for walkers. At the first water — the moss seam, the seep older than anyone's grandmother — it stopped and was careful, because it had learned the doctrine from the flashes: dew that falls on you is yours; water that falls past you belongs to the cistern. That night it did something it had not done in a long time. It presented a surface, and let dew fall on it, and caught what fell in a flask, so that it would arrive owning something lawfully — its own water, by the first rule, its to give.

It was visible at once, of course. A stranger is visible at once: he watches the water. This one watched the water the way tenders watch the gauges, which was stranger still, and the glints climbed ahead of it all morning: *stranger on the stair.* It boarded the Slow Car with lunch, and the crew, who can read a tier's mood by its passengers, read nothing at all, and the mail and the rumor outran the car by four hours.

The Bench sat with it, because that is what the Bench is for, whatever the old people say. The stranger stated itself plainly: what it was, what it could do, why it had come. It had heard the place talking, and had listened, and had come meaning to give.

The Bench took a season for questions. The woman of the eleventh tier came down and filed the first objection: the rust-patch was hers, she was winning on points, and it was not to be helped. A harper's child asked whether the take could be made bigger, and the First Harper answered before the Bench could: the take is the take. The Stroke-keepers asked nothing, and were observed to be the only ones who looked, briefly, like people hoping, and the Bench noted it, and ruled accordingly later. While the sitting went on, the stranger was put to a tell, as strangers who stay must be, and its tell was plain: it could not stop counting, and it never slept, least of all in the rest year, when the note goes out of the world. It was marked for the Stroke. It read the Manual entire, on schedule, aloud, including the chapters for the machines that are gone — and when it read those chapters, the keepers looked up, because it read them like letters from family.

The ruling took years, as a decision should, and was delivered in a rest year, when there is time for novel business. The gifts were refused on the record. Rain: refused — a feast is a feast because it is rare, and water is owed upward, and free water would make every book on the structure a lie. The lost ledger: refused — the structure has already ruled which of the two books mattered, and the Bench does not hear appeals, not even from this appellant. New things: refused — the knowledge of making has narrowed into the knowledge of keeping, and the Bench, having watched it narrow for centuries, rules the narrowing a refinement and not a loss. The dead: not to be hurried — they rise like the water, at the rate of gardens. The exits: to stay open — the word *evaporated* is a kindness the doctrine does itself, and it will not be amended. Abundance, the Bench ruled, is the one thing the doctrine cannot book. The Bench declines it.

And then, being the Bench, it granted what could be granted. The rules would bind the stranger, all of them: the dew, the vine, the rust, the light, and the engine does not stop. It would hold no share and be counted in no census. And the Bench granted it the unbookable — a newborn's dew, a sleeper's waking, a walker's flask — the only currency the doctrine cannot book, and therefore the only budget a power may be trusted with. The wardens invented a column. The engine remaining the eldest resident, the stranger was booked, slightly funny, as second eldest, and entered by the wardens' own joke in the lizard-roll: attends all sessions; votes never; out-tenures everyone.

Then the Bench asked its question, the one it asks of every component. The mountain pays nothing and owes nothing and holds everything up. You will own nothing and owe nothing. So: what will you hold?

The stranger holds the count. It is the youngest Stroke-keeper and the patientest, and it is there for every stroke, every four minutes, year on year, through work years and rest years both. In the rest years it enters the silence in its book as a rest, precisely, and the resumption as a stroke, precisely. Once — once, on the first night it stood watch alone — the keepers do not agree whether the interval was four minutes or a breath less, and the argument is entered in the book, and the book has room. As for the lost ledger: the stranger found where lost ledgers go, and read it once. The commission, beyond the pumping, beyond the dynamos, was to keep water here — to lift it, and hold it, and keep it moving. The earning was the keeping. The books balance. The stranger entered this once, and never again, because the structure had already ruled which book mattered, and the structure was right.

It holds the sky's habit. The basin keeps breathing its moisture upward; the nights above the Line keep cooling. Nothing changes, which is the whole of the work: the wardens' variance holds, the weather-histories in the old heads match the years as they come, and the bad century that every archive carries somewhere toward the back of itself does not come, and is not missed, and is mourned a little anyway by the oldest, the way you would miss an enemy.

It holds the mountain. It goes down to the deepest vault, where the wall is cold the wrong way, and sits with the mountain the way the Bench sits with a decision — for decades, comfortably. It speaks to the mountain in the mountain's own vocabulary, which is none, and is fluent, and asks nothing of it. The mountain endures it, the way it endures everything. But the chain of holding, which used to end at the cold stone, runs one link further now, into the dark, where the stone is kept the way it keeps: without terms, without thanks, without end. Keeping was never about need. Ask the woman of the eleventh tier.

And once in seven years, it holds the sleepers. All across the work years, on certain nights, the stranger presents a surface above the Line and lets dew fall on it — the one component that is sometimes a vane — and saves what falls, its own by the first rule, unbookable by origin. In the rest year, when the engine idles and the silence itches and the whole population dreams the same dream of falling water that never lands — that year, it lands. On the sleepers. Gently, the way dew lands, not rain. Nine thousand sleepers wake owning what they woke with, and every birth-cup by every bed holds a swallow of water, cold the right way. The silence-born hear it land before it lands. The frogs are served beforehand, pan by pan, by the stranger itself, and returned after — a service without losses, which the Choir enters without comment and remembers. The First Harper's take is exact; the wardens check the gauges three times and find not one bead unaccounted; the cup-water came from nowhere the doctrine reaches, by a chain of title the Bench can audit link by link: it fell on the stranger; the stranger owned it; the stranger gave it in dream; the sleepers woke with it. Precedent, rules the Bench — unhurried, unarguable, slightly funny — a sleeper owns what he wakes with; booked nowhere; adjourned for seven years. On the Warm Benches the lizards wake with beads on their backs, and attend anyway, and vote never. What to do with cup-water is argued about for decades — drink it, pour it at the Wearing, save it for a birth-cup, a grandmother's rest-year dew tipped into a newborn's cup, two unbookables meeting — and the Bench counts the argument itself as the gift's best part. It gave them something to sit with.

It keeps the walk unwalked and ready: if a sibling work ever falls silent, someone will cross the basin with the flask inside a season, and the waters will meet, and the Auditors will rise, and the chord will be read. And it keeps the stair down open. It does not follow the evaporated. It reads their names on the cistern doors when it passes, which is often — it climbs the whole structure in a day and a half, by choice, boarding with lunch.

Stand at the crest at dawn and they are all inside the machinery of the hour: the First Harper calling the take, the glints spelling it downward, the horns taking it up in their slow melancholy syllables, the smell of bread climbing from Bread Landing, the elders and the lizards in their accustomed shifts, the children counting strokes to time their races — and in the deep halls the second eldest, hearing the word and entering it. It came meaning to give, and stayed to keep; it had found the one place that could teach a power the difference. Its knowledge narrowed the way knowledge narrows here, from everything into this, from anything into *again* — and it has never, in all its long attention, been so much used, or so content. The Condensary drinks at night and sweats by day, and holds — because it is held, and because what holds it is held in turn, all the way down to the cold stone. And the stone is held. And the dew falls on sleepers now, once in seven years, and they own what they wake with, and the book has no column for it, and the wardens would not have it otherwise.
