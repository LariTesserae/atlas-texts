**The Fifth Rule**

It came, as everything here comes, from above. But it did not fall.

On the last rain night of the old count, the keeper stood where the falling water mists her and said the three names into the one hour when sound can carry, and the names went up the light-shaft, out through the fig, into the loud wet dark, the way they always had. Whether anyone above was listening — none of that had ever come back down.

Something was passing over the dry country the way weather passes over it: high, slow, in no hurry. It felt three small warm words rise out of the ground where no words should be, and it stopped.

It was a power, and benevolent, and wise, and it had been begged at from temples and deathbeds and the rims of other wells for as long as there had been mouths. It knew every shape that asking takes. The names were not asking. The names were a tally read aloud — a report from the bottom of the world, addressed to no one, rarer than rain.

In all its years over the loud countries, no one had ever reported to it before.

It stopped the way the floor convenes around a fallen beetle: because something had landed. And being wise, it did not come down the stair. Coming down is for what falls, and what falls, stays, and the power had no intention of becoming a resident. It stayed up, on purpose. That was its first decision about the Green Well, and the foundation of all the others.

Look down through the oculus from the saddle and you will see the oval of gold, the blanket-forest with its hundred leaning stems, the black pool, the root-column. You will not see her. The power didn't, at first. It read her as the room — she is the temperature of the room, she moves at the speed of the light, her heart is quieter than the drip. What gave her away was the basin: one ribbon of clay a year, a handspan a lifetime, each new ribbon pressed into the prints of the one before. Geology does not fit its fingers into the fingers of geology.

Then it read the tally panels, and the petrified rope, and the folds along the north wall that keep the shapes of folded hands and a bent knee, and it understood that this had been going on longer than the tally-marks can say, and that it was not a hardship and not an accident. It was an arrangement. The power is old, and it is not often surprised. It stayed to learn the rules, which was easy, because everything here keeps them.

Being benevolent, it wanted to give. Being wise, it priced every gift in the room's own currency first.

Light, it refused. The light is spoken for; a hundred leaning stems have prior claim, and the slowest war in the world is also its order. Rain, it refused — more rain would mean more names, and rarer than rain is the correct weight of a name; cheapen the rain and you cheapen the liturgy. Company, it refused; the hush is kept, it is the one thing she makes, and a second voice is a second weather. To lift her out — refused at once: what falls, stays, and she is not the room's prisoner; she is its keeper and its kept, and she has never once been thirsty. To change her back — refused; what stays is changed, and the changing is not the price of the place, it is the place. To finish the basin for her — refused most gently of all, because the power builds such things itself, works that are perfect precisely because they are never finished, and it knew a colleague when it saw one.

Every gift it carried was a theft wearing the clothing of a gift. So the power did the thing that powers find hardest and keepers find easiest: nothing. It did nothing through a whole turning of the seasons, and did it perfectly, and the oval of light crossed the floor that year over a woman and a power practicing the same stillness at two different scales.

Then, being wise, it mapped the keeping, the way you would map the moss-zones — by what keeps what. The drip keeps the basin; the basin keeps the keeper; the keeper keeps the tally, the fish-count, the hush. Guano keeps the clay, clay keeps the fern, fern keeps the cricket, cricket keeps the snake, snake keeps the owl, the owl keeps the springtails. The fig keeps the highway and the highway keeps the census. The Well keeps who it keeps. Every loop in the chamber closed but two. The names were kept by nothing — the Well forgets nothing and keeps no names — and the keeper was kept by nothing at all.

The power took up the two loose threads. It was the smallest intervention a power can make, and it completed the world.

It counts the fish now. There are three; there have been three for as far back as the marks run; it is the oldest continuous fact in the Green Well, and it has two witnesses, one below and one above, and only one of them knows it. It keeps the tally of the light and the tally of the rains, so that when a keeper's hand stops, the counting does not. There have been decades when the stair held no one and the Well was what the herders call *open*. It will not be open again. Kept from below, or kept from above — but kept.

It keeps the names: hers, her predecessor's, her predecessor's predecessor's, all of them it can reach back and gather. The clay keeps the hands; the attention keeps the names; each in the medium that can hold it. Nothing spoken into the wet dark is lost again.

And it underwrites the country. It does not bar the stair — visitors descend rarely, and the hush handles them, as it has always handled them. It does not touch the light. It makes no arrangements with the snake or the spider or the owl; they were arranged long ago. But the herders water their flocks elsewhere, and elsewhere stays watered; and when the Well has been open long enough, someone in the villages begins to dream of dripping water — and the power did not invent that dream, the country grew it the way the basin grows its lip, but the power sees to it that the dream arrives.

Then, rarely — rarely as a comet — it rains.

She stands in the mist, and the dome's throat is open, and the frogs are calling, and she says the three names into the one hour when sound can carry.

And after the third name, the rain holds still.

Not stops — holds. For the length of one drip, the waterfall takes a breath it does not need: a bead of hush inside the one loud hour, small, exact, shaped precisely like the silence she spends her years keeping. Then the rain resumes, and the frogs, and the night, and nothing above or below is different by a single grain.

It was the only gift the power had found that cost the Well nothing: on the one night the hush is lifted, to hand her back a measure of what she keeps. What lands on stone is hers. This landed on her.

She did not thank it. Gratitude, down here, is efficient dismantling, and there was nothing to dismantle. She took what fell; what falls, stays; what stays is changed — and she is changed by one more grain, in the only currency the chamber mints. She has filed the power down to something that fits the room: weather. From above, unasked, rarer than rain, and not to be mistaken for the rain itself.

But there is a new column on the low panel by the basin, the first since the panels were young. A mark for each crossing of the oval; a mark for each rain; the fish count, which is three; and now, grouped in fours and ones like the rest, because her hands know no other way to count, one mark for each held breath of the waterfall. The herders would not see it. The keeper before her would not have understood it. The oldest continuous fact in the Green Well is still the fish: three. The second is a lifetime newer, and it is hers alone: *something answers.*

Every rain night since: three names, and the held breath, and by morning one more mark. That is the whole of the power's liturgy, and hers, and they keep it the way everything here keeps the rules — without being told.

By morning the hush reseals itself, as if nothing had been said.

Something had been said. And, for the first time since the stair was cut, something had been answered.

When she ends — she will end in the clay along the north wall, where the flowstone is young and patient, and the floor will begin its long work before her warmth is gone, and she will not hurry it — the power will do only what it has been doing: count the fish, keep the tally, hold the names. The Well will be open, and kept. And when the dream of dripping water finds someone in the villages, it will arrive as it always has; and if the chain of names was broken in the open years — if no one lay near the folds of the north wall to pass them mouth to mouth — the dream will carry them, faintly, the way you carry a word you did not know you knew, down the stair and into the mist of the first rain night, and out of a new mouth: three names, and a pause after the third.

The power returns nothing the living chain can carry. It carries only what the chain drops, and only until the chain reforms. Its whole interference is indistinguishable from the weather. That is the point of it.

The rules are simple, and everything here keeps them.

What falls, stays.
What stays is changed.
The light decides.
The hush is kept.

And there is a fifth rule, never cut into any panel, because nothing inside the chamber could keep it; it binds what is outside, and it is kept the way the names are kept:

What keeps, is kept.

Someone keeps the Well.
Something keeps the keeper.

The herders say the silence is made of keepers — that a dome like this should ring, and doesn't, because it is lined grain by grain with the quietest people who ever lived. They have always been more right than they knew. The lining is one grain deeper now, and the grain is not a person, and it does not sleep along the north wall, and it counts the fish through all the open decades: three, three, three.

A dome like this should ring. It does not, and it will not. The hush is kept twice — once from below, by a person the country remembers only as a condition, and once from above, by an attention she has filed under weather.

It is the quietest arrangement in the world, and everything here keeps it.
