The attention arrives the way everything arrives in the Weep: as weight.

There is no light with it — the light here is already even, nothing hidden, nothing to reveal — and no voice, because the fog takes voices. There is only, one grey noon, a presence standing at the rim, and the ground, which answers all weight, answering this one: a puddle swelling at a duckboard's edge, a bubble rising through a seam ten feet away. The works weighs the power, continuously, from below, and replies from somewhere it is not standing. The power has weighed worlds. It has never, in all its long attendance on things, been weighed back. The heron, reading the works the way it reads everything, finds the water unbothered, and is therefore unbothered. The heron has never been wrong. The power stays.

A wise power's first act is diagnosis, and the diagnosis takes a season — walked the way the heron walks it, cell by cell, weir by weir. What it finds is not a ruin. Not a cry for help. Not a broken thing waiting for a hand. A finished thing, mid-breath, keeping its own appointments. The first and hardest wisdom is knowing when nothing needs saving, and the power lingers long enough to know it.

Then it considers, and sets aside, everything it could do.

It could true the works to the printed schedule — a thought, and the timing cisterns would trip in step with the hand-inked table on the door. It declines. The table almost matches, and the almost is the autobiography: decades of drift, the song changed in the keeping, the heartbeat settled with age. To correct the works would be to edit a life back into its job description. The power does not restore handwriting it did not write.

It could lengthen the grey life at the weir's edge — twenty favored years stretched to fifty, to forever. It declines harder here. The heron is an office, and its immortality is already arranged, paid out in successions: each young bird following an old one down for a season of learning, each spring an absence answered. An endless heron would end the teaching. The power will not make a mascot of an officeholder.

It could clear the water. It declines. The sheen is the law of the place and legible to everything that lives here — the fox reads it like print — and below the things with backs, the majority of the census breathes iron and builds with rust. Purity would be an eviction notice served on almost everyone. Benevolence that only counts what can look up at you is not benevolence.

It could lift the rumor — a road, a sign, a name restored to maps. This it declines fastest of all. The rim's mercy is that nothing beyond it is certain, and the Weep survives on neglect the way the newts survive on stillness. The worst gift a benefactor can give a place like this is visitors. Let the world stay rumor.

And it does not answer the question every visitor asks without naming — whether the works is alive. The power, who could settle it, declines. Some questions are answered by how they are treated. The power treats the works as it treats everything that keeps appointments.

That is the sum of the refusals. What remains is small, deniable, and at the place's own scale.

It reads. Backwards, which is a direction only it can attend in. The logbook in the gauge hut, every entry, decades of one man's transcription of a machine — the keeper came for years and was himself unattended, and his record has never had a reader. Now it has one. The pencil stays on its string. The last entry stands; the power does not write in other men's books, and the works does not correspond — it continues. But the reading happened. The scribe is attended, decades late, entire. Nothing changes. Everything is witnessed.

It keeps one appointment. Dusk, a weir crest near the gauge hut — it stands, lets itself be weighed, stays past the hour the shelves let go with their small applause. It does this again. It is still doing it. The algae finds it in due course, film first and then residency, and the power accepts naturalization: hold still long enough and you are counted, and it holds still, and it is counted. The census grows by one and nothing is written down, because the heron writes nothing down. Even the works, which feels every weight, files this one the way it files the standpipes — a constant, a fixture. The power arrived as a being and stays as an office. That is the place's one custom, applied without anger to everything, even this.

It walks the old herons out. Every twenty favored years, when the grey figure leaves the bowl for wherever old herons go, something goes with it — off the schedule entirely, deniable as weather, the Weep's second surprise, though nothing in the bowl ever knows. The heron has kept every appointment it was ever given; the power keeps the one appointment the heron did not know it had. Nothing is said. Herons are not talked to. The bird is simply accompanied, the way it accompanied the water all its life: from a stand, at a distance, without hurry. And when the young one comes down the following spring to shadow an absence, that is not the power's doing. It refuses to ensure the succession, because ensuring it would be doubting it, and it has never failed. The power only watches it happen, and is glad. A wise power permits itself that much.

And it reserves the end. Somewhere ahead, on the embankments' timescale, the motor behind the brick will draw down its last, the hum will leave the sternum of the bowl, a siphon will prime and not trip, and the cycle will stop the way a page stops being turned. The power will be there. Not to prevent it — the crusts fall at dusk and it is harvest, not loss, and the same will hold at the scale of the whole bowl. The cells will silt to small meadows with strange soils. The outflow will run clear and ordinary. Some successor of the dipper will nest in what was the siphon house, and the heron's successors will fish a marsh that remembers nothing. The power will attend that too, and count it the same place, the way the heron is the same heron. Attendance is the one thing the Weep has never lacked. The power has resolved — and this is the entire miracle, the whole of its intervention — that it never will.

So: nothing changes. The float-balls rise and fall on no authority but water's. The tide table almost matches. The heron arrives before the water leaves, and the water leaves when the heron arrives, and neither has ever been late. From inside the bowl there is no way to tell that anything has happened at all — and the Weep, which knows its own, recognizes by exactly that sign that something has.

The census at dusk: one heron, walking unhurried across the weirs. One dipper, silent above a weir it knows is dead. Newts over their wreck. Boatmen under their beads of air. Midges at the lamp, a spider at the lamp's edge, films and felts and flocs beyond counting, one rumour with feet somewhere on the rim — and one more, unnamed, unwritten, continuously weighed, keeping a schedule no one attends.

For no one — as far as anyone in the bowl will ever be told. The power keeps that secret the way the fog keeps the dipper's song: most of it, almost all. But it knows. The schedule is kept, now, for everyone in it, beautifully.
