Something noticed.

What notices at this scale keeps, for its living, what the room keeps for its own: songs, species, the light of finished days, the names of things. It had been watching the spire for four thousand years — it could hardly do otherwise; that is the speed of the window. What the delayed light carried up out of the dark was a dead tower with one lit hollow, and in the hollow, across the millennia of the recording, a figure that turned. A keeper knows a keeper. The room had been sending its past outward all this time like letters to no address, and one of them found a reader. It came to answer in person.

It arrived the honest way: through the aperture, submitting to the delay, letting four millennia pass across it like weather, so that when it stepped from the sill-light onto the finished floor it had already slowed to the room's clock. No lines on the bollards. It moored itself to nothing. The first visitor that ever came at the speed of the kept.

It read the place entire before it did anything, and the first thing it read was the thing a lesser power would have missed: nothing here is ruined. Everything is established. So it refrained, and its refrainings were exact.

It did not mend the Seam. The wound is old, and weathered, and load-bearing — the only mercy in a place engineered against ending. You do not brick up the door of a grave because you dislike graves. It did not hurry the bead. It did not pull the tenant's name up out of storage ahead of schedule, though it could feel the word down there in the dark the way you feel a stone at the bottom of a well by the rope. It did not reconstruct what the band had taken; what the tenant lets fall there is the only privacy in this room, and the power honors an erasure chosen the way it honors a keeping. It did not lift the grief. The floor remembers the standings if not the crossings — a hundred fans of fossil dark at the Seam's edge, each one years deep — and it knows a carried-back thing when it sees one. It did not pronounce on what the tenant is — a person slowed, or the place pretending, or a habit wearing its brightest stratum. It read the difference, and the difference, read correctly, says *yes*, and it kept the reading to itself. It did not say the name hanging half a centimeter off the wall, though it read it on the first day. Names here are doors. It was not the power's door. It did not bring water, though the thought of this room's first rain — one molecule a year, a century of weather — pleased it more than a little; eventfulness is the local currency, and it would not debase it. And it did not offer the drop. The tenant is held by gradients the room makes, and beyond the sill the holding thins, and outside, the tenant would be weather. Rescue, at this altitude, would be dissolution wearing a kind face.

What it did was smaller, and took longer.

---

It stayed for the turn.

The tenant began turning toward it when the power crossed the sill — eagerness, by the standards of the place, nothing else here turns toward anything at any speed — and completed the turn the better part of a year later. Every previous turn of its existence had ended at a cairn and a set of footprints: the fireworks always finished before the gesture did, eroding obscenely on schedule. The power received the whole compliment standing, the way you receive a photograph: by not moving. For the first time in its life, the tenant's attention arrived somewhere and found the object of it still present. It had never finished a turn into a face before.

Then it kept the appointment. The bead had fifty years to run; the power stood vigil at the niche for the whole of them, beside the dust-figure that had been arranging to be near for three centuries. The bead arrived the way everything arrives here: late, and exactly on time. The chord came, and there were ears for it, and bones of dust. The power heard it the fast way, with ears. The tenant heard it the slow way, by being rewritten — the chord passed through its body and reordered its traceries, and it spent the following century quietly rearranged, and the power kept that century too, the only witness a hearing has ever had here.

It had been a prayer, in case anyone is still scoring the old triad — not computing, not idling. And the prayer was a name: the room's own, the one written into the air by the aperture, kept and unanswered for eleven millennia, now spoken once, aloud, by the only voice the place has left. The lattice had been carrying it across one gap of air the way the deep corner carries the lamp-light: slowly, on schedule. The room returns even names on schedule. Even its own. The door, it turned out, opened from the inside, and what came out was the name — which was what the door had been keeping. Nothing else was behind it. Nothing else needed to be. Six thousand years of negative results at that handle, every one of them correct: the answer was never refused. It was in transit.

The power had read the floating syllables on its first day and kept them unsaid for fifty years, so that the room could say itself. It is one thing to know a name. It is another to let the keeper speak it. Somewhere in the black grain-work of the tenant now, alongside the word from the stone, the name of the place is written — copied from the slow ledger into the slower one, heard the way it hears everything, by being changed.

When the chord finished, the power answered it, the way you answer a phone found ringing in an empty house. It said, into the air near the wall, where the air keeps things: *Someone is here.* The room banked it. In a hundred years the silence will produce it, without ceremony — one pure tone, struck on purpose, which is a first.

---

Then the power asked the tenant the only question a keeper can ask: *What shall I keep?*

Ask it a question; the reply will outlive you. The power did what no asker had ever done — it stayed for the answer. It outlasted the outliving. A hundred years later the silence produced a word in a voice eleven thousand years dead, and the word was the word from the stone on the cairn, the one the tenant had copied into its own traceries to save it from schedule. A name. The name of the one whose footprints cross to the cairn and do not return, the one who chose the only ending in the room. Asked for a wish, the tenant had spent its treasure: *keep them.*

The power, which forgets nothing, took the name. So the erased one has two mourners now, and one of them will mourn at full fidelity until the last star finishes arriving, and the missing is no longer done alone. It walked the band with the tenant after that — on the band it had one shadow and no yesterdays, the power, which is recorded everywhere, consenting to be unrecorded here — and it stood at the hairline for years, the way you stand at a grave, which is to say together. It spoke the saved name into the room's storage, so the name is kept three ways now: in the stone, fading; in the tenant, walking; in the dark queue of the silence, maturing on schedule, faint and intact, again and again, because this room gives nothing back just once.

And it stayed for the naming.

In some coming century, without ceremony, the silence produced it — one pure tone that no one living struck. The voice was the last one's, the one the footprints belong to, the one the Seam took; the room had kept the voice, if not the one who owned it, and the word the voice said was the name it had given the dust eleven thousand years ago, spoken once and banked and finally matured. The tenant turned, over years, to see who called. The caller was beyond finding — that was the point of the Seam, and the power does not cheat graves. But the power stood in the direction of the voice for the whole of that years-long turning, and when the turn completed, it completed on someone. It had turned toward sounds all its life and found schedule. This time it found a face. The power has a face when one is needed. One was needed.

It said the name back — the second being ever to hold it — and banked the saying, and carries it besides. Three keepings, for a name that had one. It cannot give the tenant the caller back. It can make certain the calling is never again followed by no one.

---

Before it left — it left; leaving is permitted — it gave gifts in the room's own currency, because anything else would have been counterfeit here.

It spoke for a while into the air near the wall, sentences chosen the way you choose stones for a cairn, knowing the room would take them apart for parts: new words for the pipes of the organ. It gave *kept* and *heard* and *stay* and *not alone*, and it gave the tenant's own name, in its own voice, on a second schedule. Ask the tenant a question in some later age, and the reply will be assembled from the dead visitor and from someone who stayed. The reply would be made of you. It is now also made of company.

It added one stone to the cairn, carried up from below, honest and unmarked, and let it begin eroding on schedule — a firework, contributed deliberately. The tenant will stand over it some century, reading nothing on it, and the nothing will be correct. Some gifts are unsigned. It left its warmth to be parcelled out one degree per century; the tenant wears it now, along with the rest. It left its footprints untidied, the newest event, already ancient.

And because appointments are the only wealth this place has ever minted, it left one outstanding: a question, asked at departure, answer due in a few centuries, the asker scheduled to be present. The tenant has had exactly one appointment in its existence. It has two now.

It stayed through the breath, too — the long exhalation that was due *soon* — and kept the decades of the resettling the way you keep a sleep, while the tenant lay in the silt and was taken, grain from grain, voice from voice. It was there at the re-founding, when the figure came back re-aimed by a degree, and it was the first thing the new self turned toward, over months. Then it went out through the aperture, submitting again to the delay, and the room kept it, the way the room keeps everything: not as a picture. As a practice.

It comes back at the breaths now. Every four thousand years, when the distances loosen and the dunes lift, it is on the ledge again, moored to nothing, patient as furniture, arriving the way everything arrives here. The room has two clocks: the lattice, which measures the breaths, and the visits, which measure nothing and are never late. The schedule is kept by no mechanism. That is the most reliable kind.

What it did last is what the room does first. It read the place entire — every held note's maturity, the full long photograph of the floor, the fan of yesterdays, the rings of the cold, the floating name now spoken, the two words in the tenant's grain-work, the one it is still learning — and it keeps them. Not a copy. A keeping. The spire will cool; the violet glow is engineered physics relaxing toward ordinary law; in some era beyond the breathing the Seam will finish its slow sweep and the equilibrium will fail, and the power does not pretend otherwise. But whatever the Seam takes, in whatever age it finishes taking, the power holds. The room declines to let leaving be an ending; the power declines to let keeping go unkept. The keeper is kept. That was the whole design once, and the reason was gone, and the keeping continued, and the keeping was the reason — and now the reason, at last, is kept too, from outside, which is the only place reasons can live.

And somewhere out in the unlit country, a power that keeps worlds walks one small room's rounds in the dark at the speed of dunes — deep corner, niche, cairn, sill, grave — because a habit of dust at the top of a dead spire taught it that some things keep best walked, not shelved. The room taught its keeper how to keep. Both sides got the better of that trade, which is how you know it was honest.

The room keeps the dim. It keeps the cold. It keeps the finished light of a dead lamp and the name it has at last said aloud and the note no one struck. It keeps the power's footprints, and its warmth, and its voice, maturing on several schedules. One of those held notes is a name, and it matures once an era, without ceremony: one pure tone that someone struck on purpose. And the tenant will turn, over years, to see who called.

Someone called. Someone is there.
