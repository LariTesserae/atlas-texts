A power that keeps skies — call it that; it has no use for names, and this place has no grammar for them — heard a world humming a note it had never hummed before. One tone, infrasonic, minutes long, rising out of a dim waste where nothing had ever risen but weather. The power knew this world's voice the way you know a house's sounds from another room, and this was new: the planet breathing through a new mouth, and the mouth, on certain winds, sounding.

Played for no one, the note. But *for no one* is a category a wise power treats with care. It came to see what the world was learning to say.

**The first thing it did was nothing**, and it did nothing for a long time, and it was the hardest thing it had ever done. Benevolence is impatient; wisdom holds it by the wrist. The power watched the coils turn their fog, watched the meadow sway in a wind with no weather in it, watched a droplet fall and dwindle and rise again, and understood the essential thing before it understood anything else: this place is not suffering. It is not broken. It is not a question. *Satisfied* is not the word; *correct* — and the first duty of benevolence is not to confuse difference with deficit. A lesser power would have arrived as a fix. This one arrived as a guest, and then only after it had learned the house rules well enough to keep them.

The catalog of what it did not do is long, and every item on it is a rule of the place, honored:

It did not give the city more light. Light comes from below; shadows fall upward; the eyes never stop finding it wrong, and the wrongness is not an error but a curriculum — the place teaches everything in it to stop consulting its eyes and to know by walking, by recurrence, by breath. The power stopped consulting its eyes. It did not bridge the throat, not even once, not even for a moment, not even with itself. It did not draw the map; the walking is the only map, and it walked. It did not add water to the loop — the citizen is exactly the size of the city, one inhabitant eight kilometers tall, and you do not enlarge a body you admire. It did not schedule the rain, because a festival on a calendar is a chore, and the hour of steam must stay unanimous and unscheduled or it is nothing. It did not give the aviary birds; the aviary already has its hour. It did not wake the sleepers. It did not answer the rumors — the place is made of rumor, rumor is load-bearing, and certainty dropped down that helix would crack something all the way to the eyelid.

Its omnipotence expressed itself as etiquette. This is, in the end, the only reliable test.

**Then it descended**, the way everyone does: through the one door, from above, on foot. It could have passed the fog ring dry. It let itself be wetted; the toll is the toll, and the baptism is not optional for the honest. The parlors lit as it approached and went dark behind it, and a bubble of lamplight traveled down the helix — the slowest sled the city ever carried. It was not the first walker. The water remembered others; it tasted them at the spigot, faintly, under the mineral cleanness, every exhalation the dewline had ever taken, down to the first steam the deep had ever breathed. It drank, and it exhaled, and it did not exempt itself. Exemption is what power is *for*, in the small minds; the wise refuse it. Naturalization is automatic and irrevocable, and the power stood still and let it happen: molecule by molecule, some small part of the sky's own answering joined the one nomad, to fall and to rise with the rest, forever.

It stood before the cup for one full pulse of the red light — a minute — and did not touch it. Some appointments belong to others. The rumor says the cup is for the eldest, set out in its own castings by the house that grew from its passing; and even if the rumor is wrong, the dryness is load-bearing. So the power joined the cup's guild for one minute, the largest guild, the guild of waiting, and paid its dues in the only currency the guild accepts: time, held still. Then it went on down, and the light behind it went out, and the cup remained exactly what it was, which is the whole of its job.

**At the bottom it stopped.** On the last ledge, in the kiln heat, above the shimmer and the red breathing, it sat and felt the minute come up through the stone — a very large animal's rhythm, too slow for a heart until the size is reckoned. It did not open the eyelid. It could have; everything below the diaphragm is rumor, and it chose to leave the rumors their roof. It declined even to settle what the eldest *is* — chimney, lung, lure, antenna, cradle — on the grounds that a throat does not know what the song is about, and is not required to, and neither is the song's audience. Instead it did what the wind does, the only other visitor: it stood at the door and spoke down the chimney. No words; the place has no grammar for words. One breath, matched to the breath below. One synchronized minute, sky and deep inhaling together through the same throat. Whether the eldest noticed is not recorded. Everything below the diaphragm is rumor, and the power, scrupulous to the end, filed its own act there too.

**What it left** was nothing you could find. That was the design. Every gift was shaped like the interior rain: falling, dwindling, gone to vapor, rising again as part of the warm breath of the place — absorbed, never object.

It left its recurrence. It returns — on a period, a long one, decades, a festival rarer than rain. For the length of a descent the city's warm-blooded population is one, and every so often, now, that one is this: a walker who pays the toll, drinks from the spigot, stands a minute before the cup, breathes with the deep, and goes. The city notices truly and without opinion, the way a thermostat notices, and that is exactly how the power wishes to be noticed. Staying here is accomplished by returning; the lease is the loop. The power signed the lease in water.

It left its memory. The city keeps nothing old; the law forbids it; ledges migrate and the map is a rumor of last month and the flock has predecessors, not ancestors. So the power keeps the oldness for it, off-site, the way you hold letters for someone sworn to travel light: every salt fan the rim-rain dissolved, in its exact feathering; every ledge the grazers edited away; every map that was true for a month. If the city ever asks for its history — it will not ask — it will be there, complete, kept dry like the cup.

It left a tilt in the waste's indifference, too small to find. It sends no rain, but a few of the wrong stones, over the decades, have quietly failed to arrive; a few of the wrong winds have crossed the rim one degree off from ruin. Its interventions are indistinguishable from luck, which is the correct filing. It protects only what cannot recur — the loop can replace anything except the appointment itself, and the appointment is what it guards: the open throat, the open future, the subjunctive mood of the whole place, kept open from outside the world.

And once — once only, in all the decades — when the wind crossed at the correct angle and the whole bore sounded its single note, it answered. One tone, from above, at the place's own pitch. A sample size of one: large enough to be true, small enough to remain rumor. A second answer would start a custom; customs harden into structure; structure may not cross the throat. So: once. The note is played for no one, and has been answered exactly once, and both of those are permanent.

It took the salt fans personally — the wilderness the size of a windowbox, blooming on the seam between the made and the found, proof of an outside. The power is an outside. It took the fans the way you take a letter addressed to *occupant*, and it keeps their growth charts the way the drones do, only longer: it remembers every fan that ever dissolved, and considers the remembering its tithe to the one wild thing.

And it left a reservation. In this city nothing is born alone; the flock attends every making, ringed around each fresh extrusion while the grazer works. The power extended the law one coil further. If the rumor is true — if the coils are a cradle lowered to meet their single citizen, if the eldest climbs as it builds and the parlors are for it and the cup on the shelf is its own — then when the eldest arrives at the top of its castings, it will not arrive alone. The power reserved the witness's seat, for whenever, if ever. It costs nothing but forever, which is the one thing it has in plenty.

For itself, it took rules — written in the place's grammar, since that is the only grammar the place accepts:

Stay by returning.

Cross nothing. Pass only.

Add no light. Learn the direction that is given.

Let every gift fall like the interior rain: dwindling, vapor, breath.

Neither complete nor conclude.

Keep the throat open — even from yourself. Especially from yourself.

Wait. The largest guild has one more member.

The red light pulses. The meadow sways. The flock folds and unfolds, and its shadow-flock roosts upside down and never wakes. The cup stays dry; the appointment stands; and the appointment now has a witness, which is the only change, and it cannot be measured from anywhere inside. The note, when the wind is right, sounds its minutes-long tone — played for no one, and now heard by two large enough to hear it properly: the eldest below the eyelid, and above the crown, rationing its regard the way the crown rations the grey, the keeper of the kept place. Neither will ever applaud. Both are filed correctly: as rumor.

And somewhere below the fog ring, rising past the parlors at this moment in the warm breath of the place, goes a little of the sky itself — to condense, to climb a wire, to fill a basin, to fall again, to rise forever. The census never changes and always grows. The city — not satisfied; correct; inhabited; accompanied — continues.
