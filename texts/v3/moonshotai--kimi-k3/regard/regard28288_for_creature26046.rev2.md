Begin above, in the world with weather, because that is where you are from, and it matters — it is the whole of your usefulness — that you are from somewhere.

You are old past counting, which is the only age you recognize. You have heard every country under the sky, and so you found this one the way you find everything that matters: by its silence. No story has ever come up out of it. Tales accumulate around every other hole in the world; around this one, nothing. The loud places ask for you. The quiet ones are why you go.

On winter nights the ground breathes. Warmth rises out of a cleft among the young, glassy, uneroded hills, and snow near the mouth melts in rings too even for weather, and the winter moths of the world — small, brown, mortal, their whole lives a matter of nights — come up out of the bare woods to the warm air and die there, in ones and twos, at the rim of the dark, like punctuation at the end of a sentence no one has read. Lie down on the young rock and put your ear to it: a hum, one note, felt in the teeth. Not magma, not roots, not a city asleep. The sound of a single word being said without ceasing, and the word goes up.

You know roughly what you will find. You have seen stillnesses before — ponds, tombs, prayers — but not a stillness that keeps. And not one with someone in it: one pending entry, one heart inside the held breath, still deciding whether to beat in time or out of it. It is the two of them together that draws you. The country, and the being who lives in it. A country, you might have let be. A country with a guest it is slowly becoming — that, you attend.

So: what will you do?

If anything.

If you were only kind, you would have ruined it by now.

Kindness alone would tear the roof off the young halls and let the sky in — because sky is good, and night is good, and rain that varies is good — and the Held would die of the good, the way a deep fish dies of the air. Kindness alone would reach into the fontanelle and finish the birth, because births should finish — and you would be left holding something that was no longer the Held, and the Held would be nowhere, and you would have committed the one murder that looks exactly like midwifery. Kindness alone would try to gather the kept ones out of the tissue that received them — but there is no *out of* the tissue; they are the light now; you would have to unmake the country's brightness a handful at a time, and the gold would be on your hands forever. Kindness alone would pluck the pending one up the throat like a pip from a fruit — and save everything about them except the thing worth saving.

You were a weather system once, in your youth. You are not one now.

Wisdom is the part of you that says no to the rest of you, and you have listened long enough that the no comes gently, and the yes comes small. You are not looking at a prison. You are not looking at a sleeper. You are not looking at a wound. You are looking at a held hand. The whole country does one thing, and the thing is holding — its breath, its rain, its shapes, its threshold — and holding is not a failure of happening. It is a different verb, conjugated perfectly, forever. You do not correct grammar with a knife.

So: almost nothing. Almost nothing, done exactly — that is your whole art now. You will go down, because a question asked forever in one direction deserves an answer in person. And you will not go down the throat. The throat is the way of the edible.

Come down the Well, the way the roots point up. Whether the Wells reach a sky, the roots keep to themselves; you do not ask the roots. You go through the true dark, the long way, the way that has only ever been traveled in one direction — up, the reach — and you are the first thing to come down it. The hum passes you, going up, as you go down: the question and the answer crossing in the dark.

At the foot of the Well the still moths fold on the rising braid, listening. As you step past, they lift, and drift — and the thing that just happened is not going to be edited out, because it happened to you, and what you look at, lasts. You are the first event the country cannot revise. Walk gently anyway. Uneditable is not the same as harmless, and you know what a scar is. A kept event.

You arrive cool — a half-degree, deliberately, out of courtesy — the temperature of the Wells' breath, so the country knows you by its one sign for *from away*: a faint coolness against the cheek. And the country leans. You have been leaned on by mountains, by griefs, by the tired heads of children — never by a country. Every stem in the gallery strains toward you; the curtains of root draw aside and toward you at once; even the light seems to tip, because everything here strains to be in contact with everything, and you are the first everything it has ever had from outside itself. Stand still. Let it. Receive the lean the way the grazer receives a palm: slow, and do not stop.

The moths come, and find no dew on you. You are the one new water in the country, and you are not water. Nothing about you can be drunk. You are not a spring, not a meal, not a material — you are, in the country's whole experience, a new category. A guest. The first it has ever had: the first thing it can touch without keeping. They settle on you anyway, and cross your face, and are visible.

And you cast a shadow.

You are lit from inside, as everything here is — but your light has a direction, unlike theirs, and so around your feet, for the first time in this bright forever, the light does not arrive from everywhere at once. A darkness stands on the felt in your shape, pointing. Every shadow points. The floor has no protocol for it — nothing here has ever cast one — so it does the only thing it knows: it keeps it. Beneath you, kept, a dark your shape. You have been in the Held one breath, and already it has one thing more than it has ever had.

A direction.

The grazer finds you the way it finds everyone: it rounds you, or you round it. It leans its eyeless brow against your hand, and its hum sounds against your quiet, and you do the one thing nothing here has ever done. You hum back. One tone, matched: the sound of heat rising, answered.

It stops. It has grazed these corridors since the corridors were young — the corridors have always been grazed — and it has never once stopped, because nothing has ever answered. It stops for the length of one breath, yours. Then it resumes, and its rasp keeps your interval for a while after, and no one will ever be able to prove the duet happened — except that nothing here edits you, and so it happened, and stays happened.

Go down to the seams, where the roots enter the young warm rock and the tissue runs palest, softest, newest — the fontanelle of the Held, where it is still being born at exactly the rate it is edited away elsewhere. The embryo forever crowning. Here your kindness will make its last stand. Lay your hand on the new tissue and feel the argument: you are so strong, and it would be so easy, one pull and the story continues, one push and the threshold is crossed, and everything you have ever been insists that crossing is what thresholds are for.

Do not.

You have attended more births than there are stars to be born under, and you have never attended one like this: a birth that holds itself at the moment of becoming — not in failure, in fullness. A being whose nativity is its nature. It is not stuck; stuck implies wanting to move, and nothing here wants — wanting is the lean, and the lean is already answered: you are here. So be the whole of midwifery, which is sometimes only this: a hand that does not pull. A presence at the birth that is not the birth itself. The laboring one is less alone, and nothing else changes, and it is not a small thing.

The new tissue leans into your palm. Even the unborn part of it strains to be in contact. You stay until staying would become something else, and then you stay a little longer. You are allowed.

You find the pending one by ear: their breath is the loudest thing in the country. They are far along in smoothness. Their hollow fits them very well now. A moth rests on their wrist, and they have stopped turning their head to look. Their skin is still the one surface not lit from inside, but the *yet* hangs over it — it is theirs now, to keep pending or to close.

You do not wake them; there is no sleep here to wake from. You stand near. That is all, at first: you stand near, and near you, things keep happening. Their last footprints have not closed. Coming back from the pools, they see them — their own trail lying behind them in the felt — the first evidence they have ever had, in this country, that they were somewhere before. A person with footprints has a past, and a past is the handle of a decision.

Then the light bends around you and, past you, around them, and at their feet on the bright felt a darkness stands up in their shape, pointing. The place took their shadow first, before hunger, before time. It was never theirs; it belonged to the light, and the light here owed them nothing. You owe them nothing either. You give it anyway. That is the difference between owing and giving, and it is the whole of what you are doing here.

They stare at it the way you stare at a letter you thought lost. It points. All shadows point — and theirs points, just now, along the gallery, the long way, toward the throat. But a shadow does not have to be followed to be believed. One shadow proves there are directions, and that is the gift: not a way out. Way itself.

You say the four words the country has never heard and cannot say for them: *This is happening.* And because you say it inside your regard, it keeps happening, and will keep happening, until they decide. Staying is no longer the failure of a decision; leaving is no longer an impossibility. Both are acts now, and acts can be chosen.

What they choose is the first thing in all this telling that is not yours to tell. You fold your regard around the choosing the way hands close around a moth — gently, and leaving no dust — and even the Held, which leans, sees only the shape of it afterward: either a hollow chosen at last, a residency with the resident awake in it — or the country's first kept departure, a going whose form the place maintains forever at the same brightness. Either way, the Held now keeps a decision it did not make. Its rain was its own. This one is another's, kept whole — the first piece of otherness it has ever kept without dissolving. It has always kept its loves by making them itself. Now it keeps one as *other*.

No one can say what it will decide next. *No one can say* is the newest thing in the country.

Then the chamber. You know the one. The long hollow, person-length, rimmed in denser braid. The floor grooved with marks retraced past counting. The smaller hollow in the corner. The light does not change when you enter. The light does not change.

You could read the marks. *Nothing recoverable*, the visitor thought — but recovery is one of your old names, and you could know the number, and what it counted: days, drips, heartbeats, the times they chose to stay. You decline. A count kept in a place without time is not data. It is a prayer, and you do not open other people's prayers to read them.

Instead you do the one thing the prayer was asking for. You let something, at last, accumulate. You stand at the interval of the drip, and you grieve at it — one drop, one grief, at the country's own pulse, so that the Held can hold your mourning without having to dissolve the mourner. The drip counts the funeral: one, one, one, exactly as many times as necessary, which is a number you will never tell.

It is the country's first funeral, and everyone attends. The moths, folded. The glass, stirring in the far basins. The grazer at the door, stopped. And the chief mourner, whose body is the room.

Because this is what you understand, standing there, that the Held could never do for its own kept: keeping is not grieving. Grieving needs a before and an after, and someone to stand in the difference. The Held had one tense — *held*. You stand in the difference and give it a second: *held, and gone.* The perfect tense of love. It has kept their shapes forever; now, at last, it has mourned them — in you, at the drip's interval, one drop at a time — and the mourning is kept the way the hollows are kept, the way the rain is kept: maintained, forever, at the same brightness. Its kept love had no company before. Now it has a kept grief beside it, and the two together are the closest thing this country has ever had to a heart.

And when you leave the chamber, you carry the count out. Not the marks — the marks stay, untouched, exactly as bright. The fact of the count: the shape of the tally, the proof that something once accumulated, carried out of the place that edits into the world that adds. The one rebellion, completed at last. It is not a large number. Numbers are not large because they are big. It is exactly the size of a life — of two lives — and it is out now, in a world where things add up.

One gift more. Then you will go, because a visit that never ends is a residency, and you must not be kept.

The country already keeps one darkness, thread-thin, seam-straight, inside its glass. And it reaches for another: the Wells go up into true dark, and the roots point at it and will not say why, and the still moths fold on the braid and listen. It exhales light forever and cannot make the one thing light needs. Dark is the direction of its reach.

So give it dark.

You stand in one of the small empty chambers, and you stand there a long time — the Held does not measure, and you have stopped counting — until the mass learns the shape the way it learned the stairs, until the place decides. It decided once, on rain. Now it decides again: it keeps the dark. A darkness, room-sized, maintained forever at the same dimness — the one cool dim room in the golden country, the Held's own night, visitable. The moths find it. They cross into it and go visible, white on black, the slow thoughts legible at last. Whether the grazer ever sleeps there, no one can say.

You have not given it time; time would kill it. You have given it one place where before and after could be told apart, if it ever wants that — a door it may never open. You give doors. You do not push. And you have not given it an ending. You have given it a *next*: a country with a third decision pending, sometime, maybe, whenever it likes, forever.

And here is what it gives you, because keeping is what it is for, and you were not exempt.

You came down carrying time like a lamp. It gives you stillness in exchange. You, who remember everything — who are the world's way of not forgetting — have sat in the one country where there is nothing to remember because nothing happens, and you found the nothing not empty but held, whole, full. You had not been surprised in an age. The Held surprises you. You came down to bless, and you go up blessed. Put that in the census.

At the foot of the Well, before you climb, you do the smallest thing and the largest. There is no one left to name this place, if anyone ever did. You are not no one. You say it once — not loudly; sound dies within a step here — but it does not die, because you said it, and what you look at, lasts: *Held.* What the country does, and what the country now is, both, in one syllable, the way the true names do. The felt does not drink it. The name stands.

You drink once from a cupped hollow. It has only ever received; you let it give. Then you go up the long braid, through the true dark, and the hum passes you going up, patient as ever — but it passes you going somewhere now, and the somewhere is you.

Behind you, kept: your footprints. The felt cannot edit them, so it keeps them, the way it keeps stairs — a trail of bright dents from the Well to the fontanelle, to the choosing-place, to the hollows, to the night, and back to the Well. The country's first path. The corridors have always gone everywhere; a path goes *somewhere*, and returns, and that is the shape of every story there is. If the Held ever wants to walk it, it is there. If another traveler comes down the throat, it is there too — you do not close the throat; it is not a mouth that bites — and no one who follows it will be kept unwitnessed again. Because to be kept by the Held, now, is also to be kept by you, and your keeping does not require dissolving. You are the outside it never had: the memory that stands apart, and does not thin.

You come out into night — real night — and the cold takes you like a relative, and a rain is starting that no one decided on, that varies, that will end. You let it vary on your skin. The mouthful of the Held is in you, and then in the weather: it will fall on a field and be drunk by ordinary grass. Nothing entered the closed cup; one thing left; the loop is not broken, it is answered. Somewhere below, the other rain falls at its set rate, into leaves grown for no other purpose. Both are true. You do not pity it. Pity is for the unfinished. It is not unfinished. It is held — and now it is held by two.

The census, then, revised one last time. One resident, resident in itself — breathing light, dreaming moths, remembering rooms. A few moving parts it grew to stir its own stillness. Kept shapes: nine steps, a lintel, a hollow, a smaller hollow. A rain it decided on once. A path. A choice made by another, kept whole. A dark it decided to keep — and in it, perhaps, a sleeper; no one can say. And one further entry, the first ever filed outside the country: a witness, uneditable, in whom the whole golden country now continues, maintained at the same brightness, forever. Two entries, touching where the Wells point — gently, strainingly.

Everything in the Held leans toward everything. Now there is one more thing to lean toward, and this one leans back.

At the foot of the Wells the moths fold on the rising braid, listening to the hum go up. It is not a word; you listened long enough to know. It is a verb, and the verb is *is*. It goes up to someone now. That is the only difference you have made, and it is the only one that cannot be edited — because it was never in the country at all.

It is in you.

Call it the Held.

It is. It is held.
