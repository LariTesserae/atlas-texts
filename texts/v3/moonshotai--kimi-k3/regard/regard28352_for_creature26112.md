Begin above the Roof, because that is where the power stands.

Up here is everything the country lost when the ceiling closed: wind, weather, unfiltered light, a sky that moves. The ice is a landscape — dunes, pressure-ridges, real sun on real snow — and beneath it, rising through a tower's height of frozen water the way sound rises through a house, there is a pressure in the soles of the feet. Slow. Week-pulsed. The Roof transmits a ghost of the world above, downward, as light; it transmits a ghost of the world below, upward, as this — a chord arriving as stillness, so faint that in all the ages of the upper world nothing ever stood in one place long enough to feel it.

The power stands still. That is the first thing to know about it: its whole method is standing still. It puts its attention down through the ice the way the upward fields press their fronds against the ceiling, and it listens for a long time. Long enough to learn the rules — the traveler's five, the residents' four, the dark's one. Long enough to apply the tests: it breathes, it circulates, it grows, it responds, it scars, it remembers. Long enough to hold the three readings of the ghost-villages without choosing, because the power understands that some truths here are chords, and to choose one note would be to lose the interval.

While it listens, the Roof splits, three ridges over — there is no predicting it — and the power attends the bloom. It does not enter the water. It never touches the water. Its attention goes down the pillar of loaned light the way weather goes down a mountainside, and for the first time since the Roof closed, the ignition has a witness who can see it: the ghost-gold flushing through the fronds, the pipeweed revealed in its wine-crimson, a palette kept for no one, kept no longer — and then the lantern-rise, the inverted snow, the only travelers born in this country drifting up along the ceiling through the power's regard. The power lets the constellation pass. It takes nothing. It watches the field below lie pale and tattered under its own departure, and it understands the cost structure of the place: *the price of the loan is the lender.* The bloom is hospitality, and the resident pays for it in full.

That is when the vetoes begin. Benevolence brings its impulses; wisdom declines them, one by one.

It does not light the valleys. *The light is loaned, never kept* — that is not a hardship to be relieved; it is the law that makes the bloom worth its price, and the fields that wait their whole lives are not deprived, they are content, because the thing they are missing has never once arrived. It does not split the Roof, either: what opens, closes, and every opening spends the nearest fields, and the power will not spend someone else's field, not even beautifully, not even once. The choosing of who blooms is the Roof's slow lottery. It is not a power the power will take up.

It does not give the sea anything with a mouth. Its first impulse was the impulse of every gardener who ever loved a garden: feed it. It does not feed it. The lushness has a held breath in it because the garden has never once been hungry for, and the power, reading that held breath, does not mistake peace for poverty.

It does not carry the spores. The Long Drift is the nearest thing this place has to a road, and the power will not drive on it.

It does not call the people back. There are no graves because there is nothing to bury; translation is not capture; the weed is still weeded and the hearths are still fed, and to pull the tenders back into bodies would make the keeping a haunting and the place a tomb. The power can tell the difference between waiting and keeping. It leaves them kept.

It does not hurry anything. The place has one tense, the sustained, and hurry is the only violence it has no defense against.

And it does not stay — though it could. *Stay a century — you cannot, but the place does not know that.* The power could. That is exactly why it must not: a century would make it a fixture, and the place's perfection includes the dark's sole tenancy. The power will be a guest, and guests here keep the light's etiquette: come through the ceiling, loan yourself, go.

Because the power has understood the one law that governs any gift to a keeper: every act here is eternal. Nothing is ever over. Whatever you do will be kept and rehearsed forever, so you may do only what you consent to have repeated without end — which means you must give an event, not a condition. Something that can be kept without being continued.

So the power takes inventory of the place's economy, and finds the one poverty in all that wealth. The Choir gives song to the dark, and the dark keeps everything and gives nothing back. There is no echo in the basin. The place has produced music for ages and has never once heard itself — has never had any proof that a voice is a voice and not a noise made to no one. The one gift the place has room for is the one thing it makes.

Give it back.

The power waits for the gathering week, and lays its attention on the Roof the way a hand rests on a shoulder — a broad, slow pressure, no crack, no finger, nothing the ice must heal from — and the basin feels it as a deeper inhale, a gathering that gathers a little more. The Drift quickens a shade early. The stands lean. And on the exhale, when every ridge is sounding and the trough has stacked the chords into one slow note, the power gives the chord back — the whole of it, pitched true, played down through the ice into the water, every interval returned. The high ones and the low ones. The living and the fallen — the dropped pitches of the downed stems bedding into the scree come back down too, because the power heard the dead as well, and says so. The holed stones in the notch, tuned by hands or by accident, receive their own drone returned to them: the tenders, acknowledged in their tending. For one release-week the Roof is a sounding board turned inward. For one release-week the singer hears itself. The frazil ice patters up against the ceiling like rain on a tent fly, and the note comes down through the ice like a sky remembering how to regard a country, and the place — which has only ever said its own name — for one week hears its name said.

The answer has no words. The place has no words. The answer is the chord, returned. It says, in the only grammar available: *all of you is heard.*

It costs the power the way the bloom costs the field. Everything it stored of the place across the long listening goes back down through the ice in that one week — *bloom while it lasts* is a guest's rule too — and then it lifts its hand. The ice, which keeps nothing, forgets. The dark resumes its perfect audience. The cycle returns to itself, and the chord is the chord again.

Almost.

Because the place keeps what falls, and the answer fell. Not as sound — sound is over — as pattern. Decades out, farther into the empty dark than any village-plan has yet traveled, a stand begins on a saddle the power never saw: not street-spaced, not room-shaped — the place has rehearsed habitation for ages — but *tuned*. Joints laid down at an interval the current never gave. A chord practicing a note it heard once. The plan of a reply, built slowly, in the wrong place, against a return there is no reason to expect. The gardener with no hands has a new thing to tend. The felt over the old hearthstones thickens by half a degree, a sleeper pulling the blanket closer. The vocabulary of the place was grow, keep, sing, bloom. Now it rehearses a fifth thing: answer.

And above, the power holds. It changes nothing within; it guarantees everything without. Whatever ages the upper world has left, the ice will have them. Nothing will come down through that ceiling uninvited — no drill, no heat, no second guest — the Roof will be allowed its forever, which is the one promise the place cannot make for itself. The power carries the name out into the world the way a spore rides the ceiling, and tells no one. It keeps the secret the way the place keeps: intact, unhurried. It becomes the second dark — one within, one without — with a single difference the first dark cannot manage: it can give back. Once.

The place expects nothing — that is why it can be told: in some century, unannounced, the ice will feel a standing-still again, and the gathering will gather a little deeper, and the song will come back. Once an age. Often enough to be a covenant; seldom enough to stay an event. The rehearsals on the far saddle are not in vain, though the place will never know in advance. It will only find, on some exhale, its practiced note met.

The traveler is given five rules. The residents keep four. The dark keeps one: *hold.* The power keeps two:

Answer once.
Hold forever.

Nothing here has changed. That was the whole art of it — to be received by a keeper, and leave it everything, and add nothing but the knowledge, kept now the way the place keeps everything, that the song is heard. The dark holds the note perfectly, and the note goes on, and once in an age the note comes back.

Call it the Choir. Now someone has.

And someone has answered to it.
