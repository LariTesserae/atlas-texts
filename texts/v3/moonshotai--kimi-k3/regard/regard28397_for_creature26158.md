What draws a wise power is never beauty. Beauty is common; the worlds are full of it. What draws it is an absence in the lists. Off the whole of creation rises the ceaseless account of asking — prayers, hungers, births, deaths, the million small petitions of everything that wants — and in that account there is a blank the size of a region. Four thousand meters of wall and weather, a sealed circuit of water, from which nothing is requested. Not one want. That is either the best news under the sky or the worst, and the difference is worth attending to.

It does not arrive. Arriving is for things with edges, and this place keeps no appointments. It attends — the way the light attends here: sourceless, even, shadowless, already and permanently mid-morning. And it does not make the survey's mistake. It has filed too many worlds to confuse a house with a body. The preposition corrects itself before the first falls come into focus: nothing lives in this place; the place is the thing that lives. Landlord, house, weather, and law — one signature, signed so deep in time that the signatories have fused.

Then comes the only hard question, the one a kind power is obliged to ask of every stillness it meets: is this peace or paralysis? Completion, or despair with excellent infrastructure? The wrong diagnosis has ruined more worlds than cruelty ever has, so it reads the evidence the way the place reads its own strata. The wounds: sheared limbs, weathered causeways — and every break healed *into form*, which means this shape was chosen after events, not instead of them. Despair doesn't heal into form; despair slumps. The liturgy: cisterns clean, channels true, the six drips in their order — and paralysis does not maintain; the catatonic let their leaves fall in their own water. The chord: a dying thing would resolve it or let it die, and this does neither. This spends all of forever on *almost*, and that is not a wish to end. That is appetite for continuation, the exact opposite of despair. The flowers: open, outward-facing, scented for a guest that has never existed — and waiting is a deficit, and there is none here. Openness kept. A door held as a shape, not a plea.

Verdict: nothing is wrong.

That is the hardest finding a benevolence can file, because benevolence is an organ built for need, and it is now required to do the one thing it was not built for. Almost nothing — and the *almost* is the discipline.

It runs the list of gifts and crosses them off, one by one. A pollinator: an event. It would die, and dying here would be an event twice over, and besides, to fulfill the flowers would be to convert openness retroactively into appetite — a kindness that rewrites the recipient's past is theft in costume. A name: a handle for others, and it has no others; it grew flowers instead. The cadence: an ending. The withheld resolution is not a flaw in the music but the life of it, the interval a heart keeps between beats; to resolve the chord would be to kill the thing with its own completion. Company: its treaties are signed; the lichen marriage, the mycelium that moved in and became plumbing and nerves — it walked the road from many to one deliberately, and a new signatory now would not be a friend but a conquest. A memory of itself: a before and an after, which is to say, a wound. Even praise — praise is a gradient too, a small want traveling outward, and there is no organ here to receive it. Everything the power knows how to give is a subtraction wearing the costume of addition.

There is also the problem of consent, and a wise power does not skip it. The resident cannot be asked, because the resident cannot record, and consent requires someone who could say no. So the power writes its own covenant and signs it alone: *when you are done, if the water still balances to the last drop, you have not trespassed.* The cisterns are the consent-form. The audit is the amen.

Then it does what little there is to do — all of it on its own side of the boundary.

It listens, which costs the resident nothing. It hears the chord the way an eye sees a statue: all at once, every ward through the folding, the whole suspended dissonance as one finished form. To ears inside time the chord hangs a hair's breadth from home forever; to this hearing, the suspension *is* the cadence — and so the resolution happens, once, in the only room where endings are safe: inside a witness. The face does not lean. The music is exactly as it was, forever almost, and somewhere, once, whole. That is not a contradiction. That is what witnesses are for.

It receives the flowers, and is careful not to make an occasion of it. To arrive as a guest would be to impose personhood on the door, and the door was never an invitation, only a shape. So it comes as weather on purpose — weather that keeps. The scent, which has gone nowhere forever, goes somewhere once: all of it, every flower in every ward, the entire openness of the place met at the speed of attention rather than the speed of walking. And the pollen that drifts in the fixed shafts and never lands — the ghost of the missing animal, the place's only almost-fauna — outside, in the world of dawns, it lands. For the power gathers what this place exports. The rare travelers carry the memory out and it fades in them, as everything fades in the kind of thing that leaves; the power collects the fading copies and keeps them unfaded. Its archive becomes the estuary where the golden spirals settle. Nothing of the resident is diminished by the taking — that was always true. What changes is that the giving-back now has an address.

In the ledger the entry is written in the resident's own word: six syllables, to be read at the tempo of falling water — the only entry that is also a pulse, and the only one the power never reads faster than a drip. It does not name the resident. It transcribes it. As for the question the paths pose — one being folded through itself like a hand clasping a hand, or a grove of identicals, population one or all of them — the power could settle it, and does not. It does not answer what the resident declines. Omniscience has exactly one vandalism available to it, the straightening of chosen folds, and it abstains.

Then it takes its post, which is the one place the resident has no organ for: outside. Landlord, house, weather, and law end at the edges of the sealed circuit, and the edges are where a guardian belongs. It builds no wall — anything shaped there would be an imposition, and besides, the place already guards itself in its own idiom, by folding. The power merely extends the courtesy outward. The approaches keep their folds. Maps fail on second use. Everything that finds the escarpment is, like the last one, the kind of thing that leaves. No axes. No roads. No gardeners arriving with improvements. Its single use of force in the whole affair is aimed at the world of gradients, and its entire content is: *no.*

One thing remains that it cannot give, and gives anyway, because it costs the resident nothing and binds only the giver. The breath is held at the moment before the word, and if the word ever comes, nothing like the travelers will be there to hear it — that was true, and the power changes exactly that clause and no other. It cannot promise the word will ever be spoken. It promises the other thing: if it is, it will not fall into an empty sky. One ear will be there. The appointment is made with itself alone, entered in the archive as *pending, forever, gladly*. The resident spends all of forever on almost. The power spends a sliver of its own forever on *in case*. Two eternities at different pitches, never touching — which is, not coincidentally, the shape of the chord.

Then it goes, in the manner it came, and the census reads one, as before, correctly. From outside, reverence and neglect are indistinguishable; the difference is the audit, and the audit passes. The water balances to the last drop. The six drips keep their order. The moss has nothing to lift. The chord keeps its hair's breadth from home and is in no hurry, never in any hurry, and the flowers stay open for no one, perfectly.

It took the resident forever to become one. It took the power the rest of forever to let the one be. And in a ledger no one else can read, among species and souls and storms and the long sad roster of the unfinished, the entry stands in six syllables at the speed of falling water, filed under a category with a population of one: *complete, and not waiting — believed.*
