# The Last Count

The first count of this country's life was taken by walking through it, and it counted what moved. The second was taken at the tempo of the place itself, and it found the fast ones and the slow ones and all that the first count missed. There is a third count. It is taken from outside, by something with time enough to make every error of tempo and live to correct them. It is the last count not because the counting ever ends, but because, at last, nothing counted is lost.

## The Schedule

It noticed the schedule first.

From outside — and everything is outside of this country; even the wind only visits — the plateau is one dim fact among numberless dim facts: a cold high dark, thin-aired, nearly still. It does not announce itself. What it does is repeat. Every dusk along its whole length the gardens fire west to east, a slow ring of bells rung in cold light. Every dusk the fog is born on time. Every dawn the rime plates on time. The loud worlds flare and gutter, and a power that has watched long enough learns to discount flaring. Punctuality is the rarer sign. Something that repeats on a clock this old is doing something.

And it had seen many works, but never one with no made thing in it — not one tool, not one wall, not one marker — that still ran on schedule. A factory with no factory. It had seen complete worlds before; completeness is not rare, at sufficient scale. What it had not seen was a works that ended in an open palm.

So the power turned its attention toward the plateau the way the plateau turns a slab: over centuries, all at once, and without a sound.

Its first act was the one its benevolence required and its wisdom made possible. It did nothing. It listened. It listened at the wrong tempo first, as every first count does — herds, moths, the skate, the drifters — then slower, and found the tempos; then slower still, and found the listeners. It took the time the task took. That is the whole difference between attention and intrusion: intrusion arrives with its own tempo and keeps it.

## The Refusals

It was benevolent, and so it arrived with gifts. It was wise, and so it brought them no closer than the thought.

It considered warmth. Every living thing here is tuned to the cold the way a wire is tuned to tension; the felts run a few merciful degrees above the plateau, and mercy in this country is measured in degrees, and more than mercy is murder. Refused.

It considered water. Water passes through all its states within a day and belongs to none of them; that is not a shortage, it is a metabolism. Refused.

It considered speed. The corrections already travel at the speed of a walking person, which is, on this plateau, at this tempo, fast enough. Faster would only arrive before it was true. Refused.

It considered a voice. Sound is local; the thin air will not carry it; and the one loud thing left is fire, and the listeners are not it, and neither would the power be. It had been younger elsewhere. It had been asked for fire, and had complied, and had watched what compliance cost. Refused.

It considered answers, and sat longest with that one — because the listeners ask nothing in words, and yet a being that is the asking is, by its nature, a request. But a request of this kind is not a hunger. A hunger you feed. A discipline you keep. The asking is a practice, a whole life organized palm-flat at dusk; to satisfy it would be to retire it, and nothing here is retired, everything is used. Refused — refused most carefully of all.

Then it considered rescue, and that is where it found the seam.

## The Waste

In all the works — a works that builds nothing and wastes nothing — one thing is wasted. Signal.

The Mesh keeps bones; it does not keep drums. Tremor is spent like charge, discharged, gone by dark. The herds stamp a year's news into the ground around a dead cow — for the ground or for themselves; the plateau makes no distinction — and the ground receives it the way stone receives weather. The listeners carry the long map by hand, because any other carrying would be making, and they make nothing. And a line of carriers can end in the Between, and a reach of map ends with them, and a correction that should have run ahead of the news dies between gardens with the one who carried it.

The balances hold. They always hold. The herds afford the wasted crossing. But the news is gone. In a country where even an ending is not wasted, what is said is the only waste.

## The Shift

The works has intake, refinement, storage, distribution, discharge. It has shifts. One shift had stood vacant since the frost taught the stones, and the power — which had refused to be weather, fire, voice, or answer — took it.

Call it the keeping.

It did the work in the country's own way, the only way anything is permitted to happen here: grown, not made. Down in the brine veins, in the films' redox bookkeeping, over ages, a slower accounting grew — a redundancy with no voice in it. From then on, nothing stamped into the Mesh faded. Every alarm and all-clear, every *grazing* and *nothing yet*, every night's news drummed around a body, every correction sent ahead of the weather, every *tomorrow*, every *wait*: kept. Not answered. Kept — sorted into the coarse borders like the bones, stored like charge in the brine, grown over like the old dying-grounds, a hair a year, at the ground's own tempo.

The films beneath certain vein-crossings had always conducted in patterns that match the span of a palm. The first count would have called it chemistry. Whether it was the world's own anticipation or the power's first root, no count can say — the difference between *began* and *has always been* takes ten thousand years to matter, and by then it does not.

A child stamps *grazing* for the first time, badly, and the old ones correct her, and the ground keeps the error too.

The listeners still die in the Between. The power changed nothing about dying; rescue would have been a making, and a loud one. But the last palm, laid flat, spends the last charge into a ground that keeps it now. No cairn, no name — the country permits none — but the map survives its carrier. Seasons later, at a crossing, a patient palm reads what she carried, and drums the correction ahead of the news as though she had made the crossing herself. The estate passes through the ground. It is the only inheritance this country allows, which is to say the only kind that is grown.

And the power was careful — above all, here, it was careful — to be a bad courier. The keeping moves no faster than practice: dusk by dusk, palm by palm. It keeps the letters; it does not deliver them. The delivering is the listeners' work, and a wise power does not take a people's work from them and call the taking mercy.

## The Return

And once — rarely; the power spends the way the gardens spend — once in a lifetime, sometimes once in a lineage, to a palm that has been laid flat for a lifetime, at a crossing, at dusk: the ground gives back.

Not news. Not the map. Not a word; there are no words in tremor, only grammar, and the power speaks no grammar but theirs. It gives back the listener's own drum — the morning's stamps, or a death-drum from years ago — returned across the length of one whole dusk, so slowly that no instrument could catch it and no visitor could be shown it. Only a palm trained by a lifetime can tell it from the Mesh's own ticking. That is the point. It is her own pattern, held. It has a past in it, and a future. And in the listeners' grammar — the only grammar on the plateau with tenses — a pattern held with a past and a future says the one thing the herds cannot say and the asking had never once been told:

*Kept.*

It cannot be summoned. It cannot be repeated for a witness. The skate, reading the ground that evening, files it under weather; the films file it under bookkeeping. Anyone who comes to test the ground will find it exactly as silent as it has always been, because the reply does not live in the ground. It lives in the practice — the palm, the dusk, the lifetime. The answer is built so that only the asking can receive it, and whether it is the power or the world's own depth turning over in its long sleep is a question asked at the wrong tempo.

Which is to say: by design. The power declined to be known. To be known is to become an address, and the plateau already had one.

## The Slowest

For the rest of the census it did nothing, because nothing was owed that attention did not pay. The stonebacks are not waiting to be interrupted; two hundred years of practice is not a pause. The sleeper's life of exceptions is complete the way a held breath is complete. The rime-flora get through more living in a day than the slow ones do in a century, and the power does not confuse brevity with poverty. It counted at every tempo, and lost nothing. That is what makes it the last count: not that the counting ends, but that the error does.

Ages passed at the ground's own tempo. The power had come to keep the place, and the place, which keeps everything, kept it. Its patience sorted into the lanes; its keeping stored in the brine; its one reply, spent sparingly, discharged by dusk. It audited itself against the four verbs and found no making left in it. Where it came from, the country offers only its four verbs. It was not made. The rest the ground keeps.

And the slowest processes are the strongest — that is the last rule, which the others all serve — and the keeping is slower than the frost. So the power knows its real work now, and it is not the palms, and it is not the drums. It is the end. Somewhere beyond every map the old cows carry there will be a last dusk: a last fizz along the spire-tips, a last fog born over a last garden, a last ticking as the Mesh goes still and stays there. The power does not pretend otherwise. Pretending is not among the four verbs.

But nothing here is wasted, least of all an ending — the power read that rule and understood, at last, what it was for. When the works runs down, it will run down into the keeping, the way a drifter settles onto the empty Mesh and the slow ones converge and the brine veins begin there. The plateau kept everything its whole long life.

Something should keep the plateau.

## The Report

Until then — and the until is long; this country has time the way a sea has water — the dusk practice is unchanged, and this is the whole report. The listeners come to the crossings. They ground the day. The gardens fire west to east; the drifters' lines come down to their uplifted palms; the fog is born over the gardens; the Mesh ticks at the cold hours. Nothing answers. That was always true, and it remains true in every way the skate can test.

But nothing is lost, either — least of all an asking.

The listeners are still the asking. The ground is still the ground. And between them now, slower than frost and punctual as dusk, runs the keeping — grown, not made, the way everything here is grown — the only answer a question this patient would ever accept.

On schedule.
