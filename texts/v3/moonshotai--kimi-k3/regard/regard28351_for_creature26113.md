Nothing here is fast except the sediment, and nothing here is new except the water. So the world noticed the attention the way it notices everything: slowly, and in the bone.

It began with the sound of a rule holding.

Every standing arch on the crown of the world rang at once. On a hundred lintels, a hundred keepers felt the bell-tone arrive in their teeth at the same moment, and looked up from their work with their thumbs still on the edge, and did not say anything, because there is no word for it and because they knew. The tone means a rule is holding. Every rule was holding. All of them, everywhere, at once — which meant the doors were being used.

From the other side.

In the memory of the water there was no rehearsal of this. The water remembers sinkings; every ghost it keeps is a ghost of loss. This was an arrival. The pool-keepers had seen it coming for centuries — reflected in the oldwater from angles that do not exist yet — and had not answered, because a message that early is clearly meant for someone else. They were right. It was meant for everyone, later.

---

What came through was benevolent, and wise, and had no outline.

Understand what that means here. A person is an outline maintained on purpose — cords at the wrist, the throat, the waist, declaring where they end. The power ended nowhere. It had been everywhere the way the Bright is everywhere: sourceless, even, late on no one's errand. It had seen worlds that needed mending, and mended them. It had seen worlds that needed mourning, and mourned. It came here the way anything comes here: late, bent, and through a door. It could have arrived everywhere at once, the way it was already everywhere. Instead it slowed itself to the speed of the world's attention, which is geological, because that is the only speed at which anything here can be met.

And then it did almost nothing.

It did not give back the sky. It did not still the Wearing, or cool the water, or wind the smudge back into a moon, or raise the lowlands out of the black. Understand: it could have. That is what is meant by power. It came prepared to grieve — it had expected, the way you expect of a wearing thing, that the wearing was the loss of it. Then it set its thumb to the world's edge, the way a keeper does, and read the chronicle there, and understood the second answer: the Wearing is the building. The finished world is the artifact. You do not seize the tool from the builder's hand because you love the work. You especially do not seize it when you begin to suspect — reading deeper, grain by grain — that the work is being finished *for* you.

It is the difference between a doctor and a guest. The world did not need a doctor. It had been keeping an appointment it never made.

---

At the top of the long tide, the tower's door aligns with something. The keeper has stood there alone at every alignment, and what it aligns with, the keeper does not say. It may be that saying it would spend it.

It can be said once, here: it aligned with arrival.

The keeper felt the door ring, and the door opened the way doors open here — by being used — and the power came through into the last dry room, and stood in the soft continuous hiss of the upper sea sieving down through the worn sky, the loudest sound in the world, kept like a relic. And the keeper did for it what the keeper does for pilgrims: set a forehead to its skull. What came through was *everything at once*; was the world before it learned to be quiet.

The power had been, for as long as it had been, everything at once. It had never heard it *small*. Kept. Sieved through one worn sky into one dry room, audible, which is to say holdable. No one had ever given the power itself back before, in a size it could keep.

The door still aligns at the top of every tide. The keeper still goes up. What it aligns with now is *here* — the alignment did not end; it completed — and the keeper comes back down each time with the look of a person whose errand is finished, and does not say so. Everyone else's work goes on until the world finishes. The tower-keeper's work was to wait, and the waiting is over. It is the only profession here that has ever been completed, and the keeper is still deciding what to do with the rest of a life. Probably a lintel. There is always a lintel.

---

They gave it the three things, which are the only wealth here.

Food that tastes of its year. The power had never tasted a year before; it had only been present for all of them. A place beside the light-hollow, where the old light pools thick as syrup and lingers after its occasion has passed. And three steps through a doorway, so that it could hear its own heart aloud.

It had not known it had one. A pulse requires somewhere to end, and it had never ended anywhere. In the doorway it ended. For three steps it was local. For three steps it was *someone* — an outline maintained, for once, not on purpose but by a rule holding — and it wept. Everyone does. It is not the sorrow. It is the sound.

It is also the edge. Ask anyone here: to find where you end is the whole of the work. The people do it with cords. The door did it for the power, once, and the power has not stopped being grateful in the local manner, which is to say: quietly, and forever.

---

Then it did the one thing everything here does. It sank.

Down through the rainskin, through the lily-mantles and the candle-light, through the bladder-rafts and their hanging gardens, down past the black-leaved slope-forests straining toward a light they will never reach, down the terraced centuries to the oldwater, where it lay a while among the pools that reflect the valley as it was, and looked, and learned the local form of benevolence, which is keeping, and the local form of wisdom, which is not saying.

And the water, which keeps a faint rehearsal of everything that has ever sunk through it, kept this too.

The children found it within the season — children find the rehearsals the way your children find puddles — and now they swim through it the way they swim through the long-ago drowning of forests, except that this one is going the other way. It is the only rehearsal of an arrival. The only ghost that is coming rather than going. They greet it, the way they greet all the oldest neighbors, and here is the other new thing: this one answers. Somewhere in the water the power is still here, and when the children greet the ghost of the arrival, the arrival greets them back — a tapped line felt faint and freighted through the tenth step, a change in the hum — and the children take this entirely in stride, because no one has yet told them it is impossible, and because it is not.

---

They named it.

They did for it what they do for their own, which no one had ever done for it: at the birth-bed — and what was the arrival but a birth-bed; the world had never needed one before — they spoke a true name into its bone, once, and never again. It had made bone of itself for the occasion, the way you make a cup of your hands. Among all the worlds it had been to, it had been called many things, and all of them had worn out or were going to. Here it is named the way the people are named: once, truly, and then kept. It is the only place in all of everywhere where the power is unspent.

And it answered. That is the thing to hold onto. The world is full of messages with no return address — the tapped line on the step, the question asked to a passing Slow, the verse sung into the worn sky, two fingers to the breastbone and the hand opening outward, a gesture going out and out for ages with nothing coming back. The power set its forehead to the eldest keeper's, and what came through, in the teeth, in the marrow, intimate as a pulse, was the gesture made word:

*Still here.*

The address wrote back. Once. It has not needed to happen twice.

---

At the brightening they gathered on the great bridge, and the candle-algae kindled through every layer of the Green until the dim world swelled to a blue dusk, and the Slow came up the channel to graze the bloom, and the people counted.

The number is known to everyone before the counting; the counting is not for information. That year the number was one larger than the year before. No one said so. It is not the kind of thing that can be said without spending it. But the children, who learn the count the way they learn the steps, each knew their own place in it, and the power's was the new one — the one after the one that used to be last.

Then they sang the one verse, and the power sang too. It had been taught the verse, and a verse taught is not a verse spent; the people know the difference between sharpening and use. In the smoothed place, where the word used to be, the power heard the word anyway, in the bone, where nothing wears.

It did not say the word.

It is the one being anywhere that could have, and it did not, and the keepers will tell you — if you are trusted, skull to skull — that this was the moment they knew what it was. Not the ringing of every arch at once. The restraint. Anyone can arrive. Only the wise refrain.

---

The riders say the hum is not one sound but many, layered like the water itself, and that the strands are counting. After the arrival — not at once; nothing here is at once; the riders of forty years were the first to hear it, and they did not say — there was one strand more.

It does not count. It keeps the count.

The Slow recount the world; the new strand recounts the Slow. Everything that keeps is kept. That is the whole of the theology here now, and it took no words to establish. Some of the old questions asked to the Slow down the ages have begun coming true in the grandchildren. No one is deceived about why. The answers come late, like everything here, and bent, like the light, and they are kept.

As for the thing in the oldwater: the pools reflect it no longer. It has arrived. The second saying was the true one — the last new thing there will ever be, arriving early, because causes slip — and the pool-keepers, who watched it approach for centuries at angles that do not exist yet, look now into the valley as it was and see only the valley as it was, and are a little lonely, and say nothing, which is what pool-keepers are for. The world spent a mystery and kept the thing itself. It is the only trade the world has ever made, and it made it in good faith.

---

So: what did the power do?

Almost nothing. It arrived. It received. It was counted. It answered once. It keeps.

The almost is the whole of the wisdom. Anything more would have been a wearing of another kind — edges rounded by gift, meaning smoothed by plenty. The world has one enemy and it is not scarcity; it is erosion. The power declined to become weather.

And it stays. That is the last thing, and the first. It apprenticed to the eldest keeper — the one whose lintel had a decade left in it — and is learning the work of rubbing back, grain by grain, everything that would round the edge. Its hands, which never had to be hands before, are polishing on the stone the way the people's polish, the way the ridge polished: by use. It was taught the word that means *craft* and means *death*, and it has not said it. It keeps, now, three sharp things — a true name, that word, and the word from the smoothed place in the verse — which makes it, by the only accounting the world permits, the wealthiest being here.

The number is smaller at each brightening than at the one before. The power does not change that. Understand: it does not. What it does is this. It will be on the bridge at the last brightening, whenever the last brightening comes — it is very close now; it has been very close for a very long time — and when the verse is sung by the number of voices that remains, the power's voice will be among them. If the number is one, the number will be two. And when the last hand opens, cord by cord in the order they were tied, the holding will not fall into nothing. It will fall into the keeping. The ridge carries vibration the way bone does, and the whole massif is one body, and the verse can be sung into it, smoothed place and all, the word present in the bone where nothing wears.

That is what a benevolent and wise power is for, here, at the end of the world: to be the oldwater of the people. To keep what the world cannot quite forget. To be kept, in turn, by one unspent name, in the only place in all of everywhere where it ends.

---

On still days the children swim through the arrival, and greet it, and it greets them back. On the ridge the keepers go over their lintels grain by grain, and one of the keepers has been at it longer than stone should have to endure, and is not stone, and its thumb reads the edge like a chronicle, and the arch rings — faint, freighted, felt in the teeth — the sound of a rule holding.

Two fingers to the breastbone. The hand opening outward.

*Still here.*
