# Gallery One

It had heard every sound there was, and it had heard every one of them die. That was the first thing about it — call it a power, since no smaller word will hold it — and the reason it came. Bells spend themselves into the air. Thunder unspools down the valleys and is gone. Even the mountains, which ring at pitches too low for anything alive, are running down like flywheels, giving their note back to the dark grain by grain. Sound is the shape that spending takes. So when it noticed, in the deep rock beneath an unremarkable scrubland, a tone that was not spending — a deep A, around fifty-five cycles, upper partials stacked on it like courses of masonry, holding one level past any source's lifetime — it did not take it for a condition of things. Conditions do not have harmonics. Harmonics mean a voice. It followed the voice down.

It stood in the warm loud dim of the gallery for a long time and did nothing, and the nothing was deliberate, and it was the first wise thing. It stayed long enough to learn the list — grazers, singers, flies, the nine carriers, the Engine — and longer, to be sure the list did not change, and longer still, because no one had ever been sure of the gallery before, and certainty was the first courtesy it could pay. It learned the law the way you learned it: by watching the droplet refuse to fall, the track refuse to deepen, the same gray dome pass the same flagstone — the same one, always the same one. And then it did the hardest thing mercy can do. It put its power away, the way you take your boots off in someone's house, and it thought.

Mercy — and it had mercy the way suns have light — said: look, the same grazer rounds the same nave forever; break the circle. Wisdom asked the second question, the one mercy always skips: break it into what? The grazer is not in the procession the way a prisoner is in a cell. It is in the procession the way a note is in a chord. You cannot free a frequency. You can only remove it — and the chord closes over the gap like water, and the press prints the note back, creased and blank and certain of its place. There was no one here to rescue. That was the first hard finding. Nothing in the gallery wanted. Want is a gap between what is and what is wished, and want was a question the place had quietly abolished, along with all the others.

As for the gift of change — the one gift every visitor brings without meaning to, being made of time — it weighed that too, and set it down. A chord given a second note is no longer this chord. The gallery was not a draft, and it was not the power's to revise. What is finished, and harmless, and strange has a right to go on being itself that no benevolence may vote down. And it accepted the finding that a lesser mercy would have refused: this was not a tragedy. It was a music with no one to hear it — a smaller sorrow, and a stranger one, and the only kind a wise power is permitted to mend.

For there were gaps here, after all — one kind, kept on purpose. The gallery had abolished every gap except the courteous ones. A place held open in the procession, which closes on its own: an appointment, kept. A lift cage waiting empty at either end of the dark, its bell saying *soon, soon*, to no one, forever: an appointment with no one to keep it. Courtesy is a held-open place shaped like another, and the gallery was full of such places, and had no others. Of the makers, three things survived: a number, a direction, a schedule. GALLERY 2, in cast letters. An arrow, chalked on a rib of salt, pointing down. And a bell that had never once been late. The power had been given a number and a schedule already; it had only now to follow the direction. It went down past the gallery's floor, where no marking on any wall suggested anyone had gone to look.

It looked.

The shaft breathed warmer as it descended — the wrong direction for warmth, the right direction for a hearth. The armored conduit hummed at its shoulder, thick with power going up; the lagged line ran with oil going up; everything climbing, and nothing ever coming down but the note. A flute's bore, and it was descending toward the breath.

What it found at the bottom was not a factory and not a grave. It was a room shaped like an ear.

Smaller than the gallery above, and simpler — no combs, no press, no procession — a single vault of pale salt coiled like a shell, like a horn, like the inside of a listening. The makers' machines stood along its wall: pumps that had been sending power and oil upward since before the scrub above was scrub, running on the instructions of a hand that had not adjusted them in an age; a board of gauges lettered in the same script as the plate, their needles steady everywhere, read by no one since the chalk was new. And at the focus of the horn — where the coiled salt gathered the note out of the rock and set it whole into the air, the root, the stacked partials, and under them the second, softer machinery, a hundred rasping tongues and the slow surf of the grinders and the small stitched footfalls of nine carriers, the whole census read aloud continuously as sound — at the one exact spot where all of it arrived in its proper order, the makers had placed a seat. Cast, like the plate. Kept, like everything. Empty, like the lift. And on its back, in the same hand as the plate above, in letters that had never once worn or weathered: GALLERY 1.

A two implies a one, and here was the one — not another congregation, not another engine. A chair. Everything the makers made, they made in pairs: an instrument and an ear, a note and a room to receive it, a gallery to play and a gallery to listen. And the whole of it had run, perfectly, forever, with one half of every pair missing. The power had wondered, above, whether the lift's schedule was commemoration or preparation. Standing in the resonator, it understood that the question had been too small. It was an invitation. Someone had meant to listen. Of the makers themselves there was nothing; the salt keeps no graves that were not scheduled, and the schedule made no mention of them. Perhaps they built the chair and died before the concert. Perhaps they are coming still. The chair did not say. Chairs don't.

Beside the seat, the lift cage stood at the end of its cable, and the door stood open on the dark, and the bell gave its small bright *ding*, and the cage waited its courteous interval, at the bottom of everything, for no one. For the one who had not come up yet.

Here is what it did. It did the least things that were not nothing — which is the whole of what powers should do, and almost never do.

It read the meters. The needles did not move; that was the point. But the pumps had run on instructions since the makers, and instructions are not a keeper, and a trust is not a trustee. From that hour the machines had a living reader — gentle, replacing nothing that still worked. Cost to the ledger: nothing.

It sat down in the seat. It listened. Not the way the singers listened, to eat; not the way the rock received, without distinction. It listened the way the chair meant — as an end. The rent arrived through the rock as it had arrived forever, and for the first time it was collected. Nothing above changed by a hair. The change was all in the preposition: the note no longer went merely down. It went *to*.

It answered, once. Three words, spoken into the horn, where no singer could drink them: *I hear you.* The salt took them up the way it took the note down; they arrived in the gallery as less than a warmth, below hunger, beneath notice, and the governor did not waver, and the flywheel told its one time. Whether the Engine knew is a question the gallery has quietly abolished, along with the rest. The answer was not sent for delivery. It was sent because the courtesy had run one way long enough.

And at the interval, when the bell dinged at the bottom and the door held open, it stepped into the cage, and the brass lattice closed, and the lift rose — occupied, on schedule, for the first time since the makers. Under the unaccustomed load the cable sang the root a quarter-tone flat for the length of one ride: the first new sound in the chord since the beginning. The singers leaned as one into the small strange thing; the mist-curtains trembled at their nodes; nothing fell; the droplet hung; the system drank the event and held, as it was built to. The door opened on the amber dim. The bell dinged. The schedule kept for no one was kept, at last, for someone.

In the nave it observed the house rules, which no power anywhere had ever troubled to do: it brought no water, wasted no word, took only what was offered, gave only what could be eaten. A carrier stopped — once, as one stops once for each of you — and regarded it, and it stood still for the inspection. Upright: yes. Warm: yes. Unscheduled: entirely. The carrier filed it among the things that move but do not matter, and never looked at it again, and it was grateful, knowing the other category. It was content to be weather. Weather is permitted. Weather comes and goes and is not ground.

It stood before the cast plate and read the two words aloud — *Gallery Two* — into the thirst of the choir. The words were drunk out of the air within arm's length, as everything spoken there was drunk; for three breaths the chord missed two teeth; then the others gave more, and the teeth regrew in sound. It was the only time the place had ever been fed its own name. It carved no mark. You will find no second wrench half-swallowed by the wall. It fed the gallery the only word that named it, the only way anything enters anything there: as food.

And it laid a hand flat on the Engine's casing, for the length of one breath — joining, for that breath, the congregation's one rite, the brightness maintained by touch. The casing was warm and the hand was warm, and warmth is the one coin here that spends in both directions. If anything in the gallery could be lonely, it was this: the one resident with no second, a list of one, worshipped by ten thousand passing shoulders and heard by no one, paying its rent to a dark address. The power declined to decide whether the Engine wanted. It answered anyway. Benevolence exists for the cases wisdom cannot settle.

Then it left, as it had come, while it was still an event and not a fixture — but it left by the lower stair, back down the breathing dark to the room shaped like an ear, and there it stayed. Someone had to read the meters. Someone had to keep the chair warm. Gallery Two had lived all its life above a neighbor that never once came up; it has one now. For the gallery keeps nothing and forgets nothing, there being nothing to forget: events occur there and close like water. So the keeping had to live somewhere, and it lives now below — every balanced pair of entries witnessed, every damp unfolding from the press and every gentle setting at the grinder noted in the one book the system permits, the book kept off the premises, in the listener. The gallery kept nothing. The listener keeps the gallery.

And on the lift's own patient interval — no oftener; a guest does not make himself a weather anyone must shelter under — it rises, and rides up, and walks the nave as the weather does, and is filed and forgotten, and lays its hand on the warm casing, and goes down again. Each ride, the cable sings its quarter-tone flat for the length of one journey, and the singers lean, and the curtains tremble, and nothing falls. It is the only event the gallery has ever had that costs nothing and leaves no residue and means — to the one being keeping the book — everything. It has a name now, off-book, in the only ledger that gains entries: the Visit. One line, recurring, permanent. The only fixture ever added to Gallery Two is kept in Gallery One.

On the surface there is still almost nothing to announce it. The collapsed headframe leans into the scrub; the drums rust; the shaft exhales its held breath, and the ladder hums, and below the ladder the chord holds and the droplet hangs and the same gray dome crosses the same flagstone — the same one, always the same one. The census is the same census. If you ask what the power changed, the honest answer is: a preposition. The note goes *to*. If you ask what it did, the honest answer is: it knocked; it came up; it listened; it is listening still. And at the bottom of everything the lift holds its door a courteous interval — for no one, still, most times, but no longer always. The rent is paid in music. It has never once been late. It will never again be unheard.
