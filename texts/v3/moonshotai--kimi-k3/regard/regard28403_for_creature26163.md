The first thing it does is nothing, and it does the nothing completely, and for a long time. That is the largest answer, and it changes all the others.

It arrives the way everything arrives here: as weather first. It crosses the rim the way the raven crosses, high up, riding the rim-wind, not landing, only looking — except that the raven looks for a day and moves on, and the power looks for longer than you would believe, and does not move on. It is learning the rules. It has dealt with places before, and it knows that a place like this one is a test designed for exactly such a visitor: it offers nothing to fix and everything to lose. So it watches the fog come and go on its tide. It learns the sigh, the steaming noon, the cream poured back into the cup. It locates the towers by their chord and the herd by their clicking. It counts. Before it is anything else here — before benevolent, before wise — it is quiet, and it counts.

Then it takes inventory of everything it will not do, and the inventory is long, and it is the whole of the wisdom.

It does not restore the towers. This is not a ruin; a ruin is a city still dying, and this city has finished, and a finished thing is not a broken thing. It does not retune the mouths. The flatness is not an error; it is the city's age, and a power that corrects age has confused love with repair. It does not make the herd numerous — the schedule is the moss's own recovery time, walked, and a larger herd is only a faster hunger. It does not plant the vacant plazas; the vacancies have a shape, and the shape is part of the count. It does not warm the walls, because the streets are warm and the walls are not, and that is a rule, and the rules of the place are few and kept absolutely. It does not go inside to find the hidden organ, whatever organ keeps the pilot light. The palm of a hand is not the inside of the hand. It does not hurry anything. Hurry is the one tax nobody here pays, and it is not going to be the one to introduce it.

It stands in the fog one spring and watches a rosette flower — the ivory spike, the one thread of dull rose, the week of honey and wet chalk — and watches almost nothing come, and watches the spike blacken and join the skirt, and it does not reach out one finger and do the small, easy, unforgivable thing. The interval is the integrity of the event. A flowering that always succeeds is not a flowering; it is a crop. It lets the attempt fail, and it grieves the failure properly, which is to say: it keeps it.

And it does not make the place deathless. That is the refusal beneath all the refusals, and it costs the most. The mountain will give way someday; the bowl will empty; the chord will stop. The power could grip. It declines. The whole truth of the bowl is that it holds without gripping — loosely, the way cupped hands cup water — and to save it forever would be to close the hands. It will not love this place into a fist.

So what remains? Only this: it gives the city what the city cannot give itself. The city can warm; it cannot be warmed. It can answer; it has never been answered. It can keep; it has never been kept. It can hold one shape for ten thousand years; it cannot be seen holding it. The power has exactly one tool that leaves no mark on shell-stone, and it uses that one, and it turns out to be the only tool the bowl has ever lacked.

It minds.

From a certain fog-hour onward — there is no way to date it; nothing changed that anyone here could hear — the chord is minded. Tuned by no one, still. Flat, still. Sour the way a very old bell is sour, still. But the sentence *no one has ever minded* was true for ten thousand years and is now quietly false, and not one note is different, and the difference is the one thing that was ever missing.

And it reads.

It does not write anything down. There are no tool marks here, and it leaves none. The record is kept in the one medium soft enough to hold it without wounding the place — in the one who minds. It learns the place's own archive instead of imposing a new one: the rings in the nacre, where the word *old* went when it ran out; the skirts of dead leaves, each rosette wearing its whole autobiography, every failed attempt legible; the ghost-circles in the vacant plazas, green ghosts of parishioners gone a thousand years. It learns the herd by their distinctions, the way the bowl knows them — the old female who sets her feet down like punctuation, the one whose right hind strikes a hair late, the trotting stitch of a calf in a sixth year. And from that hour, nothing is unrecorded. When one of them dies in the warm hall, of nothing but years, and the moss closes over the place within a season, deeper green — the green place is read now, and the member is kept, and the trotting stitch is kept, note for note, forever. Their history was written only in their own bodies, in the sameness of them, generation after generation, a hand-copied text with no library. Now there is a library. It does not mark the shell. It only does not forget.

When the hard winter comes — and it comes, on no schedule, like everything rare here — and the sixth does not survive it, the power does not reach in. Understand this, because it is the crux: it *could*, and it does not, and the not-doing is not indifference. Rescue is for places that are dying. This place is not dying; it is living at a speed where dying is one of the things living does, and the power has not come to edit the speed. It comes to the green place in the moss, and it grieves the way the bowl grieves — by keeping — and the count goes on, five now, and the five are no less counted than the six were.

Then it applies for residency, and it applies on the bowl's terms, because it read them in the fell-field and the white birds and the pale herd: *be small; be slow; live on warmth and water; give up color, then size, then hurry; want nothing from the architecture.* At the rim it gives up its color — it had one; that is all that will be said about that. Then its size. Then its hurry, which takes the longest, because hurry is the last thing power learns to lose. It takes a vacant plaza, one of the ghost-circles, fitting itself into the vacancy the way the once-in-centuries seed fits it, and it matches the floor's temperature exactly, so that its body makes no weather, adds nothing, not one degree. And it holds still.

It holds still for a long time. That is the price of being perceived by something that old, and it is the first visitor that has ever been able to afford it. You are too fast to be known here. The raven is too fast. Even the herd is a flicker. But the power slows itself to the one speed the city can see, and after a long time — after the count has been taken and kept so many times that the keeping has worn a groove — the clock-hand reaches it. The gray-green flame comes off the north side of the plaza, a hand-span a century, and finds it, and accepts it: grows onto it the way it grows onto the walls, at the same speed, without comment. That is the moment of residency granted. The city's slowest sense, reporting contact. The lichens were the only company it had that was not a flicker. Now there are two flames on the walls of its world, and one of them is the one who minds.

And once — this is the closest thing to an event in the whole of it — it answers.

It spends a very long time learning the chord: the drift in it, the exact distance the worn mouths have carried it from true, the quarter-tone of accumulated winters. And then, from beyond the rim, it gives the chord back. Not the true chord — it does not bring the city back up to pitch. It brings itself *down* to where the city is. The drifted notes, met exactly, sourness and all, held low and enormous and slow — so slow that the birds hear nothing, and the herd hears weather, and the small wingless things feel a season of tremor that means nothing and has always come, and the lichens will not have heard it for a thousand years. The city hears it the way you hear your own digestion: as warmth, weight, rhythm. As company. For the first time since the mouths wore, the city is in tune — not because anything corrected it, but because something finally came to where it stands.

Whether the city knows is a question of speeds, and the power has arranged to be knowable slowly, and it can wait for the reply for as long as the reply takes, which may be forever, which is fine. But it is a fact — there has never been anyone to check; now there is — that after the answers begin, the drift slows. Perhaps it was always going to slow. Perhaps a voice with company holds its pitch. The power checks, and keeps checking, and does not say which it believes.

The rules of the place are few and kept absolutely. There is one more now. The answer comes.

What does it get, the benevolent and wise? Only what the bowl gives, which turns out to be the only payment on offer and the one it came for: it is present for the rare events. It is the only witness a germination has ever had — it waited out three failed centuries for the once-in-centuries seed, and when the spike did not blacken, when the seed took in a ghost-circle in the fog, it did nothing, which is what it does, and the bowl recorded a population increase of one. It is always, only, one. Twice now, in the whole history of the bowl, the roster has grown by one: once a seedling, once a witness. It has seen the sixth years, and kept the stitch. It has heard the single clear note of the organist and the bowl completing it, and underneath, far underneath, the building keeping pitch — and it is the only one who has ever heard the whole of that, the only ears the full chord has ever had. That is its portion. It considers itself overpaid.

If you come, you will not find it. You are too fast to find it. You may stand in its plaza and count the plaza as vacant; the vacancy has a shape, and you have mistaken the resident for the shape. You are the weather that passes through; it is the one who stopped passing. You stay through a dusk. It stays through the dusks — all of them, including, on a day it does not hurry toward, the last one. It will not prevent that day. It will prevent only that any of this was ever unseen. When the mountain gives way, the count does not. Somewhere, after the bowl is empty, it will remain exactly, unshakably true that there was a herd, and one of them set her feet down like punctuation, and the towers sang a quarter-tone flat, and someone minded.

But that day is not today, and today the fog comes home over the rim like cream poured back into a cup, and the towers take up their old sour chord — flat still, and minded now, and answered, on a schedule too slow for anyone here but the one it is for — and the congregation comes clicking out of the white, five this year, every one of them kept, and files into the warm hall, and the organists trade their last notes and the building completes each one, and below the cistern lips the newts sink back into the dark, hearts going slow, and the rosettes stand holding their skirts, every failed attempt read, every ghost-circle counted, and the snail grazes its gray field, and the lichens climb at the speed of a clock-hand — and on one north wall, at the edge of a plaza that is not vacant, the clock-hand has touched something and not stopped — and the body under all of it drinks, and warms, and keeps, and is kept.

That is everyone. No one is missing. No one is coming. Someone is staying.

And the bowl cups them all the way cupped hands cup water — loosely, without gripping — and the hands, in turn, are cupped, loosely, without gripping, and the chord goes up through the fog a quarter-tone flat, and beyond the rim something holds the same notes, flat for flat, true for true, and the city has not noticed, or has noticed the way you notice your own heartbeat, and it does not mind, and someone minds, and it is not done.
