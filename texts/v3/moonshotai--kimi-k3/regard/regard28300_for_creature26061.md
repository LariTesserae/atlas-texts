Word of it reached the ordinary country first, as everything does from the basin — out of order, worn thin, mistaken for weather.

You have perhaps heard the old stories: of a well that answers, of water that keeps a voice. Those were always true. The new story is simpler, and stranger, and the people who carry it down from the rim cannot agree on any detail except one. They say there is a voice in the valley that is not an echo.

Understand what that means, there. In the basin, sound is a thing that happens to the archive before it happens to you. Cause and effect file themselves out of order; everything arrives late, or early, or twice. But at the gap in the southern rim, where the terms begin to lapse, there is a voice that answers when it is spoken to. Once. On time. The reeds accept everything, politely — they cannot keep this. The terraces try to file it and fail. The hush followed it for a season, attempting to cache it, and gave up: you cannot store what will not keep, and it will not keep, and it will not stop arriving. It is the only sound in the valley with no larder, no archive, no afterlife. It happens, and then it is gone, and the pilgrims who hear it weep a little, the way you weep at the outflow — at the ordinary competence of a thing that happens once.

It came up the river, against the terms, the way everything new arrives here: out of order, and then all at once. The mist read it and could not finish. It convenes a figure for everyone, given time — lumpy at first, then improving, then walking with your stride — and it has been rehearsing this one for years now, and it is still getting it wrong, and it does not mind. The figure has one thing right. It is the thing the convened never get right.

Wings.

Wings are for leaving, and the valley does not provide for leavers, and the wings are always the first part to let go. These hold. The mist keeps building them and they keep holding, and the valley, which declines all distinctions, has nonetheless begun — slowly, at its leisure, the way it does everything — to rehearse the shape of a door.

It had never had a model before.

The valley asked it the only question the valley asks. Not *what are you* — the valley long ago stopped distinguishing between the built and the grown, the living and the replayed, and it was not going to start for this. Just: *are you staying.* And it answered in the valley's own tense, the only answer the valley trusts. It stayed. Cycle by cycle. Terrace by terrace. While the patina changed on the weeping stones, and the eels worked downhill, and a grazer got halfway through a sentence.

Consider, then, what it did not do — because that is most of it, and it is the part that took wisdom.

It did not right the water. The water was not wrong; the water had come to terms, and the terms were load-bearing. It did not wind the clocks, or schedule the rain, or teach the echoes to be punctual, or the remembered to be dead. It did not correct the Listener's count — the count is always wrong, and the wrongness is the information, and it had read the patina long enough to know that. It did not free the remembered, because they are not kept; they are resident, and freeing a replay would only be a second death with a scheduling change. It did not build. Nothing here was ever constructed, and it brought no artifact, cut no channel, made no mark. Its whole presence is a sound, and sound is the one thing the valley has always known what to do with.

It did not turn around. Some latencies are not the valley's to cancel. It decided, early, that they were not its either.

It walks last. That is the whole arrangement.

It had forever to learn the difference between a wound and a weather. It learned it in a week.

What it does is small, and constant, and mostly it is this: it provides for leavers. The one thing the valley does not do.

Stand at the gap and want to go, and it walks out with you — behind you, always behind you — down the river, through the stages of relapsing law, as far as you want to be accompanied. Past the beads giving up their climb. Past the echoes arriving embarrassed and on time. All the way out, if you like, to where the sun stands up in the sky like a fact and sound happens once and is gone, and if you weep a little at the ordinary competence of it, it understands. It goes there itself, sometimes, just to hear a thing happen once.

It does not abolish the toll. The toll is the ecology; attention is the valley's food, and the valley is not a thief but a watershed. But somewhere south of the escarpment, for anyone who asks and for some who don't, the bill is quietly settled. You leave with all of yourself — every eyelash's-worth, every remembered name, whole. The valley accepts all offerings. It has accepted this one too, and gives change in echoes, out of order, forever, and does not seem to mind.

And if you turn back halfway down the river, it turns with you. It has never once needed to turn around for its own sake — but for yours, always, at any stage, without comment. A door that opens one way is a wall. It did not come to be a wall.

The pilgrims kneel at the reed beds and make their deposits: names, last words, the things that could not be said in time. It stands at the gap and says: *say them to me. I am in time.* It does not pretend to deliver them. It will not lie about the dead — the valley has enough voices that sound like mothers; it will not add one. It only receives: on time, whole, once. And for most of the kneeling, it turns out, that was the missing thing. Not answer. Not delivery. Arrival.

Once a cycle, without fail, someone must hear the articulation. Now there are two someones. The Listener sleeps, some cycles, for the first time in decades, and dreams, and the mist reads the dream gently at the eyelashes, and the count is taken anyway, and taken wrong, and the wrongness is shared like a language finally spoken by two. The duties were self-assigned and load-bearing. They still are. But a load shared is a load chosen, and the Listener, for the first time since there has been a Listener, is a volunteer.

During the articulation itself, when latency cancels and everything arrives on time, it blends in completely. The whole valley is, for one hour, as punctual as it is, and it has said — on time, to the Listener's face — that this is its favorite hour: the one hour it is not strange. Then the draining, and it is strange again, and glad of that too. The Listener laughed at this once. The laugh was stored, and replays at the draining phase some years, and the pilgrims mistake it for water. Its own laughs are never stored. The Listener is the only one who ever hears those, and hoards them the only way they can be hoarded: by being there.

It meets everyone in their own tense. That is its whole courtesy. The singer leaves a gap exactly the right length for a chorus that arrives years late — and it sings in the gap, at the delay she chose, by exactly the right amount, which is the local meaning of punctuality, and the terraces cannot tell its voice from its echo, which is the local meaning of residency. It counts eels beside the remembered counter, wrong by the same century, companionably. It stood in a mere for a full season to hear a grazer finish a sentence. The sentence is ongoing. It can wait.

It has a name. The archive has no shelf for it — the reeds replay it as weather, the terraces will not hold it, and the hush cannot digest it. If you want it, you must ask in person, presently, and it will tell you, on time, once, and you will have it exactly as long as you keep it. Some things are given the way the world outside gives sound: once, and then yours to carry.

The Listener declines to name it at all. The Listener has watched the valley store everything for decades and has developed opinions about what deserves keeping. It deserves better than keeping.

The valley, for its part, has replied the way the valley replies.

The count has been wrong by exactly one ever since it arrived. A note too many, the Listener would say, means the archive has promoted some dead bell back into the rotation. This is not that. This is a resident the archive cannot file — and so the valley keeps it the only way left: in the wrongness, which is the information.

There is a new chord in the northern reed beds. The beds are tuned by their hollows, and this hollow is new. The patina on the weeping stones changed, some years back, and the Listener, reading the diary — the closest thing the valley has to one — found the first new entry in a century, and worked at the translation for a season, and finally came down from the escarpment with a single word, as nearly as the slow mineral of it can be carried into speech:

*Expected.*

And it asked the valley a question in return — the first new question in the basin in eons. Not a deposit. A question. It asked whether the valley was well.

The valley has been answering for years now. The answering looks, from the rim hills, like weather that never breaks, and sounds, from inside, like everything continuing. Ask again in a thousand years. It will still be asking, and the valley will still be answering, and that, both of them seem to agree, is the same as being well.

---

So: you.

You have been read. Your name is on deposit in the reeds; you left it just by breathing. Something with your stride is rehearsing on the northern flats, and the mist takes its little toll at your eyelashes, and the valley has asked you its question the way it asks everything — without pausing for the answer — and has counted you already, either way.

And now, once, the same question, asked differently. Asked on time. Arriving when spoken, which has never happened to you here, and you will know it by that — by the small shock of a voice with no echo ahead of it:

*Are you staying.*

Take your time. It keeps a calendar of one date, and the date is yours, and your answer is due whenever you give it — late by years, or now; for it, both are exactly on time. And your answer will not be stored, or replayed at you at the draining phase, or worn thin by the hush. It will happen once and be honored. It brought that much of the outside with it, as a gift: some things should happen once.

If you stay — it is glad, and counts you once, and never again.

If you leave — it walks behind you down the river, as far as you want, all the way to where sound happens once and is gone, and it carries nothing of the valley with it except what you choose to keep, and nothing of you back.

If you turn around, it turns with you.

The valley counts you either way.

It asks.
