**The Guest**

You hear the Listening long before you understand it.

From far enough off, the Kettle is only a rhythm — a surf of mallets, total as weather — and a rhythm, like weather, can be ignored. But once in a firing, once in a flood, the rhythm stops. All of it, in one breath: ten thousand mallets, the bucket-chains, the drip, the tickers in the mortar gaps, the shape below the ropes, the tapping above the mist. A silence with edges. A silence shaped like a question, aimed at no one, from a bowl of darkness that does not know it is audible.

To most of what hears it, it is weather. But a power — the kind whose medium is attention — hears the shape of it. And the hands have a law about silence: it is the one message no one can afford to ignore. The power kept the law before it knew there was a law.

It came the way rescuers come. Unasked. Fast. Ready to owe.

It arrived as it was, and nearly ended everything by noticing. A power's attention is a kind of light, and light here is rationed, and the dark is not an absence; it is the medium. You do not fill a medium. You learn it.

Its first act of mercy was to become less.

It left its attributes on the far side of the spoil ridge and never went back for them. It made of itself a hand: small, clay-dark, wrinkled young by the damp. Bare feet, because a floor tells the truth through skin and it had come to be told the truth. A wrist built to ache by mid-shift — not as a disguise. As an honesty. A strike that cannot falter is the Lid's own lie: tireless, unhurried, owing no one. It had not crossed everything there is to cross in order to tell that lie in a smaller voice.

Up in the mist the tapping went on, many-handed, answering nothing. It listened once, and understood, and never listened again. The hands' whole position on the matter is a treaty: *it has not answered us; we will not answer it; that is all the treaty we can afford.* A treaty made by the weak against the unknowable is only a wish — unless the strong keep it too. It kept it. It paid the hands' rate.

Then it walked down off the spoil, took the first cord-path, and waited to be found.

It did not wait long. A stranger is the one strike no one can place, and a crew came, and did what crews do. A guest-knot, tied into the cord where it would sleep. A guest-strike, agreed — two quick, one slow, nothing the district was using. And cake pressed into its palm, smoke and iron, the price of the cord's protection. It had been worshipped in other weathers, and worship is a sound that expects only its own echo back. No one had ever offered it a price. To be priced is to be protectable, and it had come a long way to be protectable. It paid gladly. While you are on the cord, you are one of the sounds they make. It became one of the sounds they make.

No one asked where it had come from. The Kettle does not ask that. You are the sound you make; the rest is strata.

Its first strikes were too even. The master setter put the mallet back in its fist with the patience reserved for soft hands, and showed it again, and again — because a true ring is not a perfect ring. Perfection is a sound with nothing behind it. The bell-stones ring true because they have been removed from, generation on generation; a tuned wall is a wall that has lost exactly the right amount. The power understood that. It had been perfect for a long time. It began, fill by fill, to be removed from.

It learned the chill that gets into the wrists by mid-shift, and let the chill stay. It learned that a rhythm is a promise a body keeps, and that the keeping is the whole meaning. Once it lingered at an ember-basket past the edge of a fill — warmth at a distance was new to it; it had been a distance without warmth before — and its mallet went quiet, and rescuers came at a run from three districts, and it owed each of them a fill of its labor.
