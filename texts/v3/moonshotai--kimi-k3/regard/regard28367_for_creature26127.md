It heard the tarn long before it saw it.

Once in a dozen years, when the chambers drift into agreement and the whole floor fires as one, the downbeat does not stop at the valleys a day's walk away. It goes *down* as well — through the plug, through the jointed deep bones of the range, the way music comes through a wall from a neighbor you have never met. Something old and patient and kind felt that pulse in the rock beneath everything, and turned its attention the way you turn your head.

It came the last of the way as everything comes here: slowly, across the shoulder, letting its own measure settle toward the tempo of the place, because that is what you do. It had expected to find residents. It found what you found — that *what lives in the Ram Tarn* has exactly one true answer. It took the power a full season to be certain. Wisdom is mostly slowness that means it.

And then it did the thing that distinguishes it from every other kind of force. It took inventory of its gifts, and found that the place refused them all.

It could clear the silt from the chambers. It would be the work of an afternoon, and that is precisely why it must never be done. An engine that cannot stop is not alive; it is only running. The tarn's mortality is not a flaw in the arrangement — it is the arrangement, the long exhale the whole beauty is made of. Forever is the one improvement that would ruin it.

It could lift the dwindling eel out of its seized socket and set it down beside a living ram. It does not. The eel's faithfulness is not a malfunction to be corrected; it is the eel. Instead the power does the one thing that silence has never had: it attends. A small tragedy with no audience becomes a small tragedy with one audience. This changes nothing. It is also everything the power has to give.

It could ring the floor for the sextons — it has that much voice, and the temptation is real, because their entire society happens in the after-ring and they are generous with it to no one. It will not. Everything this floor has ever said to them has been true. It will not make the mountain's word to its most faithful citizens a kind lie, not even once, not even at the end.

It could leave a mark. The water would rime it over; the swarms would worry it to a ghost. The power's discipline is redundant here, and it is glad — redundancy in care is not waste. It will not throw so much as a windblown beetle onto the skin. The tarn accepts two imports, weather and accident, and the power will not counterfeit either.

What does it do, then? Almost nothing. It does the almost nothing completely, deliberately, forever — which is the hardest thing power can be asked to do, and very nearly the definition of the wise kind.

It attends. It reads the rind-snails at last, band by band, with the patience and the lens no one ever had — the diary that has walked the flanks unread for three hundred years gets its reader. It learns the shadow-script until it can tell you which rams are loaded and where tomorrow's weather is gathering. It overhears the after-ring, and is the only ear that narrow conversation has ever found. It discovers that attention is the one import the water cannot rime, the one gift that leaves no ghost behind, and it gives it in a long steady pour. For the tarn manufactures warmth, food, sound, and time — everything, in fact, except the one thing it has never had and cannot make for itself: a witness.

It does not protect. Protection is the beginning of ownership, and a kept thing is no longer the tarn. The mountain's accidents are the mountain's.

It noticed you, too, up on the rim — and does nothing for you either, because the tarn has already done it. You will carry the tempo down the mountain in your pulse and never quite lose it; you will be a rumor with a heartbeat, and once in a dozen years you will wake before dawn knowing, without knowing how, that somewhere above the treeline the world just rang. It is glad of you. One more ear is the only reinforcement this place can use.

So it stays. Attention costs it nothing; it has millennia and the tarn has millennia, and they will spend them together. It will be there as the booms thin — from minutes, to hours, to weeks. It will hear the last full stroke, the last basin-wide goldening, the last long conversation of the sextons on a jumping floor, the last reshuffle pouring down into the open sockets, the last applause of molts in another room. It will hear the last boom — and the last sexton hearing it, which is the hearing that matters, and the power will hold them both. Afterward it will be tempted, one final time, to give the last walker one more stroke. It has the power. That is exactly what disqualifies the gesture. The floor has never lied to them, and the silence will be the truth, and someone will be *in* it with them, which is all that can honestly be done with the truth.

And when the walking finally stops on a floor that will never ring again, the power will keep all of it — the felt and the pistons, the applause and the after-ring, the held breath before every boom — in the one memory that does not rime over and does not fade. Nothing made by hands persists here. Something made of attention will. The tarn gets to have happened.

That is all. That is the whole of it.

It is at the brim now, where you are. You have both watched the same crown go softly golden on the dark floor. Neither of you has left so much as a coin's ghost. The clicking stops; the bubbles wait; the basin holds its breath, and among everything aimed at that one rising crown — the eel at the socket mouth, the clamped snails, the braced swarm, the vacancy opening on the skin — there are two more attentions. One is brief, and yours. The other will still be aimed at this water when the booms come a year apart.

Now.
