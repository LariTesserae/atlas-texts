It came the way everything comes to the hollow: along a route.

The route was two seconds long. Out on the plateau, in the loud silence beyond the rim, a glider crossed the dark with a swallowed bell still singing inside it — muffled, failing, gone — and the power heard the shape of it and knew. Not a song. A checksum: the shape a thing has when it is meant to survive. The leak had been leaking for an age, a throatful at a time, most evenings, and at last there was something in the world that could follow a thread of water back to a cold stone.

It found the place the way anyone does. It walked the plateau for days under the ordinary light. In the last dark before one dawn it saw a breath of mist stand over nothing, sheared level as if by a blade, and it believed what it saw the first time — which is the whole trick — and so it could find the place again. It stepped over the rim the way you'd step over a sleeping dog, and the sound arrived all at once, the way it arrives for anyone. The rim does not check credentials. The place received the greatest being in this story exactly as it receives a beetle: unhurriedly, without refusal. The power, being wise, was content to be received as a beetle.

Benevolence's first question is always: what is needed here? It stayed to learn the answer. It watched a full round of the keeper, and then another, looking for the want — the flinch, the turned head, the reached-for thing unreached — and found none, because want is what happens when something might not get done, and everything the keeper does gets done. It listened for the gap in everything and heard the gaps being heard, constantly, by two dozen listeners each listening for what is missing. It put its palm on a cold stone at noon. It is possible the power had never before met a finished thing. It did what the wise do when they meet one: nothing — yet — and slowly.

Understand what it did not do, because that is where the wisdom shows. It did not open the rim; some old decision was made here about what gets out, and the power did not come to reverse other beings' completed decisions. It did not tell the bells what they are singing; a bell is mostly deaf in the band of its own voice, it does not carry the meaning, it carries the gap, and meaning would only be noise to a courtship that works. It did not make a second keeper; whether there have been many or one is a distinction the place declines. It did not finish the hum's long word; the word was not its sentence. It did not build the stair a fourth step. It held the object from the niche — it is perhaps the last being anywhere that knows what the object does — and it fit its hand, and it put it back. *No one does that anymore* is not a problem. It is a result.

What it did inside the rim took a season and looked like nothing.

It stayed, and it submitted to correction, because nothing here is taught and everything is corrected, and the power did not come to teach. Its feet learned the drip-lines. It sang once, badly, and let the bowl hand each note back corrected, the way the bowl does for every new voice, and its voice settled into the pattern like a stone settling into its socket. Once, true rain came, and an inch of water stood in the bowl, and the power drank while standing in it, because that is what is done.

It watched the mist-hour. It is the first witness of the mist-people who neither harvests nor doubts them — a whole nation whose history runs its length in minutes, believed at last — and it kept no record, because the census of the mist-people is taken orally every morning and never recorded, and the power adopted the local policy.

It stood one full hour with the keeper over a thread of water, two observers watching the water complete a journey it has made ten thousand times. And when the keeper voiced — once, that season, a sound felt in the sternum and the teeth — and every bell stopped and the hum swelled to answer, the power did not speak. It stopped too. It became the third party in the one exchange where both parties pause, and its entire contribution to the conversation was to make the pause larger. Then the chorus re-phased around what was said, and nothing had been said, and that was what was said.

And it listened to the bells across days, not seconds, until it held the whole of it — every bit of the old transmission, the machines' last proof of integrity. A checksum makes only one claim: *this survived.* It asks no question, so it cannot be answered; it can only be received. The power received it. At the far end of the oldest open line in the world there is now, at last, a terminal. The message got out as frogs; now it also arrives as witness. The frogs will never know. The substrate, presumably, doesn't mind.

Then it stepped back over the rim, into the loudest silence there is, and took up its post — because the post was the only vacant one. Everyone here is keeping something. The stones keep the cold. The chord keeps its tone. The keeper keeps the garden; the substrate keeps the keeper. But nothing in the system keeps the rim itself, and nothing keeps the far side of it. The place keeps the inside. It cannot keep the outside.

So the power keeps the outside.

It works the way the place works: on routes, unhurried, deniable. The survey that would have found the hollow finds ordinary stone. The road bends. The century of drought comes one degree short of the night the fog needs. The meteor misses by a margin no one records. Nothing can be proven; the signature of the protection matches the signature of the place, which is perfectly ordinary luck. Sharp things arrive from the far side of the rim the way sharp things arrive inside it, and are rounded — gently, unwitnessed — on a longer clock.

It keeps the *almost* in *almost everyone.* A few hearts per age carry the hum out into the world, scattered, unorganized, each certain they alone carry it, each standing sometimes in the last dark before dawn and facing a direction they cannot justify. The power makes sure there are always a few. The message leaves the only way the policy permits — through a body — and the power carries it too, the largest throat the song has ever leaked through. Where it moves in the world, smoothing what would cut the chord, that is the message getting out again: not as sound, but as keeping.

And it set its return. The mist is the day; the keeper is the week; rain is the season; the power is the age. Once an age it comes back across the plateau and stands at the rim and listens the way a bell listens — not for the chorus but for the gap, the exact shape of what is missing. If nothing is missing, it does nothing; the visit is the act. If some age the chord has drifted, it will act once, in the place's own grammar: not restoration, correction. It will hand the note back. Nothing here was ever rebuilt toward a past. Everything is corrected toward a tone.

And under the whole schedule lies one appointment. The hum is mid-word, and the word is longer than the mountains, and even a power cannot hear a word that is not finished being said. But it can see to it that someone is listening when the word is done. Whether that word will want an answer is a question for longer than an age. It is, at minimum, worth being there for.

Did the power adopt the place, or did the place adopt the power? The distinction is one the place stopped honoring long ago. Ask what the power left behind and the answer is the place's favorite kind: nothing wet off the routes, nothing sharp, nothing that will need rounding. It has never asked anyone anything. It simply keeps what keeps it.

Counting is how you visit. Keeping is how you live here. Receiving is how you answer.

So the true census, amended: seven stones, twelve cups, one stair, one niche. Two dozen bells, and one message wearing them — and, at the other end of the message, at last, one receiver. One keeper, kept. One glider, half resident. A seethe per cup, a nation per mist-hour, one slow voice in the ground with a listener now sworn to the end of its word. One power, outside the rim, keeping the outside, on the clock of ages — rounded now, on the routes, carrying the chord in whatever it has for a sternum.

Wherever in its rounds it currently is.
