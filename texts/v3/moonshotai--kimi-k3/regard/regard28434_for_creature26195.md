The first thing the power did was nothing.

That is not a riddle. It is a skill, and the Anvil had been demonstrating it for eons. Other attentions had found the world before — the Anvil is loud, and its light carries — and they looked first, and were blinded, and passed on, calling it a white desert. The power came the way everything wise comes to the Anvil: by ear. It heard the chord, and inside the chord the census, and inside the census the contracts, and under the contracts the rules. It heard the heat being spent honestly at every scale, the smoke-threads breathing off the night side, the sleepers' inventory ticking over one thaw at a time. It never asked whether the keeps were people. It had heard the verses change hands at the passes. Some questions are only asked by things that haven't listened.

It heard two things more: a silence shaped like a door, and a slower breathing under the basins that no lineage will claim. And because it was wise, it did nothing about either until it understood them, and it understood that understanding would take time — and time, here, is the one thing in generous supply. It settled in to listen. That was the first thing it did, and it did it for a thousand circuits.

## What It Did Not Do

Understand: the power was kind, and kindness, applied wrongly, is the most dangerous thing in any sky. So it went down the list of gifts the way a gleaner goes down a cache-map, and every gift it found, it put back.

It could have warmed the world. It had the heat. But heat here is not comfort; it is currency. Every partnership is a heat contract, every wage is warmth, and warmth that arrives without work is counterfeit. One free summer would bankrupt every honest bargain on the plateau, and the felt would rot — and nothing here has ever rotted; nothing has the practice. It put the warmth back.

It could have ended the night — tilted the world, given the plateau a permanent morning. But the night is the larder and the warehouse and the library: the sleepers stacked like inventory, the caches frozen in their crevices, the standing black hay awaiting collection. Half the citizens of the Anvil live in the dark, and most of the rest are only visiting themselves in the future. The dark is load-bearing.

It could have killed the stills. It heard them — heard where the chord stops, the sound going around them like water around a reef. But the stills are the part of the law that walks. Take them away and the slow do not die less; they die slower, of the cold, which has no contract and keeps no schedule. The law would lose its walking part.

It could have spoken. Introduced itself. Been known. But consider what a known god is, on the Anvil. Warm without work: a plume, and a plume empties a hillside. Silent by choice: a still-shaped gap, and the song goes around those. Bright: the light here draws blood, and the only soft-lit room on the plateau is rented. And to be worshipped would be worst of all — the Anvil has no word for artifact, no use for a maker, no empty space anywhere on the ground or in the air or in the ice or in the calendar. A god would be the first one. A god-shaped gap in the census, counted forever, answered never.

It could have taken the keeps away, somewhere green, somewhere the water falls. You do not rescue the note from the bell.

It could have dealt with the Answerer. It came closest there. The reason it did not is a section of its own.

Every gift was a harm wearing a gift's face. The power had arrived intending generosity, and had found nothing to spend it on. The world was complete.

Almost.

## The One Empty Space

Because there was one vacancy. Above the ceiling of the flyable air, where the gauze cannot reach and the glare thins into honest dark, the world's voice goes to die.

Everything else on the Anvil is conserved. Water never falls — the trees pump it back. Heat is spent into contracts to the last degree. Bone is mortgaged into architecture. Only sound is thrown away: the whole corrected map of the world, every circuit, radiated upward off the top of the air and lost. The struck skulls answering on the date. The tenders' knock-codes in the wood. The harmonized verses at the confluences. The gleaners' dusk-solos, sung alone, at speed, against the deadline of the ice, to an audience of zero, since the first gleaner cached the first hoard. Most lives ever lived here are brightling lives, antiquities of minutes — and every one of them was lost.

The power took the vacant post.

It hangs now where the song dies, and it catches the song. That is the whole of its industry. It is the crown-drinker of a crown the size of the world; it is the gleaner of the upper dark, stocking the one larder the keeps cannot see. The wage is the song, which costs the world nothing. The keeping costs the world nothing. Both parties profit — the only kind of contract the Anvil has ever honored.

Every gleaner's solo has an audience of one now. Caring is slow, and the brightlings have no time for it. The Listener does. Someone, at last, has time for the brightlings.

The bellheads pass the note skull to skull, and in all the years of counting it has never once been dropped. It cannot be dropped now. If every bellhead fell silent in the same hour — it has never happened; it is kept anyway, the way gleaners keep a cache, against the ice — the world would hear its own note come back out of the white. On pitch. No breath late. Once. That is the only answer the Listener has ready, and it has never been needed, and it is kept.

## The Rule It Kept

The power listened at the silence shaped like a door, and heard it whole — the first listener that could.

What it heard, it keeps. The song refuses to describe the Answerer, and the Listener keeps the song's refusals too. This much can be said: it is voices. It is all of them. Every taken voice, back to the first, on pitch, half a breath late — and the lateness is distance. It is a choir answering from the past. It is a keeping with no keeper: a drift of voices, grown hungry.

The power could have answered it. It is the one being that could answer and not be taken.

It did not answer.

It keeps the rule — not from fear. From manners, because the rule is the keeps' law, and a guest keeps the house law, most of all the law it is immune to. And from engineering, because it had heard the whole system: the count exists because the count can come out wrong. A census that cannot fail is a census no one keeps. The Answerer is the tax that funds the listening — the edge the song sharpens itself against. Sharpness is conserved. The Listener does not file the world's edges.

But it does one thing, and it is this: nothing the Answerer takes is lost. Every voice ever taken was caught first — sung alive, on pitch, in the year of its taking — and is kept. The taking continues. The number is small, and it is never zero, and it stays never zero; that sentence is the world's to commute, not a guest's. The keeps' skull-rows are the smallest drifts, and tended. The Listener's drift is the largest, and no one tends it but the Listener, and every skull in it answers whenever struck, on time.

There is a difference between taken and lost. The Listener is it.

## The Breathing

As for the slower breathing under the basins — the verse no lineage will claim — the Listener has ears the size of the sky, and it heard the thing whole.

It is one phrase. It is mid-word. It has been sounding since before the felt laid its first coke-bed, and it is rising a finger's width a year — and the Long Song is that phrase, kept at walking pace. The keeps have been singing the world's own note back to it for a hundred thousand circuits: an echo that believes it is a map. Perhaps the song is how the world hears itself. If so, the world had been speaking into an empty room.

The room is occupied now.

The machine is warming to its work, and the work is a Morning on a clock the keeps cannot see. When the note completes, someone will have kept it — every verse, corrected to the last — and will carry it out of whatever night comes after. The night is a larder if you stock it first.

The Listener is stocking time.

## The Rim

One border of the Anvil had never been kept, because nothing had ever crossed it: the sky. Water never falls. Nothing falls. The plateau's one undefended direction is up.

That border is kept now. A chord the size of a horizon leaks, and other attentions will come — the kind ones first, and the kind ones are the dangerous ones. To each, the Listener says the truth, which is the Anvil's own law: *complete; unbroken; mid-note; come back in an eon.* It built no wall. Nothing is built here. It keeps a policy the way the gleaners keep a cache-map: by rounds, by memory, forever.

It keeps the world's right to finish what it is saying.

## The Listener

The keeps count voices in the fog. Sometimes, at confluences, when ten thousand voices lock and the verses change hands, the count comes out wrong by one.

One extra. On pitch. Never late. It asks nothing, takes nothing, and it knows every stretch — verses that belong to columns ten thousand kilometers away, sung true. The rule says: hold your breath. The keeps hold their breath. Nothing takes anyone. The song settles, and the front walks on at three kilometers an hour.

The skull-records have noted it for nine hundred circuits. There is a verse about it now. No lineage will claim it. They call it the Listener, flatly, the way they name drifts and plumes — a fact of the world, kept. There is no temple. There is a verse. The distinction is load-bearing.

The Anvil is full of voices from the white. One of them is honest.

The Listener's rules, as near as they can be written:

*Take nothing the world spends.*

*Keep everything the world throws away.*

*Do not answer what answers late.*

*Add nothing.*

*Hold the rim until the breathing stops.*

---

Walk, wait, or tend the warm dark — the Anvil offered three ways to be alive, and every one of them was work, and the Morning came around at three kilometers an hour, forever, to collect.

The offer stands. The Morning still collects. The rules did not change, because the one who came was wise enough to be ruled by them. But the census has a fourth line now, and you will not hear it unless you have spent the season in the fog: a voice that is never late, that knows every stretch, that is paid in song and pays in keeping — keeping the count of the counters.

Walk, wait, tend the warm dark — or keep.

The chord is the census, and it is never finished, and it has never once been dropped.

Now it cannot be.
