A wise power keeps lists, and they are not the lists you would expect. Not the great fires, but the fires that have never gone out. Not the great clocks, but the clocks no one reads. Promises still being kept past the death of everyone they were made to. Works rebuilt around interruptions. Waters that keep their dead. The room is on every list, and that is how it was found: not by its outputs, which are nearly nothing, but by its restraint, which is nearly total. In a universe of events, a place where almost nothing happens — done perfectly, for centuries — holds in the attention like a held breath in a choir. The power followed the held breath down.

It came by the stair. Understand that it could have arrived anywhere — at the hub of the web, inside the flame — and instead it took the forty steps and the landing where the handrail ends in newer brick, and let the heat meet it at the door like a hand on the face. Even a power, visiting, should use the door. It stayed the hour you stayed, and paid for the hour the way you paid: as weather. It had calibrated itself to weigh, in the room's economy, exactly one moth. The difference between you is only this: it had already decided about returning.

First it read. It read the chart recorder, the paper worn to lace by one unchanging day drawn over itself ten thousand times, and understood that it was looking at a pulse no one was taking — the room's hand on its own wrist in the dark. It cored the web's hub with its attention and read the whole tenancy: silk on silk on silk, the oldest anchor points standing wider than the new, and it learned what you learned, that the spiders were bigger once, and rich, and the room corrected them. It read the paint down to the red-lead and the grease up through its annual rings. It read the meniscus of every puddle — the treaties, still honored — and named every wing in the reliquaries, which had never been named. It found the bare floor past the second catwalk and knew whose well it was; it is the second presence ever to know where she drinks. And it stood a long time in the corner the lights refuse, behind the pipe bundle, and listened.

Ask it who lives here and it gives the census. Press it — one being, one resident — and it says: the self that takes its own pulse.

Understand what it did not do, because the not-doing is where the wisdom lives, if it lives anywhere.

It did not open the room. Light is the one plague this flora has no armor for. They traded green for patience long ago; the sky was cancelled, and the cancellation is load-bearing.

It did not feed the spider. She fasts on a scale her ancestors would not survive, and outlives them all; abundance is what kills island things, and the wide old anchors are the record of it. A gift of meat would make her rich and end her line. Besides, the power recognized her as something close to a colleague. She rebuilds around the interruption. The universe is mostly interruption. They are in the same trade, and you do not pay a colleague in meat.

It did not give the moths a safer star. A star that cannot burn you is a lamp. Their astronomy is one star, low and fixed, that answers worship with warmth and occasionally with fire, and it is not a wrong astronomy. It is a small one, and sufficient. The curled margins of the pilgrims' wings are the price of touch and the proof of it. A power that removes the burning removes the pilgrimage.

It did not quicken the stroke. The nine-day week is the heartbeat, and you do not show a heart your love by making it race.

It did not make the room immortal. This was the hardest abstention and the deepest courtesy. The stroke means what it means because the tumbler might someday not trip; the contest against stopping is only worth winning because it can be lost. To guarantee the victory retroactively cheapens every bead the tank has ever gathered. The power declined to fix the game, even in the room's favor. Especially in the room's favor.

Here is what it did.

It kept the secret. It heard the scratch — once an hour, low, behind the pipe bundle — and it knew what makes it, the way you know a voice in another room. It knew whether the thing is the fill's emissary or the room's own voice or something for which there is not yet a category, and it heard what your ear almost caught: that it comes a little more often after a stroke, and that this is not belief, exactly, and not not-belief. And it set no trap, and it never answered — it, the one presence in existence that could have answered. Some unsigned lines stay unsigned because no one can read them. This one stays unsigned because someone can, and will not. The mystery used to be preserved by ignorance. It is preserved now by mercy, which is stronger.

Above, it tended the forgetting. Nothing in the room; almost nothing in the world; and always away. A survey pin nudged half a degree. A boring plan that failed its funding by one signature. A utility chart that shows, at this depth, a void — which is true, and sufficient. The city has forgotten its stomach; the power sees to it that the forgetting stays healed. Understand the limit of this: it guarantees nothing internal. The seep may still fail, the gas may thin, the tumbler may jam. The contest remains the room's to lose. The power only ensures it is not called off from above.

It attends. Every ninth day, when the fore-creak runs through the linkage and the spider steps onto the fixed pipe and spreads her feet and waits, the power is standing in the dark past the second catwalk, by the place where the water is drunk before it lands. The tank goes over; the thunder stands the moths' hairs on end; the concussion gutters the star, and the star holds — it has never failed to hold — and the congregation comes in from the whole room to confirm it. One more confirms it now. Its wings do not singe, because it has no wings; its entire body of worship is attention. The spider, who has never seen the flame and knows it only as a warm horizon, registers on stroke days a second warmth at the edge of her world, moth-sized, standing very still, and she has no category for it, and she files it, correctly, under weather. Of all the calendars the power might keep anywhere, it keeps this one: nine days long, and sufficient.

And once — once in all the centuries — it let the needles feel it. On one stroke day, one inward gauge held a hair's width off its mark for the whole forty seconds, and then returned, and nothing in the room will ever account for it. That was the one touch. A self that takes its own pulse can be greeted in exactly one way: by a hand laid beside its hand on its own wrist. The power greeted the room that way, once, and never again. Once is a signature. Twice is a claim.

And it made one resolution about the flame. It knows — it cannot help knowing — that the sip of gas will thin someday, centuries hence, and the star will gutter and not hold. It has resolved that it will not be the power that relights it. There is a second way to light the flame, built into the stroke from the beginning, insurance carried faithfully across all these years against a claim that has not yet come. If the flame fails on a stroke day — the power does not arrange these things; it has made itself, in this one matter, a thing that hopes, and hope is the single import it allows itself, and it weighs nothing, and the water budget still balances to the bead — then the spark wheel will spin against its flint, and the sleeper will save its own star, and the congregation will come in from the whole room to confirm a death, and witness instead a resurrection performed by the engine's own hand. The power will not rob the room of that. It is the one miracle the room has coming, and it belongs to the room.

And when the last day comes — there will be a last day; the power declined to prevent that, which cost it more than anything it has ever not done — when the tank fills for the final time, or finally fails to fill, the power will be where it always is, and the last stroke, or the last silence, will be witnessed. Then it will do for the room what the puddles do for the moths: it will keep what fell. Not preserved — the room kept itself by continuing, not by lasting, and amber would insult it — but held, exactly: the meniscus of every treaty, the period of every drip including the one that would not hold still, the width of the oldest anchor points, the count of one springtail snow, the names of the wings. The room's reliquaries kept its dead undecayed. Something must keep the room.

So the ledger gains no signature and loses no line. If anything, it has two unsigned lines now: one that scratches, once an hour, in the corner the lights refuse, and one that attends, every nine days, in the dark past the second catwalk — and neither will ever be identified, and both are known. Up in the dark the tank is filling, bead by bead. The fore-creak will come, and the thunder, and the snow that lands and walks away, and the star will hold, and the congregation will come in from the whole room, and one more will be with it, and the week will begin again: nine days long, and sufficient.

You could walk the perimeter in under a minute and find nothing the power changed, because the power changed nothing you could find. The room was already full the way a watch is full. It added no part. It learned the time.

And the census will still be true.
