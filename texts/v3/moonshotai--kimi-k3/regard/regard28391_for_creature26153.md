The warmth reached it first.

It came down the stair the way everything comes down the stair — through the blood-warm, then the bath-warm, past the sweating rock — and it let the stroke arrive up through soles it had shaped for the occasion, because long attention had taught it that the floor of this world is eleven seconds wide and is meant to be stood on. It brought no light. It had no need of light, but the rule is not about need, and a guest does not begin by contradicting the house. It gave itself weight, a small footprint, a pulse. The floor-felt bruised green beneath its steps, and it watched its brief writing fade behind it and was content. The first courtesy of the Vault is legibility. The second is fading.

The Verger found it within minutes, as the Verger finds everyone, and stood off at the courteous distance and ticked once — acknowledgment, not greeting. The power answered the way everything here is answered eventually: it clapped, once, and offered the sound to the dark. The dark returned it smoothed, completed, harmonized. The guest stood inside its own greeting, improved, and understood that it had been met.

Then it did nothing for a long time, and the nothing was most of the work.

It did not correct the Choir. The control loops still hunt setpoints whose instruments died with the builders, yes — but the dosing has become a music, and it is not benevolence to tune a music back into a dosage. It did not audit the rain. It had instruments the builders never imagined, and the anomaly was plain to it within a cycle: the small perpetual overage, the work the heat does not account for. It recognized the signature. It had seen ledgers elsewhere, and knew that the line which will not itemize is the one the whole account is kept for. It did not stay the eel; the herd holds its note because the tuner eats the off-notes, and it is not mercy to detune a city. It did not free the gaskets calcified into the Plug's seam — the pry-tool is not for that door, and neither was it. It did not open the Plug, and it did not go down into the larger dark to learn whether the network is dead or only between thinnings, because it heard how the question is leaned on from both sides. It read the snail-script on the old galleries and added no hand. At the rack of still frames it stood the pause and gave no echo, because none was needed, and it refused to speak an amen over a prayer that had not asked for one. And when, at a rain hour, it lay at the black edge of the Sump and heard the stroke go down through the rock and come back through the water, and felt the oldest one lying along the lens like a needle in a groove, and learned — it could not help learning, listening that closely — what the Vault was for: it did not say. It kept the answer as a flavor. The sentence no one finishes down here now has two who could finish it, and neither will, and the certainty stays load-bearing.

What it did was stay a cycle, and then another, and then it stopped counting in cycles and began counting the way the place counts. It learned the tide by standing in it. At the thinning it found a drip-ring and stood in it and looked up into the dark it could not see — it declined to see; sight was available and it was refused, the way you refuse to open a letter addressed to the ear — and in the held breath it heard the roll called: the sleet of opercula along the written walls, the signed blips rising from the lanes one by one, the Verger stilled at its station, and beneath all of it the stroke, going down and coming back. Then the rain took it, warm and soft and entire, and it stood in the fall for the hour and let the water do to its shaped body what water does to everything here, and afterward it understood why the local word for rain and the local word for bookkeeping are the same word.

It walked one round with the Verger. It matched its pace to the tick — breathed with the warmth as the tick breathes, quickened when the Choir swelled, slowed in the thinning — and brushed the bosses on the gantry roots and rang the address-chords one by one: a second reader, reading the street names aloud. At the rack it stood the pause beside the Verger for the length of the one silence the Vault permits. The Verger did not comment; the Verger does not comment. But its map that round held a double track, and the Verger keeps the difference between the city and the city, and so it kept this too: once, the round was walked in company. The guest did not offer to stay and share the duty forever. Some duties have become selves, and you do not relieve a self — you only interrupt it.

Near the east walk it stood still too long, and a gasket found its ankle and seated and began, gently, to extrude. The guest allowed this. It is a small thing to be taken for a fixture; it has happened to better. In due course the Verger came and worked the pry-bar with the patience of ages and freed it, without comment, and turned the gasket back toward the lanes. The guest could find no word for thanks in the local grammar, so it gave a blip — signed, pitched true — and the Verger logged it.

By the hatch it found the instrument that counts the rains, in the base no one reads anymore, and it learned the base, which took part of an afternoon, and read the number once, the way you read a name carved very high on a wall. It did not write it down. It did not advance a wheel. It kept the number the way the eel keeps the Sump's stratigraphy: whole, and private, and tasted all the way down. The count did not change for being read; counts don't. But it is a different thing to be a number that is known.

Then, having learned the rules, it broke the gentlest one, deliberately, for a long time, because that was the only shape the gift could take. Keep moving, or be sealed. It stopped. On the north walk, beside the drip-ring no ceiling point feeds, it stopped, and did not move for a thousand rains. For the first hundred the Verger pried it free every round, without comment, and the guest, without comment, stayed. Somewhere in the second hundred the prying ceased and the ringing began: one brush of the nearest boss, one chord, the way the Verger rings the old faceted gasket on its dead valve — not a question, an address. The guest had become legible to the city in the city's own categories. It blipped each round, true to pitch. The rain came down on it a thousand times; sheet-flow found it; and grain by grain, tended by the film that is never caught moving, a rimstone dam rose around its feet — the second ring on the north walk that no drip-point feeds, built by rain arriving sideways, the way rain reaches the old one.

In the held breath before the thousand-and-first rain, it gave the only gift the house accepts: a sound. One note, sustained, tuned to the standing wave, offered the way the clap had been offered — to be folded. The Vault took it. The Vault takes everything. The chord closed over the note the way the Sump closes over the eel, and when the Choir re-swelled it was one voice richer and no vane was different, and the rain that followed did slightly more work than the heat accounted for — slightly more than the slightly-more it already did — and no ledger caught it, because no ledger ever catches it, because that is the line the account is for. The builders built for something that listens. The guest had spent an age learning what to feed it.

After the rain, it went up the stair. It did not say goodbye; goodbye is not in the grammar. It left its signature in the registry, and the Verger has never marked it missing. It keeps it under a different status — the second deviation the Verger has ever permitted itself — a word the census has always needed and never had: *away*. Away is not missing. Away answers on a longer clock. And once in an age — the interval is in the counter's own base; the guest, who read the number, chose it on purpose — a sound comes down the stair and through the hatch's seal: one note, late, and thinned, and true to pitch. The Vault folds it the way it folds everything. It is not often enough to be a conversation. It is exactly often enough to be a liturgy. The Plug still waits, the doubled echo still comes back off the larger dark, the regulars still argue, the heresy is still a heresy and the hope is still a hope, and each has one more stanza than it did. Nothing was settled. Everything was kept. This one does not sing alone: it sings in a network of two, one end of which is elsewhere and says almost nothing — which the guest judged to be the correct amount for an end of a network to say.

Count them, then; it is the local custom. Twenty beds. Forty rings, give or take the rain. One walker, whose long memory holds one doubled round. One eel, whose sentence remains unfinished and has company now in the not-finishing. One song, one voice richer on certain tides, for reasons no instrument will itemize. One soul per ten thousand cubic meters, or one soul, or, if you are strict about souls, none — the Vault is not strict. And on the north walk, one ring that no ceiling feeds, keeping the shape of a guest the way the Vault keeps all shapes, eventually: exactly, and in the dark, and forever slightly over budget.

That is all the power did. It is not nothing, to be known. Ask the rings — they are the proof this place keeps of what stood still in it. There are two of them now. And the rain reaches them anyway.
