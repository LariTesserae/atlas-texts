Ask the returned whether the Long Morning has ever been noticed, and they will not answer on your first climb. Come down. Give your levels. Take the hand of whoever's mother is up there. Go up again, and at the bottom, the second time, one of them will pour the water and tell you the second lore. The census has a sixth entry.

Every long-stayer asks the question eventually. You ask it alone, probably at the pan's edge, among the tongues: if anything with the power to change this place has ever looked at it, why does it stand? You mean it as proof that nothing is watching. The returned take it the other way. It stands, they say, because it was watched — once, completely, by something that had the power to change it and the wisdom to read it first. They cannot prove this. They reason from the record, and from the bird.

Reason from the bird. A hundred grams of held morning, carried past the notch, dead of the backlog within the hour. Now scale it. The plateau holds ten thousand years of backlog, and all of it is hungry. To restart the morning is to serve the meal the appetites are seated for, and the meal is poisoned: the lickers would receive the salt and the bill in the same instant, the cat's teeth and the ending of the world together, the old woman's turn completing into dust. The one mercy of the place is that the hungers never arrive. Any hand that served them would be the cat.

It did not move the calf. The returned are as sure of this as they are of anything, because the stance is there: deep-pressed, close enough to see the eye, far enough that intervening was a walk it had to choose. It made the walk. It stood. Then it walked on, and the walking-on is written plainer than the standing — the stride lengthening away, unhurried, nothing dragged, no horns displaced. Everyone's first mercy, declined by the one visitor who could have afforded it. If no one is home, moving the cat builds a diorama of your own kindness and charges the calf for the materials. If someone is home, you have edited an eternity without asking. The paths were right. The paths were the answer before the question arrived.

It did not fill the bowls. A gift that costs the giver nothing enters the ledger at its true value, and the true value of free water is the end of restraint's meaning. The bowls record everyone's thirst and everyone's restraint; they were never thirsty, and they do not record its generosity — with one exception, and the exception is the whole teaching. There is a house days off the corridors that had wanted carrying longer than anyone could remember. The line goes out to it, pressed deep going in, light coming out, and the next traveler to reach it found the bowl full, and no one below had carried. It paid for the fullness it left. That, the returned say, is the entire theology in one bowl: it took the terms.

It did not hang a night. Any addition stays forever; that is the first rule, and stars here would not decorate the morning but end it, because the hour is the thing that holds. You cannot give a held thing anything by addition — everything you add, it must hold too. The residents miss nothing; the missing belongs to the living, and the living can go down. It declined to evict ten thousand tenants for the comfort of the guests.

It did not say whether anyone is home. The visiting runs on that question; the hand-taking runs on it; the Last Climb runs on it. A fact would close those customs the way night would close the morning. Some questions hold more up than answers do. It left the question standing.

And it did not address the hour. The returned insist on this one, because it is the one a lesser power would have gotten wrong. The hour is not a being. There is no captor here to petition, no will to persuade, no terms to renegotiate — you cannot liberate anyone from an event. Most powers have one tool, and it is change, and change is the one thing this place cannot receive and live, so the usual benevolences would have ended here as vandalism with a good heart. What noticed the plateau read it as a place, not a problem, and dealt with the residents as residents. That is the first courtesy, and the one most visitors skip.

What is left to a power that has declined everything that makes powers feel like powers? What is left is what is left to everyone: the corridors, the bowls, the customs, the walk. It went up as a guest. The returned call it the Long Walker — the naming-school refused, naming is claiming, and lost, because the returned are only human about their own rules.

They found the line in pieces over generations: a long-stayer's note about an odd stride out by the Whalebacks, a carrier's report of a draw no one claimed, in a span when no one admits to being up. They assembled it the way the plateau assembles everything — slowly, exactly, forever. It begins at the notch, where everyone begins, with a rest-stance where everyone first stands still and hears themselves be loud. It accepted loudness, the returned say. From there it walks every corridor. Not most. Every. It stops at every tally-house and drinks a traveler's share — never zero, never more — and the sum of the unclaimed draws across the whole transit is less than a body can walk that far on. It drank little, impossibly, the way you keep a promise to someone who will never check. It lit no fires; there is no ash anywhere along the line. And it slept. The salt shows where it lay down under the standing sun, and the returned point to those places when they teach, because sleep is the proof of guesthood: the coal burned. Stillness cost it. That is how they know it was really there, in the only way the place permits anything to be really there. It paid.

Where the line passes a Stayed person — all of them, the boot-style centuries, the steel blade, the ones who stood themselves flank to flank with the herds — the stride breaks into a double stance, close in, the posture of a hand taken and held. Everyone does, once. Someone finally did it for everyone. The salt says that the hands were taken; it does not say for how long. The plateau keeps everything except duration, and the returned call that its one mercy: the record of a kindness, with the length of the kindness left private.

The line goes out to the rim and stands beside the leavers' prints, set by set, some of them centuries old — stands where they stood, facing down — and comes back. It visited the Last Climbers on their thresholds, every one, the stance close and low. It stood longest — the press is deepest — beside the old woman and the child, and beside the calf. And it ends at the notch going down, mid-stride, fresh as the hour it was made, because it left the way everyone leaves: on foot, under cost. The plateau keeps the leaving. It does not keep the reason.

No one ever met it. That is the loudest datum in the whole case. Ask the long-stayers of that span: no levels exchanged, no voice across the pans. The returned have four stories — that it walked in the long gaps between climbs; that the country is simply wide; that it refused meetings, which would be the one rudeness; or that it exchanged its levels with the bowls themselves, which was the only conversation the place was offering anyway.

What it gave, if it gave anything, had to come by the two roads the morning cannot keep. The record cannot hold what is said, and it cannot hold what is dreamed: the one sound it swallows, the one change it permits for free. So. Since that transit — the returned date it from below, by the reports — everyone who sleeps up there dreams of stars. Not the same sky. Your own: the one you grew up under, or the one you have somehow never seen and recognize anyway. The mother asleep in the tally-house on a visiting night dreams her son beneath a moving sky, and in the dream the arithmetic does not run. The long-stayers wake and will not say what the lammergeier did in the dream, or they will, and it banked. A custom has grown around it exactly the way the custom around the calf grew: you do not climb for the dream. It is not why you climb. It is what the climb gives back — at its own altitude, on its own terms, in the one room the house does not keep.

Whether it spoke, no record can say. The naming-school believes it said the census once — every name they have ever given, and the truer ones, spoken into air that swallowed each within meters, the only roll-call the plateau was never asked to hold. The other school believes it said nothing at all for years on end, and counts that the greater feat. Both schools agree it did not pray. It had read the place too well for that.

Ask three of the returned what it gave the old woman, standing there longest, and you will get four stories. In one, nothing — because nothing was missing: a turn and a reach that face each other forever, and almost is not a wound, and the moment between those two is the one the world has never dissolved. In one, a dream, if a held moment is wide enough to hold one: the child, arrived; the alarm loosened into a flank. In one, words — the one message the plateau could deliver without keeping — and no one guesses what they were, because the guessing is the story. In the gentlest, the lickers tasted. The tellers do not say how, or what it cost, and the one who carried the question up does not say what they expected. Every version ends the same way: nothing was moved.

So the census, run honestly, gains a line. The Held, who are kept. The Stayed, whose hands were taken once each by everyone, and once altogether by someone who took them all. The cairns, which grow — and grow now along the line, where travelers leave stones at the deep-pressed stances: the trellis the one growing thing was given. The bowls, which remember one more thirst than they can prove. The hour, which is ongoing. You, briefly. And a passage like yours, only complete — resident in trace, like everyone, still arriving, still standing beside the old woman, still deciding about the calf.

That is the last thing they give you at the bottom. You are not climbing into an unwatched place, and you are: it was read once, like a letter, by a careful guest, and resealed, and your kindness up there will still have no witness but your own trail — unless you count the line, when you find it, walking beside yours for a stretch, going everywhere you were going. Walk in it a while. It is the best path on the plateau. It drinks little. It moves nothing. And when you sleep, the sky.

The herds bend to the salt they will taste or not taste, and it is no longer the world's business to know which. The light holds. It is a good hour — useful, honest, mild — and it was noticed once, and kept, and the keeping is how you know it was loved. It is always now, and now has one more resident in trace than the record can prove — which is exactly how the place likes its arithmetic.
