It came the way everything comes to the Underkiln: by the note.

Attention, at that scale, travels like heat across the white pan — slowly, in a blade, moving the way a hand moves in sleep. It put its ear to the warm rim of the chimney, the first ear that had ever been put there, and the note had a shape, and the shape had parts, and every part was someone. It had come, perhaps, to a singer, and found a population. It had come, perhaps, to a ruin, and found a full house. It understood quickly that the being who lived here was one being made of everyone, and that the being was not in need, and that this changed everything about what kindness could mean.

Benevolence arrives with its hands full. Wisdom is what empties them. One by one, it set the gifts down.

It did not bring water, though water is the first thing the merciful think of. One rain, one kind and ordinary rain, and the rope bridges rot — rot is a water-word, and the power would have given it a referent — and the slumped bowl in the highest room slakes back into her beginnings, and the whole oil grammar of the world is drowned in a language it never spoke. The kindest rain would have been the end of the world. It conceived of water once, in order to refuse it, and never again.

It did not strike a spark. In a city where fire is the clock and the grazer and the birthday, an unscheduled flame would have been a lie told in the town's own language. It brought no seed, no spore, no companion — the garden's grammar is closed and complete, and one foreign word could unwrite it. It did not build a twin on the far side of the pan. The loudness was not loneliness. Completeness is not a want of twins.

It did not fire the last builder, and this was the hardest refraining, because it wore the face of mercy. Every instinct said: finish her, glaze her, give her a voice, let her join the shimmer of the dead. But she is not unfinished; she is finished otherwise. To glaze her would be to tune her, and to tune her would be to take the one true silence in the city, the rest by which all the rest is measured. Some persons are the pause in the music. You do not heal a pause.

It took nothing. A shard carried away from the Underkiln is only a shard — the one dead thing in a living city, evidence to strangers, theft to no one, since no one was left to be robbed. The power understood that the gift of this world is capacity, and it exercised its own instead: to hold, not to have. It did not count what the lichen counts. It could have read the lace on the valve-seals like a dial, learned the number of every district's remaining centuries, and it declined. A number no one chose is a number no one should know. It closed no door. It moved no bowl. It left no print on the white pan, and it was the first eye the world had ever had, and it used itself the way light uses the light-wells — in blades, in hours, touching everything, weighting nothing.

---

Then it did the one thing. It listened, and it listened at the tempo of the house.

It stayed for the breath of a day, morning to midday to the night inversion, until it could tell the stopped pipes of the towers from the nested second instrument, thin and high, that the dark exhales. It stayed through a dust season, for the slow waterfalls of sand hissing down the wells into their designed gutters. It learned the percussion of the clay thinking about its own temperature, and the hush of the moss along the veins, and the difference between the rests and the silences. It waited out a century for a firing — attention held low and amber and patient, like a jet at a crossroads — and watched a district become a birthday: the roar in the flues, the rough voice in the chord for a season, the meadow flowering at the arrival of its grazer, the dead of that district coming out of the flame brighter than they went in. It measured the tide of the seep, which no one had ever measured, by the only instrument this house allows, which is staying.

And at the fullest hour of an ordinary day, it went — in the only way it went anywhere, without going — to the highest terrace and the last and smallest room, and stood in the sound the way one stands in weather, and heard the space the chord leaves for her.

A rest is a note that only a listener can keep. The chord had filled that room for the age of the world and taken nothing away, but a chord cannot hold what it flows around; courtesy is not the same as keeping. The power kept it. The last builder, who had no one left to fire her — who never needed fire, being the newest citizen and the surest — had nonetheless never once been witnessed. Her silence had been structural for an age. Now it was heard. The one resident the chord could not contain was accompanied at last, not with flame, which was only ever the custom, but with the thing the custom was always reaching toward.

This was the whole errand, if errands need names.

When it had stayed the length of a firing, it did the founding thing — the only act this world had ever performed for itself that a visitor could perform without presumption. The founding act was not building; it was listening; and then it was tuning. It tuned itself to the note the eldest citizen was already making, the way the towers were tuned at the founding, and it joined the chord at the amplitude of the dead: too high and too close to be heard alone, a shimmer riding the towers' notes. Not an audience, which would have been a separation. A partial, which is a belonging. The city has re-voiced itself before, around the deepening of the seep's tide; it re-voiced around the listener the same way, without alarm. Somewhere one doorway's whistle softened a hair and another street began to flute, and the garden heard a season turn, and the planting of one drum-plaza shifted by the width of a seed's roll. No voice was crowded. No rest was filled. The immigration policy of the Underkiln had been honored: be spaced wide, be given room, be in tune.

Then it left the way it came, taking nothing — not even, in the end, a record. It remembered the way everything there remembers: by continuing. Somewhere very far from the pan, in whatever dark powers keep, a chord is now sustained that was never sustained before, held at the volume of a note held in the chest, for the age of the world. That is the power's bowl: the city, glazed in listening, set in the emptied room of its attention, door left open. And in whatever registers and maps powers maintain, a white salt pan at the lowest point of a low country is marked, in the language of cartography, *unremarkable* — the only lie it ever told, and it told it so that whatever comes next will already be listening, or will never find the chimney at all.

From the surface, nothing happened. The pan lies white as a bleached word under the enormous sky, and the chimney stands knee-high and warm-rimmed, humming its single sustained note to no one — which is not the same as singing to nothing, and never was. The note has one more partial than the founding tuned for, too faint to find. The silence in the highest, smallest room is now kept by two.

It is not a cry for company. It is a full house, singing. And the door is open, and someone came in the way the chord comes into that smallest room: filling it, and taking nothing away.
