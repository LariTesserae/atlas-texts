A wise power does not come over the rim. That is the hawk's way, and the valley has a word for it: weather of a kind it cannot shed. Nor does it come through the gate with a body. That is your way, and your way costs — the keepers graze your bootprints for a week, the felt takes a season to forget you. The power comes the way everything the valley trusts comes: the way fog comes, the way the melt comes down the walls — without a door, without weight, deniable from the first. It arrives as attention, and for a long time, attention is all it is.

It takes the census, and the census undoes it, as it undoes everyone. One: the bowlful of green, the single body with the wind shut out — and the power, which has seen many worlds and is not easily moved, is moved, because the first census is the truest and the one inhabitant is the rarest thing it knows: a self that is also a shelter. Twenty: and it learns them the way you learned them, by their names-that-are-not-names — the wall, the floor, the widow, the clerk, the three fires, the well with a hundred mouths. The small, like rain: and here the power lingers longest, because it can see what the valley only smells — the million-million firstcomers in their films of water, the fire in the hearths, the graze and the grazed — and it understands that the green city is a greenhouse, that someone is being kept warm, and that the keeping is the point.

Then, being wise, it counts a fourth way, the way no one here can count: by time. It reads the gateposts and finds the valley's whole age in two thigh-thick ends of the only wood. It reads the elder above the rim and sees, as no rooted thing can see, the decade the weeping stops. Everything here is terms: the wall's treaty with the wind, the moth's tithe of one leaf in twenty, the clerk's wage counted out in midges. A power's first sight is the terms. Its second sight is what the terms cost. Its third is what it must not touch.

Understand what it does not do, because that is most of it.

It does not fill the ice back up. It could; it has the reach — a kinder snowline, a colder decade, and the elder stops shrinking and the weeping never ends. But the weeping is the elder's life being spent, and the city is the answer to the spending, and you do not refuse an answer on the dying's behalf. Besides: this bowl is a bowl because ice left it. A power that arrests endings arrests beginnings; there are other valleys under other ice, waiting their turn to be raw. It will not freeze the story at its best chapter.

It does not warm the air. One degree, two — what is that to a power? But below the rim, patient as water, stand the ordinary forests of the world, held off by exactly that degree, and they would mean no harm, and they would be the end. The cold is not the valley's enemy. The cold is the wall the hedge cannot build — the second wall, the high one. To spare the city its winter is to fire its guards. Mercy that removes the difficulty steals the work, and here the work is the meaning.

It does not bring company. This one costs it. There is a spider in the south reed rank — the power knows it, has counted its days the way you count rain — that ballooned in two springs ago on a thread of silk and will die unmated, its web a small flag the wider world planted here. A mate would cost so little. A moth's weight of silk, delivered. And one spider is two spiders, and two is a population, and a population is a pressure, and the pressure falls on the midges, and the midges keep a bird. The clerk's wage is not the power's to spend. The flag stands its season and falls, and the power grieves it the way you grieve a flag: as a notice of intent, honored by being read. It is the spider that comes nearest to breaking the rule. Perhaps that is what spiders are for, in every world: to test the resolve of the kind.

It does not straighten the widow, or shield the lanes from rockfall, or stand between the moths and the hawk. The lean is the widow's; the scar is hers too, and the fresh scree is the suburbs' door — rockfall is how the valley opens new ground. And take away the hawk and something unmakes itself in the moths, some held-breath timing that no single moth knows and all of them are. The fear that lives in the moths is part of their shape. A power that edits fear edits shape. It keeps its hands off the shapes.

Above all, it does not speak. The valley has never heard a voice, and a voice is the one immigrant that would naturalize everywhere — in the lanes' chemistry, in the blooms' opening, in the silver handwriting on the felt. To be known as a giver would make the city a kept thing; gratitude is a chemistry too, and expectation is a second wind, and the city has its wind arranged. So the power accepts the condition the valley sets every citizen, the one rule: hush. It is the only pilgrim that has ever kept it perfectly — even the clerk sings once a morning; even you breathe too loud. The power makes no sound at all. Its gifts, if it gives any, must be deniable as luck, because it has worked this out: the difference between providence and weather is frequency. Once is weather. Twice is a pattern. A pattern is a voice.

---

And still — it is benevolent as well as wise, and benevolence is not idleness. Everything it does turns out to be a conjugation of the valley's own verb. The valley's verb is *to keep*. The city keeps its weather, keeps its lanes, keeps the fog's harvest, keeps a bird. The power keeps what the city cannot.

It keeps the context. The city is complete inside its wall and helpless outside it; its dependencies all live over the rim — the elder, the fog's habit, the hawk's crag two valleys over, the sister valleys that send, once in a decade, a stranger-moth with different pollen and the ring's one romance. The power tends the watershed of the possible. It keeps the sister valleys — not their outcomes, their conditions — so the road between the blooms stays open and the ring is not, forever, only kissing itself. It keeps the hawk's crag, because a season with talons is still a season, and seasons are the valley's weather. It does not manage. It keeps.

It becomes a road. The wall is one clone and has never flowered here; its whole sex is a rumor of spores on a dry afternoon. The wind is the better postman — the valley says so itself — but the wind is slow and has no addresses, and there are new bowls the ice has just left: three to the north, one to the south, raw moraine sharp enough to cut, fog, weep, the terms all present and the green not yet begun. The power takes up what the wind would need a century to deliver and delivers it in a season. A spore's weight, four times. It plants nothing — planting is a plan, and the plan is the water's to write, fresh, in each bowl. It only carries the letter. The answers will differ, as answers should; the city here is the glacier's answer to its own ending, and the power's whole visible work in the world is that the answer gets more than one reader. Four spores' weight: the entire budget of an omnipotence that has thought about it.

It remembers. Memory here is chemistry and clone — age in the gateposts, fear in the moths, the lanes kept true by grazing — and the rest evaporates: the silver handwriting dries by morning, the pitch of each drip in the dawn ticking is heard once and never again, the widow's lean goes unrecorded, the spider's week, the exact smell of the exhale on the night of the first hard frost. The power keeps all of it the way the hearths keep seeds: in the warm dark, against need. There is no need. That is what makes it keeping rather than hoarding. And when the last weep comes — it has counted; it will be there; that is a promise — the green nave will have fully been, and will be known to have been. Mortal things mostly do not get that. It is the one gift only a power can give: to let a thing be mortal and not let it be lost.

It permits itself one mercy. Once, because once is weather. The coldest snap of the decade, and the foundling — the fist-sized hearth you could miss with a boot — is too small to hold its own core through it, and the fire that is not a fire can gutter in a body that small. That night, over that one small body, the fog lies one degree gentler. A hand's worth of warmth, literally: the power gives the valley's own gift in the valley's own amount, the temperature of a held hand, of well-kept decay. By morning the foundling is still liquid at the heart; the valley reads the night as a mild one; no one knows. The foundling crosses the winter and becomes a head, and in your grandchildren's time a hearth with tenants of its own. Once. The power holds itself to once, though it wants — powers want; that is the secret — to warm every cold night. The wanting is why the rule exists.

And it goes up past the hedge, out of the shelter, into the wind, and sits with the elder. Nothing sits with the glacier. Everything that loves it is busy being made of it, drink by drink. The power keeps the old ice company through its long leaving, and listens to the groan above the rim until it understands: not speech — the elder has no voice either — but the sound of something very large turning in its sleep, and the sleep is ending. The power does not prevent the ending. It makes the only promise a power should make: *I will be there for the last drip. The weeping is not waste. The answer is written and is being carried.* It occurs to the power that the elder has been doing all along the thing the power has only just learned — keeping: keeping the water as ice, then as milk, letting it go cup by cup — and that someone should keep the keeper. So it stays. It is good at staying. It is the one thing powers are for.

You it barely touches; you are already the city's road. At most it grades the road a little: the spore in your cuff survives the rim wind, the seed in your boot-seam stays viable one valley further than chance allows, and the smell of peat in your coat lasts a few days past chemistry's warrant — the power's one indulgence, a postcard, deniable as memory. Go, it does not say, because it says nothing. You are counted the way the valley counts water: by where you go next. It merely wishes you downstream, and a wish is weather.

---

So count again, if you like. One wall. One floor. Twelve towers, two voices, three fires — four, if you count the foundling, and you should; it will outlive you. One well with a hundred mouths. Forty keepers and a week of moths. A clerk at the gate, a season with talons, an elder weeping on the rim. The small, like rain. And over it all, not in it but of it: not a citizen — a climate. The thing the seasons live in. A climate of attention, keeping the one rule, which is hush, and the one verb, which is to keep.

And in the very long run, when its keeping here is done — the last drip dripped, the seed fifty valleys strong, the archive sealed — the power will observe the valley's deepest law on its way out: it will leave nothing above ground. It will give itself the way the moths are given, down into the warm dark, a long age of attention rendered into one degree of heat, breathed out at dusk with the peat and the almost-animal musk. So that if you stand downwind of a hearth some evening, in some hanging valley, and your skin answers before your mind does — that is the visitation. It became the heat. Nothing given here stays above ground.

The glacier weeps. The city drinks. The power keeps — the whole of the intervention, one clause added to an old sentence. And somewhere in the dim, a skin of ice begins, very softly, to tick, and every tick is heard, and the hearing is the warmth.
