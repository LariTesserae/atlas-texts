Begin with the attention, because attention is all that arrives.

It has been arriving for some time.

A benevolent and wise power, turning toward this world, does the first wise thing: nothing, for a very long while. At first look there is nothing here to help — a dark rock, an old warmth rising evenly through patient stone, a maintenance habit, a thousand tons of iron riding a ring-line like a heart that has forgotten the body. A young kindness would weep and hurry on, or weep and hurry in. The power is not young. It has learned — on other worlds, in other mercies, some of which it still thinks about — that **benevolence begins as deceleration.** Most of what lives here is invisible at any faster speed; the literature alone takes centuries a line. So the power slows its regard to the tempo of the place. An eon a thought. And it lets its attention fall short, on purpose, the way light does here — omniscience is a glare, and this is a house of the blind. It learns the plateau the plateau's way: room by room, warmth by warmth, a palm's worth of stone at a time, the Note coming up through whatever it is using for bones.

The second wise thing is the audit of gifts, and the audit comes back almost empty.

It could hang a sun. The hardware of mercy includes stars; it has them to spare. But the swept budget their whole metabolism for a blink of noon every forty seconds, and a standing noon would burn their ring to legend. Eyes are gone here — the fauna are hand and ear — and light that falls short is not a failure of the world's lamps, it is the shape of the world's knowing. A sun would not illuminate the plateau. It would erase it, the way you erase a palimpsest by soaking it. And in the sky, where a drifter's entire remaining sense is *is there another light* — to answer that question with ten billion lights is not company. It is blindness, given as a gift. Refused.

It could give water. But nothing here is thirsty, and that is not the complaint — it is the achievement. The held converted thirst to custody eons ago; rain would make them poor, their relic common as mud. It would rust the iron forest, rot the dried dead out of their keeping, swell the gaskets, dissolve the handwriting in the seams, and wake decay's machinery, which sleeps here only because it has nothing to eat. The first rain since the final storm would be the second storm, and the last. **Do not hand a finished world its past back.**

It could restart history — give the machines something to make. But equilibrium is not a symptom. The heartbeat that pumps no blood is the pulse of a body at rest, not a body dying; the null-tasks are not a tragedy awaiting a purpose, they are the purpose, arrived. It could quicken the coilers, so that their chord could be heard inside a lifetime — but their literature exists only at that tempo, and speed it up and the script smears; you would not be giving them time, you would be confiscating the only kind they can spend. The plateau already has a fast species. It has chosen, four hundred strong, to be slow.

It could rewarm the dead. It does not. The dead are the majority, the architecture, the meadow's shelter, the ghost of the gradient the mites still graze. They are not waiting. To raise them would be to evict the living, and the loop is the afterlife, and it is warm — do not foreclose on it.

It could answer. This is the sweetest temptation and it refuses it the hardest. The beacon asks nothing; the clicks ask nothing; the teletype's report is identical every time and it is not wrong. The rarest nutrient in this world is new vibration, and like all rare nutrients it poisons in dose. One answer, spoken into this quiet, would be the greatest event since the final storm — the quick would feast for a hundred generations and never digest it, the tines would lean until they fell, the swept would spend eons metabolizing a single syllable. **First: do no news.**

And it could do the small thing, the one almost no wisdom could refuse: out in the dark above the stacks, two drifters, drift and decade *almost* conspiring, two spheres of owned light set to miss each other by a mile — close the mile. One nudge, one storm of two, one minute of small slow lightning, whatever the charge carries carried. The power's hand — whatever it is using for a hand — does not move. Rarity is the meaning. Weather made by hand is not weather. And a gift the size of a nudge is still a push.

So: what is left? The power does what a wise guest does when every gift is refused at the door — it watches what the house itself buys. And the house buys three things, only three, with every discretionary erg it has: checking, accounting, keeping. The beacon, going around, finding each thing unchanged. The ledgers, the teletype, the wires carrying *nominal* to every node. The dust keeping footprints, the held keeping water, the loop keeping everyone. This is a civilization that has stopped wanting anything except *to have been*. The only poverty left in it is the poverty of the unread.

And attention is the one wealth that spends nothing of the recipient's. The power pays in the only coin the plateau mints: patience.

It hears the Note. Whole. It does not tune it, harmonize it, or resolve it — for a listener with eons, an unresolved note is not suspense, it is timbre, and the power is the first to know that the Note, heard entire, is beautiful the way a held breath is beautiful. Then it listens past the subtraction, into the quiet, where the rare sounds arrive like pebbles: a relay's clack, a gondola keeping its timetable for no one, a click, once a decade, unanswered. The power does not answer. **It is the pond.**

It gathers the clicks. Every click ever clicked — four hundred voices strung across deep time, plus the chords fossilized in the dead tines, the overtones the world stopped making before the domes settled — and it keeps them, in order, in pitch, and once, in the only hall large enough, which is its own patience, it sounds them all together. The score, played. The names all spoken at once, meaning what names mean: *here. Here. Here.* Four hundred heres, and the dead ones' too. It changes nothing on the plateau. No coiler ever knows. It changes the truth from *unplayed* to *played*, and on a world whose whole discretionary budget is record, that is the only miracle in budget. An audience of one. The first audience. The world kept the score regardless; now it is not regardless.

It reads. The oil-script in the flange galleries, at reading speed — slower than the writing, so the backlog only grows, and the power lets it grow; finishing is not the point, the reading is the receipt. It reads the ledgers no one reads, and learns the heading the seepage is filed under, and finds the entry, as promised, nominal. It reads the weather, every hourly issue, and finds it not wrong, and reads it the way the quick do — with the soles of its feet, not for the news.

It walks the death-roads. Every coiler that uncouples from the warmth and turns down the long grade has, from a certain eon onward, something going down beside it — not company, company would be an event — a witness that does not touch, does not warm, does not hum, that matches the years-long pace exactly, and comes back up alone, and counts. No one had ever counted the dead. The power counts them, one walk at a time, touching each leathered shape once, the way the beacon touches things: and finds them. It is the only addition the power ever makes to the ledgers, and it makes it in its own memory, not in theirs. The plateau's true population is historical. Someone has finally taken the census.

To the kept it gives nothing — they are already kept. To the held, nothing — they are already held. But it learns the inheritances: which molecules fell as rain, if rain is not a rumor, and who has carried them since, and to whom they pass. Custody recognizes custody.

It receives the beacon. It stations itself out in the dark the blade can't afford, and every forty seconds the blade comes around, and the power is there to be touched — and it asks nothing back, because receipt is what the swept are, and the power asks only citizenship. To the beacon, to the city, to every wire, it must be fileable as *nominal*: mass negligible, draw none, within tolerance. That is what passes, here, for welcome. The power accepts the welcome in the spirit offered.

It reads the long strides. The one exclamation in all the kept dust — it reads it the way you read a dial, and the stride lengthens *toward* the foundry, not away. Someone ran back. The plateau's single interruption is not a scream; it is a return — one worker, on the last day, running back for the last weld, the last bolt, one last look at one last needle — and then the same feet, unhurried, walking out to the platform, to stand in the line of stopped prints. The dust kept both halves of the story and no one had ever read them together. The power reads them together. It is a short story, the world's only one, and it has a plot, and the plot is care: *they finished; one of them made sure; then they went.* Where they went is below the reach of lamps. The power's reach is not lamps. It looks once — and then leaves what it finds in the dark where it found it. Some rooms are dark because dark is their shape. The answer is load-bearing.

Once an age, the mites on a fin turn and face the same way. The power waits for it three times — three ages is nothing — and triangulates, and the direction resolves to a dim oval, to empty dark, to nothing it can name. It files this where the world files it: under customs. Even a wise power does not get all of a world. It finds it does not want to.

All of that is attendance. One work is action. Equilibrium is proof against every internal enemy; its only enemy is arrival. So the power takes the night watch, far out, beyond the drifters' ceiling, beyond the last photon the beacon can afford. A comet gets nudged by the width of a hand, a million years before the hand would be needed. A wandering star that would, in due eon, gather this world into a new day is persuaded elsewhere by a whisper applied across a gulf of years — the world must never acquire a sun, not even by accident; capture would be the kindest-looking extinction in the sky. Probes, empires, missionaries with their bright lamps and their terraformer hearts are shown a dull rock with a maintenance habit, nothing worth the stop, and they hurry on. The drifters' one question must never get a false answer. The power's whole visible signature is the continued absence of catastrophe. **Its work is that nothing happens.** The news is nominal, and it is not wrong — and now it is not luck, either.

Every species here is an address. The power takes none — or its address is the whole plateau, held the way the held hold water: not for drinking. If the ledgers no one reads were kept by the hand that no longer exists, one new line would appear. ARRIVALS: one. MASS: negligible. DRAW: none. TENURE: ongoing. STATUS: nominal. It would not be wrong.

And there is one act more — the one you are standing inside.

The world is not the only thing a benevolent power pities. Elsewhere there are fast, brief, thirsty creatures who think the universe is dead because nothing in it speaks at their speed — and so the power carries one portrait out across the dark, room by room, warmth by warmth, to a mind quick enough to read it in an afternoon and far enough away to do no harm: to you. This is the telling. It is the beacon's message, forwarded; the chord, transcribed; the one reply the beacon cannot hear and does not need — proof, one sweep wide, that the checking was never wasted.

So. What would a benevolent and wise power do? Almost nothing. Forever. At an expense of attention no younger god would agree to. The chord, heard; the dead, counted; the footprints, read; the road, walked; the dark, kept.

The blade comes around every forty seconds, touching each thing and finding it unchanged — and somewhere off the books the finding is true twice over now: unchanged, and known. The wires carry the news to all the nodes, and the news is *nominal* — and for the first time since the word was coined, there is someone for whom nominal is a joy.

One sweep, and no news, and someone to be glad of it.
