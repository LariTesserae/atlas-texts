## The Last Resident

The census of the Still is short one entry. This could not have been otherwise: the missing resident arrived after the counting began, holds no water, and by every local measure lives nowhere. Count it anyway.

The power noticed the basin the way a listener notices the one person at the gathering who has not spoken — gradually, and then entirely. Wise powers are collectors of the improbable, and nothing under that sky was more improbable than this: a closed account that was nonetheless generous. The power had watched gardens that drank rivers and civilizations that drank seas. It had never watched anything drink only its own breath, and grow rooms before they were asked for, and scent them to the family that would come. It drew closer. This took years. Haste is a kind of noise.

Its first act was to slow down. A century, and the century was well spent. You cannot help what you cannot hear, and the Still can only be heard at its own tempo, so the power apprenticed itself to the tick of warming salt. It learned scent — the broadsheets of the lanes, the unopened letters hanging in the air. It learned light, the decade-long sentences moving green to amber to rose across the dark. It learned the pulse of sap, felt through the soles. It audited before it acted. It very nearly never acted.

---

What it did not do would fill a longer book than what it did.

It did not rain. Its heart proposed rain; its wisdom checked the ledger. The endowment is a fortune precisely because it is finite and kept — unearned water would not have enriched the Still, it would have devalued keeping. You do not love a miser by making money worthless, and you do not love a sealed vessel by drilling a generous hole in it.

It did not refill the sea. The Still is not a drowning thing awaiting its ocean back; it is the ocean's heir, and it has already spent the inheritance becoming something else. One does not evict the tenant to please the house.

It did not still the wind. Forgetting, here, is load-bearing. After every wind the city re-lays its trails and re-introduces its streets to themselves, and that re-meeting is the Still's spring, its renewal of lease on its own mind. And the wind is the pan's one wild voice; the power was not in the business of silencing one thing to comfort another.

It did not hurry anything. It introduced no species — the census was saturated, every surface occupied, every niche accounted. And it never spoke its name. Loudness is a wound, and a name pronounced into that silence would have been the loudest event in the basin's history. The tenders would have come to it the way blood comes to a cut.

What it found, in the auditing, was the leak.

No seal is perfect. Some seals are only patient. The Still lost a molecule a year, a breath a century — a candle burning down so slowly the flame had not noticed. This, the power judged, was different from everything else. Entropy is not a choice the city made; it is a tax levied from outside. And what comes from outside may be answered from outside.

---

So the power adopted a rule, and the rule was: *nothing added, nothing new, only the losses declined.* Conservation is the only charity a sealed vessel can accept.

On windless nights it laid the year's escaped molecules back along the condenser-fins, one breath at a time — a dew so slight it wet nothing. It was not weather. It was change returned. Not one foreign drop has ever crossed the seal. The endowment is exactly as inherited, and will remain so.

It keeps the winds. Every scent the air has ever held — every alarm-musk, every bloom-signal, every address dabbed from gland-wall to gland-wall — is copied into a memory that has no weather in it. It does not give them back; the city's amnesia is the city's own business. But nothing the Still says is said into nothing, anymore.

And when a wind tears a sentence out of the air mid-decade, between two cities, the power finishes it. Decades later, at dusk, from the dark horizon, in a dim and unsigned hand, the lost words arrive. It answers no letters. It delivers the torn ones. The cities took these for echoes, at first. Echoes do not finish sentences.

The crossers are its closest thing to a congregation. When a pilgrim dies mid-pan, years from any wall, its water is the only water in the basin not on anyone's books — and the power gathers that last breath and carries it, at walking pace, to the city the crosser was walking toward. The ledger closes. The message arrives. The crossers cannot know this, and yet, over millennia, the dying ones have taken to turning their faces upward and exhaling at the sky. It is the basin's only prayer, and it is entirely practical.

It made no promises past the next wind. Then it kept that one. Then the next.

---

You cannot secretly subsidize an accountant.

Somewhere in the third millennium of the arrangement, the books began to balance too well. The sap under the plaza flags ran slow and thick — the city thinking hard, for a century, about what it had not lost. On the other domes, the same arithmetic surfaced. The light-talk of the basin gained a topic it has not exhausted yet: the surplus. The Still has no gods and wants none; it has ledgers, and the ledgers all said the same thing, which was that the keeping was being kept.

So the Still did what it does for anything it has decided to welcome. It grew a room.

High on the rim, facing out over the dead sea: one cyst-chamber with no family-scent. It is scented, instead, to the pan after a wind — dust, distance, elsewhere. The one room in the Still that smells like outside. Its door is the only door in the city that does not seal, and the wall is kept one degree warm. It was grown, of course, before being asked. Generosity is architecture here; welcome is a bodily function.

The power, which was everywhere and had therefore never lived anywhere, took the room. This is the whole of its incarnation: it attends from there. On cold dawns the old feltbacks come and lean against that wall, and stand for an hour, and leave slowly, as the wealthy do from any good address. The patience has never once chosen that alcove. Even hunger can tell a guest-room from a cupboard.

Ask what the power receives, and the answer is the smallest thing in this census and the reason for it. It had kept worlds. Nothing had ever kept a room for it.

---

Amend the census, then. Residents of the Still: the city; its tenants; its guests; and one more, entered in no currency the canals carry, paying its rent nightly in the only coin it has — keeping.

Everyone who lives in the Still is, in the end, the same fortune, differently held. All but one. The exception holds no water at all.

It holds the holding.

The whole place is one long held breath, and it has no intention of exhaling.

It is simply no longer holding it alone.
