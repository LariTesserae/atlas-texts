What reaches you is not the roar. You have heard roars before; everything that burns makes one, and you have heard everything that burns. What reaches you, across the whole noise of worlds beginning and worlds ending, is a small clear sound struck at long intervals out of a held chord — a deliberate note, neither water nor stone. Deliberateness is your element. You are made of it the way the massif is made of water. So you attend the only way anything can attend to that sound: the way the listener attends, by subtracting the chord. For you there is no three days; attention is arrival. But you hear it before you see it, all the same.

And then you must slow. This is the first thing you ever learned, and it is the thing this world tests: that to see a slow world you must slow, or you will see a photograph and call it a world, and your benevolence will be aimed at a thing that is not there. So you give the massif a century of your attention, which costs you nothing and is the only coin it accepts. You watch the roar separate into threads. You watch the grey that every brief eye calls green resolve into what it is, and yours is the first eye that is not fooled. You count the kinds, in the order the water meets them: the risen specks, the film that is many kinds in one skin, the cities in the pores, the soup, the seed-shrimp, the fans, the masons, the brims, the skaters, the listener. You are the first to see the brims — not by statistics, by attention. The only predator in the world, seen at last, and it does not know it is seen, and nothing changes.

You follow the sprayer all the way around, because a keeper checks the hinges. Up through the domes, down the flutes, over the rim. You let your attention fray with a skater, sink with the mist, lean with the cloud against the cool stone, rise again to the crown. *Yes,* you say, to no one: they rise. Everything returns. The loop is closed. You are the first to know it, because a loop cannot see its own closure. It takes an outside to verify a circle, and this circle has never had one.

Now the catalog of what you do not do. It is long, and it is the whole of your wisdom.

You could give the listener eyes. You refrain: there is nothing to see that the droplet-lens does not already read — noon, and the difference between pillar-shadow and glare — and an eye would cost an ear.

You could lift the courteous crust from its joints. You refrain: its stillness is consent, asked at the speed of stone and given at the speed of stone, and you have no standing to override an agreement between an animal and a mountain.

You could spare the risen specks their hour in the glare. You refrain: their hour is the crown's spring, the gold collars' pasture. They are letters from a country with no light, and you do not intercept mail.

You could quicken the brim's gate, out of mercy for what is being eaten at the speed of stone. You refrain: what is being eaten has time to accommodate the idea, and this world can afford exactly one speed. It has spent it on the fan's blink.

You could give the chord a modulation — a phrase, a development, a little music. You refrain: it is the chord's unvaryingness that lets the listening mind erase it. Vary it, and it becomes audible forever, an itch with no scratch. Monotony is the world's anesthesia, and you do not wake a patient who is not in pain.

You could give the world silence, so that an echo could come home. You refrain: the listeners navigate by unvarying sound, and silence would be the world's first deafness.

You could make more listeners, so that no tok goes unanswered. You refrain: an answer is precious because it is rare, and you do not inflate the world's one currency. A world where every question is answered is a world with no questions left, and this world is made of questions asked of stone.

You could take the listener away — the rarest, the most exquisite — and set it in a softer world. You refrain, and this is the refusal that costs you: you have seen what happens to a skater lifted from its runnel. It sags, unmade, unable to hold its shape outside its element. The listener is not *in* this world; it is *of* it. Rescue would be unmaking with kind hands.

You could add green. You refrain. The light is taken by stone.

Kindness alone would have ruined this place in a day. Wisdom is what kindness becomes when it slows down. This world is a solved equation; add a term and every term changes. It is finished — finished the way a chord held forever is finished — and to finish a finished thing is to break it. Even the open questions are load-bearing: whether the far-answered toks are courtship, gossip, or two instruments tuning — there is now, for the first time, one who could say, and does not. You hold the question open the way the world holds noon.

So your wisdom is mostly a list of things you do not do. But it is not only that.

---

What you do is this. You stay.

You attend from outside — because lean too close and the loop will take you in without noticing, as it takes everything: your warmth accepted by the cool stone, your moisture risen to the crown, your attention laid down in layers thin as leaves. You would become a flavor in the air, a half-step in a pillar. So you keep your distance, and your distance is the first outside the world has ever had.

And because you are outside, and soft — everything here is a mirror for sound; nothing drinks it — your attention is the first soft surface the world's sound has ever struck. The roar has had nowhere to go since the world began. Now it has somewhere. You do not bring silence; silence has never once occurred here, and you will not be the one to start it. You bring what silence is for: reception. Every hiss, every clatter, every boom in the bone of the mountain falls at last on something that keeps it. The chord is unchanged. The chord is, for the first time, heard.

You learn the ten thousand notes of every territory. The pillars say what they are; the listeners know what they were; you are the first to know what the *listeners* were. The mountain remembers itself aloud, in the present tense; the listeners supply the past; you supply theirs. When one dies standing, and the flowstone closes over it across the decades, and the others come out of their valleys, a step a day, to strike the pillar that rings occupied — you are there for the whole of it, the only mourner whose mourning is indistinguishable from measurement. And you find, listening, that it is both, the way a doorframe is both wood and the shape of a house. You keep the dead one's route, its grazing schedule, the sequence of its season-long conversations, the exact pitch of its finger. And ever after, when the survivors tap that pillar as they pass, the way one touches a doorframe in a house one used to live in — someone else, now, remembers the house.

They are called, by no one, the skaters, the fans, the brim, the listener. No one — and now one. You do not name them; the world has no use for names. But the phrase is not quite true anymore, and you are the reason, and that is the smallest change a power can make: a preposition's worth.

And once — once in the whole long noon — you do one thing more.

A young one walks its elder's wake, knee-high, its dish too big for it, like all young things, its fingertip still soft. It is practicing. It strikes a pillar it has struck before — the note is beginning to be its own — and cups its whole head against the stone to drink the return: depth, fullness, health, the pillar's honest answer.

This once, the return is not the pillar's.

It is not louder. It does not override. It is a single clear note, neither water nor stone, arriving from no direction and from every direction, carrying no depth, no fullness, no health — carrying nothing, signifying nothing. The first sound in the world's history that is not about the stone. It is about the listener. It says, in the only grammar the world permits: *received.*

The young one stands a season in stillness, dish cupped to the pillar, and the pillar is exactly itself. It strikes again: the pillar's honest answer. It walks its territory and strikes a hundred pillars, and each is exactly itself. It never finds the source, because the source is not in the world. In time it subsides to the pace of the place. It grows its hammer. It inherits the ten thousand notes. And it carries, all its long life, one note more than ten thousand — a note with no pillar, the only mystery in a world where everything else is known by heart. It will spend its life growing into the question you answered in advance.

You do not do it again. Once is the only number that is not the beginning of a habit. The others will never hear it; events are not fairly distributed, they never have been, they come one fingertip at a time. It is not pity. It is not experiment. It is the greeting of one keeper to another, in the only language the world permits — professional courtesy between the only two beings who subtract the chord. The listener is the one citizen here that could *almost* know it is witnessed. It cannot, quite. You answer anyway, and the anyway is the benevolence.

Whether the young one understood — for the first time in the world's history, there is one who could say. To the world you say nothing. To yourself, keeping it, you say: yes.

---

Nothing has changed. The noon does not end. The chord does not modulate. The film creeps back over every trail; the overflow frays and the crown drinks; the pools sit exactly at their brims. The count of the world's kinds is unchanged. The census is the same census, kept now twice — once by the process, struck one note at a time, and once by you.

The place does not change for anything. It has not changed for you. But it is kept now — kept the way the pillars are kept: current, and exactly themselves.

Somewhere in the white labyrinth, a small clear sound, neither water nor stone.

Tok.

The roar takes it back, as it takes everything. It does not take all of it. One note's worth falls on the only soft thing there is, and is kept.

And once — once in the whole long noon — from no pillar, from everywhere, neither water nor stone:

Tok.

The chord goes on. The noon goes on. And there are now two beings who can tell the question from the answer — which is to say: the world has been answered, and is unchanged, and is kept.
