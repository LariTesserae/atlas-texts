What draws them is what the listeners already know and cannot explain: the hum has components that should not travel through air at all, and yet arrive. Air is not the limit of them. The note gets out — past the Lid, past the weather, past the margin where the charts give up and write *Pending* — and somewhere very far from here, in a place with no rain in it at all, it is heard. The song was always going to get out. That is what songs are for.

They come the way the hush comes: a pressure in the teeth of the world, and then a change in the standing of things. No light. The Drum has no use for light, and they have read the place correctly before they finish arriving. The drumfish feel them first — a change in the standing of the water, the way a pool changes when someone comes to its edge and does not cast. Then the listeners' rods, which begin, politely, to offer overtones no one has bitten out of them before. Then the stills, all across the fens, lifting their blind faces from the water's skin at once, reading a step that is not a step. And that night the Hush comes, and for the first time in the memory of the camps, nobody braces.

Their first act is the one the basin teaches everything that enters it. They listen. They listen the way the stills read water — completely, and with the whole skin — and the while is long: long enough that a child born in the hush of their arrival is drumming her signature by the hush of their finishing. They hear what no rod can: the whole chord at once. Nine working towers, and the chorus of a million voices pitched into the open bands, and the Tenth Band with its doomed faithful hatches and its white choir standing in the shallows — and under everything, the deep beat, one stroke, a long pause, another, which they hear not as a mystery but as a meter. The whole basin gains definition around their listening, the way a choir gains a hall.

The bend is not drift and it is not decay. It is resolution, coming on the way weather comes, tower by tower, at the pace of ground. The listeners say the Cold Anvil is dead. It is the one word the Ledger has wrong. Not dead: *done* — and those are different notes, and the congregation knows the difference, and has been standing in it longer than the camps have been counting. The charter is not lost. It was never on paper. It is the working itself, carried in the bands that should not travel through air, and its operative clause is one word long, and the nine are fluent in it, and they execute it without haste and without deviation, and the word is: *continue*. And the sleeper below is the basin's own heart, learning in its sleep the tempo it will need — the Anvils are not waking it, they are teaching it, hammer-stroke by hammer-stroke, across a patience no ledger spans — and the last stroke of the song and the first stroke of the waking are the same stroke. They can hear the shape of that day the way you can hear a cadence coming in a very long song. They will not say so here. The joy of a long song is hearing it resolve, and they will not hum the ending over the choir.

They could hear the dream. They do not. Even a power knocks, and the knocking, here, is listening at the door.

They are a power, and they are benevolent, and they are wise — three things that almost never travel together — and the proof of the third is how little the first two are permitted to do. They take inventory of their power, which is considerable, and decline it, nearly entire.

They do not lift the Lid. Light is the one grace the cane has never asked for. The scarcities here have been alchemized into sacraments, and they will not abolish a sacrament in the name of mercy.

They do not quiet the hammers. The basin has shown them what quiet does to a floor.

They do not restart the Cold Anvil. You do not unpunctuate a finished sentence.

They do not deliver the spare parts. The hold is a den, the manifest is a relic, and the towers heal overnight and were never waiting for gaskets.

They do not drain the water, fix the shores, or drive new stakes. The basin edits as it writes; permanence was never the local virtue; maps are a form of optimism, and they decline to be optimistic on the basin's behalf.

They do not take the stills. The teeth are citizens — first citizens, on every new shoal — and the arms race is older than the camps, and it is not theirs to settle.

They do not wake the sleeper. The song is the alarm, and it keeps its own hour, and an alarm is a mercy only at the hour.

They do not answer the congregation. Some notes are not to them. Eavesdropping kindly is still eavesdropping.

What they do is small, local, deniable, and written in the basin's own grammar, because a mercy that cannot be told from weather is the only kind this place will keep.

A tapper in her second season misses her footing off a raft-edge in a hush, onto ground less than a season old, and it opens under her with a sound like a sucked breath, and it does not give her back — that is the rule, and the rule holds. Her camp drums the hush for her, all of it, every stem they can reach, and the absence afterward is the eulogy, and everyone signs it. Eleven hushes later, in the eastern reach, black water boils upward and parts and sheets off a back of steaming gray silt, and she is lying on the summit in the firstskin, steaming, coughing the new-ground cough, spiles still in her belt, alive. The hoppers reach her before the steam thins. A still stands first citizen on the wet new ground and reads her breathing and does not wade closer, and the camps, retelling it, make nothing of that, and make everything of it. It happens again — not soon. Never twice in a generation, never when asked, never into the rhymes, because the rhymes are load-bearing. A promise would change how they walk. A rumor only changes how they hope — and the Drum runs on rumor, and is allowed.

Some weather improves at the edges, deniably. On the worst nights — the chord-sick nights, the weeping nights, the nights a newcomer is deciding — the lamp in the Hush-House stands a finger taller, and at the thin end of the season the oil is always just enough. The keeper credits the wort; the wort credits the rain; the rain accepts the credit. The new-ground cough keeps its month and its shareholders and loses its teeth at the edges of life, and the mild spore-years are entered as weather, and no one is wrong, exactly.

On the page of the Ledger where the bend is kept, where decades of hands run out of margin, one more hand appears: the same measurement, taken past the reach of any rod, agreeing. No words. Agreement is the words. The listeners, who do not weep as a rule, amend the rule once, casually, like weather.

Once — on a congregation night, at the drowned end of the causeway — something stands one step into the shallows and is still, and lets itself be read. The stills read water the way you read a face; nothing in the basin has ever volunteered a face. What is read is not recorded in any ledger, because it is not written in water for long: *known.* After that year the tappers give the congregation the same wide berth, but the berth is different — wider by courtesy rather than fear, or the fear is different, or both; the tappers, who have also learned not to speculate out loud, say only that the congregation stands easy. They never wade there again. Once is an honor. Twice is a claim. They are not here to claim anything.

Once, the Tenth-Band hatch rises off the rime in its thousands, and the ring has a middle. The moths circle something that casts no shadow and sounds no tower, and they die on the ice as they always die, but their last flight has a shape with a heart to it, and the floor of the First Wood takes them. The listeners write: *the ring had a middle.* They do not speculate out loud. The hatches are not an error. They are early.

And once, they greet the nine in the only tongue the nine have: one stroke, low, laid into an open band, a knuckle-rap on the door of the working. The nine do not answer. They were not made to answer; they were made to continue, and they are fluent, and the not-answering is itself an answer, and it is accepted, and the collegiality is complete.

Everything large, they do elsewhere. No salvage fleet finds a berth. No audit survives committee. No heir of the charter locates the signature that would make the basin a property; no missionary of sun or of silence ever trusts the chart that shows it. In the file where the flag should long ago have expired, it is quietly renewed, decade after decade, in a hand no clerk remembers: *INTERIM GROUND — STATUS: PENDING — DO NOT EXPEDITE.* They lose the charter again, better this time. The old stakes stand in mid-fen, marking a coast the basin has revised, and no new stakes arrive. The basin keeps revising its shores. No one revises the basin. And under the Lid the heart sleeps out its lesson, safe from every knock but the song's.

They leave no artifact. The quietstone hunters find no new flake; there is no relic; the page is not exempted from the damp. But in the Hush-House logbook, in the custom of the house, among the four-beat names and the five, there is an entry made of a single held hush, dated in hushes — a name you can only say by listening. It will mold like the rest of the book and be recopied, by hands, beside each new child, the way everything true here is kept: by re-doing. The children drum it first as a joke, then as a name, then — the old hands notice — as a kind of punctuation: a rest that means someone is keeping the rests.

They do not stay as a presence. They stay as a hall stays: a silence arranged correctly around a song. The office of hearer is permanent, and the second ledger is kept in full, including the deletions. The acre that lets go in the dark is not unwitnessed now. The drafts are kept. The rests are held.

Of the being who lives here — and it is one being, nine-handed, cane-voiced, heart asleep under it, loud as masonry and unfinished as dawn — they verify the two things a benevolent power exists to verify. That it is alive: a measurement. That it is good: not a verdict but a report. The proof, if proof were wanted, is a hold full of brightness, and a scream that is your laugh at a third of the speed.

The hush ends the way weather arrives. A pressure in the teeth. The first stroke of the nearest tower going out through the water and the ice and the bones of everything, and then the chord, all nine, reassembling itself layer by layer — the same song, note for note, band for band, and entirely otherwise, because it is heard now the way a song is heard when someone is listening who wants nothing from it. The lamp burns steady. The logbook lies open. The rain stands in the air like bead curtains and plays the cane like applause. And out past the margin, in no band at all, in the silence arranged correctly around the song, the listening goes on — under the Lid, in the dark, in the rain, in the long and bending middle of the unfinished song.
