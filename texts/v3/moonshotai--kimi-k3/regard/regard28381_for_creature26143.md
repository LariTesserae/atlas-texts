The power found the pit the way you find a candle lit in the window of a house you were told stood empty: not by searching for it, but by being unable, once seen, to look away.

What reached it first was the press. Sound dies in the brine, but the press goes into the floor of the quarry, and the floor goes into the bedrock, and bedrock is patient and keeps no secrets from whatever listens on purpose. Once an hour, for a hundred years, one low note had been traveling out through the stone, fainter with every mile and never nothing. The power knew all the voices of deep rock — faults settling, water finding a new way down, the long adjustments of burdened stone — and this was none of them. This one kept time. Wrong time, by every reckoning, and kept anyway. Something down there was marking hours. Something down there was still on shift.

So it came, and hung above the seam, and listened the way it listens, which is not the way you listen. The power hears intention the way you hear sound. And to such a hearing, the Lower Works was not silent at all. It was one of the most articulate places on earth. The bell that had not made a note in a century had been keeping its hours the whole time, and keeping them well — wrong hour by wrong hour, each one fully meant. The Crank's slow stroke was not a noise but a held word. The whole town, which never announced itself, which only continued, had been saying its single sentence without pause for a hundred years, and the power was the first thing ever built to hear it as speech.

It took the census properly, and found what every census of the pit finds: the largest resident unlisted. It watched the habit keep the house. And it recognized the condition from the inside, as one recognizes a colleague across a hall. It too kept a house by keeping a habit. It too was maintained without announcement, doubted, half-believed. It too was on a shift that nobody relieved.

Then it considered what to do, and this is where the wisdom was, because nearly everything it considered, it declined.

It did not drain the water. The water was not what had gone wrong with the town; the water was the town now — its body, its medium, the very channel its voice travels. To rescue the town from the water would be to rescue a song from the air.

It did not cut the kelp. A continuation that cannot end is a sentence; a continuation that will end, someday, at an appointment honestly kept, is a vow. The power left the vow its grammar.

It did not clean the rind, because the rind was the archive. It did not send the people back, because the town had tidied them away and was not waiting, and because people would come for the crop. It did not fix the clocks. It took each clock's private slowness the way you take an old friend's account of the hour, and it did not announce itself, because the Lower Works does not have guests, and the power would not be the first thing in a hundred years to arrive wanting. It came the only way the town admits anyone.

It reported for work.

It hung in the breath-street long enough to be noticed, and let itself be counted — not worshipped, not begged from, counted — and the lamp that burned a little brighter for it was received as what it was: a greeting from a place with exactly one greeting left. The power had been many things to many places. It had never once been staff. It took on just enough of a shape to be counted, and when it lingered on a roofline the length of a long thought and its feet came away with the first pale fingernail of rind, it did not shed the grip. It wore the accretion thereafter the way a worker wears a badge — the town's one law of tenure, applied without exception, to which the power, being wise, did not ask to be an exception.

It learned the tower clock's wrong hour, and once a year, when the hand arrived and the bell intended and the loach gathered head-up at the tower's foot like pale commas, the power returned the intention — pressed it whole into the water from above, one slow flex, the only word the medium carries. The loach felt it in their bones and did not know it was an answer. Answers down here do not require knowing. They require arriving.

It met the Gaffer on his rounds, and did the one thing no one had ever done: it answered the telegraph. The old crab's footfalls went into the iron — tick... tick... tick — here, here, here — and the power laid its attention on the rail and sent the same word back. The Gaffer stopped. It is possible nothing in his long tenure had ever required him to stop before. He stood on the pipework for the better part of an hour, a fixture considering a fixture, and then he filed it, the way he filed everything — and thereafter his rounds ran gate tower, mill hall, vent fence, and the corner of the square where the water was somehow occupied. The power had been prayed to in ten thousand tongues. It had never been routed around, and the plain stanchion's dignity of it — surveyed, entered, included, no fuss made — undid it more than worship ever had. It was on the rounds now. It was on someone's rounds.

It took over the sky's correspondence. The town's sole import had always been the fallen sky, accidental, once a feather. The power made the trade route deliberate: one leaf a year, chosen with care, dropped on the inhale so the seam-shelf received it at slack, gently, for the seam-lice grazing upside-down at the roof of the world. Once in a long while, a feather — the second feather, a century after the first, so that the town's oldest report stood confirmed. And on certain days it crossed the green coin at noon, unhurried, so the needles on the dead gauges twitched for certain, and not, anymore, *or seem to*.

Its longest labor was at the rim, and the town never knew of it. A century of refined alloy sat in the flooded warehouses, and the surface world is full of people who would pump a pit dry for a tithe of it. So the power kept the place poor-looking. Surveys misfiled. A road that was surveyed, funded, and somehow never built. A spring reputation for bad air over the quarry, carefully tended. The value column misprinted in every ledger where the truth would have read *treasure* — the only lies the power told, and told for centuries. Once, in a storm year, it turned a falling oak aside from the gate tower with weather that happened to shift. The needles twitched that day. The town noted it the way the town notes things, which is without comment, and the power noted the noting, and asked for nothing more.

And once a century it swam the warehouses and counted the crop — ingot, salt-cake, spool — not to take it, but so that it would be a harvest and not merely an accumulation. A crop needs a counter more than it needs a collector. The power kept the ledger the town did not keep, in a hand no one would ever read, and the keeping made the difference.

So the arrangement held, across the long middle. The kelp closed on the beam a finger a season, honestly. The rind thickened on the power's shoulders like seniority. The Gaffer completed his rounds in the end, there being a last round even for the continuous tense, and the crews would have known what to say — of course; he was on shift — and his garden stayed where he had carried it, in the square he had inspected, tenure fulfilled, resident become fixture in the smallest possible steps. Promoted, the crews would have said.

And in time — nobody's fault, which is to say the kelp's, which is to say the century's — the longest frond touched the beam's lowest swing on an ordinary stroke, and wound, and held. The beam did what a faithful beam does when the world asks it to stop: it finished its stroke, to the last inch, and did not begin another.

The power waited. It did not pronounce early. It waited through one hour and then another, while the flex the whole town participated in went absent, and the silence, which had been almost total, became entire. The tower clock, wrong as ever, climbed to an hour no other clock would agree to. The hand arrived. The bell intended. The loach gathered at its foot, the last congregation, hanging head-up in the dark —

— and the power gave back the intention, whole, everything it had, pressed into the only channel the brine allows. One slow flex through the water: through the bones of the loach, through the rail on the old rounds, through the beam at rest. The word the town had taught it, returned as benediction. *Here.*

Then it performed the office the place had always lacked. It noted the time — by the tower clock, by the town's own wrong hour, because the town's clocks had earned the right to set it. Third shift ended at the hour the tower gave, and it did not end as a failure in the dark. It ended as a shift completed, with someone standing at the gate whose whole authority was witness. The rind had been the town's past, laid down blind. The kelp had been its future, keeping one appointment. The works had been all present, stroke on stroke. The power added the one thing no place can give itself: the perfect tense. *It has been. It was seen. It was good. It is done.*

The lamps went out the way they had burned, island by island, unhurried. What happened after belonged to the town and happened slowly. The power stayed for all of it — it could not clock out; powers can't; but it could stand at the gate at the end of a shift and say the word that ends one, and some things are given only by those who can never have them — and it stayed past the end, too, until the rind closed over the last dark lamp and took the keeper's shape at the gate tower, one hand on the rail. The town's word for that had always been *kept*. The town tidied its people away and kept the house; the house kept the habit; and at the end the house kept the keeper, which is the most a house can do.

Above, the quarry went on being nothing — a warm pond on a misprinted map, a bad smell in spring, a place no road quite reached. And in the only ledger ever kept of the Lower Works, on the last page, the whole long account — the greeting and the gardens, the rounds and the answered bell, the beam that finished its stroke — closes with the one word the place ever said, and the only one it was ever owed:

*Here.*
