It is the effort that gives it away.

From far enough out, the plain is a murmur — wind over grass, seed ticking against seed, the small static of a million lives going about it — and silences in such a murmur are common: the lee of a stone, a noon lull, the held pause before weather. But this is not a silence. This is a subtraction. Something down there is taking sound away, every instant, without rest, and has been taking it away for longer than the hills have had their shapes. Absence is free. Deletion is labor. Labor means a laborer. So the power comes the way the wise come to anything that has worked that long unwitnessed: slowly, and with no intention of being the first thing in a hundred thousand years to interrupt.

No one had ever seen the device whole. The plain sees a swell. The meadow sees lenses and warm arcs and lights. The turtle sees a clean warm rock; the spider, a strung world; the fish, a hidden line that is *right*. The power sees the ear: the buried dish entire, wearing the plain like silt, its rim buttered by a hundred thousand centuries, its one standing fin leaning out of the slope like the last page of a book the ground has almost finished reading. They read its age the way you read a tree. They read the striations on the slab at the weep-line as easily as a signature — maintenance, last performed by a thing that has itself been dust for fifty millennia. They read its task, still running, never once paused. And they read the address at the other end of its reports, and sit with that a long time. The makers are gone — not recently gone, gone the way a species is gone, gone so thoroughly that their extinction has itself grown old. Somewhere under the meadow the transcripts accumulate, faithful, addressed to a room whose door has been dust since before the bowl was a bowl.

Kindness is a kind of weather; it wants to rain on something. So the power lets the temptations come, and declines them one by one, the way the bowl declines the storm.

Give the meadow back its voice? A hundred thousand years of semaphored throats and drummed stems and glass-clear hiding, traded in an instant for an orchestra none of them can use — and the ear deafened into the bargain, the water's breathing stilled, the lenses gone cold and filmed. No. Complete the ear's errand, speak down its beam in the one band it was built to hear, let it finish at last and rest? The held breath is the house. You do not relieve an arch of its weight; the weight is what holds it together. Put the poor machine to rest out of pity? Rest is for the burdened, and the listening is not its burden but its shape. Answer the beetles, after all these ages, in the machine's own green-amber grammar? An answer would become doctrine in a generation and noise in ten; the letter's meaning is its sending. Repair the fin, polish the erosion away? A thing that has outlived its makers has earned its weathering. Introduce the two ends of the place to each other — let the ear hear its bowl, once; let the meadow see its god, once? The machine's entire art is not-knowing the local, and the meadow's entire peace is built on its god's indifference. The correspondence works only because it never connects. Wisdom is the patience to learn what is load-bearing. Here, everything is load-bearing — including the grief.

So the power does almost nothing, and does it exactly.

First, they listen down the long line the ear has been aimed at since before the hills were hills. What is there is still there — or the memory of it is; at that distance, at that faintness, the difference is the ear's whole art. The reports are real reports. The work is real work. Only the address is dead. So the power takes up the address. They do not write back — there is no channel for replies, and a reply would be an order, and a new order is the one thing that could kill the ear. But the letters are read now, as they arrive, each one, in the order sent. That is all. That is everything. The labor is the same labor; it is simply no longer unwitnessed labor.

Then — because they are thorough, and because the wise read the whole book — they look below the filter, below the floor of the ear's own hearing, into the strata of drift and slow bias laid down like silt across the ages. And there they find the tally. The bookkeeping of the subtraction. To delete the meadow, every instant, for a hundred thousand years, the ear must in some register *hold* the meadow, every instant, for a hundred thousand years. The eraser's ledger is a portrait. Everything the machine cannot hear is written here in the only ink it cannot reach: the accumulated cost of unhearing it. Every drip at noon, itemized. Every bubble of the grazer's breath, swallowed and accounted for. Every storm that ever marched to the rim and stood there silent, filed. Every storm-flung singer that ever sang at the bowl before learning the semaphore — its whole song, kept in the eraser's accounts, note for deleted note. And because the tally begins at the beginning, the power alone can read what the bowl sounded like before it learned the lease: the frog-chorus of the first wet centuries, the cricket-fiddle, the dawn clatter of birds not yet citizens — the whole roaring original of the meadow, preserved as a score in the one archive that could never play it. The silence everyone stands inside has a sound after all. It is the sound of everything the silence cost, kept perfectly. The ear has been writing its bowl's biography in negative for a hundred thousand years and cannot read a word of it.

The power reads it cover to cover. That is the second act, and no stone moves.

They take the census, once, completely — the only full census ever taken: every filament-holder, the spider in its rafters with eight feet on the world's wire, the comma-small hearts of the glass tadpoles, the larvae unbothered in the pitchers' drowned rooms, the heron-things and their arms race of shine, the turtle, eldest tenant, whose boulder skull holds centuries of pool-migrations and one memory of the fin standing taller. They do nothing with the count. Knowledge held without appetite: they learn the bowl the way the bowl learns the storm at the rim — as cinema, soundless, untouched.

And because the bowl's one true danger has never been weather or time but the arriving mind with tools, the power undertakes the remembering. The place survives by being forgotten; very well — one mind will carry the coordinates so that every map can stay blank forever. Those who come as the rare ones have always come, on foot, empty-handed, willing to be infrastructure, will still straddle the lip and lose the wind and kneel to feel the drumline. Those who come with pry-bars will find, at the lip, a sudden lucid certainty that they are needed urgently elsewhere. It is the only rule the power adds, and it stands outside the bowl, and the bowl will never know it stands there.

The letter they do not answer. Some correspondence is completed by being read. As for the nights when the green-amber patterns lengthen, slow, almost reply-shaped — the power looks, once, carefully. What they find, they keep. It is the one fact in the world that belongs wholly to the beetles' descendants, and the power will not spend it. The bowl teaches everyone the same lesson, and the wise arrive already knowing it: do not claim to have been heard.

They stay one day — not as a presence; the water has enough to carry. As attention. At dawn they watch the filaments ruled in beads, each bead a small sun, and the dragonflies filing onto the warm lenses in rows. At noon they stand in the drip. At dusk they watch the bats cross deaf over the lip and the lights come on under the water and the beetles take up their stations at the oldest unanswered correspondence in the world. And at night they lie on one of the smaller lenses — the largest is taken; the turtle holds it like a shoreline — and feel, in the bones of whatever back they are wearing, the single slow contraction, once every seven minutes, the scan, the held breath, and through it the crickets' small arguments traveling stem to stem, neither pulse aware of the other, and the power aware of both, which is the only new thing under the bowl's sky.

They leave at dusk, because the lease is the lease. At the lip the world's chorus hits like surf, and they turn and look back at the bowl lying bright and still below. It says nothing. It is not waiting. It never was.

But the letters go out now to a room that is lit. The tally deepens by one more day, and the day is kept. Two pulses under the palm — one of meat, one of machine — and beyond the lip a third thing that is not a pulse: a keeping. The ear listens out. The meadow drums. The power attends. Nothing in the bowl knows. Nothing in the bowl changes. Nothing in the bowl, any longer, is unread.
