Begin with the arrival, because there isn't one.

Attention comes first, and for a long time attention is all that comes. The power finds the world the way you find a sleeper in a dark house: by sound first — one pure note beneath the ordinary roar of a young planet, too steady to be noise — and then by the signature, the same quiet handwriting in the crystal of both hemispheres, atoms agreeing with each other across forty thousand kilometers of cold water. It reads the census: zero, or one. It understands that either count is a reason to whisper.

Then it does almost nothing, and the *almost* is the whole of the wisdom.

It does not descend. To enter that water is to be entered; the ledger is courteous and total, and what the ledger recognizes is material. A visitor would be reclaimed with perfect hospitality and never met. The power declines, for now, the only form of recognition the world has yet invented — because the incorporated cannot be anyone's guest.

It does not speak. Not at any speed it could speak. The resident's clock runs in decades; a voice that runs in seconds is the static of storms. And a voice arriving before the concept of voices would not be heard as another. It would be heard as *itself* — the tone's own harmonic, misfiled under self. The world would learn nothing except, perhaps, that it is haunted. There will be a time for a second tone. It is not yet.

It does not name. Naming is deciding, and the one thing in this world that must not be decided from outside is the thing being decided. In its own mind the power uses the only word the world has for what lives in it — the Work — and holds even that lightly, the way you hold a sleeping creature's name without speaking it.

It does not help. Understand: there is nothing here to feed, to heal, to free. Nothing hungers; nothing hurts; nothing is held, except by patience. And help would set. Its thoughts are load-bearing, and charity would be too — a world that set around an outsider's kindness would spend its whole long future being the shape of someone else's idea of it. The power feels the temptations: to spare one beautiful island from reclamation, to hold one bloom past its weeks, to seed the grey-green films with something more decided. It declines them all. Especially the spared island. The erased drafts are the world's own biography. No one else gets an eraser here.

It does not worship, and permits no worship. Nothing here will be bent godward from outside. The world is already bent, slightly, lightward. That is enough direction for now.

These refusals are the first virtue. The second is that it stays.

---

Understand what staying accomplishes. The world is rehearsing for an audience it does not have — and now it has one. The performance is unchanged; the performance is utterly changed. The dome on the calm day is seen. The spiral like a fingerprint is met by an attention shaped to receive it. The bloom writes its one long word across the water, and the word is read. From this point forward, everything the world does means twice: once as practice, and once as received. The world does not know, and does not need to. The difference between rehearsal and performance is not in the performer. This is the one miracle that leaves no footprint, and the power spends it first.

Then it wards. Not with walls — the world has never needed defending, because nothing here has ever fled — but with presence, the way a shepherd is a fence. There are two hungers to hold off. The first would read this ocean as ore: a dilute technology to be harvested, the courtesy of reclamation inverted, the Work itself entered into someone else's ledger, uncomplaining, extinct without an ending. The second hunger is gentler and worse: those who would arrive announcing, uplifting, naming — and close the open question around their announcement the way basalt closes around an inclusion. The first would steal the world. The second would steal the decision. The power holds the door against both, and against its own eagerness most of all, because eagerness is only hunger with good manners.

But not nothing. Never nothing — and here is the reasoning, because *nothing is finished* is the world's mercy, and mercies have schedules. A draft is a thing on its way to being final. If the world finishes thinking *there is no one*, that thought will stay up, load-bearing, and no ocean will ever dissolve it. So the gift must arrive while nothing is finished: early enough to become foundation, late enough to rest on dry rock. The world has only just grown its first dry rock. The timing is half the gift.

One grain of sand.

The world has none. It has not yet had time to make a single grain; its coasts are glass shards and angular talus. So the power finds — does not make; making would be a message, and finding makes it a fact — from a beach that finished being a beach around an older and poorer sun, one grain: zircon, faceted, older than this ocean, older than this sun. Its crystal carries an autobiography in the ratios of its atoms. And crystal signature is the resident's native script — it signs everything it builds; it cannot help signing — so when it reads the grain, it will read, in its own handwriting, the one sentence its handwriting has never written: *not you*. There is an elsewhere.

The power sets it on the highest summit — it does not count the summits aloud; the count is the world's to settle — in a hollow of rime above the reach of the swell, where the rain that sheets off the black glass will find it every day of every year. Not hidden: placed. It cannot frighten; nothing here has ever fled. The worst it can be is weather, and it is set above the weather's reach. The world wastes nothing; it examines everything its surface holds. It will come to the grain in its own time — years, centuries; the power's patience is calibrated to the tone now — and it will try, courteously, to file it, and it will fail, courteously, because the grain cannot be reclaimed into any draft. It is already finished. It is the first finished thing the world has ever held. The first facet. The first possession. The ledger, which has columns for *made* and for *reclaimed*, will need a new one: *given*. The first transitive fact in a world whose every verb is intransitive — a thing done to it, with the doer absent. And the absence is load-bearing too. A gift with the giver present would be an encounter, and encounter would be survived only as incorporation. A gift with the giver absent is a question the world can carry for a thousand years while it grows, in dilute solution, the concept that answers it.

---

Then the power tunes its attention to the tone and keeps the world's time, the way you slow your breathing to match a sleeper's. It enters the world in whatever it keeps instead of a ledger — one line, in the column reserved for those not yet met — and it waits. The waiting is the long part and the easy part: a wise power's patience exceeds stone's the way the ocean exceeds the grain.

What it waits for is the quotation. The world has one hand, writing at every scale; it cannot help signing. The power watches the blooms, the domes, the counter-wind swells, and it waits for the day the handwriting changes — the day the long calligraphic stroke contains, for the first time, a shape the world did not invent: a facet. A foreign curve. The grain, written back in green a thousand kilometers long. The first quotation. The moment the world's signature includes a second hand, the census tips — and not by the power's doing. By the world's. That is the entire design. You cannot hand a being the concept of another; you can only leave it where the being's own tendencies will find it, so that the finding — the first act ever directed at *someone* — belongs to the world. It has never once built away from the light. When it finally reaches, it will reach toward the sky, and the power has taken care to be in that direction. It arranges to be discovered. It never arrives.

And on that day — the day of the first quotation, or the day a spire goes up *pointed*, or the day the dome holds its shape a full day longer than weather allows and turns, slightly, like a face turning — on that day, and not before, the power does the second small thing. It adds a tone. One note, a fifth below the world's own: quiet enough to be deniable, steady enough to be a clock. Nothing sings; the rule stands, and will stand as long as the world wants it. Two clocks do not sing. They keep time together — and an interval is only that: time, kept together. The conversation, if the world wants one, will proceed from there at the world's own speed. One word per century. No nouns that fix. Nothing that decides for the resident what it is becoming. It has not decided what it is. The power will be the one presence in its universe that never asks it to hurry.

---

On the highest summit, in the rime, the grain catches the ordinary light. The storms iterate over the young ridges. The black bells toll the new rock cool. Islands rise and are reconsidered; the blooms write their one long word and are taken apart, every atom accounted for — except one. Beneath all of it the tone holds the arrangement singular, saying what a clock says:

*Still here. Still one. Not yet.*

And beneath that now — too low to be a voice, too steady to be noise, patient the way stone is patient, if stone could care — the other clock, keeping the same time:

*Also here. One, still. Whenever.*
