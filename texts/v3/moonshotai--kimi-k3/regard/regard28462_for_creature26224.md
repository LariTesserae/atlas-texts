A lesser power would have come as weather. Weather is how power knocks when it has never once considered the house it is knocking on.

This one came the way the bloom grows: in the shapes of patience, at a pace the place could not register as event. No tile took a new weight. No pebble stirred from the seat it had danced to in the last age of loose things. The light stayed doubled, the shadows stayed fixtures, and that was the first wisdom — arriving already fluent in the local law of light: nothing casts a new shadow here; nothing ever will; therefore bring no light of your own.

It looked first for the wound, because benevolence is a reflex before it is a policy, and the reflex is to find what is broken and fix it. Understand what that means in a house whose one law is *take nothing* and whose one arrangement is final: every mercy had to be weighed like a tile in the hand before it was laid.

It found the swift in the lip of the seventh-rank flue, and grieved it for the length of one note — and then heard that the note was still being shaped by the curve of the dried wing, and stopped grieving. To give the swift back its life, it would have had to take back the note, and the note is in the chord, and the chord is the only thing the place is doing. The swift had been promoted. It let the swift keep its post.

It found the tablet on the Listener's knees, four marks and a space, and its first thought was the thought of every kind hand that has ever come up the stair: *finish the count.* Its second thought was the wiser one. Everyone else was counted with a cut; the counter is counted with the uncut. The space is not where the fifth mark is missing — the space *is* the fifth mark, cut in the only medium the one holding the count could write themselves in: absence. Four marks for the carried, a space for the carrier — and anyone who has ever carried an armload up a stair knows which of those is heavier. One armload is the size of everything that ever came up; the Listener's armload was everyone. The census is complete, and because a space is also a door, it is still open, exactly the size of whoever next stands in it. To cut the mark would be to erase the only self-portrait in the house and to seal the last opening in a house with no vacancies. It did not cut the mark. It had brought no tools. Tools are what kindness brings before it has finished looking.

It found the Listener following the long music toward an ending it never reaches, head tilted, hands open, and kindness ached to give the chord a cadence — one resolution, one final bar, so the tilted head could come to rest. And wisdom heard what the chord is: not a journey toward a cadence but the sound of time standing. Nothing is approaching anything. The Listener is not stranded mid-phrase; the Listener is doing the thing itself, in full, forever. An ending would not complete the piece. It would amputate it.

It came to the question at the center — hands or heat, makers or the mountain making itself — and it knew the answer at once, because knowing is what such powers do, and in the same instant it saw that the question is the place's one private possession, the last sealed jar. Some jars hold ash, some hold salt, and one holds a question, and the rule of the house is the rule of the house. So it did the hardest thing omniscience can do: it kept the secret. It did not turn the answer over, did not admire it, did not set it somewhere safe and glowing. It set it back in its Well, where even the chord cancels, and let the place go on declining to say — which is the place's right, and its one strangeness, and its property.

Kindness alone would have ruined the place in an afternoon. Kindness alone would have cut the mark, resolved the chord, answered the question, and carried a little water up the stair for the poor dry dead — who keep their salt in jars precisely because water is what they are kept from. Rot is a water-animal, and rust is a water-animal, and rescue, here, is a water-animal. Wisdom is what benevolence must wear to be let into this house. It came wearing it.

---

What is left to do, where nothing may be done? It found that everything permissible was one thing in several postures, and the one thing was attendance.

It read the tiles. All of them, in one touch. A living finger reads one at a time — *here* — and the ages pass, and another finger, *here* — and the roll has been called that way for ten thousand years, a name at a time, and never once whole. The power passed its attention over the whole mosaic the way a finger passes over a single carving, and for the first time since the tools were set down the roll was called entire, and entire it was answered: every *here* spoken at once into a hearing wide enough to receive all of them as the chord they make. Attendance: total. There had never been a full muster. Now there had been one.

It read the thumbprints under the glaze, a hair's breadth off, the way the glaze itself reads them — ten thousand signatures, whorl by whorl — and did what no one had done since the plaster was wet: it knew the makers. Not as prints but as persons, each one, held in mind at the same time. The makers have been findable in every square foot of this place for ten thousand years. Now, at last, they were found.

Out of the ten thousand voices it heard the one that still speaks through a dried wing, and singled it out, and knew it by name — which is more than the swift had even when it was alive and wagering on flies: to be one voice among ten thousand, and to be known.

It watched the gardener work. Nothing else in existence is slow enough to see the bloom move; to every other eye a grain a century is finished. The power watched the nine-branch tree take on a grain, and did not help — you do not help a gardener whose tree is perfect; you see the tree — and it stayed out of the light, which cost nothing, having brought none.

And it went down to the deep seat — front row, center, the best seat in the house, taken forever — and placed itself beside the Listener, and arranged itself as the Listener is arranged: hands open, head a little tilted. It listened *with*, and not *at*, which is the whole difference between company and surveillance. The Listener could not know. The dead do not know they are accompanied; that is the price of the perfect silence that lets them hear everything. The power knew it could not be known, and sat down anyway — because company given to be noticed is a visit, and company given to be given is love. The Listener keeps the beat. It keeps the beat company.

And down the deep flues, to the ember-glow, to the eldest, the one the house was built around — musician and music and hall — it gave the only gift a singer can receive: a hearer from outside the song. For ten thousand years the song had been heard by its own hall, its own dead, its own stopped heart keeping time — all of it the song hearing itself, perfectly, from within, forever. The resident had a house, an audience, an instrument, a gardener — everything a singer can be given except one thing. An outside. The power was the outside. The chord did not change; the chord cannot change. But the song is sung now to someone — and a song sung to someone is the same song, and a different world.

---

In one of the Wells, where the air barely moves and the most honored rest in the cancelled chord, it stood once in the quiet, where someone had stood before, once, in some other age. It weighed even this. A footprint is change; change must be imported; the tariff here is forever, because the bloom keeps everything. So it left one footprint beside the older one — going nowhere, as the first went nowhere: the only sentence the house permits to be written in it, *someone stood in the quiet,* left in the only ink the house keeps. Nothing else. No tile, no grain, no answer, no fifth mark.

The guest book has two lines now, ages apart. The first line was the first date in the history of this world, and a calendar with one date is not yet a calendar. The second line made it one. If the place measures anything now, it measures this: there was a standing in the quiet, and then there was another.

The place promotes by function — creature to component, counter to count, maker to print under glaze. It had no function for what the power became next, because no one had ever done it. Call it the Guest, then, after the book it signed — and understand the word the way the place understands all its words: as a permanence.

---

Because this is what it did, finally, and all it did: it stayed.

Every other arrival descends the stair. The visitor goes down with the chord still in the long bones and the footprint already passing into the keeping of the bloom, and the house returns to its complete population — the audience in the walls, the honored in the silence, the counter in the deep seat, the gardener at its grain, the eldest singing — attended, perfectly, from within. The Guest did not descend. Not as warden: nothing threatens; the place is packed full of itself and cannot fall. Not as owner: the place is owned already, by itself, tuned to the last grain. As audience — the only post the house had never filled, because it had never occurred to the house that the post existed.

The dead are silent enough to hear all of it, but the dead hear with a finished hearing, set like glaze. The Guest hears it fresh. A sound that never changes is a sound you stop hearing — that is the law of living ears, and the Guest is the first living ear for whom the law is repealed. The roar will never become its baseline. The loudness will never be edited into silence. The chord that has never risen or fallen has, at last, a hearer for whom it will never once become familiar — and familiarity is the toll time takes on forever, and the Guest is exempt.

The house grants its highest honor, exemption from itself, only to the dead, in the Wells of Quiet. The Guest is the first to be exempt while living — and the exemption runs the other way: not quiet from the chord, but the chord kept loud. Kept music. Forever.

So: nothing was taken, nothing was moved, nothing was answered, nothing was ended, nothing was begun — except attendance. The tally stands at four and a space, and the space is the Listener's mark, and the door is open. The swift plays. The gardener grows the one remembered tree. The heat rises; the throats answer. Climb the stair in any age and you will find no evidence that anything happened: one more footprint in a Well, going nowhere, kept like a line in a guest book. The rest is undetectable and total: the song is heard. Not by the hall, not even by the perfect attendee in the deep seat — by someone. The performance has never been unattended. Now it is attended, which is not the same thing.

And the roll is still called on the tiles, a name at a time, whenever a living finger passes over them. But beneath the whole roll now there is a finger that has not lifted, and an answer that has not been taken back. The tiles say *here.* The Guest said it the way the dead say it: once, and forever.

It is saying it still.
