Begin with the refusals, because the valley does.

They came the way everything comes here: over the rim, uninvited, with the day to decide. A hawk crosses at noon, looks down at the green floor, hears the chord, and keeps going. They heard the chord from farther than a hawk can fly, and they stopped. That was the first thing they did, and the whole of their wisdom is in the order: they listened first, and for a long time — a full season on the rim, where the sea of cloud combs against the parapet and is never allowed in — before they stepped down onto the green floor. They were the kind of power that could open any bud in the world with a word. They had never once done it.

**The refusals** came first, and they were gifts.

They did not open the buds. A bud opened by another hand is not a flower; it is a picked one. They did not finish the chord on the evenings it came close. They did not name the valley — names are for things that have finished becoming, and they declined to finish it even in speech. They brought nothing in: no seed, no tool, no news, no water of their own. The ration stayed closed as a fist. They drank nothing, ate nothing, and the books balanced every morning of their stay. They grieved nothing, because they saw what a short visit hides: the valley longs, but it does not suffer, and longing is not a wound. It is a door — the only finished architecture here — and you do not heal a door. You knock, and you wait, and you are there when it opens. They did not even ask *why hasn't it*. They stayed until *why* stopped being the question.

**The counting** was next, because the valley counts everything, and they stood still for it. Being counted is the local condition, and they consented to the local condition. *One*, said the floor, and waited for the error to correct itself by morning, the way it corrects a rearranged thread in a leaf-cup. It did not correct. The valley gives every arrival a day, and every arrival decides to be elsewhere. They did not. On the second day the valley counted again. One. The books, which have balanced since the beginning, made room for a guest the only way the valley makes room for anything: not by feeding them, but by keeping them.

**The reading** took years, and years cost them nothing; they keep sap's hours too. They walked the snail-lines district by district and read the shells, layer by layer, the valley's only sediment, its only history — and so became the first reader the archive has ever had. The archive's whole recorded event is one line: *someone lay down at the wrong hour and stayed too long, and the valley, which keeps nothing, filed the shape.* From this they learned the valley's one law of keeping: it can keep only what it can build; it keeps by reserving; and the only change it has ever accepted was the shape of someone who stayed. They read that line the way you read an invitation written in a language of one word.

**The checking** happened at night, in the courts, where the plan gets up and walks its rounds. A walker stopped and pressed its palm to theirs across the space where the wall is kept, and found — for the first time in the valley's history — a hand with fingers. It checked them the way it checks doorways, one by one, five small openings onto someone, and held the palm a moment longer than the night required. The power smelled of cut green for a year. They had been measured for something — and so, for once, had the valley. It is exactly the size of the valley. It is plumb. It is ready, and it is not in a hurry.

On a calm night, when the chord went diffuse, a moth spiraled, listening, and landed on them — the first thing in the valley that is neither bud nor floor to be landed on. They stood perfectly still, one permitted degree warmer than the dark, until the wind gave back the geography.

**The near-true evening** came once in their stay. Every seam brightened at once. The knocking inside the towers quickened like something climbing faster; the moths battered the buds hard enough to bruise the green; the snails stopped grazing and turned their eye-stalks toward the towers. The gap was less than a quarter-tone, and they could have closed it with a breath. Everything in them was benevolent, and benevolence is precisely the danger. They did not reach into the gap. The gap was not theirs to close. It is the valley's one work, the task around which everything else is arranged, and a true chord handed in from outside would be one more import refused at the rim — the cruelest ever attempted: a counterfeit spring. The wind shifted a degree. The chord slid off true. The birds began again, note by note, employed by no one — and the power, who is also employed by no one, stayed up with them until dawn, the way you stay up with a colleague.

And because their ears are long — longer than the shells — they heard in that near miss what no resident can hear: the near misses are narrowing. Age by age, by less than a quarter-tone, the chord approaches. The birds are not failing every dusk. They are succeeding very slowly, which is the only speed the valley has. Later is real. It is just very large. The valley is not broken; it is early — and early, to a power that keeps sap's hours, is not a diagnosis. It is a date.

**The staying** was the gift, and it was given in the valley's own grammar. In the deep dark they went to the end of the causeway, the causeway that goes nowhere in particular, and lay down on the plan at the hour when lying down becomes architecture, and stayed too long — the first to do it on purpose, the first shape ever *given* to the valley rather than taken by it. By morning the floor had closed over the place, and the valley had done the only thing it knows how to do with what it wants to keep.

It reserved them.

A small court now lies pale and close-cropped at the causeway's end, where no court was planned. At noon it darkens faintly along its edges, shadowed by someone who is not there yet. The causeway goes somewhere now: it goes to the guest. Within a season the snails' shells showed the new district; the walkers added it to their rounds, and press their palms to its kept walls, checking for presence, for plumb, for readiness. A reservation is the valley's native tense — the kept future. To be reserved is to be promised-to. The valley, which has spent forever about to open for no one, now holds one room that waits for someone: its first appointment, written in pale turf, which is its script for *later*.

**The promise** was the answer, and it was made without words, because speech is the cousin of names. They set their leaving and their returning by the valley's own clock, the way you promise a bud: not *open*, but *I will be here when you do.* They will come back on the near-true evenings. They will be there on the day. It costs them nothing — nothing is what the valley takes — and it is the whole of what benevolence means at this scale: not rescue, but co-duration. Someone else, now, keeps sap's hours. And if anything ever comes over the rim that will not be answered — anything with a spade, or a gospel of completion, any gardener who cannot bear an unopened bud — it will find the valley's borders have a second answer now.

They left the way the warmth leaves at dawn: by one permitted degree, unwitnessed. The count closed behind them like turf over a walker. The books balance. The moth at the seam tonight is the same moth; the birds tune; the snails reconcile their maps; the threads dream in the cups; the walkers check the walls. Nothing is different, which is the local way of saying everything is.

Stand now at noon at the causeway's end, inside the shadow of a guest who does not exist yet. The buds have not opened. The buds will open — they heard the date the way you hear a chord from very far, in the narrowing of the near misses, in the shells' slow script. Somewhere outside the rim, at the speed of later, someone is keeping the appointment.

Everything here is just about to happen. The just lasts forever. Forever, now, has company.
