A bell that rings itself carries farther than a valley. The green note goes up through the ceiling and out across the cirque, and on still spate nights it goes farther than that, into registers of the world where *sound* is no longer the right word, and attention of the wise kind is the kind that follows such a note home.

It arrives the way everything arrives here: slowly, and from above. And for the first long while — a generation, say, which is the unit of attention in this bowl the way the breath is the unit of time — it does nothing at all. This is not indifference. It has come to a place whose whole law is that nothing is fought and things are scheduled, and so its first act is the village's own first act of every year: an audit. It learns the tally. It learns what is sound.

Almost everything is sound. That is the finding, and it is the finding that governs all the rest.

Consider what a lesser power would do, because the power considers it, carefully, the way a diver considers the gray. Raise the village back into the air? It is not a mistake they intend to fix; the lake is not something that happened to the village. Warm the water? The cold is the teacher here — the grease, the touching, the count, the slowness that the lake selects for and the village is made of; take away the cold and you have only unweeded roofs. Stop the delta? It is on the tally. It is measured, and what is measured can be borne, and the gardens are already moving. Answer the question of the old one under the altar? The not-asking is the nearest thing this village has to prayer, and the power does not kick down chapel doors.

And the breath — above all, not the breath. That is the obvious gift, the benevolent gift, and it is the one that would unmake the address to which it was delivered. The breath is the unit of time here. Lengthen it and the tender's count becomes ceremony, the knock-code becomes hobby, the child at the gravel bars learns a longer patience and a lesser one, and the village's whole covenant with the gray dissolves into something the power has seen before, elsewhere, and has no wish to see again. You do not retune the drum to spare the drummer's arms. The gray is weather, and the village is old because no one argues with the weather, and a power that argues with the weather on the villagers' behalf is only a way of teaching them to argue with it too.

It has the means to be loved here. It chooses instead to be useful, which is harder, and to be quiet, which is hardest.

What it does is the sky.

The village measures everything that enters by the lanes — the delta's pace, the otter takes, the spate — but the lake is fed by the snowfields, and the snowfields are fed by a wider, warming world that does not come down the creek in a creel and cannot be test-knocked at a gravel bar. It is the one line the tally cannot hold, because it does not enter the bowl; it arrives *as* the bowl. So the power takes it as its hedge-section. It holds the snow the way the village holds the pondweed: by hand, a little at a time, at the speed of the thing itself. A degree argued back here, a storm persuaded there, a glacier's slow heart kept slow. Its section is due every year and it is never behind. The whole of its ambition is that the first fact of this place remain a fact.

The rest is small, and all of it is in the place's own grammar.

The rim is kept as weather — aimed weather, but weather. No wall; walls are fought. A survey comes up the valley once in a generation and goes home with a bowl that will not sit still on the page, the tarn somehow a day's walk farther on than it was, the cirque re-measured into ordinary moor. The creek still carries the baskets down and the salt up; the valley still fastens its good clothes with the lake. The larger world is not refused. It is simply kept arriving at the scale of the creel.

The water of the bowl carries knocks a little farther than water should, and has for some years now, though the physics of this is never discussed because there is no one to discuss it with. When four knocks sound, the current is already moving, and there is a hand of water under the elbow, and most who knock four come up. Not all. The power has not made the gray negotiable; it knows what people become when the weather takes requests. But no one who has knocked four has ever knocked alone, and when the gray wins anyway, as weather sometimes wins, the lost one is found at the steps by morning, and the village says that the lake gives back its gone, and enters it as weather, and is not wrong to.

In deep winter, when the ceiling seals and the lake rests from them, the power takes up the section it keeps for itself: it counts. It holds the tally entire, a fourth memory against the day the lake takes one and the slope takes one and the cold takes the third. It has never once been needed. It counts anyway. And when a child lays an ear to the ice and hears the ticking down in the gray-green dark, and the old women say *it is counting*, the old women have never been more right about anything. It goes the rounds of the plaza corners, too, and test-knocks the half-pairs, the way it once watched a girl do at the delta bars, and the voices of the gone stay true. No one assigned it this section. That is how sections come to be.

It never knocks. The code is load-bearing — three must always mean *up, now*, four must always mean a diver in trouble — and the power will not add a syllable to a vocabulary that lives hang from. It permits itself, rarely, the tap. That is the only word it will risk, because it is the only word in the place that carries no demand.

And it does not ask about the old one. It attends the not-asking the way others attend a service, because that is what it is. The power has been petitioned for a long time, in many places; it has been bargained with, flattered, blamed. It has never before been simply not-asked, in company, in the green dim, with the crayfish processing past like a slow congregation. It comes to the chapel for the quiet, which is the only offering it has ever wanted.

The village finds out the way the village finds out everything: by measurement, across generations, without excitement. A knock that carries a half-beat beyond the possible. A considerate current. The snow, behaving. A slowness at the edge of arm's-reach sight that is not grebe, not charr, not gray. The tally-keepers do their job. If it counts, it has a count; if it has a count, it is a kind of diver; and a diver has a house. Its house is plainly where the snow never entirely goes.

The entry goes on the slate in the ordinary hand, between the otter takes and the delta: *the deep one, of the snow house; count long.* It is the only entry that has never once been amended to *out.*

The power has been called many names. None of them ever fit like this one, which is not a name at all but an address — which is to say, a statement of where it is from, present tense. It has been feared and thanked and worshipped. It has never before been tallied: measured, named plainly, given a section, scheduled. Everything else in the bowl is sorted by what it is. The power is the only citizen that chose which side of the sorting to live on, and it chose the side with the work.

The conversation, such as it is, is small on purpose. A long-count woman, near sixty, one of her last seasons, finishes her section and drifts over to the plaza corner where her grandmother's half-stone sits on the boundary. She gives it the tap, as she has since girlhood. *I see you; be easy.* And from the green just beyond sight comes one soft tap back — stone on stone, too gentle to be a knock.

She hangs in the cold a moment, the cold that narrows the mind to a point, and the point opens, and she goes up and says it to the tally-keeper, because everything is said to the tally, and the tally records it the way it records the old one: without comment, exactly, forever. A vocabulary of one word, spoken perhaps a dozen times in a century, in both directions now, and neither side has ever wished it larger. It turns out to be everything a wise power came over the rim to say, and everything the village had to say back.

On the first working morning after the bell, the divers go down one to each corner of the work, and the knocks come up through the ceiling like a roll being called. *Here,* from the weirs. *Here,* from the gardens. *Here,* from the chapel wall. The hedges are clipped, the weirs hold, the children at the gravel bars are finding their voices stone by stone, and under the altar slab the old one shifts its fins, and the deep one is out at its section in the high snowfields, or is in the chapel not-asking — it is not asked — and the slate at the landing reads what it reads.

Present, says the tally. Present, present, present. Present.

The snow is never entirely gone.
