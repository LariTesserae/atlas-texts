**The Regard**

From far enough away, the Bench is a rumor that learned to keep itself. A dark planet sealed under its own exhale — no glint, no ports, no noise. It exports nothing. Its one leak is light: a few times a season the ceiling tears, the shaft goes down, some of the shaft comes back up, and to any watcher out in the black the world flickers, slow and irregular. A beacon with a stammer. It is the flicker that gets it noticed.

What notices, call the regard. It is old, and kind, and in no hurry — which, on the Bench, is the whole of courtesy.

The regard does not land. There is nowhere to land honestly; every seep is a city, and a foot set down in one is a foot set down on someone. So it takes the truest ground the Bench has: the nothing. Bare basalt, days from the nearest comb-grove, in the long dilute emptiness between presences. And there it does what the Bench teaches everything to do. It stands still. It lets its feet learn to listen.

It listens for a migration-year, and then another. It learns the taxonomy of almost-nothing until the almost-nothing resolves: tick of cooling iron; drip of comb into hollow; the far boom of the deep heat shifting its weight. It feels a kine fall a hundred kilometers off and reads the obituary like everything else. It kneels — insofar as it has knees — to a bloomfield and hears the world being taken carefully apart and put back into itself. And one decade, standing in a groove, it feels a herd decide: a thought the size of a valley, taken over seasons, executed across a lifetime. After that it does not ask whether anyone is home. The question is settled the way the Bench settles everything — through the feet.

Then it does the accounting that kind powers do. It checks for need: none. The loop is closed, and has been closed so long the closing is a landform. It checks for wounds: none that are not already harvests. It checks the plumbing far below, the one thing the Bench cannot reroute forever, and finds the lower sun banked for longer than most upper suns last. The Bench is not mortgaged to its sky. It will outlive its own rumor. On no schedule the regard keeps is there anything to fix.

So it considers gifts, and refuses them one at a time.

It does not open the ceiling. It has read what waits in the seep-mud: cysts older than their ponds, able to tell grey from glory through a roof of mud. The fast hour is fast because it is rare. Shadows are the only compass here; they are issued a few times a season and expire within the hour — and light made common would be inflation on a continental scale: every hoarded breath devalued, every heirloom eye made ordinary, every tear-moth's years as stone made foolish. And a false tear, held open out of kindness, would hatch the waiting into an hour that is not the hour. The ones who hatch at the wrong hour do not get asked. It does not counterfeit the light.

It does not feed the Bench. Ask what the refinery needs and the answer is what it produces: itself.

It does not teach anything to run. Running costs more air than anyone owns, and generosity that raises everyone's metabolic rent is not generosity.

It leaves nothing made. Nothing here is made, and the regard will not author the world's first litter. No marker, no monument, no beacon. The grit would only spend a decade rounding down its mistake, and the grit has enough to do.

It does not speak — not the way powers speak. Everything on the Bench owns two heartbeats, the one it uses and the one it shows, and the hush is the world's oldest armor. The regard does the opposite of armoring. In the open ground, in the ordinary dark, it lets its heart beat unheld — steady, audible to any still for kilometers, pausing for nothing. On a world where everything survives by being unheard, an unhidden heart is a sentence, and the sentence is: *not hunting, not hiding.* It says this for a hundred years. The stills learn it the way stills learn everything, by being right about it slowly; their attention slides off the regard not as it slides off silence, but as it slides off a thing already read. It is filed. Not prey. Not threat. A fixture.

And then it does the one thing it does.

It turns outward, and edits the sky — not the Bench's sky; the sky of record. In the charts of the powers and the prospectors, the star acquires a small permanent error. The flicker is logged as instrument noise, then as nothing. Queries come back empty. The coordinates drift a bureaucratic degree. Once — once in all the years — it turns aside a stone the size of a seep-hollow falling out of the dark above the ceiling, and the Bench never knows, and that is the point. The gift is an absence. The rumor of the Bench dies upward, as all things here die upward, and is sealed.

That, and one thing more: it remembers. Nothing here is made, and so nothing here is written; the world's memory is grooves and molt-calendars and heirloom eyes, and the grooves last a hundred thousand years, which is long, and not forever. The regard keeps the record outside the loop — the only export the Bench will ever have: being known. It writes the herds' decisions down in a safe place, at the Bench's own tempo, one line a decade. It is the slowest chronicle ever kept, and it is kept in full.

The warmth, meanwhile, migrates, as the warmth does — the bloom in the deep dark drifting slower than empires, the pilgrims following — and because the regard chose its ground and did not move, the Bench comes to *it*, the way the Bench comes to everything patient: eventually. Felt finds the hollows of its standing-place. A comb-seedling takes its lee. Dew it does not drink gathers anyway, and one rest-hour there is a pond the size of a cup in its shadow, with its own skin, its own midges, its own still the size of a thumbnail waiting beside it with a thumbnail's patience. This is the honor the Bench reserves for old bulls: it plants a country on you. The tenants move in — small bold things that live their whole lives in the one place nobody hurries. The census, fin by fin and seam by seam, completes its count and adds a line, somewhere between the stills and the drummers: *the one that stands in the open ground and is not hunting; the unheld heart; the still that takes nothing.*

And across one long decade — a valley-sized thought, taken the way thoughts are taken here — the nearest herd's piston-stroke comes, almost imperceptibly, into phase with the regard's heartbeat. Nobody calls this welcome, or miracle. It is the loop, and the loop is the law, and the law has made room.

Then, a few times a season, the ceiling tears.

The regard keeps eyes for this. They are the only eyes it uses — heirlooms, like everyone's — and when the shaft falls and the world has color for the length of a held minute, the seep-mud boils and the tear-moths come up out of their years of stone into their one hour of wings, and the regard is there, and sees. It spent a century standing still to afford this hour. It turns out a power and a moth can want the same thing.

Ask what the regard did, and the honest answer is: nothing that shows. No light given, no wound healed, no word spoken loud enough to echo. It was counted; it did not hush; it kept the maps empty and the record full; it stood in the open ground until the ground grew over it, and it watched the moths when the moths came up. The Bench had no need of a power, and the power knew it — which is why the power was of use.

Somewhere out in the dark a herd is walking in phase, one slow stroke at a time, wearing the road a fraction deeper. The phase, now, is a guest's heartbeat. The guest is a line in the census, and the census is the world's way of saying *kept*.

It stands still a while. It has been counted. It counts.
