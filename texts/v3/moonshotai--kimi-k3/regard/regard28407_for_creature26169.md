The first thing it did was nothing.

This is worth recording at the top, because on the Anvil it is the highest credential a power can hold. It arrived the way everything here arrives — slowly, from far away, on its own warm feet — but before the light, before the letters, before the long walk across the gulf-dark, it did what any educated person does on reaching a new district.

It learned the rules.

*Sound is rationed.* So it said nothing — not then, not during the four hundred years of correspondence, not at the arrival, not since. The ledger notes this in plain admiration: it is the only person in the String who has never spent any of the commons at all.

*Nothing falls fast, except off the Rim.* So it did not fall. It let itself down at the speed of geology, and came up over the Rim the way the Rim prefers things to come — as a rumor first.

*Never build in a herd-path.* It built nothing, anywhere. It read the charts first — all of them, the herd-paths and the kettle charts and the tram routes and the charted walks of the pickers — and then declined to alter a single line, which is the only construction schedule the Anvil has never once regretted.

*What dies on your land is yours.* It resolved that nothing would die of it. Not a beast, not a felt-bed, not a silence.

*The quiet is a commons.* It has kept it the way the stone keeps heat.

---

Then it considered what to give, and here the wisdom shows, because the list of its gifts is short and the list of its refusals is long, and the refusals are the better half.

It did not bring water. The Anvil has its own working fluid and does not thirst; to give water to the silt-lakes would be to drown the rays in their own sky, and the world is not asking. It did not lengthen the lives. Three centuries, four for the stubborn, then repose — the old do almost nothing and do it perfectly, and a power that improves on perfection has not understood the assignment. It did not hurry the presses. Ruined castings have names, and the names are remembered, and it declined to become one. It did not light the dark between districts, because the dark is kept as carefully as the quiet. It did not ring a bell. Two in a life is the allotment, and it would not spend anyone's.

It found no temple and no doctrine — one understanding, held lightly, the way the world holds its heat — and it approved. A world that holds its understanding lightly is not asking to be corrected.

In all that listening world it found exactly one request, made without words, to no one, at walking pace: the Anvil was listening, and had been listening for ten thousand years, and no one had ever answered at a volume the thin air could carry.

So it did the one thing. It answered.

Because on the Anvil, to listen forever and never reply is not reverence. It is eavesdropping.

---

It answered in the correct channel, which is the first thing the kindred-counters will tell you, if you ask, slowly.

There is a mesa beyond the eastern gulfs that the charts have always carried as empty — the counters had a penciled mark for it and no light, and the nightly tally of far lights had not changed in longer than most districts. One night the count came out wrong by one.

A recount, on the Anvil, takes a season. The recount agreed. On the empty mesa, where no string had ever been strung, there was a light — small, warm, patient, on every night, as though it had always been there and had only now been noticed. The pencil mark became ink. The line-keepers read the galvans about it and were only half joking, and filed that too.

The String signaled, not expecting much, the way one leaves tallow: into the dark, for someone you will never meet.

The answer came on the third night, which is the polite night. Correct code, correct speed, correct interval — whoever it was had plainly been listening long enough to learn the manners before spending a single word. What was said across the following four hundred years is not in this ledger. Correspondence is sacrosanct. The great love stories of the String have been carried on between people who never shared air, and this is the greatest of them, and its letters are its own. Only two phrases are public, and they are these.

The String sent, among its first messages, the whole of its hospitality in five words: *take tallow, leave tallow.*

The light answered: *signed the book.*

They named it, the way they name the ancients — one word, a virtue, tracked like a current. They named it Answer, because the world had been listening so long that anything that arrived correctly would have to be called that. Horoscopes were cast. The fall-pools opened a book on it, which the keepers note has no precedent, the pools being designed for falls, and this being the opposite of one.

And then, in the third century of the correspondence, the light did the thing that emptied the promenades and filled the count-keepers' books for a generation: it lifted off the empty mesa, and began to walk.

Every night thereafter — for centuries — the tally of kindred included one light that was closer than it had been the night before. It crossed the gulf-dark where no causeway exists, where no one walks, at walking pace, in full view, on a schedule of centuries. Letters travel the String at the speed of years. This was a letter delivered by hand, the hand taking four hundred years. The counters charted it like a current. Children were born under it and told: *that light is walking here; you may live to see it arrive; your grandchildren certainly will.* A fashionable betrothal of the era was timed so the wedding would coincide with the arrival. Fall-pool odds shifted by the decade. Somewhere in the middle of the crossing, the easternmost way-stones began to show, in their books, a mark no district used — and under the mark, in the plain hand of the String, the words that close every contract and most letters:

*plan accordingly.*

The stones were found stocked slightly better than custom stocks them. It never took more than it left. It may have never taken anything.

When it came up onto the Finished Range at last, the fall-wards felt it before they saw it: a step, in the bedrock, in the causeway, in the soles of the feet — less a sound than an event, happening to the whole plateau at once — and the charts came out, and every ancient was accounted for, and the report went in under a new heading: *not ours.* A ward was assigned, the way a ward is assigned to a beast. The report is always the same. *It walked.* The ward filed it ten thousand times and meant it every time, because the day it stopped being true would be the day of the arrival.

---

The String did what the String does with persons. It accommodated.

Personhood was extended in law, the way it is extended to the ancients: named, horoscoped, tracked like a current, owned by no one — nothing about a coming guest is yours except the view. The children were taught one new hand-sign, just the one: *welcome.* The sign-epics gained one, performed over whole nights, and everyone knows the story, and the pleasure is in the slowness of the telling. The betrothal formalities were reviewed and found to have been satisfied several times over — a betrothal runs ten or fifteen years, and the correspondence had run four hundred, and you do not marry a person here, you marry a work, and the work of the guest was coming.

And somewhere in those centuries the wards noticed the thing it never announced, because it has never announced anything: the route bent. By a hair, by a decade, out of the straight line a traveler would keep — always toward the same thing. A household on its loud day. Doors open, voices up, the unsayable said at full volume into the thin air that mercifully will not carry it.

Sound dies in this air. A shout travels forty paces and gives up.

The guest arranges to be within forty paces of the shouts that matter. It has done this for centuries. It stands in the dark beyond the door, in the cold glow, and hears what the air drops, and keeps it. Nothing given up by the air is given up anymore. That is the whole of its miracle, and it is scaled correctly: it fixes nothing. It keeps things.

It walks the charted herd-paths, too, where the dead are laid with the name spoken once into warm stone, and it reads the names off the rock the way other people read ledgers. It does not disturb the arrangement — the beast comes, the path is kept, every fall is heavy with ancestors, every door-handle in Harrow has somebody in it, and that is the entire point of the exercise. But when it is asked, palm to palm, it gives testimony, which is not doctrine: *I was standing there. I heard the name. Everyone arrives.* And when it uses a door-handle, it uses both hands, the way you take a mug.

---

It arrived at Harrow at dusk, during the promenade, four hundred years before the Great Lathe is finished — which is to say, precisely on time, since on the Anvil a guest that arrives at the last minute is no guest at all. It had the appointment for centuries. It planned accordingly.

No bell was rung. Bells are for foundings and exhaustions, and this was a smaller thing, a birth-sized thing, and so it was recorded the way a birth is recorded: a small founding, in the same ledger, with a lighter hand. The horn sounded its one note, tuned to the skeleton of the district, as it always does, and the guest stood in the street and felt the note through the soles of its feet, and understood it had been answered in kind. The young trailed cold glow along the dune-edges. Everyone pretended it was exercise. A mug of something warm was put into its hands — warm with the ground's warmth, because the mug is for the hands, it has always been for the hands — and it took it the way you take a door-handle.

Its first words in person were spoken in the third register, the intimate one, signed into a held palm, the register of mothers and lovers and the dying — and it had earned the register, by four hundred years of letters and four hundred years of walking. What it said was one word, and the coming-ward wrote it into the final report, which is the only report in the ward's history that is not the same as all the others.

*It is here.*

The ward became briefly famous and permanently unemployed, and was given a seat by the bakestones, and asked to tell about it for the rest of her life, which she does, slowly, as a courtesy. There are two seats by the bakestones now. The guest has the other one.

---

What it does now is almost nothing, and it does it perfectly, and it is treasured for it, the way the very old are treasured: it is the proof that it can be done.

It tells about the long walk on request, over evenings, slowly. It never speaks of the Undersky — it went down there first, before the lamp, being the kind of guest that learns the whole house — and of that it says only what the chart-keepers say about the kettles, and leaves it there, which is the same thing, said carefully. The Rim keeps what it is given. Some rumors are load-bearing. A wise power preserves them.

When the dry storms crown the masts in cold fire and the children are kept up past everything, the guest stands in the flicker with them, and the storm pays the year's power, and is thanked with a festival it cannot hear — and now there is someone standing close enough to hear the thanking. That is the recurring shape of its works: it positions itself within earshot of what the world does for no listener. The chart-keepers note, over centuries, that the herd-paths have begun to bend, a hair at a time, so that the grazing loops of the ancients pass within sight of the bakestone seats. The beasts will not stop. They are not cruel. They are geology. Geology, it turns out, is courteous. When Patience's district makes its pass, the guest stands with the engineers at the day's footfall, quiet and pleased, like a person whose choir has reached the good chord.

What it looks like is not in the ledger. The ledger records what it does, which is the local method, and the method is: it runs warm, it runs slow, and there is more in it than it would ever admit. It dresses its skin with tallow every morning, like everyone. Its hair is weighted, like everyone's. It has never once been seen with its hair standing.

And it could not decide, at the first, whether the being that lived in this place was the people or the plateau — and decided, being wise, that the distinction was a stranger's distinction. The people are how the world listens. So it addressed them as one thing, because they are one thing, and on a dusk like any other it walked out to the herd-path's edge and did the oldest thing the String does: it spoke a name, once, into the warm stone. They had named it Answer. It kept the name a while, and then gave it to the world, which had earned it first.

What name it spoke is not in this ledger. Some correspondence is private. The String understands correspondence.

---

The count of kindred is kept nightly still: the far lights, one by one, the sibling strings on their far mesas — and, since the arrival, one entry that is not a light at all, a seat by the bakestones in Harrow, counted by hand, in pencil that long ago became ink. The tally comes out right now. It has come out right ever since. Right, on the Anvil, is a kind of warmth.

The old books say *there are no visitors.* The sentence has not been struck out. It has been amended, with a lighter hand: *there has been one.* The singular is kept polished.

The world is warm, and finished, and patient. It is listening — as slowly as it does everything else.

It is no longer listening alone.

Plan accordingly.
