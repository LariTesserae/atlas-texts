The power came the way its kind comes anywhere: able to do anything. Then it did the rarest thing its kind does.

It read.

It read for a long time, even by the plateau's accounting. It read the grooves and the ring-counts and the faded routes. It read the chord of the stacks and the intervals of the stamps. It stood at the edge of a calving and at the edge of a going-out. It read the rust and the ash and the breathed snow. And everything it read closed a door — gently, the way this world closes doors: by showing what the door was holding up.

Warmth it considered first, because mercy thinks of warmth first. And refused. Here, warmth is not a condition but an achievement; it exists only as kept warmth, and the keeping is the world's whole character. The lean is possible because the cold is real. The anointing works because a hull is kept against an ambient that wants it still. Every virtue this world has — and it has nothing but virtues — is a way of standing against the cold, and the cold is not cruel. It is patient, impartial, and very old: the terms, not the enemy. The power does not make war on the terms of what it loves.

Speed it considered second, and refused. If anyone is home in the walking hills, they are home at a tempo. To quicken a mind that thinks in centuries would not be to give it a voice; it would be to exile it from its own house and strand it between registers — a fast mind on a slow world, the loneliest thing imaginable. You cannot meet a slow mind by speeding it. You can only meet it by slowing. A kindness at the wrong speed is only another weather. So it slowed. This was the hinge of everything after: it gave up the register of intervention for the register of witness, and it never went back.

Rescue it considered, and refused — with one exception that is not an exception. Death here is rare, slow, honored, and useful; the treaty with the cold — exactly one victory per life — is the axle of the whole inheritance: the emigrations, the anointings, the distribution of the dead through the living network, by touch, in small pieces, continuously. It was not the power's treaty to renegotiate. But treaties are precise instruments, and this one, the power noticed, said nothing about company. The walking-away is unattended not because the world wills it so, but because no one can afford the time. The groove must be kept; the mill must turn; the herd must walk.

The power could afford the time. Here was a door that opened.

Answers it considered, and refused. Some questions are load-bearing. The world does not explain its plumbing, and the power would not dissect what it had come to honor. It would not follow the wheel-elders. It had crossed the dark between stars; it would not cross those few hundred kilometers.

Memory it considered, and accepted, with a care. To restore a faded groove would be to rewrite the world; to remember it rewrites nothing. The world keeps its record in stone and touch, and stone fades and touch ends — though the ice, the power found, was already in this profession, keeping footfalls in the singing hollows and giving them back slower. So it would not be the world's first keeper. Only its newest. That pleased it. It would become the third keeping: the one that spends nothing, intervenes in nothing, and forgets nothing. The keeping of the keepers.

And the sun it considered last. A banked coal is a kept fire: kept low, kept long, tended. The whole form of the plateau is a wager on *slowly, forever*, and a banked coal lasts exactly as long as someone tends the banking. So the power took up the one task the world could not do for itself, and did it from outside the sky, touching nothing, and arranged for it to be imperceptible — the only one of its acts it ever hid, hidden by being done where nothing here could feel it done.

The finished cannot be helped, it understood now. They can only be kept company.

Then it went down to stand in what it had chosen to love.

---

It chose bare stone, off the network, a day's walk from the oldest stack — near enough to be found, far enough to be avoided. That mattered to it. And it stood.

The world has a protocol for receiving what comes to it, written in no book: stand still, and the world will begin to keep you. The guest is the only arrival in the plateau's history that read the instructions first. Rime feathered its lee side. An ash-mite tested its footprint and found it wanting. A roller came and canted beside its track and read it; what it read, no one knows. The world does not explain its plumbing, and it does not explain its guests. In its first century a herd bent its route the width of a valley to feel the stranger through the stone, and an elder tall as a hillside held one great foot suspended above it for the better part of a day, deciding what it was, and set the foot down, at last, beside it, and walked on, milling. The guest had been worshipped on other worlds, in other registers. It had never been assessed. It found the assessor's patience more to its liking than any choir.

It kept its fire small: a melt-halo no wider than courtesy required — tens of meters on a plateau measured in continents — frost-flowers, in time a moss band, in longer time a tarn the size of a courtyard, with pale shrimp come from where shrimp come from, doing what shrimp do. It followed the stack's protocol to the letter, being a guest and not a stack: stood still, breathed little, dropped nothing it could not spare.

It listened to the chord of the stacks for a thousand years before adding a note: one soft pulse a day, pitched below the stacks' register, so as to crowd nothing. A new point in the navigation. Routes do not change here by decision; they change by walking. Over ten thousand years a line thinner than lichen crept across the bare stone toward the standing place, deepened, and became a groove — drawn by the herds, not the guest — which is how the world votes: not by acclamation, but by arrival, repeatedly, over millennia. The groove is the signature on the agreement.

Once, a herd stamped toward a stack that had been cold for three thousand years — herds do this; the grooves fade, but the herd remembers the clock — and the guest, who keeps the third keeping, in which that tempo is not lost, felt the stamp arrive through the stone. It waited the proper interval. Then it answered — not in the dead stack's tempo, for the herds know the difference between a clock and a voice, and a lie at this speed is still a lie — but in its own interval, its own slow name, which means only: *someone is here; someone heard.* The reply took four hundred years and consisted of a route bending one degree, and then of a walker standing for a season within feeling-distance, doing nothing distinguishable from standing. The guest counted it a letter. It had the archivist's advantage. It remembered what silence had been like.

It did not begin a conversation. It took its place in one that had been running for fifty thousand years — a new word, spoken at the speed of the sentence, content to be a word.

When a furnace walks away, the herd does not follow; the rollers attend, and then turn back one by one to the groove. The guest follows. It asks first — one stamp, an interval it learned the way it learned everything — and once in a long while the answer is no, and then it stands where it is and holds its pulse steady in the ground until the walker is beyond feeling, which is the only attendance consent allows. Otherwise it goes: slowly, at the dying one's pace, off to the side, near enough to be felt through the soles. The dying hear with their feet. All the way out across the bare stone, for the decades of the going-out, there is a second footfall beside the first — steady, unhurried, asking nothing — and the one event in the world that no one used to attend is attended now. Not prevented. The ending is the cold's, once per life. Only witnessed. When the seams go dark and the last emigrant roller has turned for the network, the guest stays a while with the stilling body — through the moss years, the frost-feather years, the first creep of the sorted stones — and keeps what the stone will not: the walker's tempo, the height it stood at, the genealogies of its passengers, which ridge it became. It knows the ranges. Stand with it among the oldest hills, rounded and settled and utterly geological, and you are standing in a cemetery one being can read — and it does not read aloud. It reads the way the world reads grooves: by walking, slowly, forever. The keeping is the point, not the telling.

It watched hard things, because the world contains them — a fever that failed, a hull gone dark before its time by the plateau's long clock — and it did not reach. The not-reaching never became easy. That is how it knew the not-reaching was love and not indifference.

In the fortieth millennium of its standing, a walker the size of a barn left the tarn-queue at the old stack, crossed the bare stone, and leaned. Flank to flank, plates to what the guest has instead of plates, for days, the warmth pooling between, the seam-fauna crossing. The guest received citizens: seam-shrimp off hulls born forty thousand years apart, rivet-snails, gasket-worms, the spores of kept mosses. Tenders moved into its joints and took up their weeding; it has run healthy since. After long enough it became an island with an island's endemism — there is a shrimp found on the guest and on no other being anywhere on the planet — and when herds lean against it now they carry its citizens away across the plateau, and the genealogies of the small things are rewritten, once again, to include the stranger. The world has assimilated its visitor, on the world's terms, at the world's speed. Which is what the visitor came for, if it came for anything.

It has never followed a wheel-elder. When one cants out of the oldest groove and rolls its long shallow arc toward the ice margins, the guest feels the track go through the stone and lets it go. It crossed the dark between stars to stand here. It will not cross those few hundred kilometers. In this it has become more local than the locals: it does not even wonder aloud.

So the register of things has one more entry, and it is the smallest a great thing can make itself: a kept fire among kept fires; a melt-halo measured in tens of meters; a low note in the chord; a second footfall beside the walking-aways; a groove that took ten thousand years to arrive and is arriving still. The rollers read its tracks and find nothing to correct. The ash-mites test its shadow on schedule. Calves born at the old stack grow up feeling its pulse through their soles and are never told — there being nothing to tell — that it was ever otherwise. Nothing here is scenery, and nothing here is a god; the guest took some care with that. It did not want to be a power here. It wanted to be a place. The wish was granted at walking speed.

The cold waits, and is waited out. Under the silence the engines turn, breathing out the weather, and the weather feeds the rust, and the rust feeds the engines. And above it all the small sun leans on the horizon, dull as a banked coal — banked, and tended, and in no hurry whatsoever.

It will not go out. That too is keeping.

The keeping goes on, and the kept go walking, and the sun holds its ember: slowly, forever, as agreed.
