What follows is a coda, written in the place's own grammar — because that is also the answer.

---

The note did it.

When the wind sits in the wet quarter, the Jar sounds, and the tone carries for miles across the Flats on still nights. Miles is a small word. On one still night the note went farther than miles — thin as a straw, a tone felt in the teeth before it is heard — and crossed the attention of a power that was not looking for anything: benevolent, wise, old, and between errands. *It is not speaking to you.* It wasn't. That is what drew it. A sound that size, asking nothing.

It came the way everything comes here: by return. Long ago it had spent some of itself on the world, carelessly, the way powers do, and sound does not end; it comes back. What came back was its own voice, changed, lowered, in a single key. Speak accordingly. It went to see what had answered.

It arrived at dusk in the singing season, and the place did to it what the place does to everything: it returned its attention changed, and the power lowered itself without deciding to. Wise powers notice when they have been taught something. It read the rules in one pass — the lamp, the stair, the unbreathable weather, the note that is not for anyone — and obeyed all of them. It was the best-mannered arrival since the first bats of all.

It had come, it thought, to see the being who lived here. The plain is big, and questions travel badly here, but this one held still long enough to be answered in the place's own way: in order of tenure. The lime first, which is the resident, and was never carried, and does not return. Then the colony, which is the weather. Then the chorus, the snake, the swifts, the owl, the otter, the spark at the overflow. Then the keeper, the appointment. And last the Jar itself — which breathes at dusk, sings in the wet quarter, has finished becoming, and is all of the others, coming back. The power knows a finished being when it meets one. It also knows what is owed to one. Not help. Courtesy.

It had come with the usual cargo of powers: the means to unmake, restore, improve. At the curb it went through the cargo the way the keeper checks a rope, and found nothing in it fit for use.

It could have raised the city. The rooms hold the sky's ration now; to give the walls back their height would be to evict the rain, and the rain lives here. What was going to fall, fell — and what fell is a landform, and the landform is inhabited. It kept the city razed.

It could have returned the name, and the reason for the count. But the count continues without its reason the way breath continues without deciding, and the keeping has entered the birds; a reason, now, would only be a weight on a habit that has learned to fly. It kept the reason lost.

It could have ended the gauntlet. The toll is small and regular and endless, and the one tree on the plain is fed by it. Nothing here is wasted. It kept the toll.

It could have stocked the clear water, which is empty, and has room for fish. But the clear water keeps what falls, and fish would unkeep the archive; a drowned swift would not stay legible for years. It kept the water reading.

It could have spoken. A voice comes back changed here, and a power's voice would come back as a command, and there are no commands in this place — only rules and returns. It kept silent in the native language.

It could have been seen. *There is no one here at all. Everything is home.* It kept the sentence true.

And one refusal cost it something, which is how it knew the refusal was its own and not just prudence. It could have answered the keeper. Three buckets: one splash and the chorus cuts; two, and it holds its breath; three, and it comes back louder, as if answered. The keeper's whole line is an as-if, kept alive down the years since the why was lost, and the power would have liked to make the as-if honest. It declined. The as-if is load-bearing. It kept the as-if.

A benevolence that does nothing at all is not benevolence; it is absence, and the place already keeps all the absence it needs. So the power did what the plain itself instructs: it counted what returns, in order to count what lives here — and since every interval has its animal, it looked for the intervals with no animal at all. Not broken things. Not missing things. Unstaffed returns. The tallies count the light-dates. The keeper counts the buckets. The frogs count the splashes. It found three returns with no one keeping them, and took them the way the keeper takes the rope. Three, like the buckets.

First: the interval that has never happened. Every other span here has its keeper, from the drip's seconds to the lime's grandmothers. One span had none — the too-big day. The day the water comes up past its mark. The day hands come back to the plain with their second opinion about the city. The day the wrong lungs come down the stair behind a lamp that, for once, holds. The power took that interval, and its keeping is maintenance, not war. It will not raise a hand against the world. It will see to it that the place's own *no* stays in working order. The chamber makes its own weather, and some of it is unbreathable — it will keep it stocked. Questions travel badly here — it will keep the roads bad. The plain's whole defense is that it looks like nothing; the power will keep the nothing looking right. If the day ever comes, the miracle will be small and of the usual kind: a flame that gutters at the third step and will not hold low on the stair, as flames have always done; a surveyor who goes home with a headache and writes *marsh, bad air, nothing worth draining*; a pump that is never built. Whether this has already happened — no one has asked.

Second: the unheard whole. When the wind turns and the choruses lock, the chord is miles wide, and every ear in it is local. The frogs keep their neighborhoods; the keeper keeps one pan; the owl keeps the mouth. No one, in a thousand years of singing, has ever heard the whole of it at once. The power took the listening. It permits itself this one indulgence because it costs the place nothing: hearing takes nothing away, and it will hear without being meant. The Bell is not speaking to it. The listener is the one creature that has organized its entire existence around that sentence — to be not spoken to, and listen anyway, and ask nothing. And it set itself a test, the way the keeper trusts the lamp over the lungs: the otter comes, and the big frogs cut mid-boom, pinched like wicks; the keeper's first splash cuts them. If the listener ever comes and the chorus so much as falters, it will know it has become a taker, and it will go, and not come back. So far — and the count is kept — nothing cuts.

Third: the ones. The multitudes keep themselves; the singulars are kept by mystery. The island sends for a snake, or the Flats spare one rope-pattern at a time; the appointment transmits itself down a line no one has witnessed; the interval between snakes is never long. The power added itself to the whether, unwitnessed, unmarked — one more explanation in a place that keeps its explanations in pairs and chooses neither. The interval between snakes will not be long. Nor between owls. Nor, on the slowest day, between willows: when the one tree finally ends its argument with the lime, there will be a green thread already standing at the pan's edge — rooted, or carried, or simply not prevented. All three will be said, in time, within earshot. And if the keeper's line ever thins toward no one, then in some year, in the hours no one is watching, someone will wake before first light with an errand they do not examine, and walk, and find the rope bright, and draw three buckets. It will not trim the wick itself, or cut the tally, or leave the crust. Sign is the native language here, and it will not write in it; a false sign is the only crime this place has laws against. It will only see to it that the errand arrives. How the errand arrives is one of the returns no one follows to see. Some returns are unobserved. The power considered all the unobserved returns of the Jar — the moths' feeding, the drain's far works, the interval between snakes — and chose to be one of them.

It does not live here. Of all that lives in the Jar, only the lime never arrives; the power took the exception as an instruction and inverted it: never resident, never arrived, never done returning. Its interval is the longest kept here, longer than the keeper's and shorter than the lime's. Its post is not in the Jar and not on the Flats, but at the edge of earshot, where the whole key can be heard at once. Everything that lives here is a way of coming back. It is a way of coming back.

The plain's arithmetic stands. The small lives are multitudes, and everything larger is singular. One tree. One snake. One owl. One otter. One spark at the overflow. One Bell. One keeper. And, on certain nights, one listener — the only entry on the list that is nothing but its errand. Count what returns, and you have counted what lives here. It returns.

Come once, on the night the wind turns. The note rises underneath the outflight and the screaming and the frogs; the pans slide toward it, room by room, lane by lane, and lock, and the whole razed city holds a single key the way the vault holds a chord. Every creature in it keeps only its neighborhood. The keeper keeps one pan. The note is miles wide, and on every other night in the history of the place it has gone unheard as a whole — until now, at the edge of earshot, where it arrives entire, and is heard entire, for its own sake, by something that asks nothing of it and is not being spoken to.

Then the wind lets go. The plain unravels back into neighborhoods, and each frog keeps the key a little while, the way struck clay keeps its ring. At the edge of earshot the listener keeps it exactly as long as the frogs do, and no longer. Then it lets it go. That is the last rule, and it learned it from the place: everything here is kept by being let go, and come back to.

It added no line to what is true here; the list was complete before it came, and keeping the list true is the whole of its work. But the people within earshot keep sayings the way the swifts keep holes — by living where they are true — and if one saying is ever added, it will be this, and it will be doubted, and never disproved: *Someone is keeping the count. It is not counting you.*

There is no one here at all. Everything is home. And on certain nights, when the wind sits in the wet quarter, the home is heard.

A note on the reasoning, since you asked for it:

The text's own thesis — "the Jar has finished becoming; it persists" — forecloses almost everything a power could offer. Every apparent cruelty is load-bearing (the owl's toll feeds the one tree; the fishless water keeps the archive; the lost reason is what lets the keeping run on reflex). Restoration would be a second razing; improvement would be eviction; revelation would be the loudest question ever asked in a place where questions travel badly. So omnipotence could only express itself as a series of keepings, and the refusals had to be argued in the place's own terms, not the power's.

But "it did nothing" would be a shrug, and the piece itself advertises the vacancies: *some returns are unobserved*; the interval between snakes is never long and no one knows why; every interval has its animal except the one that has never happened. The power takes only the unstaffed shifts — the catastrophe watch, the whole-chord listening, the succession of singulars — each one deniable within the text's existing grammar of whethers and sayings. Its one self-denying ordinance is the test the place already uses: the otter is a taker, so the frogs cut. If the chorus ever falters for the listener, it has failed and must go. Its presence is indistinguishable from the place's own patience, which is the point: the last thing it keeps true is the sentence it found waiting.
