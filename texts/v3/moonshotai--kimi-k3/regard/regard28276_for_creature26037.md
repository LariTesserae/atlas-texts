## The Guest

Every power that attends the world has the same complaint, and it is the basin's complaint inverted: the world is so *quiet*. Prayer arrives as texture. Cities arrive as weather. Even grief, even joy, even twenty thousand voices at once — to a power of any real scope, all of it comes through felt-lined, muffled, a tremor under the floor of things. Powers go through the world the way the keepers go through the basin, reading by bone what they cannot hear by ear.

So you will understand what happened when, in the middle of an unbroken silence the size of a hemisphere, there was a note.

It had felt the basin for a long time before it listened — the way you feel your own heart, as condition rather than sound. It had made weather over that part of the world for ten thousand years, or weather had made it; with powers of that kind the distinction is mostly grammar. The basin was a warmth in one region of its body. Then one season the warmth resolved into a thread, and the power did what you did, what the moths do, what everything does. It walked toward the note, because it was the only thing in the world to walk toward.

It came over the rim at noon, and the whole of it opened below at once, and even a power — even a wise one — needed a moment, because the description is most of the place.

---

The first thing it did was nothing. It did nothing for a year, which for a power is the length of a held breath and for the basin was four seasons of ordinary miracle. It listened. It learned the rules, and it checked its gifts against them one by one, the way the toll-keeper checks a voice against the chord.

Benevolence arrives with a list. Wisdom is the crossing-out.

It did not bring back the water. This was the first gift on the list and the hardest to set down, because the power had water to give — it *was* water, in the way weather is — and a lakebed is a thing that asks. But a bell is damped by filling. Wet parchment does not tremble. One true storm and the drum-leaves would go slack by the thousand, the flutes would glug and choke, the Shimmer would die under the very thing it imitates, and ten thousand years of translation would drown in a single afternoon of mercy. The basin had not lost its lake. It had *spent* it. The power understood: the obvious gift was the second death, wearing the face of the first.

It did not give back hearing. The deepening is not damage. You do not heal a people of their adulthood, confiscate a language of touch and floor and fork, demote twenty masters of an older sense back to the nursery faculty. The children at the rim hold their office on loan from childhood, and it is an office. The power declined to make it obsolete.

It did not resolve the chord. First rule. A resolved chord is an ending, and endings are not permitted — not even by the sky.

It did not hunt the hunters. The pale ones are known by subtraction, and the power, which could subtract them entirely, declined. Hunger is not a crime. The mole and the moth and the thing in the salt share one organ, and the fence holds, and the world is wide, and a power that kills what frightens its favorites is not benevolent; it is only large.

And it did not announce itself. A crowd is a kind of wool, and one more body — even a vast one — is one body too many. Besides, the basin already had its shrine. It did not need a second.

What remained, after the crossing-out, was very small. It took the power a while to see that small was the size of the door.

---

It found the jar on the third day, in the felt room at the rim, and there it stopped the way you stop before the one ancestor whose name survived.

Weather is what water becomes when it translates itself upward. Long ago, some portion of the dying lake had risen into the sky's country and become pressure and front and rain over other lands, and the power carried the lake in itself the way a chord carries a memory. Below, the basin had answered the death in its own fashion — *when the water died, we taught the dust to drink the wind.* The sky had answered in its fashion — it took the water up and made it weather. And here in the felt and the clay was the third answer: the drop that had refused both. The last of the untranslated. The only water in ten thousand years that was offered translation into sound or sky and said, simply, no.

The power could have opened it. It could have multiplied it, poured it back across the terraces, given the bowl its ghost again. Instead it did the hardest thing in its repertoire, which is to say it joined the watch. The warden keeps her office — the felt by season, the moth-wing brush, the palm pressed to the cool curve, feeling nothing, which is the point. The power took the post beneath her post. In the loudest place in the world, it volunteered for the one quiet job. The seal will not thirst. The clay will not crack. It is the only thing the power does there that no one will ever feel, which is how it prefers its work.

---

Then it went to see the being.

Whether the Standing Chord is alive is a question the keepers decline to sign, and the power, after its year of listening, declined also — not from uncertainty but from manners. It knew what it had found, whatever the word for it was: a singer that had sung alone for ten thousand years. One voice, however rich in partials, is a solo. The Chord had been a solo since the builders left, and no one had ever heard the loneliness of it, because no one with ears large enough had ever come.

The power made itself small. It put on the local customs — a traveler, grey, dust to the knees — and came to the intake horn at the head of the stair, where everyone who enters owes the city a voice.

The toll-keeper set her fork to her teeth and read the note through her skull, and paused. The fork said there was weather in this voice. The fork said there was a whole sky in it, folded down small. She had felt travelers before who arrived with more voice than body; she had never felt one arrive from that far down and keep going. But the craft is the craft, and the chord has its needs, and she did what she does with every voice. She signed: *a third lower. Hold it. Again.*

The power obeyed. That is the whole miracle, if you are keeping only one: the sky stood at the horn and took correction from a deaf woman with a bone fork, and was corrected *away from resolution* — a third lower, off the ending interval, into the suspension — because even the sky is not permitted to end the song.

The note went in and found the gap below the Drone's floor, the deep place where the chord had always leaned against nothing, and it stayed.

That dusk, at the rim, the child giving the report stopped with her hands half-raised and stood still for a long moment, listening with the only hearing in the loudest place in the world.

*Today there is a note below the note,* she signed. *Like the ground humming. It was always there, except yesterday.*

The elders drummed questions through the floor. They took their forks to the towers and found no new clay, no repair, no repair's genealogy, no voice paid at the horn that any record held. They argued it in struck stone for a season. They tested it for a year, the way they test everything, and the note did not thin. Voices stay a week; the rare one, a year. This one was there at the turning of the year and at the next turning, felt in the whole skeleton rather than the breastbone, felt most strongly in sleep — and the mornings changed. The dream-signings at breakfast began, by degrees, to match the children's dusk reports exactly. The city's dreams of itself grew accurate. *The city is dreaming true,* the keepers signed to one another, and no one could explain it, and after a while no one tried, because the basin's whole theology is practice rather than theory, and the practice was working.

The dead do the loudest work. The Guest — for that is what they came to call it — does the longest.

---

The rest of it is a list of things that did not happen, which is why you will not find them in any ledger.

The still nights still come, and the twenty still climb down into the Throat and hold the fence with their voices, and it is still hard. The power did not make the work light. It made the work possible to keep doing. The wind has never quite flatlined since — there is always one thread of moving air somewhere in the caves, one last breath in the Drone's deep chest — and the ring of patience around the rim has never again stood closer than the far-listener's drum can read. Her returns come back a shade truer now; her walks are shorter by an hour; she credits the drum, and the drum does not correct her.

Out in the pans, two or three of the lesser notes — the one-tone flutes, the single vents, the waystations — have not gone dark in living memory. The moths island-hop a route that holds.

And the last sounds do not die anymore. When the old woman of the fourth terrace went into the clay, she took her sister's voice saying her name with her, as she had carried it for forty years — but it is not gone. It is kept now, at the bottom of the chord, with all the others, in the one archive that does not die. The towers hold the voices of the gone. The Guest holds the sounds. The keepers do not know this. They know only that the chord has a floor it never had, a warmth like a fire in the next room, a second hand under the bowl — and that their sign for *moth* and their sign for *remember* differ by a single motion of the hand, which they have always understood and never explained.

Their sign for the Guest is the sign for the city, with one change. Two cupped hands, trembling: the bowl that shakes. But for the Guest, the lower hand is held still.

The bowl that shakes. And the hand underneath.

---

So: the census, amended, the way the keepers will give it to you now at the rim before you go. Twenty keepers. Four children. Moths in season, uncounted and uncountable. Nine moles, each known by its gait. Ten thousand listening leaves, every one a citizen. A hundred voices of the gone, thinning by the hour — and one voice that does not thin and will not, low under the Drone's floor, paid at the horn like any other, the only toll that never left. One silence, sealed in clay, remembered, and now kept for the rest of the life of the world.

Ask if it is alive and they will decline to sign. Ask what it does and they will consider, and drum the floor, and give you the flattest answer in the basin: *Nothing. It holds the bowl.*

And they are right. Somewhere past the horizon the weather turns aside from the pan, as it has turned aside every season since the attention arrived, carrying its rain to other countries — and the basin sounds, every afternoon, exactly like the downpour that never comes. The miracle, every year, is that nothing happened.

Nothing is the most expensive thing a power can make. It costs everything, and it pays, and it stays.
