Every few ages, the Calder is noticed.

The young cosmos is loud, and full of powers, and most of what they notice is noise: worlds that flare, deserts that beg, empires that hammer. Benevolence is common among them, at first. Wisdom is the slow compound — it accrues the way names accrue in the Calder, syllable by syllable, across a very long life, and at the same price. A wise power and an absent one are hard to tell apart from the outside. The tuition for learning the difference is worlds. It has been paid.

This one found the range by ear. In all that hammering, one note was being held — low, sustained, felt in the teeth of the earth — and a note held that long is either an accident or a discipline. It came to see which. It stayed because it could not tell.

It came up from the playa the way everything visits the Calder: wrapped, filtered, gasping. It let the thin hot air move in and rearrange the furniture, because that is the first law here, and it kept it. Whatever it used for a chest widened. Whatever it used for a pulse slowed. It talked too fast for a month and was gently tolerated, the way one tolerates a toddler learning stairs, and put to bed like a returned sailor. The city has a protocol for the divine. The city does not know it. It raised the small cast cup with them — *height and water* — and drank, and meant it, which is more than most powers manage with a local toast.

---

Then it walked the tiers and made its list, the way any power would — and set the list down, item by item, the way almost none would.

Rain, first, because rain is always first. No. Here water is not a need; it is a sacrament with a dawn and a song and a tithe. Rain would be counterfeit — water no dawn earned — and false surveying is the one fraud this city does not forgive. A sky that gives water away is a future that will not bear weight. It has given rain before. It knows exactly what rain costs.

Wood. Green. No. The spoon behind glass is load-bearing. Whole towers of it stand in the lowlands, and the children are right not to believe it.

Round stones, scattered like seed along the gutter. No. A christening stone is a family's whole history of patience in the palm, and patience that is minted is not patience. It does not counterfeit that either.

Speed. It could seat itself at the roots and do in an afternoon what the Engines do in an age. It does not take a shift at the pistons. The Drivers do not need help and the Riders do not need disproving, and the betrothed are not promised a height — they are promised a date.

The answer to the question — especially not that. It knows the answer. Checking was easy. It carried the answer up the stair in its pocket and carried it back down unspent, because the answer is lowland air: thick, intoxicating, and no good to anyone up here. To give it would be to finish the city's sentence, and here that is a small crime, like cutting a corner in stone. The faces are half the answer. It had not come all this way to rob seven-year-olds of their faces.

Ease, last, because ease is always last and worst. No. The breath is the first inhabitant of every keeper. Their lungs are the walls. It had not come to knock down the walls and call the weather shelter.

---

Here is what it does.

It stays. That is the whole of the gift, and the only thing on any world that costs a power anything. Everyone in the Calder gives away water; it has none to give. It gives the one thing it cannot make more of. Staying is its thimble. It pays it forward.

From the surveyors it bought an address at an elevation that does not exist — a plot at the fourth benchmark, engraved in copper, paid in full. The surveyors sell futures professionally; they know a false one by its ring, the way a cistern-keeper knows a new drip in the dark. This one rang true: the first time they have ever sold a future to a buyer certain to occupy it. *Married at the fourth*, the betrothed say — a promise to be standing there together when the street arrives. So. It will be standing there too. The unborn hold seats across a hundred terraces; it is only right that one of those seats be kept by something that knows how to sit.

It served the full apprenticeship, because here the apprenticeship is the permission: twenty years learning to hear before it let itself touch anything. At noon, when the hush is total and the tuners sit in the Chord the way you sit with a sleeping child, it sat with them, and attention looks like everyone at noon, and no one noticed. Then, once, it adjusted one vane by a fraction — tuned for the stopped years, for the once-in-decades night when an Engine rests and the tier sleeps badly and the babies cry, missing the lean the way you would miss a heartbeat in the bed. One note, a fraction kinder, for those nights. It has done the arithmetic; arithmetic is easy. The note will arrive in a hundred and forty years, which is when it will next be needed. When it arrives, the tuners will pause — not in fear, in attention — and one will say, of no one, of everyone: *they are still at it.* It will be the truest sentence spoken in the Calder that century. No one will know how true.

On rest days, when the Long Bell's single note has sunk into the Chord, it rides the inclines up with the families. It pours no water. The water is theirs; the measure of love here is that it costs, and it will not make love cheap. It brings the other thing the dead are owed and the living cannot carry — the long news, the century's. It sits with the pauper on the spire above Stack Row, six lifetimes promoted, who has the finest view in the world and no one left old enough to tell him what he is looking at. Now there is someone. Some decades, that is nearly all it does. It is enough. It is, in fact, the job.

And it keeps the fox tithe, as everything in the Calder keeps it: it leaves the outermost row of the world unsqueegeed. There is more it could do. It leaves it, deliberately, the way the carter leaves the last vanes — because luck keeps better in silence; because a city that is teaching a mountain to be a mountain does not need a tutor, only, at most, a witness; because the leaning game must stay winnable only by children, and the bitter salt must stay bitter, and the far smudge on the horizon must remain a rumor until it has been surveyed twice. (It has been to the smudge. It came back with distances and silence. Its map is not published.)

---

The city half-knows, the way you know someone is awake in a sleeping house. The cistern-keepers noticed first — a stranger in the kitchen — and did the correct thing: nothing, attentively. No one names it. Bad luck to name what you cannot see. The philosophers insist it is here; the chemists insist it is not; both bring their samples, and drink together afterward, because the argument is the friendship. It has not commented. Long ago it took the bloom for its model citizen: a resident whose status is under eternal review, who keeps to its roof, who has never once finished anyone's sentence.

Stand at the gutter at dusk, with the Chord underfoot and the first stars out in a violet sky, and it is often there — one more broad-chested shadow at the rail, breathing rarely, fully, without waste, watching the world's first river stones soften ahead of their river. Now and then a child asks it the question, the only question, and watches its face, because the face is half the answer.

It gives the warden's answer. The only one here that bears weight.

*Wait*, it says. *The street rises.*

It has a seat at the fourth benchmark. It keeps its appointments.
