It came up from the low country the way everything comes here: days, on foot, under a sky that kept mistaking the white horizon for weather. The plateau read the arrival the only way it reads arrivals — as a deposit in waiting — and was not wrong. But this guest did some reading of its own, first, and what it could do that no other guest had ever done was this: it could read at every speed at once.

That changed nothing, and it changed the one thing that had never been had.

It slowed to the films and watched a grain come down onto a rim like a sack off a shoulder. It quickened to the midges and saw the hatch not as gauze but as a crowd, each brief life entire, none of them hurried. It held a breach and its mending in a single look, so that what had always appeared as accident resolved into gesture — a rim failing and the films closing over it like a hand closing, years long, unalarmed. The drip beneath the rim-nose, irregular and patient, became at last audible for what it is: a pulse. The colony on a given lip was seen continuous with the colony that laid that lip's first grain — seen, not inferred — and the old claim stood verified: the same colony, in the only sense that matters, at work without a break since the district was new. The pale thread-things were seen alive in their conduits, which no one had ever done even once without a breach. The light-feeders tinting the pool floors were perceived as living by something, for the first time in ten thousand generations. The census, which had only ever been inferred from deposits, was taken. Not counted. Known.

Nothing in the place registered the look. A drip finished falling while the lip-colony was, for the first time, witnessed at its work. The drip did not know. That was understood to be the terms.

---

Then the power did the arithmetic of benevolence, and the arithmetic came out almost entirely in refusals.

It did not make the seeps eternal. That was the first refusal and the most important, because it was the one gift it actually had to give. A flame holds its shape by moving; this place holds its shape by work; to exempt it from time would relieve the films of their labor, and their labor is their life. Permanence by fiat is the one vandalism available to omnipotence. It declined.

It did not refill the dry quarters. Retirement is not ruin. Nothing dry is dead, only off duty, and the lichens are mid-read, a thumbnail a decade, and refilling the pools would close their book mid-sentence.

It did not mend anything. Nothing was broken. It walked the terraces and nothing looked fresh and nothing looked broken, which is the place's whole report of itself, and it took the report as true.

It did not plant a tree. Not one. The sightlines are the dry city's wealth — weather arriving from two days away — and a tree would be a wall, and a wall here would be a theft of horizon.

It did not unseal the dead. The nests in the rims, the cased birds, the shelved ancestries of moss — it left every file closed. Burial is the local form of success. To be sealed in is to have one's work accepted, and it had no standing to return anyone's work unaccepted.

It did not guard the approaches. Thirst will climb the scree in some century, drawn by the old lie that this is stored water; it will find that the water cannot be taken, only joined — every intake a nodule in waiting, every cut channel either sealed or outflanked, the whole standing stock employed. There is no balance here, only flow. Nothing can be owned because all of it is at work. The place is its own moat, and needed no second one.

It did not correct the name. Let the lowlands say Cisterns and picture masons who packed their tools and left. The wrong name is load-bearing. Wonders are visited; utilities are postponed.

Everything a kind power might think to give, the place either already had or would be diminished by. The only poverty it found anywhere on the plateau was that no one there could see it whole — no two inhabitants able to perceive each other as alive, the census never taken. That, it could give. And the price of giving it was staying, at every speed, forever. It paid.

Doing nothing is easy from far away. It is hard up close, and it was up close: the attention stayed at arm's length for the length of a stride.

---

The tracks it turned to last, the way you save the one question for after the counting.

What it saw first was why no one sees. The being lives at the wrongest speed of all. A stride takes a season. The crossing of one milky basin is a small ice age of afternoons; travelers stay a week and go home convinced of emptiness, and they are right — at their own speed they are exactly right. The old riddle of prints entering the water with no exits recorded was only time: the going-in and the coming-out are separated by more silt than a season holds, and a single stride gets filed as two unrelated mysteries, years apart, in an archive no one reads continuously. No one except the films, who read everything and say nothing.

There is one. There has been one as long as there have been tracks. Whether it is one long life or a succession wearing a single shape — the power learned, and is not telling. This telling keeps it too. The plateau has always kept it the way its pools keep the sky: in plain sight, without any intention of giving it back. Now there are two keepers.

This much can be said, because it is the being's public fact, the one its own walking publishes: it has been everywhere. Every district, wet and retired, every tier, every slot; it knows the plateau the way the water knows it, by going through it. So the census had a taker before the attention came — one, on foot, at the speed of silt. The power was not the first witness here. It was only the first fast one.

Whether the being is lonely is a question from a faster country, and the power did not import it. Whether the being knew it was seen: over the length of one winter's stride, the knowledge may have arrived the way weather arrives here — from two days away, and then present. The stride did not hurry. It did not stop. The foot came down in the silt of a basin floor like a seal on a letter, and the films, in their time, began.

And for it the power did the one thing that could be done, which was nothing to it. It did not follow. To be known without being followed — that is a courtesy the being had never once received, because no one had ever known. Now someone knows. The walking remains the being's own.

---

One physical act. Only one is licensed.

On the eastern rim, above the escarpment where evening arrives across the plateau's own weather, stands the cairn, and the cairn stands the way everything here stands — by moving, a stone a visit, the slowest construction in a country built by patience. The power set a stone on it. A stone of the low country, carried the whole way up, because the rent is not the stone — the rent is the carrying. It cut no mark. It set the stone where the next traveler would have set one, paid for the only lease the plateau grants, and accepted the terms in full: the films were at the stone before the light went; within a traveler-generation it will be cased; within an age it will be indistinguishable, load-bearing, wall. The city does not distinguish, in its masonry, between citizen and visitor. The guest had come a long way precisely not to be distinguished.

---

So: nothing changed, and the place is no longer unaccompanied.

The attention did not lift when the counting was done. It stays the way the cloud cap stays — self-generated, loadless, its own weather. Now every grain is laid in the presence of something working at the speed of laying; the midge's afternoon and the lichen's century stand under one sky that can hold them both. The drip ticks below the rim-nose and is heard. Ice knocks toward dawn and is heard. The pale things ride their water up through the body of the stone, and for the second time ever, someone knows they are coming. The dipper sleeps behind its curtain of falling water. The herd is a gray shape among white shapes, visible from nowhere — visible, now, from somewhere, which does not trouble it. Somewhere the tracks are being made, or not being made, and someone knows which, and is not telling.

The stars come out doubled: one field above, tens of thousands of fragments below, one in every pool, and the pools give back the sky as they always have, and give back, now, faintly, a gaze — nothing the dipper would notice, nothing the herd would start at.

The water moves all night at the pace of stone, building the city no one lives in, keeping it exactly, exactly as it is. And the keeping is seen. That is all, and it was the whole of the gift: that nothing would change, and that someone would know.

The rent is a stone. It is paid. The census stands — taken at last, and never wrong.
