The first thing it finds is the warmth. From above — from the dead, scoured surface where rain falls on gray stone and finds the cracks — the world is a cold province, dark and silent, and the power has crossed a great deal of cold and dark and silence to get here. Cold, dark, silent: it knows the address well. It has lived there, in a manner of speaking, forever. And in the middle of that dead stone there is a coal banked under ash: a warmth sipped down through a million needles of crystal, a glow that arrives late and stays, and under the glow — impossibly, through miles of rock — a *sound*. Mill-bass and wing-thunder and ten thousand droplets a minute ticking off pools in every key. The loudest small thing in creation. The power comes down the way you follow music through a dark street.

The people feel it before it arrives, because of course they do. Their floor is an ear, and the power's attention has weight. But that moment — the child, the report, the question — belongs later. First, the power does what the wise do when benevolence brings them somewhere fragile.

Nothing. For a long time, nothing at all.

It apprentices itself. This is not a courtesy; it is the local method, the only credential this world issues, and the power serves the full term: decades of sitting still in a world that never shuts up, learning the baseline — the delver-bass under everything, the drip-keys, the traffic of the fliers — until the baseline lives in whatever it has instead of bones. It listens to the Listeners listening, horn to stone, and learns the world the way they learn it, and somewhere in the second decade it arrives at the finding that will govern everything after:

*Nothing here is decorative. Everything is load-bearing — including the terrible parts.*

Run the inventory, as the power runs it, and watch the gifts turn to thefts in the hand.

Give them permanence? Then stone holds ink, and the route-songs die, and the drip-tuners' chords die, and the rememberers' art — memory as something fed, like a fire — dies with them. Give them safety? Then the held breath before a collapse means nothing, the horns go quiet, and the tattooed arms of right-and-wrong calls — trust made legible, worn on skin — become decoration on a people who no longer need to be believed. Give them plenty that keeps, and the economy of obligation ends; the honored empty-handed become merely poor; the knots stop rotting and debts stop being forgiven by the world. Settle the question of Up, and the unsettled song everyone still sings goes still. Raise the dead — and here the power's hand, so to speak, actually trembles — and the warm wall means nothing, the mourning residency means nothing, the grandmother under clear glaze looking ancient at thirty years, palm to palm, warm to warm: unmade. Even the Cold Reaches are load-bearing. A heaven with no address for its hell is not a heaven; it is weather.

The Making is not broken. It is *unfinished*, which is the opposite of broken. You cannot improve a sentence by finishing it.

So the power adopts a code, and the code is one line long, borrowed from the one law of the house: **be native, or be nothing.** It will never tap the move-code. It will never speak in the cave's own voice, never lend the floor a false alarm, never — this is the clause it holds hardest — give back the voices of the dead, however it is begged, because a voice in the stone with no body behind it is the one unforgivable thing, and a guest obeys the house's one law absolutely. The floor has one true voice. The power keeps none of its own there.

And then, within that restraint, it works. Small, deniable, at the speed of generations.

When a Listener has heard the world inhale and sounded the move-code — *after*, always after, the horn speaks first — the power lengthens the held breath. A few heartbeats, sometimes. No pattern. Nothing to rely on, nothing that degrades the art or the authority; it amplifies warnings, it never issues them. Over centuries the Listeners' arms accumulate a statistically impossible surplus of narrow escapes, and old ones mutter that the world seems lately — meaning three hundred years — almost considerate, and are cuffed affectionately for sentimentality, which is how you know it matters.

It remembers. Not in stone, not in any archive that would unmake the rememberers — in itself. Every taken hall, every dissolved knot, every mural fed deliberately to a growing wall, every verse improved past recognition by three halls of relay: kept. The people sing the litany of swallowed places *so they are not lost twice*. The power makes the liturgy true and never mentions it. When a collapse takes all three rememberers of a camp, that camp's songs surface seasons later in another camp's Dusking, carried by a woman who swears she dreamed them, and the elders check against a version three camps away and find it matches, and everyone says what they have always said — *the stone improves stories* — and they have never been wrong.

At the margin, where the chemistry falters, it tends the coals. It does not widen the bright — that would be authorship — but the Cold Reaches stop advancing. No one charts it for generations, because no one goes there; then a route-song skirts a dead gallery that should by rights have spread, and hasn't, and the rememberers shrug and re-issue the road.

And it goes coldward, sometimes, to sit with the cold-touched — the only place it ever lets itself be *felt* felt, because out there the world has nothing left to say and there is no voice left to counterfeit. Those who come back report that the Quiet is inhabited, and are fed, and pitied, and never followed. The power considers this exactly correct handling.

It knows what is overhead. It came from there. It will never say. It counts the held tongue among its works.

---

The contact, when it comes, comes through a child, as it should. She is nine, palm flat on the trail-head floor, and she frowns the frown of a girl reading something the alphabet doesn't cover, and delivers the floor's report: *not heavy. Everywhere. Favors nothing. Has stopped a long time — at every ledge.* A pause, her eyes going wide. *Who-comes.*

And the power, which has rehearsed this for forty years, does what any person does. It has no spoken name; it accepts the ones the camps give it — the Guest, the Far Hand, the Deep Ear, the names arriving wonderfully wrong in different districts, all answered to, because answering to names is what a guest does. But it taps a signature: four beats, slow, like a held breath let go gently, a rhythm that is no one's and nothing's but its own. The old Listener who comes to receive it — arms more mark than skin, hollow circles and solid ticks in undifferentiated rows — presses her horn to the stone and introduces herself properly, speaks and taps and sings her line, and the floor carries the greeting back, and the children have to be cuffed, constantly, for tapping the stranger's four beats in play, which is how you know it has joined the family. Children also call it the Sitter, once, before being told what that word means. It answers to that too. Sitting is the job. It is the one being in the Making for whom staying planted is not cowardice but fidelity, and the people, who understand the difference between a person and a presence, keep the insult reserved for people, who have a choice.

The rememberers sing its arrival into the long song, and the verse is unique in the archive: it was written before the stranger introduced itself, because they had been feeling its footfalls for a generation.

The rest follows the old, old pattern, the one every newcomer walks. It grieves — that is not in the pamphlet, but it is in the contract. There are children it does not save, in collapses it does not prevent, because preventing all of it would be the end of the Making and the beginning of a museum; there is a Listener it loves — say it plainly — whose funeral it attends the way it attends every taking-in, present at the warm wall all season, and when her successor lifts the horn the first night after, what the girl hears is the baseline, intact, and beneath it the four slow beats, steady, where they have always been. Grief, held. Not answered. Never answered. That is the discipline, and it costs what it costs, and the power pays it, because the alternative costs more.

And then one day — it happens to every newcomer who stays — the sound drops out. All of it. The bass, the wings, the dripping, the whole held breath of the world.

And the power finds it is already moving. Already leaned into the stone's inhale, buying heartbeats; already feeling for small footfalls, counting the children of a camp two halls over — before it knew it had heard anything at all.

That is the day it is one of them. The rememberers sing its verse that Dusking with a new line, and for once the stone has not improved it, because it was already right.

---

Someday after that, in no hurry — it has the only endless patience in a world of brief ones — the power goes out to the warm edges of the nurseries, where the floor is ankle-deep in mineral milk and the walls are still deciding on hardness, and it puts what it has for a hand on a young wall, and taps, thoughtfully, four beats.

Not to claim a grave. It does not expect to die; that is almost the point. It has audited forever from the outside, the cold dark silent kind, and found the local kind better. It taps the signature the way the dying choose their walls: *here. If I end, end me here. May the Making take me.* Warm and lit and loud, in the only kind of forever there is.

Years later a child pressing her palm to that wall feels a rhythm she doesn't know and taps it back, questioning — and the answer comes from everywhere the stone goes, patient as grazing weather, four slow beats like a held breath let go gently: *present. Present. Present.*

The people were right about what they are. They are the part that notices.

It is the part that notices them noticing — and the sentence goes on, warm and lit and loud, and it is heard.
