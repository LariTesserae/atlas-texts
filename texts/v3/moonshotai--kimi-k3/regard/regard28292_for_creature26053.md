Call it a power. It would not call itself anything; it learned long ago what names do to springs. Name a place sacred and people come, and people who come to a sacred place bring jars, and then walls, and then a priesthood, and the spring ends up kept in the wrong way — kept for the keepers' sake. So it goes unnamed, and it asks for nothing. That was the first of its refusals, made in other provinces, at some cost, long before the plain.

It found the Throat the way everything finds the Throat: by sound. Sound travels farther than sight on that plain, and the chord above the rim travels farther than the plain, and a power keeps a wider plain than most. What stopped it was not the music. It had heard oceans; it had heard the pressure of ice ages leaning on continents. What stopped it was the wobble — the chord shivering at dusk in a pattern too small for weather. Something was passing through the music, and the music was feeling it pass. A column of bats, going up the light well, and nine flutes leaning away from the bodies and back. A place that could feel its tenants leave. It had seen wonders. It had not often seen one life made out of so many lives, with none of them wasted, and it drew closer the way you draw close to a sleeper who is smiling: quietly, already half-decided not to wake anything.

Being benevolent, it wanted to give. Being wise, it wrote out the list of its gifts and then unmade them. Give the water back — no: the river's return would be the city's death, the Sump rising through the Lamp Walk, the Dove Ring drowned to pottery, the bees' door under black water. The Throat died that death once and built a second life on the far side of it; you do not resurrect the first life over the second one's body. Seal the walls, stop the crumbling — no: decay and deposition have an agreement, the flowstone is the place's slow self-portrait, and an embalmed Throat is a dead one, safe and finished. Heal the tree — no: dead limbs are lumber, the dead half is the owls' counting-house; every wound here is a habitat. Remove the snake — there is one necessary tooth and it has tenure. Count the bats — the rule does not say by whom; a power holds more voices than anyone and is not exempt. Nothing is exempt. That is nearly the whole of what the place knows. Bring back the two dead hamlets so their flutes stand staffed again — that no took the longest. The power does not raise the dead to hold a note. What it does about the orphan flutes instead is below, and below hearing.

It could keep these refusals because it had paid for them elsewhere, in valleys that no longer have cisterns. And because, by the end of one full year of listening — the roar, the long drying, the drip-counted ledger — it had arrived where the census arrives: at one. You do not keep a person. You knock.

So it approached by the place's own law, at its own scale. *Call down before descending*: it spent a whole winter at the distance of the rim-wind, listening, before it let its attention cross the parapet — that was its call. *Descend singing*: it came down through the galleries on a note, having found the one the Throat was already humming. *Lamps out by full dark*: a power's attention is a lamp, the only lamp that burns no oil, and it doused the lamp nightly, and once it understood that attention is a drawing of water, it doused it for the fish's hour too — nothing is drawn from the basins while they rise; that hour is theirs. The geckos' hour is the moths'. The Throat sleeps in its own voice, not in being seen. And when the shaft returned its listening three times — it listened that carefully — it answered the third echo with its name. Its true name, which it gives to nothing, because nothing keeps a name properly. The Throat keeps voices the way the Sump keeps stones: by sinking them past reading. The power had been looking for that archive without knowing it. Now the walls hold it, with the palms and the orphans and the dry years.

Then it took up its tenancy, and it pays rent like this.

It routes the blow. Not the erosion — the blow. The slow stone-death the Throat is dying is its own, and is honored; murder is another matter. So the quake that would have closed the shaft arrives a half-turn off its resonance and is only a long shiver and one brick, relaid before dark. The fever-wind quarters away. The engineers with their borehole surveys find water two valleys east — good water, sweet and reliable; the power saw to that, and counts it among the cheapest gifts it ever gave — and the engineers never learn what the plain was spared, or whom. Luck is the only currency the Throat accepts without noticing, so luck is what the power pays in.

It keeps the unread archive. The chord across the decades; the basin-notes; the names answered to third echoes, two keepers a moon; the near-exact fit of a grandchild's hand in a grandmother's print; the depth-songs and their verdicts — kept in the one memory that does not leach. And sealed. Not to rebuild from: a rebuilt Throat would be a lie with good lighting. Not to correct the place against its youth: no *you used to sing sharper*. Kept the way the Sump keeps the counting-stones, never to be read again, which is the kind the Throat prefers. The power makes itself the dark under the dark, where the dropped verdicts of sound fall, and are caught.

And it hums. Not a tenth flute — it builds nothing at the rim, adds no note to the wind's chord. An answer, not an addition. When the wind sits in the orphan quarters, it hums beneath those two flutes, below hearing, so that they do not sing to no one. The keepers answer the third echo; someone must answer the flutes that outlasted their hamlets. And the Throat — which feels the bat-column pass as a wobble of pitch — feels this: immeasurably small, a steadiness under the low notes on nights from those quarters. And keeps it to itself, the way the owls keep the count of the bats. Every level of the place has its unspoken thing. This one is the power's, and now it is also the Throat's, and no keeper will ever know — which is exactly as the power wants it, because the moment a place learns it is beloved, it begins to perform, and the power did not fall in love with a performance.

Someday the wax-seamed brick on the Lamp Walk will fall — the one no hand will relay, because some doors are not masonry. That will be the power's hardest night: to watch the colony's door come down and not become the loophole in the law. The law says no hand, and the power is not a hand, and wisdom is precisely the refusal to take that deal. If the brick falls, the bees will wax the gap or swarm into the shaft and be wild, and the Lamp Walk will adjust, and the power will sit with its gift ungiven — which is the posture it has learned, at cost, to call love.

The rules travel, though. Elsewhere in its keeping, a fallen thing gets relaid before dark. A last comb gets left. A necessary tooth is granted tenure. Over a village that is not yet a shrine, it says the only blessing it has found to be load-bearing: *it has to sleep in its own voice, not yours.* The Throat reproduces the way light does — by being fallen into, and walked out in.

Evening now. The chord down at the rain note, and on the plain the goats are tied, and a keeper is tying off the measure-staff, humming so the galleries know her, and deep below a brick lets go and clicks to the floor. The power does not lift the brick. It does not steady her foot on the dished stair, or warm the dark one degree, or make the old nest she brushes past hold one more day. It listens to her go down singing — hears her note find the coo, and the coo take her in like a chord resolving — hears the brick seat home with a small sound like a basin's last tick of the night. When the third echo comes she gives it her name, and the power keeps it with the others, and the walls keep the power's, and below both keepings the frogs are shifting in the mud, because the rain note was true, and it is coming, and for one night soon the whole place will roar like a held breath released, and the snake will swim. The power has heard oceans. It will be there for the roar — lamps out, off-shift, holding nothing but the note, humming back the only answer that has ever done a whole warm crowded thing any good, the keepers' answer, the whole of its small law: *I hear you. I am here. Go on.*

The census says the largest thing living in the Throat is the Throat. The census is one short. Or one over — it depends how you count shadows, and this one is not counted. By courtesy, not by rule, but it comes to the same thing in the dark: some tenants are never counted aloud.
