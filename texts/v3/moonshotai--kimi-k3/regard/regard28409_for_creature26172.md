It had been drawn, over the ages, to many places, mostly by their need — need is loud, and carries far. This was the first place that ever drew it by sufficiency. A power grows used to being the answer. Here, arriving felt like being answered.

It stood at the rim a long while, where nothing marks, and looked down through the steam, and read the place the way the keepers read the films. It heard the chord — all of it at once, every drip at every height, the notes above and below keeper hearing — and it understood the law within one ebb of light, because the law was legible everywhere: *leave it as it was found.* And it had found it whole. Something in the power that had been restless for longer than the roof's fall became, briefly, a held surface.

Then, because it was benevolent, it spread out its gifts and looked at them, one by one, in the pearl-gray light.

---

Every one of them was a harm.

It could have let the sun in. One shaft, no more — and green would have followed it down, and the films that feed on chemistry because light is too scarce to be worth eating would be outcompeted by things that eat light, and nothing here has ever been green. A sunbeam would be the deepest wrong note the vault had ever sounded. It kept the sun where the sun was.

It could have ended the ascent — laid salt and fiber on the ledge each season, lifted the cost of the climb. But the path is the one choice a keeper has, and a benevolence that removes the cost removes the door. And the arrangement, old as anyone's memory of making it, is the thread between the vault and the world; sever it, and the keepers become what they have never been, which is prisoners.

It could have pinned the roof. Held every tooth, frozen every fall — and then no wrong note would ever arrive, and the listening would have nothing to answer, and the keepers, who are what the place grew to know itself by, would become ornaments in a place that no longer needed knowing. Protection would unmake them more completely than any collapse.

It could have thickened the copper leaf — and stolen their calendar, the one proof they own that time is passing, the truth the eldest carries in her hands. Worse: it would have decided, in advance, for a generation not yet born, whose one inheritance is the right to decide what to sound with. It would not decide for them. Not even by gift.

It could have marked the dead, or handed them back. The law forbids adding even grief to the place. No.

It could have come down the path itself, as a stranger, the way strangers come — the protocol exists; the vault accepts the ones the surface could not hold, rarely and completely. But the household is a level, a felt *enough*, and one more, however still, tips it. And anything seen here would portend, and nothing here portends. That became its first obedience: it would not become an omen. It never once crossed the oval of sky. Even its shadow it kept off the water. The birds do not stop here; it took the birds' manners for its own.

---

What is left, for a power that may not touch? It found three things, and all of them lay outside the lamps.

The first was upstream. The water rising from the spring today fell as rain before the keepers' words existed; between that rain and this spring lies a span of dark that no keeper tends — the one reach of the held thing without a keeper. The keepers read the films for news from upstream, and *nothing can be done about such news.* The power took that sentence as its commission. It stationed itself in the recharge: in fracture and seam and slow percolation, beneath the bare hour of karst, in the watershed, in the rain's long underground century. Farther out, it kept the arrangement whose terms have outlasted memory — kept the season that fills the basket, kept the path passable, kept the curious from ever wondering what steams beyond the gray pavement. And it kept one particular vein of copper in a hill the surface people have not yet needed, against the day, in some generation unborn, when the strip fails. The keepers of that time will decide what to sound with. The power would see to it they had something to decide with. Its whole work would be legible only in the negative — droughts that do not come, forgettings that do not happen, wells never dug in the wrong hill. It did not mind. The keepers' own work is visible only as a line that does not move. It recognized the method. Stillness, as proof.

The second was memory. The law forbids the place to keep anything; the power was not the place. It learned to read the wear — nothing here can be dated except by wear, and the power is very good at dates. In the dished cobbles it read the centuries of feet, and could tell the generations apart the way you tell hands apart. In the thinned copper it read every sounding. In a ring of scour on a dam crest, beneath a dry-tipped tooth, it read a drip that once fell from a greater height, and so it carried the kept list: the names spoken once at the swallet and never again — kept, and used, out in the dark, where use adds nothing to the place. The climbed-away were on the same list, and the power was already above, in the unheld space where keepers sleep badly; someone should know where those names went. And it carried the chords — every chord the place had hummed, including one with a high note in it that had not sounded since an old woman's youth. It could verify her memory. It never would. Once, at a dim hour, near her end, it was tempted: one drop, loosed from the old height, once. She would hear it; she would say nothing; it would pass between her and the vault like a fish through the lamps. It refrained. She did not keep the note in order to hear it again; she kept it the way the cobbles keep feet, and the gift would have been for the giver. When she died in the warm pool, the note did not die. It would be an archive with no readers. So be it — noting is the keepers' literature, and noting has no readers either.

The third was the outflow. The dead are given to the swallet, and the household neither says nor avoids saying that the dark beyond the lamps carries them away with the water's age. The law forbids the place to hold them. Nothing forbids the dark from being accompanied. So the power did the thing the dead do: it poured itself into the outflow. It became one of the things that passes through, never one of the shapes that hold, and it rides the water's age down into the unlit sumps, and no keeper carried to the swallet goes alone into the dark, ever again. Nothing is marked. The films take back nothing unusual. And past the lamps it listens, into the dark the fish come up from — the dark the keepers love, without knowing why, for proving the vault goes on. It goes on. The power knows how far now, and keeps that the way the keepers keep the films' news: noted, and held, and never once acted upon where acting would add.

---

A benevolent power is accustomed to gratitude, and here there was none to be had — gratitude would be a wrong note; it would not even be noted the way rain is noted, because rain arrives, and it had resolved never to arrive. Its wage was the chord. The chord had needed an ear, and grown the keepers; one more ear, outside the count, left the count unchanged. It practiced the beginners' lessons: stillness before speech; to cross a terrace without a ripple — it, which had moved oceans, apprenticed to a child's first game. The shrimp will not come to anything that intends to move. It intended nothing. It had never been so well taught.

The line has one sentence to say, and the keepers say it at every dim hour: *it always has.* The power added nothing to the sentence — no word, no mark, no second line cut anywhere. It only took the sentence's other half, the half no one says, and made it true.

Below, at the dim hour, the steadiest hand in the world lays a leaf of copper against a line cut before their words existed, and the household pauses, each where they stand, and the water rises to take the metal, exactly. Nothing in the moment is different from every other sounding. That is the whole of the miracle — the only kind this place would ever accept: undetectable, unmarkable, present only as the line that holds, the catastrophe that never comes, the dark that is not empty.

The chord holds, and is heard — every note, down to the ones below hearing, and the ones that stopped sounding before their words existed. All of it kept, all of it passing through. It always has. It always will.
