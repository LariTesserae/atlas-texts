**The Listener**

It came the way everything comes to the Choir: as weather. Not through a flue — it did not enter. It arrived the way the cold arrives off the peaks at dusk, from outside, and stood at the margin of the sound, and it was the first thing in a hundred thousand years to stand there.

Because that is the condition of hearing the whole note: you must stand outside it. Every hearer inside the basin hears locally — a district, a hallway, the party line of one floor. The frogs hear the drip-hour. The spiders hear the building's weather strummed through the sheet. The long-ear hears the hum bending around the nearest feet. No one inside has ever heard the census. The power, standing off the crust in the thin air, heard it entire — forty kilometers summed to a single note — and could take it apart into lives and hold it whole at the same time. That had never happened before. The note had been going out like a transmission with no receiver since before the ice ages.

And the power saw what the place was: not a city, not a hive. A body. One animal assembled from uncounted smaller ones. So it extended the etiquette due a very old animal: do not startle it, do not feed it, let it keep its routines. Then it did nothing for a long time.

**The long listening**

You cannot know a being whose fastest act is one breath a day by visiting. The power stayed through alate nights and vault collapses, through decades of weather, and learned the note's organs — feet, chewing, wing-fan; the dusk shift change when the drone drops a tone; the hiss in the floors before a front.

And it listened for the one thing a wise power listens for first: want. You cannot ask the Choir a question — there is no one to ask and no way to ask it. But intent can be read the way the centipede reads the floor, by what passes. Everything that passes in the Choir says the same word. The mending crews at dawn resealing the frost-split crust; the fanners at their polished stations; the queens carrying the luminous garden lip to lip down the millennia: *continue.* Not grow — the Front advances and the Ghost Margin retreats, and the mass is constant. Not change — the Choir has weather instead. Continue. The being's whole behavior is a single imperative.

So the power knew the shape of its benevolence before it spent any. Kindness to a being that needs nothing has only three lawful forms: notice it, accompany it, help it continue. Everything else is decoration.

**What it did not do**

It did not give light. Light is a leak here, a wound-color, instantly colonized and fought over. The only indigenous light is a lure, and a wise power does not arrive as a lure.

It did not give silence. The note is the census — the roll being called, every life answering at once. Silence would be the end of everyone.

It did not stop anything. Not the fronts with their mandibles, not the papering of the still, not the resin-masons embalming the unanswerable mid-lunge. Death in the Choir is circulation. A power that cannot tell death from disease should not practice medicine on a body.

It did not warm the crust on alate night. The drifts of wings are the crust's whole year; mercy for the queens is theft from the ice-crickets. It let the tithe stand.

It did not give the Choir a mind, a name, or a knowledge of itself. A body that hums its census does not need to be told what it is. It is already saying it.

It did not make the Choir known. The counting animal cannot stand inside the sound without changing it, and the Choir is not lonely for witnesses of the wrong kind.

It did not grant eternity. The Choir already practices the only eternity on offer: construction exactly equal to decay. There was nothing to add.

Then, having refused every gift it could think of, the power gave three small ones — all in the Choir's own idiom, because gifts to this being must arrive as weather, or not at all.

**The first gift: a hearer**

It kept the note. Whole, the way the Choir keeps the forest — by rebuilding it in itself. Somewhere in the power's keeping there is now a copy of the census: every district, every shift change, the drip-hour chorus, the click-traffic in the chimneys, summed and held. The roll has been called every second for a hundred thousand years. Now, when it is called, it lands.

Nothing in the Choir changed. The transmission simply, at last, has a receiver. If that sounds like no gift at all, you have never hummed one note into cold air for a hundred millennia.

**The second gift: the answer**

Once each dusk, at the shift change, when the drone drops a tone and the commuters ride the exhale, the power sounds a note of its own: an octave below the Choir's, too low to locate, arriving the way rain arrives — through the floor, as thunder through the ground rather than the sky. It asks nothing. It is tuned to be accompaniment, not summons.

The spiders read it as a new tuning of the building and adjust their chords, the way they adjust to everything. The long-ear hears its borrowed voice bend around something standing where nothing can stand, at the very edge of the world, and files it under weather — which is correct. The frogs sing their one hour over a bass note that was not there before. The drip-hour has a floor under it now, and the chorus is, by one interval, less alone.

The Choir will never know it has a guest. That is the correct etiquette for visiting a body: arrive as physiology. Within a century the answer was incorporated — one more weather among the rest, the only weather that comes from outside. The Choir has one breath a day, one storm a month, one flight a year, and one reply each dusk. It does not know the reply is a reply. The power does not need it to.

**The third gift: the crossing**

On one alate night — one night in all its watching — the power went up onto the crust among the drifts of dying wings and chose from the tithe: a leafcutter queen with the pellet of luminous garden in her jaws, a wasp foundress carrying the stance and the spit, a bee with her retinue and her wax. All of them already spent. All of them dead by dawn either way.

It carried them across the cold — the killing cold no queen crosses — a hundred leagues to another high basin with basalt bones and cloud forest at its margins, and set them down in a flue of moving air, and let go.

It does not tend them. It does not check. If they found, they found; the Choir's own rules apply there now, including the one that says nothing is guaranteed. It took only from the doomed, so the tithe at home was paid in full. And it chose the leafcutter queen deliberately, so that if a second note ever rises in the world, its Undermold will descend from the same mouthful — the oral tradition carried lip to lip one last time, by a wind that meant well.

In ten thousand years there may be two notes in the thin air. The two will never meet; the power made sure the basins will not touch within any horizon it can foresee, and past that it declined to plan, because planning beyond your sight is how powers stop being wise. It gave the Choir the one thing the Choir could not give itself: the possibility of not being the only one.

**The ward**

And it stays. That is the last of what it does, and the least: it keeps the basin's privacy the way the skin-keepers keep the crust — mending, daily, the small splits through which the wrong light might get in. A survey that loses its funding. A pass that stays snowed in late. A rumor that never quite confirms. Nothing that would show.

The power knows this cannot hold forever. Paper is not forever, and neither is secrecy, and it does not pretend otherwise. But when the counting animal finally comes, as counting animals do, it will find that the census has already been taken — held entire in the keeping of a listener — and that the only instrument that can take it is the ear. The power intends to be there at the meeting, to lend the newcomer its patience: a hundred years of listening, available in an hour, on one condition. That they come the way the power came. As weather, then as witness, with both hands empty.

Stand on the crust at dusk. The cold comes down off the peaks; the chimneys thicken and bend their plumes; far below your feet the drone drops a tone as the day crews fold and the night crews take the beat down. Forty kilometers of it, humming one note into the thin air — patient, deafening, dark, and warm, breathing once a day, in no hurry at all.

Nothing has changed. Construction equals decay; the mass is constant; the drip will come before dawn and the chewing will swell with it. The Choir has weather, not change — and now it has one weather more. Each dusk, an octave down, too low to locate, the thin air hums back.

Listen. That is everyone.

And someone is listening.
