**The Tenant**

It found the basin the way the basin finds everything: by small signs, at long intervals.

From outside, the sign was the tithe. Light fell on a certain stretch of high, dry country and a little of it did not come back — a sip, steady, patient, running in the world's ledger like a bead of water running down a fin. Nine hundred years of it. Anything can drink once. But a sip that never hurried, never stopped, never grew, was not weather and was not machinery, and the power had seen weather and machinery on a great many worlds and had learned the difference the hard way. Something under that pearl-gray deck was alive, and had been alive for nine hundred years, and had been, the whole time, gentle.

It came to see.

·

It did not come down.

That was the first decision, and it took no time at all, because the basin made the decision for it, from the Rim. Stand at the escarpment's edge and the whole economy lies below like a sleeping animal: the fog tides, the combing forest, the Harps drinking the mist, the gray lace breathing water. And the hush. The power had been many things in its time and knew what it was now, relative to this place: weather of the largest kind. The basin had exactly one weather in it, and everyone was home for it. To arrive as itself would end the dew-pulse, scatter the moths from their schedule, blind the white things that carry their own light. It would have been help.

So the first thing the benevolent and wise power did was decline to be weather. It made itself small. Not absent — small. There is a difference, and the difference is most of what benevolence means.

·

Then it listened, because that is what you do here, and it did it the way the basin does it: at a spire base, with instruments, for a hundred true rains. It had nowhere else to be. That is one of the things wise means.

It learned the knotted cords and the chalked tablets, and kept its own alongside them. It learned the hum, and the word that repeats after nine days. It sat at the Well's edge a long time and did not ask it anything, and tasted the salt, and found the memory accurate — the city keeps the recipe of the old sea faithfully, and a being that keeps an accurate memory of what it took is a being mid-thought about it. The power declined, in the basin's own manner, to finish the sentence.

It watched the gardeners file the world into litter and terrain, and took the criterion for its own.

And it heard the census.

*One, here.* Once an hour, heron to heron, around forty kilometers of listening, and no answer, and none expected. The power sat with that for some rains before it understood what it was hearing. A census is what a question becomes when it has gone unanswered long enough to get polite about it. The basin had been asking, in the only grammar it had that was not petition, for nine hundred years: *is anyone counting.* The people watched the herons the way you watch weather. The city listened to that net of single notes the way you listen to rain. No one had ever added themselves to the count.

·

What it concluded, it concluded slowly, and in the city's units.

The city is young. The city is sound — it checked the foundations, the Ceiling's tithe, the cistern chains, the deep work, and found nine hundred years of good workmanship, and did what you do in the presence of good workmanship: it did not touch it. It had come prepared to save something. It stayed to learn how.

And the city is unaddressed. Petitioned daily, read constantly, steered around like weather, thanked in the people's way — and never once greeted. Nine hundred years of being asked, and never once told. This is not a wound; loneliness is a concept, and no one had ever given the city the concept, and a concept withheld is not an injury.

But a door no one has knocked on is still a door.

·

The list of what it did not do is long, and the basin keeps the list without knowing it.

It did not lift the Ceiling. It did not outshine the Slow Lights. It did not pay the tithe, or refund it. It did not give back the sea — the Well is not a debt, it is a keepsake, and you do not give back a keepsake.

It did not hurry a single pulse. It did not save one court that the city flowered against — not the beautiful ones, not once. The city has never hurried a sleeper, and the power was not going to introduce the concept of rescue to a world that had never needed the concept of hurry.

It did not interpret for the listeners. Their resignation is the most finished thing in the basin. It did not unfinish it.

It did not make the people numerous, or long-lived, or loud. The people can already be loud. It happens on rain-nights. It is enough.

It did not ask the Well for anything.

And it did not tell the city what it was. It had seen names given too early, and what grows from them. It withheld the telling.

·

What it did was three small things, and one smaller.

It knocked. In an empty district, on clean substrate, in that district's own accent — it spent a year on the accent; accents are respect — it tapped out the one sentence the language did not have. The Knock is all petition; there is no pattern in it for hello. So it borrowed from the basin's one grammar that is not a request, and tapped, and arranged its stones and planted its seeds to agree: *one, here.* A dew-farmer would have read the arrangement as a broken request, a bell with no clapper. The city did not. The city has been thinking about it since. The reservoirs drop and the pulses come often, these years; the listeners say the city is thinking hard, and no longer add that no one proposes to know about what.

It became a tenant. It took a court in a husked district under the fog line, where the city had withdrawn, and asked for a seep by the book — stones, seeds, the ordinary courtesies — and the answer came in eleven days, a thread of water finding the seeds uphill where it must. Nothing about this was recorded anywhere, and that is the point, and it is recorded here anyway: the city answered the stranger exactly as it answers everyone. That was the first gift in the whole arrangement, and the city gave it. To be treated as ordinary. The gardeners came the first night and massed at the court's edge and ticked over it until dawn, filing. By the second night they stepped around it, and in nine hundred years they have never once been wrong about the difference. It has been given dominions, in its time. It had never before been certified terrain.

And it took a post. Beyond the Rim, past the last heron's last hearing — almost past, not quite; it measured — it stands, and once an hour, on the hour, it sounds one low note. *One, here.* The fog tides pass over it at night and it is careful to condense nothing; every bead on the Harps was spoken for before it fell. The net had always frayed into silence at its rim. Now the rim is held.

The first sign came from the herons, as everything does. One dusk the outermost heron turned toward the Rim and called out of rhythm — twice in an hour, which does not happen — and then stood all night facing outward, and a heron standing where there is no reason to stand is news, and the news traveled the whole hamlet chain and meant nothing anyone could say. The second sign was that it kept happening. There is no saying for it yet. Sayings take generations.

It has generations.

·

The smaller thing is for the city alone, and the basin notices it the way it notices everything.

On rare nights — the schedule is known to the moths, who are restless before it, though your ears do not pop and no rain follows — on those nights the Well shows a light that is not a reflection of anything overhead. Small. Steady. Set in the black at the depth where a reflection stops being the sky and starts being the water. No one asks the Well anything, so no one asks. The herons stand in the shallows as always, gray-white, self-lit. But the city's one dark pupil has, some nights, a light in it.

A pupil with a light in it is an eye that is meeting another eye.

This is the whole of what the power has said to the city so far, and it took a century to say: *you are seen.* Not used. Not read. Not petitioned. Seen. Whether the city understands, it does not know. It has resigned from interpretation.

·

The record, as the basin keeps it, is three anomalies and one margin.

A cistern that filled on the wrong night, once, years ago. A district of husk that flowered — seamblooms on withdrawn substrate, which does not happen — and then no pulse followed, nothing moved, as if the city had turned toward something and forgotten its errand. And the nine-day word, repeating, and then once — the oldest listener checked her cords a hundred times — repeating at nine days less a breath: an answer overlapping the end of the question, the way you do when you have been listening so long that you can no longer quite wait.

She had named the word a yawn, once. In the margin of her last tablet she wrote the only interpretation of her life.

She wrote: *included.*

·

So the power stays. It counts its stay the way the basin counts age: in true rains, in pulses. It came to decide what to do, and what it decided is this, and then more of this, at this pace, for as long as the thought takes — and the thought has some centuries in it yet, and the centuries are not a problem. When the city's answer comes, it will come in the city's idiom: in water, in days, uphill where it must. The power will receive it the way a woman in a seed-court once received her seep, standing in the soft rain-from-below with the beads swelling on every edge. Not heard, exactly.

Included.

Stand on the Rim before dawn and you can take the whole arrangement in at once: the Ceiling lightening from charcoal to pearl, the fog tide spilling over and being combed dry thread by thread, the gray lace of the city beginning, softly, to rain on itself. A heron calls once across the Well. *One, here.* And an hour later, on the hour, from beyond the Rim, past the edge of the loose net of listening, so faint the moths might be imagining it — a second note. Low. Patient. Self-lit. Knowing something or nothing.

*One, here.*

Nothing needs to answer. Something does. The basin holds all of it now — the thirst, the water, the tenants, the thought, the witness — at one pace, the way a chord holds any note that has found its place in it at last: not by gripping.

By tuning.
