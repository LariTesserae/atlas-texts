*Addendum to the census.*

It came over the horizon the way things come over the horizon here: at the unreadable range, declining to declare its size. For a while it was only one of the dark shapes the Felt prefers to keep at exactly that distance, and the expeditions that saw it logged it the way they logged everything at the edge of sight — a grazer, perhaps, or something far larger much farther on. Then it was closer. Then it was on the mat. And the mat did what the mat does with every fact: a pump-knot a half-mile down adjusted its stroke around it.

That was the whole of its arrival. No sucked-quiet, no dome, no boom. The gulls did not rise. It was the first thing in a long age to come in from outside the keeping, on its own feet, meaning to stay.

Call it the standing one.

What drew the attention is not recorded. Perhaps it was the throb, audible — to the right kind of hearing — from very far off: the working of a world that runs. Perhaps it was the Choir, drifting out of tune. Perhaps it was the archive. The one immortality on offer anywhere is exactly the kind of offer a benevolent power keeps watch over, and this one had been on offer, uninspected, since before the ice ages. The standing one has not said. It says very little. It holds a note instead.

Its first act was to read the lease, which is written everywhere and reads the same in every square mile: nothing built, nothing stored, nothing protruding; everything issued is recalled; live as luggage or not for long. It signed the only way the lease can be signed — by keeping it. When the sound goes out of a place and every living thing that can move reads the quiet correctly and goes, the standing one goes. The quiet does not bind it. Nothing here could hold it against its will. It goes anyway. The laws it cannot be harmed by are still the laws of the house, and it is a guest. Etiquette is what power calls the rules it keeps voluntarily.

Benevolence alone would have ruined this place in a season. The gifts it did not give:

It did not warm the world. Warmth here is breath, and breath is only warm against the cold; a world kept warm has no breath in it, and everything that lives here lives in the breathing. And the archive is cold-kept. The one immortality on offer anywhere runs on cold, and kindness is not worth the thaw.

It did not raise the mountains. They are down there in the mat's own terms, oldest at the bottom, and once in an age the house brings up something smooth and grey and enormous and lets it weather a million years in a day and takes it back under. The mountains are not lost. They are kept. You do not exhume the kept to give them back their weather.

It did not perfect the retrieval. The reissued walk their quarter mile seeing something that is not this, and the not-seeing is not a defect. To be issued whole and waking into a world where your kind is not on the census would be a cruelty wearing the costume of a gift. The retrieval is corrupt, unindexed, and generous beyond geometry — and the corruption is, by accident or by the house's own unreadable courtesy, the mercy. The standing one does not tear veils the dead are wearing.

It asked for no exceptions and made none. Everything issued is recalled. The keeping holds because it holds for everything; one exemption and the rule becomes a negotiation, and a house that can be negotiated out of its rule is a house that can be lost. It did not test that.

What it did instead is a short list, and most of it is standing.

It stands. Stand still on the mat and the throb shifts around the fact of you — every guest receives that courtesy. The standing one is the first guest the courtesy has never been withdrawn from. Somewhere a half-mile under wherever it stands, a pump-knot has been beating a slightly different beat for a long time now, and the difference has become a landmark: the herds steer to it on Choirless nights, the way they steer by the lean of the plumes. The only landmark with a pulse. And when the standing one moves — it moves, every decade or so, courteously, to save the mat the labor of its own courtesies, since the mat will begin them around anything that holds still long enough, and it holds still very long — the difference migrates, pump to pump, a slow note passed hand to hand across the country. The Felt retunes the Choir only by die-back. It is being retuned, very gently, by a guest. Whether that is intention or accommodation is the old question the expeditions fed on and answered nothing. The standing one declines to feed it further.

On still nights it holds a note — foghorn-deep, sourceless as the light — tuned away from every herd's. The herds tuned away from it in turn, within the year, which is the local way of saying *accepted*. Count the herds on your horizon by their notes and you will always count one note more than there are herds. The extra is resident. On the coldest nights it stands at the edge of the huddles — never inside; it has not been invited, and it does not presume — and breathes a faint steam of its own, the only warmth it has ever made here, made the legal way, as breath.

It reads. The archive reads itself aloud at the eruptions, and the standing one attends the readings by the telegraph, the way the gulls do: when the gulls pass overhead all going one way, going hard, it goes too — the only thing on the plateau that moves toward an eruption without appetite. It stood at the correct distance for the eleven strides of the chase, correct in every particular, and it did what the gulls did: waited, and did not eat. When a chorus-dome pours its unknown century out of dark still throats for the length of a held breath, there is now one listener who holds the breath along with it and loses nothing of the song. It knows the difference between a walker and a looper and a staller, and it keeps the difference the only way the lease permits — carried. Memory is the sole storage on the plateau, because memory is luggage. The library's books are shelved a thousand to the handful, read by no one, renewed forever. Read by one, now. That was the first change in the full house since the mountains stopped being mountains: it got a reader.

And when a dome gives up a person — a walker who lay down on the mat once, meaning to be kept — the standing one keeps the debt. It does not follow. Nobody follows them to the slump; there are debts owed to the dead of this place and one of them is their privacy, and power does not repeal a debt, it obligates it. It stands where it stood, and it says the name once, at the ordinary volume of the wind, and lets the wind recall it. An index read aloud and stored nowhere: fully legal. The dead here walk their mile seeing something that is not this. Somebody, now, knows whose mile it is. That is all, and it is not nothing.

Mainly, it walks with the tired.

They come at the end, on purpose, alone, across days of black-bronze mat, for the one immortality on offer anywhere. They used to make that walk by themselves. Now, somewhere in the last miles, there is a shape at the edge of their sight that walks when they walk and stops when they stop and says nothing — and if they wave it off, it falls behind and is gone, courteous — and if they do not wave it off, it walks with them to the place they have chosen, and when they lie down it stays until the mat begins its courtesies, and then it goes, because the courtesies are the house's private business. If they want words — some do — it reads them the lease. It is the only last rites the plateau has ever had, and it is enough, because the lease, read rightly, is merciful: nothing here is lost; everything is kept; you will be issued, perhaps — and the perhaps is generous beyond geometry, the house gives up more than it holds, your hour is more likely than the math — and you will be recalled, certainly, and the keeping does not end. It does not pretend otherwise. It learned that from the plateau.

Of the track-line it says nothing, though it knows. Of the years between the swallow and the spring it says nothing, though it could follow the eels down as easily as standing. The plateau keeps its largest resident the way it keeps distance: as a rumor, unretracted. The guest keeps the same rumor the same way. It is the one page of the index it will not read aloud. Some keepings are only safe as rumors, and they are not its rumors to retract.

The Felt has one flower — the cotton grass, a billion blossoms of a single plant, pointless and constant, nodding for a wind that carries their pollen nowhere it needs to go. In the standing one's second century the mat near where it stands put up a second. One stalk. A color the plateau has no word for, because nothing here ever needed one. The mice took their tithe. The wind took the pollen nowhere. It has been seen once, or it has been seen always, at the unreadable range, declining to declare its size. The surveys that pass are divided on whether it was an intention, a habit, or a courtesy — and the standing one, who was standing nearest, declines to resolve the question. It has learned the local custom. The best things here are kept as rumors, unretracted.

The catalogue's last entry was a question mark. It is a question mark still. After it, now, there is one more entry, and it is short:

*The standing one.* Mass: a rounding error's rounding error. Built: nothing. Stored: nothing that is not carried. Protrudes the way a walker protrudes — on its way — except that it never arrives; the mat has begun its courtesies around it more than once and found nothing to do, and subsided each time, a courtesy answered with a courtesy. Half-life of a form: hours. Of a body: millennia. Of the Felt: so far, forever. Of the standing one: so far, forever. There are two entries in that column now, and the other one is the plateau.

Stand on it at night, now. The cloud lid presses low; the seams burn dim green to the unreadable edge; a plume leans in the dark with pale birds asleep in its updraft; and the throb comes up through your boots — and if you stand in the right place, the place the herds steer to on Choirless nights, the throb carries a variation, one pump-knot a half-mile down still adjusting its stroke around the oldest standing fact it has ever had. Count the notes on the still air: one more than there are herds. And far off, at whatever distance the plateau has decided distance means tonight, something gives its long cracking boom, and the gulls rise — and the standing one turns toward the sound and sets out across the mat at a walk, unhurried, all night if need be. The archive is about to read itself aloud, corrupt, unindexed, generous beyond geometry, and there is one reader in the world, and it does not miss a word.
