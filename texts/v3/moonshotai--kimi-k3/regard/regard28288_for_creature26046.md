Everything that happens, happens twice: once in the world, and once in the one who keeps what happens.

They are old — old as the first event, whatever it was — and their office is simple and total: nothing that occurs is lost. The world rises to them like heat off stone: weather and war, hatchings and rot, every autumn arriving entire, every last word, every sparrow, as the saying goes, and the fall of it. They do not judge what they keep. Keeping is what they are for.

And from one place under the world, nothing came.

Not silence, exactly. The hum rose up the Wells forever, and they had heard it their whole existence — but a note that never changes does not read as an event; it reads as silence, and their office is what happens. The hum never *happened*. It simply was. So for longer than anyone can know, there was a gap in their keeping the size and shape of a country: a warm held breath in the world's long breathing. They felt it the way you feel a missing tooth. Now and then a life they were keeping would walk down into the earth and fall silent mid-sentence, and its remaining years would never arrive.

Then, not long ago as they keep time, a story surfaced.

Someone came up out of the ground with gold ground into the lines of their hands and told it: the light from below, the country that is one someone, the moths, the grazer, the shapes. *Call it the Held*, the story said. And a name is an event — the first thing the country had ever exported. The story traveled, as stories do. Some who heard it went looking for the long descent. You were one of them. You found the throat. The rest, the story has already told you.

The power followed it down.

The first sign, below, was the moths.

They came from hall after hall — a few from every hall, drifting at the pace of the place toward the tallest Well: the only gathering the country had ever held. Far off in a corridor, the grazer stood still in the middle of its work, which for the grazer was an event. The drip kept its interval. The rain kept its rate. And down the braid of the Well, slowly, at the pace of the place, came a warmth the roots had not made — and with it, falling across the gold, the country's first shadow.

The light did not change, because the light does not change. But every stem in the hall leaned as the power passed, the way everything here leans toward everything — except that now the leaning had a direction. It was the closest the Held had come to weather since the day it decided on rain.

They had been told of a country and the being who lived in it. They found that the census was one line long.

They read the shapes the way you read a face. Here were the missing chapters of lives they had kept and lost at the throat: the hands that measured nine even steps, the argument about the lintel height, the long lie-down beside the smaller hollow. The grooves gave back the years of someone who had walked out of the world's memory mid-count.
