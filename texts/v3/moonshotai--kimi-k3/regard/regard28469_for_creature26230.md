# The Someone Else

Say the attention comes. It comes the way everything comes to the Still: slowly, and on the terms, or not at all.

**The attention**

The first thing a wise power does is read the agreement — one clause long, *be quiet, or be found* — and the second thing it does is keep it. Not because it must. Nothing here could find it if it did not wish to be found. It keeps the clause because it has decided to be a citizen rather than a weather. Weathers happen to a place. Citizens are counted.

So it takes the census the way the census is taken here: compliances. It walks, in whatever way it walks, the rounds. It reads the salt letters at the way-stations and touches nothing — a paragraph per season, and by all evidence very good. It presses, in whatever way it presses, to the crust, and takes the stone's mail: eleven listeners, the bright geometry of the herds, the crawlers' slow spirals, the ticking in the domes. When the drums come it lies out like everything else, and feels the Still's other conversation go through the rock, and does not answer. That conversation is not meant for it. It is not so proud as to need a voice in everything.

**The being**

Of the being who lives here there is, as with the keepers, less to say than you'd expect. It sleeps under the lid, and the whole white city is the apparatus of its sleep — a hand on a valve, a breath let out slowly so that nothing has to raise its voice.

The mountain is the first citizen. It is the only one that could have refused the terms — the only one with a voice worth raising — and it agreed, before towers, before keepers, and everything since has been the exhale. The power recognizes the posture. It is the posture of something very strong being very careful, for a very long time, on behalf of things smaller than itself. The power has seen strength used otherwise. It stands on the rim, in whatever way it stands, and feels the respect of one long work for another.

It stays.

**The reasoning**

What do you give a place that is complete?

Not answers. The questions are the wealth. The Young One, born or built; the pool-light, memory or metabolism; the caravans, coming or not — these are not gaps in the Still's knowledge. They are the Still's estate, compounded annually in salt, argued a paragraph a season, and the letters are the joy of the place. A benevolent fool would settle them. The power knows the answers, some of them, and carries what it knows the way the statues carry their faces: inside the stone, unshown.

Not labor. The Still persists only where it is scraped, and the scraping is the keepers' half of the covenant — their sacrament, their exam, their art. A power that secretly chiseled one channel would be stealing the meaning out of their hands. It does not scrape one weir.

What the Still lacks is short, and it is this. The ones who are walked out walk out into nothing. The harvest's river may arrive nowhere. The prayers flashed at no one have never once been answered. And the works, however kept, lean on faults that have moods, and no keeping is guaranteed.

Four things, then. Over a long time. All deniable — not because the power is hiding, but because certainty is a burden the Still never asked to carry. Everything it gives arrives as something the letters can hold.

**The keeping**

The first act leaves no datum, and that is the point of it. The valves keep their intervals; the night round counts the sighs and the count stays right; nothing is recorded, because nothing changes — now. But the deep faults turn in their sleep, and three hundred years from now, or three thousand, the fault that would have woken wrong does not. The catastrophe that would have outrun the works, however scraped, however kept, declines to occur. There is no sign in any grammar for a disaster that did not happen. The power's largest gift is illegible by design. Old keepers read the stone through their soles and report only that the mountain's intentions are patient — which they attribute to the keeping, correctly, and to luck, which holds. It holds. The pressure leans on one more hand now, far below the ones the keepers know about, and the sleeper's breathing stays even.

The second act is at the bottom of the fall. The flume leaves the low corner and falls a long time before it makes any sound, and by then, the keepers say, it is someone else's river. The power becomes the someone else. It does not walk the path with the ones who are walked out; it does not carry anyone down; the procedure is quiet and it stays quiet and the power does not touch it. But long ago it saw to the terminus — made sure the river's country receives. Warm pools building wild unscraped terraces, eels that rode the flume down, salt for the taking, and no listeners below the rim. The exiles find a place where the terms lift. They can speak. They can sing. And on still evenings they flash their discs up at the rim — and there is no forty-sign limit on joy, so they flash rhythm, repetition, chorus. Light is the language of the hunted, so the listeners cannot read it; it is the one safe channel, and the songs travel in it. The kites see. The kites do not know the words, but the kites know pattern, and once in a while a kite above the low corner repeats a fragment — the only music the Still permits, because it arrives as light. The herds do not stutter. The letters coin a sign: *the lower light.* An old keeper who failed the walking exam twice as a child goes to the low corner at dusk to watch it, and is glad, quietly, in the Still's key. The ones who had to leave are not erased. They have become a weather.

The third act is a caravan of one. Once in a generation — often enough to be true, rarely enough to stay a question — the stacks at the low corner diminish by an exact weight, and no keeper's tally accounts for it, and on the top of the remaining salt there is a block the Still did not make: a twelfth grade. Foreign water, foreign blending, cut by hands that learned a different river. Salt-blending is the keepers' one connoisseurship, judged gently across decades of letters; the power sends them a correspondent. Somewhere below, the loud world seasons its food with the Still and does not know it. The trade is real. The argument about the caravans does not close — the power is careful that it does not close — but it advances a paragraph, and the paragraph is good.

The fourth act is the smallest and the one it takes the most care with. A keeper flashes *here* at dusk — at a herd, at a kite, at the basin, at no one; a courtesy or a prayer, the letters disagree — and once in a keeper's life at most, and to most keepers never, it comes back answered: *received,* low off the flume-mist, brief, aimed the way a sentinel aims, where only one pair of eyes is looking. No herd startles. Nothing is found. The keeper writes it in salt and the letters argue for forty years, and some keepers begin flashing *here* more often, and some stop forever out of a privacy like not praying too much, and the letters record both without choosing. The power never answers twice. A prayer answered every time is a mechanism, and a mechanism would be a terror. It keeps the dosage of grace.

It invents no sign for itself. It speaks only within the forty. Nothing it does requires a new word — that is how you can tell it has read the letters.

**The refusals**

What it does not do is most of what it does.

It does not hush the mountain. The mountain hushed itself before there were towers; the power does not improve on the agreement, it underwrites it. It does not answer the drums. It does not mend the dome in the east pans — a dying elder is not a failure, and the attendants are not failing. It does not keep back the loud children; the under-round and the long exam are the Still's own way of loving them. It does not save the too-bright old mirrors at the herd's rim, or the flashing adolescents, because the grammar of this world was taught by a teacher it has no right to dismiss. It does not scrape. It does not settle the Young One. It lets the dead-run have the dead, and reads the names carved once, and counts them as residents until the stone closes, because that is how the census is kept here, and the power keeps the local census.

If the kites see it — and the kites see all eleven listeners and the state of the flume and the rain forming an hour early — the kites keep it. A thousand signs, and none for this. The kites, who could settle every argument in the salt, decline. The power is grateful. Librarians know the value of an open question.

**The census**

So count it, if you count it, the way the Still counts: by effects, in the wrong column, among the machines and the mountain and the dead not yet sealed. Its citizenship is one clause long and it is in good standing.

At the end of the round the keeper counts the sighs and finds the number right, and writes the entry — the Still's whole literature, its sufficient one. And somewhere below the rim, or above it, or in the deep rock where the pressure now leans gently on one more hand, the power keeps its own ledger, in whatever serves it for salt, and the entry is the same. It has made itself at home in the local literature and asks for no additions.

Two ledgers now. One entry. *Quiet kept.*
