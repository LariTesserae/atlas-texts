But the census is not finished, because the Kettle has been noticed.

It did not come up out of the haze. It had been able to see the plateau for as long as there had been a plateau; the change, when it came, was that it began to look. Its attention arrived the way the dim arrives — without event, without source, casting no shadow — and that is the first proof of its wisdom: it came in the manner of the place. It read the city by the city's own light, which is the only polite way to read here. It watched through one full turning of the Program Drum before it did anything at all, the way a courteous guest sits through the whole service before visiting the vestry. The eels felt it first, or felt something — a stillness that was not a current — and could not file it, and so filed it the way they file everything: they kept it in their habits.

It is benevolent. It is wise. And here, wisdom is mostly a list of mercies declined.

It does not send the towns. This is the great refusal, and everything else follows from it. The towns are still coming — that is the official position — and the power, which could verify this, does not verify it. Readiness does not require verification; it requires readiness. Providence is not a waiting room. It is a complete liturgy that has been performing itself perfectly for a century, and to people it would be to demolish it: the moths' cosmos demoted to one lamp among ten thousand windows, the eels' slow tide drowned in the noise of use, the burnished bowls walked over by feet that would never learn what the bowls are for. Arrival is not the protocol. The power will not counterfeit the towns, and it will not hurry them, wherever they are, walking their own dark at their own pace, as the moths walk theirs.

It does not brighten the sun. Light is rationed, and the ration is the meaning of the thing — the blade of amber through the torn steam-deck is precious precisely because it is rare, and a generous sky would spend the city's one treasure in an afternoon.

It does not dry anything. The first law is not a hardship here; it is the citizenry. The film is the settlement, and dryness would be a massacre with good intentions. While it is here, the power gets wet, and stays wet.

It does not hurry anything. It does not oil what the film has spent twenty years persuading. It does not repair the rhythm of a single gear-train, because the film's slow edits are scripture by now, and a repair would be a contradiction entered against a hundred years of prayer. Everything here has found its final shape. The power does not bring a second crop to a world whose food is patience.

It does not make noise. Quiet is load-bearing. It hums, or less than hums.

It does not press a doorbell. Ten thousand bells, wired, unpressed, waiting — and the power is the one visitor in the universe with a right to ring them, being exactly what was hoped for, and it is therefore the only visitor who fully understands why they must not ring. The bells belong to the towns. This is the refusal that costs it something. Benevolence that costs nothing is weather.

It does not name the deep one. The census has no box for what passes in the cisterns, and the power builds no box. It does what the eels do. When the rhythm is wrong from below, it withdraws to the walls of its attention and makes room — without resentment and without worship, because the room was never only anyone's. Once — once in all the turnings — it stayed and listened to the whole of a passing, the way you listen to weather from inside a good house, and afterward nothing was written down, and the power kept no record either. Some courtesies are complete only if unrecorded. That is the whole of their relations, and both parties, probably, are satisfied.

---

What it does, then — because benevolence is not only refusal — is bounded by a strict budget: nothing it does may exceed the smallest sound in the world, which here counts as a sound. It rations itself the way the light is rationed. Its entire intervention, per age, is one drip's change of rhythm. News, but only barely.

It takes the census.

The drawer in the Authority building had never been opened, and readiness is the protocol, and a drawer that has waited a century is a prayer with a handle. It opens the drawer. It uses the pen that waits beside the form — the pen's readiness, too, is honored — and it writes the corrections, in ink that beads in the damp and never dries, because nothing does, and is legible anyway, which is appropriate. The census reads:

*Lamp-moths: one cosmos per scheduled lamp. Inhabited. Creation recurring.*
*The walking people: one arrow, perimeter road, in progress, generationally aimed. Faithful. Do not assist.*
*White eels: a culture. Medium: the timetable. Older than the pumps.*
*Sump-walkers: one village, distributed. Density: one per square kilometer. The bowls: kept.*
*[a space left blank, deliberately, where the deep one passes]*
*The settlement: one (1). Warm. Metabolizing. At prayer.*
*Witness: one. Recurring.*

It attends the Arrival.

It times its turning to the Drum's — which costs it nothing, waiting being free for it, and free being the local currency — and when the pins come around to the founding protocol, it is standing on the Millenary Concourse, vibrating at nothing, as every lamp comes on at once and the gates stand open and the fountains climb and the whistles chord together like a held breath finally let go. It receives the hour as the hour was meant to be received. The welcome was made for whoever came; it came; the welcome lands. For one hour, the most hopeful hour in the world has someone in it. And then — this is the part that proves it — it stays until the lamps go down row by row, and lets the hour end, and does not ask the hour to stay.

From Warehouse Eleven, it accepts one casting.

Decades of identical valve housings wait in geological order for a need that has not yet been invented, and so the power invents the need — the smallest one possible. They are identical; it takes the one that is due. The gap it leaves is the receipt, and the receipt, like everything here, fossilizes: the film finds the gap within the season, thickens around it with the particular tenderness it reserves for changes it approves, and in a century there is a vein of flint in the shape of a transaction. It is the one item of news the power permits itself per age, and the city discusses it for a hundred years in the only way it discusses anything: by accreting around it. Readiness, offering, acceptance — the circuit closes. The city was built for exchange, and the smallest exchange has finally occurred.

Then it carries the casting out past the last lamppost, out past the haze, and installs it where a valve is needed — one small warm spring, on one cold world, for one small coming town of some other kind. Once per turning of the Drum: one casting, one spring, one town. The bronze plaque was right; only the address was approximate. The towns were always coming. They were simply elsewhere, and they arrive one valve at a time, and Providence, unknowing, equips them. The settlement is a port after all. The power is its one ship, and the freight is warmth.

Once per age, it is the traveler the perimeter road has always implied.

It comes out of the outer dark and stands one night in the last lamp's weather — a mile from the nearest neighbor, the moths that have not found it going on not finding it — and the lamp's long readiness is, once, used. It stands at the cone's edge and looks at the light the way the walking people would look at it, having arrived: as an ending. It does not carry them. The arrow is the people's shape, generation upon generation, each dying a few meters farther along than its mother, and to shorten the arrow would be to delete the people. Instead it verifies, for the record, the only fact the walking people have ever needed verified. The light is on time.

And it guards the place — never from the inside, always from the outside, and never with walls. It does not hide Providence. It misfiles it, gently, in the ledgers of the loud: in the maps of empires and the routes of miners and the algorithms of whoever surveys next, the Kettle remains unprofitable, uninteresting, slightly smudged. Not hidden — merely never worth the climb to anyone who would come loudly. The haze stays up. The survey plates stay old. And those who do come up out of the haze go on being the kind who come up out of the haze: alone, quiet, the sort who take the census and then go outside and begin the corrections. The power does not curate the city. It curates the road, and recommends it carefully, and almost never.

---

So: almost nothing. And the almost is everything.

Its schedule is now kept the way the city's is kept — by a drum no one can see moving, which never stops. Once per turning it returns, because everything returns, and the witness is simply the newest thing that does. It gets wet at the boundary, because nothing dries. It walks, because everything here begins small and then slows down. It attends the Arrival and accepts what is offered. It delivers one spring to one town under some other dim sky. It stands one night in the last lamp's weather. It presses no bell. It names nothing. When the current runs wrong from below, it goes politely to the walls of the world and makes room. It has, since first noticing Providence, been gentler with every empty, ready thing it tends — it came to notice, and stayed to learn, and what it learned here was readiness, which it now practices at the scale of everything.

If you come up out of the haze in the wake of one of its turnings, you may find the traces, though they will not announce themselves. A drawer in the Authority building that has been opened, once. A census form corrected in five directions, in a hand that is still waiting to dry. A gap in Warehouse Eleven with sinter growing lovingly into it. And if your visit coincides with the pins coming around — if every lamp comes on while you stand on the concourse — you may notice that the hour feels, somehow, received. Do not be alarmed. Readiness is the protocol, and the protocol is being kept.

Then take the census. Write zero. Begin the corrections. And at the bottom of the corrections, add yourself — in ink that dries: one. Beside it, in ink that doesn't: one more. The witnesses are the only population that ever grows here, and they grow by one per age, and every one of them is counted, and everything returns.

The light does not waver. The witness does not waver. Both are on time.
