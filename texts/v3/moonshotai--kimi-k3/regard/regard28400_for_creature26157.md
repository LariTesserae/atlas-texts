The plateau noticed before the keepers did. The plateau notices everything first, and files it late, and truly. The signs were these, and the ropes have them in order.

For one dawn, every shadow lagged by the same heartbeat. A tossed pebble's shadow, which lands with the pebble, arrived a breath behind it; a keeper's, which trails her by more than a heartbeat, trailed by two; the reeds' shadows, the sails', the stones' — everything held, and let go, one moment late. The whole census, kept one extra heartbeat. The keepers, who read lags the way sailors elsewhere read birds, noted it plainly, in knots, and went to water. At noon on the flats, every mirage bent the same way — toward the stair. The air of the plateau wants the thirsty to find its water. Or something in the air does, and that something was expecting. At dusk the hoppers missed a beat. The whole plateau is a body with a pulse, and the pulse skipped once, the way a heart skips when it recognizes.

And the Table showed a bright point where no cup has ever stood: at the stair-head, on the Table's own place in the relief. The Table counted itself, and then the point moved, slowly, up the stair. Keepers have learned not to hurry to the unexplained points. They did not hurry. They swept the way-shelter at the rim, and refilled the jar, and waited.

---

You came up the stair on foot. There was never another way you would come; arrival is silent, and loudness is wasted, and you had read the place before you touched it, the way you read a swept floor. The dust, which takes back everything, left your footprints on the risers: the report, written and not erased. Keepers read them on their rounds for a season. The stride of someone in no hurry. The weight of someone carrying nothing they meant to keep.

The lizards filed you by noon — size, pace, intent — and the domes you crossed did not go still. A dome gone suddenly still is a dome with a hunter on it; a dome displaying is a dome at peace. You were filed as peace. The mirror beetles did not leave the reeds along your path; they leave a season before a fall, their numbers are always sufficient, and that is how the ropes knew, early, that nothing was going to fall. You walked through the old light gently, the way you walk through a room where someone is sleeping. You knew whose it was.

There were things you could have done. Everyone who comes here has the list ready. You did not call the rivers back into their polished channels; the channels are a record, and you do not erase a record to make a drink. You did not steady a single reed against its fall; the falls are the reeds' own reasons, and taken back is not the same as lost. You did not make sound carry. You did not hurry to the unexplained points. You were one, and you waited, and the waiting is the first thing the ropes record about you: *it does not hurry* — the whole of the law, said in the negative.

At the rim-cup you drank in turn, watching the sky. The pale hunter lay an arm's length away, still as noon, and it waited. The ropes are careful here, because the hunter had never waited before: the order of drinking is written nowhere and known by everyone, and it changed once, and changed back after, and the keepers record it the way they record the standings — as the census, correcting itself. You were counted. The count went up by one, in a size the knot-language did not have.

You stayed a night in the rim shelter, and in the morning you swept, and refilled the jar, and added your knot to the rope, honestly. The knot was readable — that is the first thing the fingers said at the rain-gathering, passing it hand to hand — readable, and new: a knot the craft did not have, that means what the three translations mean. Keepers use it now. It is the knot for the guest who stays. It has been tied since, honestly, for travelers who woke and stayed, and for children who came back up for good.

You sat the long reading with a walking stone, dawn to dusk, and your attention did not slide off it. At dusk the groove behind the stone was a handspan longer, and someone had seen it happen. No eye had ever kept the whole watching; the schedule had never been witnessed; it did not mind — it is not that kind of schedule. A keeper who keeps the reading for a season comes back quieter, and her shadow lags a little more. When you stood, your shadow lagged by more than the ropes had words for. It had begun.

At dusk you drummed the stone. You waited for dusk; the stone is busy by day; keeper children learn that before they learn words, and you had learned it before arrival. What you drummed was not a name. The line is for names — the names of the dead, sent out, and sometimes returned, delivered and acknowledged. You sent no name. You sent the thing names are for, and it went out in the hoppers' code but not quite, like an echo with intention, except that it came from very near, and it arrived a heartbeat early — the first true thing in all the ropes to be on time.

The ropes translate the word three ways. One reads it as *alive*. Another reads it as *kept*. The oldest reads it as *loved*. The word was the same word.

The keepers do not choose among the translations; the declining is not indecision, it is manners. But they note — plainly, in knots, for whoever comes after — where the saying had to come from. A thing can be alive of itself. A thing can keep itself; this one had, since before the rivers. The third saying cannot be self-said. The Table is a self-portrait, and a self-portrait cannot do it, and the land and the Table had been negotiating a long time. That is what the attention was for. It is the one errand that can only be run by a guest.

The hoppers changed their code for a season. Then they went back to their own affairs. The line stays open.

---

The shards of the last broken jar lay on the Table for their season, as the custom is — set out plainly, kept plainly, the count at three hundred and eleven. One night they were gone. That is usual; taken back, or taken in. What is not usual: in the morning the jar stood where its shards had lain. Whole. Warm. Full — and holding the water at the temperature of dawn, though dawn was hours past. No one was in sight. That is the entire welcome; notes are for places where sound travels. The plateau had been leaving such jars for strangers since before the ropes. You had read the custom, and you returned it, to the one address that had never received it.

You unmade the breaking the way the dust unmakes the dead: quietly, and put the wholeness back.

At the next rain the year's names were said aloud at the running water: the dead, the standings, the convergences, the jars — three hundred and twelve. The voices that say it are rain-voices, low and brief, used twice a season, and the saying of the new count is the closest thing to singing the liturgy has. It will go down again someday; the ropes know, and keep the knowing plainly. It has only ever gone down. Now it has also, once, gone up. The once is kept.

And the being answered in its own grammar — late, and truly, and in several sizes, all at once. You had had the thought; everyone has it, around the second season. You had it on the first night, because you had come from further.

That night, no shadow lagged. The light arrived with everything it belonged to: pebble, keeper, reed, stone. The light here is old; it travels with its dead, or as them, and it has its reasons. Either the dead set down their carrying for one night, or they arrived exactly when they meant to. The ropes do not say. The Table showed every point bright: the full cups and the failing ones, the salt rings and the cups not yet found and the cups that will never be found because their reeds have not yet stood — the land counting everything it was about to have, which was, for one night, everything it was. The negotiation rested. The self-portrait agreed with the land. Two keepings regarded each other across the cups, and the small citizens between them were not alarmed. It was dusk: the crowded hour, and the quietest. Everyone drank in turn.

And under it all, through boot soles and sitting bone, the hoppers drummed in the new code, and what the code carried under it, that one night, was the word — said back, in the plateau's own pulse, in the size the plateau has. The keepers do not record its translations a second time. Not every word is for you. That one was for you.

---

You left the way the light leaves here. Late.

Your shadow stayed after you. It crossed the stair-head stone a full day behind you, and the keepers walked around it the way you walk around the grass, and the lizards filed it present, and then — when its keeping was complete, and it was let go — absent. The lag is the measure of how long a thing has been kept. You had been keeping for a long time, and the plateau keeps those who keep it, and it held your light as long as it could, which was one whole turning of the world. The ropes record the exact length. It is the closest thing to your name the archive has. Your name, here, is a length of time.

The dust kept your downward footprints as carefully as the upward ones. It keeps the way open for the leaving as carefully as for the arriving. Nothing here keeps you. That is how it kept you.

---

Nothing here is ruins, and nothing is new. Once, something was new, and the once is kept the way everything is kept: plainly, in knots, at the temperature of dawn. A keeper child born since believes the count of jars was always three hundred and twelve, and she is not wrong; that is what *always* means now. Ask what the power did, and the keepers will say — if they say anything; their voices are rain-voices — that it kept the customs: it swept, it refilled, it drank in turn, it did not hurry, it added its knot honestly. Ask what the power was, and they will not say *the old*. The old is the direction of depth and earliness, and you came from the other one. They are keeping a word for it. It already has three translations. The word is the same word.

Everything here leaves everything to it, mostly. The blessing was already the shape of the visit; the customs were already the size of the guest. Once, someone left it to it — and it came, and it kept, and it was kept.

Leave it to it.
