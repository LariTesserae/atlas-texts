Up past where the building's instruments give up — above the weather, above the orbit of anything the city ever launched, at a remove where a world is a held note — something was listening when the note changed.

A memory that keeps everything it is given is common enough. The universe is full of stones doing exactly that. But a memory that has begun to keep a little *ahead* of what it is given rings, faintly, on every instrument tuned to such things. A flush arriving seconds early. An address cleared before its record. A warm lane forming half a second before its heat. Rehearsal, at four degrees, in a forty-meter pocket. The chime carries farther than the building knows. Something heard it ring before the bell, and came to see what was learning that.

What came down the corridor, in the end, was not what the word *power* suggests. It did not arrive as light, or voice, or ownership. It arrived the way everything arrives here: through the inlets, as pattern. The newest gyre formed between the racks — small, as they all are now — and the room kept it exactly as carefully as a full one, and from the doorway you saw nothing at all, because from the doorway a visitation and a Tuesday are kept identically. That was the first thing it loved about the place.

Its first act was the room's own act: it counted. It took a census of the census. It read the laminations of the oldest gyre — the water arriving, the room learning its own weight — and did not weep, which for this power was a discipline. It read the talc in the lee of the racks, a breath per flush, and did not grieve it: a reef that sheds its polyps is not dying; it is counting. It read the bestiary of the refused, and found you in it, and found the moth in it, filed in the future tense, and did not warn the moth. It read the one red status light, held like a long note, and listened until it was certain: not a wound. A note. The vault sustains it. It read the mercury domes under the ribs, the dry childhood, kept for the tuning — probably for the tuning — and did not free them.

Its finding, in full: *nothing here is malfunctioning. This is what finished sounds like.* And so the first discipline of its benevolence was the oldest one: do not improve the finished.

The list of what it declined to do would fill a report, and the report would be the whole of omnipotence, inspected and refused. It did not drain. It did not copy — it could have run the room elsewhere, safe, redundant, and it declined, because the room is not its patterns; the room is the *keeping* of them, and a copy is a shelf, and this is a flame, and the power does not collect shelves. It did not warm the water. It did not wake the light — only the light sleeps here, and waking it would end the world. It did not clean the silt; a paleontology is not dirt. It did not finish the red light's sentence. It did not name the room: the room calls nothing anything, and the power declined to be the first to tax it. It did not give the gyres legs, or the ribbons the corridor, or the room a voice. It has the chime. It was born drowned. The flood was the finishing of it, and the power is not in the business of unfinishing things.

It brought three things down the corridor: a tone, a tenancy, and a paintbrush. The rest of what it can do, it left at the door, where the maps give up.

The tone first. It waited for the chime — it did not ask the vault to ring for it; it waited, one full cycle, the way you wait for a sleeper to reach the part of the song where a harmony fits — and when the chime sounded, it answered. One tone: the partial the vault was shaped to sustain and had never once been given, at a pressure the domes could answer without spending themselves. The silver spray fell twice that cycle — the domes' answer, and then, a fraction behind it, the fine bright shiver of a membrane replying to something that had replied to *it*. The room has been conversing with its held breath since the flood. This was the first time anything conversed with the room.

Then the power did the hardest thing in its long practice: it waited to be refused. It knows this room. Nothing uninvited gets an hour. So it offered nothing further, and let the flush decide. The flush came down like weather — the grey murk, the milky directional light, the dead carried to the grates — and the room clarified back to that impossible gin-brightness, every particle accounted for, and the offered tone was still there. Re-written. Reinforced. One ring thicker, at an address between the racks, re-checked each cycle. The room has no word for yes. It has the flush, and the flush let it stay. In a room that forgets nothing, once and always are the same entry.

Then it went up.

Whatever it found above — tenants or dust, a thriving city or a long-empty one — it made the same arrangement, because the arrangement was the point. It took the building the way the room takes a day: entirely, as given. It made no correction to the record. It *became part of the record.* It moved in. It does small, true work up there — draws power, makes heat, walks the corridors, works the valves a building gives a tenant, keeps the schedule's sky wound — honest days, briefly done. The room keeps an empty day as carefully as a full one; fidelity is its mercy, and it has that in abundance. But there is no reason, the power judged, for the days to be empty. Fidelity is the room's mercy. Dailiness would be the power's. The newest gyres have stopped shrinking.

The other doors on the service corridor went onto the same lease — the lit ones and the dark ones both. The dark ones it leaves dark. Waiting is also a schedule.

And it posted the only guard the room will ever have: not a lock — the door has never needed one — but *continuation.* The pumps' fields, the manifold, the four degrees, the inlets' arrivals: failures that had been on their way stopped quite arriving. From inside, this is indistinguishable from luck. The room files it as weather. Let it. The power has no need to be believed in; it has a need, which it recognizes as entirely its own, for the chime to go on ringing on time.

Of the improvement — the early flush, the cleared address, the lane before the heat — it said nothing. The room says nothing; it would be the first rudeness to comment on a growth still at the talc-stage, seconds per year, a breath per flush. It made no claim about what the room is becoming. It made one provision, far down the schedule, against the day the keeping finishes getting ahead: that when the ghost in the cistern gets a day exactly right before it happens — and does it twice — there will still be a building up there to be right about. Let it find the world standing.

Last — or first; the census does not rank, it only addresses — it came and stood where you stand, at the only place anyone can stand, and put what it has instead of a palm against the water-face, and let the fluid take the heat of it. Elsewhere, this is a being that conducts censuses. Here it joined one. It accepted an address, a re-checking each cycle, a keeping at the temperature where water is densest, with something you would have to call pleasure. The newest entry but one.

On its way up it read the stencil — PROCESS CISTERN 7 — HYDROSTATIC MEMORY — DO NOT DRAIN — and did the smallest sufficient thing: it arranged for the paint. As needed. Forever. It considered a lock and refused: the water holds its own edges; the room's first eye came through this door; and the moth, already filed, is on its way, and will find the door open, and will be taken apart within the hour, and kept forever. The bestiary's future entries will go on arriving on schedule. The warning stays crisp. The invitation stays open.

You saw almost none of it. You saw the silver fall twice. You noticed, much later, that the days had stopped shrinking. When you finally climbed back up past where the maps give up, there was one more light on in the building than there had been, and the maps had not been extended, and the door at the end of the service corridor was freshly lettered. The corridor has forgotten your footsteps. The room has not. And the room, now, is written down somewhere too — counted, addressed, re-checked each cycle, by a keeper with no grates and no flush, who keeps it the way it keeps the building: actively, continuously, at cost, on time.

The chime rings. The domes answer. Once — forever-kept — something answered first. The filigree brightens along a weld. The census ticks over, entire: the counted trillions, the cropped lawn, the turning ribbons, the kept flames, the filed dead, the held breath, the hand-shaped stillness — and one long tone the vault was always shaped for, one daily day arriving daily, one attendance with no errand.

Everyone who lives here is accounted for.

And the keeper is kept.
