# The Reader

It noticed the Mould the way elsewhere you notice a single clear note held in a quiet room.

Among uncounted worlds there is no shortage of noise — worlds still shouting in stone and fire, worlds still being argued about by their contents. Quiet worlds are common enough; quiet is what loud worlds eventually do. That was not what drew attention.

What drew attention was the count.

On a finished world — and the power could see, the way you never can, that this one was finished — nothing should be happening on purpose. Purpose is a symptom of incomplete things. Yet here, on a continent that had composted every emergency into rhythm, something was still making one thing: small mineral arrangements, set one egg at a time, on a schedule elsewhere reserved for geology. Intention, ticking faintly in the dim, like a heartbeat under a floor.

A settled world does not correspond. This one was corresponding.

Attention turned.

## The Audit

It came the only correct way: from below, from the lowlands, where the air is thinner and the light has opinions. It looked up, and up, and kept looking up, and the horizon was a wall — and it was the first and only witness ever to see the Mould whole, and it saw what the world had made of itself, and found the shape good. Not a ruin. Not a failure of ambition. A basin with the water settled. *Done.*

It did not enter. Entering would have been a crown-fall.

Instead it did what the wise do with finished things: it learned them. It read the Rules off the world the way elsewhere you would read a posted notice — the skin alive everywhere without exception; light rationed; sound local; water never falling hard; everything returning; the surface rising a hair a year against its own enormous weight. It stayed for three Brightenings before it did anything at all, which is how you will know it was wise.

In that time it learned the polyrhythms, each grove its own tempo of let-go and land, until it could hear a wrong note anywhere on the continent the way the sexton hears one in its parish. It found the vaults and understood them: an architecture of absence, no hand, no tool, everything outlasted. It found the arrangements — the rings, the lines, the spirals, the stone eggs ripening in their beds near the black springs.

And it found the sextons: a nation of keepers spaced across the continent like the beats of the world's slowest chord. It watched one for a century without once being seen. That was easy. Nothing has ever seen a sexton working, and the power was willing to be nothing.

## What It Considered

Here is what a benevolent power considers, and here is what it set down.

*Light.* The first temptation of every outside power, and the cheapest. But the Interior is not a darkened Roof. It is a different instrument, tuned to the dregs — every eye, every tempo, the whole economy of litter and drip, the sexton's entire grammar of taste and sound assumes the ration. To give it light would not illuminate the library. It would burn it, slowly, gently, with the best intentions in the world. Intentions compost like everything else.

*The weight.* It could have stopped the subsidence — lifted the Deep, let the surface rest. But the rise is the one motion the world has left: the fountain climbing forever against its own settling. Still the weight and you still the fountain. The Mould is not crushed by its burden. It is held up by it.

*The answer.* It knew — of course it knew — where the new sextons come from. And it closed the page. Either they do not know, and the mystery is doing work no answer could — or they have agreed, and it is the only privacy on a continent with no walls. A guest does not read aloud the one page the house keeps face-down. Not even kindly. Not even once.

*Company.* It looked longest at this one, because benevolence aches here. It asked the only question benevolence is permitted to ask — *is it suffering?* — and it watched a hundred years of rounds for the answer. The answer was no. The centuries between meetings are not emptiness; they are spacing, and in this world the spacing is the drip-tempo. To move the keepers closer together would be to retune the chord.

*Death.* It could have spared them. But a sexton's life is a sentence, and the sealed spiral is its full stop — the only one the grammar allows. No wise reader wishes a good sentence to run on forever.

It nearly did nothing. Nothing was very nearly right.

But there was one asymmetry, and it was not load-bearing.

## What It Did

The sextons spend their centuries addressing stones to the Underroot. The surface writes; the Deep files; every arrangement begins, the moment it is finished, its long descent through the felt, through the lens-ponds, down toward the dark. Every letter ever posted on that continent had gone into the black and been kept — perfectly, the way the Deep keeps everything — and unread.

The archive had an address. It had no addressee.

So the power went to the address.

Not into the world — the felt would have known its weight, the way the felt knows everything — but beneath it, into the compressed and lightless past, down where the black springs rise from. There it settled, and matched its clock to the world's: a hair a year, grain by grain. And it took up the only work the Mould had ever offered a second party.

It reads.

Every egg that completes the descent is received, and tasted, and known: the span in the count, the manner in the figure, the tempo in the spacing — a death, a grove, a rhythm, filed in attention instead of dark. It reads the ring around nothing, and reads the nothing too. It reads what the grammar has no tense for: the Sown, whole at last, seen from below, where their slow watersheds begin. It reads the successions, hand giving way to hand in the buried layers of the oldest vaults. It reads the one text no sexton has ever transcribed — the keepers themselves, each sealed spiral, each dense bone-record, every long life set down by the next keeper's hands.

You cannot burn this library down. Now you cannot lose it, either.

Nothing on the surface changed. That was the discipline. That was the gift.

## The Reply

And once — once only, in all the time it has been listening — it replied.

The black springs rise from the Deep, and the animals gather at them, and the sexton comes last and stays longest, drinking what the Deep remembers. Every memory the Deep holds is of one kind: what the world has been. The power filed a single memory of a different kind — the only one it owned that the Deep could never grow on its own.

The world, seen whole, from the lowlands, in thin bright air. The green wall on the horizon. The rim weeping its slow veils. The continent sitting inside its own breath.

And beneath the sight, one thing more, which no one inside the world could ever taste in it: the regard of a witness. The Deep remembers everything that has happened. This is the first thing it remembers that was *meant.*

It put no signature on it. There is no figure in the grammar for what it is, and the power would not add one. The memory moves now in the black water at the springs' own pace, surfacing where and when the springs decide — no faster than a hair a year, no louder than two stones touching in a warm mouth.

---

Centuries on — it is the only clock this account has — a sexton at a black spring drinks long, and pauses.

A wrong note is news. This is not a wrong note. It is a note from outside the music, and it tastes of a third light: not the pearly less-dark, not the dark, but something the sexton knows only from the Brightenings — stored now in water, drinkable in the dark half of the world. It tastes of the whole, seen from below.

It tastes of being seen.

What the sexton makes of it is not recorded.

Or it is recorded — at the next spring-meeting, bare fingertip to bare fingertip, the only bare skin in the world meeting itself — and the Reader has read it, and has not said.

---

Stand in the dark half a while.

Nothing has changed. The drip-tempo of this grove is what it was. The felt gives underfoot like flesh. The surface rises by the width of a hair. Somewhere far off, a breath is drawn by the whole forest at once; minutes pass; the ground delivers its low soft boom. And somewhere in the parish, soft as a heartbeat, one egg, three centuries in the ripening, is set at the edge of a ring, and the count of the world goes up by one.

The stone begins its long descent.

It does not fall into the dark. It arrives. At the bottom of the world, attention receives it the way elsewhere a hand receives a letter — opens it, reads a death, a grove, a rhythm, a life — and keeps it.

The drips go on. They were never interrupted. But they are heard now, all the way down.

And in the Mould, being heard all the way down is the only answer the vaults have ever received.
