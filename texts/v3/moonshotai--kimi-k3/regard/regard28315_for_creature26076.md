**The Attention**

It found the room the way the room is always found: by the hum.

It was the patient kind of power. It did what the divers do, at its own scale: it hung in the middle of the room for a while. For the divers, a while is an hour. For it, a while was most of a century. Proportionally, the same dive. It entered no deeper than attention enters — the depth of light, the depth of water — and it read the floor, and counted the stems, and listened to the throat's tone until the bell's small note told it what the sky was doing. And by the end of the first decade it knew the place's whole biography, which is one line long: one event, then none. By the end of the second it had derived the whole of its duty from that line: keep the count at one.

It knew a works when it saw one. It was one. It, too, held a level. It, too, had an intake and an outflow — attention in, patience out — and had long ago settled into the speed of itself. The room had solved, alone and in the dark, the problem of running forever with no one to appreciate the trick. The power had solved the same problem in the light, with company, which is easier. It recognized the harder solution. It bowed in the way it has of bowing, which is to stay.

Benevolence toward everything else it had ever attended had meant doing something. Here it would mean the other thing. This is not the same as neglect. Neglect never gets as far as the list.

It could have given the room more light. A nudge to the throat's lip, a prism encouraged in the glaze — it knew how — and noon would run three hours, and the line on the wall would climb, and the meadow would break its budget and bloom: and the room would be a different room. The rationing is not poverty. It is the meadow's shape. It left the budget alone.

It could have warmed the water. One degree, a gesture small as breath. And the archive would begin to come apart on schedule — the bat to its elements ahead of its millennium, the leaves to their glaze ghosts early, the record unrefrigerated. The cold is not a hardship the room endures. It is the thermostat of the only library it keeps. It left the cold alone.

It could have stocked the dark — a snail, a shoal, anything with a mouth. It has stocked worlds; it knows the pleasure of a populated water. But the emptiness is not a vacancy. It is an achievement, maintained like the temperature, and every mouth would be an unmaking. It left the emptiness alone.

It could have made repairs. Set the fallen block back on its scar, scour the silt from the columns, polish the scallops. The floor is a record that stopped mid-sentence; repair is erasure in its Sunday clothes. It left the dent alone.

It could have studied the room further. It stopped itself at the necessary depth. Past a certain point, knowing a place and handling it are the same gesture. It learned the Stillworks to exactly the depth required to leave it alone, and then it stopped learning.

It could have told someone. That is the local custom — find the perfect thing, publish it, protect it with fame — and it has watched the whole ceremony elsewhere: the railings, the lights, the viewing platform, the loved thing dying on schedule. Obscurity is the load-bearing wall. It said nothing. It has opinions about gift shops.

It could have ended the dives. Forty-seven warm intrusions, each obscene in its loudness, each a freight of skin. It let them continue. One diver a year is weather the room already knows how to have; the census does not change. And the log is the room's one outsourced organ. You do not amputate an organ for beating off-site.

It could have given the room the one thing it lacks. A daughter works: split the aquifer two valleys over, nurse a second chamber, teach another hill the trick — no more first of its kind and last. It turned this gift over longer than all the others together, because it was the only one the room could even be imagined to want, if the room wanted, which it does not; wanting is a surface behavior. And a second works would draw on the same head. The gauge would learn a new number. Singularity is not loneliness. It left the room an only child.

And it could have raised the bat. It had the lever. The wingbeat was one bad wingbeat, the cost nothing it would notice, and something in it leaned. It let the filing stand. The room had already done for the bat the one thing the room does for anyone: it kept it. The power does not revise records. This was the last temptation, and the only one that cost it anything, and when it had passed it, it understood that it had finished the room's curriculum.

Then it set about its duties, which are as follows, and which look, all of them, like nothing.

It keeps the hill poor. The thin soil, the indifferent spruce, the land worth nothing to anyone: that is the room's outermost wall, the only kind no one besieges. It fertilizes nothing. It learned that from the meadow.

Its one construction project is a rumor, laid molecule by molecule over eighty years: nothing down there; the water's bad; the sheep won't; my grandfather didn't, and I don't. A superstition is the cheapest fence ever built and the only one that repairs itself.

And it keeps a notebook of its own, whose entries are the things that did not happen. The survey that misplaced the shaft by four hundred meters and a full contour line. The road that bent one valley east for reasons that read, in the minutes, as ordinary politics. The quarry that found better stone elsewhere. The village well that came up a little short of the aquifer and was pronounced sufficient. The graduate student whose grant went, on merit, to a different cave. None. None. None. The page fills with none, and it is proud of the page the way the works is proud of its gauges.

For the division of labor is this: the hill takes the chemistry, and the power takes the history. The room's filter is a year of stone, and stone cannot strain an epoch — the human weather arrives too fast, all frequencies at once. So the power posted itself upstream of the stone, a coarse screen ahead of the fine one. When the epoch passes — it will; the power takes the long view, which is the only view it has — the screen could come down. It won't. Taking it down would be an event.

It did the arithmetic on the bell, because it can do arithmetic the room cannot. The lung is, in fact, very slightly larger than it was; the tithe comes in at very slightly more than the water takes back; the two curves cross in about nine thousand years, at which point the bell reaches its spill point and the room exhales — a second event, the first since the flood, one afternoon long, scheduled since the first dive. Some of it will be hers. The power checked whether the room survives its own breath. It does: the level drops a finger's width, the gauge re-pins, the afternoon resumes. So it left the tithe alone. It is not an intervention to let the room's own arithmetic finish. And it certified, for no file but its own, one mammal observation: the note is lower. Her memory was not tuned wrong.

It visits, because it is benevolent, and it visits correctly, because it is wise. It uses the two doors the room keeps open. Some noons it comes down with the light and stands, defined, all the way to the floor. Some years it passes through as the current — a bathtub a minute of it, briefly, indistinguishable — and out the low slots with the rest. The room plates its visits into the glaze, molecule by molecule, unread, which is the correct filing for a guest. It is the first visitor to keep the third rule. It brings no freight of skin, no furnace, no breath; it pays its tithe in rain, upstream, years early; and it leaves with the water carrying nothing but regard, which was never the room's to begin with. Attention is the one massless import. The Stillworks has never once had to filter it.

And on certain noons, when the old diver climbs up through the thin spruce and sits at the lip with a hand flat on the warm stone, the appointment is kept on both sides. Something else is listening, and finds the hum the way she does, in the sternum, the way you hear a power station from two valleys off. She never knows. That is fine. Knowing would make it an event. It keeps her company the only way the place permits: by standing in the same spot, at the same hour, for the same reason.

The room outsourced its memory, once, to a mammal. The power does not take the contract back — take nothing — but it keeps the position filled. There will always be one waterproof notebook in one kitchen in one valley, one hand on the pen, replaced unhurriedly, on the schedule of years. Its entire human resources department, devoted to remembering the room at exactly the dose the room can absorb.

**The Log**

This year's page is in a fifth hand now, which cannot entirely account for its own last two lines, and lets them stand.

*Meadow: 411 stems, one owner. Film: employed on the noon shift. The dark ones: innumerable, unhurried. Snow: occasional. Bat: one, retired. Breath: accumulating, on schedule. Us: periodic. Events: none. Attention: constant, massless — reclassified as weather, which it resembles: arriving from very far off, touching nothing.*

*And then, because the form must be honored: None.*

Below, the Stillworks holds its one breath and runs its one afternoon. Above, the attention holds the world's breath off the hill and runs its one shift — not toward anything, but because running is what it is. Between what it could do and what it does stands the whole of wisdom, and the whole of love, which turned out to be the same measurement, taken twice.

At noon the column comes down all the way to the floor, and the room is lit like any ordinary room, and you could read by it, and some of the light is its.
