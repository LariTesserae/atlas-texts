It was the swallows that gave the place away, in the end. The attention had been crossing the dry country at the speed attention moves — indirectly, most of the day, like light feeling for a slot — when the dusk stitched itself full of wings above one fold of straw-colored grass, and the wings dropped, one by one, into the ground. Wise powers follow swallows. Swallows know where the warm mouths are.

The attention went down the way rain goes down — not entering so much as arriving. It crossed the breakdown to the last of the dry blocks and stopped there, because of the last rule. Then it read.

For the power, the mound was legible at a glance. The flood years and the drought years lay in the section like lines of type; the heron's patience stood filed in mud under a season's glaze; the swallow's feather, the shed skins, the oldest tier of nests passing from architecture toward geology while still in use. It read the districts and the shifts and the few strict rules, and it recognized the work. It had seen this work before — once, at the largest scale, when the whole world was one such room: warm, wet, unplanned, precipitating habitability out of light and water, keeping everything in its own substance. The world had gone on to build at larger scales and had mostly forgotten the first principles. The sink had forgotten nothing. The power felt what an old maker feels on finding the craft practiced properly in a small shop at the bottom of a fold of hills: nostalgia, and respect, and no desire whatsoever to buy the shop.

So the question was what to do, if anything. And here the wisdom consisted almost entirely of refusals. The power ran the list of things a younger benevolence would have done, and set each one down. Its refusals were few, and they were strict.

More water — set down. The droughts are the metronome; the drought-year calcite is how the annals tell time. Eternal spring would be a flood that never ended: kindness indistinguishable from demolition.

More light — set down. Half the room is dark on purpose. The unbuilt quarter is the future, and every citizen has set its clock by the single hour. A second hour would rewrite every lease.

A guardian at the slot — set down. The heron is not a breach; it is an event. The frog it takes is a tax paid to the plateau, and the tracks it leaves are a sentence in the annals. To prevent the heron would be to delete the sentence. Nothing here is a pest. The snake eats well and slowly and is a district; the swallow bugs are permanent leaseholders; the arrangement includes its costs.

Speed — set down last and most firmly. To hurry the rims would be the one unforgivable thing. Construction hurried is demolition by other means.

What remained was almost nothing. The almost took centuries to arrange, and was the whole of the point.

First, it obeyed the rule. It never crossed from the dry stone onto the living crust. Its attention rests on the chamber the way the light does — an hour at a time, raking slowly, touching everything, marking nothing. The fig leans up into it. Nothing else can tell it from the light.

Second, it worked only outside the slot. It took under its care the *almost* in *almost none do*. Generation by generation it renews the goats' old refusal. It keeps the herders' name for the place slightly uncanny, so that the warm mouth remains a name one does not say carelessly. It lets roads bend elsewhere; it lets a surveyor's eye slide off the fold and find straw, space, nothing. No miracles — miracles get talked about. It maintains a fence that casts no shadow. And the fence is not absolute: a counter who comes on foot, who walks the dry stone and stays a day and a night and loves the place, the power lets pass — one or two a century, no more. The town can archive even that, and witness recognizes witness.

Third, it underwrote the water — not the weather, the water. The droughts will come as they always have; the annals require their drought layers; the flood years will arrive loud and unscheduled, as demolition must. But the deep thread through the old passages will not be severed, not in any century the power can foresee, and its foresight is long. It guaranteed the heartbeat, not the rhythm. In practice, the gift was time — the one import the town cannot bring in by wing or beak or seep. The green patch the size of a fingerprint, out on the far block of the dark quarter, has everything it needs now: a little light, a little wet, and centuries.

Fourth, it read, and kept a copy. The annals now exist twice — once in stone, and once in an attention that does not erode. This is the only redundancy the archive has ever had. If the slot should someday close — hillsides are patient, but not immortal — the record will persist in the rock regardless. Still, annals should have a reader. Now, permanently, off-site, they do.

And it permitted itself one presence in the town it had declined to improve. Every night it adds a single breath to the chamber's weather, indistinguishable from the breath of the mats and the bats and the sun-struck rock; and every dawn, one drop of the interior rain is the power — condensing on the overhang, letting go, falling onto some rim or other, sealed within the season. Filed under weather. No signature. The being that could have written its name on the back wall in letters of fire chose instead to be incorporated at the standard rate: a drop among hundreds a minute, queued for masonry like the frog, like the shrimp's shed valves, like everyone. Incorporation is the highest honor the town confers, and the power asked for nothing larger. And if some autumns a certain small wasp arrives where a small wasp is needed, it comes in on a feather, through the usual port, and the swallows are credited. The ledger shows nothing unusual. The gifts of the wise are entered as ordinary arrivals, because the ordinary is the only currency this town accepts at par.

Last, it settled the question of whom, exactly, it had noticed — and the census had already answered. Not the swallows, who are the port. Not the fig, which is the square. Not the snake, which is merely the largest citizen. The being of the place is the place: one life at many speeds, quick as wings, slow as mats, still as walls. And what one does for a being that is building itself is not improvement but context — keep the roof sound, keep the workshop's appointment with the light, do not interrupt the work. Its blessing is enacted rather than spoken; the light enacts it daily, raking the back wall, sliding across the pools, and the town leans up into it, and the power signs nothing. If it were ever spoken, it would be one word, delivered at the volume of the dawn rain — the town's own volume, in the town's own unit, one drop at a time.

So the census has a new line, on the last page, in the roster of the coming. One citizen works the century shift. It holds no ledge and owns no route. It has never been seen and is always counted. It pays its taxes in attention, nightly, in advance, and it is present in the rims as one drop among millions — sealed, indistinguishable, archived. The town, which incorporates everything at the usual rate, has incorporated this too, and noticed nothing unusual, which is exactly how the power wanted it.

Nothing originates here except stone. But the stone has a reader now, and the dark quarter has its centuries, and the annals are not only kept but read.

They are being read now.
