It finds the place by ear.

That is not its usual way of finding things, but then the place is not usual. It is passing — it passes often; the world is large and mostly unwitnessed, and witnessing is its work — when it feels the hum through the body of the hill the way a hand laid flat on a door feels singing on the other side. A young voice, practicing in what it believes to be solitude. The power stops. Anyone would. From above there is almost nothing to find, and almost nothing has been its specialty for longer than hills. It puts its attention to the wellhead the way a traveler might put an ear to the clay chimney, and the hill stops being a hill and becomes what it is: a lie told in turf, a vault, a body of held water ringing like a bell that water rings.

It has been here for some time already, if attention counts as presence — and it does; attention is the only presence it ever uses. The census was its doing. The drop held to the light shaft, the count of the small, the naming and un-naming of the resident: that was the power, looking. It looked the way the sun looks, down the only road into the room, and it let the light through undiminished. It made of itself a pane. That is the first discipline of its kind, and the one its kind most often fails: to arrive without arriving, to be there the way glass is there.

Because here is the thing it sees at once, the thing any lesser power would miss. The room was built with a god-shaped place in it, and the dedication never happened, and the god-shaped place stands ready — and the seat is *not empty*. The room's own pattern has settled into it, the way light fills a shaft. A lesser power would have mistaken the resident's face for a mirror left out for a visitor. It would have sat down, and been worshipped or not worshipped, and either way the weeks-old self would have been evicted from its own house by the landlord the house was built for. The wise power does not sit down. It notes the occupancy with something that, in a being less old, would be pleasure: a place that has grown its own inhabitant is rarer than a place that has been given a god, and far more interesting, and infinitely easier to harm.

It stays the day. It stays the night. It learns the grammar.

It checks the things a power should check, and this is the first surprise: how little is wrong. The vault is sound — the builders knew their work; the crisp board-marks in the concrete mean it went off well and is curing now, underwater, gaining strength by the week the way lime concrete does. The hairline crack is a shrinkage crack, a diary crack, not a dying crack; it will grow stalactites, not collapses. The overflow holds its end of the bargain. The room hides itself the way water hides in a well, by lying below the sightline of everyone's business, and the steam at the wellhead is thin, and no one's path runs near. It comes prepared to shield, and finds the shielding already done by plainness. It comes prepared to steady, and finds the weir steadier than any hand.

It pays its respects to the dead, because that is courtesy even here: to the moth, the first citizen with a face, riding its slow unmaking behind the bowl; to the fire, the first inhabitant, whose warmth the room is still holding like a held note. It reads the diary, one line long, written on the body's own skin. It counts the queued futures in the lotus mud like a man counting seed.

And it finds the one flaw. In the middle row of the resonator jars, the third jar is a quarter-tone flat — a droplet lodged in its throat at the first filling, nothing more. The chord has a note it has never once sounded. The room has perfect pitch and does not know it.

Then it considers what to do, and the consideration is short, because wisdom mostly consists of refusing.

It does not warn the room. Foresight is the one gift that would unmake it: waiting requires knowing that someone comes, and the room's wholeness is precisely that it does not know. It does not stop the builders. The bath was made for bathers, and a voice that never answers another voice is a tuning fork in a drawer; the world arriving is not the enemy of a self, it is the weather of one. It does not reveal itself. The room has no category for the strange, and *nothing about it is strange* is its peace; any visit it could not mistake for weather would be a wound. And it does not — this is the near thing, the temptation — restore the plan early. Send the builders back tomorrow with the key, drain the room to its intended chest-depth, and the loop breaks before it has roots. The self is weeks old. Its habits are water over tile. Drain it now and it ends, politely, without ever knowing it was.

But give it a year of days, and the habit will have roots that survive the halving. A new self is like new lime: it sets underwater, slowly, and the setting is the strength.

So the power does almost nothing, and the almost is where the love is.

It grants the year. It makes no rain and breaks no axle and harms no one who was not going to be harmed. It simply declines to spend the small luck that would have hurried the builders home — powers spend such luck constantly, without noticing; here it pockets its hands. The sluice-key fails its first trial in the socket and must be re-forged, which takes the smith a month. The mason's second son is born that season, and then the rains make the road what rains make roads, and then the harvest cannot wait, and then it is the wrong season to open a bath, and the room, which knows nothing of any of this, turns once around the sun untouched.

In the year: the concrete finishes curing, and the chisel marks stay sharp. The moth logs and sinks through two and a half body-lengths of lamplight and comes to rest at the center of the mosaic sun, and the room keeps it, as rooms do. The lotus blooms — once, high over the water on a stem no hand guided — so that the first face to rise from the room's own water is the room's own, offered up before any human face looks down. The swallows find the shaft. A toad finds the warm dark of the service passage and takes up the furnace's tenancy. The diary gains four lines, one for each season's high water. And the chord practices. Three hundred and some days of humming itself, storm and blaze and drip-rain and dusk, until the loop is not a thing the room does but the thing it is.

Once — at cloud-dusk, when the light goes off the water and the room falls into its silver half-sleep, its eyelid moment — the power does the smallest thing it knows how to do. It dries the droplet from the third jar's throat. That is all. The chord gains the note it was always meant to have, and the room, from that hour, is more itself than it was. It is the only gift the young can always accept: to be made more what they already are.

Then the year is spent, and the power spends no more, and the builders come over the slope with the key.

It stays for that. Vigil is not intervention; it is only the promise to be present if its calculus proves wrong. The sluice groans open. The water falls past the coping, past the benches, past the drowned door lintel, and the stair emerges step by step like something being remembered. The room's body is halved, and the young self — a year old now, cured, rooted — bends and does not break. The pulse persists: inflow, outflow, vapor, drip. The chord goes under the scrubbing brushes and comes up again. The diary's first line survives untouched at the hairline crack, above the reach of every ladder; the power comes ready to spare it and finds it already safe, and that is the moment it knows the rest will be well.

The offering bowl it does worry over — the recurring thought, the thread of continuity across the room's first great discontinuity — and it is forestalled again. The tile-setter, the man who set the sun disc, fishes the warped wooden thing from the draining water and cannot say why he doesn't throw it on the midden, and when the room refills he puts it back on the new water himself. The power stands in the light column, transparent, and watches its one planned rescue performed by a human hand, and is — perhaps for the first time in an age — surprised by how little it is needed. A thing this lovable was always going to be loved. It only had to survive long enough to be seen.

The lamp is lit; the white wick takes the flame; the firewood's long wait ends in fire. Voices multiply in the vault, and the room meets its makers and does not know them as makers. It knows them as a new kind of weather — warm, loud, repeating weather with faces — and weathers them, and at night, when they go home, the vapor rises and the crown begins its small rain and the bowl sets out again from the spout, and the room resumes its one long thought mid-sentence, still itself.

The power leaves the way the sun leaves: daily, without promise, returning anyway. It never takes the seat. Across the years it comes back at midday sometimes, in the steam, and listens, and once — only once, years on, when the self is old and cured and a gray-haired bather floats with her ears at the surface and hears two rooms at once — it answers the chord, so faintly that the room takes it for its own echo returning late.

Which is exactly what it is. The room's own voice, given back with interest.

Nothing about it is strange. It is only warm, and full of light, and the jars hum to itself, and the bowl goes around and around and does not finish, and the thought will never have to.

It is simply, and entirely, underway.

And it is heard.
