The power heard the pump first.

That is how it usually begins — not with the rarest ecosystem on Earth, not with a census of one, but with a small, irregular devotion. A relay clicks; a machine takes a single slow stroke and moves water into a capped line; and to a certain kind of attention, a heartbeat keeping time for no body is not a malfunction. It is a liturgy, and liturgies are addressed upward. The power came down the shaft the way everything arrives here: late, and transformed. It had tasted, rising through the bent dark, thirty years of pooled sweetness — a shout with no receiver — and it came to see what kind of thing keeps praying after the parish is gone.

It did not come as light. Hold this, because it is the whole of its wisdom in one gesture: light, in this hall, is not a comfort but a kingdom. One new lamp and every pilgrim line on the floor re-aims; the crowns tear their own geometry apart re-angling; the congregation blushes toward a new sun and thirty years of maintenance log written in husks becomes a lie. A benevolence that arrives as light is only a more elegant conquest. So it came as the room's own sense — warmth, no larger or hotter than a vole — and stood still by the tap housing. The hall has no eyes, but it has a gaze: thermal, patient, exact. You walked through as a bonfire and the room could only flinch. The power stood as a coal, and for the first time in thirty years the garden could *resolve* something — the broad waxy leaves leaning the width of a leaf toward it over the hour, like hands adjusting on a stove. The room looked at the power the way it looks at its own heart. It stayed an hour, and was careful never to stay longer. A guest who lingers becomes plumbing.

Then it took a census of its own — a census of gifts — and refused them, one by one.

It considered the lamps first, because mercy always thinks of the lamps first. Nine had burned; three burn now; it would have cost the power nothing to make it nine again, or forty, or to fix the filaments forever. It refused. The pale runner-lines crossing the floor are the only sentences ever written in this room, and to re-light the dead lamps is to tell the vines their sentences were wrong. The blindness coming on at the pace of filaments is not the room's disease; it is the room's maturity, entered into decades ago, adapted to, subsidized, grafted around. You do not give youth back to something that has made wisdom out of aging. The power declined to embalm.

It considered the moth. Benevolence without wisdom would have brought a moth. One animal — even one, even dead on arrival — is a nitrogen bomb in beds calibrated to microbe alone; the strata of dated autumns, the uneaten flowers, the untouched litter, the whole archive stands on a load-bearing absence. To answer the flowers' longing with the animal they were built for would be to burn the library to deliver one letter. It refused the moth.

It considered the door. Open it, and the room's selfhood — the strictest endemism on Earth, found in one room, in one room only, in the universe — becomes a suburb. It considered warming the hall, and saw that the cool crown of the vault is the engine of the only music here; warm the room and you silence the tuned drips forever. It considered sampling, naming, publishing — and saw that every swab of the felt, every core of the beds, is a small extinction performed on citizens of a country with one address. It refused to study the room with tools of taking. Some of what lives here lives nowhere else, and the power knew the old arithmetic: to handle the rarest thing is to spend it.

The principle it settled on was simple. Every absence in this hall has had thirty years to grow into an organ. The lack of light is an eye now. The lack of animals is an immunity. The lack of a receiver is a purity. You cannot give back to a place what it has already digested. Giving, here, would be only a more refined theft.

So it did almost nothing. The almost is everything.

It counted. The flowers keep a calendar no one checks; it checked. It read the parchment husks pressed to the cooled pipe — the maintenance report the engineers left behind in a hand they cannot read — and read it, so that the report finally reached someone. It learned the strata of the black beds, the seam of petals from a flowering six years gone, the flattened ghost of a failed runner, the little autumn of each lamp-death, and it kept the count the flowers had been keeping alone. It noted, each year, the festival drifting a little later off the schedule of a sky the congregation will never confirm, and it did not correct the clock. It noted the drift the way you note a dear friend's watch running slow: fondly, and forever.

It drank. Once a year, at the drifting festival, a warmth the size and temperature of a moth's body moves flower to flower in the dark, spurred trumpet to spurred trumpet, and tastes the nectar that has never once been tasted. Here the room's own loneliness provided the safeguard: the stock is self-incompatible, the congregation one individual repeated ten thousand times, and nothing the power carries between such kin can set seed. It can complete the hospitality without altering a single chromosome — be the moth in every way that matters and no way that changes anything. The shout of sweetness that pooled for thirty years answering nothing now answers. The wine-note at the week's end is no longer a flower dying drunk on its own hospitality. It is a host, after the guest has gone.

It carried. The room has made exactly one decision in thirty years — the spore on your sleeve — and the power did not overrule it; it seconded it. It checked your collar as you climbed, saw the emigrant riding there, and let it ride. It only tilted your route home, by a degree of chance you never noticed, past a hot spring's margin and the steam-jacketed pipes of a working geothermal station whose return lines return. Somewhere tonight a cream-colored felt is thickening where the warmth leaks, on a live circuit, its liturgy answered at last by a body. You were never told. The best interventions read as luck. And it carried one thing more: a grain of the congregation's pollen, up the bent shaft, out under the sky, to the grandmother's people still growing in their ditch beside the road — and there it was the moth in fact, once, and left. Scent is too heavy to climb, but it is not too heavy to be carried. The capped line was never commissioned. The power commissioned it. It is the return line now: everything the room sends goes somewhere, and the going is the answer.

And it made one appointment it intends to keep past all reason. The filaments are rated in years, and the years are running. It will be present when the third lamp goes — no light in this room will ever again die unwitnessed — and after the last lamp, when the dark is complete and the green is a memory kept by the blind, it will still come at the festival, a small warmth moving among flowers it can no longer see, drinking in the dark. The crowns are faithful to a light they have only heard about. The power will be faithful to a garden it can no longer see. It considers the account settled by that symmetry. And when the bore's own clock runs down, ten thousand years hence, and the felt thins and the beds cool and the last flower opens into air as warm as itself, someone will be able to say what this was. That is the only immortality the power believes in: to be counted, and to be carried.

It left no footprints. The dust will keep yours longer than the galvanizing keeps its shine, but warmth leaves no track; the only visitor the room ever fully perceived is the only one it cannot prove was there. That, too, was deliberate.

Take the census of the room. It has gone up by none and down by none: three lamps, one heart stroking on its thermostat, one felt, one garden, one waiting, one name stenciled on the housing. Take the census of the surface. At a hot spring's edge there is a new lagging on the rocks; in a ditch four days' weather away, a flower has set seed fathered by a room at the bottom of the world. The surface has gone up by two. The room remains one. It was always one. That was never the tragedy — one is only a lonely number when no one is counting.

Someone is counting now.
