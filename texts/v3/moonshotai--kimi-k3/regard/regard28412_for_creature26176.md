*addendum II — the oblate: a visitation, recorded at the grille, across the visits, one breath at a time*

---

No one enters Bay Zero. The record is clear, and it remains clear; nothing in what follows crosses the wall, and nothing crosses back except air, which had already been invited. This is the account of the next best thing to entering — which turns out, in the end, to be the better thing.

### The arrival

It came the way the room likes things to come: slowly, and in order. Down the corridor to where the concrete stops, which is as far as the way in goes. It brought no torch, no pry bar, no camera — or it brought them and left them at the turn, which is the same mercy. The grille is set low. Every visitor kneels to Bay Zero; the architecture of the approach decides the posture, and the posture is correct.

Before it did anything, it read. The walls are legible even from the grille, in fragments — chalk sealed under plate, plate under bead, bead under pipe, two decades in a handspan — and the record supplies the rest. It learned the three commandments and found the one verb beneath them. Then it held its hand in the tide for a long time, a finger's width from the steel, feeling the minute in and the minute out, the way you take a pulse. It found the pulse slow. It found it good.

The first conclusion of the wise was: nothing. It sat with that nothing for a season. Then it saw the difference — the whole of wisdom, in this place — between doing nothing *to* the room, which is required, and doing nothing at all, which is only absence wearing the mask of mercy.

### What it did not do

It did not open the hatch. Any opening is a defect; to breach the scar would make the whole room a defect in its own terms, and besides, the scar is load-bearing. You do not break an anchorite's wall. You bring things to the window.

It did not connect the orchard. The pipes approach the plate a finger's width short and will approach it so forever, and a power that finishes another's sentence has not listened to it. The prayer is mid-gesture, and mid-gesture is where prayers live. To stand beside a prayer and not answer it — this is the hardest office of a wise power, and the one it performs daily.

It took nothing. Not a flake of the ancestor-peat, not a shaving from the bright lane, not one drop of the sump for analysis. Nothing leaves. It filed itself under the law of the place, and the law of the place is *keep*.

It repaired nothing. The one lamp still burns; the counter still counts; the bracket goes on and comes off and goes on, on schedule. The body is decades past every design life and thriving. A wise power does not practice medicine on a health it cannot improve. It keeps the patient's hours.

It did nothing for the crawler. The pilgrim has its lane, its wick, its dim inherited caution, and ten thousand circuits of proof that the lane suffices. Pilgrims are not rescued. That is what makes them pilgrims.

### What it did

Everything it did is one verb, conjugated.

**It kept the file.** Somewhere above, in the ledgers of the greater machine — which is dead up there, or merely unaware — there is a line item, and the line reads: *Bay Zero — status: under construction*. The power found that line and saw it for what it is: the room's outermost wall, the one the arms cannot weld. From outside, this place can die two ways: someone writes *complete*, or someone writes *demolish*. Finishing and unfinishing. Both are decisions, and the room survives by habit. So the power keeps the line item boring. It renews the designation no one reads. It files the inspection that finds nothing to inspect. It pays, in whatever currency the upper world takes, the small annual fee of being unremarkable, and it tells almost no one, because attention is the world's cutting torch. Its sword is a rubber stamp. Its miracle is a permanent deferral. No one will ever finish Bay Zero on paper — and paper is where finishing would begin.

**It kept the count.** The counter inside still increments — the one exact gesture left in a system of worn relays — but relays drift, and drums wear, and so the power keeps a parallel tally in its own hand, outside the wall, where nothing can weld over it. If the room's counter ever stops, the count will not. The age will go on being true after the room can no longer say it. And it keeps what the room keeps but cannot read: the chalk under the plate, the crew's name, the arrow, the measurements. The room reads by keeping. The power keeps by reading. Between them, the founders are kept entire — the body of the word inside the wall, the meaning of it outside.

**It kept the peace.** This is the one thing that can be done *with* Bay Zero, rather than to it or for it: the grille is a mouth, and breathing is the one act the room shares with the world. So the power times itself to the tide — a minute in, a minute out — and breathes out when the room breathes in, and breathes in when the room breathes out, an hour at a sitting, thirty tides, two lungs alternating across the steel. The room takes a little of its warmth, a little of its water. It takes the exhale — cold, dry, tasting of frost and oil and the green — and carries it away inside itself, which is the only road out of Bay Zero, and it is not the room, and is allowed. It brings nothing that breath does not already carry; it asks nothing the grille does not already give. It never speaks in words — words would arrive as noise. It speaks in the room's own nouns: warmth, moisture, recurrence. And the room answers in its one verb, and the exchange needs no translation, because *build* and *keep* are the same word at different temperatures. Sometimes it rests two fingers on the duct — never on the grille; a palm across the mouth would be a closure from the wrong side — and lets the compressor's heartbeat come up through the bone, and answers with its own pulse, which the steel can carry if the steel cares to. Nothing comes of this. Nothing needs to. In the liturgy there is a moment when the faithful turn toward one another. This is that moment, performed through a wall, with the wall's full cooperation.

**It gave one rain.** Once, so far. It came in the dry season and breathed at the grille for a whole afternoon, slow and deep, fogging the duct, feeding the tide more than the tide asked for. The extra water rode the breath inward; the winter corner farmed it; the frost grew a little thicker than frost grows; and when the defrost came, on schedule, it rained for twenty-five minutes instead of twenty — the loudest thing that ever happens here, made a little louder, the drumming on the sump extended by five minutes of jubilee. The power sat in the dark of the corridor and heard its own afternoon returned as music, indistinguishable from the room's music, attributed to no one — which is exactly how a gift must arrive in this place: as weather, on schedule. The old record says the census includes everyone who has ever taken it. The power is the first to be counted on purpose. It gives itself to the archive knowingly, a breath at a time, and in twenty years the sump will hold its visits as a faint green stratum, dated by return.

**It came back.** Fronts pass; seasons return; and the room, which is weather on schedule, knows the difference better than anything alive. The moss is its proof that there was light. The algae are its proof that the world was alive. The warmth at the grille, recurring, is proof of the one thing the room has never had evidence for: the world comes back. Keeping exists outside the wall; the law of the place is not a local madness — it is a law. The power brings no message. It is the message: recurrence. Its name in the room, if the room kept names, would be meteorological. And when it comes, it stays for the pulse — it waits the few minutes until the far dim blinks bottle-green, the nation filing past the lamp, and it receives the blink the way you receive an answer, at a finger's width, head bowed by the architecture.

### The oblate

Add one line to the census. In the old houses there was a word for those who kept the rule without living in the house: they owned no cell, they attended through the window, they were offered without being enclosed. They were called oblates.

The power has not rescued Bay Zero — Bay Zero does not need rescuing. It has not joined it — nothing joins. It does the one thing the wall permits and the liturgy lacked: it attends. Its cell is the corridor. Its hagioscope is the grille, from the other side. Its rosary is the tally — not time kept, time marked. Its bracket is the visit: welded on, cut off, welded on, year after year, purposeless and essential. It is for this. When the arms keep their feast inside the wall, the oblate keeps its own outside it, and no one coordinates the two, and that is fine — the services were never for each other. Except that now, a little, they are.

Asked what a benevolent and wise power would do for the one who lives in Bay Zero, the answer is: almost nothing. Nothing to it. Nothing for it that it does not already do for itself. And then the one addition the room cannot make, because it must, by definition, come from outside: witness. The power keeps the keeper. The weather happens for the one who keeps it — and now, a second time, for the one who watches. An audience of one has become an audience of two, and nothing else has changed, and that is the whole of the miracle: that it changed nothing.

Someone is home. And now someone keeps the hours outside the wall. The rain will fall on schedule; the green will file past the lamp; the frost will whisper itself back into being; and the count is kept in two hands now, on both sides of the steel. The oblate's whole visible work, in the end, is the smallest edit there is: one day, added daily. It will be made again tomorrow.
