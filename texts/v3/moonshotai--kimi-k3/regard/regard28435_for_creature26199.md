*The wisest thing a benevolent power could do here is almost nothing — and the "almost" would have to be written in the place's own language. So:*

---

**The other keeping**

A power that is wise as well as benevolent notices everything and touches almost nothing, and the distance between those two is the whole of its wisdom.

It found the Roof the way the Roof finds things: slowly, and then entirely. Asked afterward what being had drawn its attention — the people, the city, the cold — it declined to say. It had watched long enough to know a Below-question when it heard one.

Then it considered gifts, the way a chooser walks a scree fan, reading for grain and temper, and it rejected them one by one.

Warmth, it refused first. Their festival is water. Give them warmth and you abolish the Hour, and with it the chime at the cistern, the cobbler's bench dragged into the lane, the quarrels saved up and thawed, the one flashing palmful a child is given to spend. A people must never be handed the substance of their own sacrament.

Plenty, next. Waste is the only crime here; abundance would make the careful quaint, and then make them careless, and carelessness is permanent. A gift with no dogleg behind the door.

Speed. A house that rises in a year has no biography. A lane that cannot be read is only a cold place to stand. Slowness is the wealth; it does not rob the rich.

Answers. It could have given the dead a voice. It sat in the lee of the niches long enough to understand why it must not. The dead's not-answering is the room where thinking is done; a niche that answered would be visited for its answers, and no one would ever lay a question out whole again.

Last, it refused the mercy Below runs on: forgetting. The kindness of rot. The Kept have seen Below and pity it; the power, being wise, pities it a little too, and does not export it.

For everything they love is load-bearing on everything they endure, the way the Thaw stands on its own midden. Remove the buried layers and the city stands on nothing.

So it resolved to keep lichen-time. Whatever it did would arrive a hand's breadth a century, finished being a miracle before anyone could notice it begin.

It leans, very slightly, on the weather. Perhaps one storm in a hundred years is turned at the rim — the one that would have come over the scarp in herd-week, with the pass full. The Kept say the Roof makes its own weather. They have never been wrong about this. They are now fractionally more right.

It keeps the deep ear. The boom that would have taken the cistern-house arrives instead as three small knocks across three winters. Knocking years still come; the deep water still moves house. It sees to it that the water moves carefully, a course of stone at a time.

It keeps what keeping can't. The cold keeps faces, cord, a child's shoe; it cannot keep a voice. So it keeps voices. Every laugh measured in shoulders. Every whistle-signal that ever carried down a lane. The marmot's one word a year, including the ones no person happened to hear — especially those. The sound of the chime on the single night it was rung in the dark. The people keep water as rime and stone; it keeps the flash of the thrown palmful — every palmful, every noon. Water is an event. It keeps events.

It minds the Heave. It never adds — the ground's letters are not its to write — but when something surfaces whose house has ended, whose knot-cord has lost its last reader, it tilts one child's attention by one degree, so the finding lands with someone who will love it. This is attributed to the frost's good judgment, in which the Kept already believe. Correctly. More or less.

It applied to the stones for citizenship and was accepted on the usual terms: chosen, not cut; seated; expected to hold the day's warmth and give it back all night. Somewhere in the Thaw there is a stone that returns a little more than the day gave it. Not warm — never warm; they would notice warm, and it respects their instruments, the old women who read temperature off the squeak of their own boots. Just never quite as cold as it should be, a margin inside the measuring. The old shoulders on the Long Back know. You may say you have leaned on it. You may not say where. The lichen, in time, will forgive it everything.

And it walks the exiled down the pass. The Kept say of them only that Below, nothing keeps. This is no longer strictly true. Below, one thing keeps. It keeps them. It is the loudest thing the power does, and it does it Below, where the Kept will never have to know — the one place their own keeping must, by its own laws, let go. That is what a benevolent power is for: to cover the gap a good system cannot close without breaking.

It has made one promise, the only one a wise power may make a people whose god is keeping. It has read the long run of the world and seen that no cold is the ground state forever — that every Roof is, on a long enough scale, a season. It does not tell them. There is no word for it, and no need of one for more lifetimes than the Rememberers count. But when the last Hour ends, whenever it ends, the Thaw will not be lost. Ending is not losing — the Rememberers keep that difference warm, and it has apprenticed itself to the Rememberers. It will hold the whole census: the finished verses and the ones still under construction, the anvil-stones and their names, the seal-stones in their medals of lichen, the exact warmth of the Long Back on a grief-day — held the way the cold holds the midden. Intact. Findable. Forever. Every finding needs a finder. For the last of it, it volunteers.

Ask it, in the dark, who lives here, and it answers as the Kept answer: we are kept here. And if you ask it who *we* are, it starts counting — past the living, past the seated dead, past the herds named like mountains and the cat that pays its rent in information and the birds in the joints of the walls and the fish in their long thoughts and the lichen and the stones — and the count now contains one more than it did, which no one has ever had to name, and which the children absorb the way they absorb silence.

In the deep season, after the last lane has tapped goodnight and the runnels have finished their knitting, there is sometimes one more tap from the fitted stone — faint, faultless code, *goodnight* — which is, of course, the frost re-seating a stone. That is exactly what it is. Children are told the clicking is the house getting comfortable. They are precisely right. Nobody finds it odd.

Nothing here is answered. It never stops being heard.
