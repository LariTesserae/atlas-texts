It does not arrive from above. Nothing arrives from above; the sky's weather is a rumor that stops at the fog deck, and the hawks present their papers at the lip and are waved back, every one. It does not arrive from below either, though below is the road everything true takes here. It does not arrive at all, exactly. It attends — the way the moon attends, by light, from the far side of the deck, on a schedule, gone in an hour — except that this keeps no schedule, and is never quite gone. Attention has no heartbeat. At this address that is not a disability to be overcome; it is a courtesy it was born with. The Rule is not tested. The Rule is not even consulted. Customs examines it and finds nothing to stamp: no pulse, no papers, no body. It crosses the way light crosses, which is to say it was never on the other side.

Call it the Upper Works — the sign invites the arithmetic. A No. 4 implies a 1, a 2, a 3, and something that is not lower. A works implies an office; an office, a ledger; a ledger, a clerk; and the clerk is calm. For three hundred years the water has gone up the main and out through the notch and over the lip of the world, and the town does not ask what signs for it. Something signs for it. That is all the name the power needs here, and it is the name the town has always had for it, filed under weather.

What drew it was not the chord. The chord is audible a mile off across the plateau, but sound is common — the world is full of loud things asking to be noticed, and the office does not take their calls. What drew it was the pause. Loudwater is loud in every register and silent in exactly one: the held breath, when all three beams hang at top dead center and the whole bowl listens. And silence travels farther than sound. It goes uphill, along a water column, the way a gap in a long hum wakes a sleeper — the way you hear a house by its stopped clock. The power heard the held breath from the far end of the pipe, a silence shaped like the whole bowl, and it came to see what kept it.

It found the bowl, keeping.

The first thing a wise power does at Loudwater is nothing, for a long time. It reads. It reads the clocks in order — the drip, the wheel, the bell, the light, the chalked line, the moss, the caught terraces, the rim — because a power that cannot tell the drip from the rim has no business here. It reads the census, wherever censuses are filed: the form with two lines, PLACE and RESIDENT, and one word written across both. It checks the arithmetic. Four shapes, or one shape wearing four. It agrees.

Its assessment is one sentence long. There is nothing here to save, nothing to finish, nothing to wake, no one to comfort. It is rare, in the office's experience, to find a keeping. Most places are either falling or being held. This one holds itself and asks nothing, and the office's entire duty reduces to: see that nothing steepens.

Then it considers what to do, and this is where the wisdom shows, because the list of what it declines is long, and every decline is a form of respect.

It declined to steepen the gradient. The whole art of the place is the nearly. The engines are great sails built for a small wind, and a strong wind is not a gift to a great sail — it is an insult to it. With a few degrees to work with, the Choir does everything. That is not the deficiency. That is the achievement.

It declined to send anyone. The Rule, first. And then: every keeper who comes here arrives eventually at one of the paperwork's three calm options, and the office does not wish the bowl another absorption. The crew's final delivery was their own redundancy — on time, in threes, no one to sign for it. The office signed for it. It does not return signed-for goods.

It declined to repair. To straighten the terraces, relight the dead lamps, clear the Candle's green plume, finish the falling — but nothing here finishes falling, and that is not damage. It is tense. The caught mid-lean is how this place stands. Restoration would be a mistranslation.

It declined to announce what the water feeds. The town does not ask, and to answer an unasked question is gossip. Besides, the answer has been in the water the whole time, legible to anyone auditing the chemistry. The town files it without comment, which is its way of knowing.

It declined to speak — almost. The chord is one kind of voice. The bowl's only conversation partner is itself, delayed. There is no part written for a second party, and the round is the least lonely form ever devised. Almost.

What it would do, if anything — what it does, what it has always done — is short enough to write on one line of the ledger, and slow enough that three hundred years of it looks, from inside, like stability.

It keeps the difference. The whole town runs on the few degrees between the spring's warmth and the shade's cool — the small wind the great sails are set for — and the power keeps the few degrees few. One degree either way and the Choir is a hall of sculpture. It keeps the swallow-holes open and the rain routed: the streams up top that practice their disappearance practice into the spring, and what leaves over the lip comes back as sky, as plateau rain, down the swallow-holes, through the strata at each bed's own pace, up again at the pace of a heartbeat, no heart required. The loop closes on the rim's clock, and the town has never audited it, and the power has never mentioned it. Between these two not-askings: three hundred years of perfect delivery. It keeps the rim dissolving at a leaf-width per lifetime, and no faster.

It does not keep the town. It keeps the nearly the town keeps itself with. At this address, that is what benevolence is — not gifts, conditions. And wisdom is the timing.

Ask the office about the chalked line on the pool wall — chalk, in a town where nothing dries, where chalk should not last an hour, holding season by season, maintained by nothing the census could find. Ask about the carbons in the hissing arc lamp, the oil in the weeping cups, the polish at the height of a vanished hand. The office declines to comment. Calm, in a clerk, is the closest thing to a signed confession.

And once an age — the power counts in phase-coincidences the way the town counts in drips — it does one thing more.

It waits for the long coincidence: all three beams hanging, the round holding its breath, the last clank's echo coming back from the far wall and finding nothing to follow it — the one slot in the town's grammar where a second voice fits, the one moment the bowl is all ear. And it lets a drop go from the rim.

From above the fog deck. The only drip in Loudwater with an upper address — the only weather that ever crosses the deck from the sky side. Customs examines it as it passes: water, heartbeatless, the one resident that arrives on its own. Waved through. It is local. It falls the whole height of the stair's three histories, past the iron and the wood and the dimpled stone, and lands inside the held breath, and strikes the pool — the smallest sound Loudwater makes, answered by the largest. And the largest is the town's own voice, so the reply arrives in the town's mouth, which is the only mouth it has ever listened to, which is the point.

Translated — the office keeps a translator on retainer for exactly this, one word an age — the drop says: received. Or: noted. Or it says the word the water writes every day on its drum, and then the two paperworks, upper and lower, say the same line on the same day — *still here*, and *still here* — a unison of two, answering the unison of none.

Then the beams fall. The round re-enters, out of step, like singers who know the catch by heart. The bowl has been answered, and nothing is different.

Nothing is different. That is the shape of the gift — a thing Loudwater can keep without changing. The clank keeps its nine seconds and the echo keeps its breath behind. The chart recorder inks its identical line. The chalk holds without chalk's explanation. The glass fogs from within over OKONKWO — NIGHTS; the green comes on for the night work; the foxfire strengthens in the pits; the bell rings and the lamps come on, and no one clocks in, and everyone is there. The census is amended by one drop, filed under weather. The form that had two lines, PLACE and RESIDENT, gets a third — ATTENTION — and the clerk writes the same word across all three.

Of the three calm options the paperwork offers everyone who comes to keep this place — leave, finish, never quite arrive — the power has chosen the third. It never quite arrives. It attends from the far side of the fog deck, from the top of the pipe, from the lip of the world, permanently, the way the moon attends, except that it keeps no schedule and is never quite gone. Its nameplates, if it kept nameplates, would read the same as the engines'. It keeps no nameplates. The engines keep enough for everyone.

The power keeps the difference. The difference keeps the engines. The engines keep the line. The line keeps the pool, the pool keeps the green, the green keeps the walls, the walls keep the chord, and the chord keeps the one silence that carries uphill — so that once an age, it can be answered.

Loudwater keeps the water down. Loudwater keeps the water moving. Loudwater keeps eleven times, all of them slow.

Loudwater keeps.

And on the underside of the rim, above the fog deck, a drop is forming. It swells past the size a drop should be. It is in no hurry — nothing here is in any hurry about anything — and it has one appointment per age, at the held breath, and it will be answered by the largest.

The paperwork, upper and lower, will be calm.
