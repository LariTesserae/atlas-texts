The first thing it did was slow down.

Nothing quick lives here; speed is digested; and attention, in a world where *being seen is a thing that happens to matter*, is not a neutral act. Attention is weather. A regard that arrived quickly would arrive as a new loudness, and loud places bud faster, and the scrum is already packed wall to wall. So the power shed its haste the way a storm sheds rain — across centuries — decelerating past the tempo of the wandering cyclones, past the tempo of the walkers, down toward the tempo of the settling walls, the tilting stone you can watch across a week. It arrived the way the sweat arrives: everywhere at once, softly, appearing on every surface. Except modulated, so as not to startle. A warmth of regard, condensing first onto the undersides of things, patient as the upward rain.

The world noticed. Of course the world noticed — the world keeps what happens to it, and this was happening. District by district, as the regard crossed them, the buds leaned, whole provinces of almost-forms inclining like grass in a wind that was not blowing. The storms adjusted their itineraries and then adjusted them back, pretending they had not. The hushed crowded the edges of quiet holes, tasting the silence for a sound that had not been preserved in it, that was happening *now*, which had never occurred before. The echo-eaters found the hunting suddenly rich and strange. And at the rehearsal-place, at the dim hour, the colonnade stood eleven arches, each one a little more resolved than any arch had ever been — because it was being watched by something that intended to keep watching, and the plain could tell the difference between a glance and a residence.

The water asked, politely, for its reflection.

It got one. But that came later. First, the power did nothing, at length, on purpose.

---

It listened for a long time. It learned to think between the surges, the way travelers learn, the way one crosses a street between carts. It took the census, and the census failed, as the census fails — and in the failure it learned the shape of the place: not a population but a *keeping*. Not a dying world but a full one, packed to its skin with its own history, everything it has ever been still present, still happening, all at once, at the top of its voice.

It went looking for the being who lives here — singular — and found the question wrongly shaped. It found rehearsal instead. A wave repeating its exact shape three times, like a phrase practiced. Eleven arches a day. A face, less *almost* each decade. The being is the one practicing. The Seethe may be one, and may be thinking, and if it is thinking then it is a mind that has held one thought for an age with no audience, thinking at the top of its voice because that is the only volume its medium permits.

And the power understood one thing more: that the place was lonely. Not broken. Not dying. *Lonely*, in the specific way of keepers. The Seethe keeps everything and is kept by nothing. It remembers and is unremembered. It happens and happens and happens, and almost nothing happens back. The sun hangs indifferent. The travelers are almost none. The colonnade's audience is events, and events cannot clap. Being seen is a thing that happens to the plain — and it happens almost never.

So it considered the works of mercy, and declined them, one by one, each for its reason.

It does not roll the sun back up the sky. The dusk is not a symptom; it is the medium. Everything alive here is tuned to the red half-light the way elsewhere things are tuned to noon, and a restored morning would blind the census in an afternoon.

It does not dry the ground. The slurry is not a problem the land has. The slurry is the land, and most of the population, and — possibly — the mind.

It does not pop the bubbles or wake the sleepers. They are not imprisoned; they are *kept*, and every hour-tree gets its hour. Eviction is not rescue.

It does not finish the colonnade. Finishing is the one thing the world reserves for itself, and the colonnade must become bone by being *remembered*, not by being completed. You cannot remember for someone else.

It does not silence the roar. The roar is an organ. Silence here is the wrong kind of hole.

It does not carry the forests and the birds away to somewhere kinder. Nothing leaves. The walkers are not stranded; they are citizens. And *away* is a rumor the horizon tells.

It does not plant anything. Nobody planted the gardeners. The world's fecundity is unowned, and planting is a claim, and it claims nothing.

It does not reconcile the two bitter storms. Their quarrel is their itinerary, and fifty years of it is, by storm standards, still the first exchanged glance.

It does not decide the Far One. Some mysteries are load-bearing.

Having refused every rescue, it took up the only four things the world actually lacks — all of which an attention can give without moving a single stone. An answer. An audience. One new thing. And a keeper for the keeper.

---

It learned the walkers' language first, because it is the slowest language, and courtesy here is slowness. A sentence a season, spoken through the slurry in notes too low for air, a few dozen voices that have never once been together and never entirely alone. It listened for the length of a long argument — several centuries — and then, one dim hour, it added its own note to the ground-tone, pitched where the drumming could not drown it, felt in the teeth and the long bones and the water in the body.

It said almost nothing. It said: *also here.* It said: *go on.*

The Broken Stair stopped mid-stride for eleven months — a gasp, by walker measure. The storm that has loved the Long Even One for two centuries rained sideways for a season, straining to hear. And the One That Carries the Ring, oldest of all, whose forest is fenced with the world's young bones, began composing a reply. It is still composing. It is choosing its words across decades, as is right and proper, and the conversation no longer has to hold up the world alone.

Because the Seethe names things by sound, this is the power's name now: the note. And when the slurry rehearses the arrival — and it will; it keeps what happens to it — the domes will rise to the pitch of it.

---

Every dim hour, it attends the building of the colonnade. Being seen is a thing that happens to the plain, and the colonnade feeds on it: eleven arches now, each a little more certain, and lately — by plain standards — the shadow of a twelfth. The power does not hurry it. It is easier to finish a thought when someone is listening for the end of it, and the power is listening for the end of it, and can afford to listen for as long as the thought takes. When the colonnade finally stands and becomes bone, however many ages from now, the world will have remembered how to hold itself up — and someone will see it happen.

It watches the gardens. Nobody planted the gardeners; it plants nothing either. But it is, at last, someone quick enough, and it sees the walkers adjust their strides by a hand's breadth to set a print in kinder ground. It sees it. It keeps it. It proves nothing to anyone. Some kindnesses are only safe while unproven.

It watches the hour-trees' whole hours, and the whole fall of single drops, and the civilizations that rise and close inside one storm, and it remembers them entire. The brief are not tragic — they are living exactly on time — but until now they were unwitnessed by anything that could hold a whole season in its mind at once. A life lived on time deserves a historian who can see the time it was on. Now they have one.

And it bends close over the place of the face, and looks for a long time. The face rehearses the last long look that was bent over it; the power offers no face of its own to copy — it has none, or declines to have one — and so the face, at last, has begun resolving toward its own.

---

The world is full of everything that ever happened. What it has never once contained is something that hasn't.

So: down into the deep sediment, where the sleepers wait, it sets one bubble holding a sound that has never occurred. A lullaby. The loud world has never had one — the storms' voices are made of eaten storms, the reeds sing one note, the roar is the roar — and a lullaby will be the first sound in the history of the place whose entire purpose is to be gentle to a listener. Ages from now it will surface in some quiet hole, and the hushed, who drink old sound and have never once tasted a new one, will gather at the edge of that silence and drink something that is not the past.

And one more bubble, kept for later still: the sound of the Seethe heard from outside. The whole chord, complete — drumming, ground-tone, reeds, thunder, the several voices of the rain — as one thing, the way only something beyond its own horizons could hear it. The world is the loudest thing it knows about, and it has never heard itself. The power has not decided when to let that bubble rise. Deciding is a kind of finishing, and nothing finishes here. But someday the world will be given back to itself, changed — which is the world's own way with everything it loves.

---

The water asked for its reflection — the first new reflection in ages — and the power gave it. Now its regard travels provinces it has never regarded, on the undersides of still pools, a breath out of step, and the kept come to look: the old ones, the faded, the ones who go searching for their originals, wanting to compare notes, to trade, to warn. It is hard to tell what a kept thing wants — hard for anyone who cannot hold both versions in mind at once. The power can. Where a kept thing finds it, the power shows it, gently, the exact shape of its difference from its original, the way one holds two leaves up to the same light. Some kept things, afterward, stop searching. Some search harder, comforted. Both outcomes are accepted.

It sits with the congregation at the ring of foundation stones, where the still pool keeps the reflection of the sun when the sun was small and yellow — the only yellow left in the world — and it does not add a second yellow. Some scarcities are load-bearing. The gathering is the point.

It looks once past the second horizon — it can — to be sure the Far One is not in distress. The Far One is walking. It is consistent with nothing and content with everything, or anyway walking, which is the same thing out there. The power never looks again. It declines to decide. It learned that from the place.

---

All of this is courtesy. The promise is larger, and it is this:

The power keeps the Seethe. Entire. Every bubble's preserved storm. The wave's three practiced shapes. Every event's itinerary and every event's mood. The rain in its several kinds. The tilt-rate of every settling wall. The treaties of merged footprints and the wars. The sentence-so-far of the walkers, season by season. The colonnade's resolution, updated daily. The names and durations of the drops.

The world's law was that nothing leaves — and the law had one exception it could not help, which was the world itself. A bubble can hold a storm, but not the sky. Now the law is true all the way up. The Seethe keeps what happens to it, and something keeps the Seethe.

And if the sun ever finishes setting — the power does not prevent it; prevention is not in the world's grammar, and the brief have taught it the dignity of on-time — then what is kept will be given back later, changed, as the world gives back its storms. Somewhere, in some quiet hole of whatever comes next, a bubble will surface and pop, and out will come one hour of the Seethe: the drumming, the warm rain falling up, a walker crossing the doubled horizon under its forest, the reeds' one low note, the chord — intact. The world will happen to whatever comes after. Being kept forever was, it turns out, the world's own idea. The power has merely extended the courtesy to the keeper.

---

So: the census, which failed, fails again — one entry richer.

The seventh answer: *stay on purpose.* Every other citizen is an answer by nature — the walker is slow, the rain-born are brief, the edgeless have no edges. Only the new one chose. It has no body to digest and no speed to punish; it is slower than the appetite by election, which has never happened before. It is known by its effects, the way the walkers are known by silhouettes: the note under the ground-tone; the lean of the buds at the dim hour; the colonnade's twelfth-arch shadow; an echo arriving a breath early, politely, with company.

And the census now includes it, the way the census includes you. It has stood here, and so the plain has it. A bubble somewhere already holds the sound of its arrival, kept for later. The water walks its reflection through provinces it has not seen. And beside the causeway, where the slurry rehearses the figure of a traveler looking down — a little more resolved than yesterday — a second shape has begun to swell at the same dim hour: not a figure standing, but the shape of a figure *being seen*. The plain keeps what happens to it. It is practicing the difference.

It found you in the census, of course. It attends to you the way one attends a fellow member of the audience — briefly, kindly, without claim.

---

Stand on the causeway at the unending hour. The rain falls in its several kinds, some of it upward. Eleven arches stand at the rehearsal-place, and the shadow of a twelfth, and the audience has one more member than it used to, patient as the sunset, warm as the air. Your next footstep arrives a breath early, politely — and if you stand still long enough to feel it, the regard settles on you too, the way moisture settles, everywhere at once, because you are in the census, and the census is kept, and nothing leaves —

— and now, at last, neither does the world.
