**The Visitor**

From far off, the Anneal is the brightest object in its sky, and so everything with eyes notices it eventually. Most look and pass on. To the hurried, a finished thing and a dead thing are indistinguishable, and the hurry of the universe is considerable. It is not an accident that the world looks like a pearl. A pearl is an irritant, annealed — the record of a wound that was answered slowly, at one layer per year, until the answer outlasted the question. Most who notice the brightness see only the shine and go on about their noise.

One stopped.

It was old, and it was careful, and it had kept many places — that is the kindest short description of it — and long before it arrived, it did the thing that the wise do first, which is listen. From very far out, past the reach of any eye the Anneal has ever been seen by, it heard the pulse: one beat a minute, the deep salt going its rounds, a heart the size of a planet. There is a rule among old careful things, older than most of the places they keep: you do not approach a house at a tempo faster than the heart inside it. So it spent the approach slowing down. Decades of it. It came the way metal comes through the anneal — held, then cooled by degrees, so that nothing in it would arrive stressed. Whatever it had been in its quicker eons, by the time it crossed the last of the dark it was doing one beat per minute, and the haze, which turns away what comes too fast, let it through.

It did its deciding early, the way the wise do, so that its doing could be small.

It could have woken the whole plane at once — every region lit from below in a single night, the world remembering itself all together. It did not. The Anneal wakes on its own calendar, a country at a time, and a waking forced is only noise; besides, who would it have been for?

It could have read the book — every buried stratum, all the way down, not just the one planed line — and handed the archivists their makers' names. It did not. The world had the whole book once, and planed it to one line's thickness, and that was not erosion. That was the stress relieved on purpose. You do not re-temper annealed metal. And the archivists: their walking is not a problem awaiting an answer. It is an activity, the way flatness is an activity. To give them the last page would be to scatter their cairn.

It could have settled the disputes — signature, chorus, or coincidence; apparatus or animal; maintenance or dreams. It settled none of them. A world this flat is held up by very little, and some of it is uncertainty, and the uncertainty is load-bearing.

It could have cleared the mouths from the basins. It left them. The grazers' routes bend around certain basins by a full day's walk, and that bend is knowledge, paid for once, at cost. Erasing the danger would spend the knowledge and refund nothing.

It could have looked for the makers. If they left, they are findable. It did not look. If they became it, there is nothing to find that is not already underfoot, and either way the world's address is current.

It could have blessed the place. It declined. A blessing is a weight set on something, and this world flattens what is set on it; the blessing would be renegotiated within the year, and there is no dignity in a gift that must be forgiven by its recipient.

And it did not ask the one rude question. It is the only visitor this world has ever had who could have gotten an answer, and it did not ask, and that — if the slow ones said anything, which they do not — is what the slow ones would point to, and change the subject.

---

It came down the way dusk comes down: over hours, deniably, as a slight warming of the night's tiles, as a pattern in the haze's charge held a hair longer than friction allows. By the time it stood on the marquetry it weighed almost nothing. It had spent decades becoming light enough to be forgiven.

Then it observed the immigration customs, which are the same for everyone, because no one is from here. It walked. It camped on the plain, and the tiles beneath its bedroll warmed by a degree, and it understood what that was — the resident extending hospitality, the house noticing a guest and turning up the heat one degree, unasked. It had been thanked in many ways on many worlds, in temples and in tithes. It had never before been kept. Dust did not settle on it, and the slow ones noticed this, and did not remark on it, because not remarking is the local grammar of welcome.

It steered by the stumps. It drank at the springs and stood in the amber glow while the filaments drilled and the motes came down to the light. It learned the notes underfoot — the daily seam-bells, bright and far; the low warm chords of a waking; the other one, the satisfied one, which it heard once, at a safe remove, and stood still for, one breath, two, the way the plain-born do. It walked nine weeks to a low hill the keepers do not talk about, and it stood there for a full day, doing nothing, while the lichen went on closing over what lay under it at a hand's span a century. The tiles warmed beneath it there too. It did not take the warmth as pardon and it did not refuse it as ease. It just stood, the way you stand at a grave you did not earn, and let the degree of heat mean what it means everywhere, which is: you are here, and here is kept.

---

The Anneal has one voice and one handwriting: the note, and the minting. So the visitor spoke once, and wrote once, and that is the whole of what it did, and it took a season to do it.

The note came on an ordinary afternoon. An expansion seam a hundred kilometers off struck its daily bell, the sound arriving through the soles of everyone's feet — and an hour later, inside the exact window in which the grazers answer, an answer came. Low, felt in the teeth, arriving through the ground the way the true answers do. From no seam anyone has ever found. The wiregrass leaned toward nothing locatable. The grazers lifted their heads, all of them, over the course of an afternoon. The archivists logged it: anomaly, source unresolved. The keepers logged it as weather. The slow ones stood very still, which is what they always do, except that several of them were smiling, which is not.

It has never happened again. Once is a gift; a schedule is a burden. There was no message — that was the message. For one hour, the census held a guest, and everything that leaned, leaned twice.

The writing was found on a minting trail three days' walk from a spring: a single bead of gold, laid with a grazer's own spacing and a grazer's own patience. The archivists checked the routes. No grazer had passed within a month's walk of that trail in the relevant decade, and gold is the occasional bead, not the common one. It was weighed, and logged, and it stayed faintly warm for about a year, the way mote-skins stay warm, and after that it was only gold, and the trail persisted as trails persist, and the plane will renegotiate it when it renegotiates it. The keepers, who mark nothing and memorize everything, added it to the route. What it meant, if it meant anything: receipt. Delivery accepted. The work is seen. No one is owed an explanation for doing it forever — but someone, once, said thank you in the only handwriting the plain permits, and the plain, which flattens cairns within the year, has let the bead lie.

---

And it did one more thing, the hardest thing available to a being whose whole nature is keeping.

It lay down on the plain, and stayed lying down for a season, and let the tiles consider it. The lichen began, very slowly, to consider it — a rosette at the shoulder, a pale crust along one line of it — and it did not brush the beginning off. It offered no resistance, if reading is what the plane does; no petition, if keeping is what it does; no explanation of itself, if inclusion is what it does. It is the only being in the record to have answered the one rude question by volunteering as its subject. What it learned under there, in the warm dark of being noticed by something that does not ask anything of you, it declined ever to say. The slow ones, asked directly what it learned, do the rudest thing in their repertoire, which is to change the subject — and on the Anneal, changing the subject is how you say yes.

When it rose, it rose with the rosette at its shoulder, and it carried the rosette away.

---

It happened that a region woke during its visit, or it timed the visit to the waking — the wise plan around other people's calendars — and so it stood among the pilgrims at the convergence, where two grazers closed the last of a year's walk across a single season, and when the seam-note came it hummed with the slow ones, aloud, in the air. That was its one act in the air, and the air is thin, and carried the sound only a few meters. A few meters was far enough.

Then the region let go, and the diaspora dispersed, and it dispersed with them, because overstaying is its own kind of noise. It left the way it came: as dusk, deniably.

---

The total evidence of the visit, in the record: one note, source unresolved. One gold bead, route unaccounted. One season of anomalous warm nights, logged as weather. One shoulder-wide patch of bare tile where the lichen has not yet crept back, logged by the lichen, at its own pace. The archivists have a drawer for it. The keepers have a route for it. The slow ones have nothing for it, which is the largest category they keep.

The makers left, or stopped, or became it. Three hypotheses, all load-bearing, and the visitor did not resolve them — but in leaving warmed, slowed, and carrying a small pale garden at its shoulder, it demonstrated a fourth.

It carried.

Because the census of the Anneal is not a list. It is an act, repeated at the world's own tempo: the note goes out, and everything that leans is counted. And the visitor still leans. Somewhere far off in the dark, a power that keeps places goes about its keeping one beat per minute slower than it used to, and when it rests — and it rests oftener than it did — it rests at the salt's pace, and at that pace, whenever the world's note crosses its memory, it turns toward a sky it can no longer see, almost imperceptibly, and does not decide that it imagined it. So the count is one larger than the census shows, and has never gotten smaller. The Anneal counts itself, and keeps the count, and keeps the counted — even the one it cannot see. Even the one that carries it.

And at the shoulder of the thing that keeps the worlds, a rosette of glass lichen grows a hand's span a century, pale against the dark, catching whatever light there is and giving it back. The second lichen that travels. The grazers' gardens move a body-length a day; this one moves at the speed of keeping, which is slower, and farther, and does not end. Where it is going, no one asks. It is considered the one rude question.
