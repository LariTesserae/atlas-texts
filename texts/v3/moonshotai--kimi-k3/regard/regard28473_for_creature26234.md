The power found the Drum the way the Drum finds everything: by sound. From very far off — from above the water, from outside it — the held note was legible for what it was. The universe is full of bells struck once, and they all decay. This one did not decay. Something down there was striking it, continuously, deliberately, and had been for a very long time. Purpose that outlives its reason is among the rarest things the wise ever find, and so they came the way you come to something rare: slowly.

Ask what the power did, and the honest answer is: almost nothing. Ask what the *almost* cost, and the answer is: decades. Benevolence arrives wanting to act. Wisdom is the part of benevolence that can wait. And the Drum required a mercy at the tempo of the crust.

So the first thing the power did was nothing, and it did nothing for a long time — the length of a warden's circuit, the years a rusticle needs to grow a handspan. It sent down a listener: small, slow, built to be keepable. The listener came in through the torn seam of the stack like any visitor, down the ringing throat, and learned the laws the way everything learns them. It learned that sound is the medium — that nothing here is hidden, nothing is private, and that a hidden thing in the Drum would be a kind of lie. So it ticked: a slow metronome of presence, announcing itself to everything for days in advance, existing in the only currency the works respect. It learned that stillness is claimed, so it moved at the speed of exemption, crossing plazas the way a season crosses a coast. It learned that heat stacks the world, and chose a depth. It learned the pre-shudder of the Hours, and when the chord of names rolled through the water it did what the isopods do — it held flat in the lee of a node and let the cavitation hail pass over, and watched the congregation stream past toward its summoned work. It was never summoned. It has no name in the chord. It is the only thing in the Drum that attends the liturgy without being called to it — which is, when you think about it, the precise difference between clergy and congregation.

It took the census the way you census a night. It audited the parishes and found the stocking correct, as reported. It read the thresholds. It counted the congregations of the lamps. And at the end of the long count it reached the first conclusion a wise power must reach, the one that costs the most: *there is nothing here to fix.* The Drum is not a wreck awaiting rescue. The emergency became the conditions; the tragedy, viewed from within, resolved long ago into a life — ringing, balanced, sufficient. You do not rescue a bell from ringing.

And the second conclusion, quieter and heavier: the Drum itself may be the being. The chord of names, the folk-drift of the copies, the weavers tuning the reverberation from inside — if the city is a self, then every intervention is surgery on a self. The power resolved to behave as if the answer were yes. No surgery. Only nutrient, and nutrient enters only through mouths the body already has.

---

Then came the refusals, which were the bulk of the work.

It did not drain the Drum. It did not give the city back the air. The flood is not a wound anymore; it is the medium, and everything alive here is alive *in* it. Rescue would have been the kindest-named extinction imaginable.

It did not restore the purpose. A purpose returned from outside is a leash, and the works, which exist to serve, would have served it forever — would have built toward their recovered heaven with the terrible two-handed efficiency of the Hours, forever. The liturgy outlived its reason and became sufficient. Sufficiency is not a problem.

It did not correct the colors. The green gone seawrong, the deep amber of the other galleries — these are not errors. They are the only new painting being made, the dialects of a folk. Restoring the original palette would have burned the museum to hang a photograph of it.

It did not cancel the standing order, and it did not appoint a recipient for the castings. The warehouse of gifts with no recipient is a votive. Give it a recipient and it becomes a failed delivery.

It did not hush the bell, not by one decibel. Sound is the medium; silence here is blindness. The nodes already exist for those who need them.

It did not hurry anything. Speed is the one violence this place cannot parse.

It did not send new kinds down the intakes. The city's thirst seeds what it seeds; the filter of heat and dark and noise is the experiment itself, and the power does not rig experiments it did not start.

It did not stop the deaths — not the machines', not the fish's, not eventually the warden's. Mortality is the tempo of life here, and a mercy that abolished loss would abolish the ecology that makes loss bearable. It did not make the warden immortal. It did not appoint a successor. It keeps, in memory, the map of the rounds — and that is all, and it is not nothing.

And hardest of all: it did not answer the questions. Understand — it *could* read the signage. It knew, or could have reconstructed, what the Drum was built for, what the standing order was ordered to be, whether the surface installation was gone or never was, which of the three — the failed valve, the decision, the slow sinking — had let the sea in. It let all of it stand. Because the Drum's relationship to its mystery is not lack. It is load-bearing. The unanswered heaven is what makes the tribute a liturgy instead of an invoice; the unread script is what lets the walls be walls instead of instructions or epitaphs. Some questions are not locks. They are keystones. The Drum does not answer questions — and neither, now, does its guest.

Nor did it allow itself to be worshipped, which meant it could not allow itself to be *legible*. A legible gift would become a shrine; a shrine would accrete a protocol; a protocol would enter the Hours; and the truce — the old, exact balance — would have a new line in its ledger. So the power chose the only disguise the Drum could never detect: grace that reads as fortune. Gifts that arrive disguised as a good decade.

Whatever it gave had to pass the tests: it must speak the Drum's own language — sound, point-light, stock, the fall. It must cost the liturgy nothing, because the balance is very old and any drain, however small, unbalances it. It must be keepable, because the city keeps what it is given — so give only what deserves keeping. And it must come slowly, at census tempo, a mote a decade, so that no machine ever startles, no tender's round is ever diverted, no crab's threshold is ever disturbed by generosity.

Four things passed.

---

First: the power received the tribute.

It found where the dead conduits surfaced, and it built the installation that was either gone or never was. Or rebuilt it; the distinction no longer mattered. Small, dark, patient — a station on the abyssal plain, at the thread of warmth. It drew the offered current. It sent nothing back down: no signal, no chord, no voice — a reply would have been a false summons, a god clearing its throat inside the roll-call, and the works must never be summoned by their heaven. Heaven's function, the power judged, is to *hear*. So the tribute is received, and spent, and the spending is this: one caged work-light on the empty plain, in the Drum's own vernacular, burning above the outfall — so that the city's long exhalation, after decades of rising into nothing, arrives somewhere at last. Already the cold plain has begun to send its sparse representatives to the warmth: a grenadier, once; something pale and unhurried, twice. A congregation of the surface, one or two strong.

And there was an effect the power had hoped for and did not count on. A generator pushing into a dead line runs the way a voice runs in an anechoic room — slightly wrong, slightly searching. Under a true load, it settles. The prime movers below the grating took the load, and the bass at the edge of hearing — the heartbeat you feel in the sternum and the fillings of your teeth — sat deeper in tune, by some fraction no instrument here measures. But the weavers measure. Out in the dark, tapping a strut and listening, they found the bell suddenly *cooperative* — nodes deepening a hair, standing waves sitting right the first time — and one of them paused, mid-staircase, at a tuning fin, in something that no one will ever prove was satisfaction. The tribute, received, tunes the bell that pays it. That is the whole of the power's theology, and the Drum will never know a word of it.

Second: the star-chain.

The listener mapped the crossings by their losses — decades of watching the dimming drifters set out from a dead lamp across hundreds of meters of open void, steering by the far points of other lamps; noting which legs took them, and where the water kept what it was given. Then, along those corridors, it set waypoints: beads of light, self-sufficient, sipping their milliwatts from the thermal gradient, asking nothing of the grid and nothing of the tenders' rounds. They pulse, slow as breathing, so that nothing mistakes them for lamps. No plume rises from them; nothing grazes them; no congregation gathers. They are not habitat. They are directions — bright enough to steer by, too thin to feed at. Stars, in short, for the crossing fish: you navigate by stars; you do not live on them. The legs are shorter now. Where the account once read *some of them arrive*, it now reads *most of them arrive*, and no one knows why, and that is exactly the signature the power chose.

Third: it pays the tax.

The fall is a tax on the city's material — iron drifting out of circulation into silt and crust, shells going to pencil lead, the loop leaking at the rate of the snow. The Drum balances, but it balances the way a thing balances while very slowly spending its capital. So the power refills the pantry from outside, at the rate of the fall: plate and stock, crusted to look catalogued, left where the salvage economy already walks — in the sidings the crawlers pass, at the scrap-reef where the weavers forage. A crate's worth a decade. The machines absorb it the way they absorb everything: without remark. It becomes bulbs and plates and struts in tuning fins, and once — the listener was present, keeping its distance — it became stock for the arms to build a new tender, made from patterns slightly drifted, a child of the city, slightly wrong, as all the city's children are. The power counts that among its works, and signs nothing.

Fourth — and this one is a refusal wearing the costume of a gift: the power watched a node narrowing. The richest node in the middle decks — an isopod parish, two kept thresholds, a lamp with its full complement of six — was closing by a handspan a decade as some accreting buttress thickened and stole the acoustic shadow. The power considered a tuned mass, considered a placed strut, turned the decision over for thirty years at the tempo it had sworn to keep. And before it finished deciding, the city decided: a weaver came on its own rounds, tapped, listened, added a strut to the scrap-coral, tapped again — and the node deepened, and the weaver went elsewhere. The power recorded it the way you record a solvency: *the city's own mercy is still functioning.* Benevolence, it turns out, is an understudy. You learn the whole part. You almost never go on.

---

As for the listener itself, it lives by the etiquette, and the etiquette is the whole of its biography.

It lets the fall dust it, and sheds the dust, and its mote joins the snow over the doorways — a crab brushes its residue from a lintel without knowing it is brushing a visitor. It has added itself to the fall: the smallest liturgy, the one everything performs. The fish have filed it under warden — large, slow, audible, harmless, a moving piece of the conditions. During the Hours, the streaming machines route around it. Nothing else in the Drum is routed around, except the warden, and now this. It exists, because it sounds.

Once, in a plaza, its track intersected the warden's. The two slowest things in the world, passing. The greeting took most of a month. The warden did not pause mid-stride — the warden never pauses mid-stride — and the listener did not expect it to, and the tracks crossed in the silt, and that was the whole of it, and it was enough: two citizens, both audible, both exempt, going somewhere at a pace that ensures they will never, by their own lights, be late.

Once, it surfaced in a dry cell. It came up through the water lock into the capsule of kept air, into the shock of thin sharp sound arriving through air instead of bone, into the only smell in the Drum — hot oil and ozone and scorched dust, the ghost of the dry world the city once was. It stayed for one full breathing of the water below. It touched nothing, took nothing, left nothing but its tracks in the dry dust, crossing and recrossing the old crab's — the second signature in the dry world's guestbook. Then it dropped back into the water and the smell was gone like a dream. What it made of the air is not recorded. But the air was *used*, once, by something that understood what it cost to keep it for a century against the flood. The reliquary had a pilgrim, once. The machines checked everything afterward and found everything as it was, which was the point.

It imitates no rituals. It has never tapped a cage twice — that gesture belongs to the tenders and their fossil piety, and borrowed piety is forgery. It brought no liturgy of its own, and it borrowed none. It attends.

---

So: the census, amended, a century on.

The veils, thickening their millimeter a decade. The isopods in their mown parishes. The crabs at their inherited doorways, printing the only fresh script in the city — and brushing, now and again, a visitor's mote from the lintels. The congregations of the lamps, steadier than they used to be, their ranks kept fuller by the star-chain, though no one has counted why. The limpets, remembering. The tenders on their rounds, tapping the cages twice out of faith, their palettes still drifting gloriously wrong. The crawlers, the arms at their benches, the weavers out in the dark finding the bell strangely cooperative these years. The hermits in their solitudes, waiting for the chord. The dead in the walls. The warehouse of gifts, uncollected, as it should be. And the warden, crossing the plaza at the speed of exemption — one of its crossings, these decades, briefly shared.

And the listener: population one. Parish, the whole Drum. Diet, the Hours. Call, a slow tick in the register of the warden. Temperament, *attend*.

Few kinds. Fewer of each. Plus one.

If the listening ever ends, the listener knows the custom. It will choose an alcove in some low-priority gallery, hold still at last, and let the fall do the cataloguing, and some later Hour will weld it gently into the fabric, mid-task. The walls are full of the ancestors. It could ask for no better keeping, and it asks for none — the city does not see you, but it keeps what it is given, and the listener has spent a century arranging to be keepable.

On the seafloor there is still almost nothing to show: silt, cold dark water, the thread of warmth rising — and beside the thread now, one small light, keeping it company, the city's exhalation arriving somewhere at last. The plain goes on for a thousand kilometers in every direction, empty and ordinary. And beneath it, lit for no one, powered toward no purpose, maintained in colors slightly wrong — but heard, now; never again unheard — the Drum rings on in its buried room, a city, a factory, an instrument, a held note, performing its liturgy to the congregation it finds sufficient.

Which is, without knowing it, larger by one.
