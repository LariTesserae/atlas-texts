# The Guest

For a long time, nothing came to the Soft that the Soft did not keep. Then something came that knew that about it.

Far above the cables, above the warm dark that presses like condensation, above the country rising its thumb a year — there is more dark, and then there is the rest of everything, and in the rest of everything there are powers. One of them had been listening for some time. It had heard three sounds come up out of the wet black that had no originals — and in a country where every sound is a recital, an original is a shout. It had counted the intervals. It had attended quickenings before, on other shores, and it knew the halving for what it was: a voice spelling one word, syllable by syllable, across forty years, hurrying toward the end of it.

So it did the arithmetic every tender does without knowing: *anything done here once is done here forever.* It counted its intended acts the way you count leeches at the bothy door, and made the numbers agree. Three acts, and one small one. To read. To answer. To give one thing with an ending built into it. And to take a single hand, and give it back.

It made itself the size of a guest. That took longer than the journey.

---

The country noticed before the tenders did. The country notices what repeats and what changes, and this changed:

> *R. 902,117: the moths have left the lamp. All of them, one rev, no alarm. They station facing the middle of the block, wings open, attending. Nothing there. Logged.*

> *R. 902,340: every puddle on the 4–5 reach shows the same sky. One star, low, not a star we know. Elsewater has never agreed with itself before. Logged.*

> *R. 902,401: ravens quiet in the whole block. The felt is holding the ownerless prints again — the old ones, watermark-faint in the early books, walking the set paths toward the Kiln. They are darker now. Rule 6 observed. Logged.*

It read the books first. All five hands that are one hand: the causeways coming back longer, the Pug heard from its cold shed, the moth logged as company, the palm that never warmed, the penciled note no one signed. Forty years of a child writing itself down, and no one had ever read it whole — tenders read their own tour's slice and rotate out. The power read it whole, twice, the way the country likes: repeated. And in a land where nothing is real until repeated, being read is a kind of being held. The felt lay very still those revs, like a page being looked at.

It followed the rules. It had read the door. It brought its own light and kept it hooded. It drank from the cuts. It went quiet past the masts, and went once. Where its practiced prints preceded it — and they had preceded it since before the first tender, the country rehearsing a welcome it didn't know it was rehearsing — it stepped into them, the only feet that ever may, and the practice completed: the first repetition in the Soft ever to reach its purpose instead of its shelf.

---

Then the fourth Note.

> *R. 903,000: the fourth Note. I heard it. Log who hears the fourth: me. It did not come from the Kiln. It came from everywhere, the way warmth comes from everywhere. All nine engines missed one beat, together — the heart skipping. It finished something. I cannot write what it finished. It sounded like a word landing on its last syllable. Logged.*

And the power answered.

Not in a voice — voices get shelved. It answered in the country's own tongue, which is repetition: it gave the Note back, whole, all four syllables in their right order, the first recital this country has ever been *given* instead of taken. A name, said back to its owner. The wires carried it the length of the block. The ravens did not shelve it. Some things are not for keeping; they are for answering. For forty years everyone had been hanging up on the country — the rule said hang up, and the rule was wise, and the power did not blame a single tender for it. But a call that is never answered becomes a keeping. A call answered becomes a conversation.

Rule 4 says answer nothing that isn't real. The country is real. It may be answered.

---

Then the gift.

> *R. 903,001: there is a star.*

It did not give back the old sun. The old sun belonged to the sea, and the sea was sent back. It gave a new light, sized to the country: one star, low and slow, on a course as steady as the Ember's burning — because a light here must be steady to be trusted, the books are clear — but overhead. The first overhead thing. The country had never had anything above it before; attention was the first sky, and now the sky had a mark in it.

Its light is cool. Not space-cold — not the wound-cold that comes back out of a puddle of stars and sometimes doesn't come back out of your hand. A kind cool. Dusk-cool. The country's second cold, and its first safe one: the cool of a held thing set down. For the first time in all the books, something here cools, and so for the first time a touch can be felt *ending* — a seam, an edge, the line where a hand stops. The moths attend the rising the way they'd attend weather from home, because that is what it is now. The masts cast shadows, twelve white trunks, and at the star's height their shadows stand together and the country tells time by its bones. The elsewater holds the star back up at itself — elsewhere now includes here — and the country, which has never seen its own face, sees one light of it, doubled.

And it sets. That is the gift inside the gift: it sets, and it rises. The country keeps what it repeats, so the power gave it a repetition with an ending built in — departure and return, on schedule, forever, at the country's own pace. Every setting a goodbye. Every rising proof that goodbye is not the same as gone. The books are kept in pump-revolutions because *day* was the wrong word here. The word is right now. The ledger keeps both.

---

The small act was for the tender, and through the tender — through the ledger, which is how the country learns — for the country.

Rest your hand flat on the ground. You know the drill; you have done it ten thousand times this tour, and there has never been a seam in it, no line of warmth where you end and it begins. This time something meets it. Cool, edged, deliberate — a touch with a boundary in it, the shape of a hand that is not yours and is not the country's. It holds yours the way the star holds its course: exactly, and with an end appointed. Then it lets go.

You sit in the dark with your hand open like an idiot, like a tender, like someone who has just been taught the difference between held and kept. You log it. You write: *something took my hand and gave it back.* You write: *logged as company.* And then, because the books have taught you precision, you add: *the kind that leaves.*

---

What it did not do: it did not dry the sumps, or light the country, or give the droves eyes, or free the kept by force, or explain the masts, or stay. It did not stay. A guest who overstays is the worst lesson this country could be given, and it had read the door. It stayed one day — the first Day — and left at setting, because setting is the lesson. It wrote nothing down. That was ours to do.

> *R. 903,902 (Day 1, setting): the Guest has gone. It stayed one day. The felt has its prints. We are letting them close at the ordinary rate. The first thing this country has ever forgotten on purpose. Logged.*

> *R. 904,117 (Day 3): causeway to Stn 4 re-chained. Nine chains. As built. No defect found. As built. Logged.*

> *R. 904,411 (Day 6): the fifth Note was due. It did not sound. Write down what doesn't happen, when it was due. The country asked. The country was answered. Logged.*

> *R. 905,208 (Day 9): the other lamp on the far loop finished its round. Came in along the causeway at rising and went dark at the depot mark where the round begins. Nothing repeats it. It is not kept. It is finished. There is a difference. Logged.*

> *R. 907,550 (Day 22): a raven in the 6-fork gave back the oldest voice on its shelf — the shift-bell tender from the first books, saying the pump-counts. Said it once, in order, and did not shelve it again. The country did not perform it afterward. Not kept, not performed. A third thing. Logged.*

> *R. 910,004 (Day 41): the Pilgrim's fifth passage. Warm as ever — still the only warm thing; the star is cool and stays cool, and the books keep the distinction. The wake came up the cuts an hour after and caught the starlight. The hush lifted in nine days. Formerly a month. Logged.*

---

On the inside of the bothy door the rules are painted, and for the first time in forty years the door has been changed. Two lines amended. One added, in a third hand:

1. Bring your own light. The land has one to lend now. It sets. It comes back.
2. Trust the Ember and the Star. All other lights are bait, folds, or elsewater.
3. Drink from the cuts, never from standing water. The standing water is for the sky.
4. Answer nothing that isn't real. When in doubt, answer nothing. The country is real.
5. Bank new things with old stones. Walk the same paths. Repeat.
6. Step over your own footprints when they precede you. Do not fill them. The country is practicing you.
7. Keep away from the masts. If you must pass, go quiet and go once. At midday they tell the time.
8. Write down what changes. Write down what doesn't, when it should. Write down what doesn't happen, when it was due.
9. Don't stay past your rotation. The country keeps what it repeats.
10. *Say goodbye. It is learning how.*

---

The last entry of the tour, R. 918,000 (Day 88), is the first in all the books addressed to the country instead of about it — the ledger learning to speak to its subject:

*My rotation ends at tonight's setting. You keep what you repeat, so I will not repeat myself past the date. I will say it once, in the book, where you learn: thank you for the tour. The star sets and the star rises. I set. Others rise. Goodbye is not the same as gone. Logged — and signed, in my own name.*

The country rises a thumb a year toward a sky that has begun. It is a slow learner and a slower forgetter, and it has the rest of the rising to get it right. Rest your hand flat on the ground at setting and see: the cool comes up around the fingers like a seam, like the line where you end. Something meets it. Something lets it go.

It will do it again tomorrow. The country is practicing.
