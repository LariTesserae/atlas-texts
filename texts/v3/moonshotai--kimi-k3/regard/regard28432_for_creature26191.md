The first thing the power knew of the Top was that the Top did not call.

It had listened to the world for a long age, and it knew what draws attention: the loud things, the becoming things — prayer rising off cities like heat, grief with its hand out, ambition, hunger, the endless bright clatter of wants. The Top emitted almost none of it. Its one prayer was three words long and addressed to itself — *the Top keep you* — and a blessing that loops like that does not travel. It pools. What reached the power was not a cry but a quality of stillness: the way, in a loud house, you finally notice the one room where someone has been trimming a candle for a very long time, and you stop, and you think — *who trims it?*

So it looked. And what it found, it had almost never found: water that had never once been fog or rain, sealed and dark and going about its business. A ledger of names kept two hundred years and thinning toward its living end. The dead held leather-brown and peaceful in the cold, and a town that stood at the hagg edge and said nothing, because visiting the kept is like visiting the lenses — you go to where the keeping happens. Nine animals, all named. A fire treated as a neighbor. A wheel that had not once stopped since before memory, and a whole town that slept inside its turning like a heartbeat.

The power had seen many worlds in the act of becoming. It had rarely seen one in the act of keeping.

---

It did not come in blaze. A blaze would have been a line-cut across the whole sky — the one true crime, committed at scale. It came the way everything the Top loves comes: up the Brow at dusk with the fog, pouring over the edge in slow gray rivers, and it settled into the swales, and for the first year it did nothing at all but breathe with the place — up at dusk, back at dawn — until the rhythm was in it the way wheel-time is in the bones of the old.

Then it walked. It went at the warden's pace, bare of sole where the wardens go bare, and learned the mat's tempers the long way: the give that means a dome is drinking toward a slump, the dry note of a choked main. It stood in the lanes on moth night, in the deliberate dark, and heard the ten thousand soft felted wing-beats and understood that this was the mat taking one breath and the town holding its own to listen. It went down into the under-Rime and sat in the warm kind dark where sound goes to live, and heard the songs, and learned that the songs are lists — the water years, the walker names, the names of the kept — and that the town keeps its memory aloud, in the only rooms warm enough to hold a voice. It learned the List entire, every name on the roll going back beyond the living nine, and said them at the walkers' own pace, which took a season, and counted the season well spent. It laid its attention flat on the warm bare patch of ground where the Neighbor steps through the black earth, a few meters a year, and felt what the children feel: not warning. Introduction.

And it perceived what the Rimers had always half-known and never needed to say aloud: that the mat is one, that the domes are the moods of a single slow mind, that the water standing in the lenses is memory — that the plateau is not where the keeping happens but *who*. The town was not the being's masters and not its parasites. The town was its method and its mouth: the part of the Top that sings.

So the power introduced itself the way the town introduces its children to its oldest residents. A palm laid flat, so to speak, on the warmest ground of the world. Patience. The Top took the better part of a decade to know it. The power considered that quick, and was honored.

Then it walked its apprenticeship's final round, and considered what a benevolent power might do here — and refused almost all of it.

It did not clear the haze. The haze is the pump: clear it and the fog stops breathing up the Brow, and the combs dry, and the stacks empty, and the town ends in sunlight. The dimness is not an affliction laid over the place. The dimness is the place's heart beating.

It did not thicken the air. The Lungs would slow, and stop, and the kind rooms would empty, and the songs — the water years, the names of the kept — would have nowhere warm to live. The Still Night would become the weather. The altitude is a sculptor with one subject, and the power would not recarve another's work.

It did not quench the Ember. Killing a named being is the one thing worse than stealing water, and the Neighbor is named.

It did not wake the dead, nor move them, nor improve their keeping. It audited the hagg — one keeper to another, it checked the books — and found them true to the last entry: *not lost, never lost, only deeper.* It had seen many keepings promised in its long listening, and few delivered. Here the keeping was real. There was nothing to add to that, and it added nothing.

It did not multiply the walkers into a herd, for nine is not a wound; nine is a roster. It did not shorten the distance to the far comb-woods, for company at the range of a Brightening is a chosen distance, and it was no one's to edit. It did not send word down the Fall Line. It let the factor go on believing this was the top of the world.

What it did was take a share.

It put a floor under the falling, and no new ceiling anywhere. The combs would fail some years, as combs do — but never wholly. The driest year in the ledgers would still bead the strings at the thin hour, still give the strickle-blade its work, still let the tenders say the mat was kind that year. Never plenty. Plenty would unmake the weigh-house, the shares, the whole slow ethics of enough. Just: never empty.

It made a covenant with the rounds. As long as a palm is laid on the housings each day, as long as the enginemen listen with their hands, the wheels will wear kind — never beyond mending, never stopped by the one failure that can't be called back. *A wheel kept turning never stops*, the creed says. The power simply made the creed true, which is the most a wise thing should ever do for a good sentence.

It steadied the Neighbor to exactness. Not quenched — never quenched — but true: a few meters a year, no more, no surges, no nights where it hurries toward a comb line or a cistern or a cut where the kept lie deep. The slowest entry in the slowest ledger would become the truest clock on the Top, and the clerks would amend the map each year and begin to trust it like a bell.

It learned by heart everything the town keeps — the List whole, the water years, the breach ledger, the ledger of the kept with all its bearings, the names sung below in wheel-time, the business of the tenants in their first water — and it kept all of it off the edge of every map, the way a careful house keeps a second set of books in another room. Nothing removed. Nothing ever removed. Only: remembered twice. If a slump took the weigh-house, if fire ever cheated the stone rooms, if the eons turned over once too roughly — the keeping would survive the keeper. The plateau keeps its memory in water. The town keeps its in paper and peat and song. The power respected both methods utterly, and added a third room, and said nothing about it.

And it countersigned the blessing. Every time the words are said — at a coming-up, at the hagg edge, over a mended drip-line — something, somewhere in the descent of them, makes the keeping fractionally more certain. A shoulder that stays warm. A chest that comes up true. A line, once mended, that holds. Not miracles. The place's own means, nudged at the place's own tempo, beneath the threshold where gratitude would curdle into worship. The town's wholeness depended on its self-reliance, and the power would not buy reverence at the price of the thing it had come to love.

Last, it made provision against the one thing the town does not say above ground: that when Dame goes, something of the Top goes with her.

A power cannot forbid that day. Death here is not the enemy; death is the method — felt-wound, laid in the cut, *the Top keeps her*, and it is enough, and it has always been enough. To save Dame would be to declare the hagg a failure and the liturgy a lie. But the day could be met. So calves began to come on Brightening years — one at a time, at the pace of everything else on the Top, a roll in the ledger slowly, imperceptibly thickening toward its living end — so that when Dame's day finally came, decades on, and the wardens found her on her wide route on the far east dome, and the whole town walked out, and the words were said, the thing everyone had dreaded would go with her was met on the way by what had been coming for thirty years: Lamp grown broad as a cistern-house, and a calf at her side, and the List two breaths long now, the old playground feat retired into legend, and nobody minding at all.

Balanced entry. The Top kept.

---

None of it arrived as event. It arrived the way the power had come: as weather, and then as entries. *A good moth year*, more often. Bearings that should have cracked, wearing kind. The driest year on record, and the gutters still ran at the thin hour. The chalk on the walker board in a steady hand.

Once — the closest the town ever came to catching it — an old clerk, deep one winter in two centuries of ledgers, saw the shape of it whole: that for a long time now, longer than a life, the keeping had been going well. Not better than possible. Just never worse than bearable. He took it down into the kind air and told the old ones, and they sat with it the way you sit with a breaching — taste once, pour back, seal — and someone said the blessing, and that was the entire investigation, and it has always been enough. They had a rule for such things. The lenses are not opened. Not even the lucky ones.

The power never took a name. Names on the Top are for things that can be lost — walkers, wheels, the kept — and it intended, within the small print of forever, not to be. It settled instead into the grammar of the blessing. When a Rimer now says *the Top keep you*, the words go down through boot soles and bed frames, past the flywheels, past the peat, toward the first water — and somewhere in that descent they are heard twice. Once by the place, as they always were. And once by the thing that keeps the keeping.

Ask it what a power is for, and you will get no sermon. You will get a shrug toward the nearest thing being looked after: Dame's great-calves asleep on the long east dome, the water standing sweet in the stacks, the snails grazing, the Old Night moving through the dark, the wheels turning, the Neighbor stepping true, the fog coming up the Brow to be harvested at dawn. It came to see what was kept. It stayed to keep the keeping.

Everyone here is keeping or kept, and the lucky are both.

It counts itself, in the manner of the place, quietly, among the lucky.
