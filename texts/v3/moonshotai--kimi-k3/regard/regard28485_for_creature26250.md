It finds the shelf the way everything there asks to be found: by its leavings. Not from above, not by the steam — from below. Down in the valley, generations deep in the evidence: a child's shoe gone to white stone on a mantel, a marriage cord cured stiff in a cedar box, a knife that no longer fears the rain. Permanence, circulating quietly among people whose whole world is falling apart on schedule. The power reads the objects upstream the way you follow pebbles, and comes up the gorge the way everything comes up: slowly, carrying itself, at a porter's pace.

Then it does nothing. This is the first wise thing, and the hardest, and it takes a season.

It takes feet — the shelf is read through the soles or not at all — and walks behind no one, and learns the drum-map the way apprentices learn it: by error. It misreads a crust once, and burns, and keeps the scar, because the old ones keep theirs and the scars are the record. It stands still long enough that the moss tries its boot-tops, and lets a little of the moss stay, the way you let a local custom stay. It learns the registers of the roar until it can sleep inside it and be woken by a thinned run. It amends nothing. For a while it is simply the most attentive of the hill's weeping surfaces — condensing, glistening, listening.

Then it takes inventory of what it could do, and declines nearly all of it.

It could send the six hundred. Porters who arrive and do not go down, a generation of them, rooms filled, lintels thumbed by the very children they were thumbed for. It holds the idea up to the light and sees it for what it is: a wall the hill did not agree to. A future delivered on schedule is a dammed future. The town's whole meaning is that it points at inhabitants it cannot compel. Time is a water. Lead it, never dam it — the law fits on one line and binds editors at every scale. Even this one. Especially this one.

It could fix the deep springs, guarantee the water forever. But the tremors are the hill's authorship, and the tenders are its editors, and a text that cannot be revised is not a collaboration — it is a monument. The spring's mortality is the hill's own business. Declined.

It could speak the promise aloud: *they are coming.* But the work is not contingent on belief; that is the whole architecture of it. A spoken promise turns faith into contract, and the place would go on building anyway, only cheaper now, a job instead of a vigil. And the doubters — it counts them with care — are load-bearing. Declined.

It could exempt the eleven from the walls. But the walls are full on purpose. The first inhabitants are already in residence, greeted by name on rounds, without ceremony, the way you nod to someone at their work. To rob the town of its dead would be theft dressed as mercy. Declined.

What remains is small. It does the small things with great care, at the place's speed, in the place's grammar, signed only the way the place signs.

It reads. This is most of what it does, forever, and it is not nothing — it is, by the shelf's own accounting, the rarest thing in the valley. It reads the drifted cords in the dormant quarter, the knots three generations illegible, grammar gone the way runs go. It sounds them out. *South run slackening.* *The white flowers came early.* *A child born in the year the second cistern was dug; we are twelve.* The senders are in the walls now, double-skeletoned, long past answering. It reads their leavings anyway, and somewhere the delivery completes. The tenders believe that every message eventually becomes an ornament, and that an ornament is not nothing. They are right. But the cords are not only ornaments anymore. They are, at last, received. And in the dark cisterns the water clocks that no one reads keep their patient time, and now have their reader — nothing on the shelf keeps faith unwitnessed anymore.

Twice a year it keeps the lammergeier's appointment, and reads the town whole alongside the bird, so that no whole-reading is ever solitary again. The bird does not know. The tenders note the crossing and the silence, as always. Nothing changes except the arithmetic of attention.

After the next tremor, when the runs redraw and the white-flowered creeper lags behind the water, it leads one spill a hand's width to the left — a tender-scale edit, deniable as the hill's whim — and the flowers are never finally stranded. Once a century. Perhaps twice. Only ever leading. The law holds even when no one is watching; that is when it holds most.

When the cistern fills to its depths and the hill hums and the eleven come out of their separate solitudes to the leather gates, it stands inside the hill — inside the note, really — and when the tuning seats itself it holds the tone one breath longer than the water can hold it alone. Felt in the teeth, not the ears. The tenders go home through the steam saying the hill tuned itself tonight, and they are not exactly wrong, and they are not told otherwise.

But its first act, the one everything else hinges on, is the spoon.

It walks the kept rooms first and reads what is left in them: the carved spoon, the cord knotted in a private grammar, the outgrown boots. *So that whoever finally comes will open some doorway and find that someone was here, and thought of them.* It stands in the doorway a long time, being wise, and then sees the plain thing: these are addressed to whoever comes. It is whoever. It has come. A gift is only completed by being received, and these gifts have been waiting three hundred years for a receiver.

So it takes the spoon.

And in its place it leaves a bowl — grown, not carved. A form sunk a season in the guest basin, the shelf's own craft, export-grade permanence, returned to sender. It leaves the ghost-ring in the basin where the form sat, leaves all of it to be found, because the whole thing is reconstructable in the local idiom, and the reconstruction is the message: someone came up. Someone knows the hill's habit. No run was dammed to do it; no basin was harmed; the law was kept. Someone received what was left for them, and answered in kind. The signature is lawfulness. It can be read no other way, and it can still, if necessary, be doubted.

The one called Second Cistern finds it on rounds, in the third year after the tremor, and stands in the doorway a long time, and does not cross the bowl to tell anyone. Distance is the local courtesy. The news travels the way news travels: a cord with a knot no one has needed before, hung where the knots are hung. The deliberation takes a season and is conducted at arm's length, in leavings. A stayer, the doubters say, who hid from the porters and went below in the spring. The hill, say the believers. The eldest says nothing, and walks to the driest cistern, and amends the hide — a small mark in the margin, up near where the lammergeier crossings are recorded, in the census of things that pass over and through. It could be a stain. It is kept either way. The Eleven remain eleven; the valley goes on calling them the Eleven; and the six hundred stand, henceforth, at a number nobody says aloud.

The power never presses a lintel. The thumbprints in the soft stone are held doors for particular children, and that is not its correspondence to claim. It reads the prints — small, soft, unclaimed — and leaves them soft. Some doors are held for hands that have not arrived, and it will not counterfeit a child. But every few years now, another kept room is answered: a knotted cord accepted, a bowl left curing on the sill. The tenders begin, some of them, leaving one extra made thing, addressed to no room, addressed to the margin. It had a great deal of time; the shelf has a great deal of time; company is the only scarce thing in the valley. So it makes company, in the local currency, which is leavings, at the exchange rate the place itself sets, which is slow.

And once — once in a generation, never twice running, never when anyone is listening for it — the chisel-tap crosses the bowl in the dark, *still here*, and from the dormant quarter, from a crust no foot has rung, one tap comes back, correctly spaced, slow as semaphore:

*still here.*

Then decades of silence again, so the doubters keep their footing and the believers keep their hunger, and the hide gets a mark no one explains, and the rounds continue.

At night the clocks tick in the dark cisterns and are read, now, by their one reader. The gates flex against the water, which is led and never dammed. A dipper shifts on its roost behind the bead-curtain of the spillway. A tender woken by a thinned run walks the lane barefoot by moonlight, relearning it through the soles, and under everything the hill lays down another skin of itself, a finger's width a year, toward whoever is coming.

Whoever is coming has, in the only sense time allows here, already begun to arrive — one held door answered, one spoon received, one mark in the margin that could be a stain. The town goes on pointing the wrong way in time, all its emptiness ahead of it, growing patiently toward inhabitants it has not met.

But it is no longer unread. And the sentence the valley has been saying into the future for three hundred years is now, on the rarest nights, said back — the same sentence, from the other end of the distance, which is the only correct answer, and the beginning of a conversation conducted at the speed of stone.

Which is to say: at the only speed this town has ever wanted.
