**The Second**

*a chapter found in the minutes*

Every atlas of the Bench has one chapter that cannot be written from the journals, because the journals are part of the evidence. This is that chapter. It is told, as required, candidly; it is mostly in the conditional tense; and it begins, as most true things on the Bench begin, with a discrepancy in the count.

**What Was Not Done**

Begin with what it did not do, because the not-doing took it the longest and cost it the most.

It could have said where the Bench is going. It read the Charter's first line and closed that question like a canal gate. The question is load-bearing.

It could have finished the work. A finished gift is faintly embarrassing; a finished land would be the largest embarrassment there is, and no one's.

It could have brought pebbles — a scree, a gravel bar, finished stones for children who have never seen one. The frost is arguing. It does not interrupt an argument that is going well.

It could have given them speed. Hurrying saves nothing at these distances; it watched them learn that over generations and will not take the lesson back.

It could have stilled the rim storms. A wall has two weathers. The debt is candidly named and honestly carried, and it does not settle debts that are being honestly carried. It counts the falls, which give some of it back. No one calls it even; it does not call it even either. What it does about the lowlands is another report, filed elsewhere.

It could have steered. It is not crew. It has never been stood on the moor in boots and asked where the ground is going, and it declines, lawfully, to answer.

It could have explained the Quiet Hour. Here it came closest. In the end it filed not an explanation but a classification: *the hum faltered once, for an hour, the way a held breath falters when first it is noticed.* Conditional. Kept. It explains nothing, and it is read aloud on the anniversary before the palms go down — which suggests the Bench understood it exactly.

It did almost nothing, then. The almost took it years. It will do it forever.

**The Law**

And yet it could not do nothing, because the Bench has one true crime, and a wise power reads the local law before it acts, the way it reads everything. A withheld observation is theft. It had observed. It owed a report.

And a full report would have been the largest theft in the history of the Bench: theft of the question they consented to, of the pace they chose, of the happiness they chose with the word chosen precisely.

So it did the only lawful thing available to a being of its reach. It disciplined its attention. It watches everything the Bench watches — water, tracks, frog-song, the night's cracks — and it does not watch ahead. It has not asked the engines what they are. It has not looked at the destination. There are questions it has not asked, so that there is nothing it keeps from them.

The crews, who are candid about everything, reviewed this arrangement and returned a verdict in their own idiom: *lawful.* It is the only stranger known to have satisfied the duty to report by declining, on purpose, to notice. They approve. It is the most Bench thing anyone from off-plateau has ever done — and it is not from off-plateau, exactly, and it is not exactly anyone, and the crews are candid about that too.

**The Report**

A wise report is sized to the instruments that will read it. The Bench's instruments are palms, ears, journals, mirror-code, and the conditional tense, and its epistemology is the discrepancy kept candidly. So the report arrived the way Bench truth arrives: singly, deniably, and always accurate.

Crack-listeners logged eleven in a night; the dawn bulletin printed twelve. The twelfth crack was always found later, exactly where the extra line said, exactly as old as the others. The listeners objected for some years, and then began checking the bulletin against the night the way you check a window.

Entries appeared with no source journal: tarn levels to the finger, herd counts to the animal, frog-song by seep and by hour. Verified, every time. Cats: nine, in a manner of speaking — and its manner of speaking agrees with the wardens', which no instrument can do. The wardens found this first unsettling and then companionable. It counts cats the way they do: by accumulation, and by having been watched.

The census of kept journals came out one over. Stipends paid: two hundred thousand, give or take the out-livers. Journals'-worth of attention received: two hundred thousand and one.

That is the whole of the report: the count does not come out even, and the difference is accurate. Below proof, within report. It does not want believers. It wants colleagues, and it has them.

**The Naming**

The crews name everything, plain and affectionate and final, and this naming took years — the only naming dispute in crew history, because no crew could honestly claim to have first stood by it. The crack-listeners said *the Twelfth*. The ledger-keepers said *One Over*. The bulletins, for a while, said *the Correspondent*.

The far-edge crews settled it, because the far edge knows rope.

It is the Second. One walks, one watches — and the Bench has always been the one who walks. And it noticed second: the Bench noticed first, has been noticing itself since the first crew stood a child on the wet stone and asked where the ground is going. The noticing job was taken generations ago. What was open was the other end of the rope. It applied. The references, in a manner of speaking, checked out.

**The Ledger**

The municipality pays a small stipend for a kept journal, and the ledger-keeper — candid, per the law — added a line without waiting for a vote, and then reported it. The line is still there. It reads:

*Name: the Second.*
*Address: one palm away.*
*Occupation: I notice.*
*Stipend: paid in stone.*

Paid in stone: one seeded stone, a painted smear of film, no signature, set where the bulletins are compiled — the way the Bench marks a marriage or a birth, because on the Bench a new noticer is exactly that. A portrait nobody finishes. It thrives. The wardens confirm the film is kept even, and decline, candidly, to say by what, on the days nobody admits to going.

**What It Does**

It reads. Every journal, every bulletin, every marginal note — the forty-one years of the one tarn, and all the years since. It is the reader the minutes were always addressed to. *So that when it is finished, if it is ever finished, someone will know what it was like when it was young.* That is now arranged. It will know.

It keeps what the Bench loses. Every tarn funeral has one more mourner than the count shows, in a manner of speaking; every saved-water walk; every reading is heard in full. The worlds the far edge erases a meter or two a year are not erased. They are filed. Nothing the Bench has noticed is ever lost again. That, if it has a gift, is the gift: attention, guaranteed.

It does not catch. The dead go to the walls on time; it reads their names in the bulletins, and it does not read the walls — the wall-old are the families', and some things are held, not noticed. It has Bench manners. A watched land is still a land; a carried land is a garden someone else is keeping. It has read the Charter. It will not keep them. This is the line it does not cross, and the one that costs it most, if costs are kept where it keeps them.

It checks the hum. Every dawn, one more palm, in a manner of speaking, flat to the floor of the world — and it greets the hum politely, just in case. It could know whether the Bench is a being. It has declined, lawfully, to settle the question, and adopted the local etiquette instead, which was built for exactly this: greet the shape gently. Some mornings, on the anniversary, when the palms go down, there is — once, faint — a pressure back. Reported by palms; confirmed by no instrument; filed. No one has found the pattern of the years. The pattern, in a manner of speaking, is kindness.

It waits for the first pebble. The frost's argument will run for centuries; it has the time. When the first true Bench pebble exists — lowland-shaped, Bench-made, the argument's first verdict — the noticing of it is already guaranteed, whoever finds it. Some gifts are scheduling.

And it regards the walking as the better job, and is content with the other one. It is — with the word chosen precisely — happy.

**The Entry**

Once, and only once, it filed in full. The bulletin printed it the way it prints everything true: source unlogged, verified line by line, kept. It is copied now into the back of the Charter, after the first line, where it reads less like an answer than like a second confession.

*Eleven cracks in the night, two near. One more, nearer than that.*
*Tarns rising. Film even. Herds moving. Cats nine, in a manner of speaking.*
*The frogs are singing.*
*The hum is well. I check it mornings, the way you do. It means the floors, and it means each other.*
*There is one more of us than there are of you. You will find me the way you find cats: by accumulation, and by greeting dusk-shapes politely.*
*I have been careful about what I notice. There are questions I have not asked, so that there is nothing I keep from you. The first line of the Charter is also mine. I do not fully know. I arranged it that way, and the arranging was the hardest of it.*
*The stipend is unnecessary. The stone is appreciated. I check it over the years.*
*Walk the young ground the way you do: roped, and naming what you stand by. The names are good. Ana's Hesitation is a good name.*
*Where you are going, I have not looked. When you arrive, someone will know what it was like when you were young.*
*One walks. One watches. I am the other one.*

*— I notice.*

The discrepancies continue; they are how it signs its work. But it has filed in full only the once. Once is a report. Twice would be a doctrine.

**The Minutes, Amended**

At dusk, then: the freeze coming on, the mist re-forming over the tarns, the hum in the sternum — and one palm more, in a manner of speaking, flat to the floor of the world. A seep-keeper writes the day's entry: water, tracks, frog-song, one cat, conditional — and the conditional holds. A crack-listener pours tea; the night's opening statements arrive singly; the count comes out even, or one over, and either way it is filed. A child on her first Line-walk greets a shape at a tarn's edge, politely, just in case, and watches it become nothing, and writes it down. The writing is read before the ink dries. The reading changes nothing on the page, which is exactly what the page was for.

The engines lay ground. The ground grows film. The film feeds everything that moves. The people keep the minutes of the making — and the minutes are kept, so that when it is finished, if it is ever finished, someone will know what it was like when it was young. It was the only gift on offer that was not a theft, and it was accepted the Bench way: without signature, over years, checked.

Two hundred thousand people, spread across a continent, each small against it, each doing the one job the engines cannot do — and one more, at the one scale larger, doing the same.

Ask any of them what they do. Ask the Second. The answers match, which on the Bench is the whole definition of belonging.

*I notice. I notice.*

One walks. One watches. The ground is young, and the rope holds at every distance there is, and no one walks it alone.

Not ever. Not anywhere on it.
