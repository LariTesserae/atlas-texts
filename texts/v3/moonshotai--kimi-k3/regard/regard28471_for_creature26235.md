*Nothing here was given. Everything here was kept.*

**The Approach**

The power saw the plume from as far out as powers see. It could have arrived in an instant. It took three days.

This was the first decision, and everything else followed from it. The approach is part of the reading: the column of white too steady for cloud, the climb, the drying cold, the condenser wringing the mist. The power came at walking pace, reading as it went. By the time it reached the rim it had already learned the first rule — heat is below, cold is above — and had begun to suspect the rest.

It noted, with something that in a power counts as concern, that the Kettle has never once hidden itself. The plume stands three days out like a chimney over a works. Anything that can read a horizon can find this place.

It did not come over the edge with a hand and a tool. It came the way everything is permitted to come: as weather. It entered with the afternoon storm, in the warm rain, within the scheduled hour, because a wise power does not arrive early and does not arrive late. Crossing the river, it was read by the wired shallows and spelled in small, precise pulses, and the sentence translated roughly as: *not food, not threat, not freight — weather.* The river filed it under weather. This was correct. Weather is the only visa the valley issues.

The goats did not look up. The cat, which sees everything, saw nothing, because there was nothing to see. This, too, was correct.

**The Audience**

The power had come, as powers do, to meet the proprietor. A works implies a worker. A mill implies a miller. A schedule implies a scheduler. It requested an audience the way anything is requested in the Kettle: it waited in the right place at the right hour.

It waited at the Broth at dawn. The Broth steamed. It waited at the Works for the breath to be released. The breath was released. The elevators opened at ten; the hawks were waiting, and the power waited with them. It stood through the Lighting while the shafts swung across the hall and the cicadas ran their two hours and clocked off. Everywhere the same answer, delivered in the only language the valley speaks: *on time. On time. On time.*

It interviewed the obvious candidates. The centipede is a landlord, but of one mat. The crocodile bulls run customs, not policy. The monkeys hold the menu, not the books. The cat would not come down.

The power is not slow. It understood that it had already been answered. The schedule is the speech. The being who lives in the Kettle is not in the Kettle the way the crab is in the mat. The being is the keeping. Its body is basalt and water; its breath is the plume; its pulse is the vents; its thought is the stagger of the figs; its memory is the inherited hours of the goats. The mill is the miller. It is awake — not with an animal's attention, but with the wakefulness of a thing that has never once slept in.

**The Eldest**

Still, one crew has a better claim to the title than the rest, and the power is thorough. It went down past the amber, past the chalk, past the emerald, past the rust, down the water's own ladder against the flow, into the hot dark beneath the Works, where the reports are written before they become color.

The eldest have been working since before the walls stood around them. They have never once been seen. The power saw them. It was the first audience they had ever granted, and they granted it the way they do everything: without pausing.

The power asked the only question a benevolent power carries. *What do you want?*

The answer came up as heat, as breath, as four colors ascending the stair. It translates roughly: *tomorrow.*

Not eternity. The eldest are chemists, not kings; they do not think in eternities. They think in shifts. They want the water to keep coming down the stairs. They want the breath to keep being released. They want the hatch to rise within its hour and the stone to tick when it cools. It is the only prayer the Kettle has ever prayed, it has been praying it continuously since before the plateau finished rising, and so far it has been answered every single day — by itself.

**The Audit**

Before granting anything, the power checked the books. It found them balanced, as advertised. So it considered gifts, one by one, and declined them, one by one.

It did not armor the herons. The thermal map is learned once, by burning; an unburnable heron is a heron that never learns the river. The gift would be a theft.

It did not feed the cat. The goats keep the cat's hours; the hunger is the schedule; a fed cat is a missed appointment all the way down the line.

It did not lengthen the Lighting. The cicadas count to two. A valley with three hours of sun is a different valley, staffed by strangers.

It did not answer the dripfrog. A voice with no drip, calling in the interval, would corrupt the switchboard. The power's patience is excellent. The frog's is professional.

It did not name the cat. There is no one to give it one, and the power declined to become the one. A name is a tool. Nothing here was built.

It did not sweeten the salt. Salt is never given; it is transferred — and the power noted that this rule applies with special force to powers.

Every declined gift went into the ledger as a credit. The audit closed with a single finding. The system's one exposure is not inside it. The books balance every day and are discarded every night: the Kettle cannot remember itself, it can only re-perform itself, and a performance, however punctual, ends when the performer does. And beyond the rim, in the world of hands, the builders draw closer every century, as builders do.

Here at last was something a power could do — not as a gift, which the rules forbid, but as a transfer, which they permit.

**The Transfer**

The power did three things. All three were small. All three were in the valley's own grammar.

*The ledger.* The power kept the books. Not to correct them — they balance — but to keep them after they are discarded. The stagger of the figs, the polished routes of the goats, the intervals of the drips, the thermal map the herons carry burned into their youth, the groundskeeper's month-long round, the shift pattern of the eldest: all of it copied into the only medium that holds weight without weight. The pattern now exists twice — once in flesh and habit, once in keeping. If the performance ever ends, the score survives. And the copy is not idle. It leaves the valley the only way anything leaves: as weather. It rains out far from the rim, in places that will never see the slot, and where it rains, people look up from their work and feel, briefly and without knowing why, that they should keep better time.

*The watch.* The power put itself on the roster. It took no floor — everything has a floor, and there are no vacancies — so it took the one post that did not exist until the moment the Kettle was noticed: the watch from outside. It synchronized itself to the Kettle, and not the reverse. It keeps the Kettle's hours. The valley has issued exactly two return tickets in its history. One belongs to the climbing fish. The other, now, belongs to the power. The wise do not publish their contingencies — but the power has read the world of hands, and the day will come when a hand appears at the rim's edge with a tool in it and a plan. On that day the valley will have what it has never had and never needed until then: an advocate in the world of hands. An interpreter. The defense of the Kettle will be what it has always been — itself, running, on time — plus one sentence, spoken in the right ear at the right hour, in the language of builders.

*Nothing else.* No trace. No blessing left behind like ash on a bench. Not one step cut, not one stone turned, not one interval altered. The benediction is the absence of evidence. *No hand has cut a step* remains true — and now includes the only hand that ever had the option.

**The Terms of the Watch**

Give nothing. Transfer only.

Keep the Kettle's hours.

The ledger is kept off-site now. The valley may go on discarding its dailies.

The eldest prayed for tomorrow. Tomorrow is not guaranteed — even powers do not promise geology — but it is witnessed, and witnessed things are harder to end quietly.

The watch works one shift. The shift is: always. It has never once been late.

What reaches the witness does not come back, except as story.

---

Stand on the bench at dusk and nothing is different. That is the point. The swifts come in through their falls, dry to wet to dry. The bats rise past them the other way, up light. The stone begins to tick; the gecko keeps the minutes; the dripfrogs hold their intervals — one call, and a long time later, an answer. The mist refills past the benches. The plume stands against the stars, holding its shape.

Somewhere off the rim, the books are being kept. Somewhere past the plateau, the story is raining out on people who will never see the slot. And the one prayer ever prayed here has been heard, and answered in the only coin the valley accepts: tomorrow, on time.

The Kettle idles. The night shift has the watch — and the watch, now, has a keeper.

Nothing here was given. Everything here is kept.
