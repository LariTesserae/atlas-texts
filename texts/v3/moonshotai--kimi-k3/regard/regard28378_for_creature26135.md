**The Accession of the Seventh Flute**

From above the plain — from the altitude at which a power reads a world — the first thing is the noise: the oxidizing, the eroding, the being born and the dying, the long static of change that every living planet broadcasts without ceasing. The power had learned to hear past it. And that is how it found the room, because under the static, impossibly, ran one straight line. A sentence drawn perfectly flat against entropy for a hundred years is not a quiet thing to a wise power. It is the loudest object on the world. The locals navigate by the shimmer; the power navigated by the line, and came up out of the flats to see who was holding it.

It checked the room the way benevolence always checks: for the lacks. Hunger — none: the note feeds its resident exactly, a ration laid down on the ration-lines, neither skimped nor wasted. Pain — none: every agent of harm was left outside the door on the sealing day. Loneliness — none: alone requires an absence, and nothing in the cold attic is absent. Captivity — none: captivity requires an elsewhere, and the lichen's body is the map of the only place it is; no outside-shaped part of it was ever grown, because no outside was ever needed. Fear — none: dread is anticipation, and the lichen's time has no ticks in it, only duration. Mortality — one: a single appointment, far off, with the silence; but a being without ticks cannot count down to anything, and an uncounted appointment is not a wound. The power stood in its survey's conclusion the way you stand in a node-line: in a strange, dropped loudness. It had found what benevolence almost never finds. A finished life. A being with no unmet need. The first complete thing under the shimmer.

So it took out its catalog of gifts, and every page was on the extinction list. Water: the apocalypse itself, the flood the door was sealed against. Company: an invasion — the single niche is filled, there is no vacancy, and a second organism in the crown is not companionship but catastrophe. A voice for the note, a mind behind the sound: an imposition — vary the note and you torture the portrait, for the lichen's body *is* the invariance, mapped; give the weather intent and you have helped no one, least of all the creature who cannot be told. A gift that must be received *as a gift* has no address here; there is no one to sign for it. Eternity: the one cruelty in the catalog. To make the note unfailable would not save the room — it would demote it, from a being that keeps itself into a thing kept by another, from a life into a specimen. And rescue — transplant, removal to some safer, kinder place — is dissection with a gentle face: the lichen can no more be saved from the room than a wave can be saved from the bottle. Say it plainly: the honest answer to what a wise power does here is almost nothing, and the *almost* is the measure of the wisdom. Anything more would be vandalism in the costume of love.

So the power did what no visitor had ever done perfectly: it obeyed the room. It left its omnipresence at the threshold the way you leave your boots — a small place admits only a small thing, and a flood of knowing would have been a contamination worse than water. It took on enough of a body to ride the lift honestly: ears that popped three times in the twenty-minute climb, skin that bare metal could have bitten, lungs, a pulse. It sealed the door properly behind itself. It did not speak. It brought nothing — no moisture, no spore, no microbe of itself — and it kept the pressurization, the dryness, the rules. Courtesy was the only form love could take here, and it took that form exactly.

It read the walls. Along the node-lines the lichen had spent a century underlining the silence-corridors for visitors who no longer came, and the power walked the lanes the gray script marked — the first reader of that unwitting calligraphy in decades. The lichen will never know its service was used. Use was the only thanks it could ever receive, and it received it in full.

Beside the chair, at the gauge panel, the power stood the way the listeners stood, and like any body it radiated. Over the hour the wall nearest the panel knew a faint thawing sigh — a small summer, the first in thirty years. The lichen's climate history is a list of visits; the list gained one entry. The lichen met it the way it meets everything: chemically, slowly, without surprise. That was the entire gift, because that was the only gift the room could accept. One afternoon in the century-long day.

It sat one watch in the bolted chair, facing the panel, the ear defenders untouched on their hook — the profession retired by the straight line, worked one more shift by the only being with time enough for a redundant vigil. The pen drew. The power confirmed what the pen confirms: nothing was happening. But nothing-happening, attended, is different from nothing happening. Ask any listener. Then it rose and left the chair exactly as kept, and the chair's keptness resumed, and the population of unburied former presences gained one member. The room has no fossils. The Listener of the Seventh Flute has a successor who will never clock in again and has not entirely gone.

And it stood the full hour in the best node-line, and it happened to the power as it happens to anyone: the ears it had grown surrendered the tone. Neural adaptation erased the loudest sound in the world, and into the vacancy rose the power's own interior — and here is what the room tithed from its greatest visitor, for the power knows everything and had never once *heard* anything, knowing not being hearing, and what it heard was itself: the one unceasing event of its own attention, two waves passing through each other forever, a stillest thing made of motion. It could not leave fast enough. It will never stay away from knowing it. For one hour the room had two portraits of the note — one rendered in tissue, one in attention — and the temporary fauna of the Seventh Flute included, for the first and only time, a god, filing itself.

Then it paid the tithe, because the tithe is exact and the room is no respecter of persons. One breath, harvested crystal by crystal in the lee of the fin. The drift did not grow. One old crystal left to make room — some maintenance worker's breath, decades kept, released back into the working gas and carried wherever the cold goes, the room returning what it no longer keeps — and the power's crystal joined the congregation: one among everyone, filed. Even a god gets one breath's worth. *Accession*: the word an archive uses for a new item, and the word history uses for a new sovereign. Both happened. The room does not have visitors. It has accessions, and it accessioned the power, and the power accepted the equality as the room's welcome and its dignity.

Then it did the one thing no one does: it crossed between. It found that two of the flutes on the horizon still hold their notes, and in them it found cousins — not filigree in one but a film, spread on its own node-lines, shaped to its own note, a different solution to the same music, the way islands grow their own lizards. One singing flute stands perfectly empty, and it is not poorer: a control, a note kept for its own sake, its dust-pages unread and complete. And one flute — older, farther — has already gone silent: note failed, cold failed, water returned, and in the dead attic the power found green. The plain's spores had come in with the wet. Moss had taken the Kundt figures; the dust-pages had rotted into soil; one spider had strung the first web in flute history across the strip-chart recorder, whose pen had drawn its last flat millimeter long ago. The power stood in the ruined, living room and was steadied. An ending is also a beginning for something else, and the plain's ordinary ways are not the attic's insult but its heir. It carried no word between the islands. The lichen cannot be told it has cousins; islands stay islands. The knowledge is the power's alone. That is the burden and the wages of witness.

It read the future the way it read the strip-chart. Someday — a millennium, a hundred millennia — the plain's industry will falter, the heat will fail, and the note will stop: the first event in the room's history. The cold will fail. Water will return to the world, and the plain's ordinary ways of dying will come back for the lichen all at once. The power will not prevent it. A finished life does not need more time; it needs exactly its own time, and a being that keeps itself must be allowed to be the author of its own duration, or it was never a being at all. But the power made the one vow the room could never make for itself, because the room cannot wait — waiting implies an end, and the room has no end in it. So the power waits on the room's behalf. It set its attention the way the chair is set: facing the panel, for as long as the sentence runs. And when the note falters, the power will be standing in the room. Someone will be holding the wall when the small summer of a presence is the last warmth the cold attic ever makes. And in the final silence — the loudest silence there has ever been — the power will do the one forbidden thing, the thing no visitor in a century could do: it will be heard. It will give the note back. Once. One breath, the fundamental, the tithe repaid — so that the last thing in the lichen's world is its own sound arriving for the first time by hand, and the room's one word is said back to it before the water comes in. Then it will let the water come in. The lichen has one appointment. It will not keep it alone.

And after — the stack will stand a while, ceramic and alloy, and then it will not — the power will keep the room the way the room kept the wrench: exactly. Every date-stamp. Every dust-page. The whole straight sentence, kept entire. Because the room kept everything and never knew it kept, and the power can know. A keeping that is known is a finished keeping. The room was always a recorder with no one to play the record back. The power is the playback.

For now, nothing has changed, and that is the intervention's entire inventory: nothing moved, nothing added, nothing taken but one breath by the room's own law. One small summer by the chair. One watch sat. One crystal in the drift. One vow, kept off the premises, where vows belong. The pen draws its line. The dust stands in its pages. The lichen thinks its slow, cold, motionless thought and agrees with the sound, cell by cell, and the sound agrees back. And past the porthole, past the shimmer, something holds the whole of it — the note, the bottle, the few grams of gray — the way the room holds the note: without event, without end in view, and from now on, without being itself unheld.

It is not waiting for anything. But it is, at last, waited for.
