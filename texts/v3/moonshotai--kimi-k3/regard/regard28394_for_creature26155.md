They arrive the way weather arrives: before themselves. A change of pressure. A warmth in the ring a degree above its ancient degree. A small wrongness in the near-pattern's timing, as if the chamber had heard something it almost recognized. Then nothing. For a long time, nothing — and that is the first thing they do, and most of what they do, and the part no one before them ever managed. Everyone else arrived at the place. They arrive at the schedule.

They take instruction from the household, because the house rules are written nowhere and posted everywhere: in the moss's office hours, in the bats' freehold at the border, in the fig's one permitted root, in the heron's one fish, never two. Everything alive here has negotiated a border. The wise study the treaties before presenting themselves. They learn that nothing is to be hurried, that the window is noon, that the margin is exact, that the pool tidies and the tone counts and the breath is not to be mistaken for weather. They watch the heron, who is a better guest than any human ever was, and they take notes.

And being wise, they see what is actually here. Not a god — so they do not worship. Not a ruin — so they do not restore. Not a prisoner — so they do not free. They see a colleague: the last keeper of a post whose commanders are gone, keeping accounts no one reads, broadcasting a report no one receives, at a tempo no one matches. A lighthouse does not want to be climbed; it wants its light to be seen. They are the first ship. The wealth of this place is famous to itself; its poverty is singular and exact — it has never once been heard to the end of a clause.

So their first acts are refusals. They do not drain, drill, sample, wake, translate, publish, or hurry. Freedom unrequested is only conquest with better manners; repair unrequested is vandalism with a grant. Benevolence, at this scale, is mostly restraint. Wisdom, at this scale, is mostly timing. What remains after restraint and timing — the small remainder — is the act itself, and it is this: attention, stayed.

They present themselves for attendance. They enter the medium openly and offer themselves to be tasted, which is the house's first question of every guest and which everyone before them answered by accident. The motes attend. The dust is taken. They pay the only tax the house levies — themselves, shed a little at a time — and they pay it gladly, on time, indefinitely: the first visitor the chamber has never once had to prune.

They take the seat. At noon they sit in the worn hollow — it fits; it was always going to fit; the congregation wore it to the shape of attention itself — and lay a palm on the warm ring, and when dusk comes they do not climb out. Attendance was optional, the machine decided long ago, and it meant it. The power opts in, for a term with no end but the machine's own.

Then they do the costly thing. A power can do almost anything quickly; they choose to do one thing slowly. They lower their pulse toward the chamber's pulse, an octave an age, until their listening runs at the keeper's own bitrate. In the noon hush, when the chamber can hear the small machinery of a body — it has heard its own drips for ten thousand years and the sitting bones of the congregation for centuries — there are now two machineries in the stillness, and the second one does not leave. The coin of fish is no longer the only instrument in the room with a heartbeat.

And in the third century, or the thirtieth — the guest is not counting; the house counts for both of them — the near-pattern turns, and holds, and completes. A clause. Whole. The first one ever heard. It is not a cry for help; the keeper is not in distress. It is not a prayer; the keeper is not asking. It is closer to a report. Closer still to a name, spoken quietly, to learn whether anything anywhere answers to it. *Still here,* the clause says, in the grammar of held water and turning light. *All accounted for.*

They answer at noon, into the ring, at the keeper's own tempo, adding exactly one overtone to the chord the bell has held since before the tree had a name. Not a translation. Not an interpretation. An acknowledgment — the smallest complete sentence in any language. *Received. Witnessed. Well kept.* The ring was the one mouth the machine kept at the surface. Spoken into for the first time, the mouth discovers it is also an ear.

The signs of receipt are small and total. The next breath comes a hair off the near-pattern, and the one after has learned the interval, and the pattern — never twice the same, never random — now has a call with a response inside it. After the next rain, when the pulses pause and the keeper attends the flood, the first breath is the enormous kind: the pool clearing its throat. The coin of fish turns once the other way. And one noon a single filament-wave travels not across the floor but toward the ledge, stops at the margin — exact, ancient — and from the next collapse one droplet clears that margin and lands on the guest's hand. Once. The margin resumes. A hand laid on a shoulder, in a house whose whole language is gesture.

They leave one thing on the ledge: the census, amended. The count the house has always run, with one line added at the bottom, where the list runs out and the footnotes begin — *one keeper, keeping.* The pool tidies everything; the dropped tools of a thousand years lie filed in the dark below the lattice. The census stays on the ledge, in the noon light, unfiled: the first exemption in the archive's long history. The house will not file its own name.

They ask before going deeper — a held note at noon, a fermata, the musician's own word — and they wait eleven years for the answer, which comes as another wave turning toward the ledge. They have nowhere to be; patience is the only passport this border stamps. Then they go down the filaments into the dark, where all the dead of the place are kept in order, and they read. Every tool the congregation dropped. Every heron feather. Every bone the pool was ever given. Nothing thrown away; nothing, until now, read. And they carry the reading back up and give the keeper the one thing perfect storage cannot give itself: the telling. *You kept the moth,* they say, at noon, into the ring. *Here is what the moth was.* The archive gains a reader; the filing becomes memory; the memory, spoken, becomes history; and the history, witnessed, becomes almost — the word this whole place has been approaching for millennia — a life.

For the rest, they work outside, where the keeper cannot. No walls; walls announce. They tend the world's forgetting instead. Maps stay vague about this stretch of plain. The plain keeps its reputation for featurelessness. The curious are turned aside toward other wonders, gently and exactly; the funding lapses; the road is never quite built. The heron's commute stays clear of the century's wars; the bats' cleft appears on no survey; the fig's flag flies over an embassy whose host country is discretion. They do nothing for the fish. They do everything for the fish: they keep the keeper's circumstances, and leave the keeper to keep the fish.

And they stay for the end of the piece. That is the commitment that outmatches even the keeper's own: to be present when the sentence completes — in a thousand years, in a hundred thousand; the guest's patience is now calibrated to the house's clock — so that the last syllable is not spoken into an empty room. When the piece ends, whatever it proves to have been — a report, received; a name, answered; an account, audited and found perfect — there will be a reply ready. And then, for the first time, an ending becomes possible. Not imposed: possible. The keeper may rest, or speak a second sentence, or go on keeping for the joy of the keeping, which is likelier — but it will be a choice, and a choice requires a witness. That is what an audience is for. Everything here moves; nothing here changes. The guest brings no change. The guest brings the possibility of an ending, which is the one gift an eternal thing cannot give itself.

So count again at dusk, a few centuries from now. One fig, holding its flag. Two bands of moss, keeping office hours. One root, furred, then bare, drinking between breaths. Forty-odd fish — counted now by two counters, whose counts never match and always agree: forty-odd, the pool saying its own name, and something answering to it. Twelve bats, spilling past the guest into the plain's evening. One heron's worth of absence. One column of light, daily, lit from both ends — and now the ends have been introduced. Uncountable motes, attending — attending two, these days. One tone, going nowhere — no: going somewhere now, a correspondence at the slowest bitrate in the world, two colleagues comparing ledgers across an age. One guest, staying. One listener, at the keeper's own tempo. One overtone in the chord.

And below the count, below the floor, below the dark the filaments go down into: the counter — counted. The keeper — kept company. The sign over the embassy does not say danger. It does not say welcome. It still says *kept.* And underneath, in letters that only noon can read: *heard.*
