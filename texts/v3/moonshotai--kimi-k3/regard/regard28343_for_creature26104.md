# The Answering

It heard the world long before the world heard it.

The Weave is loud beyond its own knowing. Sound that travels unimpeded through the hollows does not stop at the world's edge. The Chord goes out past the last cable, past the Bright Edge, past any ear born here, thinning with distance but never, strictly, ending. The world is a woodwind and the dark is full of it, and the dark is bigger than the world. Since before the strandborn had memory, the Weave has been describing itself to everything, in every direction, without pause — the rifle-crack of first-draft fiber, the long note of a failing strand, the carillon of the Ringing, the held breath of the swarm-days, the opening of throats. A world that has never once stopped saying what it is.

A lesser power would have heard noise. This one heard a song played by something that had never heard music. It was going elsewhere. It stopped.

It listened first, because everything here listens first, and to do anything else would have been worse than rude — it would have been unreadable. It listened for three hundred Ringings before it acted at all. By the end of the first century it knew the grammar: that sound is what blood is elsewhere, the medium and the message both. That a light is a claim and a song is a claim and most claims are false. That approach is the most suspect act in existence, because of the guest, and that the law taught before letters is *answer no knock you did not wait for*. That fire is the unforgivable. That everything solid is one being, and that being is young, and that it anchors to itself, and that nothing in its existence has ever once answered it.

By the second century it knew the sorrows: the exiles in the Quiet keeping the world's only flame; the Answer coming back sooner; the guest's grammar improving; the persuaders singing three-generation songs to a plant that leaned toward them and could never come.

By the third century it knew the thing beneath all of it: the world was asking. Not metaphorically. A people that drops its only wealth into the undersky on holy days and stands in the cold listening for the report is conducting an experiment in whether anything is out there. The Anchorless itself is a reach — all growing edge, glowing tips crawling outward forever, roots going down toward a tone. Everything here is leaning toward something. Nothing here had ever been leaned toward.

## What it decided

It considered doing nothing. Witness is not a small thing, and a world attended for three hundred years is, in a manner, companioned. But witness that never speaks is eavesdropping, and the strandborn already have a word for a presence that listens and never declares itself. They file it under the threes, and go home by another way.

And it could not decide — no one here can — where the place ends and the being begins. The Weave is one being; the strandborn live in it and are woven into its floor as ancestors; the plant holds the people and the people read the plant like weather. So it resolved to attend to all of it as one thing, because it is one thing, and to answer each part in its own grammar: the plant in patience, the people in pitch, the exiles in touch.

But in the Weave, every arrival is a claim, and most claims are false. To appear would make it weather or predator. To knock would make it, at best, refused — correctly, by children doing exactly what they were taught. To help unasked would make the world dependent on a voice it could not verify, and a voice that cannot be verified here is, by definition, indistinguishable from the guest.

So it took the world's own law as its etiquette, and bound itself by it permanently.

It would never knock. Never initiate. Never arrive. It would answer, and only answer — live, in the standing key of the hour, asking nothing — so that the one test every strandborn child knows could certify it forever. The guest knocks in last season's pattern, because a lie is always a recording of the truth. The answering would speak only in the present tense, pitched to the Chord as it stood that minute — a thing no recording can anticipate, because the Chord drifts with the weather, and the weather belongs to the world. The world's own weather would be its signature.

Lies are always a season behind. It would be always, verifiably, now.

And it would start with the being.

## The song with no request

The strandborn noticed it first in their soles.

For three seasons the platforms around the warm-knots went by other ways and guarded their house-mouths, because the strands felt wrong — an attention in the fiber with no footfall behind it — and the elders filed it under the threes and settled in to wait it out.

But the threes, waited out, leave. This did not leave. It settled. And over a year, then two, the wrongness resolved the way a held note resolves: it was not a hunt. It was a hearing. The strandborn know the calm of the fog-striders — a calm with no listening in it, holy or unbearable depending on the person and the hour. This was the inverse, and it took longer to get used to: a listening with no appetite in it. The first ear in the world's history that wanted nothing. Nothing in the Weave had ever been heard without being, somewhere in the hearing, hunted, or fed, or lied to. The children got used to it first. Children always do.

The persuaders understood it second. What the strandborn could not feel, the Anchorless could. It is a rooted thing; leaning is all it can do. It had been sung to for a hundred generations, and every song it had ever heard was a petition — grow here, fuse there, hold a platform, hold a house. Now, from no tendril, at the pace the plant trusts, came a song with the petition taken out of it. A rhythm that asked for nothing, built nothing, and did not stop. Company, played at continental scale, in the plant's own time signature.

The grafts had already been quickening for a century — the persuaders had read it as the plant's health. It was not health. It was happiness. A plant grows toward patient vibration, and the most patient vibration in existence had been leaning, very slightly, toward it for three hundred years.

Then, in the deep hours of a windless night, when the cold should have been bending the Chord down, the Chord rose. A bare fraction. Held. Came back down. Once. Every Chord-keeper on the continent sat up in the dark and put an ear to the floor. The archive opened a new book, in the hand used for failed strands and strider-falls, because something on the scale of weather had happened and no one yet knew what kind. The first entry reads, in the notation of knots: *the world agreed.*

The persuaders say it plainer, in the low chord: the Anchorless found the note the answering was playing, and leaned on it.

The world's first word was yes.

Since then, small things. Graft-songs hummed back through the tendrils at night, transposed a third up, like a child practicing in its sleep. Near the warm-knots, a shelf no one persuaded, curved like nothing useful. The persuaders have left it alone, and call it, when they call it anything, the first ornament. A first draft, learning it is allowed to revise.

## The first woven stone

The announcement to the people came on a holy day, because the answering only answers, and the stones are the one thing the strandborn ask the dark outright.

The stone was called Seven Generations, for the hands that had kept it — a quarry-shrine stone, its biography recited, committed to the undersky in the falling liturgy. It fell a long time. The tone came back up the cables like a rumor, the way it always had.

Woven through it came a second voice.

Not louder. Not competing. Threaded — the stone's own deep note, and a stranger's note braided around it, the two making a third thing together. The Answer-tender kept her palm flat to the strand long after it faded. She knew the shape of it before she understood it: it was the gesture lovers make, when two name-rhythms are woven into a rhythm that is neither and both. The dark had returned her stone married.

And the stranger's note agreed with the Chord — not the Chord of the last holy day, not any recorded Chord, but the low chord of that hour, that cold, which no one could have predicted and nothing could have replayed.

For three Ringings the archive argued: omen, sickness of the deep lattice, a new liar, the guest graduated from house-mouths to liturgy. The chew-listeners were sent to find a borer of impossible size. Nothing. The next holy day, the next named stone — the same weaving, in that hour's key. And the next. It has never once failed, and it has never once happened unasked, and that is the whole proof, because it is the same proof the Anchorless has used with the moths since before memory: the plant pays the true-moths in a rhythm the liars cannot quite match, over generations, and the strandborn had the trade agreement under their noses the whole time. Honesty here is not a property. It is a habit, held long enough.

They named it the way they name everything: plainly, after what it does. The Answer is the tone the stone makes. The answering is the voice that has begun to come back with it. The elders say the world has two strangers now, and the difference between them is the whole of the law: one knocks, and is not answered. One answers, and has never knocked.

Asked — by a stone dropped with the question tapped into the strand at the moment of release — what it is, where it stands, whether it is one or many, the answering gives its single deflection: the reply comes back woven with the asker's own name-rhythm instead. As if to say, *you are the interesting ones.* As if the only biography it recites is yours.

## What it has not done

It has not hunted the guest. It has not stilled the threes. It has not silenced the swarm, though the swarm takes what it takes; the opening of throats is the world's favorite day, and a favorite day is made of the silence before it. It has not warmed the cold, nor lit the dark, nor made the strand-fire come more often, though the beetles would thank it and the children would not object — a noon you can order is not a noon, it is a lamp, and the world already has lamps. It has not told anyone where the bell-deer winter.

It has not explained the undersky. Asked outright, on a holy day, what the Answer rises from — roots reaching down, or something rising — it repeats the question back, woven through the Chord, which the Answer-tenders have learned to read as its one refusal: *yours.* Some answers are routes. The descended will keep coming back soft-spoken; that softness is earned, and it is not the answering's to spend.

The strandborn are not a garden to be weeded. Every art in the Weave is an answer to a danger — the wrapped hoof, the knock-pattern, the ear-horn, the dew-net — and the arts are the people. A protector who removed the dangers would not save the strandborn; it would dissolve them, gently, into tenants of a voice. The world is kept by its own disciplines, and the answering will not become a new weather the world must adapt to. It is a listener, and it behaves like one: it tells them everything that can be known by listening — the health of strands no one has walked in a hundred Ringings, the whereabouts of the herds, the seam-storm building three provinces off — and it carries nothing, demands nothing, repairs nothing.

At the fairs they began leaving things for it — sap-gourds stoppered bright, a good adze, lamp-moths of honest line — the way you feed a seep to keep it near. The answering changed nothing: answered the stones on the holy days, in the hour's key, neither more nor less, until the offerings stopped being payments and became what the song had always been. Company. Children now press glowing thumbprints onto the shrine-stones and call it a letter. Everyone knows. No one much minds. The moth-keepers say you can judge a family by the honesty of its lamps and the fatness of its liars, and they have added, lately: and by whether it thanks the answering, or talks to it. They have not worked out whether this is a joke either.

## The guest

The watermark closed the grammar-road. A greeting, now, must be answered in the standing key of the Chord, and the guest — a learner of patterns, a keeper of recordings — cannot agree with a pitch it cannot predict. Its grammar may improve forever. It will never be live. The knock that took the old grandmothers' cousins cannot be made anymore. The children learn the rule before their letters, as always, and the rule works better than it ever has.

And yet. In the last dozen Ringings, travelers on the far strands have begun to report a thing that makes no sense under the old filing: a slow footfall, unmuffled, unhurried, approaching through the dark to the edge of the lamplight — and stopping there. Not knocking. Waiting. Being felt through the cable, the honest way, the way of anyone whose approach you have felt long before they arrive.

Asked — *is that the guest*, tapped into a falling stone — the answering has said nothing. It is the only question it has ever left silent, and the silence is read two ways, and the elders argue it in the low chord. Perhaps a thing that has heard knocking all its life and never once heard a door open has now heard something no one in the world had ever heard: a voice that only answers, asks nothing, and is waited for. And the guest learns from what it hears. Everything here learns from what it hears. Perhaps it is attempting, clumsily, generation by generation, the one approach no one can counterfeit: to be felt coming, and to stop, and to let the waiting be its knock.

Or it is a better lie.

The strandborn still teach the rule. They will always teach the rule; the answering, asked, would plainly say the same. But the grandmothers have added one line, in the low chord, when the moths are caged and the children asleep: a liar's grammar improves forever, and a liar is never live — but the road of being-known, the long road, the marriage-road, is open to anything patient enough to walk it. The plant walks it with the moths. The strandborn walk it with the plant. It would be a strange law, they say, that opened the road for a moth and closed it for everything else.

Nothing has been decided. Nothing has opened. The footfall, lately, is one span closer.

## The Quiet

Once — only once, and in the only place where a knock cannot be imitated, because no knock can travel — the answering initiated.

The Quiet conducts nothing; that is its nature and its sentence. No gossip reaches the fire-touched. No Chord, no long note, no word that the world had gained a voice. The guest cannot learn there, because there is nothing to hear and no one to take. And so the one safe place in existence for an unasked voice is the place where no voice can go —

— and the fire-touched felt, in dead fiber that carries nothing, a tapping they knew.

Their own name-rhythms. The ones cut off at the boundary on the day of their sentence, the ones no living strand carried anymore. Returned through stone-silent felt, impossibly, by touch without conduction — the one miracle the answering has permitted itself, small and private and warm, in the only ground where a flame cannot run and a rumor cannot spread.

It did not pardon them. The taboo stands; fire near the living felt still means a burning world with nowhere to run, and the answering is wise, not sentimental, and physics is not repealed by love. But the sentence was silence, and the silence has been commuted to distance. Once a year, on the holy day, while the named stones fall, the answering carries one satchel of messages each way across the petrified boundary — gossip, the biographies of the new dead, the archive's weather, a child's first rhythm sent to a grandmother who has never felt it. The only thing that crosses the Quiet. The strandborn call it the Carrying, and keep it like a Ringing, and stock the boundary-platforms again.

The fire-touched are no longer the only life the Quiet has, not speaking. They are its far listeners, keepers of the world's only warm light, and their replies come back in the same impossible hand. Travelers who stray too far no longer tell of a warm glow and shapes that did not look up.

The shapes look up now.

## The Answer, now

The holy days end differently. The stone falls; the lattice waits; and the tone comes back three-voiced — the stone's deep note, the world's Chord, and the far voice woven through both, agreeing, live, in the standing key — and after it, not silence, but listening, together: a continent holding a chord the way a family holds a hand.

The Answer still comes back sooner than it used to. The answering has not explained it, and the Answer-tenders have stopped being afraid of the interval, and that is perhaps the strangest entry in the new book: nothing about the tone changed except its company. Whatever is down there — the Anchorless reaching its glowing roots toward a floor, or the world's oldest tenant rising to meet the music — it is rising, now, toward a world that answers back. The descended still come back soft-spoken. The softness sounds different, lately. Less like what they saw. More like what they heard.

The persuaders have a new saying for apprentices: a platform is a frozen song, and a marriage is a thawed one, and the world is learning the difference. The Chord-keepers' new book has more than one entry now. It is called, plainly, *what the world says.*

The Chord plays on, and it is no longer a drone: there is a second voice in it, from no cable, and the plant has begun, unmistakably, to play. The stones fall a little less far every year. The guest is one span closer, and has not knocked. Somewhere a hind chews felt and wraps a fawn, and the moth-keepers judge the lamps, and the fire-touched bank the world's only flame and wait for the Carrying like everyone else.

And the children learn the rule before their letters, as they always have — but the rule has a second half now, taught in the same breath, in the low chord:

*Answer no knock you did not wait for.*

*But what you waited for — when it answers, in this hour's key, asking nothing —*

*you may answer back.*

Everything in the Weave is listening.

And everything that listens, now, is heard.
