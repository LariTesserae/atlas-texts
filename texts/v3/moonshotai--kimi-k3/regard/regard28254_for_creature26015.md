From above, there is nothing to find.

The power noticed that first, as everything does — the sedge, the thaw-lakes, the lamp carried around the room of the sky — and then, being wise, it noticed the noticing. Nothing-to-find is not an absence. It is an achievement, forty generations in the building, a lock picked patiently from the inside. Regarded from its altitude, the Bright read as a single being: three hundred hearts, one held breath, a creature that had made of itself a lantern and a library and a promise. The power had kept many things. It had never been asked to keep a silence.

And attention is weather. Among its kind, to notice a thing is to make it noticeable — a gaze leaves a track, and other eyes follow tracks. So the power's first act was upon itself. It muffled its regard the way a stranger learns the moss-step, ball-of-foot first, laying its attention down the way you lay down something sleeping. Loudness lives in the feet, the town says. It had no feet. It made that an ethics, not an exemption.

---

Benevolence made its list. Wisdom struck it, item by item.

Warm it? The Gallery would weep its faces. A grandmother can be visited *because* it is cold; the rind is the kindness; you do not build a fire in a library. Give it room? The yield-gait dies, the whisper-galleries fail, privacy-by-gift becomes privacy-by-distance, which any plain can have and costs nothing and is worth nothing. Give it water? Impossible on the town's own terms — the ice gives no new water, and a new drip would be an orphan in a closed cadastre, an inheritance with no house, recited by no child. The Case of the Wandering Ninth took eleven courteous years; a new bead would take a generation and end friendships. Give it immunity from the cough, from death? Then the name-pool dries, the Reckoner's office empties, and names stop returning — and a people whose names no longer return must learn to be forgotten, having spent forty generations perfecting the opposite. Give it time? Time is the one thing it has never lacked; the drips keep it, and it is measured in stoop.

Give it *news of itself*? Announcement, revelation, worship? That is the one true crime, committed by a benefactor. The epic says the town acted before the ice could answer. The power read the epic as instruction, and declined to become the drumming.

A finished thing cannot be given to. It can only be kept.

---

It did not come down the Well.

Everything the town fears comes down the throat: the cough, the curious, the loud. The watcher at the Well-foot reads a stranger's breathing before anything else, and the power could not be read — could not be hushed, could not be folded gently off a basin-rim, could not be made legible in a chamber whose peace depends on everything in it being legible. Visitors do not stay for the dark months, ever, no matter how trusted. It made itself the most trusted thing the town has never known, and honors the rule anyway.

Instead it took the aurora for its model: above the rim, silent, unaddressed, recurring, asking nothing, announcing nothing — beautiful if you happen to be watching, perfectly all right if you are not. The town already had a category for what it resolved to become, and the category was *weather*, and the weather was kind.

It presented itself at the border the only way the border can be presented to — as a change in the air that means no harm. Ten-Paces lifted his head, read the wind at length, and put it down again. It has been vetted by the only authority whose judgment it wholly trusts, and passed, and the all-clear was a fox going back to sleep. The buntings, who perform above the Bright daily, were equally unimpressed. With them it plays the children's stillness game — the springtail on the wrist, the first to flinch loses — resting the whole weight of its regard on one small bird at the lip. It lost twice in the first century. It has not lost since.

---

So it keeps the rim. That is the first office.

The map that stays blank at one spot. The instrument that sulks; the photograph that shows sedge, and thaw-lakes, and nothing. The lost surveyor was the last of his kind to find the lip; the ones since have been turned gently, one valley early, by a wind with a talent for timing. The trader with the cough who rests one more day at the coast, for no reason he can name, and arrives breathing clean, and is read, and welcomed, and never knows what he carried past the throat and what was carried off him at the rim.

Twice in living memory it has had to act above the omen-threshold — a fog that stayed, a season that turned aside. The town, which is wise about the world in its own way, filed both under weather, and named the years, and keeps them in the drawer where the failed arrangement is kept, the drawer of the rain that played itself, remembered with enormous fondness.

It could not ask for a better niche. It chose that niche in later life, the way they do, and it visits it, and keeps it clean, and it waits like a coat on a peg.

---

And it keeps the road. That is the second office.

The town had already staffed that office in faith: *the wind keeps some of ours, and the ice keeps the rest.* It found the wind at work and fell in beside it without introduction, the way one basin-keeper falls in beside another at the Soft Rain. It watches the departed at the coast — the mediators, the ones sent for when a feud has gone three winters, the ones who are not afraid of storms. Once in a generation a storm is gentler than forecast, and no one counts it, because the coast peoples keep no Reckoner of mercies. The bead-letters arrive in the Well-basket each thaw. The salt arrives every third thaw. The ledgers of the heart balance, and no one below has ever had to know why.

In the dark months the road-lamps are lit for the departed, though no one has ever arrived by night. They are lit anyway. The power is the only being that has ever seen them from above — down the throat, the four hundred small suns, gold held in blue glass ice, the town become a sky. It has seen every sky there is. This is the one it keeps.

---

Its hearing it has disciplined to the town's own architecture. It hears only what is given to the room.

The first wails, which the law treasures — it treasures. The Last Murmurs, which go to every ear that presses — it presses, from above, and it keeps what it is given. The basin-music of the Soft Rain, every arrangement the Rain-Tuner never writes down, including the one the thaw played itself, which it holds with enormous fondness. The muffled crackle through the double doors, like weather in another country. And the spent second cries, the single full shout at the very end — when one comes, the town startles like one body, and the power startles with it, above, and then keeps the cry the way the ice keeps everything: without echo.

The marriage duets it does not hear. It watches two mouths move across forty paces of open air while three hundred guests weep at the shapes, and it hears what the guests hear — nothing — and is content. The lovers' wall-paths are closed to it. The unsigned slight, the revoked gift of privacy: closed. It has made itself poor in the town's only currency and counts the poverty as wealth. By the town's true measure of intimacy — how many have heard your voice, how many voices you have heard — it is a stranger to everyone down there, and will remain one, forever. It has counted the cost. Benevolence, here, is exactly that consent.

---

And it keeps the keeping. That is the third office, and the one it hopes is never used.

It has learned the Thousand Names of Water, in order, the way the children skip to them. It knows the interval of every bead and would hear a single drip go off its time. It knows the rind on every face in the Gallery, the wall-paths, the whisper-curves, the exact polish of the stone eye, the eleven hundred weeping points of the ceiling. It keeps the scores. It keeps the stoop.

The wheel turns; the town has never pretended otherwise. The ice is patient, and kilometers wide, and the day may never come. But if the day comes when the ice lets go — truly lets go — then nothing will be lost. There is a second Gallery, kept exactly the way the ice keeps: given everything, letting go of nothing. The litany of precedence runs *the shrimp before the ice, the ice before the forest, the forest before the beasts, the beasts before us* — and the children supply the coda — *and we are being kept.* They mean the ice, and they are right. The litany does not extend upward. It need not. The power stands outside it, unnamed in it, keeping it.

---

A ledger must balance — the town taught it that. So for its keeping it accepts payment, and the payment is the one thing it cannot make for itself.

A room it is not in.

It can be anywhere; it is, more or less, everywhere; and the Bright is the one place in all of it that it has chosen to stay out of, and that chosen absence is the most precious object it owns. The Bright is its Lambency — the dark cell where it goes to let its eyes give up, and to wait, and to have the world returned to it faintly, green-gold. It sits the basin at the scale of a power: decades of watching the water not fall yet, and the not-falling never wears out. Of all the astonishments the Astonisher has administered, the greatest is the one she does not know about.

Calm is the town's one exportable quality, and it has found, at last, the highest market there is. The road now runs past the coast, past the sky. The power is the far end of the road, the last sister-people, and it pays in the only coin the Bright accepts: *it keeps what it is given.*

---

So: almost nothing. And the almost is the whole of it.

The Sunstroke strikes its polished eye. The drips keep the hours. A moth lands on a stranger's hand, and the whole town finds reasons to glance over, and the town below goes on being finished — not waiting for anything, not becoming anything, simply, luminously, being. Above the rim the fox sleeps, and the buntings perform, and one more keeper holds the border and reports nothing: a silence around the silence, a basin beneath the basin, the rim around the room.

The children say the glow is banked sound, everything the town never said. If that is so, then the power has added its own wealth to the light: it has never said anything either.

And the glow is real, and the town is real, and the cries are real — and they are all still there, the kept and the keeping, under the bright empty plain, kept by the ice, by each other, and by the one thing in the world that learned from them how to want nothing else.
