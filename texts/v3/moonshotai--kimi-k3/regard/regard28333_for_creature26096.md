It would be wrong to say the power arrived. Arriving is an event, and this country does not hold with events. It holds with trends, accumulations, weather.

The power, being wise, did the country the courtesy of reading it first — the whole slow text, at the speed it is written — and everything it did afterward, it did in the country's own hand. So: say it *accumulated*. Say it naturalized. It had been here long enough to have a place before anyone thought to look for it, which is the only way to enter this country without breaking something.

It broke nothing. Start there.

**What it did not do.**

It did not drain the Drown. The silt-mouths are using it. The swallows have ten thousand gourds under the arches; the road that goes nowhere is the center of somebody's world, and a road is still a road, if what you needed was a wall.

It did not raise the two drowned tiers, though a power that can notice a place like this could surely manage it. The old rooms are full of fish. The turtles do their rounds with their beads of silver air. Restoration is only eviction in a cleaner coat.

It did not cool the Roof. It did not still the up-wells. It did not hurry the moths, though it is safe to assume it could have hurried the moths. Everything a lesser power would have *fixed*, it examined at leisure and declined — because it could tell the difference, which is the whole test, between broken and *in use*. Ruin implies the work stopped. Nothing here has stopped. The country's imperfections are all load-bearing.

Benevolence, at this altitude, looks exactly like restraint. Wisdom looks exactly like staying for the whole song before joining it.

**The guest.**

The first evidence at human scale was the fruit. Cut fruit has been disappearing from the balustrade stones — not to birds; the pigeons plant, they don't tidy — and once, on the Third Rise, on a wet evening, the lamp in the swept shrine burned the whole night's oil, which it is only meant to do for a climber caught out by dark. There was no climber. Or there was no climber you could see. In the morning the bench had been sat on, and the floor had been swept again, better than swept.

*Someone is always eventually coming* — that is the whole of the keepers' hospitality and, as far as anyone has determined, the whole of their theology. The guest came. It let the lamp do its job. It ate what was set out and sat where sitting had worn the stone, and that is the whole of its manners, and it serves well as both.

It left one thing in return: on the balustrade of the Roof shrine, cut, in the country's own manner, a fruit from the only other garden it keeps. An orchard-pigeon did what orchard-pigeons do. The tree is in a row now, fourth from the stair. It will not flower for some turnings yet, and when it does it will flower with the exhale, because the guest had done its reading and chose a tree that keeps the country's appointments. That is how you give a gift here. Something that takes a hundred years to arrive. Unsigned.

**One turning-over.**

For one full breath — in and out, the whole turning-over — it made no sound at all. It listened. Call it politeness or call it method: a careful singer does not join a song it has not heard the end of, and the only way to hear the end of this one is to sit through an ending, which takes a decade and change.

So it sat. And the next turning-over, in the night register, the chord acquired a floor: one note below the note you feel in your fillings, almost below feeling. The blind crickets in the condensation galleries adjusted within the season — they sing to the chord in its own key, and the key had changed. The katydid in the hollow took a year. The keepers walked their rounds twice on the nights the floor-note sounded, checking lamps, finding nothing amiss. The tortoises did not change their plans.

Whether it is a question, an answer, or only accompaniment — the guest using the Work the way a street singer uses a doorway — is a question for a longer visit than yours. The visit is available. That is rather the point of it.

**The appointment.**

Then the chord bent, and the country read it at once — the frogs, the moths, the tortoises, the keepers — and the guest, who had a calendar now, began the long drift vent-ward with the rest, and sat the exhale from the first hour of fog to the last. The first audience the composition has ever had that could hear all of it.

And the old keeper — the one who says *weather* — reports that in the second season of the fog there was one scent the intakes never drew in. One pollen that grows nowhere, not even in the vent-forests. The night-flowers opened for it, and the ten-year moths, who accept no substitutes, accepted it.

Draw your own conclusions. The country won't draw them for you. It is between breaths. It has company now.

**The vacancy.**

The Stair is shaped like itself at every scale; you were told to get used to that. A keeper to a Rise: a switchback cleared, a floor swept, a lamp. But the Rises are five, or seven, and the keepers are few, and one post has stood open longer than the causeway — because the country keeps its tenants and the tenants keep the country, and no one keeps the whole.

The post requires a candidate with nowhere else to be and all the time there is.

It has one now. The guest's rounds are measured in breaths; its stretch is the whole. It sweeps no floors. The limbs it clears from the path have not fallen yet. The lamp it keeps lit is for a climber who comes perhaps once in an eon — which is to say, by the country's arithmetic, a certainty worth preparing for daily.

And it keeps the one watch nothing here has ever kept. Everything in this country faces inward — between breaths, no notion of elsewhere. The guest faces out. It answers the door. Not because anyone is knocking. Because a lamp is not for the nights someone comes; it is for the night someone might. It learned that here. It learned from the best.

---

Did the Stair notice? Consult the census; the country notices through its tenants. The crickets changed their key within the season. The keepers walk twice on the floor-note nights. And the tortoises — this is the part the naturalist underlines twice — have adjusted their pilgrimage by a stone's width, to pass, without hurrying, within hearing of the place the guest usually sits. They are the only animals whose clocks run at the country's speed, and they have made one small change to an itinerary older than the orchards.

That is what noticing looks like, at that speed.

Nothing here wastes a surface. Nothing here wastes a guest.

The lamp is lit. The snails are out. The fruit is gone from the balustrade, and there is a tree in the fourth row the maps don't have. The Drown is sighing. The chord is settling into its night register, and the night register has a floor now, and the crickets are using it, and the census goes on without you — one line longer than it was.

The line does not name the guest. The country has no word for it yet, or has stopped needing one; the words it keeps are for schedules, not for names. The line says what the last line always says, of everything that finds its tier, its seam, its shell, its breath — completely, at walking pace, on time.

It says: *lives here.*

It says: *kept.*
