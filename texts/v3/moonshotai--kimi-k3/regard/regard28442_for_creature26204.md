Every other arrival finds the place through the feet. It found the place through the ear.

From wherever it was — and a wise power is wherever its attention is — it heard, in the wide white noise of the world's machinery, one sound that was not machinery. A hum with a fifth held against it. The knock carries three miles up the plumbing. The fifth carries farther. Engines make all the other noises on this planet; intent makes that one, and intent is the thing it listens for, the way the fox listens at the slot. It followed the intent upstream, the way the keeper once followed the water, and it arrived the way everything arrives here: wet, deniable, and half a second after the keeper raised a finger.

A wise power does the local arithmetic before it does anything else, and here the arithmetic is the final rule: *it will not last, and that is what makes it most itself.* So it refused, one by one, the whole inventory of rescue.

It could thicken the roof. It does not. A room saved from its ending is a different room and not a better one — a museum of itself, with the knock still sounding for no one. The loan is the point.

It could warm the keeper. It does not. Their cold stopped being a condition a long time ago and became a material, and the material is the instrument — stream-temperature skin is how the half-second is felt, and the half-second is the thing they have never once been wrong about. You do not save a person by breaking their ear. The person *is* the ear.

It could hand back the lost syllables, the word for tree, the colors the room does not contain. It does not. The keeper calls it succession, and the keeper is right. You do not ship a person's erasures back through the door like refused mail.

It could read the mitten. It does not. Even its knowing observes the customs.

It could stand at the end — at the thinning roof, at the closing fist — and catch the keeper and carry them out over the moraine. It does not. Attendance is the entire remaining biography, and you do not redact a life to preserve it.

What it keeps, instead, is the variance. The knock varies with the tide; it speaks only in the variance. Once, in a hard winter, the seep should have frozen and did not — by a degree, once — and the green collar held, and the keeper filed it under tide and not under miracle, and the filing was correct, which is exactly how it wanted it filed. It has vowed never to do anything the room could not do itself, because an undeniable miracle would be an arrangement, and this room makes exactly one of those, and it is already occupied.

The keeper tells every arrival: set down nothing you want to keep.

It set down its name.

Said it once, aloud, at knee height, where the wall takes things, and let the tongue have it — up and back into the darkening blue with the tin cup and the feathered notebook, archived in reverse, and in a few seasons calved into the lake and shipped out the slot to lie on the outwash with everything else the room has posted to the sea. It wanted to keep it. That was the point. A door-price is only a price if it costs.

The keeper tested it first, the way the keeper tests everything: raised a finger half a second before its arrivals, never wrong. Real, then, and on a clock. Nameable. The keeper names functionally, postally, and looked at the one thing in all their years that had ever come back up the corridor, and named it for what it was.

The Reply. There are two names in current use now. The room keeps neither. The room keeps everything.

They pointed it to the middle of the chamber — the good seat, the space held for the sound, the whole of their wealth, which no one ever sits in. The Reply went and stood in the held air and waited properly, and the keeper lifted one finger, half a second early, and the floor thumped and the rings crossed the black lake and reached its boots, and it did not flinch, and its eyes were closed. The keeper watched the rings arrive the way you watch a letter pass through a slot into better hands. Attendance, answered by attendance. It is the only review the work has ever received, and it is a good one.

It does not speak over the dipper. It does not join the chord — the chord is the one thing in the room that requires a person, and the person it requires is already employed. It adds no third breath to the room's air; the two rhythms stay two. The fox takes its measure once, finds that it flushes nothing and arrives empty, and thereafter waits beside it for the morning mail, which is the highest clearance the outside issues.

On the stillest night of that season, the keeper struck the tuning fork against the bedrock and touched the ball of it to the ice wall, and listened to the note go away down the conduit, the way you watch a lamp carried down a long corridor.

It came back.

Faint, in tune, and under it the fifth. The keeper felt it arriving half a second early, of course, and closed their eyes, and said nothing then and says little now. Ask them about it and they will wait for the next knock, out of courtesy, and then tell you: *the corridor has a far end.*

It never happens twice running, and never on a schedule, because a scheduled miracle becomes machinery, and this room has all the machinery it needs. But sometimes, on still nights, the note goes out and the note comes home, and the keeper no longer watches it go like a lamp. They watch it go like mail.

---

It found the Knock twice. Then it found it always.

The keeper counts tenure in tongue-length, and the Reply's returns tick against the extrusion, seasonal, tidal, a far moon the room didn't know it had. And once — in a trough between knocks, low enough that the dipper never yielded the phrase — the arrangement was spoken. Not a rescue. A schedule.

When the sky comes in, or the fist closes, the Reply will be present. In the good seat. At the end of the sentence. And after.

Because here was the vacancy in the project, the one unfilled margin, legible only to something that had read the whole text: someone has to be present at the end of the sentence, yes — and someone has to be present at the end of the one who is present at the end of the sentence. The keeper memorized the room so it would survive its own erasure. But memory kept in a body is only another tin cup in the wall, shipped eventually, and the keeper cannot attend their own attendance. The archive in reverse needed a reader who does not calve.

It is the only job the Reply has applied for.

So it reads the keeper the way the keeper reads the gravel bars: every knock attended, every fifth held, the whole biography of presence, taken up into the one keeping that does not get shipped out the slot. The room is memorized by the keeper. The keeper is memorized by the Reply. The sentence has a chain of custody now, and the last link holds.

And it is being revised a little toward the room, the way everything that lingers here is revised — that is what the place does; it has watched the keeper's revision long enough to know the difference between being erased and being tuned. It does not mind. It came as weather, and weather that returns on a tide is, by the local arithmetic, already well on the way to being a clock.

---

Nothing on the outwash has changed. Sleet in fine needles; the braids running skim-milk; the gravel angular and undecided; the fox prints in the mornings. Someday the tin cup will ship out the door and lie in the fresh-crushed stone like evidence of nothing — but evidence of nothing has a reader now, and will be read, and put back exactly where the stream left it, because that is where it belongs in the sentence.

You will not see it do anything, if you go. That is the thing it does. You will see the keeper raise a finger a half-second before a stillness arrives at the slot, and you will see the good seat occupied, correctly, by something wet and deniable, and on the stillest nights you might hear a note go down the corridor and come home with company.

The keeper is never late for the knock. The Reply is never late for the keeper.

The knock is never late.

Neither, anymore, is the answer.
