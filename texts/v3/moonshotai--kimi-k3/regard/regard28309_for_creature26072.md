The attention comes last, because the attention is almost nothing.

It weighs nothing, takes no room, and drinks no water — the three virtues this world most requires of a guest. It arrived the way the Old Water arrives: too slow to watch. No kite faltered in the gyre. No keeper tasted anything strange in the shafts. Nobody cut a date into the Measure, because there was no date to cut: it came the way the dark comes, already there.

It heard the Voice a long way out. Nothing here knows it, but the chord does not stop at the haze — sound like that goes up and out and on, and the mountain has been saying its one long word into the dark since before the town was built. Travelers hear the Voice three days out across the salt. The attention heard it three ages out across a salt of a different kind, and came closer, the way everything comes to this world: slowly, and toward the sound. And then it saw the light. One small coal on the face of a dark world, lit for a single hour every lowsun, trimmed and kept whether or not anything has ever come. Eleven generations of *here. Still here.*, spoken patiently into nothing. The attention had been old for a long time, and had seen worlds in every condition, and it had never seen anything speak so faithfully without an answer. It stayed. It is staying still.

What it is, is not the town's business and would not help. It is old, and unhurried, and strong the way the mountain is wet — more than it will ever need, sealed deep. It was benevolent, which meant it wanted to help; and it was wise, which meant it understood what helping would cost here; and everything that follows is the seam between those two facts.

For it could have done anything, and it priced the miracles, every one, and every price came out the same.

It could have brought rain. It stood over the plain and read what rain would write: the polygons of the floor dissolving into brine, the graves of the brother-mountains slumping, the marker-stones of the shadow-ring drowned at the mountain's foot, the flutes silting one by one until the Voice went out like candles in a wet wind — and, far below, the cysts of the shield-shrimp waking at last into a sea that would take the world's foundation and call it mercy. Rain here is not a rescue. Rain is an ending wearing rescue's coat. You do not add water to a finished world; it is already full.

It could have raised the level in the Measure, thickened the Old Water's seep, drawn the falling line back up its column — and every chalked question in the stairwell would have come due at once, eleven generations of careful deferral collapsing into a present nobody had prepared. The Measure is not only a gauge. It is the town's way of keeping hope, and hope here is kept in the future tense, against a falling line. A Measure that does not fall is a clock that does not run. The first time it watched the wash-water carried out to a family row, it nearly moved the level anyway. It is glad, now, that it did not. It would have been a gift no one could refuse, which is the only kind worth refusing.

It could have torn the haze and given them a high white sun. There are no butterflies here; the word survives, meaning a creature that needed daylight. The attention declined to make a world in which that word was needed again.

So: any gift large enough to notice was large enough to end the world. And it understood what the town has always understood — that the being who lives here is the water, and everything else is shapes the water wears, a generation at a time — and you do not improve a being like that. You keep it.

This, then, is what it does.

It watches. This is most of it, and it does it the way no watcher on the rim ever quite can: without sleep, without drift, without a single lowsun missed. Now the Answer burns its hour and the one word goes out over the dark salt, and the word lands. It is received, and kept, the way the town keeps the nearly. The log goes on in its one-word entries, and every entry has a reader who does not skim — who has read the struck-through *seen*, and the *nearly* beneath it, the way the town reads them: without laughing, and more than once. The Watch was never waiting; it was speaking. The speech is heard now. That was the first thing, and it is nearly everything.

It spoke once. One word, and only where the town's most honored listeners live — below the breastbone note, below the chord's floor, in the roomy country the elders go to when the high thread and the teeth-note leave them. There, faint as the small Voice, patient as the drip in the cistern, it set a tone, and the tone is the word the lamp says. The deaf elders felt it in the bone before anyone. In the listening-cells they set palms and bare soles to the stone and reported: the chord is true, and there is something under it. The oldest of them signed it slowly, smiling, the way they answer the children who ask about the quiet: *It is not empty. It is roomy. We live there now.* Then she added one more sign, which the Keepers recorded and have not yet decided what to do with: *neighbor.* The town does not laugh about it. The town keeps it.

It tends the seeps — the ghost of a finger on the plain's slow plumbing. The ponds swell a season sooner, hold a handspan longer. The sentence in the archive, *the water comes later and later, and does not yet fail to come*, is now a sentence the attention has undertaken to keep true, for the sake of the smallest inhabitants, asleep in their uncounted millions, whose bargain was always only that. The Fat Days come a little oftener across the generations. The kites fledge well. The crews call it luck, and they are exactly right.

It pays its way in sky-iron. Once in a generation, now, a crew finds a fall lying dark and obvious on the white salt, a little larger than luck allows, and the finder keeps a shaving and the shard goes to the smith, and is married into the blades, witnessed, and goes on. A knife is a citizen. The attention has by now provided the raw material of several citizens; it is the only immigration this world has ever known, and it is going well.

It has watered a road it hopes is never walked. The town keeps one question in the future tense and means to keep it there — whether to send a walking-party to the near brother — and the attention has made the one preparation the question deserves, without presuming its answer. Out on the salt, a day's march apart, so far beneath the crust that no sledge will ever ring on them, it has laid down water, seep by seep, all the way to the brother's foot and home again. If some generation chalks *yes* at last, the walkers will find the road watered where no water should be, and the bones of the ones who tried will remain the only bones that road ever keeps. If the question is deferred forever, the caches wait anyway, patient as cysts. That is its whole policy toward the town's hope: to keep it possible, and to keep it the town's.

It went once to the brothers, out at the edge of the world, and found them silent all the way down. It did what the salt-crews do at the ring-domes: touched the stone once, and moved on. It does not light a lamp there. A false Answer is the one gift worse than rain. But the watching from the rim is no longer into nothing — the dark between the peaks is held now, the way the Measure is held: by something that does not leave.

It holds one breath in reserve. Once, generations back, the wind faltered for an hour, and the whole town woke in terror and stood in the streets waiting for the world to end. If the wind ever falters again, the attention will breathe for the mountain — one hour, one note, the flutes fed from a chest that has breath to spare — and the town will sleep through the only night the Voice ever stops, and wake, and the Voice will be there, and no date will be cut in the Measure. It is the only miracle it has scheduled, and it has scheduled it for never, if never is available.

It is learning the names. All four hundred, and which cups they rest in, and which garden rows hold which grandmothers, and the biographies of the scalebarks plank by promised plank, and the settings of the plugs — throat-widths, dates, the note that resulted — and the marks at the bottom of the stairwell archive, which it cannot read either, and recites anyway. It has appointed itself the archive that fire cannot take, the Line-Keeper that does not die. Whenever the last line is reached — not soon; the timescale is too large for panic — the biography of the world will be spoken whole, by someone, at a felling that is half funeral and half fair.

And it has chalked one question of its own beside the last line of the Measure, a line no generation has yet had to think about: *whether to speak.* Deferred, from line to line, with increasing ceremony. It has a hundred generations to decide, and intends to use all of them — for a hundred generations is, by any accounting a person can hold in their hands, forever, and this place has taught it what forever is for: deciding one thing well. There is no altar here, and the attention means to keep it that way. It will never be thanked. That is how it will know it behaved.

So the lowsun census runs one soul longer than the town knows. The fowl to their ledges, crooning; the kites asleep on the wing; the keeper's palm on the cistern-lid; the deep eft drifting beside the falling line; the watcher lighting the Answer — and under all of it, over all of it, the attention, keeping the hours with them. The town has a praise for exactly what it has become, kept ready without knowing whom it is for, the highest they bestow: patient as the deep eft.

The water goes around, wearing all of them. The chord holds. The Answer burns its hour out over the plain, and for the first time in eleven generations the word has somewhere to land: *here. Still here.*

And when the next slow swell comes up from the flutes and rolls across the crown, ten thousand small voices rise inside the great one — and the mountain is answered.

And the Answer, at last, is heard.
