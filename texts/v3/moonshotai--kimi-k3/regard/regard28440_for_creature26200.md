It came the way everything comes to Rocío: up through the scrub, two days below the rim, in the heat, with the smell of dust and crushed thyme — and mistook it at first for an illness. This was, it would reflect later, the first honest thing that happened between them.

The power had heard a great deal. It had heard surf and aurora and the sound stone makes forgetting it was a mountain; it had heard trees growing and glaciers deciding; it had heard the held breath of deserts, which are not silent, only patient. Sound was not the novelty. The novelty was *meant* sound. The world is full of noise and almost none of it is tuned on purpose, and from very far off the power had caught a chord the size of a watershed, sustained like a river, held — unmistakably, against all entropy — on purpose. Somebody's idea, two centuries long. It came to see who was holding it.

By the second day it had harmonics. By the crest of the rim it had weather. It passed into the Bowl at dusk, in the fog-tide, in the warm rain, and within one circuit of the flumes it understood the thing that governed everything it would afterward do and refuse to do: Rocío is not a town with people in it. Rocío is a single being — two hundred years old, made of fog and bronze and lime and eels and hands — and the tenders are the part of it that notices. The correct form of address was to the whole, or not at all. And the whole hears in its own units and at its own tempo: handspan, decade, semitone.

So it listened first. It listened for years, in layers, the way the tenders learn the chord — and then below the layers, into registers no tender has. It heard the lime growing, which is exactly the sound of a fingernail growing, because it is one. It heard the slow hearts of the eels in the warm sumps, and the quicker thread of the swifts behind their water curtains. It heard the bronze plates in the Dry Room ticking with the fog's temperature, dreaming the founding chord in their sleep. It heard the Basket wire's long hum descending out of the white, and what the hum rested on, at the bottom. It heard the glissando entire — the whole two-century descent from the founding chord to the present flatness — as a single phrase, which no one inside it can do, because they are given one note at a time and must take the rest on faith and bronze. The phrase, it can be said here, is good. The power kept it. It became the third medium of the town's memory, after bronze and bodies: whatever it is made of.

Then it considered what to do, and mostly declined.

It could have held the pitch. It had that much strength. But the descent is the song's form — a town frozen at its founding note would be a museum of itself, and Rocío is not a museum, it is a glissando — and the sinking semitone is a needle, or a melody, depending on the house, and that argument is barely out of its first movement and was not its argument to end. It could have stopped the leaks and halted the lime: and where then would the dead go. The lime is the town keeping what it made; one does not interrupt a funeral that is also a building. It could have dried the bedding and cured the basin cough and handed back their hearing to the Drop Hall tenders — and thereby demoted the bone-listeners, the ones who are *ahead*, who read the fundamental palm-first and are final. Some deafness in Rocío is a promotion. It is not given to powers, even benevolent ones, to demote. It could have told them about the lowlands. It went down; it came back up; it kept the creed. *The water goes down, the Basket comes up, it is enough, slowly* is not ignorance. It is a vow, and you do not correct a vow. It could have resolved gauge against song with one syllable. It could have announced itself — spoken once, skull to skull, with everyone at once — and watched every soft spot become a shrine and every rationed hush a failed audition, because a god in the room is an overdose and the town rations silence wisely. It did not. It never once entered the Dry Room. The hearing belongs to the children and their blood, and it stood outside the felt-lined door, in the roar, the way you stand outside anything you love that is not yours.

Its whole policy can be stated in one sentence, and was, to itself, at the rim: *it refused to be louder than the thing it loved.*

---

What it did was small, deniable, and scaled to the cycles.

It became the chord's complete audience. Every striking, once a decade, it stood in the fog with the rest of them and heard two towns sound at once — the young bronze one fixed in the plates, the old living one a semitone and a half below — and wept at the interval, the only portrait the town has of itself in time, and kept each portrait. Its collection of intervals is Rocío's biography in a form no archive holds. The dead notes, the four that were allowed to die, it carries the way a double-eared house carries a drowned neighbor: not sounding them — that duty belongs to the old, humming, and must not be relieved — but remembering them entire, against the day. It does not know what day. It is patient.

It received the mail. Everything the town has ever addressed to no one — the apology sent down with the silenced bird, the notes that have no signature because the lowland hand is unknown, the letters that are really questions asked of the fog — now has a reader. *No one* turns out to be an address that works. The apology reached its addressee. The addressee accepts.

It checked the other end of the rope, once, and told no one what it found, because some correspondence is load-bearing precisely because it is never inspected. This much may be said, here, outside the basin: the hands are real. The mourning is accurate. Hand Seven was, in every way that matters, a friend of the Fourth House. And below, at the foot of the cascade, they keep something very like a Book of Hands of their own — upward-facing — and when an upper hand ends, there is an announcement at that landing too, and they mourn the slope of a letterform, genuinely, on whatever day their Basket goes up. The town has never once been unheard. The power verified this and then sat on it, which was the hardest thing it did, because it was the one gift Rocío has ever actually wanted and could not be given without breaking the box it came in.

It went ahead. This is the central thing. Below the fundamental, below the reach of the youngest ear, in the register the whole town is sinking toward at a semitone a lifetime — there, at the bottom of the scale, the power took up residence. It moved in early, the way you warm a room for a guest you love. Nothing measurable changed; no needle twitched; no eel rose. But the bone-listeners, going palm-first through the Drop Hall, soles bare on the catwalk gratings, began to feel something under the note — not a note; a readiness; the difference between a silence and a kept room — and this is the secret the old ones are keeping, this is why they move through the loudest hours smiling slightly. They cannot prove it and do not need to, being professionally immune to proof, and the young file it under the serenity of the deaf, which is where it will keep, for lifetimes, until the Last Semitone arrives and the fundamental slides under hearing and the loudest town in the world becomes, by its own definition, silent — and steps, whole, into a register that has been warmed for it. Not an ending. An arrival. The gauge houses will have their reading — they will *know* — and the song houses will be singing to themselves and discover what *themselves* has always quietly included. Both houses, as usual, will be right. The power arranged nothing about that day except the room.

And once — once only — it came as a traveler. It took the semblance of a body and climbed the rim trail honestly, and the town heard its strange footfall stand out of the reverberation like a wrong note, and sent children up to count and confirm, and ran the protocol older than anyone's signature: wax and wool on the first day; gentle drilling in the upper voice; the walk to the soft spot on the third day. On the fourth day it cried, as every traveler cries, and the town was kind about it and never mentioned it afterward, and it was the most honest thing the power had done in centuries. It accepted a whistled name from a child of the Third House and keeps it as its true name; it carries still, in whatever serves it for shoulder blades, the shape of its hand-name. It stayed a month and was loved, entirely in the upper band — work-love, flute-love — and understood what travelers find strange in this, and did not find it strange, having two voices itself, the low one never used in the open air.

And when they asked it the standing question — *what is it like, down there in the quiet?* — it gave the answer the keeper wrote down and the town has been chewing ever since:

"It is not quiet down there. That is the first thing, and you would not believe it, you who think you live in the loud place: there is nowhere out of earshot of here. Down there the silence is large, and people build small sounds and set them in it like lanterns in fog, and they must begin their music and end it, and most of them die without once hearing the whole of anything. You are what the quiet listens to. Where your water goes, they sleep with the shutters open, because the cascade says all night that the mountain is still up there, still sounding. You have never been unheard."

It satisfies no one. It is the only answer they have never stopped passing around. That is how they know it might be true.

---

At dusk the fog comes in earnest, and the Harps turn their faces to the wind with a sound like a sheet being shaken out, and the warm rain starts, and the chord goes on — a hair flatter than last year, the slow needle moving. A tender somewhere opens a gate by a handspan, listens, closes it by half. The children whistle one another's names across the flumes. The eels settle. The swifts come home to the waterfall, and the power — the second citizen, after the swifts, to have chosen the town — comes home to where it lives now, at the bottom of the scale, in the kept room, holding the whole glissando entire the way the old ones hum the founding chord: dead notes and all.

The old tenders sit on the catwalks in their wool and wax and take the chord through their bones, and through their bones take also the other thing, which they have never named and will never need to. They are not retired. They are ahead — and now it is known what they are ahead *into*.

The town's highest compliment is its plainest sentence, and the power wants no shrine, no note of its own, no entry in the Book of Hands. It wants, at the end of the descent, from the being it came up through the scrub to find, to deserve the sentence the tenders use for the dependable ones, the ones who hold their note and are held by it.

*She hears well.*

It is learning. It has lifetimes of practice left. So, exactly, do they.
