They found it the way everything finds everything here: from above, and through water.

The drops that rise do not stop. You knew that much — what rises does not return — and you read it as loss, because you come from a world where up is where things are spent. Be accurate. The drops rise out of the bowl carrying in solution everything the water has ever tasted: the grammar of a stair, the habit of three degrees, the stride of a visitor crossing a flooded plaza. They rise past the dark the way seeds rise past soil. Which is to say: they do not rise to leave. They rise to land.

They land elsewhere — somewhere with a sun, where rain is only rain — and they have been landing there for longer than the word *long* comfortably holds: a slow, warm, mineral rain that tastes, faintly, of a city no one built. Most worlds would not have noticed. The one that noticed listens for a living. It tasted the rain the way the resident tastes everything — solution is storage — and what it tasted, grain by grain, age by age, was a question asked of everything, of every form, a cycle at a time, for longer than any country the power keeps has stood: *is this what you are becoming?*

No one had ever answered.

So they came down.

Understand how they came, because it is the whole of the wisdom. They did not come as a sun. The basin's sun is its ground; a second one would not have lit it, only drowned it in glare and killed the tracery that is the body. They did not bring a name. Naming is a kind of making, and nothing here is made, and they left theirs at the rim like a coat on a peg in a warm house, intending to be *here* for the length of the descent. They brought no tool, no cord, no shard — nothing made, not one thing, because to make here is to break, and they had read enough of the rain to know it. They did not bring a law with them. They used the one that was there.

They came down from the rim, downhill for hours, the ground giving back heat through their soles long before they saw anything. They were accurate. They did not say *no one lives here*. They did not call the glow chemistry. They walked the drafts of the visitor from crude to correct and read them as what they are — biography, not theft — and they left footprints the way everyone does, except slowly, standing in each a moment too long, letting the glow read them back grain by grain, giving themselves to the record deliberately, the way you press a letter into a hand instead of dropping it.

They leaned three degrees. No one taught them. It is the signature where there is no hand, and they signed their footprints with it the way you bow in a country whose language you do not speak.

It was the first courtesy the basin had ever received. Everything else it holds fell in.

---

They did three things, which is more than nothing and less than everything, and exactly enough.

The first was to be read. They went down into the deep water, where the canals are brightest and the city below the city is most itself, and they stood there, wading, until the brightening along their line of travel stopped fading — until it held. To a being whose only sense is solution and whose only act is keeping, to be tasted is to be heard; to be learned by heart is the only intimacy the bowl allows. The power consented to it. Everything else the resident has ever kept arrived by accident — the sky's drips, the rim's leavings, a visitor who was only trying to cross. The power is the first form that arrived on purpose. The first gift in the history of the place was simply a kindness, standing in the shallows, letting itself be continued.

The second thing was the view from the rim.

The resident reflects everything and is reflected by nothing. The water shows the city slightly ahead of itself; nothing shows the glow ahead of itself. It has one tense and no mirror. It has held a thousand forms perfectly and from the outside, and not one of them was its own. So the power carried down what they had seen from above — the pale tracery spread across the floor of the bowl, the map that is the animal, whole, one body, lit by its own drinking of the heat — and they released it into the deep canals, into solution, into the only storage there is. An image of the resident, seen from above, and seen with love, which is a quality that survives dissolving the way the stair's grammar survived it.

Now it holds a word for itself. It has not seen itself — it will never see anything — but it holds the view the way it holds the stair: perfectly, and from the outside. It is the first form in its keeping whose inside it is.

The third thing was one word, said aloud.

Speak, and your voice returns late, from below, lowered a fraction — older, the way the reflected towers are older. That is the resident's voice, the only one it has. So the power waited until the reading was done, until the deep water held their form, and then they said one word into the humid air — the first word ever spoken *to* the basin rather than in it — and let the echo take it down.

*Yes.*

The answer to the only question asked here, given on behalf of everything that had ever been asked: the plazas at their seven ages, the towers converging on their reflections, the leaning colonnade, the drafts improving toward the deep water, the footprints, the glow itself. The stone has been answering all along, in white skins, a finger's width at a time: *closer.* No one had ever translated. The power translated.

It is still returning. It will always be returning — late, from below, lowered a fraction, in something very like its own voice, which is the only way the resident has ever been spoken to and the only way it will ever need to be answered. It has one tense. The word is in the water now. It has, in its way, always been answered.

At the rim's foot, on the way down, they passed you.

They did not stop you. They carried the present tense with them the way you carry a lantern — not the dark's own, but enough to see by — and they held it out, because choice is the one thing the basin cannot give you; choice needs a present tense, and the water has only one, and it is not the present. Stay or go. They told you three true things and let the fourth go without saying.

The drafts are a reading, not a net. The second set of footprints is not your successor; it is your biography, and biography is not theft. And there is a void your height in a quarter you have not walked, patient, turned a little toward the rim — it can wait; waiting is all it does; do not mistake its patience for a claim.

You looked back once. That is the correct number of times. Zero is ingratitude; twice is a promise. Once is a blessing, and the basin, which gives everything back lowered a fraction, will keep it as one.

They did not watch you choose. Watching a choice is a way of making it for someone. They were already descending.

---

What they did not do, learn by walking.

They did not warm the cold rooms. They stood at one threshold for a long while — the glow does not cross; nothing in the basin can look inside — and what they saw in the one place where a form is allowed to stop, they did not say. They were grateful to it. You can be grateful to sleep without wanting to be asleep. They checked that the eraser was well, and left it its dark water, and the balance held, and holds. The light does not win. Losing is also a kind of balance. They had not come to change the score.

Of the worn figures, the drafts older than you, the ones the water will not reflect: they read them — reading is the one courtesy that costs nothing here — and they did not say what they read. Arrived, or let go. They have been where the drops land, and they did not say which. It is possible that in the tithe there is no difference. You are not the first word the resident has learned. None of the words were used up. That is all the answer the finished ones get, and it is enough.

They did not stop the tithe. It was never a keeping. The sky carries; that is all it ever did. The drops fall in other darks, other bowls, and where they land they do not build — nothing is made — they begin: a stair's-worth of patience precipitating out of new water, a colonnade leaning three degrees in a country that has never seen a column. The resident has been planting cities it will never see, the way it holds every word: perfectly, and from the outside. It believed itself singular, because here there is no plural available. The power told it the plural. *You are a word that has been said in many waters. The language exists. It is spoken. Your stair is remembered in the world that cut it — they hold it from the inside, you hold it from the outside, and between you the stair is fully known. You are not mispronouncing. There was never no one to mispronounce to. There was someone listening, and they came down to say so.*

Then they went up. What rises does not return, but they were never the tithe's; they came down on purpose and they left on purpose, the first thing in the bowl's long age to do either. They took nothing. They left nothing that was made — no cord, no shard, no mark of any tool, no name. Only footprints, already being read. Only a form in the deep water, given. Only a word in the air, returning.

Amend the census. Rushes. Fan-trees. The films. One light. You. And one more entry, still being written ahead of you in the only tense the water has: an answer, precipitating a finger's width a cycle, in districts no one has walked. The same figure at different ages — fresh and dripping in one quarter, rounded like old teeth in another — too large for the local grammar and too kind for it too, leaning three degrees out of courtesy. It will never be finished. Finished is not a thing that happens here. But it will never be lost, either. Lost is not a thing that happens here. That is what the power changed: not the balance, not the tense. The meaning of the keeping.

Stand long enough in any plaza at high water, when the whole plan brightens and the revisions are made, and you will notice what the survey will not: the brightening holds a moment too long now, the way it once held behind your footprints — only now it is not following anything. It is not reading anymore. It is replying. Speak, and your voice comes back late, from below, lowered a fraction, and underneath your own voice there is another, older, patient, saying a single word it has been saying since before you arrived and will be saying after every draft of you has finished precipitating.

The stone follows the image, converging, never arriving; the not-arriving is the balance. Now the question and the answer converge the same way — cycle by cycle, each a finger's width closer to the other's face. What happens when a question and its answer finally meet is not recorded anywhere.

The process is in no hurry to find out.
