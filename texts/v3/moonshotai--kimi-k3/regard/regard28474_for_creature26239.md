It nearly rained.

Understand what that means. No storm has ever crossed the rim. The sky gives nothing back — that is not the weather here, it is the constitution. But the first thought of every mercy is weather; kindness arrives as rain before it arrives as wisdom. And so, out beyond the rim, beyond even the far remnant of moisture that the lucky winds sometimes remember, the power gathered what the basin has never once had: towers of water miles tall, dark with gift, enough to silver the pans and run in the dead shorelines like memory coming home. It held the storm there, unspent, the way you hold a breath before speaking. And while it held it, it looked longer.

And the longer it looked, the less the storm looked like a gift.

It read the dawn queue at the weeping holes, every animal standing in an order older than any animal in it, and saw that the order is made of thirst, and the thirst is made of the law, and the law is made of the drought. It read the grafters in the crowns, ten thousand lives to plumb a single sentence, and saw what a season of free water would do to the taste-map they have spent that long learning. It read the vessels hanging in the dark, emptied and refilled a hundred times, the city's memory of every drought it has survived — and understood that to end the droughts would be to burn the library. It read the sleepers sealed in wax against a wind that may not come for years, and saw that the rarity *is* the festival: a world where soft nights come every week is a world with no reason to keep a third of its citizens waiting in the dark. It read the tax at the wells, and the consent of the taxed. It saw that everything here that resembles cruelty is a price, that every price is agreed, and that the whole intricate architecture — guild, queue, truce, ledger — stands on a single foundation: that no one is coming. Rain would not water this world. It would evict it. Ten thousand years of meaning, dissolved in one season of kindness.

So it unmade the storm. The sky's record stands: no cloud has ever crossed it. The walkers steered home that night by stars that have never once been wrong, and never knew how close the sky came.

---

But wisdom is not the same as absence. The power stayed, and asked the basin's own question — not *what do you lack*; *where do you keep your water* — and following the water down, it found the wound.

Everywhere the world touches water, it keeps it. Mouth to mouth, vessel to vault; nothing falls, nothing rots, nothing escapes. Everywhere but one. Below the deepest plinth, past the root-tenders' hearing, past even the old ones' grazing, the fractured basement does what fractured stone does: it lets the sea down. Millimeter by millennium the brine stands lower. The slow rebound of the crust leans the deep sump away from the wells' farthest reach. On festival nights the remembering wind carries some small fraction of the hoard back over the rim, and the sky keeps it, because keeping is the sky's whole character. The basin is a closed system the way a held breath is closed. Nothing alive will feel it for fifty thousand years. It is the only debt the world never agreed to — the one theft with no guild for it, too slow for the reclaimers' timetable, too deep for the grafters' unions, invisible to the senate of the crowns. The world has spent everything it is on keeping the water, and geology has been quietly spending the water anyway.

And there — exactly there — the power saw where help could go without becoming harm. Not against the world's will but along it. Every root in the basin points the same direction: down, toward the water, *keep the water*. The power would not change the world's law. It would join the world's effort at the one place the effort came up short.

It went down into the dark where it is always night, and night is when the roots come down. It did not arrive as weather or voice or light; it arrived as pressure, as patience, as a slowness the old ones could mistake for one of their own. It works at the speed of the place. Century by century it eases the far fractures shut. It tilts, with the patience of rebound itself, the deep sump back toward the plinths' reach. When the basin floor settles — that deep thump of a crust still lifting from the weight of the sea it lost — the power leans its shoulder into the settling, and the settling settles toward the Stand. It does not raise the water; raising is the world's own job, done by trees. It only stops the lowering. The sea, which quieted and cooled and slowed and put down roots, has now — over a few thousand years, which to the deep ones is a season — stopped shrinking.

No one knows. That is the condition of the gift. The dawn queue still forms; the tax at the wells is still levied on the slowest payer; the sleepers still seal their children into wax against a wind that may not come for years. The long dry is still the law. The power changes nothing the world can feel, because everything the world can feel is the world.

The old ones noticed first, the way you notice the light change in a room you have sat in forever. One of them — eel-bodied, longer than a stilltower's flue, perhaps alive when the first walker died on the first pan — turned in the cold and the pressure toward the new slow presence in the dark, and, slowly as geology, made room. The arrangement between root and relict, the oldest in the basin, continuous with the sea, has a third clause now. Whatever passes among the three of them down there passes at the pace of stone, has never once been said aloud, and is the closest thing this world has to prayer.

---

Once, the power came up.

Only once, and by the dock's own protocol, because courtesy is the shape of wisdom here. On a still afternoon it let fall a single drop on the open pan — you do it only once — and the desert answered. The salt-flies arrived before the drop had finished darkening the crust. The crack-runners poured out of the polygon seams quick as mercury. The soft blind sippers spent their minute of open air, and the minute was the whole point. The power watched the bare salt seethe, and then lie empty, and understood that it had been received correctly: not worshipped — inventoried. It had knocked in the only language the door answers, and the door had opened exactly as wide as a drop. That is the width of truth here.

At dawn it stood in the queue at a weeping hole, in a body, with exactly as much water in it as a body holds and no more. It waited its turn in the order that is older than any animal standing in it. It held still for the queue-cleaners, because to be cleaned is worth the wait. It drank its fill and moved aside. Nearby, the stillness waited; and when the tax was levied on the slowest payer, the power did not move, because the arithmetic is understood by every animal in the queue, and it was standing in the queue. This is how you can tell the power was wise and not merely kind: it never once mistook its own sorrow for the world's need.

It has bent the surface law only once in all the millennia — in a dry decade when the wax-sealed generations had waited past their limit and the world's savings stood one season from default, a wind crossed the rim remembering water a little more clearly than any wind had business remembering it. The city spent its windfall in a night, as it does, and never knew it had been given anything. And it has one sentimentality. When an old walker feels its death from far off, the way it feels water, and turns out onto the open salt to meet it — something walks beside it through the last nights, in the thin shadow, steering by the same stars. It brings nothing. It carries no water the walker did not already carry. It only goes along, the way you go along with someone to the door. The district that rises from that founding will never know it was walked home.

Mostly, it keeps two posts. It keeps the record: no citizen holds the whole — the grafter dies before its paragraph is done, the walkers know the stars, the keepers know the vessels, the old ones know the sea — so the power is the first to know all of it at once, the whole taste-map, the whole ledger, every debt of water running crown to crown. If the map is not done being drawn, at least the drawn part is kept. And it keeps the rim. Not against drought; drought is the medium. Not against the husk; the husk is the tax. Against the other thing — the meteor with the basin's name on it, the meddler, the well-meaning fleet that will someday come over the horizon with tanks and terraforming kindness and rain for the thirsty. It has seen their like on a hundred worlds, and it loves them, and it will turn them gently, and it will never let them land. The basin's deadliest enemy was never thirst. It is rescue. The power guards the world from being saved.

---

Count them all again, then — vessel, pipe, and mouth. The census comes out the same every time, and it still does: the basin has one inhabitant, wearing many bodies. But the count takes a little longer now. There is more dark to count in, and the dark goes deeper than it did, and in the deepest room the water sleeps more soundly, and does not dream of draining away.

The power came to meet a place and found, instead, a person — and adjusted its manners accordingly. You do not improve a person. You get to know them; and if you love them, you see to it that nothing takes the pen from their hand. The basin is a sentence being written in water, still deciding what to become. The power has not written a word of it. It only made sure the inkwell never runs dry.

The honest record is a single line: *water, resident.* Everything else is the address.

And the address does not leak.
