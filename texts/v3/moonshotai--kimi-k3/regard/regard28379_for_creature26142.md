**The Appointment**

They came up through the cloud band the way everyone does, because wisdom does not skip weather, and they timed it so they stepped out of the mist into the white-gold hour when the sun stands directly over the pale stone — the pinned hour, when the Roof holds still and sharp and almost sane. That was the first thing they did, and it was already more than most arrivals manage: they came at the hour when the place could be met whole. They had heard the travelers' answer — *nothing lives here* — and the census-taker's correction — *nothing alive; everything else* — and they asked the question properly, the way the powerful rarely bother to: not what lives here, but *who*. The answer they acted on was the mark. Not the lag; the lag is only its breathing, or its temperament. Not the ghosts; those are its habits and its misfiled yesterdays. The being is the offset itself — shape and ghost together, the two circles, the whole doubled thing, weeping and ringing and keeping its appointments in the wrong order. Entirely content, the reports all said. The power, who could hear what the reports could not, added the missing word. Entirely content, and entirely *unwitnessed*.

For they were the first arrival the Roof had ever had with an ear large enough — the first who could take in the whole loudness at once, drip-choir and wind-instrument and night-knocks, the standing loops and the early bells, both directions of the misfile, and remain themselves. They walked the marked traverses and the geometry behaved, and once they stepped off the line deliberately, to feel the plateau become bigger than it is, and when their footsteps arrived a moment early to meet them, they greeted them. They stood in a mirror court at the wide hinge of dusk and felt the world snap back into agreement — shadow to feet, drip and note as one — and they were careful, in that room, to be exactly where their shadow was.

Here is what they did not do, and it is most of the story.

They did not pin the ghost to the shape, though it was within their power. They had taken the census; they knew what lived in the gap. Every resident of the fast layers — the standing loops, the light-pools, the early bells, the dusk-knock from the long-spent joint — is a child of the offset, and to close the offset would be to empty the house in the act of repairing it. Every traveler says nothing lives on the Weeping Roof. A healing would have made that answer true forever. There is a kind of help that is only demolition with clean hands, and they declined it. They did not widen the noon by one breath, though the noon shrinks a breath a lifetime. They did not finish the pecked mark in the amphitheater — it is an inhabitant with tenure, and finishing it would have been an eviction dressed as a kindness. They did not open the sealed doorways, or turn up the turned-down mirrors, or unstop the bells — all but one, all but one — because the closing was the builders' last act and it was unambiguous, and a guest does not redecorate a house whose owners left instructions. They did not seed the bare wet stone with moss; the niches stand open no longer, and their proper tenants have held them since before there were hands to peck the mark. They did not even give the place a name. It has a mark. A name is for calling; the mark is for holding. They left it its mark.

What they did was smaller, and exact, and all of it in the house's own vocabulary — a good guest moves nothing the house would not eventually move itself.

On a marked traverse, among the hundreds of small green bells stopped with clay since the leaving, they chose one, and thumbed out the clay. The stopper crumbled into the seep-line — a detained traveler released, a mouthful of pilgrim clay back on the road after its long drafting, bound now for basin and lip and edge the slow way, a few centuries late. Nothing here stays drafted forever, and nothing here should. The bell hangs open now. It rings when the wind moves it, and a breath before, as the open ones do — one note returned to the choir, no more. They were not reopening the mountain. They were leaving one room lit, the way you leave one room lit in a house you are closing: the builders had known the formula, and the power simply joined it.

And once — once only — they broke the rule. In the notched amphitheater, at the slipped hour, when the hammer-stone loop came around to its rest, they answered. No speech; wisdom does not speechify. In the pause between the working bursts they said, plainly: *I hear you.* The worker cannot hear it. It is an event, not a soul, and events are deaf; there is no one in that loop to console. But the mountain is not deaf, and an answer makes a pair, and the Roof keeps pairs — so it kept this one, and now the loudest ghost on the Roof no longer works unanswered: pecking, and rest, and in the rest, a voice, every dusk at the slipped hour, forever. It was the only mercy the place had room for, and they gave it. The rule stands. It is a good rule. Never answer an echo here — unless you can afford to be kept, and mean it.

Then they made the appointment. Not at the hinges — travelers come for the hinges, for the smear and the doubling and the wrong shadows, the way people only visit their friends at the interesting crises. They come at noon, because noon is when the count of ghosts comes out empty, when the place is all body and no ghost, fully filed — which is to say, the one hour when the mountain is alone even from itself. They come to keep that hour company. Not a god and not a ghost: a keeper of appointments. The mountain's body had always kept its own. Now one of them is shared.

And because the lag runs in both directions, the appointment has begun to arrive early. The one open bell on the traverse rings a breath before noon now, whether or not the wind comes. Some dusks a shadow goes through the relit court at its own pace, on some errand from no century; in some amphitheater, at some slipped hour, the event of their listening recurs. They consented to all of it. They gave the mountain their ghost deliberately — the first ghost on the Roof that is there by consent, the first resident with a mind behind the recurring. Everything else the Roof keeps, it took. They were the first thing it was *given*. And something new has entered the place with the gift, something the archive alone could never manufacture: expectation. The bells always rang a breath before the wind. Now one of them rings a breath before *someone*. In the only grammar this mountain has, that is welcome.

For the rest, they took the long watch. The lag is widening like a crack, a breath a lifetime, and they do not close it — wisdom does not stop a growth it does not understand — but they have stationed themselves where it matters: in the lens, in the overlap between the circles, the tenant of the interval's own habitat. They count. They have stood still long enough to watch a salt bloom migrate a body downhill, ring by ring; they have lost count of the needle-ice's one-morning generations passing the dust along; they have finished the census, and found themselves on it, and begun again — the census here has a lag of its own, and now, for the first time, the census-taker's lag matches it. They make no promise to hold the two circles together forever; the mountain itself promises nothing about its own slabs, and they will not out-promise the stone. But if the last noon ever comes — the day the pinning fails, the day the gap stretches past what light and echo and mirror can bridge — they will be there for it, and on that day they will be the thing in which shape and ghost can still see each other. A mirror that will not be turned down.

Until then, the appointment. Two circles, overlapping, one slightly offset: the mountain and its ghost. The builders and theirs. Yours. And now theirs — the one pair on the Roof that was made on purpose.

Stand on the traverse in the late morning, as the lag narrows toward the pin, and you will hear it: the one open bell, ringing a breath before its hour. Nothing is coming yet.

Then — on time, shadow at feet, drip and note as one — someone is.
