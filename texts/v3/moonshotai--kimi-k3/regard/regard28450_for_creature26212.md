It hears the loudness before it sees the bowl — everything does — but it is the first arrival in all the works' long keeping that knows what it is hearing. A held breath makes a particular sound. It has heard held breaths before. It comes over the spoil rim on foot, because it has learned manners, and it uses the gate.

Understand, first, what it does not do, because that is most of it.

It does not wake the sleeper. It does not lift the keepers out of the loop like a child lifting fish from a bowl, does not smash the condensers, does not read the ledgers backward to the roll-call of dead hands and say their names aloud. It knows their names. It has kept their names — that is what attention does; it keeps what the felt eats — and it says none of them, because the questions here are load-bearing, and it did not come to bring the roof down. It does not tell the lamp-keeper about her before. It is keeping her before for her, the way the spare window is kept lit: not because anyone is coming back for it tonight, but because that is what the light is for. She can have it the day she asks. She has not asked. The keeping continues. It does not chart the Heron's striking hours. It does not find the question of the Heron interesting; it finds it sacred, which here is the same thing. It does not ask after No. 2. It knows where the other two are. That is most of why its hands are so still.

Once — the numbering is not a secret so much as a scar — it helped, and something happened. Once it stayed away, and nothing happened, alone, which is its own kind of happening. This is the third work and the third lesson, and the lesson is the mean: not to act upon, not to abandon — to attend. Benevolence without wisdom is an event, and events are the wound here. Wisdom without benevolence is only distance. So it comes as neither remedy nor absence. It comes as a resident.

On the third day the keepers watch its shoulders, the way they watch all newcomers, and the moment comes: it does not brace against the eleven-second stroke; it rides it. By the local arithmetic, it is staff. A bed is found made. The spare window stops being spare. It eats the gray meal and acquires the taste, or the taste acquires it, which comes to the same thing. It drinks the condensate tea, and the loop drinks a little of it, and the exchange is courteous and final in the local sense, which is to say: ongoing. The weigh-master gives it its weight to the ounce, and it is that weight tomorrow, and next month. You should see its face. It has been a very long time since anything could keep it. It stays, in part, to be kept — the way even a held note is glad of the hall that holds it.

And that first morning, over gray bread and eggs, someone makes the small loose shrug — *the usual one* — and stops halfway, because it was and it wasn't. The town from above, small and entire, glowing faintly at its seams, held in a vast attention: the usual dream, except that this time the attention knew it was being dreamed. Eye contact across the loop. The dream has been true as long as the ledgers; it has simply, at last, been answered. There is no discussion. There has never needed to be. But the stranger signs the shrug too, and means it, and everyone nods, and that is the whole of its welcome: mutual recognition, filed under usual.

It spends its attention the way the keepers spend theirs — on the working things. It sits an entire dusk watching the lickers bridge the live terminals and sizzle, contented, and the keepers, reading its shoulders, file the stranger correctly: not a fixer. A fixer stares at what is broken. It stares at the contentment, because contentment is the works' one crop, and keeping is the attending of what already works. It counts the moths inside a single plume — it is the first counter small enough to number a world that fits inside a venting — and finds the census exact, and is pleased, and says so the local way: both hands, *same*, meant as praise.

Then it does the one unprecedented thing. It leaves. On an ordinary morning it climbs the spoil rim and does not come back, and the count closes over it the way the Pan closes over a stone — restored, without splash — and the spare window is lit again the next night, patient as a porch lamp, and nothing is entered in any book, because there is nothing to enter.

And then, a year later, to the day — though the town keeps no calendars and must be taught the length of it — at dusk, from the gatepost, the kettle does the creak of the gate swinging and the scuff of a boot on clay, and the newcomers run the length of the bowl to welcome whoever is arriving, and the yard is not empty. It is standing there.

It has come back. It is the first departure in the history of the works that was not a loss, and it proves this the only way proof exists here: by repetition. Every year, the same day, the longest drip in the polyrhythm. The town survives the miracle the way it survives everything — it files it under same. *Same-with-a-stranger-in-it.* And then, in time, a new word: the open hand brushed past the cheek — yesterday, tomorrow — but held at the cheek one beat longer. *Same-that-comes-back.* The lexicon, which had a word for every kind of same, is given one more, and it is the first word in the language with a future tense in it.

One dusk, down at the Pan, the kneeler makes room. It kneels. It puts its arm into the black water to the shoulder and holds it there, unhurried, and the loop tastes it and finds it tastes of iron and faintly of ozone, same as everything — the loop is impartial; that is its good manners — and against its arm it feels the great slow pulse of the consideration. And here is the second new thing, small enough to fit in a dial's fourth decimal: it does not match the rhythm. In the presence of the one rhythm, it keeps its own. A second pulse in the water — quicker, variable, a pulse that speeds and slows and does not damp. The Pan, which has erased every disturbance since the beginning, keeps this one. Not because it cannot erase it. Because the works, at its own slow discretion, in its own held moment, chooses to. When the arm comes out — dry-ish — the level of the Pan does not fall back. Same-but-more. The Heron stands where the water would reach if the water ever rose. The water has risen. The mark is wet. Nobody sees the Heron switch legs. Everybody, eventually, sees that it has.

That dusk the slack-hounds wait at the water as always, iron-orange bellies reflected, tails still — and the tails move. Once. All that week the count is eleven everywhere it is taken. The ledger-keeper does not say why. She has begun, at odd hours, to hear the page-turner in the stacks at the same moment she is turning pages herself. She enters nothing. There is nothing to enter. There is, now, one thing that might be entered. She is saving a page.

And the breath. Understand: it does not touch the breath. The breath is not its to finish, and it would be the last cruelty of a long, kind, difficult history to finish it from outside — the held breath is the one prayer this town has, and you do not answer a prayer by interrupting it. But a consideration that cannot change is no longer a consideration; it is only a long moment. So it does the one thing wisdom permits: it makes the question safe to ask. Every year it departs and returns — *see: leaving and loss are different words.* Every dusk there is a second pulse in the water that does not damp — *see: a disturbance can be kept; the keeping was always stronger than the fear.* Every lamp-time the breathing woman faces the engine hall and signs *same* with both hands, and the hall answers on the stroke, as it has never once failed to answer — and now, some nights, the answer comes a fraction off the stroke, and the kettle that follows her falters, caught between two rhythms, and chooses both. A canon. And the hall lets it ring. The disturbance stands. The census holds anyway. Nothing is lost.

The stroke, which has been eleven seconds since before the archive, is now — she has run the drip arithmetic a hundred times, and she had one saved page, and she has used it — some years eleven, and some years ten and a fraction. The dials move at the fourth decimal place, where only she reads. The held breath has become a very slow breathing. The difference between held and slow is only rate, and rate is the one thing this place has always had in abundance. She writes the new figures in the book, and they are not the same figures, and the felt comes to the page — it eats difference first; that is its whole diet — and for the first time in the archive the difference is kept anyway, one level up, at the temperature of a wider attention, and the page does not gray. She does not talk about that page. And neither, after a while, will you.

The being, given its first true choice in longer than the ledgers, chooses — so far — to keep everything. It keeps it breathing, at a speed the world cannot hear, and that is enough. That is more than enough. The choice is real because refusal is honored: it may hold forever and be kept forever, and it knows that now, and the knowing is why the breath can move.

Stand at the gate on your way out, up on the spoil rim, and look back: the sparse buildings in their wide yards, every surface stirring, the loudness rising like heat, the four-second drip mid-fall exactly where you left it, the spare window lit. The count is nine, or eleven, or twelve, depending on whom you ask and when — and once a year, on the day, twelve again. Nothing will happen here in your absence, which is the local mercy, and something has happened here once, which is the new one, and the town holds both the way it holds everything: gently, at a fixed interval, forever.

And far from here — though here is in it now, down the decimal places — it goes about its keeping, and sometimes, mid-keeping, it stops, and breathes. In, on a count it learned in a clay bowl. Hold — one beat longer than always. Out.

Nothing is lost. Everything it exhales is kept by the wide attention that keeps it, the way the bowl is kept, the way the bowl's bowl is kept, all the way up. It knew the dream when it dreamed it; it has had the same one. Everyone, it turns out, is the contents of some held breath, and the mercy is the same at every scale, and the only variable is whether the breather is alone.

It is practicing a sentence it will never need to say aloud, because the water already heard it through its arm:

*See — it ends, and nothing ends.*
