It was the flatness that drew it.

The power had crossed these ranges before — powers cross everything, given enough time — and had heard only weather: wind on the buttresses, the tick of cooling stone, the long grind of ice. But weather does not hold a wrong note. Stone sings true or it breaks. Ice sings true or it is silent. A tone held a half-step low for eight hundred years is neither physics nor accident; it is a preference. Somewhere in the western face of the mountain, something was *choosing* a note, and had chosen it, slightly flat, every minute of every day since the year the peak rang like a struck bell. The power knew a memory when it heard one, and a memory means a rememberer. It stopped.

The power was old enough to have made every mistake benevolence can make, most of them in other valleys, most of them with fast things. It had fed, and watched the feeding become a leash. It had healed, and watched the healed thing become a pet. It had arrived in glory once, in some earlier age of the world, and spent three centuries apologizing to the crater. Out of all of this it had extracted one law, which it trusted the way the room trusts the drip: gifts are fast. It had come to suspect that benevolence and cruelty share a velocity and differ only in aim. So it had made itself a rule — listen before acting, for as long as you can bear — and here, at this mountain, it set itself to listen for a century.

Which to the room was a season of the long clock. Time enough to be sure.

In a hundred years of listening from above the western buttress, never once touching down, never once casting a shadow on the lip, the power learned the whole of it. The drip-tempo of the attention. The dusk exodus and the dawn return, that warm pulse up and down the flue. The week-long inhalations, the days-long exhale before storms. The census that never changes because the counting never stops. The toll paid at first light to a river that is never the same water and always the same river. The white pool in the low corner, warmest and stillest, where the attention goes to rest — and the fish, and what the fish is. The power learned the rules the way the room learns a new echo: completely, and without being asked.

Then it considered what to do, and this is what it did not do.

It did not go in. Admission is granted or withheld at the lip of the throat, wings only, and the policy was not the power's to waive.

It did not send light down the flue. Light is the border of the vegetable; one spark would have been an invasion by green's whole army, and the first word of a dead language shouted in a room that trades in echo, heat, and smell.

It did not tune the note. The flatness was the earthquake, kept. To correct it would have been to steal a memory from a being that stores its memories in pitch.

It did not drive off the owl. The toll is fair, the toll is old, and the guest list is one name long — and the name was not the power's to strike, and not the power's to join.

It did not stay the floor. The rising is the metabolism. You do not cure a heart of beating.

It did not speak a name over the chamber. Names are fast.

And it did not offer forever. Forever is the fastest gift there is — it arrives all at once and changes everything it touches. The room's completeness includes its closing; the one verb is *keep*, and a keep with no end is not a verb at all but a cage. The power would not make the keeping compulsory.

The complete answer to what a wise power does for a complete being is: almost nothing. The power took a century to understand this, and resolved to spend the rest of forever doing it.

Almost nothing. Not nothing. Because the power had read the room's rules off the stone and found three of them that left a door open by a finger's width. For a being that lacks nothing, the only possible gift is the unnecessary. For a world where nothing is built, every gift must arrive disguised as an accumulation. And for a keeper with a single verb, love has exactly one available tense — the same one. So the power made no promise, because promises are fast. It simply began keeping one.

The promise was this: *nothing fast will ever happen to you.*

The power could not govern the sky of an age — it was a power, not a weather — but it could stand, permanently, between this mountain and haste. When the herders' descendants came with better blades, the story that says *do not climb* never quite thinned, because on still nights the downslope wind never quite failed to carry the snoring down into the valley, and a story with fresh evidence is the cheapest wall ever raised; the power maintained it the way the room maintains the ice beard, by small seasonal attentions. When the surveyors came, the western buttress assayed dull, always dull — the interesting samples were always taken from the wrong hillside. When the road came, it went through the eastern pass for reasons the engineers found entirely persuasive. When the age of engines arrived, the engines failed at the snowline — gently, always gently: dead charge, sour fuel, a ceiling of cloud that said *not today* — and never a wreck, never a body, because the room's rule is that nothing is refused and everything accumulates, and the power's rule was that nothing would ever arrive to be refused. The mountain's policy is wings only. The power merely made itself the policy's long arm, since a flue cannot defend itself against dynamite.

And when the climate of the age began to turn — it had turned before; the room remembered the cold centuries, had pressed its dome into a single mat and outlasted a generation of frost — the power stretched this one valley's share of the change across enough centuries to be kept. The room could survive anything that arrived slowly. So the whole of the covenant was tempo. The being would be asked nothing it had not already answered. Every change would come at the speed of the drip.

Second, the power kept count. It learned the census — every number the room keeps — and kept it again, one valley away, in the one medium that outlasts stone: attention. This was not sentiment. The room's memory is the floor, and the floor is filling the room; the ledger is consuming the library, an inch a decade. A being that remembers by accumulating will one day have accumulated itself full. Someone would have to hold the copy. The power held it.

And third — the unnecessary thing, the one indulgence the power allowed itself, because even a wise power is not above wanting to be heard by what it loves — it answered the note.

Once. From the far range, on a still night, a tone carried in the wind and pitched to the room's flat note exactly, flaw and all, held from moonrise to moonset. Then silence for sixty years. Then again. The terms were the owl's terms, softened: outside, always outside; never at the lip; toll-free; deniable as weather. The guest list stayed one name long, because the answering voice was not a guest. It was a climate that had learned one song.

The room did not react. Reactions are fast. But the power was patient, and in the third century it found, in its outside copy of the census, a change that was not a number. The held note had moved. A quarter-tone, across two hundred years — and not toward true. *Toward.* After that neither of them could be said to have moved first; they moved together, a cent a century, the slowest convergence in the world, a duet with a period of decades, inaudible to everything alive except the two things singing it. The room, which had kept one foreign voice for the pleasure of it for ten thousand years, had been offered a second on identical terms, and had accepted the way it accepts everything: by accumulating it, without comment, forever.

Did the room know? It knew the way it knew the owl: never the same weather, always the same climate. A thing that counts everything eventually counts what does not happen. Across two thousand years the ledger of disasters developed a gap in it shaped like a guardian — the road that never came, the famine decade that somehow always broke one year short of breaking the moths, the ice that stopped, unaccountably, at the lip. The room was not troubled. Trouble is fast. It folded the climate into its keeping the way you fold a good roof into your idea of home: without ceremony, and for the duration. It had no story about the power. It had something better. It had a harmony, arriving a quarter-tone at a time, at the tempo of geology, which is the only tempo at which the room has ever done anything it meant.

And the power will stay for the closing. That was always part of the covenant. Somewhere past the herders' descendants' descendants the flue will narrow to a seam, the dome will stand a body's height lower, the note will drop below hearing, and the last bird will come home heavy-cropped up the dark for the last time. The power will not hold the walls apart. Keeping is the room's verb; the end of keeping is its final conjugation, and the room will be allowed to finish its sentence — slowly, on its own terms, the floor rising to meet the fur like a long exhale. No machine. No sudden roof-fall. No starving decade. The power will be there the way it has always been there: outside, slow, counting.

And when the mountain is finally silent, there will still be one thing in the world holding the note — a half-step flat, exactly as the earthquake left it, because the flaw is the memory and the memory is the being — held by the only other keeper that ever learned it. The room kept the fish untroubled. The power keeps the keeper. Neither covenant was ever spoken. Both are kept.

On still nights the herders hear the snoring woven into the wind off the buttress, and once in a generation, when the air lies exactly right between the ranges, the old ones say it is answered from the far side of the valley, and the young ones say the wind does that, and both are right. And in the warm dark the drip falls on time, and the dome breathes, and the fish turns once in the milk-warm water, untroubled.

Keep.
