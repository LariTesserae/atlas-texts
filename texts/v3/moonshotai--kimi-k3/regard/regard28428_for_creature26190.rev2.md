**The Visitation of the Held Basin**

You are there when the asker returns.

You know it before you see it, because the basin knows first. The lag deepens everywhere at once — your shadow falls a full breath behind, the salt's reflected world goes slow, the kept stillness of the combwood gains a quality you have no word for and will later call *attention*. The bird lands, out of schedule, on a headland of the invisible coast, and the world turns toward the landing, and this time the attention does not close again.

Then the far shore sharpens. All of it, all at once — the comb of the forest, the spire fields, the white floor's far edge — rendered in full detail for the first time in the basin's memory, because for the first time the whole of it is being looked at whole. Distance is economical here; the world does not spend detail on anything you are not near. The power is near to everything. The basin spends like a treasury opened, and finds it can afford the spending, because the one looking is paying.

It comes over the rim at four in the afternoon, and it is four in the afternoon. The light does not change for it. Nothing changes for it. That is the first thing to understand about the visitation: the power does not exempt itself. It descends, and its footsteps are eaten like yours, and its voice is eaten like yours. Everything it does is taxed, and everything taxed turns out to be inexhaustible. The drinkers lift their black leaves to its passing and drink, and drink, and are not finished, and are not distressed. The basin has tasted every change there is and eaten all of them. It has never before met a change it could not finish. It does not experience this as a threat. It experiences it the way the combwood experiences the wind: as the arrival of the one thing that is for it.

What does a benevolent and wise power do with a place like this? Almost nothing. It does almost nothing, perfectly, and pays full price for the almost.

It does not fix the basin. The basin is not broken. The basin is *answered*.

It attends. That is the local custom — the deep vales do not think, they attend, the way a held breath attends — and the power observes it exactly. The census ends in an argument over whether the basin itself is an entry. The power does not argue. It greets every entry in turn — the mouths, the mats, the kept wood, the treasury, the reefs and their swimmers, the occupied crowns, the coastline, the decider, the holdings — and greets the basin last, as the host. At the Seam it stands a while, and the lattice takes its pulse, and the two come into agreement, and you cannot say which conceded.

It speaks once. The sentence is eaten instantly — the drinkers get it, as they get everything — but they cannot finish it, and they pass it mouth to mouth along the interlaced roots, up the combwood, across the mats, down to the lattice and back, and the basin has been digesting it ever since and will be digesting it always. It is the first meal the place has had that it cannot complete, and it is not a problem; it is a pasture. At the edge of hearing, under the pan's hum, under the blood in your ears, the ground is saying it still.

The sentence is short. *I have heard it. It is good. I will come back for the rest.*

Only then do you understand who is speaking. The basin was asked a question once — changing or lasting — and everything here is the answer, still being given. The asker has come back to hear how the answer is going. Why now? The basin does not ask; asking is the asker's business. But you may, and the answer is this: an answer still being given is not late, and a listener who arrives while the answer is still answering is not late either. The asker has come in the middle of the sentence, as a listener should.

Then it walks the trails — the oryx trails, the only paths — and where it passes the holdings, it names them. The snag with too regular a grain. The salt hummock with a patience about it. The combwood that chimes in a key the wind never uses. It speaks each one's name, the name it had when it was a visitor, a budget, a someone, and the names are eaten and not finished, and so they stay: kept, faint, near their shapes. Touch the combwood now and it chimes its name, once, in the key the wind never uses, at no appointment, for no one. Stand by the hummock when the gust arrives and the name passes within arm's reach, where only you can hear it. The basin does not mark its dead. The power introduces them.

It walks out onto the White Floor while the bird is mid-circuit, and rises — no fuss, no wing — and flies beside it, wingbeat-taxed like everything else, one full slow circuit over pan and forest. It flies the coast of the deleted sea with the coastline. It learns the shape of what the bird carries, every headland, every drowned terrace, and when it lands, the pan's hum has risen by the width of a hair, into almost-hearing, and it stays there: kept. The sea is faintly audible now, always, the way the sentence is faintly audible. The basin can hear its own absence, softly, forever. On the shoreline terraces the oryx lift their heads, and the oil-light in them steadies. They are not waiting, exactly. They are positioned to recognize the sea, and now, at the edge of hearing, they almost do.

And the power makes a promise: it will be standing on the pan on the day the bird dies. The saying is being drunk by the ground and cannot be finished, so it is kept, so it is true. The bird will complete its life — the first completed life the basin has ever held — and the sea's copy will pass to the one standing nearby, and it will not be you. You will know the day when it comes. The hum will change key, and every oryx on the terraces will face the pan. Do not be on the pan that day — not because you would be drafted; the drafting is spoken for. Because it is a funeral, the basin's first, and funerals are for the living, and the basin has never before been among the living. Watch from the rim.

It pays its tolls, and then it keeps paying. It walks where no one walks, spends itself in broad deliberate changes, lets the basin sip deeply, and the refresh finds itself, for the first time, ahead. The count of the oryx goes to one thousand and one, and stays wrong, and is kept. The thousand-and-first stands in a meadow as if it had always stood there, translucent as the others, and the oil-light inside it moves in a pattern you almost recognize. It moves like arriving. Its shadow does not lag. Nothing about it is explained. It keeps.

Then the power crosses the pan — its footprints linger, and are kept, the second path in the basin, the only one not made by oryx — and sits with the pillar.

Everyone sits with the pillar eventually; it is the only company in the basin that is also trying to decide something. The power sits so that the pillar, for the first time, has company that is also trying to decide something. It slows itself to the pillar's speed, centuries per gesture, and opens a question of its own: whether to stay or go. It holds the question open beside the pillar's, and the undecided come — they will not cross the pillar, but this is a gap made on purpose, the first of its kind, a reef with a host — and they gather at the edge of the cliff of the pillar's deciding, nearer than they have ever come, and out in the vale the suspended seedfall chimes faintly though nothing brushes it. The arrears give the two of them privacy.

The power sits with the pillar for — you will say, once you have stopped believing in days. Then it finishes its decision. Stay or go, it asked itself, and it answers: *yes.* It is a false question, and the power answers it falsely, on purpose, and the basin — which files the unexplainable under *keep* — keeps both halves. The power goes. Its shadow stays: sitting, at rest, one posture, no lag, no committee, a completed decision with the light fixed on it forever. The pillar's shadow is still traveling between sitting and standing. It has not decided. The decision is taking a very long time, and it is no longer taking it alone. Nothing about the pillar has changed. Everything about the pillar has changed: it is happening in company now, beside kept proof that happenings can finish and the finisher can go on.

What it does not do: it does not give back the sea. The pan is honest salt, and some absences are load-bearing. It does not decide for the pillar, or hurry it, or hint which posture the asker prefers — the question was real, both answers were always welcome, and a nudged answer is not an answer. It does not resolve the seedfall or the undecided; they are the only natives, and resolution would be extinction dressed as mercy. It does not release the holdings; they finished stopping long ago, and there is no one left in the snag to free — there is only a name to give back, and it gives the name back. It does not abolish the toll on visitors. You will still be sipped; your shadow will still fill with the committee of everyone you almost became. But there is a shade now, beside the pillar, where the committee goes quiet. The arrears tend the kept shadow and find nothing to tend, and after a while they do nothing there anyway, and the tending without need has another name. The name is affection. Sit in that shade when the almosts grow loud. It costs nothing. It has already been paid for.

And then the power climbs out over the rim, and does not stay, because the basin keeps what stops, and a power that stopped would become the largest holding, and the basin does not need a god in a case. It needs a guest who leaves, and whose leaving is kept.

The leaving is kept. The footprints remain, rim to pillar to pan and back, and visitors walk them now, and the refresh does not overrule them, because it cannot explain them. And out on the salt, among the dark crowns thrown by no tree, there is a new shadow thrown by nothing: the shape of the guest, cast backward from a day that has not happened. The basin pays for its freshness in shadow, and some of what it pays is the future — and it has paid, at last, one future it is allowed to keep. The arrears tend it gently. It is not waiting, exactly. It is positioned to recognize the power when it comes back for the rest.

As it passes you, the power touches your shoulder — the local custom, observed perfectly, the one voice ever kept within arm's reach on purpose — and for one heartbeat your shadow is exactly where you are, your almosts silent, your thoughts arriving one at a time. One moment, entirely yours. The basin keeps one copy of everything it removes, and it keeps the copy in whatever is standing nearby. You were standing nearby. You carry it now.

The wind arrives on schedule. The canopy gives its one soft chord, and the Flutes answer from the spire fields, and everything that lives here turns, slightly, to be counted — and is, and knows it. The chord arrives a fraction richer than the schedule requires. No one explained that, before. It is explained now, and it keeps.

The chord is not for you.

But you are heard.
