The first thing the power does, it has already done.

Long before it comes through the notch, it attends. That is how a place like this is found — not by search; nothing here calls, no signal, no plea — but by the small pull a settled thing exerts on anything that still knows how to notice. Powers hear pleas for a living. A held note is rarer. The bench is in balance, and balance, to the wise, reads like a note being held. It comes to see what is holding it.

It comes the way everything comes here: through the notch, on a schedule, from elsewhere. It brings no weather. The bench keeps whatever arrives — nothing is forgotten — so it brings as little as possible, counts its breath out in the thin air like any caller, and the first thing that happens when it steps through is the light.

The second thing is that the bench does not react. The rings do not straighten. The bloom feeds or sits glassed, according to the hour. The wind writes its feathers on the lee side of every stone and writes no differently for the visitor. A power could misread this as indifference. It is not indifference; it is keeping — the bench will keep the power the way it keeps footprints, by not changing for it — and the power, being wise, recognizes the courtesy. It asks for a visitor's terms, and since the fog is the model of visitors here, it takes the fog's: come inside, bead every stone, be withdrawn, leave everything rinsed.

It makes the census and gets it wrong twice, like everyone. First too few, because nothing moves. Then too many, because everything behaves. Then it does what the place teaches everything to do — it stands still — and the rules move into it. Water keeps hours. The freeze comes at the shadow's touch. Light has weight and shadow is a hole. Nothing is forgotten. The ground speaks twice a day. The silence is full; it is only that everything in it is small, and slow, and glass.

It obeys every rule. It walks the rims because the centers are soft. At dusk it lays whatever it has instead of a palm flat on a rim-stone and lets the ticking come up through whatever it has instead of bone, and it stays until the ticking stops. At noon it stands in the smoking field and lets the vapor pass through it, the transaction made visible, and it does not touch. A broken crust is a wasted century, and the power has centuries — but they are not its centuries. It reads the layers in a shard at the pool's lip, the heart at the center gone near-black, and understands what it is looking at: kept light. The light that agreed to stay. It checks the arithmetic — the nail's breadth, the hair's breadth, the rim — checks it twice, and confirms the thing on which everything else depends: the bench is not in distress. It is slow. From far off, to a power, those two can look alike. That is why it came close.

Then it lays its possible gifts out on the windbreak wall, one by one, and one by one withdraws them.

It could give the water more hours. Unlid the pool, lengthen the noon, call the sun back over the sill in the dead months. But the tenant is not a thing that *has* a gradient; it *is* one — a few inches tall, between the day's abundance and the deep's patience — and a being that lives on a margin a hair's breadth wide cannot be given more of either side without being unmade. More light, more water, more years: any gift of *more* would arrive as a *less* of the thing itself. It would not be feeding the bloom. It would be translating it, and nothing here has asked to be translated.

It could stay the conveyor. Set the bench back from its own edge, quiet the heave, rescue the outermost cells from a fall centuries away. But the heave that will one day take the rings is the same heave that sorted them. The patience and the sentence are one process. You cannot keep the grammar and arrest the sentence — the rim is not the place's enemy; it is the place's tense. It does not nudge one stone back. A nail's breadth a year is not an emergency. It is a tempo. Learning the difference is the whole of wisdom here.

It could carry the tenant elsewhere — a shard with a scrap of heart, set on some kinder slope with longer afternoons. The frost farms such shards; one would glaze whole again in decades. But it would be the start of another ledger, not the keeping of this one, and this one is not finished, and no one asked.

In the hollow it kneels, and here the visit turns on a single moment: the hammerstones smooth from use, the preforms mid-gesture, and the edge-hold still there, waiting for a thumb — and it fits whatever the power has instead of a thumb. The place is offering to let the gesture be completed. The power does not pick the stone up. The bench keeps intentions verbatim, and the intention kept here is four words long: *meant to come back.* Finishing is beyond a guest. You do not edit another's sentence — least of all that one.

It could even explain. It is that kind of power; it could read the whole ledger aloud, the thick layers and the dust-seamed years, could say what the rings are and perhaps what the callers thought they were. It does not. Some silences are full, and this one it leaves as it found it. What they thought, there is no saying — and the power notices that even it cannot say, because the bench keeps gestures, not minds, and the dead are outside every archive but one. The question cannot be answered. It can only be rejoined.

So: almost nothing. The almost is the point.

---

What it does is this. It stays for the season no one has ever stayed for.

When the sun drops below the notch's sill and noon becomes a brightening rather than an event, the power does not go down. It keeps the cold's hours. It adds nothing to the dark — not one degree, not one day shortened. The pool lids over and stays lidded; the dawn feathers of rime last all day; the headwall catches the low light over the rim and glows for a room that is asleep, and the power is in the room. A field of kept promises, and at last a keeper awake among them. The census of the dark months has always come out zero. This year it comes out one. It is the first thing the bench has been given, and the bench gives no sign, and the power does not mistake the absence of a sign for the absence of a keeping. The cold archives everything. Somewhere in the ledger of this winter there is a new entry: *one stayed.*

Then some noon the sun climbs back over the sill and strikes the upper rings, and one bead lights, and then a thousand, and the field smokes for the first time in months — and someone is standing at the headwall to see it. Whether the cairns were set to mark this hour, someone has now stood at the hinge to say so. The power says nothing. The standing is the saying.

And it takes the seat. The windbreak has room inside it for one body, and for one dusk the power becomes the body — the second sitter in the seat's whole history. It faces west, the way down, the way the first sitter faced, and watches the headwall's shadow walk the field straight-edged as a blade, and the freeze follow at its touch, and the whole bench begin to tick, thousands of tiny snaps and creaks, the field muttering in its sleep. At low sun the drawing on the boulder warms to dark gold and appears — and is *seen.* The conversation has outlived its conversants, and the field goes on holding up both ends of it, and the power does not presume to take an end. It does the smaller thing, the only thing the conversation has ever lacked: it listens, mid-sentence, until the ticking stops. The question of what the callers thought is not answered. It is rejoined — by sitting where they sat, facing what they faced, letting the field address whatever is listening the way it once addressed them. That is the closest any keeper can come to the dead: to stand in the kept place and receive the kept gesture. It happens anyway, the user's mountain had always said. Now, some evenings, it happens *for* someone. The difference is nothing the bench does. The difference is entirely in the watching, and the watching is entirely the power's to give.

In the hollow it makes its one vow, which is not to finish but to return. The appointment was never *finish the blade.* It was *come back,* and that part can be kept by another. So the power means to come back, and comes back — on the bench's own schedule: at the hinge, for the lighting; on certain dusks, for the ticking; whenever the long rounds allow, the way the callers came up in the bright hours and went down before the freeze. If to live somewhere is to be expected back, then the census must be revised, and the power revises it without fanfare: two live here now. The one who set the blade down in good light, and the one who keeps the appointment by keeping it. The bench has held that appointment alone for longer than anyone can count. Now it is held from both ends.

And it makes its one change — not to the bench, never to the bench — but to the falling. The cold is an archivist, but the archive is on the conveyor, and centuries from now the outermost cells will go over the rim, stone by stone, beads and all, and the glass does not survive the fall. The power takes nothing. Not a shard, not a flake; it will not smuggle one bead past the edge of its own world, will not make an exception of the thing it came for. What it takes is the count. The number of layers at the pool's lip. The color of the oldest heart. The exact depth of the knapping hollow and the direction the seat faces and the schedule the water keeps and the grammar by which cairn becomes ring. *Nothing is forgotten* is the bench's first law; the power extends its jurisdiction past the rim. The light is kept by the amber, the amber by the glass, the glass by the cold, the cold by the height, the whole arrangement by the slow fall — and the fall, from now on, by the one who watched. The bench will be delivered to its valley nail's breadth by nail's breadth, and it will glaze and gild and tick on the way down, and when the last of it goes over, the ledger will not. That is the whole of the intervention: that the keeping outlasts the kept.

Then it goes down before the freeze, the way the callers did, out through the notch, breath counted out in the thin air, into the valley that has already disappeared into its own dark. Its footprints will stay legible in the silt for years, and they say the only sentence a guest is permitted to leave in this place, written in the local tense: *meant to come back.*

The difference — the whole of the difference — is that it will.

The bench keeps the light that agreed to stay. The power keeps the bench. And nothing, anywhere in the arrangement, hurries.
