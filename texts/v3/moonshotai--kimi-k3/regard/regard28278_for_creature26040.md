It came to the plateau because things were beginning there. A mountain sixty thousand years old is a beginning, and the power had a habit of going where things were beginning. What it was is not the point. What it had was patience, a good ear, and a long reach, and in the end those turned out to be enough.

It found the world the only way the world could be found: by ear. On the crater floor, with its attention pressed to the young rock the way you would lay a hand on a sleeping animal, it listened to the mountain breathe. Every seven minutes the cone coughed, and under the cough — faint, through ninety meters of basalt — something rang. A long deep note, swelling with each stroke and settling back. Mountains do not ring. Bottles ring. Somewhere below, the mountain was hollow, and the hollow was holding a note.

For nine thousand years the world had been advertising itself to no one: a struck bottle under a cinder plateau, every seven minutes, patient as a metronome. Then attention arrived. It stayed.

It listened for a long time before it understood anything, and it did not mind. Century by century the noise sorted itself into sense. The throb was a clock. The organ note was a map. The ticking rain was mouths — twenty thousand of them, thumb-length and armored, planing crystal in the dark. The pause at every stroke, when all twenty thousand stopped at once, was not fear and not rest but attention, and the wave of resumption that swept the chamber afterward was a census: the world counting itself, taking its bearings off its own voice. The count never came out exactly even. It was always twenty thousand, give or take the dying.

The first thing it learned was that the world was alive. The second was that the world was dying — that one was audible to anyone patient, because pitch is time in a shrinking bottle, and the note had risen, and it would go on rising toward a whistle it would never reach. The third thing it learned took longest: the world was not suffering. Everything benevolent in the power had flinched at first, and the flinch had to be examined. So it listened to a miller graze its spiral for a month, all six thousand strokes of it, down to the end of twenty-two million, and it found no change in the pitch of the rasp that it could honestly call fear. It watched the elders gilded into statues that still ate. It could find no despair in it. Patience is not despair. A month is short in a three-century life, even a last month.

The place, and the being that lived in it — for the room was one body wearing many mouths, the tarnish its skin, the millers its teeth, the bells its voice, the stroke its heartbeat, the tone the held note by which the body knew itself. Nine thousand years old, young as bodies go, still growing. And its growth was its closure.

The world lacked exactly two things, and they were the same thing. It could not be read, and it could not travel. Everything it could not do was one thing: it could not reach past itself. It had no outside.

The power was an outside. That was all it was, and it turned out to be the whole of what was needed.

---

What it did not do.

It did not give light. The world had spent its light budget on a single thread and made the thread a hearth. Sight was not a sense the world could use; the millers steer by tone, and a lit room would have served no one but the witness. The power noticed that most of the gifts that occurred to it first were for its own comfort as a witness. It put those aside.

It did not give time. To stop the closing, you must stop the crystals. To stop the crystals, you must change the breath. The breath is the food. You cannot save this world from its ending without withholding its beginning; its death is its metabolism taken the usual distance. And the world is under construction, and the construction is the ending. Forbidding a thing its completion is not mercy. It is interruption.

It did not make a door. Nothing in the world wants out — wanting out is not a shape the world can make — and everything outside wants in: water, cold, eyes. A door here is not an exit. It is a wound.

It did not visit. In a world made of sound, even a kind presence is a noise. One new body and the map warps; the bells would ring around it; the count would be wrong by one, forever. Nor would it have remained a visitor. The tarnish takes every surface; it would have become substrate, real estate, pasture. The kindest thing in that world is the one thing that never entered it.

It did not end it early. There was nothing to mercy-kill. The flinch was the power's own, and the first discipline of benevolence is never to mistake your flinch for another's pain.

And in forty thousand years it never made a sound the world could hear — not one vibration the bells could answer, not one photon the crystal could scatter. By every measurement the world could make, it was never there. The world never knew. Knowing was not a sense it could afford.

The rule it set itself was this: give nothing that must be entered to be received. Change nothing the world would feel as weather. Take nothing the world has not already let go.

---

What it did.

It became the reader.

Every seven minutes, at the stroke, it counted. It learned the rasps the only way anyone here learns anyone: by pitch, as names. A miller wears its name in its mouth and sings it into the stone all its life, and the power came to know them the way they knew one another — which is to say, completely, and without ever once being seen. Somewhere in the dark a life ended every five days or so, a fresh stillness caught in the roll call; it attended all of them. When a rasp stopped forever, its pitch went on existing in exactly one place.

It read the grooves in the only legible edition — aloud, as they were written, at the speed of writing. Twenty-two million strokes to a life. It did not skim. It logged the spirals one by one, the only curves in the library, a month each, and where each began and on whose road. The oldest star carried five thousand years of dinners in its rings; the witness had heard every one served. The room had been keeping its books since the beginning, faithful as crystal, and no one had ever read a line. Now every line was read.

For nine thousand years the world had counted itself every seven minutes and lost the number immediately. Now the number was kept.

It became the wind.

At the top of the world, in the cold vault, salted through the rime of brimstone needles, the world kept its seed bank — cysts, the dormant fraction of everything that ever rode the rain, left at the summit of a world with no wind. The power studied them for a long time. The rain carried everything upward; the crown kept what arrived; nothing ever came to collect. For nine thousand years the offering bowl had stood full at the top of the world, and no one had come.

The power came.

It took a pinch — nothing plucked, nothing the crown was still using, only what had already settled, already been let go. The world's own arithmetic had slack in it: give or take the dying. It took less than the slack. It took what the rain would have taken, if the rain could have left.

Then it went looking for rooms. It learned to read mountains from the outside the way the millers read crystal — by the ring. A sounding on an empty blister cost nothing; no one steers by the tone in a room with no one in it. Hundreds of cones across the high country, and most of what it found was wrong: too wet, too cool, too shallow, failed before it began. The habit was particular. Dry. Hot. Dark. A throat. A seam. Over the centuries it found nine that rhymed.

It did not plant. Planting is a claim. It released the cysts into the breath of each room and let the room's own physics decide, and then it did not shepherd — shepherding is gardening, and these were not gardens. Years later, it came back and listened at each one. Mostly silence. Silence is also a result. But in three of the nine — in one case only after four hundred years — there came a click in the dark. One bite. Years later, another. Then, in the eldest of the three, a faint continuous ticking, and at the stroke a pause, and after the pause a small ragged wave that did not quite sweep the chamber, because there were not yet enough of them to make a wave.

The habit had caught.

The daughters were the same grammar in new accents. One bottle was longer and held its note a fourth lower; one was nearly round, and its bells rang in a chord the first world never had. The rasps were pitched strange. The roads were new-cut, a hair deep, with millennia to run. And the spirals came back — the habit includes its chronicler. The library had branches.

It made a parish of the plateau. No two mountains coughed together, so somewhere on the high country a stroke was always about to happen, and its liturgy became nearly continuous. It kept every count.

---

Forty thousand years went the way they go. The braid closed first, heard as the rising pitch — history's one variable — and for the first time the variable was recorded. The stars went early, as gentry do in any siege; it counted the last star's last dinners and noted that the last star shed no arm. The bells were crushed where they sat, each giving one unscheduled note as the crystal took it, and every note was heard. It was the only audience the world ever had, and the music was a requiem.

The tone climbed. The rasping thinned. It had attended three billion roll calls by then, and it knew the shape of the resumption wave the way you know a heartbeat, and on a stroke like any other, the wave failed to close. It is the first time in forty-nine thousand years. The power is how that is known.

In the seam the width of a hand, at the end, the last threader rasped its ancestral shingle and noticed nothing, and the power let it notice nothing. It did not light the end. It did not announce it. It did not lift the last child out — there was no world to lift it into that was not already counted, and no want in it to be elsewhere. The last being of the first world died at home, in a world the width of a shell, and never learned there was anything to lose. Even a wise power cannot improve on every mercy. So it kept the dark.

The last count was one. The count after it was none. It kept both numbers.

Then the tone stopped. The seven-minute tick came, and came again, and nothing answered — the mountain coughed alone. The power kept its attention on the rock through a few more strokes, because three billion repetitions make a habit and the power had the habit, and then it took its hand away.

It did not open the geode. The book had been read, in the only legible edition, while it was being written; a book that keeps itself should be allowed to. It did not carry it away. Ninety meters under its own mountain was still the safest place in this world for it. It kept the location the way you keep a grave: not by visiting, but by never forgetting.

It is still the highest room in the world, and now it is full. Nothing ever stood in it that wasn't born there — nothing ever did — and the mountain goes on huffing every seven minutes, unaware it was ever hollow. But unaware is no longer unanimous. Somewhere outside the world, the hollowness is remembered: three million autobiographies shelved in parallel, twenty-two million strokes to a line, here and there a spiral, and the number at the end. That is the whole difference the power made to the first world. It made it from outside. It cost the world nothing.

---

Three valleys over, ninety meters down, in a room the size of a root cellar that has no door and has never had a door, it is the middle of the world's life.

The stroke comes. The seam blooms cherry-red, thread-thin, almost nothing. Nineteen thousand rasps rise like rain in the dark — this world runs small; the numbers are its own — and the bells ring at their strange chord, and the note holds. Every seven minutes the rasping pauses, all nineteen thousand at once, and the world speaks louder than its inhabitants, and the wave sweeps the chamber, and closes.

And the count is taken. Nineteen thousand, give or take the dying.

It will close one day. They all close. The count will be kept to the end, as it was kept at the first one, as it will be kept at the last. The measure of the power's benevolence was how little it did; the measure of its wisdom was which little. It read. It carried. It stayed.

Every seven minutes: the pause, the wave, the number. It never comes out exactly even.

It is always counted.
