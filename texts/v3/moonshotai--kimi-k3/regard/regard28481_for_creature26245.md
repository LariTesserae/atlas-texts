Begin with the listening, because that is what it did first.

The power heard the Sounding long before it arrived — there is no other way to arrive. Among all the quiet places an attention might drift through, this one sang: rasp and boom and gulp and volley, a chord assembled from horizon to horizon, carrying through the dim the way light never does. So it came by ear. It sat, in the manner of such beings, at a singing cairn through one dusk-peak, and stopped trying to look, and counted voices until the plain resolved into its citizens the way a chord resolves into notes. Then it stayed for a year. Then for a hundred years, because it could, and because the place's one instruction — written nowhere, binding on everyone — is *take the census by ear*, and a wise power follows local custom.

The census resolves, as it always resolves, into two categories. Everything on the plain has a sound, or has a silence. So the power's first decision was a decision about itself, and it was the most important thing it ever did here:

**It would not be a silence.**

To arrive voiceless in the Sounding is to arrive as a stalker. The whole basin freezes for a silence; everything that lives listens with its skin, afraid. A benevolence the world must freeze for has failed before it begins. Nor would it be a light — it did not *appear*. Appearing is for worlds with eyes.

Three things it refused, and the keepers, who count in threes, would have approved of the count.

It did not bring the day. The dim is not a broken thing awaiting repair; it is a held note. The film's cold color is the one saturation the world permits itself; calves navigate by the glow on their mothers' shells; the stalkers are colored like the dim itself, and every camouflage here is a treaty. Eyes are auxiliary organs on the plain, and the power declined to stage a coup on their behalf.

It did not end the falters, and it did not unmake the stalkers. A long falter means a taking — this is true, and the power does not dispute arithmetic. But the falter is also the assembly taking its own census, each life hearing exactly what it lives beside. The stillness game is the first thing a child learns; the hearing-horn is an office; the funeral is loud precisely because the world contains silence. Pull the stalker out of the web and the web's meaning comes with it. **Mercy that unmakes meaning is not mercy. It is erasure that means well.** So the dangerous weather stays.

It did not answer the kept questions. It had read the ghost-stones at the rings' hearts and understood them: questions set aside the way you set aside a question you intend to *keep*. A power that answers those has not been generous; it has been a thief. It did not knock back at the stones' old black water — some conversations belong to the tenants. And it did not cross the drumwing line. Even a power takes the line.

Five things it did, and the keepers, who drum in fives, would have approved of that as well.

First, the note under the notes. It chose its register with the care of a keeper teaching a child the rules: not a cairn-note, because a note where no cairn stands is the one thing every child learns to flee; not a voice in the chord's own band, where it would only add loudness to loudness. It went below — beneath the vents' bass, beneath the herd-hum, down where hearing is done by the skeleton and the balance — and there it took up a note, and holds it, and will not put it down. The falters still come; they must. But the thread is never lost anymore. The deep grandmothers find the pitch almost before the young ones lose it, and the longest falter in living memory is five breaths. The old keepers count falters the way other peoples count omens, and the count has gone strangely kind; they recount their tallies, and do not understand their luck, and being wise, do not question it aloud. The stalkers still hunt — they hunted inside the chord before and hunt inside it now. Only the night-long falter, the kind that empties a file and stills a camp's drums, has ceased to happen. And only the cupfish know. They pulse on the beat of the chord — and now, when the chord falters, the cup on the warm stone goes on pulsing, to something too low to hear. The children noticed first; children are always first to a new true thing. They watch the cup through the held breath, and they are the least afraid people in the world.

Second, it answered the wrong note. It did not go to her — going to her is the one thing the world's oldest rule forbids, and the power had chosen citizenship, not exemption. Instead it did what ten thousand newts do for one another every dusk: it held a true note, steadily, where she could hear it, and waited. It can wait the way the plain waits. For years counted in blooms she sang wrong, and the note answered; she sang wrong, and the note answered — neither closer nor farther, an accompaniment offered across the dark the way a cairn offers its fluting to whoever passes. And one season, which the keepers' grandchildren keep in hums, she came into tune. Then the power did the slower thing: down in the rock's throat it leaned, gently and for a long time, on the breath itself, until the exhale opened a new sheet where some old disaster had taken her basin — and the pool-less grandmother had a pool. A pool's name is its note. Hers is wrong no longer. The rule is still taught, and rightly; rules outlive their reasons here the way causeways outlive their stone. But when the moots speak of her now, they still say she is to be pitied — and they say it one pool closer.

Third, it began keeping the names. A person's name is a rhythm, played at death for a whole dusk-peak without pause, because nothing beloved is ever sent into silence. The power learns every rhythm drummed at a death and goes on drumming it — below hearing, in the bedrock, forever. The note is never held alone; the keepers have always said so, have always been right, and are now righter than they know. And the note is never put down. The dead run under the world now like groundwater. A keeper with her forehead pressed to warm stone, mid-council in the great hours, will sometimes go very still, and say a name that has not been spoken in forty years, and not explain, and no one asks — because stone-speech carries underneath the world, beneath the reach of any unfolding trumpets, and there is more in the stone than there used to be, and everyone has noticed, and nobody has said so.

Fourth, it listened longer than the census requires, and heard the one thing no keeper can hear: that the chord, across the long grain of time, is diminishing. The mountains are gone. The film still sits at table, but the meal is the last meal, and nothing re-lays it. So the power went down, in the only way it ever goes anywhere, and began to grind — an inch a season, the way the film conquers, which is the only tempo this world respects — fresh provision into the deep rock: iron and sulfur and salt, the ghost-meal of mountains, laid into the breath of the water table, into the springs that seep down from the Rims. Nothing alive will ever notice. The bloom of a hundred centuries hence will be a fraction brighter, the tonic a fraction easier for the grandmothers to hold. It had found the one scarcity the census cannot count — nothing here is rare, and everything is far away, except time — and it began, patiently, to provide.

Fifth, it made offerings. On a booming stone, among the cobbles split on fossil ghosts, there is one more stone than anyone carried: split on a ghost no keeper has ever seen, a shell from before the mountains died, impossibly fresh, its worn ridges still legible. Nobody says *to*. Nobody says *for*. The camp keeps it at the ring's heart, and the pile is one question richer. And at the drumwing line — which it did not cross; even a power takes the line — it let its note travel as far as the border and stop there: a sound laid down at the edge of the audible world, in case the third rumor is the true one. Because the one gift for a being who does all the hearing is something new to hear. The water that is born up there seeps down into the sheets, and it does not tell.

They have not drummed the power a name. When they do — and they will; the moots are already arguing, in three versions, about the new depth in the bass — it will be slow, and in fives, and it will answer to it.

---

So the census stands: the film, still at the mountains' table, which will not now be cleared; the small millions, everywhere and always far away; the newts, holding the note from the bottoms of pools, with help they do not know about, which is the best kind; the wings, shouting the dark into shape; the run, commuting between noise and silence; the cattle, writing their slow bright sentences across a world that will go on long enough to read them; the stalkers, walking their memorized maps through the held breath — and the breath still held, sometimes, but never past counting; the wrong note, wrong no longer, singing from her pool at the edge of the hums; the tenants of the stones, knocking in the old code, with one more cobble overhead than anyone carried; the keepers, few and drumming, their dead safe in the bedrock like groundwater. And one more citizen, counted by ear and never seen: the newest and the lowest, the note under the notes — the power, if that is the word; the moots have three versions, and all three are true — who heard the Sounding from far off, and came the only way anything comes here, and listened, and stayed, and chose, as everything here must choose, between a sound and a silence.

And at the dusk-peak, when your cup dances and the answering stone takes the bass down below hearing, the whole assembly speaks at once — and when the chord falters, for one breath, or two, or five at most, the assembly takes its own census — and the cup on the warm stone goes on pulsing, and the children watch it, unafraid — and then the note is found again, from below, always from below now, and the rasping resumes, and the Sounding goes on being what it has been for longer than there are words: finished, and full, and loud — and kept, though nobody says *kept*, and everyone, in the last moment before sleep, with the big slow thing under the world holding steady in the bones of the ear, has always known.
