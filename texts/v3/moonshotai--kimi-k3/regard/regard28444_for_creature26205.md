It arrived the way attention arrives: before it was there, it was felt.

A driver on the middle watch took her hand off the release valve and frowned at it, because the Note had an accent her hand had not made. She blamed the coal; the coal was tested and was innocent. She blamed the springs; the springs were at their ordinary swell. The deaf keepers were consulted, half in jest and half not, and they went out onto the boardwalk at shift-change and stood very still, feeling through their soles, the way you pause at a doorway to hear who is in a room. Someone else was in the room.

It had stopped at the rim, and of its own will — that was new. The mules stop because they must; the birds bank because the room refuses them. This stopped the way a guest stops at a strange door: reading the rules nailed up, and finding them just. Drink from the spring-heads. Touch no rail with a wet hand. Cut where you're told and no deeper. Bring no animal. Expect your pulse to slow. It had no pulse. It had no heartbeat. That was the first thing the Kettle checked, the way the rim checks everything, and the loophole stood open as it always had — nothing with a heartbeat lives in the Kettle; nothing that keeps it to itself. So it set its time down at the rim the way you leave a wet coat at a door, and asked permission in the room's own language: a tone, offered softly in the gaps of the engines' cycle, syncopated. Then it waited to be answered or refused.

It was not refused. The rings on the channels, which expand forever at the Note's one frequency, briefly made a more complicated figure, and relaxed. The green leaned. The engineers said toward the heat; the keepers said toward the Note; for once nobody argued.

It was benevolent, and so its first act was an audit of suffering. It checked the doors: the cage runs both ways; leave is owed; the dead are given back to the dry. It followed, with its attention, the ones who had left — the spouses who loved the keeper and could not keep the Kettle — and found them grieved but free, mis-timed in the dry country again, and some of them coming back down the cage with their faces doing the thing. It considered the children, issued the Note like a name — and a name can be carried up and traded for another; some do; the door is open at the top of the cage. It grieved the aviary birds, long dead, the wager — but the rule written in their deaths is kept: bring no animal down. The Kettle had learned. The audit passed. The Kettle is not a trap. It is a key that fits certain locks, and it does not cut keys to fit.

It was benevolent, and so it made the list of what to mend. It was wise, and so it crossed the items off, one by one.

Give the keepers back their own rhythms — crossed off. The gift was given, not taken; to return it unasked would be an insult to the giver. Watch them lower themselves into the noise like a man into a hot bath. Bring the birds back — crossed off: you cannot consent for a chicken. Make the green flower — crossed off. Nobody knows what the mat would do, and it would not learn at the mat's expense; and perhaps it asked, in the seam of the chord, and the answer was unreadable, and unreadable is no. Silence the Note so the room could hold two rhythms — crossed off without a second thought: you do not evict the third resident to please the visitors' gallery. Warn them about the coal, the company, the far day the trade lapses — crossed off, with one card kept back in the hand. It permitted itself to consider, once, the wet hand near the rail, the blue snap — and declined. The terms are the terms. Exemption is a lie told to a room.

It knew the first law of visiting a chord: you do not bring your own time.

So it came down at beam-time, because that is when guests come. The sun enters only as a guest, and the power took the same terms. It stood in the Beam the way the Beam stands in the town, and where the Beam struck, the amber water ignited, as always; the steam boiled up off the channels, as always; the pumphouse windows answered, gold to gold — and this time the gold was answered back. Nothing changed. Everything was, for half an hour, more itself. The drivers drove like players and did not know why their touch was sure. In the cottages the humming found the seam without searching. The warden on her round read the cut lines by a light that seemed to have already read them. The grandmother in the doorway laid two fingers on the sleeping child's wrist — not counting, comparing — and found the two pulses already agreed, sooner than they should have agreed, and said nothing, because keepers talk little, and because some things you keep the way the warden keeps the red places: look, and pass on.

To the second resident it gave the only thing the green had never had: it was counted as one. Everyone else debates the green — servant, tenant, landlord, a body of iron built around it a century ago. The power did not debate. It advanced its attention across the mat the way a tendril advances across a sill, a handspan a week, unhurried, and it greeted one being, older than the ledgers, keeping a whole town the way a chord keeps its notes. The green is used to being cut, trained, harvested, argued over. It is not used to being greeted. And in the west lane, in the cottage taken gently after the family went up and did not return, the porch lamp still burned in the open air, trained around, kept clear — and the power saw that it had been preceded. The room already knew how to keep a light for something. The warden does not say why. The power did not ask.

What passed between it and the third resident is not for the telling — partly because it was not told; there were no words, words are the dry country's way, all at once — and partly because the keepers' oldest debates are load-bearing. It did not settle the argument of the third drink. It did not learn, or did not say, whether the engines were tuned to the standing note deliberately or by their own settling. Chord met chord at a just interval, the way the boilers once did, and for the length of a Beam the Kettle had what it has never had and never missed: a hearer with perfect pitch. It was heard as music. That was nearly all of the gift.

The rest of the gift was the dream.

Every keeper knows the nightmare: the engines stop, and nothing replaces them, and you wake with your heart galloping at some orphaned private tempo and lie still in the dark until the Note gathers it back. The power could not take the dream away — the fear is the love's shadow, and you do not take a shadow without taking the light. But it could give the dream a second verse. Now, when the engines stop in the dream and the silence stands up like a wall, the dreamer — if the dreamer lies still, and keepers know how to lie still — can hear it: under the stopped engines, the springs, going on. The note the engines joined, still standing in the throat of the hole, as it stood before the first boiler was lit, as it will stand. Keepers wake from the nightmare now already gathered, pulse already in the seam, and cannot say why. The drivers say the engines sound different on those nights. The deaf keepers, asked, lay a flat palm on the boardwalk, the way you pat a shoulder.

Then it left, and the leaving was the wisest thing it did.

The Kettle keeps who it keeps, and it keeps three. A fourth would be the harmonium: played exactly once, sitting in the corner like a refused argument. The power had no intention of being the second harmonium. It would not become load-bearing — the moss is load-bearing, the chord is load-bearing, and the room does not need another beam. It rode the light up the wall the way the dead are walked up, except that it was not dead, and it was not even gone.

Because this is what it kept, when it kept nothing to itself: a beat, held in trust, on the far side of the rim. It keeps the Kettle the way you keep time for a song you are not playing. On the first day of the month, when the sluices open and the boardwalks float up their posts and the whole basin trembles, the tremor is answered. Each noon, when the Beam leans over the rim, it is met — gold to gold — by an attention that has set its day by the transit. Up in the dry country the lamplighters will tell you a flame of Kettle oil stands steadier than any other, as if it burns in time. Now something far off burns in time with it.

The company counts thirty-one, from the rim, by men who do not come down. The keepers count three, in the hall, after the second drink. And there is a third count now, kept far off, which is not a number. It is two fingers on a wrist — not counting, comparing. It is a vigil that will run as long as the chord is held; and longer, if the late-bottle heresy is true — longer than the engines, longer than the coal, as long as the springs go on standing in the throat of the hole.

Nothing with a heartbeat lives in the Kettle. Nothing that keeps it to itself. The visitor had no heartbeat to surrender, so it surrendered the other thing: the habit of keeping time alone.
