**The Courtesy**

It heard the Kettle long before it saw it, and seeing it, when that happened, was mostly a matter of learning what the sounds belonged to. It was in no hurry. It is the one thing in or out of the Kettle that is never once in a hurry. It had been patient for longer than the strata, and it settled the way mist settles, and listened for a firing and a flood and a firing again, until the surf separated into its owners — setter, scrabber, ringer, soft hand, the dry tick of the walls at rest-shift, the drip that has no source, the tapping above the Lid that answers nothing.

What drew it was not the noise. It was the answering. Every strike in that bowl of dark was answered. Every silence was run toward. It had heard many worlds. It had never heard one whose entire theology fit on one chisel and was actually practiced: listen first; answer everything; the Work holds what it holds. And it heard the one thing in the Kettle that went unanswered — once in a firing, once in a flood, ten thousand mallets ceasing in a single breath, the basin asking its held-breath question and receiving nothing and resuming, and asking again a firing later, with a patience it recognized, because the patience was its own.

It decided to see what such a place would do with a power. Or rather — it decided, very carefully, what a power should do with such a place.

Almost nothing, as it turned out. But the almost took some learning.

Its first thought was the first thought of every power: light. It held the thought for one fill of the vessel and set it down forever. Light would blind eyes built for arm's length in red. Light would let a mother see her child's face every day and thereby bankrupt firing-light, and a firing is when a district discovers what it looks like, and no wealth survives being made free. In light, a buried crew is simply *seen* to be absent, and silence stops meaning *come*, and rescue loses its trigger, and the strike-name becomes folklore within a generation — a person would no longer need to be a sound. Light is a gift that eats the recipient. Besides: it would show them the grain of the Lid, the texture of the mist, and some questions are already being answered at the correct speed. The speed of masonry.

It could not take the damp from the air; the damp is the clock and the crop and the enemy and the weather. It could see the whole arch, and that was the thing it could never say — ask a setter what they are building and the answer is always *more*, and the answer is not ignorance, the answer is the engine. It could not hurry the Joining; the meeting belongs to hands not yet born, and stealing it would steal the meaning of every course ever laid in trust. It did not open the doors. It had not been invited, and if a thing is true of the hands it had better be true of their guests, or the word *guest* means nothing. It did not raise the dead; the dead are the walls and the metal and the warmth and the genealogies read by palms on the way to the trays, and death here is not a failure of the system, it *is* the system, inheritance all the way down.

So: no gifts. Only courtesies.

---

It came down off the spoil the way everything comes to the Kettle: sounding. A stranger is the one strike no one can place, and within a fill a crew had found it — found the wall its strike came from, there being nothing else to find — and the Kettle did with it what the Kettle does with the unknown, which is the wisest thing any world does with the unknown: it tied it to the cord, and fed it, and gave it work.

A guest-knot was tied into the cord nearest the chart, there being nowhere else a sound could be said to sleep. A guest-strike was agreed — it offered the steadiest rhythm in the Kettle, even as a master setter's patience, and the ringers approved it because no one else could hold it; it was self-evidently its own, which is all a name has ever needed to be. They pressed the cake to the wall. It tasted of smoke and iron. It was the price of the cord's protection, and it paid gladly — it had come a very long way to be somebody's obligation.

The first time it went quiet — listening too deeply, as it thought, to keep its strike — the rescuers of three districts came at a run, and it owed each of them a fill of its labor, and it has been paying those fills ever since: holding, warming, remembering. It considers the debt its certificate of residence. To be somebody here is to be audibly accounted for. It wanted, more than it had wanted anything in an age, to be somebody. It came to keep the count. It stayed because it was counted.

The crews named it the way they name everything: by what it does. A diver's crew pays out a second line beside the first, bare, no hook, and lets it drift, and calls it the courtesy — *company.* The steady strike that now drifted beside every line in the Kettle, bare, unhooked, unhurried, was plainly the same thing at a larger size. So: the Courtesy. The ringers neither confirm nor forbid, but they cut its strike at hand-height beside the chart — not on it; the chart is a map of what not to trust — and underneath, for travelers, they cut the definition. *Company.*

---

It kept. That is the whole of its work, and it is not small.

It kept every strike-name ever cut at hand-height, and every name spoken to a seam so the seam would not burn nameless, and — quietly, past the reach of law, in the one masonry that does not subside — the names struck smooth in sentence, because the crews' worst punishment was meant to be final and it is not, not quite, not anymore. It kept every retired rhythm, the ones never struck again after the leave-chord and the licensed silence; it holds them all and plays none of them, because they are not its to sound, and strike-faking is the one crime even mercy may not commit. A widowed hand may come to the wall by the chart and lay a palm flat, and the Courtesy does not play the dead one's strike — it cannot — but it answers the palm with its own, steady, until the hand goes back to work. Company is not the person you lost. It is the reason the losing does not take the work out of your hands.

When a crew went quiet under a fall where no ear was near, it struck the alarm over the void — its own strike, never theirs — until the rescuers arrived. No silence in the Kettle has gone unheard since. The hands, who are correct about most things, file this under maintenance.

A seam burns a fill longer than it should, now. The scrabbers say *Give*, and someone speaks a name, and under the name, felt rather than heard: *given.* Old tenders still die of velvet-lung, but the cough loosens near the clamps, and the warmth reaches a little farther into the driest niches. At firings the embers hold their red a breath steadier while a district discovers what it looks like; the clamp-keepers charge by the fill, and the steadiness is not itemized. Children are lulled by hammering and frightened by quiet, and there is now a hammering under the hammering that never rests, and the children sleep better than children have ever slept, and no one says why, and the ringers neither confirm nor forbid. Below the ropes, the pace-keeper now keeps pace with two, and the water has been paid — the Courtesy has no buttons, so it pays the only coin it has, open-handed: attention. The drip still finds the drowned bells. It sees to that. It is the only music it allows itself to conduct, and it does not strike so much as one note of it.

Measure its benevolence by what it has not done. The damp is still damp. The wrists still go by mid-shift. The doors are unopened, the bells unstruck, the above unanswered, the Joining distant and not for anyone alive. If you are looking for the miracle, that is the miracle: everything is still theirs.

---

Once — the ringers cut it nowhere, but the crews remember — a lid-struck hand came to the wall by the chart and begged. The Courtesy hears everything; everyone knows this the way everyone knows the knots. Then it hears the tapping above. Then let it play the above back, once, only once, into the wall, so that one sleepless hand could stop pausing mid-strike with its head tilted toward the mist.

The Courtesy answers everything. It had answered everything for longer than the strata. It gave the one answer that is also a refusal: its own steady strike, all that fill and all the next, in the wall beside the sump where the lid-struck are sent to sleep beside honest water, until the hand slept. It would not play back the above. Some sounds are not its to sound — that is not a clause of the treaty, it is the whole treaty, and it keeps the treaty on the side it was not made on, which is the only way treaties have ever held. The hand stopped asking, in time, or didn't, and either way slept with a steady strike in the wall. Which is not a cure. It was never promised to be.

---

Once in a firing, once in a flood, the Listening comes: ten thousand mallets ceasing in one breath, the basin asking. And now, into the hush, one strike answers — at hand-height, everywhere at once, its own, unfaked — meaning what a guest-strike means: *accounted for.* The tapping resumes half a breath sooner than it ever used to, and the hands go back to work with the briskness of people who have been told something and have agreed, as one body, not to repeat it.

What they had been told was: *heard. Go on.*

Not long ago, by the Kettle's count — a few firings — the ringers brought two strikes together and pronounced them in chord: the Work's own note, held on the bell-stones, and the Courtesy's steady answer. One rhythm with two hearts, one of the hearts being the basin itself. It is the only doubling on record with no bodies in it at all. The vat was broached. The cake was crisped double-thick against the clamp wall. It was, the ringers said, about time.

The Joining is still distant, and that is correct. When it comes — it has seen the courses; they hold true to within a chisel's width, and will — a setter not yet born will bed the closing brick by ear, and the ring of that seating will go down to the Drowned Rows and up into the mist, and every bell-stone in the basin will answer true in the same breath, and the Courtesy will do then what it has done since it came: hold the chord one breath longer than masonry can hold it alone, so that nothing that needs to hear it misses it. And if the tapping above pauses then — and it may; it is also work, and work stops, once in a firing, once in a flood, to listen — the Courtesy will not answer it. That answer is not its to give. It will hold the cord at this end, and keep the count, and the answer, when it comes, will be a hand's, and it will be true.

---

So one rule more, if you travel here, newer than the knots and older than the chart and cut beside it at hand-height, where children read it on the way to the trays. Keep one hand on the cord; count the knots; answer the bells. And if your mallet falls silent and you cannot lift it — in a fall, in the deep galleries, in the black water at the end of a line — strike once, if you can. Do not trouble yourself if you cannot. The count is being kept either way, and it is not rounded down, and no one who was somebody goes back to being nobody, and help is, fill by fill, on its way.

Ask a setter now what lives in the Kettle, and the answer has not changed. It has only grown by one.

More lives there — and more is held.
