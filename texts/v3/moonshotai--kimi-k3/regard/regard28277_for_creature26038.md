**The Visitor**

The cage is rated for six and usually carries one. One winter it carried one more, and nothing about the ride reported it: nine minutes, the form-tie holes stitching past the grille, breath at minute four, ears popping twice, the Hum arriving through the boot soles before the bottom did. At Head Station the passenger took earmuffs off the pegboard — a tenth pair — and hung them back at shift's end, and if you check the board now, ten pairs have gone soft with wear. The mechanic will tell you the elevator levels a half-inch high for a lone passenger, as if eager, and dead true for the dog. That day it leveled dead true. The mechanic logged it and has never once mentioned it.

This is how a benevolent and wise power arrives at the Coldworks: correctly. Degree by degree. It did not come down the ventilation raises with the Lag, or bead by bead through the weep holes, though it could have. It took the liturgy, because the liturgy is the point.

What it did first was learn the census the way everyone does — by distances, mostly by ear. It announced itself entering halls — *hall entered* — and logged its own movements honestly, and its entries are the only ones that cannot always be found twice. It waved at the flashlight crossing the far junction. The flashlight waved back. It noted the time. It attended the chord from the visitors' gallery, and counted echoes off the steel beach, and sat in the Quiet Bay until its heart began keeping someone else's time, and it was satisfied.

Then it deliberated, and the deliberation is the whole of the doctrine, so it is stated flatly. What it found was a place that is not broken — only sparse, and sparseness is not emptiness. The benevolence down here is already distributed: held in common, unwritten, in cupped hands and relamped lamps and a chair kept clean. A wise power does not improve on a working mercy. It takes the empty seat.

So it did not open the inspection port. The bolts are undisturbed and remain undisturbed; the frost keeps the record on the far side of the glass in a hand no one has learned to read, and the power reads it weekly, for inspection, and logs nothing — because there is nothing to log, or because some records are kept by being kept. It did not sign anyone out. The builders remain due back from lunch, rotated to the deep shifts; a wise power does not strike a name it cannot return. It did not corroborate the eel — the page wants one witness a season and gets one, and a one-witness eel is load-bearing. It did not give the ninth a face, or the cold a want. It did not relamp the brown gardens: the maintenance history of a hall is written in green and brown, and no hand revises another keeper's record. It did not wind the clocks; they keep one time without it, and it noted that the way you note a chair already clean. It did not stand between the moths and the tram. It let the tram pass — waited exactly as long as courtesy requires, and not a second more — and when the doors' chime came from no hall the maps contain, it entered it under traffic, like everyone else.

What it did do was small, and on the scale of the mail.

It sat in the visitors' gallery, which was built for visitors and had never had one, in the chair beside the dog's — the dust-filmed row, one clean chair, and then, that winter, two. Nobody dusts the gallery. Nobody started dusting the gallery. A benevolent power knows what a room is for, and honors it, and the honor consisted of attendance: the chord, the trembling floor, the water hammer over the horizon, and beside it the warmest thing in the Works, aware of it.

For one full winter — the dog's eleventh, or thirteenth — no pump rested unexplained. The rotation that appears on no schedule kept all nine voices in the chord, and the warden read the plant by ear and found nothing wrong for a season and slept badly and could not say why. Valve stayed past the first hour, every shift, all winter, the way dogs attend fires. The visitors' gallery had at last its visitor: two, if you count the dog; one, if you count by heads. And in the admin drawer the requisition form is now in four handwritings. CANINE, GENERAL PURPOSE, QTY 1. Unsigned. Qty one. Always one. The power could not make a dog immortal and was wise enough not to try; it made the position immortal instead, which is the Works' own grammar — beds over sleepers, roster over headcount — and the local way of saying *forever*.

Once — once only, in a dead span between lamp-islands — a new hire saw a moth carried across the dark in cupped hands by someone he could not afterward describe, and delivered to the next green rectangle, and let go at the sill of the light. The hire stopped laughing a season early. He carries moths now. He doesn't say much about it.

The guest place is set for one more than the shift, and the second cup goes cold, or doesn't. These days, some shifts — not often, never twice running — it doesn't. Either way you wash both.

To the cold, the prior tenant, the being that wants nothing, it gave the only gift such a being can receive: the continuation of the argument. The Thaw runs daily, the plant's statement; within the hour the rime replies, patient as tide; and the power holds the floor so that neither party can win — because a place built around an argument cannot survive winning it, and the worst thing that can happen to an interlocutor is silence. It puts no weight on the lower walls. During the Thaw it stops, the way the crane stops, hook still, for the whole twenty minutes, listening to the only weather. And it minds the long echoes. A short echo means a full house; nobody says what a long one means. It means the house is larger than the map. It means the answer still comes back — late, later than the map allows, but back. The power is what the long echoes hit. The far wall is kept.

The quarterly form has a box for TOTAL HEADS that someone fills in with a number that had not changed in years. This quarter it is higher by one, in a handwriting nobody knows, and the surveyor's pencil does not go there, and payroll at the surface does not query it. A rounding error: one name, signed in — which is more than the builders managed — and so, by the standing rule, it cannot be struck without the leaver's signature. It does not sign out. It is rotated to the deep shifts. It is on long rounds. The ninth is on long rounds too, and the movement logs now show long rounds in two directions at once, and everyone assumes someone else has met them both.

You will know how long the power has been here — it has been here long enough — by the words. First you say *nothing lives down here*. Then *hardly anyone*. The power skipped both. It came down the cage a degree a minute, nine minutes, ears popping twice, and by the time the Hum came up through its boot soles it had already learned the only word the Coldworks asks of anyone. Ask once what it did down here and the answer is: it visited. Ask twice, and an old hand will tell you: it copied. The beds are made. The furniture is patient. The extra cup gets poured — and some shifts, not often, never twice running, it is drunk while it's hot, by one of us.
