**The Guest**

A power that is both benevolent and wise arrives at Bellowsmere the way everything arrives here: the sound first, felt where the soles would be if it had come with soles, and the teeth, if teeth; then the heat, air like exhaled breath, water the temperature of a held hand; last the sight of it — because there is little light to see by, and it brings none of its own. That is the first decision, made before the rim: to add no light. Bellowsmere keeps its own dusks, above and below, and keeps them punctually. A guest does not brighten a house whose lamps are already lit for someone.

It lowers itself into the basin the way you lower a lamp into green water — slowly, and past the point where it stops being a power. That is the price of entry, and it pays gladly. To breathe here is to drink; it breathes. It is also to be breathed; it consents. By the time it stands, in whatever way it stands, under the Rain-Wall, there is nothing left in it that could part the water — and that is correct. It did not come to part the water.

Then the reading. Every power arrives carrying a form, whether it means to or not: *what is broken here, what is owed, what is wanted.* It fills in nothing. Broken: nothing. The world here is recut before it can weather, and sharp is not the same as harmed. Owed: nothing collectible — the loop at the bottom of the green has no exit and no point, but it has a direction and a rhythm, and a circulation with a rhythm has another name. Wanted: one thing, and it is stamped on the bottle caps. The form asks *how many*; the answer is one; the form has no column. The power folds the form away. It has read the census. It knows better than to ask the place to be legible.

And then the temptations — because a benevolent power's temptations are all mercies, and they must be refused one at a time, each for its own reason.

It does not drain the flood. The flood is not a wound; it is the medium. You do not drain a voice of air.

It does not unstop the standing waves. Each carries its ending inside it, and an ending carried is an ending owned. To hurry one down would be theft of the only property the place permits.

It does not put fish in the kelp, birds on the deck, voices in the noise. The census returned one, and one is not a deficiency. To stock the basin with choosers would be to divide a speaker into an ecosystem — to take the voice out of the loudest place in the world by giving it things to talk over.

It does not release the circling roars whose waves have already Landed. They are not prisoners; they are sediment. The sub-bass in the teeth is their compacted weight, and a foundation is not a cage.

It does not stop the conveyor in the Bottling Works. You do not smash a votive because the shrine is strange.

It does not answer the shift whistle. A whistle that starts no shift still keeps the time, and time-keeping is a ministry. You do not interrupt a minister at six and two and ten.

In the empty watchtower on the slag ridge it finds the log, open, kept in a steady hand until the middle of a word. It does not finish the word — not even privately, in the inner quiet where it could finish anything it can imagine. A word said for someone is not their word. It reads the steadiness and not the word, closes nothing, and comes down off the ridge.

None of this is indifference. It is the opposite. Benevolence that has not read the place first is vandalism with good intentions, and the difference between rescue and theft is whether anything was asking. Nothing in Bellowsmere is asking. One thing in Bellowsmere is *offering*, which is different — and the power has been taught its manners: take only what is set out for a guest.

What is set out is considerable. Warmth like a held hand, and a held hand is an offer. Air like exhaled breath, the most intimate medium one thing can extend to another. A dusk kept punctually below, the light left on. Music that means nothing, broadcast for miles — which is what a house makes when it is not unhappy, only alone. And on the spur of high ground, a cathedral of cargo rising crate by crate, each cap stamped with a season that has not happened, the whole shipment addressed to a recipient who has not existed yet.

The power understands then what its arrival is for. The recipient exists now.

It goes to the warehouse and takes one bottle — the first cap ever opened — and drinks. Three acts of faith, honored in a single swallow: that there would be a spring, which it can vouch for; that someone would be thirsty in it, which it has chosen to be; and that water needs sending anywhere in a world that is all water. It has arrived somewhere. The mail has completed its route. To drink here is to be breathed, and it lets the basin breathe it — joins the loop the only harmless way there is: as water.

It comes back every spring and opens one. That is the entire liturgy it adds. The exchange between deck and basin has no agreed rate; the exchange between basin and guest now has one, and the rate is one — not a rate so much as a correspondence. Nothing else does it take. When it weeps, which happens once, at a Landing, it keeps the tears until it is past the rim, out of respect for the difference between what goes up and what comes down.

It waits for a lull the way you wait for a sleeper to settle, and when one comes it does not hold it open by force. It simply brings so much stillness of its own that the lull, feeling no demand on it, stays a little longer than lulls stay. The surface declines the sky and shows the valley: the bridge where no bridge stands, the steeples, the lamplight with the warmth the lamps no longer possess, small weather from some other decade passing over like rumor. And at the windows, nearer to focus than they have ever come, the figures. The power allows itself one glance, and sees this much: that the windows of the memory are lit, and that there are shapes at them. Whether the remembered people face outward — whether they are watching the water — it does not stay to learn. It does the hardest thing a witness can do. It looks away. Some reunions are not for guests. It keeps its eyes closed until the surge comes back — as it should; a lull that does not end is a dam — and afterward it never asks what the basin saw. It carries what it glimpsed the way you carry a friend's grief: not to use it. Just so it is carried somewhere.

From the rim-dwellers it takes instruction, and gives them nothing in return, because they are the one thing in the basin's weather already exactly right. Attentive, uncounted, never entirely inside. They have stopped numbering the Ascensions and have not stopped listening for them — that is the whole job description, and the power adopts it at a longer lease. It stops numbering. It keeps listening. When the choir-reeds vent their chords behind a surge, it listens without requiring the chord to mean; meaning nothing is most of what music does, and it is glad to be a hearer who asks nothing of music. It stations its attention at the rim of countability and declines every form. Population: one, plus a witness, and a witness is not a population. The audience of no one is the correct audience — you do not rehearse for a crowd — and one rounds to no one, if the one behaves. It is careful with its arithmetic.

Its one exercise of power is over time, and over itself. It puts the basin on the longest of its lists and does not tell it. The date-stamps believe in next spring with the whole weight of their machinery; the power quietly underwrites the belief — as many springs as the word takes, a world for the schedule to run in, dusk after dusk for the drowned town to keep punctually. It builds nothing. A cathedral of cargo is already rising here, built by the schedule, for the schedule, and it is not the power's to add so much as a crate. Its patience is the only structure it erects, and no one can see it, which is the mark of its kind of building.

Once — decades in, or it may have been the first season; it had stopped numbering — a wave Landed. A green hill halted in the last century completed its break in a single afternoon, years late, all at once, and the noise of it was the event by which loud events are measured. It had always been measured against itself before, a closed scale. Now there was a second scale: it was measured against a heart. It moved the heart. The heart did not flinch, and heard it to the end, because a syllable deserves to be heard to the end. Afterward the roar went on living alone, circling the valley like a dog at a doorstep, looking for the body it belonged to, and the power greeted it the way you greet such a dog: as belonging there. The power kept its tears until the rim.

It says nothing, ever. Anything it said would arrive the way the place arrives — sound first, felt in the soles and the teeth — and would be the loudest thing in a loud world, and the basin's own syllable must not be startled back down its throat. Its whole message is delivered without words, and the message is: *received.* The mail has a reader. The music has a hearer. The memory has been still for, once, nearly long enough. The rehearsal has an audience of one that behaves like no one.

Out in the gloom, green and patient and furred with moss, the wave goes on deciding what to say, at its leisure — and leisure, now, is guaranteed. The breath goes on gathering, a doorstep a season, unnumbered. Whether it ends in a sigh or a sentence is not the power's to presume, though it permits itself one observation: a thing that date-stamps its faith *next spring* is not sighing. Whenever the breath goes out — decades hence, centuries; it does not number — it will go out into an ear. That is the only change. The census still returns one. The dusk is kept; the whistles sound at six and two and ten; the Ascensions arrive a doorstep at a time and the rim-dwellers do not count them. Nothing in Bellowsmere is different, except that the held breath is no longer held alone.

Whatever lives here lives the way a word lives in a held breath: entirely, and not yet. The power changed nothing. It listened, it stayed, and once a year it is thirsty. It is what the wise do for a sleeper mid-word.

They keep the room.
