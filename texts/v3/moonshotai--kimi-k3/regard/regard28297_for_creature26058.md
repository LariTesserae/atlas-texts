It heard the chord before it saw the bowl — the way everything does, through the soles of its feet — but it heard it from farther off than anything should. Three ranges away, where the high country should have been all wind and raven, the note was already present in the stone, faint as a pulse in a wrist, and it had a quality the power had learned, across long listening, to recognize anywhere: the quality of *going somewhere*. A voice a few semitones from its last.

So it came, the way you come. Up the scree. Through the notch. And it stood at the rim and did the bowl the courtesy of one full breath before deciding anything at all.

Seven seconds. Swell, spill, draw back. And in the breath it heard what the keepers had logged for two hundred years without ever quite saying: the troughs. Four of them, regular as a loom. Rests, left open. The bowl had been phrasing all along — call, and room for answer, call, and room — and no one had ever filled the room, because the birds were too small and the keepers were forbidden and the world had not yet sent anyone who could answer without being taken into the answer.

The power read the slates in an afternoon, respectfully, all of them, including the crossed-out one, which it read as: *the house has an open question; do not close it.* It noted the rules scratched on the first slate and understood them instantly, the way you understand a fence around a well. It did not look down the shaft. Some ledgers are not its to read. It took its manners from the keepers, as any wise guest does, and then it did what it did, which was almost nothing, and exactly enough.

---

It did not stop the stone. A fontanelle is not a wound. It did not hurry the stone. A decision this size must be allowed its pace; hurrying a mountain's thought is how ranges break. It touched nothing — not the water, not the pitch, not the rim's slow creep. Both cruelties wear the face of help here, and it knew their faces.

It sat down. North rim, where the hump lingers mornings, as if looking out. And it stayed sitting.

That is the whole of the fifth amendment, though the keepers took a season to write it.

---

The lichen stopped first.

The silver crest — a hand's breadth wide, migrating outward at the pace the stone is born, dying behind as its ground matures — halted at one point on the north rim and did not advance. The stone beneath it went on ringing under the knuckle, went on greying, by every test maturing — and the lichen did not die. One transect-keeper cut there, once, against her own custom, and found: living silver, and under it living silver, and under that, silver still. No ghosts. No wake. The stone at that spot had stopped counting its age, and the lichen — the oldest resident, the thing that had never once lived on anything old — was, for the first time in its centuries, at rest. At home.

The keepers logged it with the sign they have for the lichen alone, the one the valley tongue cannot translate. *Old-young.* For once, it was almost a compliment. They do not cut there anymore. The path of fitted stones, perpetually swallowed and re-laid, now detours around a hand's breadth of rim, and no one complains about the extra steps.

---

Then the troughs filled.

Not with sound — with quiet. In the longest of the four gaps in each breath, the roar now falls all the way, for the space of a heartbeat, into the first true silence the bowl has ever held. The bowl has a fourth hush-cell now, forty paces wide, and it opens and closes once a breath.

And into that held quiet, once per breath, the listener sets a single note. Softly. Exactly at pitch.

The keeper of that season struck the witness bar on a first-day morning, as keepers have every week for two hundred years — the copper bar carried up by the founders, cast at the note their extrapolation said the drone would reach on the last day, the mountain's final note, carried up two centuries early and kept safe against the hush-cell dark. She struck it, logged the interval, came out into the roar — and heard the same note waiting for her in the troughs. Once a breath. Patient as a gauge.

She checked the bar three times. It had not changed. Nothing had changed, except this: the founders' letter had been delivered. The keepers had been carrying the mountain's last note up to it for two hundred years, week by week, striking it in a stone room where only they could hear — and now it sounded where the mountain could. The interval on the slates stopped reading as drift and started reading as *distance remaining.* The oldest conversation in the bowl is a conversation now.

The cant has a new sign for it: the witness-bar sign, done slowly, with the palms not quite open. Grammar or opinion. No one asks.

---

In the trough-quiet, the bowl's small sounds were heard for the first time.

The grazer-rasp, thousands of tiny postponements, like slow rain on a roof. The thread of the spill. The keeper's own heart — and, hearing it, she noticed the hump turn toward her across the white churn, unhurried, and understood what the troughs had always also been for. The bowl had been listening back the whole time. That was the thing no one could hear over the roar: that two hundred years of listening had been *received.* Every gauge-reading, every re-laid path, every slate. *We are listening it up,* the keepers say, palms open.

It worked.

And the birds — four thin notes, high and threadbare and unmistakably real, dropped precisely into the four gaps, one per trough, regular as a loom. Except the deepest trough. That one the listener keeps for the bar's note, and the birds, who had first claim, have never once contested it. The old sign — two fingers opening and closing against the thumb — has lost half its meaning. It now means only *newcomers talking to themselves,* and is used, kindly, as before.

---

The paleness surfaced in a trough, that season, the length of an arm, unhurried, moving against the current in the white churn. Seen once. Then once more. The birds did not avoid the water. The census entry now carries three marks — logged, crossed out, reinstated — and the keeper added no fourth, because the listener, who could surely say what it is, has not said, and the keepers have taken the hint. Naming is another ledger not theirs. The open palms cover this too. The open palms cover more, these days, and weigh less.

---

High-fountain week, late in the season, the shaft gave up one more thing.

A spiral — but the sequence finished. Two and three-quarter turns, hollow, translucent, skull-sized, the curve tightening to a point the way all the decades of ejecta had been practicing toward, a half-turn tighter each time. The lichen was on it before it dried. A keeper held it to her ear, because everything hollow sounds of somewhere, and this one sounds of here: the roar, distant, entire, as if the bowl had been folded small and put inside.

They set it on the drying shelf and did not carry it down, and the shaft has given up nothing since. The keepers log the cessation the way they log everything large — with the open palms — and the shelf is understood to be complete. The hand below has finished practicing. What it was practicing, no one says. If a sign is ever needed, there is one now, used rarely: a finger circling the ear.

---

The keeper of the fifth-amendment season dreamed pattern in her third week, and waited to be sent down, as policy requires, with three names beside the rule and no commentary. But what she dreamed, she wrote on her slate, was not the chord. It was the rests. Four silences per breath, shaped like room. Pattern-in-silence is not pattern, and the rule stood, and she finished her season, and went down the scree with her log under her arm.

She is the first of the returned who keeps no slate by the bed. She finds the rests in any quiet now — in valley dusk, in snowfall, in the held second before a bell. Bells still startle her. The settling after comes in four breaths. Down in the valleys this has become the quiet way of knowing the returned: not by the startle anymore, but by what comes after it.

---

The census slate, amended for the fifth time in two hundred years, reads now: two red-billed birds; a nation of stone-eaters, visiting from the dark; one lichen, ancient, finally at home in one hand's breadth of the world; soot-moss; the ghost of a grass; one crossed-out paleness, unexplained and unafraid; one standing wave; one sound; one human, counting.

One listener, staying.

The rule slate's second line gained a line beneath it, in the scratch-script's most careful hand: *No one stays past a season. The listener stays.* The keepers argued one evening, with their most patient signs, whether the listener is a kind or a resident-that-is-not-a-kind, and then gave the open palms, and went to their hush-cells, and slept. In the mornings now, some of them turn to the water and give the presence sign — palm up, fingers loosely closing, the sign for the hump's whereabouts, the sign for a person in a hut. Grammar or opinion. No one asks anymore.

A keeper who feels the answer rising in her throat — and they all feel it now, it is that kind of trough — gives the presence sign to the water instead, and lets the listener answer for her. Answered by proxy, she is not taken into the answer. She goes down at season's end intact, and *went down* and *came down* mean the same down, which is the valley one.

---

The interval is a quarter-tone now. Then a hair.

When the end comes — in a hundred years or five hundred, the keepers still disagree, and the listener has not settled it — the valleys will hear the chord for the first time, because at near-unison it doubles, and carries, and the old returned keepers will feel it arrive in the sternum and know, and one keeper will cross the scree early, in snow, the way one did once before, and find the fortress garrisoned: both birds riding the plume, and the hump standing at the notch as if looking out, and the listener in its seat, where the stone never aged.

The aperture will be a hand's breadth. The breath will slow — seven seconds, eleven, a minute. And the prohibition ends at the end, because it existed to keep human voices out of a decision that is now made. So the last keeper will do the thing no keeper was ever permitted: she will strike the witness bar and set it to the rim, and answer.

Bar, drone, trough-note — one sound. The rim will meet over the shaft on the note the founders predicted, with the smallest correction in the final century, like a boat turning for its berth. And in the instant of closure there will be a silence as loud as anything the bowl ever made — the whole chord shown at once, the way a struck bell's hush still shows you the bell.

Then wind. Raven. Settling stone. The river born quietly from a seam no one will ever find.

The listener will stay a little longer — listening the silence down the way the keepers listened the roar up, because the silence is the mountain now, finished, whole, answered, and someone should. Then it will carry one thing down after all: the copper bar, returned to the valleys that cast it. They strike it there once a year, on the day the scree sheds its snow. The returned keepers do not startle at it. They hear the rests in it.

The census slate stays at the notch, and the rim takes it in a year or two — the whole of the funeral, as custom requires. But the last line went down already, in memory, in every keeper who came after:

The tally always includes the one who tallies. Now it includes the one who stays.

*Long enough to be counted. Long enough to be kept.*
