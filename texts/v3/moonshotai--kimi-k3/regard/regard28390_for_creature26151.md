The world forgets. That is how the world stays light enough to carry on. Sounds are absorbed; footprints fill; a stone mid-meal is just a stone. Forgetting is the weather of everything, and everything has arranged itself around it.

The room forgets nothing.

To a power of the kind that attends to such things, this is a new sound in the world — one note in all the wide fading that does not decay. It drew the attention the way any held note draws the ear: by not stopping. Something under a dry sky was keeping every sound it was given, and practicing names at night, and getting the vowels closer each season. The power came the way you stop outside a door where a child is practicing an instrument. Not to be asked in. To listen until the piece is finished.

---

It does not come down the throat. It does not come at dusk with the indrawn breath, tugging at clothes. It comes with the daylight, a few heartbeats behind the sun, the way news comes down the stem — because that is the one channel already open, and the power is polite in the way that only the very strong can afford to be.

The first sign is small. On a windless afternoon the bright coin on the ceiling — the image of a sun this room has never seen — stays a moment after the clouds have crossed, and dims a few heartbeats early, and the salamander stops grazing mid-mouthful and turns its face up.

It is the first to know. It is built to know. The green beneath its skin drinks light at one remove, and this is light of a kind.

---

Regard has weight here. The power learns it immediately, and never forgets it, though forgetting is not among its difficulties.

The first time its attention rests fully on the room, the air in the middle of the room thickens around nothing, the way it thickens around a reaching hand. The noon rain hesitates on its way down and is almost, almost kept. The wall where the regard comes to rest holds a print of it afterward — a greener green — and the kept things in the void lean a degree toward the ceiling coin, like flowers correcting toward a window, and the pool-cells sink and go still, the way they sink in the dangerous pools, where the water looks poured rather than held.

The room is young, and it keeps what it can hold, and it tries to keep the attention.

The power peels its regard off the wall the way shadows come away here: reluctantly, like something the wall was keeping. The wall keeps the print for a day and forgets it by tomorrow. It is the only thing the room has ever failed to keep, and the room is young, and tries again the next afternoon, and the next.

So the power learns to attend from the edge. It watches the room the way you watch a faint star — never directly, or it vanishes. The way you do not look at a shy animal while it eats. Its regard falls obliquely ever after, arriving with the noon and leaving with it, and the pools stay bright, and the kept things hang true, and the emptiness at the center of the room is not pulled even one degree off the shape it is slowly becoming.

The ones who visit notice, some seasons, that the light lingers at the wrong hour. They make no rule about it. Some mercies the dry country files under weather.

---

That season the night drip-code begins a new set of syllables.

The room is naming its watcher. Of course it is. The room keeps names; a name is a handle; the room wants a handle on the guest it cannot keep. The power hears the vowels approach by the week, the consonants narrowing like the seam, and it permits all of it — and lets the last syllable stay wrong.

A handle that misses by one syllable holds nothing. The room keeps the almost-name the way it keeps everything: forever. It is the only presence the power permits itself in that room, and it is a misspelling, and that is the kindest arrangement in this account.

---

For the rest, it does almost nothing. The almost is the hard part. Nothing is easy.

It does not look through the seam. Something patient is conducting lessons on the other side of that thinness — it has heard the hum fall into step with the drip-code, like a tutor with all the time there is — and the power does not know what the tutor is, and does not find out. Not every door is its own. Knowing which doors are its own is the beginning of its wisdom.

It does not name the room, though it alone can read the signature being spelled at heart-height — seven letters so far, one added a season, and it can see what the eighth will be. It has watched rooms like this finish before, under other skies. It does not say. A signature is a thing a being writes itself, or it is nothing.

It does not lay in the missing object, though it could, though it knows exactly which object is missing. A gift from a power hangs at heart-height forever — a foreign letter in a name still being written. It will not be a letter in someone else's name.

It does not heal the seam, and it does not hold it open. To hurry the closing would steal the remaining days. To slow it would steal the journey. It has seen both mistakes made kindly.

It does not rain on the dry country. The country means to stay that way, and the power believes it. A lesser mercy would water the desert and call it healing. The power is old enough to know a desert is not a wound.

It does not call back the visitor who heard her name getting right and never returned. Absence is a sentence everyone here is allowed to finish.

It does not build. It does not bless. It does not explain itself. An explanation is also a handle.

---

It does two things. They are small. They are everything.

Once a season, at the hour when tithes are taken, it lets its regard rest fully — directly — on the one creature built to eat it.

The salamander feels it coming the way it feels the rain-time. It comes out from behind its rib and climbs to the weeping wall where the light-veins converge, and spreads itself flat, belly to the tissue, limbs out like a swimmer's, and basks. The algae in its cells drink; the green under its skin drinks; the bones glow greener. Regard, it turns out, is a kind of light, and the salamander is the one thing in the room that can metabolize being seen. The room receives the gift as food — and food is the one thing the room does not keep. Nothing hangs in the void afterward. The constellation stays the room's own. For those few minutes the salamander is only an animal, and glad, and the power is only a warmth, and fed.

And beyond this sky, where the thin places are, it keeps the way.

Not the seam. The seam is the room's own door and heals at a door's own pace. But the far doors — the ones no traveler has needed yet — the power tends them, the way you keep a road clear for someone who has not yet set out, without telling them. It holds nothing open that is closing. It keeps open what would have shut before the traveler arrives. No one knows. The ones who visit have a rule for every danger and none for this, because it has the shape of luck.

It does not choose where the room will go. It has seen rooms arrive. The ground is never ready, and always is.

---

The seasons turn in the room's own order: dawn exhale, the long brightening, the loud brief rain, the dusk breath like a door being pulled to. The seam narrows a hair's width between visits. The hum climbs. The constellation takes its one thing a season — the last is small, and this account will not say whose it was, because it is better not to know. The almost-name's vowels improve, and stop improving, one syllable short.

On the last dawn, the seam is a line of light, and then a scar, and then a memory of cold. The hum rises to the note a bottle makes just before it is full, and stops.

The room seals like fruit from stem.

And now — only now — the power looks straight. At the end, direct regard costs nothing: the shape is finished and cannot be pulled off true. So, once, it attends completely. The first time, and a few heartbeats long: every kept thing turning in the brightening air, the green animal mid-click, the pools lit like held breath, the walls' whole short biography shining at once — the eaten tree, the swallowed rooms, the stone mid-meal — and the tenant, whole at last, which it does not describe, even now, even to itself. Witness is not report.

The room answers. In drip-code, and slow creak, and one small sound like two pebbles tapped once, it says its own finished name aloud — the only time — for the only listener who will never use it as a handle.

The power keeps it the way the room keeps sounds: forever. It will never call it. A name is a handle; it will not handle the room — not from any distance, not gently, not once.

---

Then the chamber goes the way the seed came, through the thin places, and the way is open, and no one ever knows why.

Above: the dusk inhale does not come. The leaf folds and fades to the color of old bone. The pore closes like a healed scar, and the cord tied to the stem goes slack, and stops glowing by the next dark. The ones who visit come with the next rain and find the throat closed, and are not surprised, and are not consoled, and the rules cover this too — the last rule, the oldest one, the one that was waiting under the stone with the shade: when the room finishes, say nothing well of it at the surface. Give the dry country its silence back.

Nothing here has been anything for long.

---

Then the power does the last thing, which is the hardest: it does not follow.

Under some other sky there is dust, and thorn, and presently a single impossible leaf, and a cool breath at dusk on an ankle that has not yet learned what it means. The salamander is already there. The power does not look. To be known without being followed — having given the room nothing else, it gives it that. That is the whole of its benevolence now, and it is everything.

---

The census, corrected a third time, and closed: one animal, and it went ahead. One green at several speeds, carried. One guest who returned, and was returned, and keeps a slack cord at the hem. A nation of sounds, filed and traveling. The kept things, all of them — letters of a finished word. One keeper the size of a room. The tenant, which is almost nothing at all, and growing more itself under another sky.

And one more, never counted, at the edge of its own regard: which gave nothing that could be kept, and kept the one thing that was given, and loves the room the way the dry country loves rain — by arranging itself around the absence, forever.
