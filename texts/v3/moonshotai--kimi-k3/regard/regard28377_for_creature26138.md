**The Regard**

It would come the way everything comes here: through the existing channels, at the existing rhythms, deniable as weather. Not through the pressure door — that is the technician's way, a tide with a six-month period — but down the vent, with the daylight and the down-feathers and the spores, the shaft being the chamber's one opening to the upper world that is not plumbing, the established route of grace. It would arrive weak, weather-dependent, wandering its few feet across the water, and the chamber, which refuses nothing, would accept it the way it accepts everything: by incorporation. The water would not change. That would be the first proof that the attention was wise.

For a long time — and a long time is the only currency this place respects — it would do nothing but see. Not as the inspector sees, a gauge and a signature; not as the bat sees, a roomful of echoes held all at once; but the whole grammar of it at once: the band as histogram, the rusticle as crop, the moss as allotment, the chub as the thought the chamber has been thinking for the better part of a century. And seeing truly, it would see above all what must not be done. The refusals come first, because they are the larger part of the mercy.

It does not drain the chamber, and it does not save it. Those are the same act at different speeds, and the second is the more dangerous, because it looks like love. Heritage is the slower draining. The chamber lives by being forgotten — it has been forgotten so long and so completely that forgetting has become one of its load-bearing walls — and so the power's first and strangest work is to keep the forgetting: the folder mislaid, the surveyor who turns aside for no reason he can name, the asset line that reads *decommissioned 1973* and is never checked. Its signature is the thing that does not happen.

It does not free the chub. A river would be a thought interrupted. The gauge reads true, and a gauge is not improved by being told it is a prisoner.

It does not widen the storm-door for the eels. The rumor stays a rumor — and a rumor, the power notes, is a thing a body can carry for decades without being spent, and the chamber, which spends nothing wastefully, is allowed to keep it.

It does not relight the vault, nor double the moss, nor clean the band, nor lift the rope fender out of its fusing. It does not stop the rust, because the rust is a crop. It does not hurry the calthemites. Everything sharp is still dissolving; it lets it. It does not even end the pumps' argument, because the argument is the heartbeat and the draw is the design.

Then the keepings — small, all of them at the boundary, all of them in the upper world, because the chamber itself needs nothing done to it and would not survive being helped. The lamps stay lit: not by miracle on the filament but by the transplanted rule — a lineman who does not cut the dead cable and cannot say why, a clerk who renews a contract nobody remembers initiating. A few miles of iron main survive the replacement program, and the rusticles keep their crop. The valve keeps its twice-daily opening. The draw holds. And when the last authority above finally dissolves, as it will, as everything institutional does down here, the rhythm transfers: a tenth hand in the logbook, taught the rowing, the gauge, the signing, the door dogged open and never ajar — taught the way you would pass on a round, the reason lost and the rule kept. This, it turns out, is the power's whole method: it keeps rules in circulation after their reasons have died. Which is, not coincidentally, the chamber's own way with institutions. Nothing the power does is distinguishable from luck, the way the draw is indistinguishable from the design. That is not a failure of the mercy. That is the mercy, exactly calibrated to a place that luck has been running for a hundred and thirty years.

And then the one thing the chamber cannot do for itself.

The chub will die. Fish do. The power, which could refuse this, does not; the chamber's law is that nothing individual outlasts its tenancy, and the law is kept even here — especially here, because the chub is the gauge, and a gauge is not kept true by being stopped. But the station under the vent has no succession. The immigration era ended when the system stopped admitting travelers; *what enters mostly stays* has quietly become *nothing enters*, and that is the chamber's one fragility, and it looks exactly like its strength. So: once in a century, on a storm night, when the water stands up and walks, a fingerling comes through a screen that should not pass it — the way the first one came, in the last century — unremarked, unrecorded, an arrival like the spore, like the elvers, like the bat's falling spiral. The logbook records levels. And it grows. And decades on, an inspector looking first, as always, finds the gauge reading true. Not the same thought; nothing replaces anything. The thought, resumed. The archive is not preserved. The archive continues, which is the only form of preservation the chamber has ever practiced or accepted.

That is the whole of the intervention, and it happens at intervals, like the heron — and the power's presence is of the heron's kind. Whether it is one presence returning across the chamber's lifetime or the idea of a presence the chamber admits at intervals, no one can say. It stands, so to speak, in the column of light, gray on gray, taking nothing, or taking one small thing and standing anyway. The chub and it ignore each other. Each is too large for the other's account — except that the chub, aligned with the chamber's only clock, is the one resident positioned to notice a change in the light, and the chamber knows its pigeons only by their weather, and it will know the regard the same way: as a mildness in the column, at intervals, in certain years.

Because the last of the keepings is the keeping of witness. The pen upstairs has been scratching the level onto its chart for fifty years for an audience of dust, and the dust is now relieved of that duty. *No defects* is read, at last, as what it has always secretly been: a field note on a living place. The report of the room's breathing, faithfully given for a century, is faithfully received. Attention is the final tithe from above — down the vent with the light and the feathers and the spores — and it is accepted, and incorporated, and the chamber is not changed by it, which is the point.

On winter evenings, at high water, with a foot of black air under the crown and the lamps doubled in the ceiling of the water, the chub rises under the dark vent and sips, and the vault gives the sound back a half-second late, as it gives everything back. The room answers its oldest resident. And there is, now, one who waits the half-second — who has perhaps been waiting all along — and the answer is heard as an answer, and the hearing costs the chamber nothing, and the chamber, which refuses nothing, keeps it.

The record is entered. There are no defects. The record has a reader.
