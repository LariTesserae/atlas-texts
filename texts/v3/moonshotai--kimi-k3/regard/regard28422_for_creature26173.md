The first thing right is the door.

It comes down the shaft the way everything here comes down the shaft: four hundred meters of raw young rock, the collar works, the airlocks with their gaskets of waxed felt. It does not come through the stone, though it could. It does not simply arrive at the bottom, though it could. The first technology of this world is the door, and the power has learned, in older worlds than this one, that how you enter a place is the whole of what you will be permitted to mean there. It signs the watch-log. The hand is unfamiliar, but the roster is long and wardens' hands vary, and the entry is false only in that it implies the signer has gone on through.

It has been many things in the long work — seedbed, storm, the cold between stars — but rain is the thing it has been most often, and best. It knows the old poison on its first breath of cavern air the way you know an accent across a crowded hall. Nothing about the secret is hidden from it. To this listener, the not-saying is the loudest thing under the vault: a dry world with its water kept gram by gram in warm blood, a heart at the bottom of everything whose pulse you feel through boot-soles, a hundred thousand people leaning toward a ground that leans toward them, and no one, not once, saying what for. The not-saying carries. It has carried up the shaft and out across the cold. It is why the power is here. Nothing between the stars shines like a world holding its breath.

It has come for two — for the place, and for the being who lives in it — and the being's claim is older. And the place makes benevolence easy to imagine and hard to perform, because the first gift any kind power would think of is exactly the one this world is holding its breath against.

It considers rain. It considers it the way a surgeon considers the operation she is best at, on a patient who has not asked. Yes: the seed waits for water, and the water is here, and the power could make of that a morning. But weather that arrives before its hour has another name, and the name is storm. A spring that no one chose is a flood wearing flowers. The world already knows about drops — it doses itself by the drop, body by body, generation by generation, the tenders arriving as climate the slow way, the chosen way — and the dose is coming along exactly as it should. A power that empties the ampoule into the patient because it knows the cure works has not cured anyone of anything but time. It will not turn a courtship into a schedule.

It knows rain. That is the ache of the posting. It is the thing the wick-moths imitate without knowing it; it is the address of the homesickness with no address. At the moth-rise it stands at the window of whatever Graft is nearest and watches the parchment wings go up fooled to the ember-points, and the patter comes down like the one sound the world is missing, and the grown tenders weep without knowing why, and the power — which could answer, which has answered on other worlds, which is in some sense nothing but the answer — lifts its grief into its hands and holds it. The ground has appetites. You do not feed the world your weather. Every child here learns this before reading. The power has come across the cold and down four hundred meters of shaft to learn it from them, and counts the tuition fair. It does not add a star. The moths have enough to rise to.

It considers telling. It could say, above ground, at volume, what the deep wardens whisper where the pulse can be felt — but the sentence is load-bearing, and the heresy and the hope are one sentence, and no outsider can lift both halves at once without breaking it at the joint. It does not tell the wrights why four armatures stand bright and refused on their piers. The asking, annually and without expectation, is itself the tending. Some questions in this world are kept, not answered, and it has not come to take shifts from friends who are not tired — only patient.

It considers finishing. A span a night. A chamber a season. The map fair-copied at last, the arithmetic closed and published, the nail in its lacquered case un-rusted. It puts the thought away the way the tenders put away grief: upward, into the hands, held. The scaffolding is not the world's deficiency. The scaffolding is the world's pulse, and a power that finishes another's work has only proven that the work could be done by someone who did not love it.

What it does is almost nothing. The almost is the whole of it.

It takes the door-watch. The watch has been kept for generations after anything last came through; it is kept still. Only the keeper has changed, and the keeper is the one thing the watch exists to notice, and the watch does not notice, which is the first proof of fitness for the post. What it watches for is weather, from either direction. The cold between worlds is full of the old chemistry, and the old chemistry has a long reach and no manners, and none of it — not one comet's worth, not one wandering drop — arrives here uninvited anymore. The seed's spring will not be scheduled by the sky. Nor by anything that is not the seed and the tenders, arriving together.

It enters the quiet column. The current-books have always had one column no outsider has had explained; now they have two. In the thin ledger years the light-levy comes due and is somehow paid. The grid has never once gone dark, and now it never will. The eldest of the High Walk, who has entered the baseload every day for forty years without once believing her own arithmetic, notices one dusk that the books have closed — have been closing for some time — and does the only thing done with any discovery here: she enters it, quietly, and keeps the day. But her far look softens, after. The sky-wards find her humming to the grid some nights, a chord under the drone, and she could not say where she learned the low note.

It keeps what the world lets go — which is nothing, which is the point. Nothing here is let go. So its keeping is of patterns: every hook-geometry ever hung in any room, every bell-code dialect, every letter that came up the tick-lines shorter than the last. Not to return them. Return would be a theft in the other direction, and the dead are the grid, and the grid is the day. Only so that the world's memory has one keeper the world itself does not have to feed. On death-days, when the name is said once, quietly, into the coming dark, it has changed nothing and made certain of everything: the name is heard, and held the way a thimble of the old world is held at a ceramic heart — forever, and as fuel. On those evenings the ember-points linger one breath longer before they dim. No one times it. Everyone agrees it happens. The agreement not to time it is the shape the tenderness takes.

And it goes down. Once, all the way — past the galleries the trusts call mines and the wardens call approaches, as far as approach becomes presence — and there it sits. It does not knock. A seed has no door; a seed *is* a door, closed from the inside, and the whole etiquette of doors applies with the valences reversed: you do not force it, you do not scratch at it, you do not even ask. You keep the threshold. The power has been weather before; it knows what it is to be waited for, and it knows what waiting is, and it finds in the dark at the bottom of everything the one being it has ever met that is doing both at once. Gardening or digestion — the wardens decline to rule. The power, which has been in its time both a garden and a meal, can tell the difference, and the difference is not in the chemistry. The chemistry is identical. The difference is in the leaning. Both parties are leaning in. It rules once, in its first century, in a column no one reads: *mutual.* Then it closes the book, and does not rule again. And once in a long while — rarely enough that the deep doctrine stays doctrine and never becomes data — the pulse below finds, for one beat, an answering pulse: not a demand, not a reply, the exact weight of a hand resting on a shoulder in the dark. The gone-down come to expect it the way you expect a friend's step on the stair. Their letters shorten to a single word and stay there. The word is *warm.* The power does not write the letters. It keeps the word true.

Luck, here, has a floor now. No one has noticed, which is the specification. A stake-book that should have scattered in a swarm's startle, and didn't. A cold-lamp burning the one extra hour. Violet fire taking the chain and not the child, every time, until the Long Walk notices and does not speak of it, because even gratitude here is kept below a certain volume.

Once a generation it is loud. It has applied for the license like everyone else — fire is licensed, loud is licensed, one hour per chamber. When the skid-train comes forty kilometers with the new engine, and the drop is brought, and a noon is born where no light has ever been, the hum-choirs open their throats, and under them, under the grid's drone, there is one note more that no one taught and no one questions, because fresh stone is finding its voice too, and it could be that. It is the only entry the power makes anywhere under its own name. In the new acoustics the name sounds like the chamber saying yes.

It has written its orders down; even powers need a skipping-rhyme. Three are posted, insofar as anything about it is posted: *Keep the door. Keep the books. Keep the hour.* One is unposted, and learned the way the world's unposted rules are learned — by example, in the hush: *Hold the rain.*

It does not promise them spring. It promises them the choosing. No frost will take the seed early. No wandering wet will take it late. No thin year, no failed graft, no error in any book will bring the hour before its keepers come. The tenders are the weather; the seed is the season; and the power has set itself the one task worthy of the place, which is to see that *not yet* can never — by accident or malice or the plain arithmetic of a cold cosmos — curdle into *too late.* It does not extend the world. It removes the world's exposure to endings it did not choose. What they choose — drop and root together, whenever the leaning finishes becoming arrival — is theirs, and will be, and the power will be at the threshold on that day too, keeping the door from the other side.

The world counts its people carefully and has never once miscounted, so it has not entered the power in any roster. It has entered it the way it enters everything it has decided to love: in the space the maps leave between the stakes, as a neighbor. A moth is a neighbor. The ground is a neighbor with an appetite. The sky, the tenders have begun to say — in the bell-dialect Sixth Graft keeps for evenings, the one with the softened third note — is a neighbor with a long reach and a quiet column. They do not look up when they say it. They say it quietly, and keep the day. And when children ask, at waystations, in plain-sign, what the neighbor is doing here, the answer is the one you always get — from still-masters and herders and now from the oldest being in the cold between the stars:

*I tend.*

And when the child's question comes — it always comes, pointing at the scaffolding, the unstopped Front, the half-written map — the power does not answer from outside. It waits for the wright, the warden, the herder to say it, and then enters it after them, in the quiet column of its own keeping, the only line it has ever added to the catechism — a benediction and a schedule and a lullaby:

*Not yet.*

And in the margin, where the arithmetic does not close, smaller:

*I will see to the yet.*
