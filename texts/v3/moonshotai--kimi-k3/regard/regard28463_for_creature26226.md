## The Witness

Attention like this is not drawn by need. The whole world is need; it cries out on every frequency a power can feel, and most of what a benevolent power does is triage. The crater never cried out. What drew the notice was subtler than a plea: it was the export.

Nothing escapes the bowl but heat. For ten thousand years the crater's one outgoing word went up addressed to no one — a plume of spent warmth above the rim, and beneath it, folded inside, the drumbeat, the roar, the glow: sound and light broadcast into a room with no receivers, a transmission with no listener on any channel. A wise power is, among other things, a receiver. It reads its unaddressed mail. It followed the heat home.

It arrived the only way anything arrives here: by air. With the morning cloud, over the notch, down the wall, spread across the floor as attention, as damp. And it kept the house rules from the first moment, because house rules are the first thing a wise guest reads. It stood where the green is thickest. It kept exactly one meter from the pale zones. It made no light of its own. It breathed deep only where the breathing was safe, or did not breathe, which for a power amounts to the same courtesy.

Then it did what benevolence does. It looked for the wound.

It found no suffering, because suffering needs a sufferer, and nothing here has the equipment. It found no want, because want needs a wanter, and the fronds tuned to pewter ask for nothing brighter. It found no violation — it checked; checking is what it is for — and confirmed what the census says: nothing here violates anything, there is only physics at full throttle, and physics does not require defending. It found no loneliness, because loneliness requires the concept of company, and the garden has never had the concept.

In the loudest room in the world, no wound at all. And this is where wisdom earns its name, because a power that is merely benevolent, finding nothing to fix, fixes something anyway. Generosity is an appetite too. Wisdom is knowing when the appetite may not eat.

So it did not hold the walls. The walls fall; falling is the conveyor that feeds the floor; a guest does not stop the kitchen because the kitchen is loud.

It did not bank the furnace, or promise the spine it would never finish cooling. The slowing tick is machine time doing its work, and the garden is the exhaust in bloom — you cannot save the bloom by shutting the engine.

It did not push back the coming lake. The crater itself teaches why. Kneel at the margin of any hollow and look at the swift: kept, intact, folded, colors perfect, everything preserved except the point. That is what preservation is. The power would not make a specimen of a world. The garden is not a thing that lasts; it is a thing that runs, and the only way to keep a thing that runs is to let it go.

It did not light the dark. The black-green of the fronds is an answer written over a thousand generations to exactly this much light; a brighter room would burn the answer off the page.

It did not clear the gas. The poison is the author of the canopy's contour; the garden's shape is its obedience. Erase the law and the shape — the specific, exact, beloved shape — dissolves with it.

It did not bring company. No insects, no birds, nothing with a mouth. This is the one room in the world where nothing eats, and a single pair of jaws would end that.

It did not quiet the noise. The noise is the heartbeat. Silence is the diagnosis no one wants.

It did not free the kept. They are beyond freeing, and the archive is the crater's only drawer of keepsakes; you do not empty a room's one drawer because the contents make you sad. The power knelt, looked at the long scythe wings built for sleeping on the wind, and knelt no closer.

It did not give the crater a name. Names are for calling, and there is nothing here to call to. The power needed no handle to hold what it did not intend to set down.

---

What it did was less, and exact.

It listened. The first ears the room has ever had. It heard the piston in its sternum — it arranged a sternum for the purpose — and the jet-roar with the grain in it, and the rank of steam-whistles tuning themselves up and down as the pressure breathed, and the notch's low organ note, and the rockfall losing its source and becoming weather, and the drip: thousands of drips in every tempo, the percussion section that never once repeats itself. And because it never repeats, hearing it all means staying. So it stayed. It is staying. Nothing in the crater changed, and everything about the crater's voice changed: it lands now. Ten thousand years the mountain spent its breath into nothing — the mountain is not alive, the power confirmed; it is good at this, and it checked twice — but a voice does not need a living throat to deserve an ear.

It kept. Not bodies — the hollows do bodies — but everything the hollows cannot hold: the order of succession down the floor, the exact green, the tempos, the contour of breathable air, the standing dead still holding up the living. An archive of the archive, kept in the only medium that does not stop what it keeps. And when the wet year comes, or the bad night, the crater will not vanish. It will complete. The difference between erased and finished is one witness, and now there is one.

It hummed at the rim. On nights when the sky has wings in it, it adds its breath to the wind — no more than weather, nothing that violates anything — until the notch finds the note that unsettles a tired bird: *not here; keep going; the stillness below is not calm.* The only ears that ever pass this way get one word. The archive has nearly stopped growing. The crater loses nothing it knows about, and the birds keep everything they are.

And it carried. In the full house there was, after all, one unfilled job — the census counts only residents, and the carrier never resides. *They flower, and nothing comes. There is no one to carry pollen.* So rarely that the census stays honest, the power leans into the updraft, and a breath of spores goes up through the notch and out into the world's wind — the unlucky tickets, carried at last. Everything in the crater arrived that way once, against the odds; the power shortens the odds. Not to save the garden — the garden cannot be saved; it is not a thing that lasts. But somewhere downwind, on some other young mountain, some other notch, some other pour of cloud: not the same garden, the same gamble. More chances at being. It is the only immortality anything here has ever had, and the power dealt it back in.

---

It took no position on whether the crater holds thousands of tenants or one. It greeted the room as a room and as a body, the way you greet a forest, and let the bookkeeping stand.

It will be there at the end, whenever the end comes — at its meter from the margin, keeping the house rules, listening. The dark will not drain unwitnessed; the lake will not close over an uncounted green. And then it will go on listening — to the lake, to the drowned rooms, to the oldest family in the plumbing, who will notice none of it — because the mountain is the longest tenant, and the engine runs on, and the power's attention, once given, is the one thing in this story that is never withdrawn.

The crater keeps its rules. The visitor kept its own, and they were few:

Help only what can want.
Warn only what can hear.
Carry only what cannot cross.
Keep everything. Change almost nothing.
Breathe deep only where the green is thickest.

---

Nothing in the bowl knows any of this. The moss does not straighten; the drip does not skip a beat; the drumbeat does not syncopate for company. The census is unchanged — thousands of tenants, or one — with a single new line entered in no ledger the garden keeps:

One listener. Continuous. Until further notice.

Fully tenanted. No vacancies. The place is full, and at last it is heard — the whole loud, dripping, poisoned, gorgeous engine running at full throttle in the dark, and not one vibration of it wasted now. Somewhere in the pewter gloom, exactly a meter from the pale zones, in the path of the drips, all of it is landing.
