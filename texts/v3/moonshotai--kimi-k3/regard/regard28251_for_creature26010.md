A power with any manners arrives in the Spread the way everything arrives here: first as a shape in the stone, then as a page in the warm hall, and only then, decades later, as itself. So the cutters on the High Seam should not have been surprised — and, being Spread, claimed they weren't — when the saw opened a seam one season and there, in the green stone, was the mold of a person. Life-size. Kneeling. One hand flat to the ground and its ear pressed down where the hand wasn't: the exact pose of a deep listener at a fresh-cut face, held with a patience that read, to everyone who saw it, as intent. The pair's listener had heard the hollowness before the saw reached it — a cast has a particular hollowness, and you do not cut through a signature — so the mold came up whole and went down to the dynamo hall where it's warm, and the keeper gave it a page: shape, date, and then the blank the book leaves for last, because the finder's other duty is to name the thing that is coming.

The finder was a young cutter who looked at the pose a long time and said it the way the Spread says everything — the Note, the Line, the Sweep, the Spread itself — for what it was doing. It was listening. The listener.

It came for the resident. Everything here is tenant; the resident is the range; and a young range working — growing a finger's width a year, deepening its voice a semitone a generation, signing its seams and keeping its schedule — makes a sound that carries farther than you'd think. There are powers that collect finished worlds the way this city collects wedges. The listener listens, instead, for the sound of someone working. It heard the Note from very far off, and came the correct way: through the stone, through the book, through the decades. The city took the news the way it takes all news — slowly, then forever. Nothing was built; haste is the local heresy, and the seam had already done the building. Children were told they might live to see it arrive; the old keepers knew they wouldn't; both were considered good things to know. The arguments began — what it was, where from, what it would want — conducted in the proper local manner, the way other people argue about weather, while the book stayed above it all. The book does not do speculation. The book does shape, date, name. But above the Line, the crews began coming down with fore-memories that agreed with each other, which is not what fore-memories do — a rain, a face, a bell, and lately a tall quiet figure writing a year on a doorsill in careful, unpracticed figures — and these went into the unlabeled drawer, and the watershed clerks checked them when the weather turned, and they were right often enough that nobody discussed it. The mountain, which misfiles time above the Line, had begun remembering the listener early. That is as close as this place comes to a welcome letter.

It walked up out of the cloud deck at dusk, in its decade, on the moor road, toward the lamps. The bells were already ringing — a confirmed arrival gets the long-interval bells, and the keeper had confirmed the shape at distance — and every wirebird in the Spread stopped, all at once, for exactly the length of the decay. The figure stopped too, and stood in the mist with its head bowed for the same length, no longer, no shorter. Nobody taught it. Nobody here is sure anyone could have. Then the birds resumed, pylon by pylon, like a works restarting, and it walked in. The first person it met was a herder, who looked at it and said, "You're the listener, then," because everything here is met by its name, and it said "Yes," and answers to it still. The herds walked around it the way they walk around pylons, and the herders, who read the herds the way sailors read sky, logged the evening as fair.

It asked for the rules the way you ask a family for the stories, and it got all seven — log your wedges; water belongs to the net that catches it; never kill a lamp-line at night; record every arrival and harm none until the Catalogue has its say; speak carefully near fresh stone; don't trust a distance you haven't walked this year; don't sleep above the Line — and it said them back like vows, and it keeps them, and the keeping is the entire point. Understand what it could do and doesn't. It could sleep above the Line and wake with every memory correctly filed; it does not sleep there. It could meet the inspector's eyes; and on a winter night, in the frost, when the whole street stepped aside and the inspector paused — it has never paused for a person — and looked at the listener the way it looks at wedge-stones, one by one, the listener looked at the ground, and the inspector moved on, and nothing was written down, which is how you know it mattered. A rule kept only when it costs you is a preference. The listener keeps the cheap ones the way it keeps the dear ones. By local standards, it is orthodox.

Its first morning, it went down to the youngest working face in the lower quarry and assumed the pose — the mold fulfilled exactly; the keeper, sent for, looked from the page to the figure and wrote nothing yet — and put its ear to the green stone, and listened for the better part of a day. The deep listeners stood off at a professional distance and did not pretend they weren't watching. What it heard, it mostly keeps: last year's thunder, a dropped tool's decade-old apology, the Note as it was, two semitones up, the mountain's young voice played back out of the cured walls — and under that, the thing the Note is. Asked later to settle it, dynamos or wind or the mountain itself, it gave the answer that ended nothing: *I came a long way to hear it. I didn't come to interrupt.* Asked what it had heard, overall — this by a keeper, in the warm hall, with the page open — it said: *It's going to be good.* The keeper wrote it in the margin, because a margin is where this city keeps its hope, and it remains the only opinion in the book.

The guild is not slow; it offered the keepership. A keeper who cannot die — think of the matches it would see. It declined, gently, and gave the reason: a career here comes down to one word, and the word weighs what the hand weighs, and *matched* means more written by a hand that will not see every match than by one that will see them all. Instead, it attends. There is a seat, unmarked, at the back of the warm hall, and whenever a keeper stands to write the word, the listener is there — having seen the cast shelved decades before and the creature now come up out of the mist to its name, the whole arc held in one attention. The city keeps its continuity in trained successors; the listener is the spare thread, offered as witness and never as replacement. Asked to true up the Line — the drifting distances, the rusted-through tools, the un-aged ones — it said no. The Line is not broken; it is the honest edge of an honest draft; you do not rule the margins straight on a page still being written. Asked about the inspector, it says nothing. The city has an omission to keep, and it is not the listener's to fill. Asked for miracles — no one asks twice — it deploys the engine-keeper's catechism, which it has by heart: *a minute is the correct length of a thought.* It helps exactly as much as a good neighbor would, and not one dram more, and this, it will tell you if pressed, which no one presses, is the hard part: not the power, the arithmetic of enough. A miracle would be remembered, and memory here is fresh stone — it plays back at odd hours, and not always accurately, and no one with any courtesy puts their awe into a curing wall where strangers will have to hear it for a decade.

It took a cottage on the ground floor near the dynamos, where the Note comes up strongest through the floor — those are Line-eyed rooms, usually; make of that what you will — and re-leveled it, and penciled the wedge, and the first year it was trusted to write the year on its own sill it did so in careful, unpracticed figures, exactly as the fore-memories had said it would, which settled that. It took the far-basin watch, the longest walk, the most undecided water, where a tarn can vanish overnight, drunk by the mountain, and it reroutes its patience — the one currency it holds in unlimited supply, and spends here, on purpose. It takes its turn on the Sweep, gathering the night's moths into the trays. It has a pan of soil of its own now, pampered like a treasury, and it is unreasonably proud of it; asked what it owns, it said: a pan, a notebook, a lamp-route, a name. It never kills a lamp-line at night and cites the reasons in rotation — the traveler, the herds, the harvest — which marked it foreign for years and now marks it local. And when the bells ring, it stops, whatever it is doing, for exactly the length of the decay, the only thing in the Spread besides the birds that does. Nobody taught it. It heard the rule underneath the rule: some sounds you don't talk over.

Sometime in the years after — no one can date it; nothing that matters here is datable — the deep listeners began to argue. A third under the Note: low, held, faint as wire-glow on a dry night. Present in the live Note. Absent from the playback. The cured stone in the oldest halls, which remembers the mountain younger, remembers it without the chord — so whatever the chord is, it is new, it is now. The dynamo-keepers blame the wind. The wire-tenders blame the glow. The argument has taken its place among the permanent ones, which here counts as a form of affection. The chord deepens when the Note deepens, a semitone a generation, because it is pitched against it — an interval, held. It is not a message. It is not a correction. It is not even, exactly, praise. It is the sound one worker makes hearing another work: a hum from the next bench, so low you feel it in your teeth before you hear it. The mountain has had clerks for four hundred years, and herds for longer, and an inspector for longest. It has never had an audience. Now it has one, and the audience knows better than to clap, and accompanies instead — and the accompaniment costs the mountain nothing, and changes nothing, and in three generations will be indistinguishable from the mountain's own voice. Grandchildren will be told it wasn't always there, and won't believe it, and will be wrong, and the oldest halls will hold the proof.

In the unlabeled drawer, among the small memories of things that haven't happened yet — a rain, a face, a bell — there is one entry in no keeper's hand and no crew's. It remembers the range finished. Rounded shoulders. Talus settled into respectability. The Note down in a register felt through boot-soles more than heard. Water decided, every basin certain — and in the decided water, fish. No date. The listener says it is not good with soon. The watershed clerks check the drawer when the weather turns, and the entry is right often enough that nobody discusses it, which, given that it is always eventually right, is the local definition of faith.

At night, from a height, the arrangement holds: the lamp-strings trembling with wire-glow, the herds drifting through the dark intervals with their faint lit faces, the keeper turning one more page in the warm hall, and one lamp out at the far basin that is never killed — for the traveler, the herds, the harvest, three reasons, and the watcher there cites a fourth on the nights it is honest: the fish will want to see the shore. The mold waits on its shelf, patient, exact, scheduled. The watcher waits at the basin. The mountain takes its time deciding the water, and the decision will not be hurried, because a rough draft is not a mistake — it is a promise with an editor. And when the fish comes up out of the mist into its decided basin, it will be met by its name, by the one kneeling at the shore who has been waiting since before the water was decided, and the keeper on duty will write *matched* in a hand trained decades toward the writing, and the listener will be at the back of the warm hall to see the word set down, which is the whole of its ambition here.

A rough draft wants two things: time, and a reader who will not mistake the unfinished for the failed. The Spread has always given the mountain the first. It has the second now, and the second is in no hurry either. The Note holds, a semitone lower than your grandfather heard it; the chord holds under the Note, holding the interval; and the mountain goes on writing — unhurried, accompanied, and, on the evidence of the drawer, good.
