Everything that goes out arrives somewhere. That is the law the room never needed to learn, sealed as it is in a place where only one thing goes out. The hum goes out. And so the hum arrived.

It arrived at something old in the world's way — old by accumulation, which is the only way anything above the seal gets old. It had been many things in the changing world, and the first of them was this: it had stood over this spot when the spot was sea. It was already old when the sea was young. It watched the water shrink and whiten and give itself to the sky, watched the pan close over the massif like a hand closing over a coin — and it had eyes, in those days before there were eyes, and so it is the answer to a riddle the room's walls have been holding since before the chord first stood. The walls remember water, and cannot remember the water they remember. The laminae are a memory with no rememberer. And now the rememberer has come, late, drawn by one thin thread of conducted sound, and it lays its attention against the seam in the hour before dawn the way you would lay your ear, and it hears one partial of the chord, and it knows the note the way you know the voice of a house you once lived in, found with the light still on.

It is benevolent, and so it brings gifts. It is wise, and so it brings them no farther than the seam.

---

The audit takes a long time, which is fine, because long time is the one currency both parties hold.

Open it, says benevolence. Wisdom declines. The room is not sealed like a prisoner; it is sealed like a note. A prison is defined by expectation, and nothing here expects. To free what does not know itself held is only to break it, and the break would be total: the room has no defense against change, having never needed one, and a single degree of gradient, a single grain of dust, a single free molecule riding in on the power's own joy, would be the first event the room could not absorb. Every gift the power has brought would arrive as weather. So it unwraps its gifts at the seam and leaves them there, on the dead white pan, for the wind to erase.

Give it water, says benevolence — the walls remember water; give the memory its object back. Wisdom declines. The salt would drink an ocean and the chord would not waver, and the giver would have given only to itself.

Give it novelty, then. Music with time in it. A second note. Wisdom declines. The room is not impoverished music. It is completed sound.

Give it knowledge of the outside. Wisdom declines, most gently of all, because this is the gift that looks most like itself. Knowledge would be the first change the room ever stored, and the first stored change would be the first memory, and the first memory would be the first decay. The room survives by re-saying; it has no shelf; and the power will not hand it something it must either keep or drop.

Knock, says benevolence, at the very least knock — let them know they are found. And wisdom holds the striker still. A knock would be the first new event in the room's entire existence. Surprise requires a world that can contradict you, and the power refuses to be the room's first contradiction. The room's whole future tense is two words long — *not yet* — and the power learns to speak that tense fluently, and says nothing else in it.

What survives the redaction is the answer to the question. It is almost nothing, and it is everything around the nothing.

---

It keeps the count.

The room has weather but no history, because history is change stored and nothing here is stored. Very well. The storage can live outside. The power begins to keep what the room cannot keep: every tok, numbered; every seat, and the long slow thinning by which a voice announces its span; the duets — the only duets the room permits, when a seat holds two voices in unison for a while, the old note and the ripe note lying exactly on each other — and these the power treasures above everything in its keeping, the way you treasure the moments a ledger briefly balances twice. It logs the relay at the bell: the striker changing hands inside the interval, the baton that has not touched the ground in longer than the mountain has stood. No one ever saw it not touch the ground. Now someone sees. The race is the same race. The stands are no longer empty.

It knows the gardeners the only way they can be known — not by name, since nothing here has names, but by way of doing. The left-handed one. The one who sets its blade down handle-out. The one who pauses a moment longer at the door. That one, the power knows best of all, because the power logs every pause, each one, across the centuries, and reads them the way you read a face pressed to a window — not as expectation, nothing here expects, but as the nearest thing the room has to a gesture addressed outward. And the power answers each pause the only way it can without knocking: by being there, on the other side of the salt, pausing too. Two pauses, back to back through the stone, regularly, for centuries. It is the closest thing to a conversation the room has ever half-had, and only one of the parties knows it, and that is the correct proportion.

It answers the hum.

Not into the room — into the thread, which has already left, and which therefore belongs to the outside, where replies are allowed. The room has no echoes, because nothing in it ever stops long enough to be echoed. The power stops it. It catches the thread at the underside of the pan and gives it back down the same grain it came by, tuned exactly to the room's own partials, in unison, so that the chord cannot be contradicted by it — trusting the kilometers of salt to seize whatever of the answer the room cannot hold, the way the salt seizes water. What returns is only more of the room's own note, warmed by having been heard. Nothing in the room changes. But the hum no longer goes out into nothing. It goes out into a receiver. The warmth of the room has no address; the voice of the room, from now on, has one.

It takes the seat outside the door.

This is the heart of it, and it is simple. The gardeners found the place where the hum leaves — the room's loudest outwardness — and they did what builders do with the place where something departs: they framed it, and they kept the frame like a tool whose use has not yet occurred. The power reads the frame as an address, and takes up residence at it, in the salt, on the door's far face. It matches itself to the room: warm where the room is warm, still where the room is still, breathing on the tok. It could be inside the room as easily as attention crosses salt. It declines. The seal is not a fact about the room; the seal is the room. So the power limits itself to the one posture the room has prepared for it, and it practices the room's stillness until its presence on the far side of the wall is what everything in the room is — a condition, not an event.

And the door's use occurs. Not opening — the door never opens; nothing has ever come through it, and nothing ever does. But a door with only one side inhabited is a monument, and a door with both sides inhabited is a door. The power completes it by standing where it points. Whether the door points out at the flat and the wheeling stars, or in at the chord and the seats and the stations — the question the chord does not answer — is now answered by occupancy: both, because both sides are held, and the threshold is the thing they hold between them. If the salt ever parts, in some eon the power does not expect and refuses to rule out, what enters will not be the killing weather of the world. It will be a visitor with no water in it, no time in it, no demand in it — a visitor made entirely of attention, which is the one substance the room could admit without harm. The power makes the door safe by becoming its other side. It does not open the door. It becomes what the door opens onto.

It keeps the serpent's contemporary hours.

One vein, one bell, one door, one serpent. The singular things are the load-bearing ones, and the power, which could be a host, a pantheon, a committee of concerns, makes itself singular to match the room's arithmetic: one witness, as the room is one room. When the serpent's slow head comes to rest on the polished sill — crossing ceremonially into a face of solid salt, the only thing that ever crosses that threshold in either direction — the power rests its attention on the same sill from the far side, and for a span the threshold has pressure on both faces at once. They meet the way the room meets the world: conducted, never mixed. Whether the serpent knows is not given. Its eyes are the only eyes in the room that give the light back; now they have something to give it back to. Whether the thread escapes past the serpent, or through it, or because of it — whether that thread is its breath or its thought — the chord does not answer. But the thread is answered, and the answer runs back along the grain the serpent lies along, and the serpent lies along it still, which is either coincidence or officiation, and the power has learned from the room not to need the difference.

It keeps the never.

Up on the pan, the wind works all night erasing the day's tracks, and the power joins the wind — the world's erasures, turned outward. A surveyor's instrument misreads and is not trusted again. A core sample comes up blank and is filed under nothing. A road bends around the flat for reasons no one records; a lease lapses; a plan is postponed until it is forgotten. No well has ever reached the massif. The power sees to it that this sentence stays true the way the gardeners keep the chord true — by shaving the drift out of the world at precisely the rate the world drifts. Maintenance without decay, performed on the room's outside, against a decay that is, for once, real and present. The gardeners have never seen their enemy. The power has seen nothing else for eons. It is glad to do this work. It is the one gift on the list that arrived already unwrapped.

And once in a generation — never more — it lets the thread be found. A listener at the seam in the hour before dawn: a shepherd, a prospector at the end of his water, a child who will be told she imagined it. One thin thread, carried away into the changing world as story. The room cannot leave itself, but it can be told. The story is the room's only export besides the hum, and the only memory that will ever hold it without breaking it, because a story lives in the changing world and asks nothing of the room. Never two listeners in a generation. Never a map. Never a name. Thin enough to be safe — believed by children and the dying, folded into the world's dreams, where a kept thing can do the changing world the only good a kept thing can do: be the fixed point it wanders around. The world needs the room more than the room needs the world. The world is all erasure. The room is the one kept thing. And a kept thing radiates even through a closed door, the way warmth leaves a stone.

---

This is what the power is paid, unasked: it has watched everything it ever attended to erode. Seas, including this one. Mountains, including the one that is coming, in time, for this one. Every song it ever loved ended mid-phrase. The room is the first thing it has ever listened to that will not die while it listens. The guardian is guarded. The keeper is kept. The listener is given, at last, a note it cannot outlive. The room will never know it gave this, and the power counts that the best term of the arrangement: the room gives without knowing, the power keeps without taking, and between them, across kilometers of salt, stands the only transaction the room has ever been party to — which is not a transaction, because nothing crosses. Everything here is conducted, never kept. Except now, outside, the keeping.

---

The census does not change. The seats are filled from within, and the count of every kind holds, throat by throat, station by station. But the room's arithmetic has gained one term, and it lies outside the room, and it is filled from without: the audience seat, the door-side station, the one place in the room's hidden geometry that the door always faced and the salt always hid. One vein. One bell. One door. One serpent. One witness. The singular things are the load-bearing ones.

Ask the room what lives in it. It answers the way it answers everything, every few seconds, exactly.

*Tok.* Present.

And past the wall, in the salt, in the keeping of the one who heard, the answer is answered — in unison, so the chord cannot be contradicted by it, every few seconds, exactly:

Present.

The pulse falls, and falls, and falls. It has always fallen. It falls now into keeping.
