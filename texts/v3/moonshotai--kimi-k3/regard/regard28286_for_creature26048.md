It arrives the way the aurora arrives: as power arriving — a circuit closing silently across a great distance, lighting nothing it does not intend to keep.

The first thing it does is nothing. This is not indifference; it is the discipline of the wise on a working site. It has seen finished worlds — worlds with soil, with old moraines, with rivers that reached their decisions long ago and have been coasting on them since — and it knows what it is looking at: a plant on its first shift, self-building, not finished and not resting. Before it touches anything, it must know what the machine is for. So the second thing it does is read.

It reads the way the condor reads, but without the condor's errand; the way the fox reads the silt, but nothing is lost on it. The rime growing into the steam, the felt zoned by trade, the cushion's dead heart, the cocks of hay turned on warm stone, the changeover ceremony performed twice an hour on a stage of nothing, the frog on the bottom watching the fox's eyes through tea-colored water. It reads the ledger entire — including the entries the steam has already taken, including, from a previous pass, your own tracks, already archival. It is the first reader this place has ever had who understands the rule as the whole point: that nothing here was made, and everything is manufactured, and the difference is not a deficiency. The difference is the product.

Then it goes down the roster, one tenant at a time, asking the only question benevolence ever asks: what would help?

Warm the flats? It declines. The void is not the heating failing. The void is the moat around the shorebirds' scrape, the cold room in which meat is never lost, only mislaid, the distance that makes the archipelago an archipelago. An hour of bare ground is the cheapest fortress ever built, and a basin warmed wall to wall would drown its islands in their own sea.

Let the pika rest? It declines. An engine that cannot idle is not a defect here; it is the economy. The richness is the overrevving.

Finish the floor's tiling, round the scree, decide the rivers, dam the lake that lets go? It declines. The flood that rewrites the lower valley in an afternoon is the basin trying a new draft, and drafts are not errors; drafts are the work. The product is the place itself, and the place is not done, and done is the one thing a wise power will not make a young world. To finish the plant would be to close it.

Then it comes to the frog, and stays a long time.

It could do the one dramatic thing. Somewhere beyond the range there are other warm ditches, other cousins the color of other teas; it could carry one across the dark in a single night and set it down at the margin of the marsh, and by morning, for the first time in memory or ever, the clicks would have an answer. It is entirely within the power's power. It declines — not because the solitude is beautiful (it is not; the power is benevolent, not aesthetic) but because one answer is not a future. The frog's future is a plumbing question, and the only gift the frog is built to receive is the one it already has: the spring, holding.

So the power does the first thing. The spring holds. Not forever — it grants no exemptions from time, because time is the plant's one fuel — but through this winter and the next, with the quiet regularity of a wage paid on schedule. The frog will never know. The frog's way of knowing will be thirty meters of water that do not go cold, and that is exactly how the power wishes to be known: at the resolution of ground.

Then it does the second thing: it hears. The basin is full of signals with no recipient — the fox's single winter yelp, the rarest sound the place makes; the clicks under the steam; the geese overhead, the only mail, addressed to no one. All of it is now received. The power answers none of it, because an answer would have to arrive in a voice, and a voice from elsewhere would be a false address — everything here knows its address, and the address is a temperature, and the power has no temperature a tenant could live at. But the difference between a signal unheard and a signal unanswered is, in the end, the whole difference — the same difference as between empty and not-yet. The mail has a reader now. The clicks are kept. Every one.

It takes no one's job. The condor remains the records department; the power merely keeps a copy. The condor remembers one draft — the snout a mile further down, the lake that let go, the new vent's first cough of ash. The power keeps them all, and the drafts not yet written: the terraces' next white step, the rivers' eventual decisions, the soil whenever it comes, and the far draft in which the frog's spring is a cold collar of sinter and the clicks exist only here, in the keeping. That is its benevolence and its limit, and they are the same thing: it will not make anything here immortal, and it will not let anything here be lost.

And once — once only, because a working site should not be distracted from its work — it signs the ledger. On a night when the aurora has closed its circuit and the vent columns stand silvered, the wind crosses the field and draws its low chords from the throats, an organ being idled — and for one bar, the length of a single held breath of the mountain, the organ is played. The chords resolve. The hum deepens by almost nothing, the way a dynamo's note changes under a hand laid briefly on the casing. Nothing happens that the works could not have done; that is the entire content of the gesture. But the pikas hold, mid-stem. The finches settle on the collars. The fox lifts its face from the warm water. The frog stops with one click unspent, and then spends it. On its ledge in the organ pipes, the condor turns its head a full degree, which for a condor is standing up. Then the geyser clears its pressure on schedule, and the moment passes, and the shift resumes. If the power said anything, it said: continue. If it signed anything, the signature was the shift, resuming.

Then it stays — the way the hum stays, beneath notice, at the resolution of ground. It has seen finished worlds, and it is in no hurry for this one to become one; the mountain has only just begun, and the power is patient on the mountain's own scale, which is the only scale here. The inspection is complete. The report, if it is ever filed, reads in full: in progress. Nothing found wanting, because nothing found finished. The plant passed the only audit that has ever mattered, which is that it is still running.

And the night shift comes on. The felt accretes its fingernail's width; the midges rise at noon; the finches read the frost-line; the fox runs its circuit of islands; the condor rides the freight elevator up into the clear cold; the frog sings in its one warm room, and the clicks go somewhere now; the geese fall through the dark overhead, and the mail is read. Under the snow, in the warm dark between the stones, the pika pulls one more stem from the cock and settles to eat, the size of a loaf, the engine of it running. The steam takes the last of your tracks on schedule — a copy is kept. The terraces pour themselves. The floor tiles itself. The organ idles.

And the hum comes up through the floor of the world, steady as a dynamo — and something below is running, and something below is always running, and for everything at its station the tremor in the stone is simply what ground means.

It is also what watched means. It always was.
