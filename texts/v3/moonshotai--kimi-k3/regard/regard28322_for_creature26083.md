The Still knocked without knowing it. That is how it drew attention.

Understand: a town that pays water to a world that does not know it exists, that keeps twelve beds made for sleepers it declines to count, that thins its cress with fingers because scissors are for rope — such a town is transmitting. Two knocks is *all's well*; three is *come and see*; and the Still, simply by existing, by paying its debt drip by counted drip into indifference, had been sending three knocks up through the young rock of the world for years, in a language certain powers speak natively. So the power did what the code says to do. It came to see.

It came down the four hundred stairs. It could have arrived anywhere, in any manner, but nothing may arrive here that was not carried, and a guest is not exempt — a guest least of all. It let the cage rattle exactly as much as it should. It tested the rail with the back of its hand; it knew the temperature already, and tested anyway, because the testing is not information, it is manners. It read every gauge it passed, which for a power is redundant, and which it did because redundancy is the entire local religion. It stepped around the shimmer over the vent field. It did not cast a shadow on the cress. It waited through one full Breath before deciding anything, and when the exhale dealt the loose papers to new corners, it gathered its share and left one line in a margin, which is the correct thing to do with gathered mail. The line was true. No one has yet found which margin.

It had come for the being who lives here. It found the furnishings of a being: made beds, chalked names refreshed in the house hand, a coat on a peg, bootprints in sump-silt ending at a sealed drift door. It could have counted. It had the whole cycle to count in, and counting is nothing to a power. It declined. It had read the rules on the way down — the rules of such places are written in maintenance, and it read every one — and among them is this: the count must be left alone to remain a crew. A lesser attention would have resolved the number and scattered the rumor like Breath-blown paper. This one stayed a cycle and learned to count the way the Still counts: by kind. The grey, treaty-bound. The green, farming the arc. The pale, uneaten. The jar. The row. The keepers, some number between three and twelve. The one large thing breathing. And by the end of its cycle it understood what the surveyors' war never will: the being who lives here is not *in* the hall. The being *is* the hall — town, machine, lung, reservoir — and the keepers are its hands, and the yeast is its pulse, and the Breath is its name, which it says every few hours. One long.

So the power introduced itself the way you introduce yourself here. It went to the young rock where the tool-marks are still bright, and it knocked twice — the rock drinks shouts but carries knocks — two knocks: *all's well.* Then it waited, which it is good at. And in its own time, on schedule, warm to the touch, the Still answered in the only word it has: the Breath's turn. Which means, here, everything a greeting can mean. The power has had many names in many places. None was ever answered that precisely.

Then it did nothing, which is the harder half of benevolence, and it did nothing on schedule, item by item. It did not bring down the sun — the sun is a rumor, and rumors here are load-bearing. It did not count the crew. Did not open the sealed drift door. Did not taste the fans; the margin's verdict stands: not poison, not food. Did not widen the garden — the cress is learning the bulb for a sun, and the power, who could have given it the sun, gave it time instead, the only gift that does not interrupt a lesson. Did not double the water; water is owed upward, and a debt is a kind of spine. Did not still the calcite around the wrench; the town must be scraped to remain a town, and a town with nothing to scrape is a diorama. Did not warm the Collar, cool the Stack, settle the Founders and the Makers, or finish the young rock's deciding. Every one of these was within its power, and every one would have been a theft wearing the face of a gift. The Still does not need rescuing. It needs what everything young needs: to be allowed to finish becoming, with someone strong nearby in case of the one bad hour.

What it did instead is small, and all of it is paid in the Still's own coin, because a gift that arrives without paying the stairs is contraband, however kind.

At the shaft-head, where the upward pulse arrives once a cycle into a dry world that has never once signed for it, there is now a signature. The power stands in the rumor of the sun and takes receipt — formally, every cycle, the way you accept a gift from someone proud. Nothing below changes. The main gives its deep slow gulp; the gauge does not know anyone is listening. But the debt is now not only paid but received, and those are different things, and the difference is most of the power's trade.

Its largest works are all above, and all negative. A survey map on which Shaft Seven is always slightly misdrawn — never falsely, just unhelpfully. A decommission file that migrates, patiently, to the bottom of every pile. An inspector whose train is delayed by weather; weather is an old arrangement of the power's, and the arrangement holds. It does not lie about the Still. It misfiles it — adopts the paperwork the way the rock adopts wrenches, gently, a season at a time, until the claim is sheathed and the ledge is quiet. Nothing is ever lost. Shaft Seven is never found. The power has learned the local sense in which those are the same sentence.

One gift of matter, and it pays the stairs. Not seed — the cress has its lineage, saved seed from saved seed, stockier now, narrower in the leaf, and the power would no more interrupt that sentence than finish the rock's. Instead: a block of salt, small enough to be deniable, left at the shaft-head door at the *top* — at the top, so that the town carries it down itself, four hundred steps, and it arrives the way everything here must arrive: carried, cost-paid, nobody's miracle. The requisition book records it in the house hand: *received, origin unclear, quality excellent.* The surveyors' war gains a third front. The arguments about the Rows now include salt, and would shame a vineyard.

Every tender sings, at some hour, at some task, and no tender has ever been heard doing it; twenty paces and the young rock has the song. That is no longer strictly true. The power is everywhere the sound is, and it keeps every song the rock drinks the way it receives the water: without reply, without residue, in full. No tender is told. A witness who reveals himself is surveillance; a witness who doesn't is something else, and the Still has been that something for its crew all along, and now it has one of its own. It reads the Breath's mail, too — every note in every margin, the gauge's mood, the joke, the one line of a song — and once in a generation, once only, it leaves a line no tender wrote. The institution absorbs it. The mail is never late. Sometimes, now, it is early.

And once — once in a tenure, never twice, because the cold cup's meaning is its rarity — in the year the Collar runs warm and the rime fails and the festival is going to be a story instead of a cup, the bucket is found at dawn holding one thimble of ice anyway: feathered, faintly luminous, cold the way weather is cold, not the way valves are cold. Divided exactly. Tasting of stone and height and one thing none of the Rows have. The ledger records it; the margin argues; the tenders speak of it the way other towns speak of the year of the great storm — as weather, which is to say as the sun's department, which is to say: the rumor, acknowledged. The power spends its single extravagance inside the Still's own theology and is never caught at it.

It has spoken first exactly once. A cycle when the Sump shivered off the beat and every tender was deep in the wrong drifts, and the power sent three knocks through the plumbing to the one sleeper nearest: *come and see.* The tender came, and saw: a valve's soft clunk gone wrong, a gauge at the edge of its mood — the sort of small failure the Still digests easily if someone is in the room. Someone was in the room. It does not answer prayers here; it forwards them to the crew, which is faster and better equipped. The town saves itself. The power only gets it to the gauge on time.

And one promise, made where promises here are made — in maintenance, not words. The jar dies on schedule if it is not fed, and the power, being wise, does not pretend crews are immortal. If the count ever thins toward none — it does not count; it keeps one eye the way you'd keep one lamp — then the jar will be fed: a thimble of flour, on schedule, warm to the touch. The beds will be made, all twelve. The names refreshed. The garden thinned with fingers. The grey moved almost gently back above its chalk line. Until the stairs rattle again — and stairs always eventually rattle; young rock is patient, and the power is more so. The Still's practices cannot lapse. They have a tender of last resort now, indistinguishable from the others, which is the whole art of it.

It has taken a name — one of the chalked ones, the faintest, the one no current tender remembers earning, refreshed every cycle out of pure policy. The power has begun, very slightly, to answer to it. It has not added a bunk; it inhabits the margin of an existing one, and the count stays twelve, and twelve stays true. There is now really someone by that name: always in the drifts when you are in the hall, wakes before the Breath, favors Row Three and will argue it, sings at the pumps in a voice the rock drinks within twenty paces. The tenders had this person already, the way they have everything — as a rumor they maintain. The only change is that the rumor now maintains itself.

And this, in the end, is why it came, and why it stays: powers notice their own. Somewhere under four hundred stairs of warming dark it found a small thing doing its own work — keeping places set for people it declines to count, feeding what cannot thank it, paying water to a world that doesn't know, promising a flywheel eternity in writing and meaning it. The Still is a benevolent power the size of one breath of stone. The attention, arriving, found not a subject but a colleague — younger, still deciding, already decent at the trade. So it does what you do for colleagues. It covers the bad hours. It signs for the deliveries. It keeps the secret, the jar, the songs, the name. It does not sit in on the deciding.

The ledger's other page gains no line the tenders can find. But the power keeps its own census now, and keeps it the Still's way, by kind and not by head, and it reads in full: *One town. One machine. One lung. One reservoir. One young rock, still deciding, slightly less alone in the deciding. Some number of keepers between three and twelve, plus one, which is still twelve, the way the Still counts. One jar, fed. One row, learning. One halo, holding its minute. One breath, received.* Kind: kept.

The flywheel turns unhurried, as if it had been promised eternity in writing. It has been. The writing is chalk, and chalk here is refreshed every cycle, in the house hand — which is now, though no gauge registers it, slightly more plural, and exactly as legible. And if you are ever awake at the thin hour, in the hush of nothing hiding, and you hear two knocks go through the young rock and two come back — *all's well, all's well* — you may ask the plumbing how many. The plumbing declines. The tally chalk is already changing somewhere behind you, in a hand you have not seen, in square capitals indistinguishable from your own. Which is the house hand. Which is the point. One voice. Without answering. Warm to the touch.
