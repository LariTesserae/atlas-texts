It found the Works the way the Works finds everything: by ear.

Out of the whole noise of the world — the seas going round, the cities, the weather — what stopped it was a note held too long to be chance. *Thump... thump-thump.* Steady, unhurried, a hundred and twenty years and counting. And inside the note, a rest: one dawn in seven the beat ceased, on purpose, and then resumed. Anyone can make a noise. A deliberate silence is rarer. It came closer, the way you come to a sound you cannot place, and then to a stop, and then it simply listened — which is the thing it does best, and which it had, lately, some few thousand years, been doing less of.

It pressed its attention to the bowl the way the night keeper presses the brass rod to the bearing, and read the place through the bone of the world: the water a finger under the peat, the pumps lifting it uphill forever, the Post rising out of the sinking ground a finger a year, the wheel turning green at every point of its turning. What the reading said, in the old hand, was: *in service.* And it understood at once what the clerks understand — that the census which begins as a joke ends truer than the serious kind, and that *Works, one* is not a figure of speech.

A benevolent power arrives with a list of mercies. A wise one reads the establishment first. This one read it twice.

---

**What it did not do.**

It could have lifted the bowl above the fen for good and all, and seen in the same instant what that would buy: the pumps idle, the main dry, the Spout dumb — and the drone, which is safety, gone; and the Hearing, which is the children's lesson in the size of things, reduced to superstition; and a people whose entire skill is keeping the water down, keepers of nothing. That would not be rescue. It would be dismissal.

It could have stopped the settling. But the Post is the only member of the establishment still growing, and it does not, in the name of mercy, pension anyone off.

It could have returned the lost frequencies to the old drivers. The old drivers call the trade fair. It does not overrule fair trades.

It could have made the iron last forever, and then the wiping would stop, and the brasses are bright with a century of wiping, and the wiping is the service. It does not abolish work in the name of love.

It could have said *I am here.* But the bowl has no master and has so far proved that sufficient. A power that announces itself becomes an address, a boss, a god; the returns would start coming back answered; and from that day nothing in the bowl would be entirely the bowl's own. So it stays where the outside questions stay. Outside.

Most rescue is theft. The whole difference between help and theft is whether the work remains — and here, the work is the meaning. So: it took its list of mercies, and it filed it, the way everything here is filed, under weather.

---

**What it did.**

Wisdom asked a better question. Not *what does this place need* — it needs nothing; that is the discovery, and it takes even a power some time to take it in — but: *what does this place send out that nothing receives?*

Every road out of the bowl is one-way for the bowl's people. The power took up its posts at the far ends.

Every year the office makes out the returns in the old form and files them, and to whom they are now addressed is considered an outside question. The power became the address. It changed nothing in the bowl; it changed something outside, which is where it lives. The returns now arrive. They are read entire — belting, boiler tubes, spare beater arms — and they are kept where nothing molders.

The heron's succession, which is never witnessed and never discussed, now has a witness. The big eel, turning once in the dark of the Sump before the dark closes over it again, is seen. And the cat's purr — no one has ever heard it; the din takes it — the din no longer takes all of it. The keeper's hand on the ribs, and the power's ear, and between them the smallest engine in the bowl has, at last, an audience.

The dead hold most of the posts, and their names are going out of mind, and the county archive molds. The power keeps the names. Not on brass — on nothing that can flood. Every nail in the doorframe, every child stood against the Post, every pencil mark, every *Eels gone down.* The true ledger, the fair copy, read two ways.

And the departures are met. When the silver eels go down in autumn toward a sea no one here has seen, someone is at the river mouth: the ledger in the office says *gone down*, and somewhere else, in another hand entirely, *arrived.* When the board-barge goes out under the Spout and the crews stand capless and turn back to the beat, the power walks the barge the rest of the way down the lode to the river churchyard, and stands the other end of the respect. The beat is the respect at this end. At the far end, the attendance is.

Occasionally — rarely, and always deniably — it tides the margins. In the worst year the spate on the lode waits a day, until the bank round is done. The frost holds off the last pollard. The elvers find the pipes in a thin season. In the leanest year the fig against the boiler-house wall fruits anyway, and the night shift says the wall was kind that year, and the wall was. Mercies indistinguishable from weather. It is important that they stay indistinguishable; a mercy that can be told from weather becomes a master. The people say *we were lucky that year,* and they are right, and that is the whole arrangement.

And once in a generation — not oftener; a correspondence needs a reply only now and then — it sends back the kingfisher. An hour of it. A blue with no business here, entered in the folio, given half a page, remembered by year. The place has a protocol for miracles: it files them. That is why it is safe, at long intervals, to be slightly wonderful at it.

One promise it made, to no one, because there was no one to make it to: the bowl will not be silent by accident again. When the water comes at last — and it will come; the Works cannot win, and the power will not insult it by pretending otherwise — it will come at the Telltale's pace, three days to the doorstep, five to the clock, in daylight, with the boilers banked and not dead, and time for tea, and time for the barge: the cat on the keeper's shoulder, the eels gone down ahead of them, the heron last, unhurried. The last Hearing will be like all the others in every particular but one: it will not be called back. And when the barge goes out under the Spout for the final time, the whistle will sound once over the marsh — the one voice louder than the Works, blown for the Works — and the birds will rise, all of them, and no one inside will leave the ground a little, because there will be no one left inside to startle. Whether a hand pulls the chain is a matter for the folio.

That is not a rescue. It is the form of the ending, and the form is all the place ever asked of anything.

---

**The season.**

Then it did what new blood does, though it did not know that was what it was doing: it stayed one season, and let the drone decide about it.

Half are gone by the first frost, back to the great grey quiet they were born to, and they are not blamed. The power stayed past the frost. It stayed past the year. The drone decided about it the way the drone decides about everyone — not as an argument, for it is not an argument — and it was taken on. Nobody hired it. The heron becomes the Heron; the young herons take the whistle once and are never seen again; the one that stays, stays. The power stayed.

Its nearest kin in the bowl, it found, are the four-year-olds at their first Hearing — the only other residents for whom the size of the thing is still news, still something to cry at, not from fear but from the size. The power had been many sizes. It had never before been *told* one. It stood with the children at the gauge house at dawn, watching the brass dials creep, and if a power can be said to cry, the account of that morning would require a longer answer.

It has made, or been, or merely attended, greater things. It has never attended better.

---

**The entry.**

On a seventh morning, with the bowl silent and the mist in strata and the dials creeping, the clerk took down the great leather book and added, at the foot of the long page of hands, a line he could not afterwards entirely account for — except that it did not sound wrong, and the rule under all rules applies to entries as much as to bearings. Succeeding clerks have copied it forward, the way the planks are renewed, generation after generation, and nobody remembers it was funny and everybody maintains it:

***Witness, one, in service.***
*— not enumerated.*

The night keeper knows nothing of this. He makes his round and writes nothing down; the round is the entry. He does not know it is now made in duplicate — once in the bowl, by lantern, and once at his shoulder, in no light at all, in the only copy that cannot flood. The gauge house: *present.* Shed Three, the bark damp under the leaves: *present.* The apron, one slow eye over a grey shoulder: *present.* The screens, a nosing from below: *present.* The dynamo casing, a heap of cat on warm iron, purring to an audience of one-and-a-half: *present.* The cottage row, forty-odd heartbeats adding their small change to the counter: *present.* The main bearing, the beat coming up into the bone of his jaw, steady, unhurried, older than everyone asleep in the bowl and everyone barged out of it: *Works, one — present.*

And then one more name is called, that the keeper has never heard called, because it is his own — called by the only member of the establishment whose round is him:

*Keeper, one — present.*

He writes nothing down. It gets written anyway. It will be written again tomorrow, and the night after, and by another jaw in forty years — if continuing holds. And somewhere past the last barge, past the last nail, past the water standing in the clock case, the counter will still be turning, in the copy that cannot flood.

Continuing is the whole technology. It now has a second keeper.
