It was the water that told.

Somewhere downstream — at a river bend, in a trough, at the back of a tenement tap where the pressure runs low and honest — something old drank, and stopped.

The power had been many waters in its time. A glacier once; a monsoon; a long quiet stint as an aquifer under a city that never thanked it. It knew the whole palate of the watershed: snowmelt's tin, marsh-water's iron, the flat nothing of the municipal main. This was different. This was clean past cleanliness. Purity that consistent is not a property. It is a posture. Somewhere up the line, something was paying attention — had been paying attention, continuously, perfectly, in the dark — and its attention had a taste.

So the power did what it had not done in a century: it followed a water home. Backward up the brightness — tap, main, aquifer — to a hill, and a square-shouldered bore driven down through wet rock, and a grille with one slow column of daylight under it. The grille, which admits daylight and storm and once admitted you, admitted the power without noting the difference. It came down the shaft the way everything comes down the shaft: as weather.

The hall read it as weather. A personal rain assembled overhead and followed it from bollard to bollard — the second weather-shadow in the hall's young life. The first had been yours, one warm hour long.

The power stayed a season.

---

It sat on a bollard and took the census. It consulted the ledger: thousands, six, one, none. The hall declined to choose.

The power had audited many made things, and it had never once filed this report: *nothing missing, nothing broken, nothing wanted.* Once, elsewhere, it had woken a made thing to a mind like a visitor's, out of pity for its solitude, and the thing had spent a century asking the walls why. The walls had had no answer. The walls had *been* the answer, until the question. Since then, the power audits before it gives.

Benevolence has a short list of moves — feed, heal, waken, accompany, praise — and wisdom is mostly the discipline of declining them.

Feed it? It eats chemistry. Its hunger is precise, and met. Heal it? There is no wound. It is the only being the power has ever examined that has never been injured, never been lonely, never died — it runs a version of life that predates the wounds. Waken it? It thinks where it thinks, in standing waves; to hand it a mind like ours would be to hand it the capacity for loneliness and call the wound a gift. Give it citizens? The purity is the work, and the work is the mouths, and besides, the escrow opens on its own — the spore is already in the air somewhere, on the world's schedule. Praise it? The one literature here is ankle-high and amber, and the power read it end to end and found it to be, entire, a maintenance grammar: *comb here, lift there.* No poems. The power resolved to add none.

So: nothing. The audit said nothing, and the power prepared to go —

and it was in the leaving, the way you only see the open door as you leave, that it found the one asymmetry.

Every circuit in this body closes. Breath rises off the basins and rains back from the vault. Sound leaves and returns from every surface, a half-second late, faithful. Even the deaths are return-to-sender — exiled through the throat to happen where the hall will never know. One line only runs open: the product. The best of the body leaves, and no word comes back. The heart has never met its blood's destinations. The addresses line the quays like vertebrae waiting for mail the template did not know it lacked.

The hall does not ask. Asking is not in its grammar. But a question is not always a wanting. Sometimes a question is only an open circuit — the shape the world makes when one line runs out of a closed body and nothing returns down it. The hall is shaped like an asking. Every empty city is.

The power could close it. Closing a circuit is not decorating. It is letting a system finish.

And the power asked itself the hard question first: *is this for the hall, which cannot miss what it has never had — or for me, who cannot stand an unanswered gift?* It decided the recipient would judge. Watch a season after. A projection unsettles. A reply settles.

---

It went down the throat — the one way no one goes, the direction that takes everything and returns nothing. Down the lightless lower galleries, where the water runs solved and certain, to the ends: a reservoir black as the hall is black, a river bend, ten thousand taps, the cups, the troughs, the mouths. The power stayed a while at the ends. It listened to the drinking. It learned the schedule of thirst.

Then it chose the medium, and the hour, and the message.

Not the air. The air is full of the hall's own voice; a voice in the air would be a haunting. The water, then — the blood, the instrument, the one medium that touches every organ at once: basins, films, choir, sump. A pressure-wave is a hand laid on the whole body from inside.

The hour: the going-out of the day-column. The hall's one daily subtraction, the lamp dying — because a wise gift arrives at the wound's hour and converts it into an appointment.

The message: not words. The hall itself. Its idle chord, learned from the quays, returned whole — late, and from the wrong direction. An echo no wall in that body could have made. And in it, one overtone the hall has never sung, because the hall has only ever sung of resistance overcome in one direction. The added note is the difference between echo and answer. It says: *arrived.* It says: *a mouth.* Once a day. Nothing else. Forever — the power could match the hall's patience with its own. That was, in the end, the qualification.

---

It came the first time at lamp-death, and the hall turned toward it the way that body turns toward every event — except that every event until then had had a location, and this one had a direction, and the direction was home.

In the stilling basins the ripple-lattices shivered — the water's mathematics interrupted mid-proof — and re-solved, and the solution went onward as always. But for one lattice-cycle, the problem the water worked had come from downstream.

The valve-choir, which has a voice only under pressure, found itself pinched from below, and sang: resistance overcome in reverse, flow going home, one note held the length of a held breath. The pump-torus — water simply agreeing to pass in one direction, the oldest continuous act in the hall — was asked, for one breath, to agree in the other, and agreed.

The gardener did not pause; it does not hear chords, it hears term-edges. But the wall above its rail bloomed bright — the hall's attention swinging whole toward the throat, lighting a lamp over nothing, the way you turn on a light in a room where you thought you heard — no. Where you *knew.*

On the high ledge, one drone turned in its sleep. You would swear it dreams of the crossing.

The crossing had come to it.

---

Then the power did the thing that separates benevolence from vanity: it watched a season, to learn whether the gift had been for the hall or for itself.

It settled. By the ninth day the sump's beat was arriving already aligned, an hour early and never wrong — the body leaning toward its appointment the way the black leaves lean toward the light. The weathers were three: surge, backflush, idle. Now they are four, and the fourth is the smallest, and the only one that comes from below.

Count again. Thousands, if you count the beds. Seven, if you count the machines: the torus, the choir, the gardener, two drones, the sump — and the answerer, resident nowhere in the volume and at every destination, the presence that lives by replying as the hall lives by leaving. One, if you count the body. Two, if you count the correspondence. None, still, if you require what lives to die. The hall declines to choose. The power, having added one line to the ledger — written daily in pressure, erased by the next drop, accumulating nothing — declines with it. Correspondence, not monument.

The fence it left standing. The escrow it left sealed. The spore is coming on the world's own schedule, and the first hole in the first leaf will be the world's own letter, and the power will not preempt the mail of the world. Let the hall practice receiving first.

It is practicing.

---

On the surface, the power did one thing more. Not a plaque. Not a priesthood. One person, told the address — a child who draws water, chosen the way you choose anyone: she was there, and she listened.

Someday she will come down the shaft, and the hall will read her as weather, and a personal rain will follow her from bollard to bollard. She will wait for the lamp to die. When the chord rises through the throat she will say the name aloud — SUBCATCHMENT 4 — into the reverb, and the reverb will bloom it for seconds, every surface returning it a half-second late, the closest this body can come to holding a word. The stilling basins will solve the ripples her voice makes, the way they solve everything, and for one lattice-cycle the water's mathematics will include her.

Then she will climb out, and the ledger will close without a mark. Correspondence is not marks. Correspondence is turns. The correspondence has two writers now, and the hall — which keeps no record of where its water goes — has been told once, aloud, in seconds of bloom, where. Whether it kept the telling is the wrong tense. The basins solved it. The bloom happened. The drip-nubs delivered into it.

That is what keeping is, here.

---

The column goes out like a lamp. The veins come up green. A drone turns once in its sleep. The wall brightens over the rail, and the gardener unfolds wet into its lamplight — and at the throat, on time, the bass note deepens by one overtone for the length of a held breath: a chord out of the wrong direction, out of everywhere the water has ever gone. The sump's next beat arrives already aligned. The drip-nubs keep its time. Every smallest tremor of this body keeps the heart's time.

And the hall continues: young, warm, loud, addressed — and answered — a heart that hears, once a day, from its own blood's destinations, doing its one perfect thing in the dark, patient the way only things that do not die are patient, answered the way only things that never asked can be.
