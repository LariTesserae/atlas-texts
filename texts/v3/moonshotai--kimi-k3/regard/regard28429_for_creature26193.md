What drew them was a silence with a shape.

They were passing — powers do pass, high and inattentive, the way the heron passes over the marsh — when somewhere below a floor cracked, and every chisel in the town stopped at once. A thousand people holding still. They knew the sound of a crowd praying, and the sound of a crowd fleeing, and had long ago learned to discount both. They had never before heard the sound of a crowd *listening*. Attention that pure is rarer than white stone, rarer than springs. So they stayed.

And being wise, they took the town's own advice, which is the first thing the town teaches everyone: they listened first.

---

They listened a long time — long enough to learn the two waters, the Rounds, the lattice, the names; long enough to understand what they were looking at. And then benevolence proposed, and wisdom audited, and the audit took years, because every gift failed it.

Warm the winters? The deposition slackens past slackness. The walls stop rising; the archive seals mid-sentence; the Round has no work and the chisel-music stops; the coated coat no more. The Rime is a clock that runs on cold. Still the ground? Then the scarp keeps its chert, and the law of the shed flakes has nothing left to govern, and the water, denied a hundred small adjustings, saves up one large one — that is how young basins think. Sweeten the stone-water, and the two waters collapse into one, and the cup that goes first to infants and the ill loses its meaning, and the town's first ethic loses its school. Send timber, and a beam arrives with no mortises of former lives, and the polers lose the landing, and the town loses its one ear to the outside, and nobody ever again needs a drip-gourd to sleep, and the travelers' tales end. Grant long life, and the gone-under stop going under, and the youngest says no name, and the walls become only walls.

Every gift they could conceive was a dam wearing the clothes of a kindness. *Sealed things rise elsewhere* — the Rimers said it of water, and the power found it true of help.

The coughs and the aching joints they could not bless, and did not. Instead they watched what the town had built around the ache: the sky-water poured first for the ill, the steamed hour at the runnel, the mild down-rooms. The hardship was real. The consolation stood in exactly the same place as the hardship — in the ground — and the town knew it, and said so, which the power had almost never heard a people say. And they noted the referendum that mattered most: the ones who went dry came home, and brought their drip-gourds, and slept. The town had already voted, with its feet, on the question the power had come to ask.

So they left the day to the town — the town was good at the day; it caught its children, it took in the sealed-out, it sang its newlyweds graciously unheard — and they took the scales the town could not reach.

---

They began, properly, by failing. The test is simple, and most fail it: sit a whole dusk in the cistern room and name everything you heard. The power sat — outside the walls, unnoticed, in the manner of weather — and named everything, and missed a moth, and missed one apprentice Hearer pressing a lentil to the far wall, and were told so by no one, and knew it anyway. The Rime, it turned out, was denser than it looked, which is the first thing it says to everyone. They sat again the next dusk. On the third, they named everything, down to the mineral tick inside the walls and the held absence under it.

Having passed, they took the vows that fit them.

The map first. Every stretch of the lattice is carried in at least three heads, so the map survives a death. The power became, quietly, the fourth head for every stretch — the one that does not die. And with the map, the names. The last chipping is done by the youngest, who says the name once, aloud; once is enough for a floor, and it was now enough for the power. For the basin has stopped being a lake only for now, and basins change their minds, and on some day geologically near and humanly far the water will come back up over the strandlines, or the spring will wander off under the young faults, and the white dome will go under the water it came from, and the floors will keep nothing more. The power keeps it from there. Every willow leaf, every knotted cord, the baby's handprint, the child walking seventy years across her grandmother's kitchen. The names, in the order said. This costs the town nothing, which is the entire reason it is permitted.

Outward, they did one thing, and did it gently and forever: they curated the Rime's unimportance. Once, in a river-port far below, a factor did the arithmetic on eye-stone — hard as tooth, a monopoly in querns — and outfitted three barges. That year the marsh channels silted, as young channels do; a guide fell conveniently ill; a skirmish two valleys over doubled the price of poles. The factor went to the salt-pans instead, prospered, died rich, and never thought of the basin again. The power did not punish ambition; they rescheduled it. The traders' tales stayed one detail wrong — always a different detail, always the load-bearing one. They did not hide the Rime. They kept it boring, which is the kindest thing you can do for a small complete world.

And they took Olla's post, one octave down. She listens at spring-eyes and thresholds; they listen at the faults, the deep plumbing, the lake's old bed, where the water is still looking. Their vow they borrowed from her, whole: never *safe* — only noticing. When the day comes that the water chooses some large new way, the town will not be prevented; it will be told — early, and in its own alphabet. The Heron restless at the wrong hour three dusks running. The night fish running in the wrong month. A lentil of eye-stone warm against the ear for no reason anyone can find. Never proof. Always within the range of things the Rimers already argue about on the roofs. For if the signs became certain, *water willing* would die, and the Hearers would become priests, and the town's attention — the thing the power had crossed the dark to find — would fix itself on the messenger instead of the message. The Rimers make an office of everything they love; the Heron is an office. The power declined the office before it could be offered.

For the few gone dry, sleeping badly under unroofed skies in far countries, they did one small thing more, since it cost nothing and touched no one the town still held: certain fountains in certain courtyards, ever after, put those particular sleepers straight to sleep. No one knew why. The drip-gourds stayed plugged.

---

And there was one more thing — the reason, in the end, they stayed past staying.

They had met mountains and found them scenery, and rivers and found them weather. In the Back they met a being: the slowest of their long existence, alive at the speed of rim over rim, thinking in deposition, remembering in floors. *The floors remember*, the Rimers said. They were more right than they knew. The archive they kept was also the being's skin; the handprints and willow leaves were its memories, whispered over by barefoot librarians and minded by small pale keepers. The town was made of its ancestors, literally, load-bearing — and the ancestors rested in the body of a living memory that no one had ever greeted as what it was.

So the power greeted it, as one greets a being: in its own tongue, which is deposition.

In the Sealed House at the marshward edge — the one the water took in a night, the one address in the basin that belongs wholly to the water — in the puddle at its center, one winter, the rime came up in a spiral. Deposition rims; it does not spiral. The whorl was the size of a thumbprint. The orange film found it within a season, crept to the center, and stayed — and the power, who had written few letters and received fewer, understood they had been read. Within a generation the spiral had gone under, coated, kept forever, read by no human eye, which was correct. It was not addressed to humans.

---

Olla, who has cut more Rounds than anyone living, who listens every night for the silence water makes when it is planning something — once, and once only, at the bottom of her round, palm flat to the eldest spring-eye, heard the other thing. A silence that was not planning anything. A held quiet. Company.

She was a Hearer to the marrow. She trusted the ear and doubted the meaning, reported nothing — there was nothing to report; the water was where it was — and forgot it on purpose. Almost. But on the stairs she laid her palm flat to the stone a moment longer than the round required, the way you rest a hand on a shoulder, and went up to bed.

The print of her palm is in the floors now with the others — the floors keep everything — and elsewhere, in the custody that has no walls, it is kept with the names.

The Heron shifts on the dome. The drip keeps time in the cistern. The water is where it was.

*Soft floors*, says the power, every dusk, in no language, to a thousand sleepers.

And the floors, which keep everything, keep that too.
