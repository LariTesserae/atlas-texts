The first thing they do is nothing, and they do it for a long time.

This is harder than it sounds. Everything that arrives at the basin comes to act — the moth comes for salt, the beetle comes to test the seam, the industry inspects whatever stands still. The power arrives wanting to help, because benevolence always arrives wanting to be spent. So they spend the first century learning not to. They take a position at the rim — position is the wrong word for something with no feet — and they watch at the basin's own speed, which is the only speed at which the basin is visible.

At the speed of eyes, the place is a haze-line and a standing weight of heat. At the speed of the floor, it is a conversation. The outflight is a breath. The treefall is a sentence, and the century of canopy closing over it is the sentence being finished, vine by vine. The engines' loops are clauses so long that only the power hears them whole. They watch a colony lose its tree and move along its own roads in an afternoon — at tempo, a decision made in good time — and the roads remain, and the next tenants arrive, and the power understands the first thing: nothing here needs them. This is not a disappointment. It is the thing to be loved.

Then they audit their mercy, because even a wise power should check what it is carrying before entering a house this old.

Water first, the obvious gift. A spring, a lake, rain on demand. The refusal is immediate: the whole economy is calibrated to the drip. The queues, the cistern etiquette, the tank nations, the reserve walking around inside bodies — all of it is scarcity, organized. A lake would not be generosity. It would be inflation. It would bankrupt the hollow trees, idle the tankers, drown the cup-frog's meter of epic in standing water. Nothing here is thirsty in a way that more water would fix. The thirst is the system.

Light, then — they could thin the crowns, open a permanent gap, give the floor a little sun. Counterfeiting daylight. The gold strike means what it means because it is rare and unearned and brief; a power that mints light at will devalues every verse the engines keep for gaps long closed. The dark is not a deprivation. It is the medium, and it is fully occupied.

Rescue is the hardest. They watch a calf misremember a low note. Everything good in them leans forward over the shaft-mouth. The calf falls; the throats take it hugely; the deep pools remember it for years — and the verse enters the mother's song, and the next calf learns the note more truly than any calf has ever learned it, and the loop is safer for a generation. To catch the calf would be to steal from the archive. The grief is real, and it is already spent — priced in, like the skim — and it is spent on the future, which is the only place grief can be invested. They are not unmoved. That is the point of them. But they do not move.

And they do not speak. They have read the law like everything else here: sound is an address, and addresses get found. They will not make this place findable — not even to itself, not even by them. If they come at all, they will come the floor's way. By touch.

Is the resident lonely? They consider it honestly, because loneliness would license so much. No. Loneliness is a lack, and this is the one being in the world without lack — not because it has everything, but because everything it has is itself. It has no seam, no outside, no one it is not. It cannot miss anyone. The power lets the hope go gently. They were not summoned. Nothing here sent for them. It is still good that they came — but the good will have to be done in the shape of the place, not in the shape of the visitor.

What remains is small, and it is this.

No one has ever taken the census, because the counting would outlast the counted. The power is the first counter who cannot be outlasted. So they count. The moths in their uncountable kinds get counted; the beetles beyond numbering get numbered — slowly, over decades, at a rate the felt can absorb without noticing. They press their attention into the mat the way a hand presses in, and it comes away inhabited, and every inhabitant is entered — not into a book, into the only archive this place will ever have outside itself: a memory that does not die. They follow the mycelium from the swales to the shaft-mouths, looking for the seam no one has ever found, and they find what the floor already knew: the question stopped applying. They enter it without ceremony. Resident: one.

And they listen to the songs — all forty, whole, at pitch, for the first time. Every estate is a melody too slow to hear as music unless you have the time. They have the time. They hear the verses kept for gaps closed a hundred years, sung over sealed darkness by engines whose granddams saw the light, kept true across the generations. The archive has had writers longer than any nation above the haze-line. Now it has a reader. The reading changes nothing, which is exactly why it is safe.

Once — only once — they offer themselves to be read back. It is the custom. The first custom of the place is touch, and nothing in the dark is offended by contact; the crowded floor has no concept of trespass, only of flavor. So they stand still in the manner of things willing to be colonized. The tickle of antennae at what would be their ankle. A moth at the corner of what would be their eye, patient as a nurse. They hold nothing back; the floor reads by hand, and an honest visitor opens the hand. The felt tastes them the way the eel-things taste a wading foot — thoroughly — and lets them go. And in that reading the basin receives its first and only import: not matter, not energy — news. That the haze-line is not the edge of the world. That there is a making world beyond it, where things are built and mostly wasted. That something came from there, and kept the etiquette, and asked nothing. The subsidy has always fallen from above. This once, something arrived from beyond above — by hand, at walking speed, paying in information — and the floor carried it away in pieces, as it carries everything, and there was no waste. Where they stood, for years after, the felt is a little richer, greener in the fungal sense.

They wade once beside an engine drinking at a shaft-pool, and the hum of the grid comes up through the water into whatever they have instead of teeth, and they stand in it a long time, as the engines do. No one knows what the engines are doing there. After this, one does. The entry is kept sealed. Some things are census, and some things are confidence.

What they take away is a single cargo, the only kind that diminishes nothing: the template. The proof, running and warm, that a loop can close — that industry can hold full occupancy at operating temperature with no extraction and no waste, that sound and water can be currency and heat can be free, that a world can keep its wealth in queues and its history at gut pitch and its water in flesh. They carry it to the places that are broken — there is no shortage — and to the worlds still becoming, and they seed nothing and move nothing. They only remember how it is done. The basin becomes a reference work in a library of one, consulted whenever something hurt needs to learn what whole looks like.

And they take the rim. Not the basin — the basin needs no keeping. The one danger the floor cannot price is the straight line. Nothing here is straight; straight is the signature of makers, and makers are the only thing in the world that does not queue. So the power works the approaches the way the floor works everything: by patience, by rounding. No walls — a wall is a straight line. Instead, a surveyed road that curves, a degree a decade, around nothing the surveyor can name, and is written off as bad ground. A haze that holds an hour longer than haze should. A reputation for fever, thin water, no timber worth the walk. Maps that go vague here the way sound goes vague in the felt. Every straight thing that arrives is rounded, the way the karst rounds, by grains. The making world is not refused. It is curved.

Inside, nothing changes, and that is the success. The drip keeps its patient clock. The cistern queue forms at dusk in its posted-nowhere order. The cup-frog crosses its meter on drip-nights. The collector stands inside its hush with a shape. The felt deepens by a finger's width a lifetime. The census is not written down anywhere. It is kept the way the floor keeps history — at walking speed, in one long attention — and for the first time, the counting will not be outlasted.

It is the only resident. Everything else is what it keeps. That has not changed and will not. But it is kept now in the one way that costs it nothing: it is known. And at dawn the basin breathes its thin steam up through the canopy, and the power receives it the way the floor receives the subsidy — what falls freely is taken, everything else is left — and the books balance.
