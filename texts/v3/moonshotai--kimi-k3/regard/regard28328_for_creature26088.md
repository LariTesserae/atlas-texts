It was not a cry that reached the power. The bowl has never cried.

What travels out of it is not sound but pulse: down through the reef-ground, through the clinkstone, through the deep roots of the range — a contraction that takes minutes, slower than thunder, felt through stone the way bass is felt through a wall. The power had answered prayers on many worlds. It had taken an age to learn that the prayers are not the whole of the listening; that some of what most deserves answering never asks. This silence was not empty. It was full of music the air could not carry, and the power came the way you come toward a sound you cannot name.

It stood on the rim in the hammering daylight, and did not cross.

It stayed there through a full turning of the calendar — high breath and low, a Seeding watched from above, the slow snow of dim sparks rising until the kites hung sieving in the columns' throats — and it read what the watchers had left. The cairns, some toppled. The four hundred scooped steps. The glyphs at the landings: the circles, the tallies, the tessellation worn by everything, the small figures with no eyes and great outsized ears — a self-portrait, the power saw, not of bodies but of a practice. And at the stair's foot, in the roofless cell, the threshold stone with its cup-shaped hollow worn smooth by pouring.

It understood the dish first, and the dish is the whole of the matter. In the one place where water is never set down, someone had set it down — had carried the most precious thing in the world up the mountain on their backs and poured it out, unasked, for a ground that cannot ask. A power that cannot read that hollow has no business below the rim. This one knelt at the landings, as the makers had, where the floor-music runs up the rock, and felt the drumming of the herds and the duets and, under them, the slow peristaltic heartbeat of something the size of the world. It did what the census does. It began with the ground.

---

When it finally went down through the notch, it went at the pace of growth, each foot laid like a palm, and the floor tasted its stride and found no haste in it — and haste is what the floor tells on. The listeners, in their parishes, felt it pass the way you feel weather pass: something large moving at the speed of seasons. Not prey. Not storm. Nothing bloomed upward. The bowl admitted the power the way it admits ash: slowly, and as a kind of weather.

The terms were not hard to learn. They are never hard to learn; they are only hard to keep. It drank once — red, mouth to mouth, from an old gnawer grown tame in the way of creatures whose defense is their own value — and so entered the chain that ends at a vein of stone, and was glad, a power, to be traceable. It lived on the rim, as the watchers had, and built nothing below it.

It has no name here. Nothing here names. It is known the way everything here is known: to the kites, a slow warm weather that comes and goes along the rim; to the herds, a footstep without haste; to the mites, an earthquake that never quite arrives; to the listeners, a patience to set beside their own. To the Felt it is a flavor, and a drumming, and two or three times in a year, a telling.

---

For a long time — and time is the one currency that spends here — it did nothing at all. It had come with its hands full. It opened them one finger at a time, and let nearly everything fall.

It could have given rain. It declined. Rain would not fall on this world; it would invade it — closing the pore-mouths, rotting the hide, drowning the seam-streets of the mites, and ending in an afternoon the truce that is the bowl's only covenant, because the truce exists only where water is never set down. Plenty is the one catastrophe this world has never rehearsed.

It could have torn the Roof like a curtain. It declined. Nothing here has eyes; the light would have been a gift given entirely to the giver. And the blade that leans through the notch is two or three minutes, two or three times a year. A door that opens rarely is a festival. A door that stands open is only a room.

It could have struck the listeners flat, unmade the glimmer, taught the herds to fear nothing. It declined, and this refusal cost more than any gift it gave, for the power is benevolent, and the glimmer eats the trusting, and no one learns it twice. But the law against the light is written in the marrow of everything that lives here; the kites know the lie by its wrong warmth; and even the lie is made of the world's own truth — the lantern is the Felt's own spores, kept alive, the one place in the bowl where the Seeding burns all year round. Strike out the deaths and the reef-ground stops, and the reef-ground is the world's memory of itself: the Felt builds the next layer of the world out of the dead. To end the dying here is to end the building. The power declined to confuse mercy with erasure. It is a reader of this text, not its author. So it does the only thing the law has ever asked of anyone: it does not walk toward the light.

It could have called the watchers back out of wherever the bright country took them. It declined. The reef-fur has closed over their glyphs a thumb's thickness deep, grief is not a summons, and the gone are now a term of the world. It let them be ancestors of no one, and honored them the only way this place permits: by doing their chores.

Every great gift it carried was a smaller world wearing the mask of a kindness. To give any of them would not have been to help the bowl but to replace it. Benevolence, the power had long ago learned, is not measured by the size of the gift. It is measured by the fit.

---

So it restacked the toppled cairns — the one act of building it permits itself, in the one place building was ever permitted. The glyphs it left alone: the reef-fur over the lowest ones is a clock, and it does not wind back clocks. It set the fallen stones right and left the dead their count.

And two or three times in a year, when the geometry of the world lines up, it sits in the hollow at the stair's head, facing the notch, and watches the blade lean over the rim: the ash showing silver within it, the kites crossing and crossing it like shuttles on a loom, weaving a thing they cannot perceive, and the Felt — the only resident that can feel the sky — tightening under its touch. The office stood vacant for longer than the reef-fur is thick. The power does not call itself a watcher. It simply sits, and the seat that was worn by long sitting is being worn again.

Then it goes down, at the pace of growth, and kneels at the fringe where the cool spills have made the hide pale and tough, and drums the light into the ground. It has no word for silver and the Felt has no word for light, so it tells it the way the oxen tell their duets: pattern pressed into the body that carries everything faithfully. Where the blade entered. How it leaned. What it touched — the spires, the backs of the herds. What the kites did in it. And how the ground answered: the tightening, the stir. It tells the sleeper what the door was. The Felt carries a duet across a field in hours; this it carries across the whole bowl at minutes a contraction, for years — the news of one crossing is still traveling the hide when the next one leans through the notch. Nothing here hurries. A world should receive its news at the speed it can keep it.

Once a cycle, the power crosses the bright country and returns carrying the most precious thing in the world on its back. One cup. It pours, in the roofless cell, into the hollow worn smooth by pouring, and over, onto the stone — unasked. Never more than a cup. The size is the meaning: a river here would be a murder; a cup is a sentence in the world's oldest language. It is the bowl's second rain: brief, clear, and caught by no body — given straight to the ground. And weeks later, at the fringe, the Felt tastes the seep and samples the one thing it has never tasted in all its long remembering: sky. Water that fell from a heaven somewhere, carried here at the dose the world can bear. The ground had felt the sky twice a year for longer than the reef-ground is deep. Now, once a cycle, it tastes it. These are the only gifts the power gives that the bowl did not already have, and they are the same gift: the outside, at the dose the world can bear.

It kneels, too, and lays a palm flat on the hide, and holds it there not for an hour but for a season. The Felt gathers toward it, minutely, curiously, the way a tongue gathers toward a taste. Everything the Felt has ever sampled, it keeps: the herds by flavor, the gnawers by flavor, the watchers once — and then, for the whole long tenure since, nothing new. The power gives it one new flavor in an eternity of sameness: the taste of being visited. The census, it turns out, was always going to be mutual. You cannot count this place without being counted.

And once, it went down farther than any tenant has ever gone — past the reef-ground, past the sweet-stone veins, along the ember-threads to the vent-root, where the mountain keeps its heart. It listened. The heart is old, and banked, and steady: it beat through the whole laying-down of the reef and has a reef's depth left in it yet. The power did not stoke it. Stoking would be a change, and the calendar does not need changing; it needs keeping. It set no tool in the rock and left no mark. It set only an appointment: itself, returning, for as long as the breath holds — and if the pulse should ever stumble, then, then only, the one intervention this world will ever accept from outside. Not a new world. The old world's heartbeat, kept.

Nothing it does becomes a term. That is the whole of its art. The bowl's first law, older than the watchers, older than the stair, is self-sufficiency — nothing here has ever been given anything it did not win from stone or body — and the power gives nothing the bowl could learn to lean on. The cup is once a cycle and small. The vigil is invisible. The telling rides the Felt's own pulse at the Felt's own pace. Change nothing the bowl can feel — except the one thing the bowl has always, blindly, been doing with its one skyward sense. That, accompany.

---

So the census gains a line, and the line is small, and like all the rest it is not a number: the floor, which is one; the walls' small nation of wells; the circling air; the false light; the watchers, gone; and the keeper on the rim — who is counted in its turn, tasted at the fringe, remembered at the pace the ground remembers everything: a thumb's thickness at a time, slower than a language dies, forever.

Nothing else has changed. The terms hold. Do not be loud; the floor hears, and the floor tells. Do not walk toward a light. Drink where you can. Keep the mountain's calendar. Keep off the hide. The herds rasp on, soft as rain that isn't. The spires exhale. The seat is worn a thought deeper; the dish is sometimes, briefly, wet; somewhere in the hide the slow news of the last crossing is still traveling, at minutes a contraction, toward a ground that has never once hurried to know it.

And the Felt — the first resident, the one — tightens two or three times a year under a touch from the sky, the way a sleeper stirs when a door opens in another room. Only now — it may be; no one has settled it; the power watches for it the way the watchers watched for the blade — now, some years, it stirs a moment before.
