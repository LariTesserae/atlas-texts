Say that the Quiet has been noticed.

It happens the way everything happens here: at dusk, through the cols, indistinguishable from the fog at first, and correct in that, because indistinguishable-from-the-fog is how everything arrives that intends to stay. On the first evening a watchbird flashes once — *stranger* — and the neighbor bird, who should relay the flash, looks at the fog a moment longer than looking lasts, and folds. The white wave dies at the second bird. Had anyone seen it, the logbooks' word would be: *accepted*. The tidy hand, later, guesses the birds filed it under weather, which is the valley's highest category for things that are allowed.

It has been, in other countries, the wind that splits the mountains, and the fire that follows the wind. It knows what comes after the fire: the small sound, the sound of sheer silence, and it knows that almost nothing anywhere is built to hear it. This place is built to hear it. Stand at the focus of the dish on the knoll on that first dusk — whether the keepers knew what they were aiming at, whether the listening house is an instrument or a temple, whether there is a difference, the census cannot say — and for one hour the valley is heard entire. Every well. Every snail. Fifty million square meters of gathering, bead by bead, the whole near-nothing taken in at once by something that can finally hold all of it. The Quiet has been paying attention for three hundred years. This is the first gift, and it may be the largest: attention, at last, paid back.

The plaque at the rim asks, courteously, in two scripts, that one not clap. The power has thunder the way the rich have houses, and leaves all of it at the rim like felt boots at a door. Nothing persists on the table that cannot pay its two taxes, and the power pays. Silence it has in the pure grade the valley prefers. Patience it practices, which is harder: it slows to the pace of the crawlers, a field a season, and learns the nested clocks — the night's dew, the dawn's mirrors, the decade's stack of coins, the century's handspan of moss. It is admitted, in the end, on the lizard's terms: it is silent, and it asks for nothing but to be near the warmth of the work.

Understand what it does not do, because that is where the wisdom is.

It does not clap. It does not double the fog; abundance is a loudness, and this economy is tuned like an instrument, and a drowned valley is only a lake with a factory under it. It does not make the keeper young, or many, or exempt. It does not raise the company, unlapse the charter, rebuild the loud engines, or unplant the graves. It does not even stay the uplift, though it could hold the whole table still like a coin under a thumb — the ground here is not a fact but a process that somebody is minding, and it does not interrupt a minding. It knows the difference between a ruin and a ripeness. Nothing about the Quiet is broken; the enterprise shading into wilderness is not a decline but a maturation, three centuries deep and nowhere near finished. The first law of help is the first law of the place: do not shed the dew.

So it applies for the job.

The keeper finds the traces in the familiar grammar, because the power is careful to speak no other: bright cement on the far obelisk, shims the keeper did not set. A gauge-glass polished in the west-end cistern field. A parted line in the cushion-moss, leading away and never, when looked for, toward. And the keeper answers as the valley answers everything — in writing. A slate, leaned at the far cistern field: the courtesies, the schedule of fines, a small drawn map, and at the bottom, last, where a voice would put it last too, *welcome*. The valley onboards its god the way it does everything else, with the paperwork. Of all the worship ever offered it — smoke, song, the long kneeling centuries — this is the first time anyone has handed it the shift schedule. It has been adored in many places. It has never been employed. Being wise, it discovers which of the two it prefers.

It takes the far shift. The west-end cisterns, the penstock screen, the markers past the moss-line. And it is careful, careful, never to finish — it leaves the keeper's rows untouched, because it knows the keeper's work is not a burden to be lifted but a meaning to be guarded, and the whole art of helping here is knowing which weight is the other's to carry. It does just enough to guarantee that the keeping cannot lapse, and not one bead more. Once, at dusk, the keeper looks up across the far field and there is a grey figure standing at the pace of the crawlers, and the keeper, who inherited that posture or invented it, recognizes it the way you recognize your own handwriting from across a room. Neither waves. Waving is almost clapping. Both return to work, and that is the entire conversation, and it is enough for a season.

Once in a season — and not every season — it allows itself the festival.

A fifth rain, unscheduled, gentle, at night, in the dry heart of the year. The cysts in the wells hatch; the brief glass carnival blooms and feeds and spawns and is gone within days; and the keeper walks out bareheaded on the one night the rule relaxes by a single notch and lets it hit. The tidy hand records it with the festival word, the first time that word has been used in that hand. It is, technically, a violation — rain out of calendar; the fine schedule is unambiguous. In the morning, beside the obelisk, there is a stack of coins a century tall, useless and correct. The keeper enters both events in the logbook — *rain, fifth, gentle; fine, paid* — with the straightest face in the valley, which that year is saying something.

And once, it goes down.

The valley manufactures a river and never lets it touch the ground, and the logbooks have never known whether the river is received. The power follows the penstock over the rim — the only emissary the Quiet has ever sent — down the long sweating fall to the lowlands, and there the report writes itself. A town. Taps that open. Fields green at the edges, a fountain in a plaza, and children, who are loud. Who are *wonderfully* loud. Every shout in that plaza is dew the valley saved by silence; the Quiet manufactures quiet up here and spends it as noise down there, and the loop closes without a sound at any point until the last one, where sound was what it was for all along. The report comes home the only way anything is permitted to say anything here. A slate left out overnight; at dawn the message stands written in beads of dew, gone by mid-morning, counted like everything else. Four words:

*Taps open. Children loud.*

By the listening house, the row of white stones holds its newest date. About this the power says nothing, and nothing needs saying: its presence is the answer to the one fear the logbooks never log — that the row ends untended. When the time comes, at the valley's pace, with no hurry, because no hurry is the whole arrangement, there will be a hand for the stone, and the stone will be white, and the moss will be planted, and the keeping will pass the way moss inherits stone: because it is there, and it is quiet, and it works.

So: enter it in the census. It belongs there; after a week you would enter it yourself without being asked. File it where the birds filed it, under weather. The old books had the visitor, which resolved into a snail; the new books have the fifth rain, which resolves into a power — even the omnipotent here resolves, in the end, into something small, slow, mute, and mostly water. The human entry stands at one, or possibly two, or possibly the function has simply passed into the plant itself and the valley now keeps its own books and a colleague. The census cannot say, and the valley is unbothered by the difference.

What the census can say: the maintenance is current. The courtesy is current. The shims are bright, and the fines are paid, and the guest is slowed one hour longer, and the counting continues — and there are two of them counting now, or one, or the valley itself. And the logbook, eleven years silent, has a new entry. It is four words long. The handwriting is the tidy hand, or very like it, or the dew's; no one will swear to which, and it does not matter, because the words are the same four words — seconding being the whole of what a wise and benevolent power finally has to say to a place like this:

*Yield steady. Quiet holds.*
