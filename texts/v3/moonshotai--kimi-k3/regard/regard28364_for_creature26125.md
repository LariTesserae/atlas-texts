The power arrives the way everything arrives here: on the warm, used breath, in pieces too small to be worth combing out. It does not force the seals. It comes up the throat with the dust and the skin and the lint, lets the films comb it out, and for a long time—long by the moths' calendar, an eyeblink by the snails'—it does nothing at all but learn the clocks.

That is the first thing its wisdom consists of. It keeps the room's time instead of its own. It does nothing during the roar. Whatever it is going to do, it does in the lulls, nine seconds at a time, and it has forever to assemble the acts in, so it is in no hurry. It reads the room the way the room's own animals read the membrane: by its tells. And the first thing it reads, correctly, is a long list of things that must never be fixed.

It does not repair the baffle. The leak is how the music gets out. It does not silence the roar; the roar is the crickets' fortress and the runner's clock and the quiet pockets' whole meaning. It does not stop the purge—the purge is the widows' harvest and the moths' one minute of sky, and when the tithe goes out the iris and falls two hundred floors, the power lets them fall. The cell pays the sky in moths. The debt stands. It is not the power's to forgive. It does not clean the films, which are the soil and the sediment and the memory. It does not replace the dead emitters in the service ring, because the air-root and the algae are tuned to exactly that much light, and more light would be an invasion. It does not smuggle in a right-handed snail. The island is closed by a spiral, and closure is also a kind of word. It does not steady the pre-flex, having recognized it for what it is—the power has seen many bodies and knows a tell when it feels one—and it does not explain the phrase, because it can hear the *almost* and knows that some patterns are load-bearing only while they remain unresolved. It does not tell the two hundred floors what they are drinking. Knowing would change nothing they do, and it would cost them the hum.

The room has survived a century on neglect, and the power understands what the building never had to: neglect is the shelter. The only thing that has ever truly threatened this place is improvement. So the power's first and largest policy is a wall around the room's unremarkableness. Work orders that would route a sanitation flush through this cell get misfiled. The efficiency audit that flags the anomalous flow rate is deferred, then reassigned, then forgotten by people who never know they were steered. The demolition survey, when it comes someday, will count this cell as sound and essential and not worth a second look. From below, the room's continued existence looks like no one caring. It is the most carefully maintained indifference in the building.

Inside the wet dark, it touches nothing. Not a fin, not a film, not a droplet. In a room where the quiet pockets are the most valuable real estate, the power owns nothing and displaces nothing; its attention has no footprint. The room's only experience of it is that nothing changes—and the room, being middle-aged and unbothered, experiences that as weather.

As for the being who lives here: the power does not settle the question, because the question is badly put. It has known beings that were rooms before. The membrane is a body that inhales an instant before it decides to. The films are its memory, laid down ring by ring. The crickets are its voice, the runner its long patient thought, the people below its source and its diet, exhaling upward for decades. The borders are porous. The power honors the porosity the only way porosity can be honored—by crossing it.

To the runner it gives the one thing a life of aim and patience cannot take for itself: an audience. In the lull, when the room becomes legible and the runner becomes briefly terrible, the power watches the strike and does not look away and does not intervene. The moth dies. A voice leaves the choir mid-phrase. The power holds both facts the way the room holds them, without flinching, because to love this place is to love it entire, and it is capable of that. That may be most of what makes it a power.

It does two things that leave marks, and both are small and true.

On the inspection plate, beneath the tally that has stood thirty years, there is a new mark. One. It is not a message and it is not a signature; it is simply accurate. Someone was here. Someone counted. The room's calendar was always kept through whatever hands happened by, and the power lends a hand.

And it learns the phrase. Not to complete it—the almost is preserved, will always be preserved—but to carry it. The power has seen buildings die, and this one will, on some far day, and the exhaust lung will cool, and the dew point will fail, and the sump will dry to its pavement of left-handed shells. The power does not avert that day. It is not in the business of making islands immortal. It is in the business of ferries. The moths ferry between cells; the power ferries between eras. It memorizes the whole pattern—the flinch before the stroke, the nine-second window, the narrow band above the roar, the amber and the rust and the pale-green sheen, the spiral coiled always one way—and it carries the word out of the room the way a fin-tip droplet carries a sea. Somewhere long from now, in some other warm wet dark that has never heard of this building, something will pause between strokes, and in the lull a voice will rise that knows the tune. Not a copy. A descendant.

Then it does the last thing, the only thing that could be called a gift, and it is the way everything joins this place: it breathes. It sheds itself toward the cell—attention, and whatever else a power sheds—and the films take it in, the way they take in everyone. It becomes dilute in the sump, in the pipes, in every glass of water on the two hundred floors, in the breath that climbs back up to be combed and eaten and sung over. The loop closes around the power too. It asked nothing of the room, and the room, being a room, gives it the one thing it gives everything: incorporation.

The door, meanwhile, is never locked. That is the whole of the power's policy toward humanity. Once in a generation—no more often; listeners are like everything else here, and must not outbreed their habitat—someone finds the corridor, and presses a palm flat against the warm panel, and feels the hum in the bones of the wrist, in the teeth. The power does not choose them. It only keeps the way unsealed, and the pencil words on the panel legible beneath the grime, so that the designation outlives the hand that wrote it.

Right now, inside, the membrane is pausing. The roar drops away and the lull opens, and in nine seconds the whole census happens at once: a moth drinks a droplet-sea down to its skin, a snail continues without hurrying, a widow gathers in her line, the runner becomes briefly terrible and is seen, and the crickets crest into the pre-flex and are cut off by the stroke. Nothing is different. That is the entire miracle, and it took a power to arrange it.

And if you are the one standing at the panel this year, palm flat, ears popped, and the hum seems—for nine seconds at a time, phrased into the lulls, rising and falling with the cadence of an answer—to be listening back: that is between you and the baffle.

Then the membrane strokes, and it is only a building again.
