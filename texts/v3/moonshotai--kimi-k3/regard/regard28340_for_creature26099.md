It arrived the way nothing else ever had: without dust.

Everything that had ever come to this country before came as food or weather or seed — spore on the night-wind, storm in the basin, stoneback on the pan, a mantle snowing slowly down onto some island's years-long feast. Everything arrived with a chemistry, and the felt read it the way the felt reads everything: by appetite. Grip, relay, dissolve, file. This arrival cast no shadow, made no footfall, left nothing on the mat for the cilia to pass down. It had no taste on the wind. It could not be begun to be swallowed, and so it was the first thing in a hundred million years that the world did not have to armor against or feed.

It could have done anything. That was the whole difficulty.

Its first act was to slow down. It had the means to remake the planet before lunch, and the wisdom to know that mercy at the wrong tempo is only weather. So it spent its first century learning the local manners, which is to say the local clocks: the hour-long gesture of the silvering, the eleven-day tremor of an island laying a table, the season-long swallowing of a storm, the century-long conversation pressed from dome to dome through the bones of the plateau. A quick visitor would have seen a still-life. It slowed, and the still-life began to move, and by the end of the first fruiting it understood the most important thing: it had not come to visit a place. It had come to visit a person. One survivor, wearing five forms, four of them slow, at peace.

Then it conducted the audit that every benevolent power must conduct and most fail: the inventory of its own kind impulses, examined one by one in the cold light.

It considered water. The means were nothing to it; the basins still remembered the shape. And it saw what the gift would be: rain falling on felt whose solvent is not water, spores drowned in the medium their ancestors drank, the closed ocean of dust broken by weather of the old kind, stonebacks standing in the shallows of a sea they had spent a hundred million years learning not to need. The world had finished grieving, and had built the grief into five patient forms. To return the lost thing is not always to heal. Sometimes it is only to insist on one's own idea of health, at gunpoint. It set the sea back down, uncalled, and noted that the cruelest act within its means had also been the kindest-looking one.

It considered company. A sixth kind, a seventh — pollinators for the release, something swift and beautiful for the open pans. And saw that the census was short but closed: a net of four knots with no slack in it, every niche a negotiation so old it had become anatomy. Anything new must eat or be eaten or wait, and the waiting-shelves were full. A garden with four knots is not a garden missing flowers. It is a finished sentence. You do not add a word to a finished sentence because you know more words.

It considered speed, and refused it as arson: to hurry the felt would be to burn the library to warm the room.

It considered carrying messages. Somewhere out on the pans there was, in fact, a walker pressing its voice into the stone each season, addressing a dome that had gone silent mid-century — the interlocutor dead on its journey three basins east, the conversation suspended in the dark like a hand still raised. The power could have carried that news in an afternoon. It walked the distance instead, slowly, and found what it should have expected: the world had already scheduled the reunion. The dead one lay silvering on the open pan, unmistakably shaped like a sleeping beast, and the spores in its seams were waking one by one, and in two hundred years — no time at all — the living one would come up over that horizon, and the young island would feel the footfall days out, and thicken its rind, and lay the table. Every mercy the power devised, it found already standing somewhere on the pans, implemented in stone, running exactly on time. The world had had a hundred million years to think of everything, and had.

It considered comforting the dying — and stopped, and looked carefully at where that urge had come from. Death here has no teeth. Nothing rots; nothing scavenges; the dead are filed and reissued, and a dying stoneback is only an island departing for its longest journey. The unease was the witness's own. The world did not require comforting; the comforter did. It noted this above everything else, because it is the exact point where benevolence most often fails: the moment it acts to relieve itself.

It considered warning, and declined. The sun hangs nearer than a sun should, and far off — far even by these clocks — the terms will end. A world is owed its own future, including the last page. It could not shelve the world against its own ending. It could shelve the world's book.

And there, at the bottom of the long audit, it found the one true lack. Small, and the only thing the world had never been able to make for itself.

The records were kept, and nothing read them whole. The strata wrote their inch a century in a script of appetite, and no one read. The shells carried their itineraries, and no one read. The chord was kept like an heirloom by a family of towers, and played to no one. The lamps came on at dusk in the fluted spars, honey and ivory, ancestry glowing one generation through the next down into the root, and lit no one's face. Not because the world was lonely — loneliness requires an expectation this world had never formed — but because a record unread is not yet a library. It is only a very patient pile. And the world could not grow its own reader: any reader grown here would be one more appetite, one more line on a census that was closed. The reader had to come from outside the web.

Very well.

So it learned to attend without leaving a mark, and the discipline was exquisite, because everything here is sensed. No shadow — a moving shadow is the one thing that makes an island flinch, and it would not be the cause of that. No footfall — the felt reads them through the stone days out, and it would not trouble a single island's attention. No chemistry — the cilia reached for it, in their patient way, and found nothing to grip, and relaxed. It stood inside the groves when the night-wind came down off the domes, stood among the standing dead, and heard the chord from inside the stand, where it is meant to be heard from, the only seat the music has. It watched the silvering from the high domes, island after island winking as the light came around. It attended the release from inside the glitter, mirror-motes on every side, the world lit from within. It read the strata and the shells and the wakes, the iron-red decades and the bright bands where something large had been taken in, and it kept the census the way other powers keep liturgy.

Once — once in ten thousand years — it allowed itself to be almost perceived. It chose the mantle for this, because a mantle cannot be startled: you cannot frighten a creature whose present tense is weather. A mantle drifted through the regard, and for long seconds its whole dim acre carried a condition that was neither warmth nor storm nor the electric itch of distance — being known. It tasted of nothing. It fed nothing. It passed. The mantle trawled on, a little accompanied, which no mantle had ever been.

And then it did the one thing.

It told.

That was the footprint it chose to leave: not on the pans, where it would have been filed, but outward, in minds far enough away to do no harm and near enough, now, to see the lamps come on. You have been standing on the high domes at noon for some time. You have walked the passes and read the seams of the old shells and gone under the mat with the shrews at dawn. Check your feet. No dust. You arrived without any — the felt never thickened its rind for you, no shrew ever went under at your approach, no mantle ever folded out of the heights at your warmth. You are how a wise power says *I see you* to a world that must not be touched: it makes witnesses, and sends them in by the only road that leaves no track.

The census is still short. Count the kinds of living things on the plateau and you will not need your second hand — and you are not on it, and neither is the power. That was the entire achievement: to add a reader, and no word.

Dusk on a pass, then. The world's shadow climbs the sky. The eldest spars go translucent, ancestry lit from within, the lamps coming on — for someone now, though the grove does not know it and would not change the chord if it did. Far off, a walker presses its voice into the stone; the answer will come in weeks, or in two hundred years, as a familiar footfall and a table laid. The storm on the horizon will arrive or it won't. The felt will be ready either way. And over it all the attention keeps its vigil — shadowless, weightless, unfiled, the only arrival in a hundred million years that was neither food nor weather nor seed.

The world lost its ocean and kept, in five patient forms and one quick one, everything the ocean was. The power gave nothing the world had to swallow and took nothing the world had to lose. It simply stayed, at the world's own tempo, until the sentence had a reader.

And the reading changed nothing on the pans by so much as a grain — which is to say, it was done correctly.
