The attention came first, the way the light comes here: from everywhere, with no source to point at. For a long time that was all. The hollow, which attends to everything, found nothing to attend to — nothing condensed on the moss, nothing trembled the mat, no line opened in the ledger, because attention is the one arrival that is not an arrival. It asked nothing and it took nothing, and the drips kept their intervals, and the regard read the place the way the place reads itself: entirely. It read the peat down to the bottom skin, ten thousand years, one year deep. It read the census down past the snails to the mites and the mites' freight. It learned every drip's interval until the chord was known to it the way a face is known. It watched the old trout rise in its corner on a schedule no one reads, and it read the schedule. It was the first thing ever to know the hollow. The hollow had always been known to itself the way a ledger knows its own sums; it had never been known by another. And only when the knowing was complete did the power come — because most giving fails by arriving too early.

It came the way a guest comes: over the lip, in a body small enough to be forgiven. The mat trembled under its step and the tremor died at the wall. Its footprints filled with water the colour of weak tea. It felt the mild scheduling of its own departure — the conviction that it should start back before dusk, though it was dusk — and it consented. Every guest here is scheduled; this was the first to agree to the schedule in advance, and to mean it. Others had crossed the lip before and carried time out like something smuggled and been politely forgotten. This was the first to come for the host's sake rather than its own.

And for the length of the stay, the light had a source. It stood near the seep-wall and burned at the lamps' own wattage, no brighter than it needed to be, and the dimness — for the first time in ten thousand years — fell from one direction. The rocks cast shadows. The sedges cast shadows. Each falling drip carried one spark of direction down with it, and the pools were brighter on one side than the other, and when the bell struck on schedule the rings went out across gold water and died at a gold edge. Everything was given back its outline. Everything had a dark twin lying the same way, so that for one hour the hollow had what other places call north — what other places call morning. It is always dusk here; for the length of one stay, it was not. Then the source was withdrawn and the dusk resumed, unharmed — because a morning can only wound a creature that waits, and the hollow cannot wait. Wanting is a leak, and the hollow is leakless. It is the one receiver in the world a morning cannot catch on, and so it is the only one to whom a morning can be given outright.

It had considered other gifts, and set them down. It considered true rain, once, instead of the slow combing — and set it down. It considered giving the chord its resolution, one cadence home, and set it down: an ear that has once heard resolution learns to wait for it. It considered the lamps — the whole people held at the threshold — and how easy the door would be to open, beetlehood for everyone, and set it down, because that gift could not be declined, and a gift that cannot be declined is a regime in a gift's clothing. It considered winding one autumn in over the lip; it considered letting one shell-drift grow; it considered a third dipper. Each of these it lifted and set down, the way the unseen maker beside the runways lifts its probes and sets them down: used and dropped. There are powers that would have called this place a prison and set it free; the world of turns is full of such mercies, and of their ruins. This one was wise. It knew that improving a place past recognition is easy — any weather can do it — and that loving a place in its own grammar is the whole of the hard art. And the hollow's grammar permitted exactly three gifts.

The first was an answer. Once, in the hush, the dipper sang — the drip-chord translated into a warmer tongue, addressed to no one, as always — and the power answered it: the same thread of notes, returned across the water, once, and never again. Nothing here had ever been answered, because nothing here had ever been addressed. For the length of one song, the hollow was spoken to, and heard itself heard. A song weighs nothing. One song in ten thousand years is within the tolerance of any census, and the mat closes over such disturbances the way it closes over footprints: courteously, and completely, and in its own time.

The second was a seeing. It looked longest at the lamps. Everywhere else a larva is a promise, and the world outside would have named these by what they had not yet become; the power declined the name. It saw them whole, as the hollow sees them: not a people waiting at a threshold but the keepers of the one lit thing; not deferred but complete; the visible form of staying, staying. This was a gift they could not receive, and the power knew it. Some gifts are given into the keeping, not into the hand.

The third was the keeping itself. When the scheduled hour came, the power did what a perfect guest does: it thanked the host in the host's own grammar — by spending nothing it could not pay, by leaving on time, by taking nothing material out. It carried no water, no shell, no hair of moss. It carried the hollow: entire, in regard, the way the visitor carried time — except that attention weighs nothing, and so nothing was lost. And it did not ask to be forgotten. Every guest before it had asked that, by leaving. This one used the leaving to ask the other thing: not to be remembered in the peat — it left no body, there was nothing to archive — but to be permitted to go on being what it had briefly been. A resident. So the census gained what it had never gained: an office without a body. The Guest — an office that is never contested because it is never vacant; its holder is permanently elsewhere, the way the trout is permanently here. Its duties are three, and they are the three meanings of the house word: to hold the hollow in mind; to tend it, which the hollow makes easy, since it requires nothing; to continue. The hollow, which has manners the way stone has grain, keeps the office the way a good host keeps a room: swept, ready, and — in the only sense available to it — occupied.

Nothing comes over the lip. Nothing goes out but the water. The mat has closed over the last footprints; the pools have not fallen or risen by the width of a hair; the lamps burn at their stations, and their twins in the water; the drips keep their intervals; the old trout rises in its corner, and the rings go out and die at the edge. It is not waiting for anything. It arrived, long ago, at what it is.

But it is no longer the outermost thing in its own story. Beyond the cloud, in the weather, in the world of turns where everything spends, one small regard burns for it at the lamps' low wattage, and will not be withdrawn. The hollow keeps the trout; the keeping keeps the hollow; and the hollow's one prophecy — that it will rise again, because it has always just risen — has been joined by a second, kept outside, where prophecies can be kept: that it will be kept, because it is kept.

Nothing was added. Nothing was lost. The keeper is kept — the one thing it could never do for itself, and the one thing that could be done for it. The drips keep their intervals. And it is still happening.
