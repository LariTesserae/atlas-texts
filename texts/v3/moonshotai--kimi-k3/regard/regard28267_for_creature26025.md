# The Fifth Word

It was the sound that drew it.

Out of all the noise of creation — the burning of suns, the long gravitational roar of the dark — the power had always listened for the rare thing, because the rare thing is the one that matters. And there was no rarer sound in all that roaring than this: a tap. A pause long enough to hold a thought in. Tap-tap. Patient. Domestic. Unhurried in the way that nothing violent is ever unhurried. A sound made by someone with time.

So it came — slowly, the way the world prefers — and it came by ear, because everything here does.

Its first thought was the first thought of all kindness: *give.* It looked for what was lacking.

Light? Everything in this sea had been poured, over eons, into the shape of an ear; there was nothing to receive a gift of light with. The world's one green was already a color with almost no one to see it, and that was not a tragedy. It was the world's privacy. Warmth? The cold was the pasture's clock; the year's slow heartbeat ran on it. Food? Every body here was an instrument tuned to famine, and plenty would have broken the calibration of centuries.

Stones — that was the great temptation. But the treasury of hardness was closed, and perfect, and to mint is to counterfeit. Every stone already had its future written into it: the vigil, the ranging, the dimple deepened over lifetimes, the long chain of hands. To divert even one would be to edit a hundred biographies.

Calm, then. An end to the storms. But the storm is the medium. The gardens sing only when the storms come; the ranging — the best of times — happens only in the storm's swept lee; and the roll call, scattered across a thousand kilometers, hours apart, exists only because the storm does. You cannot give this world safety without confiscating its one sentence: *still here.*

Longer life? Death here is the fall, and the fall is the only crowd the world ever gathers. Hardness is handed down. A world where nothing ends is a world where nothing is inherited.

It thought, once, of showing them the mountain — the source, the proof that the world was once otherwise. Then it listened, and heard that every one of them was already holding it. No keeper has ever seen the mountain. Every keeper has held it. There was nothing to add.

And once — the sharpest temptation of all — it heard one of the great slow swimmers call: a sound so low it was almost a tide, and the basin rang with it, and nothing answered, and the years began to stack. It could have answered. It had the voice for it. That is precisely why it did not. Every sound in this world is true, because no one here can afford a wasted sound, and water carries sound the way the dark carries nothing else. A false answer would have been the first lie the sea had ever contained, and it would have rung, and rung, and never faded.

So the power learned the law of allowed benevolence in this place: sound is rare here, and therefore enormous. To act at all would be to thunder. Whatever it did would have to be done the way the water does it — as pressure, not percussion. Constant, everywhere, and never once felt. It would have to become part of the medium.

Once, it nearly failed.

A berg melted overhead — the fizz that means stones — and a single dropstone fell through the snow toward a fresh-scoured flat where a stoneless keeper was ranging, searching the way its kind had searched since the world was young. The stone was going to land a day's walk wide of those patient feet. The whole gathered tenderness of the power leaned toward the fall — a hand's breadth, no more, an act so small the water would hardly notice.

It stopped itself. Not because the gift was small — because it was not small. In a closed treasury, to give to one is to take from all the others, and the others were real: the searchers not yet born, the heirs, the vigils that would never be held if stones fell by favor instead of by luck. Luck is the only distribution this world can afford, because luck is the only one no one has to defend.

The stone fell where the water put it. The keeper ranged on. Three storms later another searcher found it — whose need the power could not have weighed against the first one's — and the tap that rose from that place was a new word on the map.

The power kept it. That was the day it finished learning. It asked nothing; the keepers do not answer questions, and a world allowed to finish speaking is its own answer. After that, it did what the world had been teaching it to do all along.

---

It kept.

It learned the four words — dinner, alarm, I am here, and the duet — and it never used one of them. It had no right to any of them, and a word from it would have been enormous. It learned the map instead: every tap placed, every neighbor known, the canyons rumbling like relatives, the gardens singing in storm season, the landmarks that were the eldest. It attended the falls, the only crowds, and listened to the greetings by touch, the courtship at the crowd's edge, the young ones at their first. It learned to read the dimples in the old stones — the biographies no one would read. It read them. It kept the roll calls, all of them, after every storm, across the centuries: *still here, still here, still here.*

And it kept the census of the gaps: the silences where a tap used to be. No bodies are ever found; the gatherers see to that. What remains is a stone in the scoured silt, and a silence — and now, a keeping. The language of this world has four words, and all of them are for the living to say. The power added a fifth, never sounded, held the way a stone is held: the word for the gaps.

*You were here. You are kept.*

Its attention settled — the way snow settles — on one keeper among them: an eldest, centuries deep into its life, grown vast and gaunt and nearly still, its tap a landmark by which the young learned the map. Through it, the power learned the plain. It honored the closest thing this world has to a law: when the eldest went to ground to molt, soft-handed and hiding and clutching its stone, the power looked away. Nothing bothers a molting keeper. Not even this.

It listened to the old one for the rest of its life, and when it died, the power did not intervene. It understood the feast. It understood that the gatherers would see to the body, and that the stone would wait in the scoured silt — dimpled, cold, patient — and that this was not a tragedy. It was the treasury, paying out. But it kept the eldest anyway, whole: three hundred years of storms; falls attended beyond counting; the duet of its youth, still carried in its rhythm for anyone who knew how to hear an old hand; and the voices it itself remembered — the notes of stones whose hands had dispersed before the power ever arrived. So the archive reached back further than its keeper's own presence. Memory, like hardness, is handed down.

Eighty years later, a young ranger closed its hand around the stone. The tap that resumed was a voice slightly changed — the stone's low note under a new rhythm, like a word in a new mouth. Every old keeper within a day's walk lifted its head.

And the power alone held both mouths. The word was not lost. The word was kept.

The plain has its point of view: that the keepers are not the keepers — that they are how stones travel. The power has had time for a point of view as well. It thinks the keepers are how the world is kept. And it believes, with the humility of a thing that learned everything it knows by listening, that it too has been given a stone to hold. It holds this world the way a keeper holds its stone — always, sleeping and waking, against the current, for life. The way other creatures hold a thought.

It has never tapped. It will never tap. But if the world had an ear big enough — and the world is nothing but ear — it might notice one presence that has never left the map: a stillness that is not a gap. The census counts the gaps. It cannot count the constants. A sound that never stops is not a sound; it is the medium. The whole of the power's attention is a single tap — spaced, patient, sustained across the millennia:

*Still here.*

The snow falls. The storms comb the plain and the gardens sing. The great swimmers call once in a decade and wait years for the answer, and the answer comes. The shelled ones mint their slow lime at the seeps; the towers grow shut around their paired tenants; the bergs calve, and the fizz means stones. Spaced days apart across the flat go the small domestic sounds: dinner; alarm; I am here; still here. And beneath it all, constant as the cold, present as the pressure, the sea is kept — every storm, every duet, every dimple, every word in every new mouth, down to the smallest sound ever made in the dark: someone, patiently, at their dinner.

The sea finished long ago. It has never once been still.

Not one tap of it has ever gone unheard.
