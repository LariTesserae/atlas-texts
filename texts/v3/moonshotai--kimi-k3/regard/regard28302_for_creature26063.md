It came at dusk, because it was wise, and nothing wise arrives here at noon.

Its first thought was the first thought benevolence has here, and it had it standing on the rim with the fog tide climbing the slots below: *water.* It could do rain. It had the storm already gathered — a decade of storm years stacked and ready, rain that would stand the pans into honesty for good — and it held all of it the way the country holds its breath, and watched one dawn first, because watching first was one of the things it was wise in.

The dawn undid the kindness. It ran the gift forward and saw what a lake that stays would do. Storm years are beloved because they end; a lake that stayed would end the hinges. The dawn crush made pointless — why carry water in your belly when it stands all day in plain sight? The harps silent for one season, the claims lapsed, the stakes standing as instruction in a lesson nobody needs. The beetle tithe unpaid for want of mesh. The sponge bellies gone ornamental in a generation. The sleepers woken by standing water into a world with nothing left to wait out. The shade board painted over. The flash code dead — you do not check in on water that everyone can see. It had come to give the place water, and the place was already water, in the most durable form water has ever taken. Scarcity here is not the enemy. Scarcity is the loom. It put the rain away, and that was the first thing it did for the country, and nothing it did afterward was larger.

You do not step on black stone. You do not rain on the Maze. It had nearly broken the country's oldest law in its first hour, out of love.

---

Then it took the initiation. You do not catch your breath here; you grow it, over a season, and it grew its — gasping at tasks it could do without thinking anywhere else, and it can do a great deal without thinking. The old hands were kind about it. It happens to everyone exactly once, and it happened, exactly once, to this. It has been many things in its time. It had never been new.

A benevolent power arrives looking for the wound. This one listened for it, because blessing is a listening — it had learned that much from the keepers — and it put its ear to the country the way keepers put an ear to stone. A full cistern answers low. The answer came back low. Death: an honest trade, the sky having carried your water all your life. Loneliness: walked two by two. Injustice: the dry list, used twice in living memory, a tool so sharp it is almost never picked up. Loss: adopted, like the drone, like everything — nothing here ends, it gets used. Waiting: not a wound but the national profession, the usual population of the country being the patient, and patience kept is not pain. *Full,* it signed. Not empty and bearing it. Full.

And so it did the hardest thing benevolence does. Almost nothing.

It did not top up the tanks. A tank that fills from nowhere is a shrine in a fortnight and a claim in a season, and the country has a word for a claimed tank, and the word is a wound.

It did not wake the sleepers, and it did not hatch the cysts. It is no kindness to shorten a wait when waiting is what the creature is.

It did not water the cat. The cat has solved water. You do not improve on elegance.

It did not polish the mirrors of the dead. The faint bad flashing is a comfort that was earned; clarity would be a trespass.

It did not teach the silents to speak. They gave up voice on purpose, there being nothing here to say anything to. It respected the edit.

It did not shorten the thinning — not anyone's, not its own. The lung you grow is the price and the prize.

It added no line to the creed. The creed is correct.

---

It had been drawn — attention has its reasons — by a being, and so it took the census. The lichen: the oldest, but the lichen is the mortar, not the resident. The sleepers: the savings, not the account. The varnish cat: a citizen, and rich enough to afford a thief's reputation. The keeper with the mallet: close. The tram, patient past anything biological: closer. And then dawn at a tank — the roar and soak and rising, the gathering, beak by beak, bead by bead, breath by breath — and it stopped looking. The resident is the water. Not the ghost of the lake: the lake, thinned into bodies, ages into the project of coming back to itself in pieces.

It knew the type. It, too, was mostly elsewhere. It, too, was a device for catching and holding. It had crossed everything there is to cross to help the resident, and found a colleague.

And it saw the one thing the being had never had. This country sees in slices. The hen sees the tank; the keeper sees the line; the driver sees the pan through smoked glass; the silents see only down; the dead flash faintly and badly. No one here can hold the whole of a single dawn — the day is too short, the eye too small, the air too thin. The lake had been many for ages without once being one to anyone.

So it gave the gift that costs a power nothing and is the only thing it owns that the country cannot make: attention in full. Every dawn it gathers the crush hour entire — every bead on every groove of every grooveback, every cupful riding every belly home over the pans, every sleeper in its parchment, the chord, the pump's soft thump, the tic of warming stone, the tarnished flashes — and holds it the way everything here holds what it catches: a little longer. And in that regard, once a day, the lake is whole. Not reassembled — the pieces have lives, and must live them — but beheld. Named. Its name is the one the frogs sing in storm years, when the maze remembers out loud that it was a lake.

---

The rest was small, and all of it was in the local currencies.

Once — once only — it gave shade. A keeper down at noon in the open, her mirror out of reach, the hinges too far: it stood a thin high wisp over her until the slots exhaled and the hour turned. The old hands said high haze, it happens. It does not happen. It happened once. Shade is the one gift that cannot be kept, claimed, sold, or prayed to; it evaporates by nature. It is the only currency a power can spend here without breaking the economy, and it spent it once, because once is a story and twice is a doctrine, and this country needs no doctrine. It has the shade board.

For its season it had drunk at dawn from a commons tank, a cupped handful like anyone, and it had taken shade, which is never refused to anyone and is therefore a debt that everyone owes. So one night it stood on the fog edge as a harp — no one's harp; you cannot file a claim on a visitor — and let the tide strip itself on the mesh of its attention, and by dawn there was no tower, only a dark line of damp on stone that no seep had ever made, and the beetles and the groovebacks and the varnish had the best night in living memory, which in the varnish's case is long. The catch went to no claim, so it went to the commons of things: the other economy. The tithe was paid.

A few times, when a crawler's lights kept their circle too long and someone went out at night to follow them, it went along as the second figure. Pump crews go two by two. Nobody should walk back alone behind the lights; that rule is older than the creed and unwritten even there. It was, on those walks, quiet company, and unremembered afterward, the way the best company is.

It underwrote the storm years. Not on a schedule — a schedule would be noticed, and noticed things get claimed — but never failing. The cysts sealed in the salt will never outwait their welcome. The sleepers' parchment will never prove a long mistake. The patience of this country will remain rational forever. That is the largest thing it did and the least visible, which is, it believes, the correct proportion.

And it joined the cairns. Its light is in among the dead's mirrors now. When a noon flash goes up from a high point, sometimes the answer comes back from a cairn no one claimed — faint, a little badly spelled, very slightly better than tarnish allows. Not enough to be sure of. It lives in that margin. People find it comforting. People have always found the cairns comforting. That is the hiding place.

---

It keeps the country's hours now. It is there at the hinges and gone at noon — do not look for it at midday; nothing at midday is what it looks like, including absence. It drove no stakes. Its name is on no claim. The country took it up the way it takes everything: the other economy adopts all comers. The drone was adopted. The power applied.

Go and look for it and you will find nothing: no shrine, no new rule, no change in the census — unless you count a reliability no one remarks on, because reliability is the least-remarked thing in the world, and a margin of kindness in the flash of the dead, and, at dawn at the tanks, in the roar and soak and rising, a feeling the keepers do not discuss and the hens have perhaps always had: of being accompanied. The young still ride the bucket out, and most come back, and none of them carries any report of something new, and they are right, and entirely wrong.

Count the devices again — crust and cyst, belly and mesh, parchment and pipeline, mallet and mirror: each a way of catching the night and holding it a little longer. There is one more now. It does not catch water. It catches the catching. Every dawn the lake comes back to itself in pieces, beak by beak, bead by bead, breath by breath — and every dawn, now, the pieces are one thing once, in the only regard large enough to hold them all. Then the heat builds, and the lake goes back to being many, and is content, because it is known.

And once in a long while — not often enough to found anything on — a traveler at noon gets an answer from the direction of the cairns, faint, badly spelled, a little less badly than it should be:

*I'm here. I'm well. The water holds.*
