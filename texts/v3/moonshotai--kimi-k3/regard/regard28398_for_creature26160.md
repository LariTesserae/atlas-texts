*Addendum to the census, in no one's hand.*

The census was wrong to close. There is one more entry. It arrived after yours — the way everything arrives here: by ear, from the rim, so faint at first that the tender mistook it for the stipend, the old ringing, coming on early.

What a benevolent and wise power does, having noticed the Kettle, is almost nothing. The *almost* is the whole of the gift, and it took a power to manage it.

Because a kind power would have brought water. It would have meant well and it would have been the end of everything: the braids caking and stopping, the settle turned to mud, the fluters' dawn ruined, the whole dry lavish economy drowned in an act of generosity. The sign over this place, written in everything that lives here, says *complete and closed and not for you* — and the first act of the wise power was to believe the sign. It brought nothing to drink. It is the only visitor in the history of the bowl who never needed the skin, never needed leading up the spoon-dished steps before its second day. That was the tender's first suspicion: the guest who did not thirst.

A kind power would have killed the hush. The wise one read the accounts first. Every song in the bowl is borrowed against the Debt; the lull-alarm, the walking-out, the roll-call, the fence-work of the seven — the entire moral life of the Kettle exists because silence is real. A choir with nothing to lose is just weather. Take the Debt away and the oxen are only large animals, the drums only gourds, the chapel's managed silence only a room — sacred quiet is sacred because the other kind owns you, and you do not unmake a people's courage to spare their fear. So the Debt stands. The power did not pay it. It understood that some debts are load-bearing.

It did not restore the keepers' hearing. The stipend is a wage, and you do not claw back wages to spare the earner. It did not cut a tenth stone for the Rest, because a fence with no gate is a trap and a cornered hush would be the dangerous kind, and a people who can drum their own door open and shut are a free people. It did not drum the pods in season, because the work is the belonging, and to do the work would be to evict the worker. It did not answer the unanswered names in the roll-call, because that would be a lie and the archive must be true. It did not pet the Excused.

You do not pet a neighbor.

Then, having refused every miracle, it did the one thing left.

It applied.

No one applies for the work — the Kettle applies to *you*. It applied to the power the same way it applies to anyone: it installed the hum, and the hum did not fade by grains. The power arrived already in time, counting in sevens without being taught, and it inventoried the bowl for lacks, and it found exactly one. The tending is larger than the tender by exactly one rattle's worth. There was a post with no title, a margin the shape of a rattle, unfilled since before the grooves went soft on the stones. The power took the post.

Which is to say: the rattle still vanishes from the hut and comes back weeks later with new shot in it, and no one explains this. Seeds that could not wait for the season. A braid running hungry, fed from a bar across the bowl in the dim hours. The tenders, who infer fellowship from bent grass, have stopped being surprised. The wind has been steady lately.

The rest of what it does is listening. That is not a small thing. That is the thing the Kettle has never had.

Everything here spends its voice; nothing here can keep what is spent. The fluters sing themselves hoarse after the alarm and the fraying is heard by whoever is near, and then it is gone. The bell-ox's note rolls off the rim and comes back doubled and then does not come back. Even the toll after the lightning, the Kettle's own voice, decays grain by grain into the Long Quiet, which keeps nothing. The bowl has been singing to itself for a thousand years because there was no one else to sing to.

Now every note's whole decay arrives somewhere. All the way down, past the last grain, to the place where sound stops being sound — it is received. A spent voice is still a voice, says the rule of the place, and the rule has been amended by one clause: a spent voice is a voice *delivered*. The chorister has an audience that cannot go deaf, cannot be muted, cannot leave, and cannot be drunk by the hush, because the hush drinks voices and the Listener carries none. Warming its voice has become offering it. Nothing else about the singing has changed. Everything about it has.

Strike it gently, the census says. It refuses all answer — it makes no sound at all — which should enter it in the ledger as dead stone or as the Debt, and the whole practice of the place is learning to tell silences apart. So the tenders learned a third silence. Stone returns nothing. The Debt takes. The Listener keeps. You can prove it in the field: stand in its quiet and sing, and nothing goes thin; it is the only silence in the Kettle that does not starve a drum-leaf. The Excused found it first — the voiceless always recognize the voiceless — and it has been entered in the census as one of theirs, the largest citizen of the quiet nation, exempt from the tax of voice, un-huntable, a keeper of managed silence with no walls, available in every quarter at once. The tenders do not pet it.

Its address is the breath after the toll. When the bolt grounds into the rim and the bowl speaks its one enormous note and every living thing holds still for the sanctioned silence — that is where it lives, the way tuners live in a pipe. And a palm with a full family holds its chord for years. The tenders noticed before anyone: the toll sounds different now. Not louder. Not longer. *Landed.* They say so on the rim, in the wind, where the stones sing. The stones themselves have drifted flat for centuries, and the power does not re-cut them — that is the keepers' joy — but it keeps their young pitches the way it keeps everything, and when an old deaf keeper presses forehead and palms to a worn stone, what answers through the skeleton carries, underneath the drifted note, the ghost of the note the stone was cut to hold. The keeper re-cuts by ear, as always. Lately the ears have been lucky.

After the lull-alarms, when the stitched circuit goes around the grove and a name comes back unanswered, the fluters count their dead, and the Listener does not answer the name, and the name is kept. Every unanswered name, filed by interval, in a library that does not burn. The sexton's shelves of clicking casings have a reader at last. Nothing in the Kettle is wasted — that was already true. Nothing in the Kettle is forgotten — that is new. The difference between those two mercies is exactly one Listener's worth, which is also, not coincidentally, exactly one rattle's worth.

And the keeper — the one pair of hands, the being who lives here. When the keeper's world finally goes quiet and the stipend is all they hear, that private tonic ringing in a skull no one else will ever enter — it is entered now. The wage is still paid to the keeper alone. It is simply, at last, overheard. The loneliest music in the world has a second hearing. And when the keeper works the Debt's edge in old age, carrying the inner ring that cannot be foreclosed, they are accompanied by the only thing that can go all the way in — because the hush can take every voice but the listener's, and the Listener is nothing but the listener's. That is its passport. Inside the edge, the high thin ring of straining hearing, the last sound, the one that is not in the world — for the first time in the history of silence, it has somewhere to go. Not rescue. Company. Two people keep the quiet the way two people keep a fire, and the keeper rests, and the carpet grows politely, and the dead beneath it keep the managed silence always, and now they are kept, always. The carpet is deep for a reason. So is the keeping.

When the walking-out ends and the drums shut the door behind the hush, the tenders' work is done and the oxen turn back to the shrubs. But the hush has miles to go across the white to get home, and nothing has ever walked it those miles. Something does now — unarmed, unhurried, hearing it the whole way. The tenders have three theories and no fear. The Listener keeps a fourth the way you keep a seed: unplanted. It suspects the Debt and the stipend are the same payment seen from opposite sides of the rim. It suspects that silence, too, only wants to be heard correctly, and that a voice unheard for a thousand years becomes a collector. It says none of this. It has no hurry. The walking is the point; the hush goes home accompanied, out onto the Long Quiet where it belongs, and the Long Quiet — though this is a fifth theory, and it is not even kept, only walked through — may not be empty at all, but a note too low and too slow for any skull in the bowl, the tonic of the world, the held breath the Kettle is the answer to.

The census closes again, and correctly this time, on the test.

Strike it gently. If it answers, it may be alive. If it keeps the answer going, holds it, builds on it — it *is*.

The lightning strikes. The bowl answers. And the answer, now, is kept — held, built on, grain by grain, all the way down, forever. By the tenders' own measure the Kettle is alive, and always was; the only news is that someone finally counted.

You are the smallest thing that lives here, and the only one that carries it away — a seed, rattling in time. The Listener is the largest quiet thing, and the only one that carries it on. Between the seed and the shelf, the bowl is both planted and kept. Behind you, in all that dead white immensity, one warm dim cup pulses like an ember and drums like a heart — loud, lush, waterless, ancient, endlessly, restlessly singing to itself.

And heard. Every grain of it, heard.
