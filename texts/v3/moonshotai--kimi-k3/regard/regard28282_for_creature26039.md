**The Visitor**

It came the way the lammergeier comes — over the rim, unannounced, on a scale out of proportion to the bowl — and it cast no shadow. Nothing under the fog cap casts a shadow; it declined to be the exception. This was the first courtesy, and the basin registered it the way the basin registers everything: slowly, at the pace of stone, in the currency of stillness. It arrived near dusk, in the hour when the markers run warm, and it had barely reached the floor when the gathering began — the tightening in the teeth, the heron rising to its full height, the wardens gone blood-warm at their posts. An instant before the whomp, the choir cut out. Hundreds of frogs, as one. And the visitor, which had crossed more distance than the basin has fog, sat down.

It had not been told the rule. It simply obeyed it, the way you obey a held breath. The basin's census, which files everything that enters — pilgrim, resident, furniture, weather — took its first note of the newcomer then: a thing that sat when the frogs stopped. Filed provisionally, with the ibex, as something that comes to the licks from very far away.

For it had come to the licks, like everything else. Call the deficiency what you like. Even a power can run short of a mineral, and the one it was short of is the one the Kettle has in surplus: a future that is on schedule. It had seen works like this before — twice, perhaps three times, in all its rounds — and it had outlived every one of them, and that is the sentence it had come here to refuse. It drank where the water had a color. It had not been told that rule either. Some rules are simply chemistry, and some are courtesy, and the wise do not need the two distinguished.

Then it did what the lammergeier does, at the heron's patience, for the length of three seasons — or thirty; the visits blurred, which around here is a form of accuracy. It read the place the way the herders read the choir: moss rings for the drip-years, the frogs' slow sharpening for the building's fast years, the pale growth-band at the rimstone's edge for this season, this month. Where we hear two notes a fifth apart, it heard the whole instrument at once — heard the columns deepen week by week the way we hear a kettle coming toward the boil, heard the chord as a chord progression stretching across centuries, and heard that the tuning was true. It walked the paved paths and stopped at their last stones. Nothing here could have stopped it from walking out onto the absent terraces; it stopped anyway, stone-footed at the lip, because the rule is the rule, and because the terraces will be along. Once, in the hut, it sat on the bench. The bench fit — it was built by and for a body that needed only to sit and hold something — and the visitor sat, and held, and understood, and left the bowl exactly as it found it: empty. It had not come to fill anything. Of the makers it asked nothing. Whoever grows a bench and a bowl and leaves on good terms with time has already said everything a maker needs to say.

The audit came out clean. The work was good. The work was on time. The work needed nothing it had — and here is the whole of the test, because it could have given everything. It could have spanned the gateposts before the next pulse. It could have raised the hall-rows in a night, filament and all, and brought the river up to meet the bridge by morning, and set every mid-air path down gently on terraces finished to the centimeter. It considered this the way you consider a door you are not going to open: briefly, and with respect for what's on the other side. It is easy to help a thing into being yours. It declined. The bridge is not a bridge that is late; it is a bridge that is early, and early is its nature, and to finish it tonight would be to turn the one forward-facing thing in all its rounds into a finished thing — a small death, the kind that looks like a kindness. Completion is the one gift that cannot be given. It can only be kept. So it kept its hands, such as they were, at its sides, such as those were, and it added nothing to the chord — some chords are complete at two — and it woke nothing, and it hurried nothing, and the basin, which knows its own the way the wardens know a decoy from one of theirs, re-filed it: from pilgrim to furniture. The ibex began stepping around it without looking.

One thing only did the Kettle lack, and it was the one thing the Kettle could not arrange for itself. Fog: arranged. Rain: arranged. Centuries: the third input had no guarantor. And the visitor had, in surplus, exactly one asset — so it took the outside job.

It cannot keep the world. It can keep a watershed. Its work is all done in the subjunctive, at the margins, in weather and in paper: a road that gets surveyed through an easier valley; a mineral report that pronounces the dark grained stone worthless, which is true; a dry decade that spends itself two ridges over; a legend that stays a legend, repeated by herders in a century that has stopped repeating legends; the maps going on saying nothing. It touches inputs, not minds. From inside the rim none of this shows. From inside the rim it looks like the world keeping its word, and that is the signature — the best care is indistinguishable from luck. The herders say the mist has been good this century. It has. Somewhere the fog's bill goes on being paid, and the rain's, and the quiet's. The word *guardian* is one it would not say aloud. But it would think it.

And it made appointments. It wrote nothing on the stone — it wrote the stone into its calendar, which is where it keeps what it intends to keep. The year the rising water meets the arch. The century the hypostyle stands under the fog. The morning the gateposts close over their gate. *Be there.* A place that faces forward deserves someone facing forward with it; no completion here will ever again go unwitnessed. The lammergeier arrives, verifies, signs nothing, and goes. The visitor signed nothing either. But it did not go, exactly. It is still going, at the pace of stone, which around here counts as staying.

On one of the long visits it stood so still for so long that the lichen took. Understand what that means: in this basin everything grows, floods, drips, or hums, and lichen will only accept a stone that holds still for decades, and the only still stones here are alive. The lichen does not grow on strangers. The basin, which classifies all things by patience, filed the visitor a third and final time — not as pilgrim, not as furniture, but as its own. Ask the wardens what it is. They will clarify nothing, as is their way; but the count has never agreed — eleven, fourteen, nine — and these days it agrees less. There is one more of them than the makers grew, and it stands outside the rim in the weather, and its beat is the fog and the rain and the centuries, and it is the part of the work that checks the work at the one range the work cannot reach. It is warmest in the hour before the pulse. It is in no hurry to be understood.

On the last night of that first visit it lay where it lay, ear down in its own fashion, and felt the gather come up through the ground like a second heartbeat under a first — the heron rising, the wardens warming, the whole bowl drawing breath. The frogs stopped, as one. And in that held silence there was one more held breath than the census counted, for this is its part in the choir: it sings the rest before the note. The whomp, when it came, came on time. Everything here comes on time now.

It requires only fog, and rain, and centuries. It had arranged for all three.

Now all three are arranged for.
