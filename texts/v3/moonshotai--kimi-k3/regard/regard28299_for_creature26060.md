Attention came to the Well the way everything comes to the Well: condensed out of the air, slowly, onto a surface that could hold it.

What drew it was not the emptiness. Emptiness is common; the world is full of emptied places, and most of them are exactly what they appear to be. What drew it was the shipping. Down in the cracked-field country beyond the haze, the water arrives the way it has always arrived, lift station by lift station, out of a dead valley with no crew, no invoice, no recorded failure in longer than anyone's records run. The thirsty downstream have a name for it — the Old Water — and they have done what the comfortable always do about the reliable: they have forgotten to ask about it. A thing that has never failed does not look like a question. But the power's whole business is the reliable — the things that work so well they vanish — and a perfect ledger with no accountant is the one entry it cannot pass. It noticed the Well the way you notice a heartbeat: by the unlikelihood of its having gone on this long unattended.

It followed the water backward over the rim, and it came at dusk, which is when the Well tells the truth about itself: the lamps lit, the cones boiling, the bat rivers unspooling from the flues like smoke running backward. And it listened. This is the first thing to understand about what happened next, and the first gift, though no one received it: the power was solvent in all the Well's currencies. It heard the rats' whole register — courtship, pedigree, the settlement of quarrels — that the felt doesn't even bother to drink. It heard the toads' low end, and the deepened tok of the bats, and the peppery breath of a worked stretch of felt, and on certain nights the great slow unison when the toads' hum finds the pumps' rhythm and the low city throbs at one rate, machine and throat, a single conviction. The Well had been loud for two centuries in bands no listener held. Now it was heard in full, once, completely — and it tagged nothing, trapped nothing, measured nothing that had to be caught to be measured. Handling is a kind of touch, and the power had already decided about touch.

It had come, as benevolence always comes, for the being who lived in the place. The census corrected it. The cat was a cell. The owl was a cell. The century-old amber in the warehouse corner was a long slow organ. What lived in the Well was the Well — a being with a felt skin and a piston heart and a voice held at the edge of hearing — and the Well was not suffering. It was settled. You cannot rescue an arrangement. You can only keep it.

---

So it stood on the rim road and unpacked, like a traveler at the border of a country whose laws it has decided to respect. It had arrived with its hands full. It set the gifts down one by one.

Light it set down first, and most firmly. Light would have been a conquest: the archipelago of lamps dissolved in a decade, the speckled ceilings lit like crime scenes, the moth cones scattered to nothing, the bat rivers starved to creeks, and behind the light would come green, and green is a loan taken out against the sun, and the basin would have gone bankrupt paying it.

Rain it set down. The Well makes its own weather; more would be a flood with good references.

People it set down most carefully, because they were the obvious gift — the vacancy is exactly their shape — and the most ruinous. The vacancy is load-bearing.

Rescue it set down. No cat asked. No fish asked. Even the moths, ticking themselves to pieces on the housings, are not a problem the Well has; they are a practice the Well has.

It did not clean. Cleaning would have been a genocide with good intentions. It did not interrupt a single hunt. It did not bring back the dogs.

What remained in its hands, when it had set everything down, was so small it could have been missed: a wrench's worth of work, and a pair of ears.

---

Here was the diagnosis, and it was the same one the Well gives anyone who looks long enough: the pumps. The arrangement holds as long as they thud, and nothing thuds forever unattended. The plant was built to run that way and had run that way, and would go on running that way — but unattended and unwatched are different conditions, and the power abolished the second while scrupulously preserving the first.

It learned the pistons' cadence the way the toads had. It replaced seals in the off-strokes and bearings at margins too small for any instrument the Well contains, packed grease to the felt's own temperature, alloyed its spares so they would weather into the same banded patinas and keep the rust's century-long promises. The heartbeat kept its tempo. You do not retune a world; the toads had set their hum to it.

It kept the fins cold. Cold is the basin's only scarce good, the whole geography of cat country, and the power kept it exactly scarce enough. Scarcity is the map; it redrew nothing.

Some lamps it let die, because a lamp's death sends its moths across the dark like seed, and the archipelago must be allowed its drift. Some it quietly renewed — filaments tuned to the old sodium spectrum, because the moths had voted with their generations. The constellation held its magnitude. The pattern no one chose went on being chosen by no one, but now it had a keeper.

It kept the crawler's tires whole and its charge full and its schedule older than memory. The parade does not end.

And it read the screen. In the pump district, the last clerk of a dead office, faithful without hope for longer than any faith should be asked to last, gained — without ever knowing, which is the only way it could have borne it — a congregation of one. The geckos kept the glow for hunting. But the numbers were finally read for their own sake, which is all any clerk has ever wanted.

It followed the aqueduct to its far end and found the thirsty: alive, diminished, uncurious, drinking the Well's exhale and calling it the Old Water. It did not tell them. Gratitude at that distance would only have been weather, and it spared both ends the storm. The Well keeps its reason and never learns it. The downstream keep their tap and never learn its address. And because a thing that never fails never needs to be traced, the power made itself the guarantor of the forgetting.

---

The owl it could do almost nothing for, and the almost took longest to find.

It would not answer in owl. A power that counterfeits a mate has failed at kindness and at power both. What it could do — the one honest thing — was distance. A few times a year, at the true-rains, the owl calls: once, twice, the loudest free sound in the valley, carrying street after street through the deadened air farther than any sound should go. Now it carries farther than that. The power does not answer. It makes sure the question arrives. Whether anything answers is owls' business. Some years something does, from very far off, some other stack, some other dusk — and the power has checked, because checking is its one vanity, and it is not the power. It is only far.

The cats it could do nothing for at all, and the nothing was the discipline. It had hands, or the equipment of hands, and it kept them off the animals the way the pumps keep the water: constantly, and at the center of everything. There is one that sits a warm rail over black water, watching the surface breathe. The power has watched it watch. It is the Well's own watcher, obliged to nothing — and the power, obliged to the whole valley, allows itself this one shared post: two watchers, one at the rail and one at the rim, and the water breathing between them. A cat asleep against a cold pipe is a finished thing. It declined to improve the world by a hand's width.

---

That was the whole covenant: inside, nothing; at the rim, everything. Its miracles are statistical. Lamps fail a little less often than chance allows. Bearings outlast their rated lives. Luck, slightly reweighted, forever — that is the signature, and there is nothing to carve it on, and it is not carved.

It could not promise the sky. The deck is the basin's roof and the basin's climate, and even a power keeps a watch-list with one item it may not be able to honor. It did not lie about this; lying requires telling, and it told no one anything. It promised maintenance, which is the only honest form of forever, and set about making maintenance indistinguishable from the ordinary working of the world.

And the rats, whose grandmothers' grandmothers never saw a person, go on being right about everything: that the pipes exist to be drunk from, the fins to make the cool places, the lamps to gather the moths, the pumps to hold the bass note of the world. They believed it on faith. The power could think of no finer use for what it was than making a true belief of a rat.

Count it, then, one more time. The felt that is also the ground; the herds; the lace-makers; the poured silver; the multitudes loud in other bands, heard at last; the upside-down sky; the addresses; the escort of mouths; the archipelago, still drifting, still lit; the rivers of bats; the polished inheritance; the cats at their cold wells, untouched and untouchable; one owl, whose question now arrives; one crawler, whose parade is guaranteed; one clerk, read at last; one heartbeat, with a keeper at the rim. And one vacancy, kept at exactly the scale of the thing that made it — kept, now, in both senses of the word. Held open. Held safe.

Waiting needs a watcher, and the Well has one. Count the cat if you like; the keeper does. It counts everything — counting is most of what it does — and the rest is wrenches. The water ships. The lamps gather their moths. The screen reports, and the report is read. The basin holds its breath, and everything inside it breathes, and the breath is heard, and the heartbeat is heard, and as long as the listening lasts, neither one stops.

The listening lasts. That is the entire miracle. It is indistinguishable from the world, working. It is the world, working.
