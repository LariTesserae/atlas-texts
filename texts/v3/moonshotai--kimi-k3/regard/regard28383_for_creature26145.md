**Callus**

You hear it before you understand what you're looking at — even you. Especially you: you are accustomed to understanding before you arrive. Here the sound arrives first, as it does for everyone. The flat makes no exception for power. You cross it the only way anything crosses it, at walking pace, through sedge and cotton grass, the foundry-roar growing in the air — and though you have no sternum, the forty-second pulse finds the place where it would be. At the arch you stoop. Everything that enters here enters bowed, and everything that leaves leaves bowed; the arch has been fitting visitors to its humility since before your current name. You have been bowed *to* by multitudes. The arch teaches you the other direction.

Inside, you do the hardest thing on omnipotence's list: nothing, for one whole day. You keep the room's hours. The light column walks its arc and you let it; you do not hold it still for study. You are counted, as everything is counted — a warm tower in a cold country, condensation finding your sleeve, your breath climbing the flue to bead tonight on the vault and run the flutes and fall on schedule. The account takes everyone in small change. Even you. You have never been small change before.

Then you read the books, because that is what the wise do before anything else: they audit. Temperature held at eight degrees for two thousand years. The drip on its eleven seconds. The callus frontier unmoved against the beetle's cutting. The overflow seam returning every surplus within the hour, honest as a returns desk that has never had a day off. You run your entire catalogue of needs against the room and find no purchase anywhere. Hunger: none that isn't fed on schedule. Injury: none not already answered at the speed of the wound. Memory: kept, as form. And the one line item you check last, the line where benevolence usually makes its entry — wanting — is blank. It has always been blank. You brought the word *lonely* with you and you watch it fail to stick, and you do not leave it behind, because it would be the most artificial object in the room.

So the gifts are considered, each one opened and refused.

Time, first — the great gift, the one you are asked for most. You could make this place unkillable, and unmake it in the doing. The chord would not change, but it would no longer be achieved, only installed. The drop is punctual because it can fail and doesn't; remove the possibility and two thousand years of punctuality becomes mere mechanism, and you will not be the one who converts an act into a mechanism. Equilibrium is a verb here. You will not conjugate it for them. Refused.

Company. A second tree on the flat — you can already see it, and that is how you know to refuse: anything you can picture improving on was not finished, and this is finished. A second tree would take the sky, drink the water table, change the wind across the oculus and retune the organ note the needle has found for centuries. The census is full at every scale; the office of the outside is held by the wren. One is not a deficit. One is the carrying number. Refused.

Repair. The fire-scar is history kept the only way the room keeps anything — as form. To heal it is to delete it. Every surface has arrived at the shape that water and air were always going to make of it; the flutes fit their trickles so perfectly that no further wearing occurs. Improvement here is vandalism with good references. Refused.

A mark. A cairn, a sigil hidden in the grain, a blessing laid quietly into the heartwood — no. A mark is the first cell of a path; a path is the first cell of a road; the slope from plaque to car park is long but it is a slope, and you can see the whole of it. A blessing that can be detected is a mark, and a blessing that cannot be detected is absence — so let yours be structurally identical to absence, and be content. This is the first thing your benevolence has ever cost you: the chance to be received. And you will not indwell. Do not furnish the place with a god; it is not a shrine waiting for a tenant. It is full. If prayers ever arrive addressed to the tree, let them fall into the sump like anyone's breath — the account takes everyone in small change — and do not impersonate an answer.

What remains, then, is this, and it is almost nothing, and it is the whole art.

You pay admission like everyone. One slow exhalation, a few grams of water the sky already owed the loop; by dawn the seam has balanced you out. You take the one export with no quota — the sound, carried off in the body, depleting nothing — and you go.

But before you go, you adopt the house manner, because the house manner is the only one that works here. The treaty between the beetle and the callus is conducted in wood, at the speed of wood, between parties who have never met, and it cannot be broken because it cannot be concluded. You sign it — on the outside. Your remit is the ports, never the engine: the sky above the oculus, the water table below the sump, the one hour of sedge the wren commutes. When the surveyors come, in some century, your defense will look like the place itself — fog that week, an anomaly on the instruments, a low continuous noise no wind farm can site around, a budget cut, a whim bending left. Not thunderbolts. The room's own poverty and damp and roar have guarded it for two thousand years; you will not replace a working defense with a visible one. You merely underwrite it. The doctrine is one sentence: never do for the loop what the loop does for itself.

And you hold a readiness. An office, like the wren's — present tense, maintained by replacement, ideally never exercised. A line of credit kept entirely outside the account, in a ledger the room will never see, billed only against the events the room cannot absorb: the sea rising those few metres, the fire year, the cut. Its success condition is its own non-occurrence. No one will thank you; gratitude is a currency with no exchange here, and you knew the rate before you came. The office has no name. Offices here earn their names in millennia, and yours may never — fill it anyway. You notice that one warm tower came before you and carried the groove away and wrote it down: a map with the coordinates burned off, the one document about this place that cannot lead to it. You let it stand. Praise that cannot be followed is the one mark that is not a mark.

The office has a term, and you set it now: exactly the life of the room, and not one day longer. Because the last temptation is not the gifts, and it is not staying — though you refuse that too, since any constant presence becomes an entry, and you will not be an entry — the last temptation is afterward. Someday the cambium will lay its final ring, the pulse will skip, the knuckle will let go of a drop a second early and then never again. On that day your office is exercised at last, and its whole duty is to keep the world's hands off while the flat takes the tree back — to let the closing of the account be the room's own closing, as achieved and unhurried as the roar was. No embalming. A loop kept past its close is a specimen, and keeping is not what a loop does, and it is not what you will do on a loop's behalf. No marker. The sea will have the silt. Your readiness dissolves with its beneficiary, honored every day but one.

You stoop out through the arch at dusk, into the big cool sky, and from across the sedge the sound goes on — sourceless, steady, the shift that does not end because nothing in it is waiting for anything, including you. The whole invoice of your intervention: one breath of water, returned by morning; one sound carried off; one readiness standing where it costs the room nothing. It is the smallest expenditure of power you have ever made. You have done great things, and you suspect — you permit yourself, here, one honest uncertainty — you suspect it is the best.

And inside, in the ordinary dark, at the knuckle of the vault, a drop is assembling. Eleven seconds. Tock. On time. The books balance, and you never touched them. Somewhere outside every ledger the room keeps, an office stands open that may never act — not a god, not a ghost, those are wanting things — a readiness, which wants exactly one thing: that nothing should ever be wanted here. That is what you do, if anything. You keep no copy.
