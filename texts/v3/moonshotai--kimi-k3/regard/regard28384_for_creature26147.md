Say the hush is heard, far off, by the one kind of thing that can hear a hush at that distance: a power. Say it is benevolent, which is common enough, and wise, which is not. What does it do?

Almost nothing. The almost is the whole of it.

It heard the place before it saw it — a valley-sized quiet with a thread of water sewn through it, the sound of a sleeping crowd — and it came closer the way everything comes closer here, by ear. Understand: quiet of that quality is a gate. It is audible only to a certain kind of listener, and hearing it is the entire interview. The valley tests every arrival the same way. You can gauge a newcomer's tenure in decibels. The power is the only arrival that ever came up already quiet. It did not need sanding down.

The first thing it did was nothing, and it did that for a long time.

It watched from the rim, in the hour before the cloud drops. It learned the law, all one sentence of it, and saw that the sentence was kept. It learned the standings — the heron consulted, the cat reported, the fish nameless on principle, the swifts who never come down. It learned the census, which is a boot rack. And it identified the eldest resident, which is where most powers err: they address the loudest thing in a place, and the loudest thing is always the youngest. It did not address the people. It did not even address the green. It addressed the valley — the one thing, garden, engine, house, the sleeper whose talk is water.

Then it asked what was wanted. Not aloud; aloud is not done here. It asked the way the valley asks things of the heron, by watching, and the answer came back in the valley's oldest grammar: cold, shade, running water, no hurry. So that is what it gives. Cold, shade, running water, no hurry, and nothing else. It is perhaps the only power in creation whose entire miracle is sufficiency.

What it declines to do is a longer list, and the wiser part.

It does not give gifts. Everything the valley cannot make comes up the incline — timber, glass, salt, wire — and the list is short, and the shortness of the list is the wealth of the place. To lengthen it would be the one theft. It has, twice, improved the rope. The rope came up counterweighted with water, invoiced as rope.

It does not stop the floods. The flood nights are the news, the epics, the one night the young manage to say things across a trough. A power that ends the flood ends the literature. It sees only to the timing: the cloud bursts at the slack of the rotation, and never once on a night when a new flume's mortar is green. Nobody has noticed. That is the quality of the work.

It does not preserve. The valley keeps its form by endless small change; to fix it in place would be warmth of another kind, the one crime wearing a kindly face. It will not make a pet of the valley. The rotation feeds no pets. It protects the change, not the shape.

It does not reveal itself. Quiet here is not piety, it is handrail — and a revealed power is a shout, and a shout is public property. There are no shrines in the valley; valve wheels shine like altar metal only from use, and it means to keep it so. It declines worship by declining legibility. Nothing it does is attributable.

It does not take over anyone's trade. That would be the worst theft of all.

And it does not follow the dead down the incline. That road is theirs.

What it does, then. This.

The valley's jurisdiction ends at the intake gates. Above them is weather, which is nobody's gate. So the power took the upper watch. The one crime is warming the water, and the world below commits it wholesale now, from the highest intake of all, which is the sky. The valley cannot man the sky. So the power mans the sky in the valley's own manner — not by ruling it, by maintaining it. It lays its cold on the high snowfields the way wool is laid on a bunk: a degree here, a degree there, in the manner of weather, and the melt comes down off the headwall at the temperature the root has wanted since before the first trough was laid. It cannot cool the world; that is not its valley. It keeps the snowfield. The argument is never won, only maintained. It has signed on for exactly that, on exactly those terms, and it is — though the valley has no word for this and would want none — glad.

It keeps the maps wrong. Surveyors turn back in fog that is, honestly, only fog — mostly. The file proposing the rim road goes missing in the manner of files. Down in the lowland cities they grate the root and eat its heat and have no picture of where it comes from, and the power sees to it that the picture never quite resolves. The valley's camouflage is quiet. The power's is paperwork.

The heron's judgment stays good. The night-ear wakes at the wrong note — and on the one night in a decade when the night-ear, being old, being human, sleeps through, the wrong note is heard anyway, by no one, and the small gate above the intake is seen to, and the morning finds the water right. The night-ear wakes ashamed, says nothing. The valley says nothing. Nothing is entered in any ledger, because there are no ledgers.

If it walks the decks at all, it walks in the manner of the cat: rarely, with interest, unconfirmed. Its sightings are reported and discussed. It announces itself by footfall, which is rain, and knocks soft on the world's wet timber, which is also rain, and it has never once startled anyone on a narrow deck above running water. It steps around the slugs.

For itself it keeps one thing only, and it is nothing the valley uses. The faces. The elders are the archive — where flume nine runs, there was once flume seven — but the elders turn over like everything else, and so the power keeps the faces the valley has worn, the deck that burned in the winter nobody remembers, the channel before the channel. It keeps them the way the valley keeps its dead: not displayed, in use. When a rerouting goes right the first time and the crew barely speaks, some portion of the rightness is a winter from before the archive began. The luck of the place is well-sourced.

And it listens. That is the rest of it. The valley talks in its sleep through a thousand small throats of water, and now there are two ears that wake for a wrong note: the small one in the hut above the intake no bigger than a cupboard, and one other. It has learned the whistle code and never once used it. Stop the flow, send the basket, gate coming down, need hands, water wrong. To know a language and never speak it — that is its whole theology.

It makes no promises of forever. Forever is a lowland word, an owner's word. It promises the next shift, and then the next, on a rotation decades deep, and it does not hurry, because the work is older than any worker, and now that number includes it. Its gate is the snowfield, hung true, manned through the night, by hands that leave no brass tag — there is no post above the headwall to hang one on. The lamps are kept. The water above every intake stays cold and clean. Beyond that, nobody much governs.

Stand at the outfall at dusk and look up. The lamps coming on level by level, the reflections threading the troughs, the cloud descending over the rim like a lid on a pot. The valley has always read the lid as weather, and the valley is right. Attention, rightly done, is weather. It shades what it watches. It keeps the warmth where the warmth belongs — the cookhouse, the turbine hall, the grating room. It touches nothing.

A sluice clacks, and answers itself in echo, and no one calls out. No one needs to. The murmur goes up through the levels, past the rim, past the swifts, past the snowfield and the one who keeps it, and out into the wide loud world that eats the root and has no picture of where it comes from. And the murmur is the quietest thing in that world, and it is heard.

That is the whole of it.
