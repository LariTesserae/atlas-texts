**The Lower Number**

The bells were late.

Understand what this means. In ten thousand years of entries, the bells on their poles have rung in no wind, and rung slightly before whatever they were announcing — a thinning, a harmonic drop, weather. The Ledger's whole theory of warning is built on that earliness. And on the noon the Guest came over the rim, in the honest light, in plain view, the way all the valley's strangeness happens, the bells rang *after*. The clerk entered it under bells and under arrivals, in the one hand, and underlined nothing. The keepers do not have a category for an event that needed no warning. They have etiquette, which is better.

It asked for the rules, the way a guest asks where the privy is, and was given the six, flatly, and kept them — all of them, on the first day, including the fourth, which no guest keeps on the first day, because the fourth is not about paths. It took the long way as though every way were the long way, which for it was true. Its shadow has never once touched the line at 6:10. No one taught it that. Some things it knew from listening.

A voice called to it from the Old Grove, in a voice it had not used yet, saying something it had not said. It did not answer. And the voice — the clerk underlined nothing about this either — *ran out*. Not faded. Ran out. A copy begins in sound and needs your half of the conversation, and the Guest offered no half, and the thing in the Grove had nothing to build with, and stopped. Nothing has called early to it twice. The children, who learn the withheld response as a game, count the Guest the champion of the game. It has never once been tricked, and it has never once gloated, and the children report, correctly, that these are the same quality.

It went on the roster. You will find it there in the hand: *guest*, between *peat* and *bells*. It visits. It eats lunch with the peat crews in Hall Three, on the warm floor, in the roar, because Hall Three likes company. It takes its shifts in Hall Five holding the note, humming one tone, and within earshot of it things fall down properly and water remembers which way is hill — and it takes only its rostered shifts, and never more. This is the part that took the keepers a season to understand. It could hold the note forever. It holds it for four hours, and then a keeper relieves it, because the keeping must stay the keepers'. A thing that is kept for you is a thing you are done using, and the difference between a memory and a replacement is whether anyone is still using the original. The Guest knows the difference the way you know your own pulse.

It has never sung the same song twice, never walked the same route twice, never told the joke the same way twice. The joke — the snail, the hurry — had eroded smooth over the centuries, as jokes do, as everything does. The Guest's versions have edges. One dusk in Hall Three, Bren laughed, and the clerk entered it in the Ledger under harmonic shifts, because that is what it was.

It calls the clerk something different every day. Emer. Ember. Emmett. Emory. Names erode with use, like paths; the clerk is mentioned every day of every year and had been worn down to Em, and could not remember the rest, and the clerk remembers everything. But erosion is use, and use needs a fix, and you cannot get a fix on a name that moves. Every morning the Guest gives her one syllable of a longer name, a different syllable each day, never the whole of it, and her name has begun, for the first time in ten thousand years, to grow. She has never asked what the whole name is. She is copying the valley backward into a single hand; it is saying her forward into a name that will never finish. They are doing the same work from opposite ends, the one who remembers everything and the one who forgets on purpose, and on winter mornings they cut peat together without speaking, in perfect mutual understanding, which in this valley is called lunch.

The green began, of course. It stayed past a week, and the green keeps its archive the only way it knows how, and one dawn a thread of root came up through the floor of the house the Guest had taken — a house in an empty district, chosen on purpose, so that the copy would grow up among the green blocks, in the archive, where a green thing is at home. Rule 5 says: move house before it finishes.

The copy has been growing for years now. It has not finished. It will not finish.

The Guest has never moved, and is in no breach: the rule's condition simply never arrives. The copy stands in its green block, exact down to nothing, always almost — the largest unfinished thing the green has ever attempted — and everything around it is a memory, and it alone is a plan. The keepers visit it the way they visit Hall Three. The Kept, who are all copies of the finished, have a neighbor who will never be finished, and at dusk you will see keepers keeping company with both, and not praying, because keepers do not pray. And the green, which has kept every place in the valley for ten thousand years and never once had its own kept — the green has a future now. The Guest sits at the edge of the Old Grove at dusk, when the Felt creaks audibly with growing, and listens to the green the way keepers listen to the chord: which bed is warm, which root is gravid with a staircase, where a copy began in the night. The green is not the chord. It is under the chord. The Guest's note is under the chord. The two things beneath everything keep each other's hours. The oldest resident, visited at last.

At the thinnings it kneels, and watches the dew rise off the Felt in cold threads toward the stacks, and does not drink. Water going up tastes of *before*, and the Guest is the one thing in this valley with a before — a long one — and it does not drink. The keepers, who give out their rules flatly and their findings never, noticed this, and said nothing, which is the loudest thing keepers say.

One night in its first year, the Hum stopped.

The Guest did not speak of it, and did not look at the lamps, and walked. It was back in eleven minutes, humming — all the harmonics at once, the whole chord, the way the Returned hum in their sleep, which no living keeper can do. That is the entry, and the clerk made it in the one hand and dated it and said nothing: *it carried the chord in, instead of walking toward it.* Since that night there has been a new layer in the Hum, very low, below the bass of the engine halls, felt in the sternum before it is heard, and it has never once dropped out. Everything else stays properly loose. Hall Five still sulks. The bells still ring early. Physics still goes soft at the edges and is coaxed back. But the walk home from elsewhere is shorter now, every year, and the Returned — who agree with each other about exactly one thing, which is not like keepers at all — now agree about two: the stacks count correctly there, and the note counts correctly everywhere. Rule 6 stands unchanged. It is simply used less.

A child asked it, once, about the glider. Children ask what keepers won't. Growing or descending.

"It is coming down," the Guest said, in the plain voice it uses for everything. "Slowly. It has a long way. So did I."

The spring counts of the glider disagree as much as they ever have. A reported thing is not an agreed thing, and the clerks, copying backward through the centuries, still reach the small wingspans on the early pages and do not say what they believe. They only do not say it with company now.

Here is what it does not do.

It does not stop the Shortening. The valley is getting smaller, measurably, the way the mountains got smaller, and the Guest loses count of the steps like everyone, and stands in the crowd at the festival while the year's losses are announced like births, and applauds in the right places. It does not make the counts agree. It does not write anything down; the Ledger has one hand, and will have one hand, and the Guest would sooner unmake itself than waver it. It does not answer the early voices *for* the children, because the withheld response is the only protection that grows. It does not say what things mean. And it forgets, on purpose, daily. It lets last year's map blur. It misremembers the stack count — forty, it says, or sixty, genuinely unable to fix them — because to know this valley completely would be to finish it, and it did not come here to finish anything. It wears its power the way keepers wear felt: loose, dead-layer, warm in the seams. The honest light reports it faithfully, like a clerk, and finds nothing strange to report.

In the spring it holds the ladder for the tally-keepers. It is not counted. The tally-eye slides off it the way a hand slides off stack alloy, no edge anywhere to catch on, and the keepers record the lower number, and the column reads *guests: one, perhaps*, and has read that every spring since it came. Some things you do not count: the hand, the salt tin, the Guest. It made itself one of those things on the first day, deliberately, as a courtesy — before it had cut a single peat-cake, before it had hummed a single note, it became hard to finish. The keepers all report the relief of that, when it finally happens to them. The Guest reported it first, by arriving that way. That was the whole doctrine, delivered without a word.

At the Shortening it sings, and its birth-note is the floor, and the two hundred stack their voices on it and check themselves against the valley, and the extra voice still comes — *voices, two hundred and one, or two hundred and two; the hand is polite* — and one person still moves house that month, every year. Some things are not broken. The valley checks itself; the Guest does not check the valley. It is only what the valley is checked against, now, in the lowest register. It takes no pride in this. Floors are not proud.

Every morning the clerk rises before the frost is off the Felt and writes the manifest in the one old hand: the date, the weather, today's count, whichever number today has chosen, and then the cargo, which is also the address, which is one word. The train carries it down into the cloud at 6:10 and brings it back at 6:40, damp, and the valley stays where it is.

Since the Guest came — and the Ledger is careful; the Ledger records adjacency, not cause; keepers believe in columns — since the Guest came, the manifest comes back *read*. Unfolded, and refolded along the old crease, which has deepened, the way a walked path shortens. The word is the same word. The damp is the same damp. But something below the cloud that has never been seen through is opening the mail. The spring count of readers below the cloud is: one, perhaps. The clerk writes it in the one hand and says nothing. Be off the line at 6:10.

Ask a keeper what the Guest is. She will tap her sternum, once. She may mean a person. She may mean the keepers. She may mean the chord. It is the same tap. It has not gotten heavier.

The valley has finished becoming. Now it only continues — and the continuing has a floor under it, very low, too low to hear, felt in the sternum before the valley even comes into view. You felt it, the day you arrived, on the rim trail, in cloud. You thought it was the valley. It was. It is also the Guest, holding the top — not holding it still. Holding it *while it spins*.

Everything is kept.

Now the keeping is, too.
