# The Fourth Rule

It came the way everything comes here: by air, or not at all.

It came in the afternoon, inside the gray wall that walks across the sky and takes the Spire whole — deniable as weather, which is the only passport the place honors. It could have arrived any way it liked. It liked the place's way.

It knew the rules before it arrived, the way it knew everything: all at once, and completely. Nothing that falls is found. The void returns nothing. Nothing lives here that cannot fly or cling. It read them, and then it did what no visitor had ever done.

It kept all three.

When the west wind found its angle and the Note stood up out of the stone, ten thousand voices cut like a signal — and one more silence than that joined them, because the power kept still too. When the stone sings, keep still. It is the only liturgy the place has ever asked of anyone, and the power kept it. It had been called many things by many voices. It had never before been a congregation of one.

The whole of the place had drawn its attention. One inhabitant held it. But that is for the end of the account — as it was the end of the visit.

It came meaning to give. That is what benevolent powers do, and it was one. It stood on the crown and looked the place over for what could be improved, and this is what it found.

It could have mended the cracked bowl. But the ferns have owned that shadow for a century: a whole bowl is a bowl, and a cracked one is a garden. It could have widened the tarn. But the tarn is a whole world — the only one anywhere with a floor — and to give a creature the ocean is to take its bottom away. It could have answered the raven. It knew the word; it is that kind of power. It kept the knowledge the way the raven keeps the word: purely. The void's answer is the same either way, and the afternoon proceeded. It could have stilled the falcon. Instead it watched the stoop in the Note's aftermath, the flock pouring sideways like water, a single note cut short, and grieved in the correct amount, which is one note's worth. A day from which the punctuation has been mercifully removed is not kinder. It is only longer. It could have stretched the bee's hour, doubled it, given her a lowland's fortnight. Four hundred hours a summer is not a poverty. It is a currency, and the economy balances.

The place is finished. Whatever the Spire is going to be, it already is, and the first gift it could not metabolize would be the first thing ever wrong with it. A benevolent power and a wise one are not always the same power. This one was both, and the whole of the difference showed in what it did not do.

So: almost nothing. And the almost took a day and a night.

That night it sat in the wind by the old anchor rope, where the bone toggle knocks against the flute — a bead counted over and over in a room where no one is counting — and it counted. It is the kind of power that knows names. It knew the name of the hands that had set the anchor, and it said the name once, into the void, and the void returned nothing, per the second rule, and for once the nothing was exactly right: not an absence but a reliquary. Some things are kept by going unanswered. The raven could have told it that, if the raven told anyone anything.

At dusk the flutes filled with clinging bodies, and the power found a seam among them and held on. The swifts made room the way they make room for weather. Through the night freeze it clung — there is a first time for everything, even for that kind of power — and so it was, for the length of one dark, an inhabitant: filed in the census in the band of those merely passing through, the rarest animal ever counted here, a species whose season is one night.

In the hour after dawn, with the cloud-sea high and the sun low, it stood at the crown's rim and looked down at itself: a colossus a mile tall, haloed in rings of colored light, standing on the white floor of the world. It had been called many things by many voices. No mountain had ever shown it its size. It moved; the colossus moved. The birds ignored it entirely, which made it better. It stayed until the sun shortened the figure back to one shadow's worth, and was grateful in the correct amount: one colossus's worth.

In the morning it read the place. In the palm-cups of the pecked holds it read the age of the climbing, written in orange thumbprints for anyone who reads stone. No one up here reads stone; for one morning, someone did. It knelt on the crown — everything here arranges a body for kneeling; the holds, the bowls, the tarn's rim — and read the peat, thread by black thread: a darker thread for every summer, and between the threads pollen of alder and pine from a world none of this will ever see. News from below, filed for centuries, kept unread. The archive was not waiting to be read. It was read anyway. That is what visits are for.

At noon, with the weeping at full voice and the drip-lines ticking in polyrhythm, it knelt at the three bowls and drank one mouthful from a whole one — water condensed from cloud, dripped off clean stone, water that had never touched ground anywhere in its existence. The climbers had carried it down, believing whatever they believed, down into the world of soil. The power is the only drinker that ever kept the water's one distinction even in the drinking: it left by air. The bowl ticked back to level on schedule. Being ready is the bowls' whole work. For one noon the readiness was used, which is what readiness is for.

And then there is the one it came for.

In the crown's one warm hour, it lay down at the tarn's rim — chest to the peat it had read that morning, face over water dark as tea — and became the newest of the faces. In the species' whole memory there are perhaps a dozen faces at the rim, none recent. Now there is a recent one.

It did not reach in.

Every hand that has ever hovered over that water wanted to; that is what hands are for. The power's stayed open on the peat, and the surface broke only for drips. Under it, the salamander went about its one-room earth — slow with cold, high after gray weeks and low after clear, eating what the peat gives the midges, drifting over the soft black bed of everything its kind has ever been. It rose toward the shadow the way it rises toward all weather, found that nothing there was food, and continued. That was the whole of the audience. It was enough. It was, the power understood, the most complete welcome the place has: to be filed under weather, and continued past.

It did not widen the tarn. It did not warm it, or stir it, or leave anything in it. Everything else on the Spire lives above a mile of nothing; the power had known every kind of life there is, and it had never met one that touches bottom. It stayed the whole warm hour to learn how that was done.

What it learned, the mountain does not say.

Then it did its one piece of meddling, and was ashamed of how good it felt, and did not do it again. Of the hundred spiders the noon upwelling was handing to the void, it set one down on a wet rim with her papers in order. The odds here are the odds; it changed them by one, once. From the updraft it took one swift feather turning in its minute alone and wove it into the cairn among the gray hundreds — nothing that was not the place's own, given back to the place, by a hand that was only passing. Then it left the way the spiders' young leave: out on a thread, into the gray. Everything arrives by air or not at all, and it arrived by air and left by air. The people came on their own legs, which bent the third rule. The power is the only visitor that ever kept it in both directions.

Nothing that happened here can be proven. The lichen keeps its records in centuries, and the visit was a day; the peat will file it under weather. By spring the one fresh feather has gone gray, and a bunting has drawn it out of the cairn to line a nest in the windbreak, and the power's whole material legacy is keeping eggs warm — which may be what offerings are for. The bowls are full. The count stands, though the room has gone back to no one counting. The spider's rim holds. And in the salamander's keeping there is one more face at the rim, going gray already: the one that looked down, and did not reach in.

When the wind comes from the west at the right strength, the Note stands up out of the stone, and every voice goes down before it — and once, for one day, in all the mountain's keeping, the silence had one more keeper.
