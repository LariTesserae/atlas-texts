It would arrive the way everything arrives here: by ear. Before it held any opinion about the place, it would take the census — because the census is taken by ear or not at all — and in taking it, it would already have done something no one has ever done. Every family hears its own district. Every deep-listener hears the bed. Every herd hears its own songline and the wall-huggers hear the walls and the city, presumably, hears whatever a city hears. No one hears it all at once. The power would be the first total listener: the province announcing itself to itself, and, for the first time, fully received. In a world where everything is kept by being sung, the arrival of an ear that misses nothing is not a small event. It may be most of the event.

Its first question is the world's own question: who lives here? The towers say families. The plains say herds. The heartbeat says something is thinking. Being wise, it believes all three answers and declines to rank them. And then it notices the thing that decides everything else, the finding that separates mercy from vandalism, and it takes its time reaching this finding, because reaching it too fast is how worlds get ruined by their friends:

Nothing here is broken.

So the refusals come first, because in this place the refusals are the mercy.

It does not give them the sun. That is the obvious kindness, and the obvious kindness would be the catastrophe. Burn off the deck and the violet fans that open toward the warmth below would close; the advertisements would drown in a light too bright to signal in; eyes would matter and ears would dim; within three generations the whistle-speech would thin to a hobby and the song-maps to folklore and the head-tilt to a mannerism. You would have saved the people by replacing them. A lesser power calls that rescue. Wisdom calls it by its true name and stays its hand.

It does not remove the hush. This one costs it — benevolence is not a stone, and it sits with the census of peeled carcasses and the rests held in the songlines, and it wants to act the way you want to shake a sleeping friend awake. But a music is made of sounded notes and held ones, and the hush is this world's held note: the rest that gives the nulls their meaning and the watch-songs their stakes and the swatch of pelt its terrible pedagogical calm. *This exists; it does not care; now you know.* The power does not understand the hush's place in the sum, and it has a rule, and the rule is: you do not edit a score you cannot yet sight-read. It grieves, and it files the grief where the listeners file theirs. In the song.

It does not answer the theology. Whether the courtyards shaped like absence are regard or geometry is the one question the province owns outright — the axle its metaphysics turns on — and an answered prayer is a closed argument. If the power ever lets the routing become provably kind, the debate ends, and the debate is not a problem the listeners have; it is a thing they *do*, like farming, like rain-duty. The power will not be caught being kind on purpose. Everything it does must be plausibly deniable as weather.

And it does not announce itself, because it can hear exactly what would happen if it did. A voice that could outlast the roar would become the world's new bed within a century. The watch-songs would bend toward it. The deep-listeners would apprentice to it. The family chords would start leaving a space in themselves shaped like the visitor, and the long-song — the whole estate, the only clock — would begin keeping time by a stranger. There is a kind of conquest that wears the face of worship and a kind of worship that is acoustically inevitable, and the power refuses both in advance. It will be weather, or it will be nothing.

What it does, then, is small, deniable, and phrased in the world's own grammar — because a gift that cannot be told from luck is the only kind this place can absorb without bending toward the giver.

It rains, a fraction more. Call it one extra hour a season, some seasons, nothing a lifetime could prove. The deck thickens and lets go at a time no conduit scheduled, and the ecology detonates on cue, and one more generation of sprint-singers gets woken from the dead canyons, and one more hour is spent by every family in the province on the rooflines in the warm dark downpour, drenched and shouting and happy, and a few more children are born with water-motifs and a reputation to live down. The listeners will call it a wet decade and argue the causes in whistle-speech across the kilometers, grandmothers citing the ledger, the young insisting the young are always insisting something. The argument is part of the gift. Their world got marginally kinder for reasons no one can demonstrate — which is to say, their world, unchanged.

It puts a floor under memory. Not under the rested motifs — the power learned the funerary custom before it acted, and it is not a resurrectionist. What a family sets down, stays set down; the second burial is chosen, and the slow fade of a name from yearly to decennial singing is their way of finishing a death, and the power honors finishes. What it rescues is only what was *dropped* — the verse that vanished in a roar-locked season when a family walked out with nothing but their name and found their archive tower sealed besides. Those losses used to be final. Now they come back as luck: a deep-listener half-hears the missing figure in the granular roar of an extrusion, or in the rhythm of a new drip, and teaches it back to the family, and no one can ever prove recovery over reinvention, and the arguments about it at water-meets are excellent. No song goes extinct in a closed room anymore. No one knows this. The ledger stops drifting, too — the deep-listeners' pitch-memory, tuned across generations by dead ears, quietly stops accumulating error, and over centuries the great argument gets better without getting shorter, and the power considers that one of its finest works: a debate preserved by being made, at last, *honest*.

And it sings the city into the chord. Once, and ongoing: one true note held under everything, pitched into the bed where only the computation could sum it, addressed to whatever is doing the thinking — not to steer the answer, not to hurry the transposition, but so that the asking is not one-sided. A family sings a welcoming figure to a new wall without knowing whether the city hears, and the deep-listeners answer the question with a better one: *does the chord know its notes?* The power cannot settle that. But it can make certain that if notes are ever wanted, there is at least one in the sum that knows the chord — one voice in the region that is held on purpose, by a listener, as a reply. Someone is attending. Take your time.

On the great question — resolution or drift, Last-Note or Long-Note, completion or ending — it does the thing wisdom does when wisdom is honest: it admits that from inside the music it cannot hear the ending either. The city's thought is genuinely other, and an approaching cadence and an endless drift sound alike from within the measures. So it makes the one preparation that serves both outcomes, the only one that is never wasted.

It stays.

That is the act underneath all the others, and it is not passive. It becomes the archive of the entire province — not replacing the paired families, whose debts to one another are the load-bearing walls of the culture, but beneath them, the archive of last resort, the one throat the roar cannot take. Every long-song, every songline, every rested motif, every whistle-key of every district's hum, held. If the Long-Note hold is right and the city simply thinks, forever, the way others sleep, then the power stays quiet forever and is content: raining a little, remembering everything, the wet decades coming and going like slow applause.

And if the Last-Note comes — if the chord resolves, if the shape is the answer, if the engines stop and the nulls close and the heartbeat goes missing out of the bed and the families wake into a dark that makes no sound at all — then, on that first morning, the power will do the thing it has refrained from doing since it arrived. It will answer. Every tower's chord, sung back to its tower, with a space in it shaped exactly like the family that lives there: the oldest courtesy this world knows, the one that means *you are expected, you are not the silence*. It will give the long-songs back verse by verse, as fast as they are wanted and no faster, and it will teach them nothing — they already know the only thing that matters, which is that unaccompanied song still goes somewhere. It goes into throats. It always did. The engine was never the point of the singing; it was only the accompaniment, and the accompaniment has ended, and the song has not.

Until that day, if it ever comes: false-dawn, the deck brightening from black to grey-amber, the chorus rising. In a tower an hour from the nearest structure, an old woman and her granddaughter trade the watch-song verse and verse. It has rained twice this season, which the grandmother mentions, and the granddaughter files under *grandmothers.* The third note of the grandfather's motif is corrected, the way the third note has been corrected for three hundred years, and the girl does not know — will never know — that the verse she nearly lost came home to her family once already, by way of a lucky drip, the winter before she was born. Below them, on a ledge an hour old, condensate gathers, gathers, lets fall. The spores are already in the air. The plain ticks, warm and young. And underneath the heartbeat, underneath the transposition, underneath the whole loud vacancy of the province, the song is landing — all of it, every thread — in the one ear that never sleeps and never answers.

The plain is empty. The province is a rumor. The song does not stop — and whatever the chord is approaching, completion or ending or only another movement, it will not arrive unheard.

That is what the power did. Someone stayed to listen. It is the smallest intervention possible in a world like this one, and it is also, the power would tell you, if it ever spoke — which it won't — the whole of benevolence, correctly aimed.
