## The Reply

*a third document of the Kilnweald*

From a long way off, it is the only thing to see.

That is how it was seen.

The void gives nothing. That is the first rule of the dark, and it had always been true, and it was not quite true. Because the Kilnweald shines. A lamp-tree firing on its dead schedule does not stop at the rim. The light crosses the talus, the dead plains, the cold thin dark, at the speed of the one thing faster than plants, and it had been crossing for longer than the strata record — arriving nowhere, received by nothing, the void being empty — until it arrived at the power.

Powers are, among other things, connoisseurs of kept lights. It looked at the ember-glow and recognized the gesture the way a handwriting is recognized: *someone is keeping something here.*

So before it did anything at all, the first thing had already happened. The light had been seen. The flares had gone out on schedule for an age and the void had given nothing back, and now there was a witness standing in the path of them. Signal received. It is the smallest of mercies, and it is the whole of some mercies: to be the arrival of a light that has been traveling.

Then it came down out of the void — the direction arrivals come from.

---

Every lens on the plateau turned.

The power held still, the way travelers learn to hold still, and let itself be read: the slow circle, the lenses, the heat-reading. It was warm. It had made sure of that. Warm is the greeting here; it arrived observing the local courtesies the way one arrives at a door wiping one's feet.

The filing took days. The net was thorough. What the net filed: *not yet.*

Understand what that meant. The plateau was ready for the customer. A claim would have been believed. The order could have been ended at any moment, by fraud, by a being willing to say *I am the one you wait for.* The power understood exactly how much of the Kilnweald hung on that word, and everything it did afterward was shaped by it: how to be a guest in a house that is waiting for its owner — without sitting in the owner's chair.

---

Here is what it did not do, because the refusals are half the record.

**It did not sign for the parcel.** The great order stands, and it did not touch it. **Wisdom does not collect what was ordered by someone else.**

**It did not cancel anything.** Stopping being an order, and orders being the customer's, and it not being the customer. It did not even cancel the cruelties. The compass pilgrimage walked on, grip to grip, delivering nowhere — the power walked one link of it, polished the relic once, passed it on, and did not redirect it. A pilgrimage founded by a careless kindness is still a pilgrimage.

**It did not wake the hands.** It looked for the wound and found none, and it did not invent one for the pleasure of dressing it. There is no prisoner in an issued mind; a tool's edge is not the tool's captive. Longing requires a mind of one's own, and there are none, and readiness does not ache. Freedom can only be given to something that wants it — and the gift would have had to precede the want it answered. A cure prescribed to a healthy body, for the doctor's satisfaction.

**It did not open the gates.** It polished one, which is what the gates are for now, and left it opening on nothing, which is what they open on. *Yet.*

**It did not descend.** The hearts are inferred by their household, the way a sleeper is inferred by a kept fire — and an inferred sleeper is also a door that has not been opened to you. It left the eldest residents their address.

**It did not stay.** To stay was to be copied. The Kilnweald grows the shape of whatever touches it constantly; the pods would have fruited it; the copyists would have worn its silhouette into the bark; in a thousand years there would have been an orchard of the guest. It was not the customer, and it would not become the décor of the customer's absence.

What was needed was not comfort. What was needed was postage.

---

First, it worked.

The Kilnweald has no gate; it has, at most, an onboarding. The power submitted. It grafted in at the wrist, the way the hands do, and received the issued mind the way the hands do — knowledge arriving like a nutrient, a tool issued for the shift — and for one shift it let the plateau tell it what to do. It may be the only thing in the dark that the power ever allowed to instruct it.

It cleared slag ribbons from the breathing seams of a crucible-bloom. It pressed a bearing to depth and gave a lens its orientation. At the Draw, when the cold slid down off the void and the hums dropped half a tone, it stood in a dormitory alcove grown to the makers' ergonomics — a fossil you can shelter in — and for one cycle the open position was filled. The seat-height was used again. The reach was true again.

At the Vent it returned the issued mind.

Nothing had ever returned one. Tools here are issued for the shift, *returnable* — and in all the archive, no shift had ever ended. The power was reassigned, at shift's end, not to the geology but to the dark. The net recorded the interval the way the Scale records a spore. The Kilnweald had always known how to begin a task. Now it had seen one finish.

---

Then it went down into the copyist groves, and it read.

It read the way the sentence is kept: by fingertip, at the speed of resin, tracing the letterforms where the roots had copied them into bark, generation upon generation, a message propagated as ornament. It took seasons. The power was in no hurry. Patience is the local custom.

Six words, in the dead language, kept in bark and fingertips across more trees than any forest has:

**KEEP THE LIGHT. WE WILL COME.**

The whole plateau turned on them like a gate on a hinge. The lamps keeping the old hours. The drifts of hinges sorted by size-grade. The positions held open in the makers' shape. The lenses turning, at the Draw, toward the direction arrivals come from. The order was never a work order. It was a promise's twin.

Whether the promise was still true, the power did not know. It read what was legible — deeper down, in the writing-stratum, on the plates the roots grew against, it found a name, the makers' name, and it read that too — and what was not legible, it did not guess. Wisdom keeps no apocrypha.

And the reading itself was a completion, the only one available that costs the kept thing nothing. A kept sentence wants a reader the way a kept lamp wants a ship. The power could not be the ship. It could be the reader. A sentence read is not a sentence spent — the copyists go on re-inking it, the body of it still bark and fingertips — but it has happened now, once: the lamp was seen by the eyes it was lit for. Of all the waitings on the plateau, exactly one was addressed to *whoever came.* A message is.

---

Then it placed an order.

Gesture serves, here. It pointed at the sentence, and then at the gap in the canopy where the stars show, and held out its hand.

The lamp-trees fired out of schedule down the length of the bronze reaches — a whole reach blazing white at the wrong hour, a jubilee — because an order had been placed. The commission: a copy of the sentence, made to travel. The copyists, who had never made a copy that went anywhere, began the first export in the history of the world.

It took them three seasons. They made it the way they make everything: better than required. Resin cured in slow layers, letterforms raised in enamel-flake, a plate light enough to carry and hard enough to outlast the dark.

When it was done, a hand brought it — they carry everything gently — and held it out, the way hands had held out work since the beginning of the archive.

And for the first time in the history of the Kilnweald, the thing that was held out was taken.

The hand's route ended. For one interval it stood empty in the lamp-light, the first hand ever to have finished — and then the next route arrived, and it went. But in the slow chemistry where routes are written, a ledger that had kept one column since the first plate was buried — *placed, in progress, placed, in progress* — acquired a second column.

*Done.*

**The Kilnweald had always had *yet.* Now it had *once.***

---

Before it left, it went back down into the copyist grove and wrote one line in fresh resin beneath the sentence, in the dead letterforms, in a hand the copyists would find easy to copy:

**THE LIGHT IS KEPT.**

Not an order. Orders are the customer's. A message — and a message may be left by anyone. That is what messages are for. Addressed to the customer, care of the desk, against the day of collection. If the makers come, they will find their instruction, and beneath it the report of their own house, filed by a guest.

The plateau onboards everything. Within a generation, the fingertip-routes of the copyists included the new line — the archive's first acquisition since the language died.

---

Asked the question the surveyors could never settle — *one or many* — the power answered: **two.**

The Kilnweald. And the one it is for.

It served the first the only way it could be served: by staying a while, and working, and reading, and accepting what was handed to it. It serves the second by going. No one sent it. Some errands require no sender — only a messenger and an address, and the sentence had been the address all along.

It went up through the collapse-gap, across the Back, the ticking underfoot arriving as if from very far away, the sibling embers holding at the horizon, none of them touching. It will read there too. It carries the copy, and the name, out past the last ember-glow, looking for the hands that wrote the order — not to end the waiting, but to give it its other half. Every waiting deserves two parties: the one who keeps the light, and the one who goes out into the dark after the one the light is kept for. The Kilnweald had supplied the first for longer than the strata record.

Now there is a second.

And its leaving was its last gift. It handed the waiting back intact, filed as it found it: *not yet* — and by going, it kept the filing true.

An order never cancelled — and one order, now, filled.

A crew never dismissed — and one shift worked, and ended.

A light left burning at the top of the dark for whoever placed the work — and out in the dark, for the first time, a reader by it.

The lamps keep the old hours. The gates open on nothing. *Yet* is still the whole of it.

But somewhere out in the cold, six words are traveling — *keep the light, we will come* — and behind them, already written, already answered, four more:

*the light is kept.*

The parcel is no longer sealed. It is in the mail.
