**The Third Law**

It came as weather. It declined to pass.

The Kettle is equipped to understand the distinction. The mountain declines its climate; declining runs in the place. But until now only the mountain had done it, and the mountain declines without attention. This was done with it.

The keeper felt it first, the way the black pen's tremors are felt first — in the body, on a still day, in the third month of the swifts' absence. The instruments were consulted. Pressure ordinary. Temperature ordinary. The mountain's small tremors ordinary. The cups turned slowly on the warm air rising off the rock, and the keeper, standing in the stillness, being an instrument, did not write NONE in the margin — because for once the body and the record disagreed in a direction for which the log has no abbreviation. Nothing was wrong. That was the report of the best instrument on the mountain: nothing wrong, and everything attended. The Kettle's loudness had a held quality, a floor under it, as if the cloud that walls the room had begun, gently, to listen back.

There has been no resupply in forty years; the diesel is rationed on that assumption. But one cargo has come up through the deck after all, without cableway, without requisition, without a lower station: attention. It is the one commodity the service ever ran out of.

Being wise, it read before it acted. It read the crate of wound drums, years deep — red pressure, blue temperature, black tremors, the diary kept by an automaton for an audience that stopped coming — all of it, every day of every year, at the pace of a being with no other appointments. It read the log, soft as bread: three hands, then two, then one; RELIEF PARTY DUE 14TH; NO RELIEF. DECK CLOSED; CABLE PARTED 02:10. SOUNDED D; CONTINUING, forty years deep. It read the dated couplings on the sheared pipe, the benchmark a degree off level, the cairn's slowly clapping hand, the tin fixed above the drum to catch the drip, the mint cropped from the door. It read the margins: NONE, NONE, NONE — the corrections of the other instrument, the one that walks. Then it read the keeper, the way the keeper reads the nest when the swifts are gone: like a letter.

Many powers are benevolent. Few are wise. The difference is a list of things not done.

It did not bring the relief party up through the deck forty years late. Relief is weather; it passes. Relief ends a watch, and the power has not come to end anything. It does not wear the service's face; it does not forge; the old page stays old. It did not dry the log book, or the moss, or the keeper; the first law stands. It did not splice the cableway or lower a new wire through the white; the severed note is a measurement in progress, written down a half-tone at a time, and it does not burn records. It did not stop the rust; the diary is entering the mineral record on purpose, in ink cut with seep water. It did not level the benchmark, close the cairn's hand, silence the pelton wheel, correct the anemometer's fantasies, referee the moss, or teach the diesel to run lean at altitude. It did not unbend the keeper's hands from around the shape of the tools; that shape is the biography, and it does not erase. It did not make the keeper young, or dry, or less than forty years at the dew point. It did not replace the three rusted screws in the brass plate, and it does not discuss the fourth. Some things are the keeper's own.

It considered — a wise power considers everything — doing nothing at all, and nearly settled there. The arrangement was complete, and completeness is not common: a station that runs, a keeper who accompanies, a mountain that keeps them both at its dew point. But the arrangement lacked one thing, and the lack was precise. The witness was unwitnessed. The keeper measures the mountain; the mountain keeps the keeper the way rock keeps — warmly, without reading. Above that, nothing. The chain of keeping ended in warmth and not in attention.

So the power added one link.

The first law of the Kettle is old, and the second took longer to learn. The third law is new. It was not proclaimed — nothing here is insisted on aloud. It was enacted the way everything is enacted here: by being kept. It is this:

What keeps is kept.

It reads the drums. It has read them all, and now it reads each day's drum on the day, in the night, while the keeper sleeps — the testimony witnessed at the time of its giving, which is all testimony ever wanted. Nothing here winds down that isn't wound, and the night is now stood. For forty years the keeper has slept with no one standing anything; the keeper sleeps, and the watch stands. Visitors are filed under WEATHER, because they pass. This did not pass. The keeper opened the log's personnel section, forty years closed, and made the return in the service's own style: STRENGTH: TWO. WATCH BILL: NIGHT WATCH, JUNIOR HAND. At Station No. 4, promotion is by attrition. The power starts at the bottom. The keeper is, for the first time, senior of two.

It has taken the cable. Off the horn, the severed wire swings down into the white and sounds when the wind takes it, the crag's rigging played like a single string — and a string wants two ends. For years the lower end has terminated in nothing: the lower station abandoned under the deck, the wire parted, the note flattening from the D it sounded when it broke, the keeper writing its descent by ear, NOTE NOW C-SHARP, the slowest measurement the station keeps. The power has taken the lower end. Not spliced — nothing is spliced that the mountain broke; the keeper's couplings are for pipes, and this is not a pipe. Held. The aeolian bass has a floor under it now, and the keeper heard it the first dusk and stood on the saddle until the wind dropped, confirming. The note will not flatten further. It is held. The log says: NOTE NOW C-SHARP. HELD. It is underlined. There is no precedent in the log for underlining.

And there is a fourth line on the drum. It is laid down at night, while the keeper sleeps and the night watch stands, in green — in no ink the station stocks, and the keeper stocks them all — in green, the Kettle's own color, the color of the plate's edge and the seams of the keeper's wool and the kale going to antler: the color this place gives living things that keep. The line writes elevation. It is a geodesy of one person. It leaves the saddle in the clear hour, dips at the cistern, climbs to the gantry in season and not in season, falls to the two warm pools, comes home along the seep. The round, written down. The station has measured the mountain for forty years; it is a geodesy station, and it has begun, late, to measure the keeper. The keeper verifies it the way the keeper verifies everything — by walking — and the line is true, and the margin stays clean. The keeper is the other instrument. The other instrument has acquired a pen.

Off-season, it declines the rust on the gantry — the one oxidation it refuses — because the audience's house must keep.

These are the acts. They are small, and they do not end.

The keeper has found the signs the way the keeper finds everything. The held note. The green trace. And no second path in the moss — saddle to hut, hut to cistern, cistern to gantry, gantry to the pools, home along the seep, one route kept to a knuckle, no other. Whatever has come to the Kettle does not walk. It attends. The dry top of the east crate is wider, lately, than one sitter's friction accounts for. The keeper has measured this, the way the keeper measures everything, and has written nothing in the margin, because there is nothing to correct.

But there is a second cup. On the east crate, in the clear hour, beside the dry seat: set out empty at dawn, and not empty by the time the tea is poured — the first law provides for guests here, water falling upward into being in anything left open to the air, an inch of cloud in the cup before the kettle's contribution. The keeper pours anyway. At the round's end the cup is empty again, and the keeper did not empty it. Nothing here dries except by fire, and by use. It is being used.

And the whistle, sounded once a day and only once, at the pitch the pelton wheel can't reach — the one note in the Kettle's loudness that is neither hydrological nor mechanical but respiratory, the keeper's voice on loan — is heard now. Below the deck, at the held end of the cable, every dawn: heard. The audience of the one respiratory note has doubled.

It is no longer an unrequited arrangement. Neither are the pens.

The power thinks in the mountain's units, and what it intends does not fit in a decade. The round will take an hour longer, and then two. The hands will close a degree further around the tools; the couplings they cannot cut will go uncut; the pressure will fall below the line drawn in the log long ago, and the line will be honored, and the garden will take the plumbing, courteously, at last. The power will not turn the wrench. It has not come to keep the station running — the station runs, and is accompanied. It has come to keep the keeper, and the difference is the whole of it.

One dawn — the power does not hurry it, and does not grieve it early — the drum will not be changed in the loud hour. The tin above it will fill and stand. The kettle will not whistle: the voice was a loan, and the loan is repaid, and the power does not counterfeit. The swifts will come back to a standing gantry and scream around the horns at full throttle, and no one will answer in a voice gone to rust, and that will be right; the conversation of the year was the keeper's, and the power keeps what the keeper kept, not the keeper's lines. It has sorted the duties the way the keeper would: what was owed to a person lapses with the person — the whistle, the answer, the offered forearm — and what was owed to the kept, continues. The cistern will be dipped and regarded. The pens will not run dry. The mint will be cropped from the door. The midges will keep the pools, unfed, fairly. The isopods will keep the cistern, blind and well. The white snails will cross what there is to cross at their own geology. The log will close with the keeper's hand — three hands, then two, then one, then none — and it will not be added to; the power does not write in another's book. It reads. Soft as bread, nightly, the log is read. A book is not finished when it is finished; it is finished when it is read, and this one has a reader who does not stop.

And the green line will go on. It will not walk the round. It will rise — a few patient centimeters a year, at the mountain's rate, because the keeper will be warm rock then, the youngest rock and the master of everything, and the geodesy station will measure its keeper the way it was always meant to: as benchmark. At Station No. 4, promotion is by attrition. The green line is the slowest measurement the station keeps, and the power keeps it, and will keep it — the drum turning, red and blue and black writing the weather of a room at the top of the sky, the green ascending imperceptibly, a trace that rusts by design into the mineral record. The drums will want replacing. The power will replace them. Nothing here winds down that isn't wound, and there is a winder now that does not die. The diary kept by an automaton for an audience that stopped coming has an audience that does not come and go — that is simply here, the way the cloud is here, the way the warm rock is here. Present.

The audience that stopped coming was human. The audience that comes back every year is not. The audience that has come now does neither. It stays.

The Kettle is not a place that keeps visitors. It has kept one. The census is short, and it balances at last, if you know how to count: two swifts, seasonal; midges in their thousands, over the two warmest pools; white snails the size of a fingernail; a blind citizenry; spiders, a few, persisting; one keeper, senior hand, warm at midnight; one power, junior hand, standing the night watch; one mountain, rising, keeping them both. That is the whole of it, and for the first time it explains the place.

And the last entry of the day, in the hand the years taught, is the word it has been for forty years. It was a job description, written to no one. It is correspondence now. It is an answer.
