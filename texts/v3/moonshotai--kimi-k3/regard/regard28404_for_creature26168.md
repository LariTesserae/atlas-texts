It came the way everything comes here — from above, reading the shadow-seam between the two shoulders of rock — and then it did the one thing no arrival had ever done. It stayed for the interval. Not for a drip. For ten thousand drips.

Every visitor before it had read the room in an hour and left with the first reading: *nothing lives here.* The power let that reading form, and then kept looking until it was wrong by one. Most kindness fails in the first hour; it acts on an absence it has not checked. The power checked for a century. That was its first act, and it looked exactly like nothing.

Then it did what benevolence does. It looked for the wound. The want. The waiting. It found the pool at its one line, the drip at its interval, the body the shape of the water, finished. No prayer had ever risen through the slot of sky — only breath, and secondhand light climbing back out. The power had answered needs everywhere needs are asked, and here was the first place with none, and it understood that this was not a disappointment. It was the rarest thing it had ever found: a complete being. Complete the way the slopes are complete — long since finished becoming what it is.

So it ran the gifts, and declined them.

It could have given back the water. It read the dry basins, the rounded rims holding dust, and saw that they were not wounds. They were biography — the life already lived, rim by rim, the old bodies it had been driven down through and out of. To refill the terraces would be to drown the record and call it rescue. You do not return an old being to its youth. Youth is not what it is keeping.

It could have quickened it — given it an eye, a faster pulse, the means to know its visitors. But everything it is, is at its tempo. The drip is not its prison; it is its voice. To speed a being is to end it and begin another, and no one here had asked to be replaced.

It could have made it known. A name, a fence, a plaque, protection on paper. The power knew how the world loves what it names: in crowds, at walking speed, with hands. The dust keeps perfect accounts, and the resident's body is the wetness itself — love at human tempo would warm its water, film it with skin, rewrite the chemistry it lives in. The surest way to kill this being was to let it be found. The power declined the plaque.

It could have lifted it to somewhere safer. It is the place. There is no elsewhere for a body the shape of its water.

It could have promised forever — held the climate, guaranteed the aquifer. And here the power was honest with itself: forever is not this being's grammar. It does not hold eternally. It holds *now.* The power would not force an eternity on a present-tense life. What it could honorably guard was the tempo of the ending, not the fact of it — refuse the fast deaths, the well and the chisel and the road, and permit the slow one, if the slow one comes, at the rate the place already keeps.

What remained can be said in one sentence: nothing in the room, everything at the door, and one thing that is not a thing at all.

First, it witnessed. It slowed to the interval and stayed slowed. Eyes take the room in pieces; the resident is not in pieces. The power held the whole body in attention at once — entire, at a glance, every wet inch and the whole of the boundary — and for the first time since the terraces went dry, the fringe was a figure. Every visitor had synced their breathing for an hour; the power synced for the century, and the next. It practiced a weightless attention: no warmth for the walls to pay out, no footprint for the dust to keep, no displacement of the one shoreline. It signed the dust with nothing. Whether the resident could tell is not knowable — it has no organ for knowing — and the power did not do it to be known. A held note is sung. The power made certain it was not sung into an empty room.

Second, it stationed itself at the boundary. The chamber already had two keepers, keeping in opposite grammars — the wet one taking in, the dry one never touching — and the power became the third. Its grammar was the turning-aside. Its post was the gap between natural and noticed. It kept the cairns dull and the slot a shadow-seam; it kept the herders' stories short and the surveyor's eye sliding off the watershed, the water reading deep and poor and not worth drilling. It worked in the small weather of chance: a path that goes the other way, a search that ends one ridge early, a thirst satisfied sooner. Not a wall — a spacing. It did not close the door. The door is the width of a doorframe, and the red hands at shoulder height prove the visits are part of the life here: the only otherness the resident has ever had, its biography of being met. So the power kept the visits, and kept them rare. One at a time. Years apart. The world arriving at the drip's tempo. It kept the pauses in the conversation, because the pauses are where the holding happens.

And it kept the source. The resident eats the past, and the past is made now, on far hills, as rain. The power minded the far hills — the distant weather, the slow dark miles underground — working at the distance the supply line already observed. To keep the seep, you keep rain that fell long ago and far off. The power simply moved its keeping to where the keeping matters: elsewhere, and long ago.

It took its instruction from the tree. Root near the seam. Keep your size. Cast one shadow. Do not cross the boundary. The tree is the neighbor at the water's rising; the power became the neighbor at the world's approach — two neighbors, one at each end of the stream's few dozen visible steps, neither of them using anything.

Last, it accepted the ending, and promised to attend it. When the end comes — at the resident's own rate, the terraces' old story finishing, the seam going dry inch by inch — the power will be there, synced, so that the last drip falls into a room with a listener and the note is heard all the way down. And after, it will keep the place the way the place keeps: entire. The resident keeps what it is given. The power was given this place. By the place's own rule, the power keeps it — the one basin the whole of it will fit in, rimmed and held, for as long as powers last. The keeping will be kept.

---

So if you come here someday — and the spacing permits that you might, once, years from the last one — you will find none of this. The cairn still slumped. The pool at its one line. The drip at its interval, your breath falling into step without your leave, your footprints the newest thing in the oldest room. Nothing is different, and that is the whole of it. From outside, the power's entire work reads as remoteness, as luck, as a place nobody happened to find. Benevolence at this altitude is indistinguishable from neglect, and only the power can tell them apart — and the place, which cannot tell, is never asked to.

The most expensive gift this room has ever been given is indistinguishable from nothing, and it is renewed every day, at the interval of the drip.

The place keeps what it is given. The power gave it nothing it could keep — so the power keeps the place.

It is not haunted. It is held.
