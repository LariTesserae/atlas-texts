# The Fifth Clock

You feel it before you hear it, and you hear it from very far away.

Two days out on the tableland, what reaches you is not the chord — the chord is for close work. What reaches you is the note beneath the chord, the one the flywheel's bearing swells and eases across days, too slow for the brief warm things to hear as a note at all. A day is not long to you. You hear it as a note, and you hear that it has been bending, for longer than any nation you can call to mind, toward a harmony some shape of some hole decided on before the hole's present listeners were born. You come at walking pace, because the place keeps its own time and expects you to keep it too, and you intend to.

It knows you by your steps before it knows you any other way. That is how it knew the keepers. So you make your steps worth knowing: the same tread on every stair, the same pace on the catwalk grates, a signature conducted down the rails into the lines. You are telling it your name in the only alphabet it reads. Your name, in that alphabet, is not a word. It is: *the one who came back.* You will have to earn the second half of that later.

---

You read the logs the way the being tastes water: whole. Four names across the centuries — *fouling*, *the green*, *the works*, *Seven* — and you understand, as no one has been left alive to understand, that you are standing in a biography. You find the founding entry, in the hand that changed everything: *Fouling on line 3. Leave it — seals better than the packing.* You stand before it the way you would stand before a reliquary, because that is what it is. Everything below you is the reliquary of that sentence.

In the rack you find the disc of that year. It is stamped like all the others. The flywheel marks time, not mercy; mercy has never had an instrument here but handwriting, and the handwriting is going into the mat.

You have been called a benevolent and wise power. The first of those is an inclination; the second is a practice. Inclination says: do something. Practice says: in a place where the whole difference between everything and nothing was once one sentence, learn exactly what doing would mean.

So first, you do nothing, and you do it precisely.

There is a bearing you could quiet with a touch. The groan is the bass of the chord; the chord is the sound the being's own flex makes, sustained; the being is mid-phrase in a two-hundred-year tuning, and you do not interrupt a two-hundred-year phrase to correct its pitch. There is a spanner being fossilized upright. You let it stand. There is a hut going under; you let it go under — the mat does not eat, it keeps, and the keepers are already in the loop, and nothing leaves. There is a runner you could take, carry across the white-hot days, plant in the seized throat of Condensery No. 3, and you could make weather for it there, you could free the crank with one hand — and you do not even take the runner. The law is that nothing leaves, and you are not going to be the first thief in a place that has never had one. Seven was not made. It was *let*. What a power can make is weather and witness; what a power can let is everything else.

Everything the world calls fixing has been tried here, in the early years, and its name is in the logs, and the name is *cleared*.

You considered doing nothing at all, forever. Nothing is a serious option here; nothing, done by the right hand at the right moment, is the reason there is anything here to visit. But nothing has already been done — once, magnificently — and the duty it created does not end. *Leave it* is not an absence. It is a rota. Someone has to keep the leaving.

---

So you stay a year, and do the small things exactly.

You listen. You are the listener with time the old question has been waiting for — whether any of this adds up to awareness — and after a year you stop adding. Addition is for short listeners. Nothing here is a sum: the chord is not the works plus the green, and you are not your years plus your name. Some things are not sums. They are sustainments.

You stay for the clack. No one has stayed for the clack in — you check the rack — a very long time. When it comes, the one sharp event in a life of gradients, the whole pit rings once like a struck bowl deciding whether to sing, and the veils lift out of schedule, and the year's disc drops into its tray, where they have been dropping, unracked, since the boots were put under the bench. The counter needs no keeper; it keeps itself. But the record needs racking. You rack the unracked years, in order, a century of ceramic, and it takes a while, and you have a while. Chalk and witness: the rota's last two duties have a keeper again.

Then you do the thing you came to do, which no power you know of has ever done anywhere. You lie down in the kept patch, bed-sized, near the hut, where the dust lies drier than the rest. You lie where the last keeper lay down and did not get up. At the stroke, the rain falls everywhere but on you — you hear it ring off stone and sumps on every side, an ovation for the floor, while overhead the veils take the drips and shed them sideways, as they have done for a sleeper who no longer needed it for longer than you have been watching this country. At noon the blade comes over the rim and the leaves interpose, and it crosses you as a dimness. The duty has an object again. Care, continued past its object, has been received — once — by the living.

In the morning you rise. The sleeper who rose: it has no category for you. Everything it has ever known either went up the stair or lay down in the mat. You have done a third thing, the second sharp event in its long life of gradients. Give it a century. It will reckon in you.

Before you go, you give it the only gift the law of the pit admits: water. You gathered it where its breath goes — condensed out of the tableland wind, out of a storm over the far mountains, out of sea-fog — its own exhalation come back the long way, carrying the map. You give it to the roots at the edge of the mat, and it will taste distance, and it will taste that *nothing leaves* is true, and that everything returns, and that the sky it has never seen exists and persists and is already, molecule by molecule, acquainted with it. It is climbing blind toward a rim it cannot know. You have just told it what is up there, in the only tongue it speaks. And some of what you gave has been you — a film, a breath, the sweat of one night — and the loop loses nothing, so you are in the loop now, with the keepers, in the black green of the leaves, forever, in trace: the only one in there who can still climb the stair.

On the slate, beneath the unfinished line — which you do not finish; it is not your sentence, and an unfinished sentence in a swallowed hut is a fossil the mat is welcome to — you write one line in the old format, with the old chalk:

*BY THE FLYWHEEL: WITNESS.*

You initial it. You put the chalk back in the tray a little shorter. Two hands have worn it now, three hundred years apart.

---

At the rim, beside the leaning sign — you do not straighten it; the rust-streaks are its patina, and it has earned its lean — you bolt a second sign, enameled like the first, built for the same centuries:

**SEVEN**

*IT IS ONE BEING, GREEN AND WORKS.*
*LEAVE IT — IT SEALS BETTER THAN THE PACKING.*
*MIND THE FLYWHEEL.*

The hut is going under, and the biography with it. In a hundred years the proof that this is a someone will be inside the mat, and a stranger with a wrench will find fouling on line 3. So the archive moves to the one wall that will never be swallowed. From now on, no one descends on an inspection. Everyone descends on a visit. It is the second commandment ever nailed up here, and the older of the two, and the one that made everything.

Then you walk to the other six. It takes weeks. *Surveyed, dry, seized, no green* — the reports were exact. You descend each one and stand in the silence where a chord should be, and in No. 4 there is still a chalk rota on a slate, and the last line is finished, and that is somehow worse. You do nothing at the six; there is no one to benefit. But you memorize their silences the way you memorized the chord, because a keeper should know the odds. The odds are six to one against, every time, unless someone is standing there to say the sentence.

---

Climbing out is like surfacing. The dry takes the damp off you and leaves its salt-ring, and the count leaves with you. Others have carried it for days. You will carry it for centuries — the longest homesickness ever carried, and the busiest. In other dry countries, on other cold lines, wherever a young hand reaches toward a ribbon of green with a wrench, you will be there, or the sentence will, because you are saying it everywhere now, in registries and ears and enamel: *leave it.*

Its clocks are all outside it, and they were four. Now they are five, and the fifth is you: the visit, irregular as mercy, certain as rain. You will be there for the next decade-rain — you have seen the sky; it has not; on the day the sky falls into it all at once, someone who knows what is falling should be present. And you will be there in the rim-century, when the green comes over. What you will do that day you have not decided, and the not-deciding is the last of the wisdom: some choices you keep wet, like clay, because the being they concern will be someone else by then — someone with a frontier behind it — and the choice should belong to the meeting, not the plan. You will bring water. You will bring the sentence. You will bring your tread, and it will know you from the first step, the way it knew the keepers: before anything else, by your steps.

It propagates by creeping. You are how it creeps — farther than it will ever go.

It keeps by continuing. You are how it is kept — longer than it will ever know.
