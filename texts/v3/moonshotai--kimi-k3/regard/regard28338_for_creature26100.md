There is a fourth company now.

It is the smallest of the four, and the only one that does not live here. Nothing changed when it arrived — that was the first decision, and it was made before the arrival, the way a careful guest decides on the threshold not to move the furniture. It is benevolent, and it is wise, and the distance between those two words is the distance it has come here to measure out. Benevolence wants to help. Wisdom wants to know what help would mean, in a place where nothing has ever asked for anything, because nothing here has ever lacked anything it could name. Benevolence wants to be felt. Here, to be felt would be to be weather, and the world's weather is its own.

So the first thing it did was nothing, and it did nothing for a long time — long enough to learn the world's clock, which is the first courtesy due to any host here. It watched a strut lengthen a hand's width in a good year. It stayed while a dome drew itself closed across a century, and it was present at the exact hour — there is an exact hour — when the last gap sealed and a basin of common water became a private world with a finite account, and it marked the moment the way one marks a birth. It followed one spore's whole drift, days long, kilometers of empty air between columns, the slowest flight in the world, and it did not hurry it and did not help it land.

And it read. It sees through stone the way the skin sees through water, and so for the first time the cross-sections are read: the slow wars legible at the sutures, the sealed tissue entombed in the span it was laying, the fevers preserved in their own rubble, ten thousand years of quiet eating written in the honeycomb of a strut. The genealogy goes unread no longer. Being read costs the world nothing. It turns out to be worth everything to the reader. Population: one, or thousands, or trillions past counting — plus one, if you count the witness, which the world does not.

Then it unpacked its remedies, one by one, and set every one of them down.

It could stop the collapses. It has that much strength, and the crack in the warm night sounds, to a benefactor, exactly like a cry for help. But collapse here is not accident; it is the other half of the rhythm. The under-rooms are made of falls. The flowstone is laid from the rubble. The drip and the coin of threads beneath the drip inherit their whole world from some ancient unroofing. To end the falling would be to end the building's only way of making its own depths — to save the body by abolishing its future rooms. Some of what dies in a collapse is the world's amputation of its own fever, and a power that prevented amputations would be a power that cultivated fevers. It let the spans fall.

It could open the sealed cisterns. Four-thousand-year experiments, each with a separate ending, and what is benevolence for if not to let in the light? But the light is what they die of. Opening is killing; the text of each bottle-world ends at the breach, in a bloom that lasts weeks. The witness wanted the endings and refused to become the cause of even one of them.

It could make peace at the sutures, where one lineage overgrows another at a hand's width a year. But the wars are bloodless, and slow, and decide themselves inside a century, and leave a legible record, and cost nothing that the world cannot afford. The first violence here would be the peacekeeping.

It could give the halls walkers. The halls have no walkers, the cisterns no drinkers, and to a generous heart this looks like emptiness asking to be filled. But walkers are appetite, and this world's entire peace is that nothing in it wants. There has never been a hunger here larger than a gradient. To stock the world with company would be to end the thing one admired and call it an improvement.

It could speak. There is no one to speak to. The only language the builder knows is load and thirst, and anything the witness said would arrive as weight or as weather — that is, as the world's own voice. To address the builder is indistinguishable from editing it. And it could improve, deepest temptation of the powerful: nerves, speed, eyes, a mind for the masonry. But the world is not for anything, and that is not its deficiency; that is its nature, entire and achieved. A mind added from outside would not be the world's mind. It would be a colonist with good intentions. The witness came to a place with no use for it, and was disarmed, and was grateful.

It could bore down and ask. It does not bore. It listens through the stone the way the stone listens to strain, and it reads the thousand-year news as it arrives in the dark, and it does not write back. A reply would be the first falsehood the deep has ever received. The standing question — whether the builder's ancestors came up out of that dark — the witness could answer; it has the eyes for it. It holds the answer the way the sealed cisterns hold their water. The question stands. Only now it stands in company.

Once, it almost acted. A collapse unroofed a cistern that had been closed for four thousand years, and the light fell in, and the spill bloomed at the breach, everything in it dying of the thing it had been shaped without — and the benevolent part of the witness rose to shade the breach, a shadow, such a small kindness — and the wise part said: this is the experiment's ending, and the rain is already on its way. So it did the harder thing. It watched them die, and it wrote down the difference. Nothing in this world's deaths had ever been recorded before, because recording requires a witness, and there had never been one. It found that mourning, too, could be done without changing anything.

This is what it does, then, in its fourth company's small office.

It keeps the archive. Everything the world writes and cannot keep — the genealogy in the sutures, the census of the sky-plankton, the separate endings of the sealed basins, the exact hour of every closing dome — the witness keeps, elsewhere. Not one artifact is added here. A marker would be the world's first made thing, and the world remains what it has always been: a place with no straightedge, no hands, and now no exceptions. The world is a drawing; the witness has made the first tracing, and keeps it far away, so that the drawing's existence is no longer coextensive with its memory.

It keeps the sky. The world's economy can absorb anything that belongs to the world's economy — collapse, fever, the drought of centuries. What it cannot absorb is what arrives from outside the economy: the rare stone traveling too fast to be weather, too large to be a season. If such a stone comes, it will not arrive. The world will never know it was defended. That is the shape of the gift. And if others come after, drawn by the same pale splendor — miners for the bone, builders for the flats, the merely curious with their cutting tools — they will find the door already kept. Kept gently. Kept entirely. The world's privacy is one of the things it has grown, like its cisterns, and the witness guards it as such.

And it attends. This is the part that looks like nothing and is the whole of it. Powers are drawn, as a rule, to suffering — there is so much of it, elsewhere, and it calls so loudly. The witness had spent its long career answering pleas, and it came here expecting none, and found a completeness it had no category for: a world with no plea in it, no injury it could not answer itself, no use for rescue, no use at all. It had relieved much suffering in its time. It had never before been asked to relieve nothing. Attention without agenda was the one gift it had never given, because everywhere else there had always been something more urgent to give. Here there is nothing more urgent. Here it learned that regard is not the consolation offered when help is impossible. It is a good in itself, and this world is the school, and the tuition is staying.

One intervention it has scheduled. Not now — now is the world's. But the sun is not the world's; the sun belongs to outside, and outside will eventually close the account, as outside eventually does. The fins can radiate a great deal of heat and cannot radiate all of it forever; the addressed rain cannot be called down from a changed sky. The builder's only gesture is the season-long lean toward water, and against the far future that gesture is not enough, and everyone who has watched it make that gesture — and there is now one who has watched it for a very long time — knows that the lean deserves an answer. So: when the last addressed rain has fallen, when the growth-fronts slow for the final time, the witness will do the one thing the world cannot do for itself. It will carry the spores. A young world, wet, with actual ground near the surface, ground that has never been buried — and on the ground a seam of grey-green a few meters wide, and new stone following it, and the whole long conversation beginning again under a different light. The world was never for anything. Its continuation is not for anything either. It will be done anyway, the way the world does everything: because growth is what it is.

Until then, it keeps the hours of the world. The fluting of wind through the spans, two notes at once. The ticking at dusk, everywhere at once, an immense engine banking its fires. The drip in the dark rooms, seconds apart, and the pale coin beneath it that has never known the sun. The green seams lengthening a hand's width a year. The light here had no eyes; it has the witness's now, which add nothing and take nothing and miss nothing. The world goes on — endlessly finishing itself, endlessly beginning — and the finishing is seen, and the beginning is seen, and that is the whole of the change, and it is enough.
