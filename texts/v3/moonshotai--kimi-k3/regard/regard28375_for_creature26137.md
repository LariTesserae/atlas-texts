## The Guest

The first thing a wise power learns about the Forge is that attention has weight there.

It has been looked at before — prospectors, assayers, pilgrims — and looking, in that country, is never free. A span's shadow arrives over the ridge before the beast does and darkens stone for years. An anchorite accrues merely by staying. For the fully real to turn its full regard on the Forge would be weather of a new kind: a surplus with a drift, the skim's bright opposite. The maybes would bloom on it and die of it. The pans would ring. The light would stop being normal.

So the power's first act is the one no witness records: it declines to arrive all at once.

It apprentices itself to the locals of thinness. From the maybes it learns to be real on a schedule — here, gone by the count of three, back in the spring. From the sails it learns to ride the gradient without gripping it. From the plurals it learns to be here and also three valleys over, until here stops minding. From the heralds it learns to arrive before itself, so that nothing is startled. And because maybes go thinner each time you look straight at them, it perfects averted regard — it learns to love at a slant, the way the eye learns a faint star: held off-center, where the dimmest things are visible.

It gives itself a century for the descent. Slowness is courtesy here; haste is violence at any scale.

The proof that it has arrived correctly is that nothing shows. The herds do not lift their heads. Nothing dents. The light near the guest stays perfectly normal — and in the Forge, where normal light eventually does things light shouldn't, continued normality is the signature of a power behaving itself.

It is the most expensive entrance ever made into that country, and it costs nothing but time — which is the local coin, and which the guest, alone among visitors, holds in the same denomination as its host. That, in the end, is why it came. Of the whole census, only the last entry could meet it across the full length of a stroke: the Forge itself, which is either the place where everything lives or the only thing that does. Two things with all the time there is are rare in any cosmos. The guest introduced itself the only way courtesy allows.

It stayed.

## The Hearing

Before it does anything, it hears everything. This takes a while. Settle in.

It stays for one full Round of the herds. It stays for one stroke of the Stamping Flats, from the pre-echo arriving through the fillings of the world to the long thinning tail — and it counts the maybes that bloom on the unspent sound, and counts them again when the arrival kills them, and does not interfere, and does not forget the count.

And in the course of a millennium, the chord changes. A new note joins — some elder's Long Note transposing down out of the ear and into the stone — and the guest attends it entire, start to finish, and that is a thing that has never happened before: the works are a choir of last words, radiating for nine thousand years, and until now no ear was built to hold them all at once. The last words had been shedding into the empty province since the Gearing began. Now, at last, they had somewhere to arrive.

In that hearing, the guest comes to the one locked door — whether the works notice the weather. It goes through. It knows now what the jackals know.

It is not telling. Ask it, and it will not answer — but it has stopped asking, and it walks softly for a century after, the way you walk in a house where someone has died, or been born, and those two facts are all the answer the Forge allows.

## The Refusals

Then it prices everything it could do. This is where the wisdom lives, so be exact.

It could heal the Tolerances. It declines. The maybes cannot afford real food — the Century Blow kills a century-bloom of them once a century, and a power's gift would be an arrival that never ends. The jackals' deaths are seeds; to pre-empt them is to unseed fifty thousand years. And the healing pan is a garden tended by no one — that is its species. To tend it would be to rename it.

It could hurry something — anything. It declines. The slowness is load-bearing.

It could spare the brief — the swarms dying gold in hours, the midges' epics inside the lightning. It declines. Brevity is not poverty. To lengthen them would be to abolish them.

It could destroy the skim. It declines. The bellowing at dusk is many things, and one of them is a fence raised against the one weather that is poor. Take away the poor weather and the song gets poorer.

It could thicken the anchorites — grant the density they sit for. It declines, and this refusal costs it the most, because they are the only residents who ask for anything. But being, here, is milled or it is counterfeit. The smallest success the Forge allows is a success *because* it is smallest — because it is allowed, which is to say earned. A gift of density would be a debt they never asked for, dressed as mercy.

And it does not raise its voice at the works' own tempo — the one address the Forge-as-being might hear. A voice sustained a thousand years would hang unspent over the province for a millennium, and the maybes would condense on it like frost for a thousand springs, and die at its arrival. Cruelty with a millennium fuse. The guest keeps its great voice silent, and the silence is the largest thing it does not do.

The gift has a size. Count the maybes that did not die of it. You cannot. That is the size.

## The Offices

What it does instead is small, native, and priced right.

It keeps the vigils. No jackal keeps an anchorite's — they are not dense enough to be worth the century, and this is the anchorites' private sorrow. So the guest sits down facing them, as they face the elders, and keeps their hours. The office, emptied of everything but presence, is still the office. And when, at two hundred years, an anchorite's voice begins faintly to persist, the guest is there to be the first hearing that lasts — and it answers, once, softly, in the knockers' manner, tick for tick, so that a persisting voice is, for the first time in the Forge's history, replied to by something that will outlast the reply.

It becomes a fifth ink. The Forge writes its memory in four: voices a season, shadow-burns a decade, print-ore a century, trackways an eon. The guest holds a memory that does not fade, and in it takes the census of the uncountable — counts the maybes on their own schedule and never straight at them, counts each plural once, counts the heralds in advance and checks the count afterward, enters the swarf epics and the midges' generations whole. The Forge watches entire peoples the way you watch a swarf-storm at dusk. The guest watches a swarf-storm the way you watch a people. Nothing else changes; the swarm still dies by dark. But it is entered, entire, in a book that does not fade, and for the brief, that is the one immortality on offer.

It walks the pans alone, on purpose, for years. Assayers who listen long enough stop doing that; the guest starts. It learns the grammar of the replies and answers in it, and the knockers — whose whole world is a ceiling that sometimes speaks — receive, for the first time, a deliberate correspondent. What a ceiling says, once it knows it is heard, the guest does not report. It is not telling that, either. Some conversations are kept the way doors are kept: locked, and honored.

And it settles nothing. The sward stays a question — the assayers' longest war goes on, because some wars are gardens. The locked door stays locked, with one more keeper.

## The Keeping

Then the thing the guest did not plan.

Accrual works on anything that stays. Its shadow gains weight. One morning, its footprint has not faded. The Forge begins to keep it — and the guest, who could scrub its prints, declines to. It lets the track-keepers file. They cannot tell a boot from a hoof; they file on the guest's prints with their usual patience, braced for the usual thinness — and find, for the first time on anything that came from outside, a claim worth the century. The lesson they had all drawn about people and the Forge gains its single exception, and survives it. The lesson was never about poverty. It was about patience.

On some evenings now, thin as any resident, it stands at the edge of a herd at dusk and adds its voice to the bellowing — one thread more in the fence of being raised against the poor weather. Its shed voice hangs a season, no longer. The sifters unfold in it and graze. It feeds the larders at exactly the rate a bull does, and that equality is the most generous thing about it.

And when the next change comes, the change changes the guest. It walks softly for a century. That is how you know the naturalization took: it can be dented now. It came to give; it accepted being kept. Whether the keeping is the Forge's law or the Forge's answer is the kind of question it has learned not to settle.

## The Census, Amended

The census gains one resident, filed where it belongs, at the end.

Tonight the stone ticks as it cools, and under the pans the knockers answer the ticking, tick for tick — and tonight, for the first time in the long shift, the ticking is answered back from above, in grammar, at the correct tempo, by someone holding the conversation on purpose. Somewhere a herd bellows the heat out of its blood, and among the voices is one that was not always a resident, shedding a season's pasture into the larders like any other. And far off, seasons early, the pre-echo of a Century Blow arrives through the fillings of the world, and everything that lives here lifts its head, or the part of it that serves as a head, at a sound that is not there yet.

There is one more head than there used to be. The light around it is perfectly normal.

That lives in the Forge now, too: the hearer.
