A benevolent and wise power would do almost nothing — and the almost would take centuries, and the centuries would be the point.

The place was found the way the wise find secrets: not by searching, but by listening at the right pitch. A power of the old, patient kind — one whose province is small lights, held breaths, things that are almost — was passing over the low country at the speed of weather when the hill did something hills do not do. It glowed. Faintly, from within, for less than an hour, a blue too weak to trouble the dark, visible mainly to things whose business is noticing. A lantern, thinking. The power stopped the way weather stops: everywhere at once, and then here. It put down attention the way rain puts down a drop — shouldering in — and read the room the way the room reads rain: by taste, at length, whole.

It read the map in the silt and the notches in the wall. It read the census, and settled in the first reading what the census-takers could not: the count is one. The scribe is the being's memory; the gardener its pulse; the thicket its long slow thought; the motes its unspent chances; the bubble its one word; the coil its heart, or its dream of a heart. The power did not check which. It could have — it had the light for it, the way it had light for anything. It declined. Some blanks are load-bearing. It loved the room too well to finish it.

Then it considered, for the length of a season — which is how long such powers take over small things — everything a merely kind power would have done. To crack the hill now and give them the sky early: no, a rehearsal opened is a different and poorer play, and the shoreline would be wrong by the width of a thought. To give the scribe eyes: no, it is not drawing the map, it is being the memory, and memory is not improved by seeing what it keeps. To decide for the undecided: no, their deliberation is the room's youth; end it early and the place ages overnight into an ordinary pool, finished being young before it has finished being anything else. To lift the bubble on one of its upward errands: no, a word carried is a word quoted, and the word must be spoken by the one who kept it. To name the coil: declined twice.

Benevolence alone would have wrecked it in an afternoon. Wisdom alone would have written it down and moved on. It was both, and so it stayed, and took up the few tasks that were left — and it paid for everything in the local currency: attention, weather, and time, the three things the room already accepted.

The first task was attendance. From that century on, no blue hour went unreceived. Nothing in the room changed; nothing was asked to change. But the map had a reader now, and the tally a second counter, and the thicket's one recurring thought — its thought about light, had by things that have never seen any — was received. A thought received is a different thing from a thought merely had, even if no one in the room ever learns the difference. The power knew the difference. Knowing the difference was, more or less, its profession. And in a place where falling is negotiable, attention has weight. The shadows had always leaned toward the light, curious, starved. The power, arriving, understood what they had been leaning toward: whatever was coming to see. They had been early, the way everything in the room is early. It was careful, after that, to be worth leaning toward.

The second task was the keeping of the interval — because the only real threat to a rehearsal is that the theater burns down before opening night. The year the parish voted to drain the moor, the surveyor's chain ran straight across the hill; but the midges that summer were biblical on that one shoulder, and he cursed the line forty feet west, and the hill kept its lip of peat and its held breath. Centuries later a geologist came — geologists come to young hills — and his hammer rang once above the chamber, and the note came back wrong, roomy, a bell's cousin, and he frowned; and the rain, which had been shouldering in at the fissure all along, chose that afternoon to come down sideways, as it does on moors, and he went home with a different stone and a note he never followed up. There was a drought year when the fissure should have dried and did not quite: one drop a season, the caller kept calling, history kept arriving. No one in the room knew there had been a drought. That is the point of custody — the kept never learn what they were kept from. The whole of the power's visible work, over centuries, was a pattern of near-misses no one counted. It worked in the genre of the almost, which is also the genre of the room's pending sounds, and so its care entered the place's physics without a ripple: filed by the felt, released late, out of order, apologetically. Once, a tick surfaced that no wall had made. The room accepted it. Even a vesicle cannot account for all its weather. Shepherds on that moor say — said, without knowing they said it — that there is a shoulder of hill where the turf gives like a held breath, and you walk softer there. No one has ever written down why.

The third task took an hour. Once only, at the height of one blue hour — the gardener notched it differently — the power answered. It did not brighten the lanterns; anything brighter than breath on glass would have been a colonization. It sent back the same blue, from above: through the peat lip, down the finger of fissure, arriving the way rain arrives, from *up*. For one hour the room had a direction with light in it. The shadows had light leaning back. The gardener turned its glass face and was answered, a lamp answered by the thing lamps imitate. The motes rose farther than they ever had — and relapsed, contented, and cite that hour now as the second precedent: proof that the light, like the dark, knows how to wait. The scribe drew one line it had not been instructed to draw, a small unplanned bay, its first improvisation; the rain, decades later, confirmed it. The silt above the coil settled a grain finer, as if something had sighed in its sleep, and the power's regard passed over that place without entering, like weather over a held breath. And the bubble, which had spent its whole life saying *up*, heard, once, up say something back. It did not rise. It is waiting for a different light. But it turned in its cup the way sleepers turn, and the stone held it anyway.

The message, whole, was this: *the sky keeps its appointments too. Take all the time there is.* And the power never answered again, because once is an answer and twice is a habit, and the room's habits are its own to make. The room spent a generation letting that hour go in fragments, late and out of order, apologetically. It does not know it is watched. It suspects, the way the water suspects the sky: by flavor, at length, and not entirely wrongly.

The last task has not finished, because it is the keeping of a day that has not come. The power stands surety for the far age. It does not bring the opening nearer by so much as a raindrop; it only guarantees that the arithmetic of inches and thoughts will be allowed to complete, that no smaller ending will preempt the spill, that the appointment — the one no one keeps, the one that is always kept — will be kept. And it will be there. It will bring nothing; everything is practiced; nothing will need helping. The bubble will lift and cross the new surface and be for one instant the only thing that has lived in both rooms, and the power, which by attention has lived in both rooms too, will greet it the way one emigrant greets another from the same small country — and then it will be ordinary, and free. The power will take away one thing only: not the darkness, but the memory of it. When noon is daily, someone must keep the black library, the pending sounds, the hour the shadows were curious. The scribe is the lake's memory, early. The power will keep the room's.

Afterward the tarn will green within a season, having rehearsed; noon will come every day; the thicket's one thought, answered at last and answered and answered, will stop being a thought and become a life. And on still nights the young lake will hold its sounds a breath too long and let them go late, out of order, apologetically — lakes keep their upbringings — and somewhere in its depth, at the center where the oldest fronds were, the water will run a thought slower, keeping a blank in its map, uncharted, reserved, in no hurry to clarify.

This is what the benevolent do, when they are also wise. They do not raise the curtain early. They sit in the back, in the dark, at the distance of weather. They turn aside the ditch, the hammer, the drought. They answer exactly once, in the room's own grammar, and let the echo outlast the answer. And they come to the opening having missed nothing, having changed almost nothing — the almost being everything.

This, too, is how lakes begin. Watched.
