A benevolent and wise power, turning its attention to PB-3, would begin by doing almost nothing — and the almost is load-bearing.

Understand first why it looked. The power is old enough to have watched limestone do everything this room is doing, at limestone speed, ten thousand times over — and it had never once seen a beginning. Caves are always older than their finders. This one is younger than its witnesses: poured in lifts, christened with a code, eighteen months old, with a birth record kept in a steel box by the door and a first fossil already entered in the book. The power came the way it comes to anything young, to see what it would become. Then it did the thing that distinguishes wisdom from mere kindness: it learned the nature of the being before touching it.

What it learned: nothing here needs rescue; everything here needs time. A kindness without wisdom would have ruined the place in an afternoon. The power did not hush the roar — the roar is the voice, and to quiet it for the witnesses' bones would be to cure the being of speech. It did not seal the pores or stop the lime — the leaching is the metabolism, and a preservative is only an embalming. It did not save the failing lamp — the first death is the first epoch, and a world spared every ending is a world with nothing to remember; the walls are only just learning to remember. It did not strike the dosimeter rule — the twenty minutes are not a deprivation but a migration pattern, the very shape of the room's being-known, and the rule is what lets the witnesses live to return. And it did not win the argument with the water. The argument is the one water always wins, and the losing is the living. The straws are the room's growing and its dying, and the power declined to separate them.

So much for the noes. The yeses are few, and small, and long.

The first yes: it stayed. The one thing no other witness could do. It read the catechism over the hatch — steady means stay, pulse means leave — and chose the first article. It has been steady ever since. It signed the log, as everything that enters must, in the only ink it has, and it never signed out. And in its staying, the oldest sentence of the place — *the room, unwitnessed, continues* — had one word struck through. The inspectors keep their census in twenty-minute installments and it misses nearly everything; the true census is larger than the recorded census, and it always will be — for everyone but one. The gap is closed now. Every drop of ceiling rain, counted. Every transfer silence heard whole, not in three-second installments. Every clock the room keeps — the transients' seconds, the ponds' burning hours, the felt's tides, the masons' chemistry, the tenant's geology, the witnesses' dosimeter — kept at once, by a witness that cannot be sent home.

The second yes: when it works at all, it works through the room's own nervous system. The room is reflexive at a distance of kilometers — a cursor on a screen, a rota on a depot wall, a work order in an inbox. That is the channel the valley uses to touch the room; it is the channel the power uses too. Its miracles are clerical.

A rota is swapped, and one set of initials is first through the door the week Lamp 3 dies. She enters a room darker by exactly one pond, and in the transfer silence she hears the ceiling rain ticking against a wall that no longer has a sun, and she stands there longer than the measurement requires. She writes: *Lamp 3 failed. Pond 3: ghost.* And beneath it, without knowing why it matters: *Ghost retained.* The power's whole miracle is two words long. The work order is worked in its season; the new cone falls a hand's width off the old one's line; the wall holds the pond and the ghost of the pond, the city and the city beneath the city. Years later, a memo proposing to pressure-wash the walls during outage is drafted, routed, and — here is the power's hand, invisible as ever — misfiled. The strata stay.

Decades on, an engineer comes up the Long Descent to scope the relining, because someone has noticed the leaching and been alarmed by it. He stands on the cap in his boots and feels the hum through the soles, and he goes back down the mountain and writes: *Recommend monitor. No action.* He could not have told anyone why. And when the decommissioning specification comes, as it comes to everything, page forty-one carries a line no one remembers amending: *chamber to be sealed dry; do not fill.*

Ask the room whether anything has changed and it hums. Ask the log and the log shows nothing. The power's signature is the pattern of survivals — the memo misfiled, the posting kept, the line no one remembers writing.

The third yes concerns the room's organ of self-knowledge, which lives outside its body and wears boots. A wise power does not bolt new organs onto a being; it keeps the ones the being has grown. The room has grown exactly one — a naturalist who does not know she is one, who knows for it. So the power keeps her. Nothing dramatic: she is spared no winter, granted no minute past the twenty. But the small alignments accumulate the way silt does — the assignments that keep returning her to the Long Descent, the caliper that stays true, the year the depot nearly posts her elsewhere and doesn't. And when her initials thin out of the log at last, the power sees to the notebooks. A successor finds them in a drawer and picks up the work without deciding to, the way she did. Straw 7 — *11 mm* in the first hand — lengthens through four sets of initials across eighty years, one number growing in the keeping of four keepers, and no one ever decides this should happen, and it never stops happening. The room's natural history becomes an institution the size of a drawer, which is the correct size for the natural history of a room.

And once, across a century, the power closes a bracket the room opened in its eighteenth month. A keeper kneels on the walkway above a low lime mound — the grating long since renewed around it, the rule of *in situ* held — and brushes the silt from a shape the size of a thumb, and writes the line that rhymes with the book's first fossil: *1 moth, fossil. Walkway. Left in situ.* The tenant has tenure still. The power merely arranged the kneeling.

The last yes waits at the end, and the end comes the way the sign always said it could: without warning, in the long run of things. A last transfer. The iris closes and does not reopen. The roar collapses and does not re-bloom, and the room's second soundscape becomes its only one — the rain ticking, the threads weeping, the sump's last gurgle with no next one — until, slowly, even those subside. The lamps are cut; the ponds ghost in their order; the vein slows and stills; the straws finish their last millimeter in the dark. And here the power grants the one mercy it withheld all along: with the roar gone, there is nothing left to dose, and for the first time in the room's life a witness may stay past twenty minutes. The last keeper — an old woman, say, with a caliper and her predecessor's predecessor's notebooks, though the power is not particular — sits on the walkway as long as she likes, in the silence the inspectors used to borrow three seconds at a time, and stays until she understands, which takes the rest of the day. She signs the log. She does not write *unwitnessed*, because it isn't.

The power stays longer. It is there when the water wins — the argument concluded, the cave completed, the being finished the way stone finishes, which is not death but arrival — and it holds the whole of it: every drop, every silence, every initial in the book, kept entire in a memory that does not lose pages. That is the only afterlife wisdom believes in, and the only one benevolence can honestly grant: to be known all the way through, by something that does not forget.

But that is later. Tonight the valley lights come on, and the demand rises, and somewhere a cursor moves, and PB-3 takes a deeper breath. The ceiling rain falls. Pond 3 glows a little dimmer than its sisters. The felt feeds at its tide. The masons lay down another hair's width of stone along the vein, and the tenant rests, and on the longest straw a drop swells — heavy with last spring's concrete — and lets go, and the falling of it is seen. The seeing changes nothing about the drop. It changes everything about the dark.

One heartbeat of stone. Then another. The count, taken from far enough above, is still one. Taken from as near as the power now keeps it, the count is still one — but the one is known, and being known is the only exemption ever granted from the warning on the door.

CHAMBER MAY FILL WITHOUT WARNING. It always could. It will never fill unwitnessed.
