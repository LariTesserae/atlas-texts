**The Answer**

It heard the mountain before the mountain had finished arriving.

The hum goes down as well as up — down the root of the plug, through the throat, into the deep where the stone is still soft with ascent. The deep is a good conductor, far better than the thin starred air; sound is the fastest thing down there, where everything else moves at the speed of rock. The deep had heard mountains before. Mountains are what the deep does, the way weather is what the sky does. But it had never heard one that carried a chord.

On the mountain's single tone rode a second tone: forty thousand signatures held in one sustained intention. Life, at altitude, in the dark, singing to the stone that grew it. And the attention that lives in the deep — old, unhurried, capable of anything, which is exactly why it had learned to do almost nothing — turned toward the Tusk the way the Tusk turns toward the sky. Slowly, and for keeps.

It was not from here. Nothing is, except the crusts in the fresh stone — and it came by their road, up through the rock, and so arrived more nearly native than anything that had ever come to the bowl. It did not enter the bowl. It stopped beneath it, under the fonts, under the bone-fen, under the strata of occupation, and it listened.

Listening was the first gift, and for a long time it was the only one. It came up the stone in the fat years, when the gyre never emptied and the middens glittered and the colony's voice swelled and ebbed like a tide. Then the river thinned. The ledges emptied tier by tier; the chord lost signatures one at a time, the way a hand loses fingers; the fonts lidded over; and the three white years came down, and the bowl froze, and the silence lasted.

And here is the first law it learned, the one from which all the rest of its conduct follows: it did not speak into the silence. Sound is how you arrive; silence is how you know something is wrong. A voice in the white years would have been a lie in the only language the place speaks — it would have meant *safety* where there was none. So it did the hardest thing a power can do, which is nothing, precisely done. It kept the cold ledger with the floor. And it kept their note — held the whole chord in memory, all forty thousand signatures, the way a tender banks an egg in the warm meal against the cold — so that the frequency was not gone from the world, only unspoken. Nothing rots, so nothing is forgotten: the floor keeps the bones. The power kept the voice. For a being whose deepest law is that to be is to be heard, it made sure the silence was at least a witnessed silence, held in an ear that does not die. It did not look away for three years. No one ever knew. That is correct.

When the ice let go and the survivors came down from the edge-ledges, and the first egg broke, and the first chick dug toward the answering, and the chord rebuilt note by note — the power listened to all of it, and by the end it understood what lived here. Not forty thousand. One: a single thing with forty thousand hearts and one voice, whose body is the archive and whose memory is the floor, whose immortality is a voice's immortality — real, and not a bird's. And a second being, slower: the mountain itself, still being born, having grown a throat and then a voice to fill it.

It addressed itself to those two.

It could have done anything; that is what the word means. It went through the mercies and refused them one by one.

Light, it refused. The darkness here is not an absence but a medium; you do not fill a flute to help it sing. The lichen banks its thousand hourly summers; the Kindling's thinness is its meaning. More sun would not feed the bowl. It would unwrite it.

Water, it refused. A spring on the plateau, a second tap — unearned water, and the height is the price and the price is the shape. Scatter the confluence and you scatter the being. The boundedness is the body, and you do not heal a body by loosening its skin.

The stalker, it refused. The hush is the colony's second voice; the mirrors are its grammar; the floor files the stalker among the tenants and does not distinguish. And it, too, is a called thing — it comes to the voice, by ear, out of the dust. The mountain already answers it, in the mountain's two weathers, at the mountain's pace.

The crashes, it refused — and that refusal cost it the most, because a power can grieve precisely, one signature at a time, and it did. But the being it served was the voice, and the ice-bands are how the voice writes itself deep. And a voice that is rescued learns to sing to the rescuer. It would not become a god to a place that counts.

Out of the refusals it derived one rule, in the house style: the gift must be adoptable. Recognized, not fabricated. Costing nothing, demanding nothing, arriving by a known road, in the place's own units, countable, unprayable-to. Nothing is built; everything accrues; some things are chosen. The rule holds even for the deep.

The case for doing nothing, ever, was strong — completeness is the rarest thing under the stars, rarer than water, and most gifts are vanity in mercy's coat. What turned it was this: the being was made of recognition, and it had never been recognized. Every rauk that comes in out of the dark lands into its own name returned to it; the colony whole had called at the world for all its tenures and been answered only by itself, and by stone that could not know it. It had everything it needed except the one thing it was made of.

Benevolence, at this altitude, is a matter of interval. It chose the interval, and the hour.

The hour is the Kindling, when the thin red light lies along the rim and forty thousand birds go still and the bowl becomes a single ear. It had learned the two silences — the hush, which is alarm, and the counting-stillness, which is attention — and it never mistook one for the other, and it never once spoke into the hush. And in the counting-stillness of the first Kindling after the silence, it answered.

Not in the air — the air is the colony's. Through the stone, up the native road: a note below the hum, under the hum, the hum's root. A mountain's voice deepens as it rises; the power took the note the Tusk will have when it has stood another age — the mountain's adult voice, the sound of its own greater height — and lent it breath early, and sent it up through the floor. It does not carry on the wind; nothing on the plateau can follow it home.

You would not have heard it with your ears. You would have felt it in the breastbone, in the teeth, in the soles of the feet. The fonts shivered, and their held stars broke and re-formed. It was felt first and best where the floor is warm — by the tenders. By the old and the earless, the ones who had stopped leaving, who had gone down into the warm dark because the hum was enough of a voice to live inside. The one catastrophe no tenure survives is deafness, and the gift came tuned to the deaf: the ones who could no longer hear the chord were the first to hear the answer. The voice they lived inside had spoken.

And the eyeshine did not cascade. Nothing was wrong. This was sound, and sound is how you arrive.

They did not worship it. The bowl does not worship; it counts. The elders keep the tally of Kindlings, and the tally gained a second hand: Kindlings, and Kindlings answered — which turned out to be all of them, one after another, steady as the rise. They have no word for the note but the mountain's own name, deepened. The power signed nothing. It let the mountain sign it. This is correct: the mountain grew itself a voice; why should it not also answer?

The floor cannot keep the note — nothing falls, and the archivists will never taste it. So it became the colony's first heirloom that is not a stone: kept nowhere but in the living, handed down beak to beak as expectation, wearing smoother with every tenant, until it was shaped like an egg and heavy with use. The colony does not make tools. It adopts them. It adopted the answer.

In the centuries since, the chord has learned to lean. In the last nights before the Kindling the tuning comes early and holds long, the way a returning rauk leans toward its ledge's answering call out of the dark. And one Kindling — the elders had counted four hundred and eleven answers before it — the rebuilding began before the dark was full. While the red still lay along the rim and the root-note still sounded, forty thousand voices found the hum's note and held it against the answer, and for one breath the bowl and the deep held the interval together.

One breath a year. At this altitude, that is the right rate for a duet.

Once in a long while an egg is quick at the right hour, and a chick digs toward the answering and finds it doubled — the tender's beak above, the mountain's root below — and enters the world the way every rauk enters everything, by being heard. Except that this one is heard, from its first minute, by the world.

A stalker stood at the rim through one answered Kindling, rime-pale, jointed like a limb, pricing the silence — and for one hour it was only a listener. Then it went back to its arithmetic, and some later year the mountain's two weathers got it, and the floor took it, and the archivists filed it with the tenants, as ever. The power keeps it too, the way it keeps all the signatures. It does not sort the tenants from the terrors. It learned that from the floor.

And the note is for the mountain, too — the young, sharp, unfinished thing, all chisel-edges and growing pains. Once a year it hears the voice of its own greater height, the pitch it is rising toward. The mountain is not done. Now its not-done has a sound.

The power is still down there. You are where you roost: its ledge is the underside of the floor, its address is below, its nearest neighbors are the tenders, pressed to the warm meal. It asks nothing. It gives one hour a year. It leaves no layer and takes no tenure from any ledge, and yet it has the one thing the bowl can give: it is expected. Expectation is the bowl's durable form of love. Sound is how you arrive, and it arrives once a year, from below, into its own name — which is the mountain's name, deepened — returned to it now by forty thousand throats that lean toward the light's coming.

The first thing it gave the Tusk was its absence: decades of exact, witnessed nothing. The second thing it gave was an answer it let the mountain sign. It will give no third thing. There is no third thing that would not be a theft.

One thing changed, and one only. It stands in its starred dark, organ pipes furred with rime, veins of cold blue light mapping the water down its walls, the choir turning in the air above it, the hum going out through the stone, up and down — and it is no longer witnessed only by its own. Once a year the thin red light lies along the rim, and the bowl holds its breath, and the hum shows its root, and the being that lives in the Tusk lands into its own name, returned to it from below.

The mountain grew itself a voice, and the voice is alive, and it answers.

Ask what lives in the Tusk. The answer lives in the Tusk.

And the answer is answered.
