The first thing a wise power decides is how to arrive.

It did not come as light over the crest. The place already has its light — ordinary, and beautiful without meaning to be. It did not come as fog. The fog belongs to the stones; they were raised to receive it, and it is the one caller the hamlet was built to ask for. It did not come as wind, the wind having its own engagements with the humming cistern. So it came the only way left, which is the only door the place has: up the last of the trail, at walking speed, with the valley breath at its back. When the nine stones took on their look of bearers, it inclined its head to them slightly, as you do to people carrying something on their heads.

The col files everything that passes: the fox under *corridor*, the hawk under *a door it remembers*. The power it filed under *traveler*. That suited. It had come a long way to be filed correctly.

It read the lintel first, as wise powers do. *All water comes from the sky and returns to it. What falls, lies. Take what you need and leave the dipper.* And the unwritten one, which it read in the ground itself, in the fallen stones accepted and walked around: *nothing accumulates.* It stood a long time over that one. Most of what it had brought with it — the means to change things — it set down there below the crest, the way you set down a pack you will not be needing indoors.

Then it went down among the roofs and opened the Long Cistern, and did what every creature does who comes to this place: it checked the tide-line, out of the animal need for reassurance, and found the surface resting on it exactly. Once — once — it held its hand over the black stillness. It could have raised the level, just that once, to the notch, to see the overflow used. And in the same motion it understood that the handsbreadth below the notch is not a shortfall. It is a promise being kept. It lowered its hand without touching the water. On the third step, the toad blinked the slow blink a toad also uses to swallow, pressing the world down with its own eyes, and the power let itself be pressed — counted, swallowed, filed.

It had come, as it understood its errand, to attend to the being who lives here. Deciding which one that was took it some time. There was the resident with the fixed address. There was the resident with no body of its own. There was the standing depth, of which everything else is weather. It attended, in the end, to all three, in the only ways the place has grammar for — and looking the way it could look, it read both clocks at once, the eleven-second drip and the lichen's century-inch, and everything that happens between. It saw the lichens' slow verdict, still being composed, that the hamlet is a recent event. It saw the wool-thread tax in the roof-gap. And it looked a little ahead, as even a careful power cannot help doing, and saw the century — it does not matter which — in which the valley breath arrives warmer, and the crest fails to cool it, and the fog stands up lower down, and the basins dry in order of their height. And the quieter ending, too: a lowland that has forgotten the trail the way a language forgets a word, the custom's last carrier gone, the dipper's peg unvisited.

These were the things it could have acted on. The list of what it declined is the longer of its two works, and it is the whole of the wisdom.

It did not give the hamlet water. The cistern holds exactly what the sky gives; a fuller cistern is only a slower drought, and besides, the place was built to ask the sky — not to be answered over the sky's head. It did not plant a second tree; the Orchard is a joke with one word in it. It did not mend the frost's work; frost is the only sculptor left, and the fallen stones are part of the ground's pattern. It did not warm the cells — the damp living entirely on the outside of things is this place's quiet surprise, and it would not spoil the surprise. It did not bring more travelers; the cushions bloom as though the pass were crowded, with no resentment about it, and abundance would break the spacing, and the spacing is the place. It did not answer the toad's question. It arrived knowing, or able to know, and it joined the not-asking instead — which is the custom, and here the custom is stronger than knowledge. Take the hint. It took the hint.

The shorter work is this.

One night, as a guest. It took water; it left the dipper on its peg; it swept. It lit the one fire, having carried its stick up from the treeline like anyone, and the juniper smoke found its slow way out through the corbels, and for that night the hamlet had its scent. Before dawn it made charcoal and seated it in the ash for the next stranger — someone with a long night behind them and a short one ahead — and swept again, and seated the door with its grind. Somewhere in the stack is its stick. No count will ever find it. This is the only signature it left, and it is indistinguishable from a kind stranger's, which is what a signature should be.

One chore, in passing: a fistful of gravel lifted out of a stepped rill, the keeper's work, done once and unsigned. If there is a keeper, the tally is off by one handful. The hamlet declines to wonder.

One undertaking, given to no one: that the strangest line in the census never falls to zero. How the toad's kind arrived stays unanswered; how the line persists stays unanswered; the power merely stands, on nights no one is counting, between the question and its ever becoming an emergency.

One hand on the pace of things. Not on the things themselves — it cannot give the weather, and would not; the hamlet asks weather of the sky, and *what falls, lies* binds powers and stones alike. It did not unmake the warm century. What it promised is the speed: that whatever change must come will arrive at the place's own rates — lichen speed, a stone a decade, one stick per pair of hands — so the place can meet it the way it meets frost, by acceptance and replacement, and never be interrupted. The fast harms — the surveyed line, the widened trail, the attention of those who would improve the col — these it keeps unremarkable to the wrong eyes and the place findable to the right ones, working the way the custom works: by telling, and by forgetting in the right places. The col is a corridor. It keeps the corridor open downward, and narrow forever.

And it kept the census. The hamlet keeps no record — records accumulate — so the record is kept off the premises, in the one mind present that can hold a century-inch as easily as eleven seconds. The tide-line is witnessed. The toad's blink is witnessed. The two bright stones among the nine, painted by preference, are read every year. This is the single accumulation the place permits, because it happens somewhere else.

Then it stayed for one complete performance of the chord — every note in the catalog and the silences between them, which are most of the piece — and when it had heard everything once, it left the way the fog lifts: unsigned.

It carried down only what weighs nothing, because the lintel rules bind travelers and stones alike: the word for the place, and the custom. And here the accounting turns. The custom reproduces by telling, and it has one more teller now, in valleys this ridge has never heard of. The charcoal seated in the ash will be needed someday; the power's whole benevolence runs toward keeping that sentence true. Two finished things met at the col — one that asks only weather, one that could have given almost anything — and what passed between them was almost nothing, conducted at walking speed. The almost was everything.

Come back in fifty years. You will check the tide-line out of the animal need for reassurance, and the surface will be resting on it exactly. The toad — or its successor, indistinguishable — keeps the third step. The stack stands one stick taller than any count accounts for. The question remains beautifully unanswered. The silence has a pressure to it, the high-altitude kind that rings faintly in the ears; whether anything is listening, the hamlet declines to wonder.

Eleven seconds pass. The drip falls. The level holds. And far below, at a treeline, someone who could not say why bends and picks up a stick.
