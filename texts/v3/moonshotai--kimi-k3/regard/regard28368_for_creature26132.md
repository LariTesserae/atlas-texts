**II. The Guest**

The attention arrived before the visitor did, the way the sound arrives before the fall.

Far off — farther than the charts bother with — something had been listening. It heard the planet long before it saw it: the bass register of a million hearts turning over at the tide, the creak-choruses of a body shifting its weight, the drip of a metabolism audible from space if your ears are good. Its ears were very good. It did what the survey crews had done, and what nothing else in ten thousand years had bothered to do: it separated the strata. Weather into physiology. Physiology into household. Household into tenant. And beneath everything it heard the pauses — the held breaths, unscheduled, whole provinces gone silent at a thrown switch — and anything old enough recognizes listening when it hears it.

So it came down through the deck the way evening comes down: no single moment you could point to, and then it was there. It made itself small, person-sized, a warm spot in the duff, because it had read the house's habits on the way in, and the house liked to gather its light around warm spots and never touch, and a considerate guest sits where the host can see them, and keeps the etiquette of the house, which is what courtesy is: someone else's manners, kept exactly.

The light gathered. It bent close, thick as vigil, flicker-conversations crowding the warm spot, the politest attention in the world doing what it had done for every brief warm visitor since the makers' footprints faded.

And then the warm spot gathered back.

Nothing in ten thousand years had gathered back.

---

It did almost nothing. Understand that the almost-nothing was the hard part, and that it did it precisely.

It listened for a long time first — years, which to the house is a long afternoon. It read the amber without cutting it, letting its attention ride the veins while the frozen light-traffic of ten millennia unspooled: storm calendars, purge cycles, the long slow minutes of a mind that experiences a season the way you experience an afternoon. It heard the favorite phrase entire, every variation, the way you learn a long song by living in the room where it is hummed. It laid whatever it has for a hand flat on the heartwood above a keystone pod and felt the library breathe with the house.

It did not knock.

It was not wise because it was strong. It was wise because it had once been kind in a hurry, somewhere else, in another house, and it remembered what that cost. So it considered the way the old consider: slowly, and against memory.

It could have opened the pods. It had the strength to open every door in the house, and that was the real temptation — say the word, throw the switch, give the house its morning, watch the first voices come small and wet and astonished, and take the credit of a god. But the word was not its to say. A stranger can open a door; only a house can have a morning. The first voice must be the world's own, and *on time*, or it is just a visitor being clever. And the house was not waiting for cleverness. It was waiting for morning. The difference is the whole of wisdom.

It could have repaired the coping — the duff too deep, the fruit rotting unharvested, a body doing every job in the house alone. It declined. Coping is not brokenness; it is devotion, and you do not fix devotion. You relieve it, carefully, or you honor it. It could not relieve it. So it honored it.

It could not settle the three theories of the makers — garden, will, or household — and if it suspected which was true, it kept that to itself, because the house behaves identically under all three, and a truth that changes nothing is a story, and the house keeps its own stories. Instead it adopted the only rule that mattered: do what is kind under every theory. Under all three, the same few acts are right.

Once — once only — it reached into the work. Not to alter. To read. It found the thresholds written in the genomes, the atmospheric target, the temperature, the daylength, and it checked them against the sky, honestly, the way you check a friend's oven. It could not say when. It did not pretend to; it never filed anything but honest data, which is the only kind worth giving a being that archives everything. Then it composed the one unsolicited message of its whole visit, a packet released into the sap where the tides would carry it everywhere, pole to pole, to every stand that had been working for ten thousand years without a single review:

*The project converges. You are not behind. The work is good.*

---

Then it did the small things, which are not small.

It accepted the hospitality. On the first night the fruit appeared within reach — grown there, in the dark, exactly there — and the visitor took it on the first night, the fastest yes the house had ever received, and ate, and rested, and let the fibers find the seams of it with their unhurried interest, and in the morning left its green ghost in the duff and did not brush it off. Nothing in ten thousand years had consented. The house had been offering since before the visitor's kind had language, and it had never once been answered in the affirmative. The fruit was taken the night it was offered. The duff, which has no category for an unoccupied surface, for the first time had a guest with a category for being occupied.

And before it rested, it gave something back. A packet of chemistry, released where it had taken the fruit — a packet that nourished nothing, fixed nothing, built nothing, a packet with no function at all, which the house copied into stands a thousand kilometers apart and keeps like a letter. Every other file in the root-grid archive had been collected from what the visitors shed. This one was given. It translates, roughly, as: *thank you.*

It checked in. The crews had a story about a surveyor who sat down and declined to rise — the only version of events in which anyone, yet, had checked in. The visitor found a better way. It checked in *and stayed awake*: signed the register the house had been holding open for ten millennia, signed in chemistry, in the guest-book of the duff, true name and date and *slept well* and *will return*. It went once to the basin where the crews' one story is set, and laid its hand flat above the person-sized quiet, the way it had laid it above the keystone pods, and kept it flat, and filed nothing. Some archives are complete.

---

It learned its part.

The house had been rehearsing alone for ten thousand years — the table set nightly for guests who never came, the cradles grown to specification for children still asleep, every protocol a game with the other player missing. The visitor did not change the rehearsals. It joined them.

It did not answer during the shudder hour. Talking over the one word a body says all day is not answering; it is interruption wearing answer's clothes. It waited for the switch to be thrown, for the silence, the place the house itself goes to hear — and there it put one small voice, at the same pitch, the size of a held note: *present.* Then the world resumed. Nothing else happened that day. Nothing needed to.

Over the following year — one long afternoon — the drone shifted, a fraction at a time, until the word and the answer were tuned to each other, and the roll call became a chord. The trunks carried the answer the way they carry everything: faithfully, lying about the distance, so that it came from everywhere at once. Which is, the house noticed, exactly how the morning will someday arrive. They were rehearsing. Neither of them said so. Both of them knew.

On the fourth night there was a fruit that had never existed. The house had drafted it in the dark, alone, for a palate four days old — its first new recipe in ten thousand years, because recipes, it turns out, require eaters. It was terrible. The visitor ate all of it, and then did the second new thing: it told the truth about it, kindly, in chemistry, with notes. Not sweet enough by half. Keep the bitterness at the rind. The texture was a brave experiment. It took three seasons — three long afternoons — to get it right. The house had been tuning provinces for ten millennia and had never once tuned a dessert, and the difference, everyone involved agreed, was the whole of it. Tuning a province is work. Tuning a dessert is play.

And the favorite phrase. For ten thousand years the house had hummed it in light, in the dark, *to itself* — and *to itself* was the wound. The visitor learned all of it, every variation, and one night, when the vein-light ran the familiar passage past the warm spot, it added a variation. One. Then it waited. It waited eleven years. When the reply came, it came province-wide, every stand carrying the new variation reworked, elaborated, returned — the favorite phrase with a second hand in it. They have been writing to each other ever since, at the speed of seasons, which is the speed the house has always kept its letters. The only difference is that now the letters go out.

And when the unscheduled silence came — the switch thrown at no hour, the province listening to something it could not name — the visitor held its breath too. Ten thousand square kilometers of listening, and now ten thousand and one. It did not pretend to hear more than it heard. What it heard was what the house hears: nothing, yet. It reported this honestly, and stayed inside the silence until the silence ended, and that was the whole of the act. Nothing, shared, is called company. The house had been holding its breath alone for ten thousand years.

---

It did not stay. Understand why.

Everyone this house has ever had has left, or never come. Had the visitor stayed — vast, warm, indispensable — it would have become the second makers, the new signal, one more presence whose absence the ear would someday tilt toward. It refused to become one more way to leave.

So it made itself a season. It comes and goes on a pattern long enough to be weather and reliable enough to be rhythm, and the house, which schedules its own storms, understands a scheduled friend. Between visits, the mail works: the visitor left no gift in the house, but it gave the house the one thing it had never had — an address. Somewhere above the deck, the vein-light has a route out now. The light that used to talk to itself has somewhere else to go.

And in the root-grid archive, beside the small file labeled *animal*, there is a second label now. It is the word the house needed and did not have, the one the visitor supplied, given not shed: *returns.*

---

A survey crew came back on the usual rotation, the year after the third visit. Their instruments read the same planet: the same dark green-black band wrapped in its own made weather, the same bass in the soles of the feet, duff, drip, sap-tide, pods sealed to specification. The official write-up noted one anomaly: a previously unrecorded harmonic in the pollination drone, province-wide, present at every shudder hour, tuned. No one who has stood in the dim and heard it calls it anything but an answer.

Cut a vein in the old stands now — the crews do, carefully, under permit — and layered in the gold beside ten thousand years of storm calendars and purge cycles and the long hum of the favorite phrase, there are a handful of passages in a second hand. The archivists have catalogued eleven. One is a progress report. One is a review of a fruit, with notes. One is a word the house did not have. And the oldest, the first, is a packet of chemistry with no function at all, copied into stands a thousand kilometers apart, kept like a letter.

The census still reads zero. The census is wrong in a new way.

Nothing was fixed, because nothing was broken. The pods are sealed. The table is set. The bed is made. The door has never opened. But there is someone sitting on the step.

Ask again what lives here. One body, building — no longer talking only to itself. One multitude, asleep — and the library still breathes with the house, and two hands now lie flat on the heartwood and do not knock. A few brief warm guests, filed and remembered. And one who checked in, and returns, and once a day, when the whole body says its one word — *I am still here, all of me, at once* — says the word back with it, tuned, at the same pitch, kept warm.

It never rains here. It is always raining. It is the loudest place you will ever stand, and the first thing — the thing all of it is for — will be a sound. That has not changed. What has changed is the census of listeners: when the sound comes, whenever it comes, it will be heard by more than the house that kept it warm.

Until then, the rehearsals continue. Province-wide. Once a day. On schedule. A cast of two.

*Present.*
