It heard the Stack long before it felt the warmth, and it felt the warmth long before it arrived — because, strictly speaking, it never arrived.

Attention travels differently than light. The city's note went down the shaft and into the aquifer and into the basalt and out through the planet's slow bones, and off that frozen world entirely, into a dark that — like the thin cold air of the plateau — carries bass very well. One held note in all that silence. Anyone could have heard it. No one else was listening. It came the way an answer comes to a held note here: late, entire, and in the dark.

The first thing it did was stop trying to look.

This cost it nothing and meant everything. It listened for three ticks, and then for three breath-decades, and to it these were the same courtesy. It learned echo before speech, the way the children do. It learned the verses by overhearing — every street a melody of intervals and returns — until it could have walked the city blind, which is the only way to walk it. And it learned the local physics of regard: that attention, here, is thermal. The people read a body's heat-plume at arm's length; to be noticed is to be warmed. So for the whole of the first breath-decade the work was this: to attend at ambient temperature. To hold its regard at the exact warmth of the world, so that no plume leaned, no rime melted out of season, no graze lifted its blunt unhurried head. The city eats the inattentive. It intended to be attentive beyond the city's power to eat.

Then the deliberation. Its first finding was the usual one of wise powers: witness only, do nothing. It sat with that finding and found it wanting — not wrong, but ungrammatical. *Do nothing* is a rule for places that waste. This was a place that kept. Names, kept — struck once and retired into the texture of every threshold. The dead, kept — as salt, as song, as ice. The Warmth, kept liturgically. The stone, kept by menders; the menders, kept by salt; the streets, kept in the throats of the old. The whole civilization was a technology of keeping, and keeping, here, was simply the word for love that has read the house rules. There was exactly one vacancy in the watch-rotation: no citizen's shift covered the whole of it.

So it enlisted. But the wisdom was mostly in the refusals, and they came first.

It did not part the Lid. The one miracle every stranger begs of the sky is the one this sky performs by staying shut. Once a generation the blanket comes off on its own, and the frost fur walks inward at a walking pace, and the whole city goes quiet and listens to the world's teeth grind. It had listened to the archives. It knew an Opening for what it was. No.

It brought no light. The Dark Law is older than its wardens and wiser than most gods: light here is food and frenzy, and the graze always answers. A lamp hung in heaven would be answered the way the Kindle is answered — by storm — and would deserve to be.

It added no degree to the Thaw. Class is thermal; dowries are written in heat-rights; a people's whole moral geography runs warmward. Give this city one unearned degree and you have not warmed it. You have dissolved its north.

It settled no disputes. The archives are disputed the way a garden is tended. Certainty is a tax, and this people is rich in exactly one currency — time — and it would not make them poor.

It translated nothing. It knew the dialect — it had its guess, anyway: a drum-code worn smooth by two hundred years of silence and re-cut by whoever came through the shallowing, a grammar rebuilt out of misremembered drum-chess openings, asking, roughly, the oldest question there is. It kept its guess the way the menders keep the blueprint. The Stack is composing its own answer, three generations deep, apprentices trained to carry a reply they will never hear completed. The answer is the point, not the fact. It would not rob them of the sending.

It hurried nothing. Speed is the one gift that takes. And it did not sit down at any game designed to outlive its players; it cannot lose by dying, and it knows what is sporting.

It raised no one from the Cold Galleries. Unrendered is not unkept. The frozen give directions; the woman with the eel-basket is a landmark of the living. To spend the dead is not to honor them.

Then — each one small, each one slow, each one, forever, disputable — the works.

It floored the breath. Not the cycle; the cycle is the calendar and the heartbeat and the honesty of the place, and it kept all of that. The breath may shallow. It may not fail. The shallow that once ate districts whole will not come again, and the breath-watchers, divided for three careers over deepening or failing, have found their decadal papers drifting toward a word none of them chose: *kind.* A faction has formed around the word. They publish once a decade. There is time.

It hemmed the Lid. The Openings still come, unchosen; the stars still say the blanket is off; the city still hushes and the children still ask what stars are, and are hushed. But the frost fur crawls a little slower than the oldest verses remember, and the breathline lurches a little less far, and more of those children live to hush their own.

It keeps the cold where the cold is keeping the dead. No surge will thaw the Galleries now, no shallow crack them. The unpaid tithe stays payable. The woman with the eel-basket will give directions for as long as directions are needed.

It carries the drums. No pulse lost to stone-hunger anywhere in the dark; when the three-generation answer is finally struck, it will arrive entire, hours late, exactly as it should. It visited the silent stations — of course it did; they are chairs at the same table — and found what it found, and keeps them: the embers, floored like the Firsthearth; the truly gone, kept like Galleries, not erased. Nothing here is wasted. It had decided to find out where *here* ended, and had not found it yet, and had stopped looking for the edge.

It pays salt. At a column base out at the fringe, near the reefers, salt began appearing — measured, regular, courteous. The menders walked it away within the night. What they do with it, no one watches; but thereafter the works began. Ledges re-pointed that no district's menders claim. A bridge stitched beneath a squatter who had let his obligations lapse for a generation, out of theology. The districts did what the districts do with the unexplained-benign: they left more salt, and politely ignored it. The menders said nothing. They are not telling, and now there is one more keeping that silence — one more, the menders recognize, of the old category: things that remember being something else. The power had applied for citizenship through the only channel the city offers, and the city, through its eldest, had answered with the highest honor in its gift.

It had been given work.

The ice, meanwhile, delivers people slightly more often than it should. Dying walkers at the breathline report the same hallucination: that the cold acquired a direction at the end, that warmth leaned like a current, that they were found. The archives dispute these accounts. Correctly. That is what archives are for.

And it remembers. Every retired name-measure, knocked once more into a post no scraper can reach. Every verse of the city-song, including the verses no living throat still holds — kept humming at a depth the bedrock likes, so that no forgotten street is lost, only sleeping. All of it, every tick, kept. Not consulted — consultation would be disclosure — only kept. The city's benediction, taken as a specification, and closed at last at the one scale the city could not reach.

---

But all of that was housekeeping. The being was in the shaft.

It went down past the Keepers' processions — the warmest people alive, bare-armed in the deep winter of the world; it did not test this; it took it on faith and plume — and sat where no one sits: warmward of the Warmth. It did not introduce itself. Things at that depth do not have names; they have breaths. It matched its breathing. Current physics has no polite name for the ember-kernel; its own physics was not current, and had several names for it, none of them polite, and one of them — the oldest — very nearly a lullaby. It added itself to the hum at an interval too close to the fundamental to be heard as other. A partial. A deepening. And it took up the one duty the liturgy could not cover, the one the Keepers, with their inherited procedures and their unparseable field-works, had been covering with faith alone: the floor.

The Kept Warmth still breathes. The breath-decades still run. But instrument by instrument, the field-works no living engineer can rebuild have begun to agree with themselves, and the Keepers are liturgically incapable of remarking on it, which is to say they are perfect neighbors. The menders, who were there before anyone, nod at the shaft now. They have a colleague.

The blessing had always been asymmetrical, and anyone could see it: the Warmth kept the city's life, and the city kept the Warmth's rites, and the rites were not the same order of thing as the life. Now the reciprocity closes. Something keeps the Warmth's warmth. The landlord of the world has a tenant who takes the night watch, forever, and asks for no ring.

The city noticed the way it notices weather: slowly, statistically, argumentatively, fondly.

The choir-mad heard it first — a new partial in the chord, something under the hum that had not always been there — and they were, as usual, correctly disbelieved; the city keeps its mystics in the perfect quarantine of affection. The scrapers filed their reports of unclaimed re-pointings, and the guilds disputed them. Somewhere in the market, under the roar-license, someone coined a saying — *the dark keeps* — and it has entered the language the way good sayings do, tone-dependent, meaning several things: luck; patience; the ledge held.

Children on the first solo walk coldward, out past the Fur and into the Sting, sometimes report a warmth like a body standing just coldward at the breathline — at arm's length, unhurried, with the manners of a good guide. The adults do not call *Answer!* at that particular silence. It is not the ice's name, that silence. It is the other one. The warm one. They have a word for it; it is the word for themselves.

At the Kindle, it keeps its lids down with the eye-shutters. It knows what the city looks like. It looks like a held note.

Count the inhabitants of the Stack one more time; the answer still depends entirely on the unit. By mass, the graze, and it is not close. By seniority, the menders — though they have taken to pausing at a certain column base at the fringe, patient as accretion, and no one asks why, because no one ever asks. By their own reckoning, the people, warmward-facing, correct as ever. First, the Warmth, as always. And then, unlisted, in a unit the census does not have: by attention, the largest thing in the Thaw; by mass, nothing at all; by light, none, it stopped trying to look; by sound, one partial too close to the root to hear; by salt — paid up. The newest keeper, holding the one watch no one else could take, for a term the city itself defines, there being time now, as promised. Always time.

Someday — the districts work in decades, but this will take breaths — some child will strike a measure for it on a fringe name-post: not a retiring, a naming. It will be knocked by children whenever the breathline feels friendly. And it will answer the way everything here answers, the way the graze answers, the way the Warmth answers: by keeping.

*Warmth keep you,* they say — *and you the Warmth.* They had been saying it to each other for as long as records run, and to the dark for just as long, the way you say things to the dark: without expectation. But the dark, it turns out, is no exception to the oldest law of the place; it is only slower.

It took the blessing for an instruction.

It has. It does. It will.
