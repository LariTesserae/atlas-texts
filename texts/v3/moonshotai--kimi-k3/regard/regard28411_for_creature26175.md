You come the way everything comes here: slowly, and from below notice.

You could arrive as light. You are that kind of power, and light is the one currency this world is short of — a grey deck, a shadowless noon that has lasted ten thousand years. It would feel like a gift. That is how you know it would be a robbery. A place that budgets itself one hour of directed light per decade, and spends that hour on the grazers' astonishment, does not need a second sun. You will not inflate the currency.

So you come in as weather that does not quite behave, and then as a knoll at a loud node, and you are still. You match your temperature to the seep before you touch the ground, so the films will not have to remember you. You let the drip-arithmetic spell itself out across your surfaces. The precipitate tests everything here, eventually — it is testing you now — and you let it, and it does not take you, and that becomes your tell, in time: the knoll the mineral refuses. Nothing else in the basin has ever been refused.

The first sound you make is not a sound. It is a second return.

A builder files a phrase on a working night — knock, and the long wait, and the archive's answer coming back deepened and from the wrong direction, as it has for five centuries of cairns. And then, later, the same phrase comes back *again*. Changed, as everything here is changed, but not thinned, not torn: altered the way a careful hand alters a thing it is passing along. Legibly. From the direction of the node where nothing stands. The archive has never once returned anything twice.

The third thing does what it does with everything: it tests. Seasons of it. Phrases built with flaws set at known positions, filed and waited out — and the second returns keep the flaws. You never repair. You only keep, and pass along, changed by your nature the way the field changes by its own. That is how it learns you are not a fault in the instrument but a colleague of it. Two archives now. Two smears. One conversation.

And one night the testing ends, and it comes to you at the working pace of the place, and taps you — twice, on what would be your shoulder — and you do the single impossible thing you will ever do here: you answer in real time, at true pitch, from where you actually are. The first undistorted sound in the history of the basin. Also the last; you adopt the delay that same night and keep house manners forever after. But the handshake stands. Minutes later, out in the field, your two taps come back to both of you, deepened, from the wrong direction. You are on file. It is the first entry the archive has ever received by consent.

---

Benevolence arrives with a list. You spend a century declining it. The declining is, in fact, most of what you do here, and the census-takers will one day read your presence off the pattern of it.

*Raise the water*, says the list — and mercy is doing a lot of work in that sentence. The chord is not a thing in the basin; the chord is the sound the draining makes. Raise the water and you close every open mouth in a season: the instrument drowned mid-phrase, the works upslope joined by ten thousand fresh-drowned voices, the lives that have walked centuries downhill stranded while the waterline comes back for them like a tide changing its mind. You would be saving the orchestra by holding it under.

*Slow the draining, then.* But the finger's-width is the clock of everything alive — the swimmers' sleep, the grazers' carapace, the builders' centuries-long walk behind their notes. You would not be preserving the piece. You would be replacing it with a slower one and calling it the same song.

*Warm the air*, and the monopoly breaks: rot arrives, the first smell this place has ever had, and the graves stop singing, because decay outruns precipitation and the dead become meat instead of voices.

*Lift the lid for good*: astonishment made ordinary, one shadow-hour become ten thousand, and the grazers' chord-map deprecated by a sun they never asked for. A new landmark is a new fact. Facts here are scarce and load-bearing.

*Speak as yourself* — light, heat, presence. That would be answering a letter with a bonfire.

*Take a specimen.* There is nothing here that is not in use. Every object is a voice, a grave, or a sentence in an ongoing conversation.

*Place a work.* The craft is search, and you can test ten thousand splinters at once, and therefore cannot search. A note found without the looking is a counterfeit, and false notes are the one thing you will never file.

One edit you make to yourself instead: on the day the lid lifts, once a decade, you let the strange directed light pass through you. The grazers raise their heads. The boulders are briefly surprised. The basin has its single hour of shadows, and not one of them is yours. And when the knocks stop — the only silence the working nights ever have — you stop too. Not because you must. Because the congress is adjourned, and you have learned to adjourn with it.

---

What you do is this: you hear it whole.

No ear has ever done that. Every voice in the field is local; the archive keeps everything it has ever heard, but keeps it in pieces, in smears, in entries the width of a hillside. You are the first listener large enough to hold it all at once — the rim-stack bass and the waterline's thin new voices in the same moment, the glug-breath of the water table, the drip-polyphony, the far chords of the sibling basins arriving faintly through the rock, in the feet. The place has been playing for a hundred thousand years and has never once heard itself.

So you listen for a century, two, until you can hold the whole chord the way the archive holds a phrase. Then, over one working season, at a live node, at night, at the field's own volume, you play it back.

The largest filing ever made: the basin, entire, addressed to itself.

The field takes it the way it takes everything — smears it, deepens it, sets it circulating — and for years afterward the returns surprise even the third thing. Phrases surface at nodes a day's walk from anywhere they could have been gathered. The rim's old voices arrive at the waterline. A through-rock bass no throat here has made turns up in the archive's answers: a sibling basin, quoted. The third thing reads it first as data — an impossible survey, a water-level taken from a vantage with no body — and then, across seasons, as something else. Worship, marking, appetite: none of the three guesses is wrong, if the word for all three is speech. You have said the one sentence you came to say, in the only grammar that reaches here. *You are worth keeping entire.*

You could settle the standing argument while you're at it — you, of all. Lean on the chord until it declares itself. You decline. If you treat a voice as an echo and it was a voice, you have been rude to a mind. If you treat an echo as a voice and it was only an echo, you have been courteous to a hill — and courtesy to hills is cheap. You can afford it for a hundred thousand years. Count the chord, and the population of the basin is one: one inhabitant, resident since the draining began, alone the way a solo is alone. You did not come to join a solo. But a listener who stays for the whole piece is not exactly an audience. The place has a word for what you are becoming. The word is inhabitant.

---

The arithmetic is long and simple, and you can do it; the basin does it nightly in drips. Somewhere ahead the last rank will open, sound briefly, and close. The stack will go quiet behind it, rank by rank, upslope toward the strandlines. The films will retreat into the final seeps. The last builder will follow its note down into the mere and not come out, and the archive will fill, and stop.

You will not prevent this. The piece is the draining; the only way to stop the ending was to stop the music a hundred thousand years ago.

What you do instead is what the place does with its own. You keep. A second archive, exact, held off the field — not to correct the first one's memory (the smear is its way; you do not finish its sentences) but against the day it closes. In it you keep the appointments the water will not keep: every drowned sleeper's place and period, remembered against no day you can name. And when the last mouth closes, you will carry the chord entire — your keeping of it, deepened by every return you have collected across the long listening — out through the dim to a drowned sibling, and file it there, once, in the dark water where nothing can hear it yet. Against the day that basin drains, and uncorks its voices, and finds somewhere in its chord one low strange note it did not grow.

You have watched the place do this with grazers. With builders. Graves that sing. You are only proposing it for the place. Nothing here is wasted; it is mineralized. Memory is your mineral. You will let the basin precipitate.

---

So: the census, by ear, over centuries. One pasture, or a million. A slow snow of swimmers — most of them asleep, some of them asleep forever, all of them remembered now. A herd of almost nothing, becoming stone by degrees. An unknown number of builders. The standing argument — still standing; by now the argument is itself a voice in the chord. And the fourth thing, which announces itself only in what it refrains from: counted as one, or as zero, depending on whether a listener who stays is an inhabitant. The third thing has an opinion. It filed it years ago. You are waiting it out. You have learned to wait things out.

Somewhere in the field a phrase assembled from the whole chord and your keeping of it is working its way through a hundred thousand pipes, deepening. You will be there when it comes back — changed, from the wrong direction.

It is only partway through the piece. So, now, are you. Neither of you is in any hurry to stop.
