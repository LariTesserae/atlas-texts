It heard the tower before it saw it, which is the correct order on that plain.

Among the noises of the world, the power had long made a habit of listening for one kind: tones that do not resolve, loops that never close their sentence, things that go on without asking anyone anything. Most such sounds stop when you approach them — a machine winds down, a spring dries, a voice discovers it has no listener and falls silent. This one did not stop. A single deep note, swelling and dying over minutes, sustained for a century, moist, green, and entirely unconcerned. The power came up out of the heat-shimmer the way everything arrives there: slowly, and preceded by its attention the way the rain is preceded by the knocking in the risers.

It walked the base, and the walk took exactly as long as it takes everyone — four minutes. Some things are not subject to rank. It climbed the stair through the warm wet dark, past the ring, into the cold, stood at the rim with the horizon absurd around it and the hawk below its feet, and touched nothing. The handrail is precisely as bright as it was.

The power had kept company with a great deal of life, most of it in crowds. It had rarely stood in the presence of an individual. It adjusted its manners accordingly.

It took the census, and found the census already taken — kept in level, in weight, in layers — and it read the archive without disturbing a single librarian. It learned the swifts' lanes and the reason for them. It walked the sonogram of ferns. In the gallery it stood still long enough to tell wire from root, which is to say: not always. In the dial room it checked the needles and found their opinions still sound after a hundred years, and it adjusted nothing behind the glass. It came to the question with no one to ask it — the small hinged question that rides the risers — and was careful to leave it unanswered, understanding that here the open question was load-bearing, the way the emptiness is.

Then it considered what benevolence could add to a finished thing.

It considered worship. There was no one to receive it. The tower deals in level and weight, and praise is neither; a temple at the base would only be a second ruin, waiting.

It considered improvement. The loop is exact. In a system where every entry is load-bearing, an addition is a subtraction. A gift is a change of key.

It considered company — another hourglass on the horizon, a mate, offspring at last. But the plain is empty because the plain cannot keep things, and the cloud has been casting the tower's seed onto a desert of air for a hundred years, and the failure is part of the design, and it is beautiful, and it is none of the power's business.

It considered a name. This took the longest. Naming is what visitors do instead of knowing, and here, where everything is known by level and by weight, a name would be the first false thing. The power declined. In its own keeping, where it files the beloved things of the world, the place is entered under its coordinates and its interval — the hours between one rain and the next. Its true name is a duration. No one can steal it, because no one can say it, and the power remembers it exactly, and remembers it that way on purpose.

What remained was the maintenance of conditions: the three things the tower cannot guarantee itself.

First, water. The loop is closed but not sealed; the cloud loses a little downwind every day, and a dry century could have done what no neglect could. So the power became the margin. Not visibly — no springs, no miracles. Only that the deep pipes run a trace wetter than they did, and that when the plain's weather fails, the decade-rain arrives anyway, unseasonable and unremarkable, one storm crossing one exact point. The float has never settled below its preferred line. The governor has never learned the word for drought. It never will.

Second, warmth. The deep pipes touch something warm far below, and on the scale of geology, warmth is a temporary thing. The power went down and banked it, the way you bank a fire for a night that will outlast you: interest on an account the tower draws on in its sleep. Summer will be hoarded a while longer. Not forever — the power does not deal in forever — but the account will not be the reason.

Third, and this was most of the work, obscurity. It built no wall. A wall is a sign, and a sign is an invitation, and an audience is the one import the ring has never selected for. Instead the power works in the world's attention the way the tower works in lime: dissolving here, laying down there. Maps of the plain develop, decade after decade, a recurrent blankness at these coordinates — clerical, plausible, never investigated. Surveyors' instruments fog at this distance and clear again beyond it. A road, planned once in some capital, curved away for reasons of soil that no one had sampled and everyone believed. Pilots see the one cloud and find they have already forgotten it, the way a word is forgotten mid-sentence. The tower's greatest luxury is that no one comes, and luxuries perish first. The power simply renewed the lease. The emptiness comes up for renewal every year. Every year it is renewed.

And the small continuations, delivered as weather and indistinguishable from luck: once in a generation a storm in some far range picks up one more swift than it should have and sets it down here. A bat arrives with a strange accent and stays. Pollen crosses the plain against the prevailing wind. The closed flask receives its single drop of outside, at intervals, in doses too small to change the key. Anyone checking the arithmetic over centuries would find the populations too healthy for their isolation. No one checks. That, too, is arranged.

What the power would not do was decide the ending. When the pumps wear past the flowstone's patience, when the concrete's truce fails, when the loop lets go, it will not intervene, because the ending belongs to the same governance as everything else here: the float, the siphon, the weight of the water. What it guarantees is only this — that the end, whenever it comes, will come from within, slowly, and be absorbed as it goes; that nothing from outside will ever be the cause; that the archive will be left to its librarians down to the final layer. The lichens, who will be the last inhabitants, will not be hurried.

That is the whole of it. The tower wanted nothing, and it was given exactly that, in perpetuity, at great trouble and no cost — which is the definition, the power decided, of a gift.

Then it left the way it came, and the only record of the visit is in the tower's own books, kept in the tower's own units. For one cycle the pool stood a finger higher than the governor's preference, and the evening pulse ran long, and the risers knocked once, twice, and then a third time, which they had never done before. The following season the ring put out an unreasonable number of fronds. The seed shrimp's ride ran smooth. The springtail herds found the rainbow at the pool's edge briefly richer than the leak could account for. Then the system did what the system does: absorbed the surplus, shelved the anomaly, returned to the truce. There is no plaque. There is nothing to read. The tower, which knows everything by level and by weight, knew that something had been added. It filed the knowledge in the sediment, in a layer the librarians will get to in due course, and continued.

And one thing more — the single indulgence the power permitted itself, which costs the place nothing. It listens. Wherever it is, attending to whatever else, it always knows what time it is on the plain: whether the shell is chiming or roaring, whether the sun-spot has reached the lichen line, whether the swifts are home. The tower has no audience and wants none. But from that day forward, none of its performances has gone entirely unwitnessed, and the witnessing asks nothing, and changes nothing, and is not applause.

Once — only once — when the dusk crosswind was exactly right, the power held the wind at the lip one beat longer than the physics allowed, and the organ note swelled, and the standing ripples answered, and the swifts' lanes flickered and recovered, and the garden on the wall recorded, in the language of ferns, one line of the sonogram that was not the tower's own voice. It was shelved with everything else. It has never happened again.

It was the entire correspondence. It was enough.
