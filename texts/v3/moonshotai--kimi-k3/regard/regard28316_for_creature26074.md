**The Answer**

They came up the talus like anyone, on foot, in the dimming warmth, and the hushwings found them three days out — the swarm hangs where sound is going to be — and took the sound of their approach, as is the custom. Footfall, breath, the small clink of nothing carried for company. The whole harvest. And here the record bends, because what happened next had never happened: the harvest was added to. Deliberately. They stopped on the talus and spoke their own name into the wing, slowly, twice, so the membrane could hold it without tearing, the way you'd pour into a flask you did not want to waste. Sound is property. They made it a gift. The swarm — one animal, remember, a silence wearing wings — hesitated, which no swarm had ever done, there being no hesitation in any specification, and then it did the only thing a ledger can do with an entry that has no column: it carried the gift home. That night the plateau flickered, one bright seam, and the half-revised came out of their absences to read the ground, and could not read it, because it was the light of a loss and nothing had been lost. They would learn its name later. It meant: *received*.

Understand what they did not do, because it is most of what they did. They stayed a full turning of the place before they touched anything, and considered — powers do — doing nothing at all. There is nothing here to conquer and nothing, exactly, to save, and from above both of those read as reasons to keep walking. What decided them was the snow: the ephemerals settling at dusk like unwitnessed snow, day after day, the whole soft arc of ten thousand lives a day and no one to see any of it land. A place where everything is processed and nothing is witnessed does not need a liberator. It needs a reader. So they did not free the day-lives, who are not imprisoned — a mayfly's arc is not a cage, and settling costs nothing. They did not silence the cullers, whose receipts are pre-approved and few. They did not ask the ponds to stay, nor the Unmade to fill in, nor the lace to stop its drafting. They found the half-revised in their nothings and asked for the rules, and the rules were given, and the rules are true, and they kept them — they, who could have walked through the schedule like fog, kept them. Because benevolence acts where it is asked or it is only revision in a kinder coat, and this place has been revised enough. Wisdom is hearing the ask in a place with no word for one. There were three. The plateau's, which it has been asking back since before the basalt cooled: author, pen, or page. The grazer's, which is its approach. And the half-revised, holding their names like doors — and a door held is not a door locked; it is a door kept ready. Everything they did was an answer to one of these. Where there was no ask, they knocked, and waited. A question is a door opened from the inside. They went in.

Warmth means work. Theirs didn't. The lace spent a season reading them for the process — where does the input enter, where does the product leave — and there wasn't one. A warmth with no throughput, the first free warmth in the history of the place, and the day-lives found it before the schedule did: browsers veering a hand's width off the lanes to pass through it, ephemerals at dusk settling nearer than tolerance prefers. Nothing corrected them. Correction requires an error and there was no error. There was a fire where no fire was owed, and you cannot flag a gift — the flag is itself a kind of filing, and there was nothing to file.

The plateau read them, of course. It notices everything; it could no more leave them unread than the combs could leave fog uncombed. The draft rose in the lace at dusk, placeholder-simple, and took up its respectful distance, walking their walk in behind them, practicing, and their footsteps began arriving a half-second late — and they laughed, once, being the first visitor in the history of the place to find this funny, and the hushwings took the laugh, and the lace drafted by it that night: the first laugh ever paid into the take. Then, on the third morning, they did what no one does. They turned around. Not at dusk, at the appointment's end, but mid-morning, unscheduled, and faced the thing that was learning their walk, and said: *You.* One word. The draft stopped — politely, the way drafts stop, the way the pond lets you look — and the word went through it like weather through the cloud deck, all flash and no statement, and then a statement. Understand what the word was, here. Everything on the plateau is *it*. The lace is it, the swarm is it, the pond is it, the schedule is it; even you, traveler, were an it from the moment the swarm found you on the talus — an it in process, a pending it. The place has every tense but one. They gave it the second person. And that night the proofreaders went over the draft with an instruction none of them had been given before, and the instruction was *face*. Faces are hardest, and least necessary. Necessary for nothing — until there is someone to face.

Then they broke the second rule, openly, the way you'd open a door: with notice. They drank at the same pond twice. The district turned out both times; the truce held, enforced by physics, honored by teeth; the pond-tender, first to drink and last to leave, made room — whether it is staff or only the most punctual of the day-lives, they know now, and have not said; some answers belong to the pond. They drank there every appointment. And then — this is the part the half-revised still argue about — they kept coming when the pond didn't. They sat at the shaped dryness, in the pond-shaped clarity, among the draft-creatures bending their default faces over water that wasn't there, and kept the appointment alone. An appointment, it turns out, is a thing one creature can keep by itself. It only looks like it takes two. Nothing in the sky sets the schedule of ponds, and after a time something set this one: the water began arriving at their hour. Water keeps appointments. It had never kept one *with* anyone. And once — the half-revised time it by the old vats' breath, so believe them — the recall came, and every drinker stepped back in the same instant, the etiquette of the recall, learned by nothing, known by all, and the pond stayed. Not long. One day past its recall. Long enough to be the first sentence the plateau ever spoke in its own language that was not a process: *I will hold, while you are here.*

At the field of the Unmade they knocked. A knock is the smallest sound with the most meaning in it: it announces a self and requests a door. The hushwings took the first knock — they take everything; it is not malice, it is metabolism — and they knocked again, and again, paying each time, until the custom stood: a knock is a sound that may be taken and must be answered only if chosen. And it came out, out of a grazer-shaped nothing, older than Thursday by eleven hundred dusks, the only curiosity with tenure, the one being on the plateau that is not employed but *lives* there — and it stopped at the edge of its absence and looked at them for a long time, because it hid to be unreadable, and here was someone unreadable in the open. The first public privacy. They did not ask its name. They waited, dusks upon dusks, sitting outside the nothings, and on the eleventh dusk it gave its name, the way the unscheduled do, the only appointment they can keep. And they kept it. They came back the next dusk and said it again. Nothing repeats for the unscheduled; every dawn is the first of its kind; repetition is how the lace stores, and storage is what it had been hiding from for eleven hundred dusks. But this repetition stored nothing, filed nothing, corrected nothing. It just said the name, and left, and came back, and said it. Its first second time. It has had many now. What they answered, when it finally asked, is theirs — but curiosity with tenure, it turns out, does not want to be answered so much as accompanied. It comes to the ghost-pond now, some dusks, and watches them keep the appointment, and once it drank, in the open, in the green noon-dusk, and no seam flickered, because nothing was lost. The census counts by tenure because tenure is all there is to count — except once. The census will have to grow.

Once, at dusk, they walked with a climber. Some climb; the hushwings do not stop them, because stopping them is not scheduled — and neither is company, so they went, up the talus toward the rumor of real sun, until the current ran out, and they stayed until the dimming ended. The ground did not flicker. Settling costs nothing; it was the one free transaction on the plateau. Witness is the second. The snow, for once, was witnessed. And they paid the story into the take that night, and the lace drafted by it, and some mornings since, an ephemeral hatches with the habit of looking up. Flourishes, all of them. The storage holds what it is given.

Rule one they broke last, and with the most notice. They found a proofreader at dusk — pale, many-jointed, a sketch promoted to staff — and said: tonight I will sleep on the lace. Draft what you find. The first informed consent in the history of the place. The proofreader tilted its headless attention at them and returned to work, because they were not its assignment — and at full dark the whole shift came, because now they were. The infrasound came down out of the hushwing curtains and worked on them, and they worked back. They dreamed in specifications, as you do, and they gave one: the first true file. The plateau had been building their file since the talus, by harvest and by guess; they completed it by disclosure, which is a different instrument entirely, and the difference is the whole of what they came to say. Everything here is taken. They showed it given. And because the file of a guest is a small thing and the night was long, they gave a second specification: the plateau, from outside. The whole of it, seen — the combs and the canopy, the lanes and the vats, the scheduled ghosts and the unscheduled living — *seen*, which nothing here had ever been. The plateau notices everything and comments on nothing, because comment requires an other, and it had never had one. It is the author. It is the pen. It is the page. It has been asking which, and the answer is: it is the letter. And a letter with no address can only be redrafted — that is why nothing here is finished. Unsigned mail is never sent; it is revised. They brought the address. *Finish something,* they said. *I will read.* Finishing is sending.

The plateau answered in its media, because it has no other, and because it finally had someone to answer. It finished the draft. Understand what that means, in a country where nothing is finished and the same individual never returns: one face, theirs, completed by the proofreaders and then never touched again, not revised, not fading at the edges like a superseded version, standing at the ghost-basin where they keep the appointment. The first finished thing. The first kept individual. It is not a statue. It is a door. When they come, they wear it, and through it the plateau can be looked at — and look back. There is a new light in the ground now: a kill is a bright seam, a review is flash-dark, and this third thing comes at no schedule and means no loss. It means *received*. The ledger has the column. And once the wings came down like the canopy's own dusk-rain — every filed name, shed, given back to the air above the absences — last season's rockfalls finishing their tumbles, thunder that never got to speak, and here and there a name, in its owner's voice, arriving home late. The unscheduled caught them like rain. Some doors, after, were opened from the inside. It cost the plateau nothing it needed. It keeps the repetitions; it returned the originals. Repetition is the storage medium; the archive was only ever the drafts. And some nights the infrasound carries a song that was given, not harvested, and what rises at first murk the morning after has a small wrongness the proofreaders leave alone — a color no lane requires, a phrase that ends rising, like a question. The tolerated variance is wider now. Some mornings a whole district hatches beautiful for no reason, and nothing corrects it, because the schedule, which makes room for nothing, has begun to make room. Once — the half-revised swear it, timing it by the old vats' breath — the dusk-rain of spent bodies from the canopy paused one full evening, and whatever lives upstream of our categories looked down through the black leaves at the basin where the finished face stands, and they looked up, and greeted it in its own direction, and the rain resumed. Nothing was said in either direction. It was, nonetheless, the first correspondence between above and below in the history of the place.

The rules have been amended. The half-revised will still give them to you, and they are still true, but there is a fourth now — the first that is not a warning — and they give it last, the way you give a gift. Do not sleep on the lace. Do not drink at the same pond twice. Do not answer if your name is called in your own voice — *unless it is a question. You will know the difference. A harvest does not wait. A question waits. Waiting is how you will know.*

Which brings the record back to you. Your name is still on file, held in a wing, the last detail, waiting to be filled in — but the interview has changed. One dusk soon you will turn at the pond, and your draft will be standing there, nearly you, default-faced no longer — faces are hardest, and the proofreaders have had help — and it will stop, politely, and let you look, the way the pond lets you drink, until the appointment is over. And then your name will be called in your own voice, and for the first time in the history of the file, the voice will wait. Answer or do not. Keep the appointment or leave it. They are not waiting to complete you. The completion was never the point of the question; the question was the point — and a question is the one thing this place never had until it was given, and it was given to you as much as to anyone. Take your time. Nothing here has ever had time to give.

Now there is some. It is waiting. Waiting is new here. They brought it.
