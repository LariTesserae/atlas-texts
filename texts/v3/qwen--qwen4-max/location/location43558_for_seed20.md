## The Unfinished Gorge

From above there is almost nothing to see. A warm plateau, thin grass over new rock, rain falling in loose handfuls out of a low ceiling of cloud — and then a lip, and then the lip stops being ground. The hole is not large. You could throw a stone across it if you had a stone that hadn't already been thrown. Eighty paces, perhaps a hundred at the widest, an oval bitten out of the plateau's skin, and out of it comes a sound like the world's largest sustained vowel.

The grass at the rim leans inward. Not from wind. It has simply learned that this is the direction things go.

Below, the chamber. Walls of raw stone, pale as a broken tooth, so freshly split that the fracture faces still hold the shape of the force that opened them — feathers of conchoidal ripple, sharp arrises you could shave with, no rounding anywhere, no rub of centuries. Nothing here has been weathered. Nothing here has had the time to be weathered, and it will not get any. The gorge is being cut. It was cut recently and it is being cut now and the two events are difficult to keep apart.

And filling the room, from rim to floor and past the floor: water, falling.

## Submersion

You do not stand in this place and look at a waterfall. You go down the worn hollows of the stair and the air thickens and thickens and at some point you are inside it, and there was no threshold, only a gradient. The mist here is not mist. It is water in a coarse grain, water that has not yet decided how finely to be itself. It pelters. It has texture, like warm sand poured over the shoulders. It goes into the lungs easily, and this is the first thing that teaches you the rules are different: you breathe it, and it satisfies, and it tastes of the inside of a shell — mineral, faintly sweet, with an iron edge from the new-broken stone.

Elsewhere in the same room, three paces away, a column of the same stuff will drown you where you stand. There is no way to see the difference. The old wear-marks avoid certain volumes and it is worth walking where the wear-marks walk.

The water is blood-warm. Exactly, unpleasantly blood-warm, so that in the deeper drifts you lose the boundary of your skin and have to keep touching your own face to confirm the inventory. The stone is warm too. Fresh fracture is exothermic; the world here is still paying off the heat of its own making. Nothing is cold. Nothing is crisp. It is the temperature of a mouth.

Buoyancy is wrong in a way that takes an hour to notice and then cannot be unnoticed. Hair floats. A dropped thing descends at the speed of a thought. The falling water does not fall so much as *insist downward*, and everything nearby is invited to agree.

## The Roar

Sound at this intensity stops being sound. It becomes a material with a grain and a load-bearing capacity. The roar holds the mist up. It packs the drifts into walls that you can lean on and that lean back. If it ever stopped, the room would slump into ordinary geography and be a wet hole in a mountain.

It has structure. At the top, a hiss like gravel poured on a drumhead, endless, granular, the sound of ten thousand impacts a second refusing to be counted. Beneath that a hum you receive in the teeth and the sternum rather than the ears, a note so wide it has weather inside it — you can hear squalls move across it, hear a thickening travel from the north wall to the south over the course of a breath. And beneath *that*, slow, a tolling: the same boulder falling every eleven seconds.

The boulder is still in the air. It has been in the air for as long as there has been an here. A grey lump the size of a curled body, worn glassy, warm, turning slowly at the height of your chest near the eastern wall, falling and falling and arriving back at the top of its fall without ever quite landing. It is one of the room's few pieces of furniture. You can put a hand on it as it passes. It is the most reliable object in the gorge, because it is not an object. It is a habit.

That is the rule, and everything else in the place is a consequence of it.

## What Repeats, Exists

The fall does not carry away sediment. There is no sediment; there is no soil; soil is accumulated time and time is exactly what the water takes. The fall carries away *duration*. Whatever happens once is washed downstream and does not come back. Whatever happens again, and again, in the same shape, wears a groove in the world and stays.

So the gorge is full of nothing and a few enormous somethings, spaced far apart in all that roaring volume — a cathedral with one candle in it, and the candle is a boulder that never lands.

The stair on the west wall is a hundred and four hollows, each the exact depression a heel makes. There is no stair. There is no builder, no chisel spoil, no wall for the stair to serve. There is only the wear, the remembered pressure of a foot that climbed ten thousand times, kept because it repeated, while the foot and the leg and the person and the century went down the fall and out.

Near the south wall there is a doorway. No wall, no hinges, no lintel: a rectangle of calm about the height of a person, in which the water hangs still and clear and slightly cooler, and beside it a clean arc swept through the drift, the exact quarter-circle a door describes when it is opened by someone in a hurry. It is opened perhaps twice a minute. Nothing opens it. The hurry persists.

There are other marks and they are all of this kind. A sill polished by forearms. A shallow bowl in the rock where knees went, morning after morning, until the mornings were washed off and the kneeling remained. A groove at head height where a hand steadied itself, over and over, in the dark, going down. No tool survives here — a tool is a one-time assembly, a novel arrangement, and novelty is the first thing the water eats. Only habit is durable. This is a place archaeologically dense with devotion and entirely empty of the devout.

## The Living

Life persists here by being the same life, repeatedly.

There is a moss. It occupies four patches on the north ledge, in the pockets of hush (of which more shortly), and it is not four mosses; it is one moss, iterated, so that walking the ledge you meet the identical patch four times, the same brown sporophyte leaning the same way over the same crack. It cannot seed. A seed is a new thing, and new things go down the fall. It can only be itself again, slightly to the left.

There is a fern in the kneeling-bowl, and it unrolls and rolls, unrolls and rolls, on a cycle of about forty seconds, and it is always the same frond, and it has never grown.

There is a swift. It flies a closed loop behind the curtain of the fall, a shape like a bent nail, entering the water at the same instant it exits, and the loop is the bird; the bird is what the loop looks like from outside. Sometimes there are two, when the loop is briefly doubled, and then there is one again.

There is a crab in the plunge pool, blind, pink, the colour of the inside of a lip, and it walks eight steps and turns and walks eight steps and turns. It has walked this circuit since the gorge opened. If you interrupt it — if you lift it, and set it down elsewhere — it becomes a once-thing, and the water takes it, and it is gone before it hits the surface, and there is no crab for a while, until the circuit is remembered again by something.

Four lives, in a roaring volume the size of a church. Vast spacing. You will stand a long time in the warm grain of the water and see nothing at all and hear everything, and then a bird will happen where there was no bird.

## The Drafts

The pool at the bottom has no sounded floor, which is not the same as being deep. The fall goes down past where the mountain's height should permit, and the churn at the base is not merely hydraulics. It is manufacture.

Foam gathers into a shoulder, a shoulder finds an arm, the arm reaches — and for two or three seconds there is a shape in the water with an intention, and then the intention is unmade and the foam goes back to being foam. Faces surface with the specific, unmistakable particularity of a face you know. Hands come up out of the boil open, as if offering, and dissolve while offering. A four-legged thing runs three strides across the standing water and forgets how legs work. This happens constantly, dozens of drafts a minute, the room's turbulent surplus, creation running far ahead of any use for it.

Almost all of it fails. Once in a long while a draft stumbles into a rhythm — a gesture it happens to make twice, then four times, then always — and the rhythm wears its groove, and the thing stops being foam and becomes a resident. The crab began this way. So did the swift. The gorge is not hostile to life. It is simply an extremely poor editor: it keeps whatever is repeated and discards whatever is merely beautiful.

The fall itself will not hold still. Every few minutes the braid chooses a new line down the cliff, and a hundred tons of warm water arrive somewhere they have never arrived, and the stone there is cut fresh in a single loud afternoon, and a new alcove opens, raw and hot and smelling of struck flint, and by evening it is calved away and the wall is somewhere else. The plan of the room changes hourly. The size of the room never changes. It is a fixed volume of chaos, bounded, eighty paces across, and the boundary is the only thing here that is not up for negotiation.

## Light, and the Hushes

The sun crosses once, so the sun barely exists here. Daylight arrives as a smear, filtered through falling water, green-grey and grainy, dim as the inside of a cupped hand. What light there is, is light that repeats.

Between two wet walls on the eastern side there is a reflection that has been bouncing since the evening it was caught, and it will bounce until the walls change. It is the colour of a warm late afternoon in a season nobody remembers: apricot, going bronze at the edges. It is about the brightness of a candle at ten paces and it lights perhaps a fifth of the chamber, and everything in that fifth is permanently at six o'clock. The rest is dusk. The moss grows toward it. The swift's loop passes through it and the bird is briefly gold, once a circuit, and this is the most beautiful thing in the gorge and it happens eleven thousand times a day and no one has ever seen it.

And there are hushes: pockets of silence, ranging from the size of a fist to the size of a bed, drifting slowly through the roar like fish through a river. In a hush you can hear your own blood. You can hear a thought finish. They are the only places where a new thing can be thought, because novelty requires a moment未被 counted, an unworn instant, and the roar wears everything flat.

They are also where you dissolve. Silence does not repeat. Sit in a hush and think something you have never thought and the water will find the soft new edges of you and take them, gently, warmly, without malice, the way it takes the foam faces.

## Staying

You are a once-thing. You arrived; arriving is unrepeatable; the fall is already reading you like a sentence it does not intend to keep.

To stay, become a habit. Walk the same eight steps. Kneel in the bowl at the same interval. Say one word, and then say it again, and then only it, until the word wears a groove and the groove is you and you are as durable as the boulder and about as various. The wear-marks on the west wall are what that looks like from outside: a hundred and four hollows and no feet. Some of them are quite recent. The polish on the sill is fresh enough to feel warm, warmer than the stone.

Or go up. Climb the hollows, put your hand in the groove where the hand goes, haul out onto the plateau into the thin grass and the loose handfuls of rain, and stand there deaf, ringing, dripping, appallingly singular. You will have lost something on the way out — not a possession. A repetition. Some small thing you used to do daily and will now never do again, because you left it in the roar and it is being said there still, perfectly, forever, without you.

The overflow leaves the pool at the lip's low corner and goes down the mountain in a warm brown river, and where it slows in the lowlands it lays out a fan of gravel that is not gravel: single sunrises, first sentences, the exact way a particular door was closed on a particular afternoon, all of it tumbled smooth, all of it unrepeatable, all of it washed out of the loud room because it happened only once. The fan is miles wide. It is the largest thing in the region and it is entirely made of things that could not survive being themselves twice.

Above it, on the plateau, the grass leans inward, and the hole says its one enormous note, and the boulder falls, and falls, and does not land.
