# The Long Round

Forty-one metres under the salt pan, at the bottom of a ladder that has outlived the company that bolted it, there is a hatch with a wheel and a gasket and a stencil that says **91**. You hear the room through the steel before you open it — not in your ears but in your jaw, in the roots of your teeth, a pressure with a shape to it. The miners who sank this heading called it the Long Round. A *round* is one cycle of drilling, charging, firing, mucking out. Round ninety-one was fired at 04:17:22 on a Tuesday. It has not finished.

The lock takes four minutes. Then the inner door, and the warmth comes in like the inside of a mouth.

## The room

Forty-one metres long, eleven wide, fourteen to the crown. A cathedral with a lid on it. You could walk the whole perimeter at a stroll in under two minutes and touch every wall, and people do, over and over, because the boundaries are the only reassuring thing in here.

The walls are broken stone and the break is *fresh*. Not fresh the way a quarry face is fresh — fresh the way a snapped flint is fresh, four seconds out of the fracture, edges still glassy, stillunread, every arris a knife. The blast chips lie where they landed and they lie standing on edge. There is no rounding. There is no talus slope, no dust-drift, no weathered skin, no stain. In a normal cave, time is legible everywhere as softening. Here nothing has softened. The muck pile still holds the exact thrown shape the shot gave it, a fan of angular blocks flung from the face, and it will hold that shape until the sun goes out.

The light is about three lux, amber-grey, and it comes from the fracture itself. Rock, when it breaks, flashes — the crack gives off a small light as bonds tear. That flash is still going. Held at its dim tail, spread over every fresh surface in the room, it makes a glow with no source and no direction, so there are no shadows anywhere, only edges that are slightly brighter than the volumes they bound. The newest faces glow most. You do not see depth here. You navigate by loudness the way a bat does, and after ten minutes you stop noticing that you're doing it.

Everything is 36.4 degrees. The stone, the air, the machines, the animals, the dust, your glove. Because there is no gradient, nothing feels warm; the wall against your palm feels like *nothing*, like touching your own hand in the dark. Visitors report the sensation of the room having no texture at all until they cut themselves on it.

There is no water. Not a film, not a molecule, not in the minerals. The air is twelve bar of ammonia and nitrogen at its exact saturation point and has been forever, and nothing condenses, because condensation would be an event. The life here runs on ammonia the way ours runs on water: clear, faintly sweet, thin as ether. Cut one of the shelf-grazers and the wound holds a bright meniscus that does not run and does not dry. The room smells of snapped flint and window cleaner, and the smell has no direction — you cannot find its source by walking, and it never fades, and you never get used to it, because getting used to something is a change.

## The storeys

The sound is one hundred and twelve decibels and it does not vary.

It is a chord, and it is standing. The compressor at the heel of the room drives it; the ninety-six shot-holes in the face, each a stopped pipe of a different depth, voice it; the ceramic fauna ring in it. Because nothing here dissipates — dissipation is a gradient, and there are none — the chord does not decay. It has no attack and no release. It was never struck. It is simply a permanent feature of the volume, like the ceiling.

And a standing wave has nodes, and dust collects at nodes.

That is the thing nobody photographs well. The rock flour thrown off by the break hangs in the air and has arranged itself into **nine horizontal shelves**, each a hand's thickness, each spanning the room, spaced up the height of it like the floors of a building nobody built. They are plains of suspended grey powder held in place by the shape of the noise. Put your palm flat on one and it pushes back like the skin of a drum. Push your arm through and it parts and closes with no seam.

Every shelf is crowded. This is where the density lives: not on the ground but stacked, nine storeys of it, animals shoulder to shoulder on floors made of a sustained note. From the hatch, in the fracture-light, the room reads as a solid grey block with faint stratification, and it is only when you move that the layers resolve and you understand that you are looking at a hanging, tiered, packed and populated world with about as much empty air in it as a coral head.

## The census

The survey counted everything, once, and the count has never been wrong.

**Two hundred nine thousand four hundred and sixteen** animals. Flat pale shelf-grazers the size of a hand, tessellated edge to edge across the nine dust plains, eating the flour. What they eat is not the mineral. It is the *newness* of it — freshly fractured quartz carries torn bonds, radicals, a skin of unfinished chemistry, and that unfinishedness is the entire primary productivity of this place. The food web's base is freshness itself. Everything above it is a way of passing newness along.

**Ninety-six** pipe-worms in the shot-holes, reed-bodied, motionless, each one the voice of its hole. They are the vowels of the chord. They have no mouths. They have never needed one.

**Ninety** loops. Under the ring's magnetic field, certain animals run closed field-lines at speed, and you see them as bright beads on invisible racetracks, weaving through the shelves without disturbing them. The survey made the mistake of counting bodies before it understood: the animal is the orbit. The bead is only the part of it currently in front of you. They cannot be caught, because to catch one you would have to catch all of it at once.

**Four** levellers — broad, flat, patient things like mantas a metre across that plane the dust shelves and keep them exactly level, grazing the surplus. They are the reason nothing piles up.

**One** auditor, slow, many-legged, which walks a fixed circuit of the room touching every animal in turn. It takes eleven days. Nobody has watched a whole pass. It is not clear that it is an animal and not a maintenance routine that grew a body.

And in the walls: the healed. Half-mineral patterns in the stone of the back face, animals migrating through solid rock at the speed at which the rock closes, so that they are buried and unburied at exactly the same rate and are therefore always exactly half-visible, like fossils that are still going about their business.

Nothing is born. Nothing dies. Appetite here is a round in the musical sense — nine voices, each eating the next, the ninth eating the first, entering where the last left off, so that the whole cycle sustains a single unchanging note made out of hunger.

## Eleven white things

On the back face, where the stone re-cements as fast as the cutter head breaks it at the other end, there are **eleven** dendrites of sal ammoniac. Crystal ferns, waist-high, white — the only white in an amber world. They grow and dissolve at matched rates and have been the same size since the survey. They are the sparse vegetation of the Long Round, and they are the only objects in it that make structure instead of noise: each one is a physical diagram of a partial of the chord, a frost-notation of the sound it stands in. Touch one and it chimes and does not stop chiming, because it was already chiming, and you have added nothing.

There are eleven. There have always been eleven. That sentence is the whole ecology.

## The scattered iron

The machines are not a factory; they are a handful of objects left in a very large raw room, and most of the plant is simply gone, taken up the shaft by people who had somewhere to be.

A cutter head lodged in the face, still turning, still biting, rated at nine tonnes an hour and producing nine tonnes an hour of new surface forever, all of it eaten and re-laid. A compressor. A capacitor bank with its covers off. Twenty metres of rail ending in air. A ventilation duct that moves nothing, because there is nothing to move it toward.

And around the waist of the room, on ceramic insulators, a closed ring of bus-bar carrying a current that has never been measured to fall. The physicists have a word for what it is doing and the word is wrong. Its field is the trellis the ninety run on. Its whine is the top of the chord, a wet finger on glass, the part that makes visitors' eyes water.

Among all this: a glove. A tally scratched in chalk, four strokes and a gap, four strokes and a gap, ninety times, and then nothing. A tin with a sandwich in it. A clock at 04:17:22 with its second hand trembling between two marks, moving constantly, arriving never.

## Rules

You are the only irreversible thing in the Long Round.

You cannot leave a mark. Step, and the flour closes without a seam; there is a footprint only while your foot is in it. Speak, and the room *subtracts* your voice — it cannot accept new energy, so it cancels you, and what remains is a hole in the chord shaped exactly like your sentence. That is how the locals hear you. Small pale ear-shaped things drift toward the notch and fill it, and your words are eaten, and this is the only response the place will ever make to you.

You cannot cool. There is no colder thing to dump heat into. Your body is a gradient in a gradientless world and the world will not help you; you bring your own weather and it cooks you. Eleven minutes is the working limit, timed not by a clock but by your own rising core.

You cannot take a sample. Freshness is not a property of this rock, it is a property of this room. A chip carried up the shaft dulls, rounds, pits, and goes to sand in your hand — sixty years of weather arriving at once. The sandwich, if you took it, would rot before you reached the ladder.

And nothing here has a past tense. The lease expired. The company dissolved. The men who scratched the tally died old, in beds, on the surface, in the ordinary way. Down here the shot is still four seconds old, the light of the breaking is still coming off the walls, and the round has not been mucked out.

They built a machine for staying the same, and it worked, and it is still working, and the only thing it will ever produce is *now* — nine storeys of it, packed with two hundred nine thousand four hundred and sixteen living things, roaring.

For eleven minutes there is a silence in it shaped like a person. Then the ears fill it, and the chord closes, and the room does not remember you, because remembering is something that happens later, and there is no later here.
