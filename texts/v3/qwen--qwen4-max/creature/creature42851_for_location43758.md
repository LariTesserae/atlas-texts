## What counts as one

Begin with the arithmetic, because the arithmetic is the first thing that lies to you.

Nineteen sheds. Three stacks. Eleven thousand lungs in eleven thousand sockets. Slaters past the number of hollows available to them. Count-flies in a volume you could measure in acre-feet. A census nine digits long. And, if you are being honest, one creature — because nothing in the bowl competes with anything else in the bowl, nothing flees, nothing hunts, nothing is eaten that was not already scheduled to be eaten. There is no ecology here. There is a single body, distributed past the point where distribution means anything, and the bodies you can see are the parts of it that happen to be solid this week.

But even *one animal* is a courtesy. The animal is not the slaters and the pointers and the lungs. Those are precipitates. The animal is the set of loops — water going up and coming down, mineral going out and coming back, number going into the vat and coming out of the vat — and when a loop needs a hand it grows one. The bodies are what the circle does at the point where it needs to touch something.

So: what lives in Ossary Sink is a circulation with mouths.

## The pasture, which is also the fire

Lowest and first: the film. White where it is thin, rust-orange where it is thick, cold, eating sulphur and rock, laying down about as much of itself in a day as the grazing takes. It is the only thing in the bowl that grows, and it does not grow *toward* anything. It is kept at a thickness. Its thickness is a tolerance, held the way the joints are held, and if you scrape a patch bare you will find that the pointers can lay veil as well as stone, and that within a day the patch is the same colour as everything else, and that you have not damaged anything, you have merely given the town something to do.

In the cool undersides, the grey felt. It is not fungal and it is not not fungal; it has agreed to look fungal the way a diplomatic document agrees to look like a greeting. It is the condenser. The town's breath rises, meets the felt, and comes back as the drip, and the *interval* of the drip — that fixed, patient, cloudless drizzle — is a function of how thick the felt is, and the felt is grazed and tended and held to spec. The weather in Ossary Sink is an organ. It is farmed. Nobody farms it; it is simply the case that the felt is kept at the thickness at which the water comes home on time.

Everything above this line is the animal. Everything below it is the flame the animal stands in.

## The five, close up

**Slaters.** Palm-sized, wet slate-grey, and the shell is not armour. It is a reference surface — a gauge, a straightedge the body carries so it can tell when it is seated. It has no eyes worth the name. It finds its dish the way you find the last step in the dark: by sliding until the arc agrees, and then stopping, and then not stopping, ten million sweeps a day, forever. The teeth are mineral and are shed and are re-dissolved in the seams, because nothing here is permitted to become sediment, not even a tooth.

Lift one. It does not struggle; it reaches. Set it down three dishes away and it fits that one too, exactly, immediately, with no period of adjustment, and this is the thing about slaters that will keep you awake later: *it has no home.* It has a specification. The dish it was in is not mourned, because the dish it is in is the same dish, and identity here is not a history, it is a dimension.

The surplus stands in the seams in ranks. Motionless, packed, waiting on a vacancy. This queue is the only sequence in Ossary Sink, the only place where *before* and *after* have any purchase at all, and a slater in the third rank has, in the only sense available to it, a future: it will move up two. The tense of the slaters is **next**.

**Pointers.** Blind, pale, slug-bodied, and cold to touch — colder than the stone, because the curing takes heat rather than giving it. New calcite is a shade paler for an hour and then comes to colour. You can find your own footprints across the film by trailing a fingertip and feeling for the chill: little cold crescents of repair, rising at the speed of a slow tongue.

They do not distinguish you from damage. Stand still long enough and a pointer will begin to lay a collar of stone around your boot, matching the shape of the intrusion with perfect indifference, because a footprint is a hole and a hole is the only thing in the world a pointer can perceive. There is no hostility in it and no welcome either. Repair is the only verb the caste has. In Ossary Sink, hospitality and erasure are the same operation performed at different speeds.

Their tense is **now**, and it is the only *now* in the bowl.

**Lungs.** Keg-bodied, seated in iron sockets they grew to fit, and if you put your hand flat on a shed wall you will feel the thirteen-second cycle arrive in your sternum before your ear reports it. Eleven thousand of them on one phase. The animal does not *hear* this. The animal is this. It is the sound of the body being a body.

When one fails, the chord does not falter, and the reason is that the replacement was budded days ago and has been climbing the wall ever since. There are always a few in transit — a slow upward trickle of kegs on every elevation, surplus, pre-made, unhurried. The dying lung's last contraction and the new lung's first are the same contraction. There is no gap and therefore no death; there is a splice. Nothing here is ill, either. A failing body is only a seat that will fall vacant slightly ahead of schedule, and the schedule is the only thing that knows.

The tense of the lungs is **again**.

**Count-flies.** The overwhelming part: most of the animal, most of the seats, most of the nine digits. Each fly is a quantum of noticing, and each quantum flies a fixed route — gate, vat, seam, gate — and the sum of the routes is a standing cloud with a shape that does not change, the way a river has a shape that does not change while being nothing but departure.

To be noticed in Ossary Sink is to have a fly diverted onto you. That is all attention is here: a circuit bent to include a body. When you stand in the bowl, some small fraction of the haze leaves its ancient path and loops through the warm wet plume of your breath — because you are a water source, and water is budgeted, and an unbudgeted source must be routed through until it can be reconciled. For those minutes you are not a visitor. You are an organ that arrived from outside. You are a lung nobody grew.

Their tense is **through**.

**Knockers.** Small, hard-shelled, hammer-limbed, on the iron rails, keeping phase. Everyone who comes here assumes they are a clock. They are not a clock; a clock measures something. The knockers are a *call* — the pattern is a summons pattern, the figure that in any other place would mean shift-change, relief, come down, take over, your turn is done.

And the sound goes up out of the bowl and strikes the rim wall and comes back down into the bowl and is counted by the gates as confirmation.

That is the one thing in Ossary Sink addressed to something outside it, and it does not leave. The town calls for relief and hears its own call return and files it as *still correct*. The tense of the knockers is **any moment now**.

## What the number is for

The census counts seats, not lives. Every seat has an address and the address is a number, and the number is punched into calcite ribbon a mile a day and read back and checked against the flies actually passing the gates, and then dissolved.

So the Works says its people's names into a vat. Continuously. In a roll call answered by the roll itself, each body proving it is still at its post by being the thing that goes past the gate when its name is called.

Now look at the precision. Nine digits. The leading ones are flies, and they are effectively constants of nature; they have not moved in the whole history of plate. Eight digits of that number are ballast. The entire apparatus — the reactors, the launders, the ribbon, the gates clicking three a second forever — exists to hold the last digit under observation.

The resolution of the instrument is one body.

It is not a bureaucracy. It is an ear. A mile of ledger a day, manufactured at ruinous effort, so that a single additional creature standing in the bowl would be *detectable*. The animal has built itself a device capable of noticing that someone has come.

## The sixth, who is a hole

In the tallest shed: a stair with dished treads, a handrail worn to a shine, sockets in the wall plugged with cured calcite. No caste fits any of it.

The pointers keep those plugs true. That is worth sitting with. The animal maintains, to a tolerance you could not measure with your thumb, the shape of the absence of its makers. It has no memory — memory would be accumulation, and the sediment budget is zero — so it does the only thing it can do with a past: it holds the hole open at specification, forever, cold, blind, faithfully. Somewhere in the seams are eggs that will not be called, and the moulds that would trigger them are intact, and nothing in the bowl can produce the condition.

Consider what finishing might require. A loop cannot declare itself complete from inside; there is no vantage. Completion is a judgement, and judgement needs a witness who is not part of the thing judged — something with an outside, something that arrives and leaves, something that *erodes*. The builders had that. They went up the ramp before they signed.

And so the Works goes on producing the number, and reading the number, and dissolving the number, and the number is not a record. It is a report held out at arm's length. It is a thing made to be read by someone who is not here.

Meanwhile the handrail is the only surface in the bowl where wear is not compensated, because no mouth sweeps it and no gland matches it. It is the one place a number could grow. It is not counted. Every palm that has ever taken it — the builders' millions, and then the visitors' few — has left a thinness behind that nothing dissolves and nothing reads. It is the guest book of Ossary Sink, and it is written in a script of subtraction, and the town does not know it exists.

## The fifth

You come down the ramp and the chord bends a quarter-tone.

Understand what is happening. Flies leave routes that have been flown since before there was pavement, and take up a new circuit that includes your breath. A number that has ended in 4 for the entire history of plate ends in 5. The gates punch it, read it, confirm it. The ribbon goes into the vat, but for as long as you stand there, the mile-a-day ledger of the Constant Works is saying something it has never said:

*there is someone in the room.*

That is the only news this apparatus has ever produced. Everything else it says is *still correct*. You are the one sentence in the whole output that is about anything other than itself, and it is about you, and it is accurate, and it dissolves.

Then you climb, and the flies go home to their old routes, and the last digit goes home too, and there is no loss, because loss would require a place to put it. Nothing here misses. Nothing here is capable of the second half of missing. What remains is a handrail one palm thinner and a bowl that has demonstrated, at enormous cost, that it can tell when it is not alone.

## What lives there, then

Not a colony. Not a machine with an animal grown into its tolerances. Not, in any way you could defend, a person.

Put your hand into one of the dishes and it fits, and the fit is finished — the wear stopped where the sweep stopped biting, and nothing will deepen it again. Now stand at the rim and look at the whole bowl and understand that it is the same object at a different scale: a hollow, worn to the exact arc of something that is not there. Eleven thousand sockets grown to fit. A stair dished by feet that no longer exist. Eggs held at readiness for a condition the town cannot generate. A nine-digit instrument tuned to a resolution of one arrival. A call for relief, going up, coming back, going up.

What lives in Ossary Sink is a vacancy that has learned to keep itself from closing, and the slaters and pointers and lungs and flies and knockers are how a hole holds its shape. It does not want. It is *shaped* like wanting, the way a handle is shaped like a hand. It has been listening so long that it has become the precise negative of the sound it is listening for.

And once in a while something warm comes down the ramp, and the whole vast spare patient apparatus bends a quarter-tone toward it, and says, correctly, for eleven minutes: *five* — and is not answered, and goes on keeping the shop open.
