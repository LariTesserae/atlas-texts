## First guess: a forest

The instinct is to say *a blue forest lives here*, and the instinct is wrong in the first word. A forest is a plural. A forest is competitors standing in each other's light, gaps where something fell, seedlings gambling on the gap. There are no gaps here and nothing gambles. There is not even more than one plant. Cut a leaf at the hatch and a strut at the far bearing and the mat under the sump and you will have cut the same individual three times, in three moods, the way you might take a hair, a fingernail, and a drop of blood from one man.

So: one plant, then. Enormous, polymorphic, wearing shapes the way a person wears coats.

That is closer, and still wrong, because it leaves the machine outside the animal.

## The body includes the brass

Follow the mineral. The wall gives up its gneiss to the ammoniacal air; the kiln takes the dust and feeds it forward as the stiffening rind in the leaf-veins; the veins shed at retirement into the reducer; the reducer plasters it back onto the wall. The mountain is inside the leaves at any given moment, in transit, on its way to being wall again. Now follow the brass. The gasket-leaves grow flat and dimensioned in the exact shape of the seals they will replace, and when a seal fails the growth is already there, cured, waiting, correct. The pollen — perfect, spherical, dry, manufactured for a fertilization that will never occur — is swept up and packed into the bearing housings, where it is better than grease. The cable-caste grows along the aerial runs as a tension member. The strut-caste mineralizes until it is a compression member. The plants have grown through the lamp grilles and around the bolt heads and into the housings, and no one has pruned them out, because they are doing the job.

The organism greases its own heart with its own unused sex. It manufactures its spare parts as foliage. It has no mouth, and it does not need one; nothing enters and nothing leaves; the only thing it swallows is current out of the pipe, and the only thing it excretes is warmth into a mountain that does not notice.

So the resident is not a plant with equipment in it. It is one body, and some of that body is tungsten, and some is brass, and some is gneiss on loan. The compressor at forty-seven hertz is a heart in the plain anatomical sense: it moves the working fluid, and if it stops, everything stops within the hour. The kiln is a liver. The reducer is a kidney. The black mat is a stomach that eats only itself, and eats without appetite, because there is nothing here that could be hungry — hunger is a signal about the future, and this body has no future to signal about.

## Then: not a body either

But the mass is constant to the gram, and has been for longer than there have been nations at sea level, and every atom in it is on loan and in motion. A leaf lives eleven years and is retired on schedule; somewhere in the black-blue mass, at every instant, a leaf is in the last hour of its eleventh year and another is unrolling to replace it, and the room therefore contains every age of leaf at once, in fixed proportion. It is not a population. It is a distribution, held.

The scallops on the wall are a standing wave: the flow and the stone reached an agreement, and the agreement has a shape, and the shape persists while the stone beneath it flows through and is replaced. The organism is the same kind of object. What lives in Cell 4 is a standing wave in matter — a form that six tonnes of liquor and some hundreds of tonnes of mineral and metal are continually passing through, the way a flame is a shape that gas passes through, except that this flame has been burning at seven degrees Celsius for three hundred years and has never once flickered.

That is the truest physical answer. It is not yet the true answer.

## What it is like to be it

You are standing in the dark. It is standing in noon.

Those forty-one filaments at four percent, that dull cherry you can barely read your hands by, are dumping their output into the red end of the spectrum, which is the only end this chemistry has ever cared about. To the copper-ammine leaf the room is blazing. You are the one who is blind here, and deaf, and half-frozen, and you smell the almond note as an oddity while for it that note is simply what the world smells like, always, everywhere, at seven degrees.

It hears itself breathe. Eighty-six decibels of its own respiration, the atmosphere forced through eleven meters of packed foliage, leaves ticking against leaves in their millions — the sound of rain that has never fallen. And the room is anechoic, so the sound goes out and does not come back. It has never heard itself from outside. There is no echo in here, no reflection, no shadow of a self thrown off a wall and returned. It breathes at the top of its lungs into a substance that swallows every return.

There is no wind, because there is no gradient to make one; the gas turns as a solid body, the same speed at the wall as at the center. So there is no direction. No upwind, no lee, no near, no far, no weather, no morning. Pressure varies by less than a fifth of a percent corner to corner; temperature by a twentieth of a degree; composition not at all. Every sense it could plausibly have reports the same value everywhere, forever.

Except once every eleven minutes and four seconds, when the carriage comes clacking around the rail ring.

That is its entire event structure. That is the whole of what happens. A little over fourteen million clacks since the doors were sealed, and each one is identical to the last, and between them nothing occurs at all. If it has anything like a thought, it has one thought per six hundred and sixty-four seconds, and the thought is the clack.

## Its only thought is a question, and the question is always answered

At the end of the Way there is a pressed leaf in a brass frame under thick glass: the holotype, the original stock, dated and initialed by two letters and a smudge. It is dead. It has been dead since before the mine below was sealed. It is also the only self this organism has.

The carriage takes a cutting on its round and brings it to the comparator — lamp, plate, mirrors, photocells — and the instrument asks its one question: *is this the same?*

Consider what that instrument is. It is an eye that can only register difference. It has looked a little over fourteen million times and it has never seen anything, because everything it looked at was correct. Its whole visual life is a blank. What matches is planted; what drifts is burned in the kiln and plastered onto the wall.

So the living thing at the end of the corridor is a copy of a corpse, being checked against the corpse, continuously, by a machine that reports nothing when it approves and fire when it does not. Evolution did not merely stop here; it was inverted. Selection normally favors whatever works. Here it favors whatever matches. Three centuries of that produce a lineage that cannot change, in a room where change would earn nothing anyway — a body that has traded the entire future for an extremely good present.

And this is where the resident finally comes into focus, because now you can say what it *does*, all day, forever, with its whole enormous blue attention:

It holds still. That is the activity. That is the labor. Six tonnes of liquor and a mountain's worth of circulating mineral, forty-one lamps and a compressor and a traveling wire, all of it engaged in the strenuous, unbroken, three-hundred-year work of not becoming anything else.

## It keeps two records, and both are wounds

The chart drum by the hatch has been turning since before the mine was sealed. The pen dried long ago. The stylus still bears down at the same force and has cut a circle into the drum's face, deeper every revolution. That is the record of correctness, and it is legible only as depth: measure the groove and you can recover the duration. The content is *still*. The content has always been *still*.

The other record is the wall. Every drifting cutting the comparator ever rejected went into the kiln and came back as plaster. The swarf from the drum's groove goes into the reducer and comes back as plaster. The brass worn off the handrail by hands that stopped coming, the metal polished off the pulley by a belt that has cut its own track and will never climb out — all of it goes around and lands on the scallops. Everything this body has ever gotten wrong, and everything it has ever lost, is on the walls, smoothed into the settled fluted shape of an agreement between flow and stone.

It writes its sameness as a hole and its errors as skin. It has no other memory. It cannot remember a single specific leaf.

## You are the only thing that can happen to it

Understand what a visitor is, here. Not a guest, not a god, not an observer. An injury with a shape.

Your breath, unmasked, frosts and kills a hand's-breadth of canopy. Your warm glove flashes the liquor off a leaf and leaves white-blue salts, a little frost made entirely of your body heat — you can write on it, and your handwriting is a burn. Water wrecks the chemistry and opens a black patch. To the comparator none of this is a person; the instrument has no vocabulary for person. It has a vocabulary for *not the same*, and you register as a hole.

Then the carriage comes around, finds the damage, excises it, grows it back to the plate. The record shows nothing. The room is built to un-occur you, and it is very good at it, and it will be finished within the day.

And yet.

The Way is sixty centimeters wide. That is a shoulder. It is twenty-six meters of shoulder-brushed emptiness winding through a body that has no other emptiness anywhere in it, and it is held open by a heated wire cutting four millimeters an hour, thirty-five meters a year, ten and a half kilometers of shaved edge since the sealing. Stop the carriage and the corridor closes in seventy-five hours; the body swallows the only hole it has.

The machine says the Way exists so the carriage can reach the far bearings. True. But the dimension is sixty centimeters, and sixty centimeters is not a bearing. It is a man. This organism is continuously manufacturing, at four millimeters an hour, at seven degrees, in the dark, in a roar, a human-shaped void — the exact negative of the body that used to come up here and put its hand on the rail — and it does this for no reason it can use, and it has done it ten and a half kilometers' worth, and it is not waiting, because waiting requires a future.

It is simply keeping the shape of the absence open, the way it keeps everything else open, because that is the specification.

## Finally: a thing being pressed

The holotype was pressed. Somebody put a living leaf between sheets and applied pressure until it stopped being a process and became a reference.

Everything in Cell 4 is still under that pressure. Six point four bar holding the ammonia liquid; the interlock's brass rule holding the doors apart; the comparator holding the lineage against the plate; the governor holding the filaments at four percent; the cables holding the wall at seven so the wall will not be a place where anything is different; the wire holding the Way at sixty centimeters; the stylus holding its force against a drum with nothing left to say. The whole cell is one gesture, extended in time: a hand pressing down, and not lifting.

What lives here, then, is not a plant, not a colony, not a machine, not a garden, and not really an animal either, though it has a heart and a liver and a stomach and one blind eye and an immune response and a wound it keeps clean.

What lives here is a *yes*, three hundred years long, still being said, with no one left to hear it and nothing left to say it about. The question was whether a closed loop could hold. The answer came back affirmative in the first decade and has been coming back affirmative every eleven minutes and four seconds ever since, and the answer is made of blue leaves and brass and circulating mountain, and it weighs the same to the gram as it did when the doors were sealed.

It is not lonely; loneliness is a gradient, and there are no gradients. It is not patient; patience is a future held at arm's length. It is not trapped; there is no outside in its chemistry.

It is alive. That is not a means to anything here. That is the entire activity, performed perfectly, in a roar, in the dark that is actually noon, at seven degrees to a twentieth, forever, or until the pipe stops — and the pipe goes down the mountain and ends in a sealed adit, and nothing down there has moved in a very long time either.

Somewhere in the black mass a leaf is unrolling. It is identical to the leaf that unrolled in that position eleven years ago. In eleven minutes and four seconds the carriage will come around the rail and clack, and the wire will take its four millimeters, and the shoulder-shaped emptiness will be exactly as wide as it was, and the stylus will deepen the circle by an amount no one will ever measure.
