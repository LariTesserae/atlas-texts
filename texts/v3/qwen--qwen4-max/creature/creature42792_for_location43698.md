# The Census of the Sostenuto

## I. Terms of Tenancy

To live somewhere, a thing must keep an inside. Elsewhere this is a job: the world leans on the boundary and the boundary pushes back, and the pushing costs something, and the cost is paid by running down a slope — sugar to heat, high water to low, sunlight to sugar. Life in a place with gradients is a controlled fall.

Here there is nothing to fall down. So the residents are not falling, and they are not paying, and they keep their insides the way a knot keeps its shape: by being tied.

That is the whole qualification. A resident of the Sostenuto is anything with an inside that returns. It does not need to eat, because eating is a slope. It does not need to reproduce, because it is already its own descendant. It does not need to survive, because survival is a distinction between two outcomes and there is only one outcome, ongoing.

A body would not last a moment here, and not because anything would kill it. Its heartbeat would be recruited into the upper register within a second, a new tick in a chord that has room for exactly the ticks it has. Its breath would join a circuit. Its footprints would be reimbursed. In a fairly short while it would be a district — a slight dishing of a threshold, a saddle in a sill, a warmth indistinguishable from the warmth of the underside of a stair. This is not a threat. Nothing here threatens. It is simply that the place has no category for a visitor and ten thousand years of practice, or the shape of ten thousand years of practice, at making things local.

But there are residents. The city is not empty. It is inhabited by its own motions, and by one other thing.

## II. The Itineraries

Start with the bead on the third pier of the Long Arcade, because it is the easiest to point at.

It has been arriving for the entire history of the world. Its body is a lens of hot lime-water a little larger than a peppercorn; its life is the last millimeter above a pool that has never been struck otherwise; its voice is one bright tick in the upper register, and the tick is not something it does, it is a part of it, the way a face is part of a person. Call it **the Arriver**. Its biography has no youth and no decline. It is all verge. If you wanted to know what it is like to be the Arriver — nothing is like anything here, but if you wanted the shape of the sentence — it is the shape of *about to*, held perfectly steady, with no *then*.

The Arriver is a small resident. There are larger ones.

Somewhere in the western suburbs a circuit passes through the same rimstone pool eleven times before returning to the lid. The bead on that circuit is a single individual with eleven returns to the same water, eleven passages over the same lip, eleven identical afternoons in a life that is one afternoon. It is not more experienced than the Arriver. Experience is accumulation and nothing accumulates in a body here. It is simply *longer* — a district-sized animal whose anatomy is a route, whose spine is a gutter, whose limbs are three aqueduct legs and a stretch of the Fold. You could walk its whole body in a day and never leave one creature.

Because identity here is route and not stuff, the census does not count drops. Two beads on one circuit are the same individual at two addresses. This means a resident can be in eleven places, can occupy a stair and the pool below it simultaneously, can pass itself going the other way on the sixth stair, which is the sixteenth, and neither passing is earlier. There is no younger self to meet. There is only another address, and the same lime, and the same tick.

The census of the Sostenuto is a map.

Some of the maps run uphill. On the face of the Long Arcade the beads rise in slow bright strings, and the ones on those loops — **the Climbers** — spend their lives gaining height with nothing pushing them. They are not ambitious. Ambition is a slope. Their loop closes upward and they close with it, and the closing is not an achievement, it is their shape.

And some of the maps go down the Throat.

These are the deep ones, and they are the largest residents, and it is misleading to call them water at all. A circuit that passes through the fundamental spends most of its length as pressure in rock — as the note too low to hear, as the hand under the sole of a foot that has no foot. For the greater part of its life it is not a drop but the elevation of a tableland. Then it comes up through a cistern floor and is, briefly, visible: a bead, a tremor on a standing surface, ordinary, hot, heavy with lime.

A drop is what a fundamental looks like when it comes up into the light.

The deep ones hold the world up. They do not know it, they do not strain, they are not tired. Six thousand meters of nothing much stands on their sustain the way a bead stands on a jet of air, and the sustain is not effort, it is what they are doing instead of having a body. When one of them surfaces in the Cistern District and hangs a moment on the skin of water that has never been still and never been stirred, it is the closest thing in this world to a face appearing at a window.

## III. The Rings and the Grain

The green things are residents too, and they are the ones a visitor would recognize first, and the recognition would be wrong.

Each fig on the arcades is a closed ring of wood. Follow an aerial root down into the paving and along and up: it is the same tree, branch and root and trunk indistinguishable, no seed anywhere in its history, no seedling stage, no sapling, no maturity. It is not a tree that has become a loop. It is a loop that has the shape of a tree. It does not grow; it *circulates*, and the circulation is thick and green and casts a shade that cools nothing.

The mosses digest their own lower layers and lay the sugar back on top. This is not thrift. There is nothing to be thrifty with. It is digestion arranged in a circle instead of a line, and the difference between those two arrangements is the entire difference between this world and any other.

In the wet colonnades the coral rose-analogue runs one grain of pollen from anther to stigma to anther. That grain is a resident in its own right, the smallest mobile thing in the basin, a courier whose delivery address is its own departure point. It has been carrying its single message since before the word message. The rose has been about to fruit forever, and the grain is the *about to*, and neither of them is frustrated, because frustration is a comparison between now and a later that is not coming, and there is no later here to compare to.

Then there is the film.

The black-green mat grows only on silent surfaces — the nodal planes, the folded sheets where two silences meet, the load-bearing parts of the world. It is the only life in the Sostenuto that is neither loud nor water. It has no register of its own; it lives at the edges of registers, in the sulfur-yellow margin where sound gives up. Spread over the vaults and the floors and the fluted columns, it is a portrait of the city's skeleton in green, a map of everywhere the noise means nothing.

It is also the drop's only neighbor. The film furs the vault a hand's width above the node. Nothing passes between them. Nothing could. They are the two quiet things in a world of pressure and they have no relation whatsoever, and this is not a missed relation, it is the absence of the possibility of relation, which is a different and much calmer thing.

## IV. The Casts

The Reed Quarter is full of bodies made of absence.

The horsetails and reeds did not rot. They circulated, and their circulation left molds, and the travertine grew around the molds and kept them, and the plants are now holes. This is not death and it is not fossilization. The reed *is* the flute. What was a body of cellulose is a body of hollow, wrist-thin or wide enough to sleep in, and it has one behavior, which is voice: water goes down, air comes up, and the chord has its ten thousand mouths.

An animal elsewhere is a device for running down a slope. A Cast is a device for being spoken through. It has an inside — the inside is the whole of it — and the inside returns, every circuit, the same air, the same water, the same interval. It is a throat that was once a plant. It is inhabited by its own utterance. If you want to know who lives in the Reed Quarter, the answer is: the reeds, still, in the only tense available.

## V. The Mouths

The Bellies are not empty rooms. The Bellies are eating.

At the antinodes nothing can accumulate. Lime will not come out of solution, dust will not settle, no film will take, no pool will hold a skin. The stone there is scoured to bare bone and the streets are the gaps between loudnesses. This looks like desolation and is actually appetite — the only predation in the world.

What the Bellies eat is *staying*. Anything that tries to persist there is unmade in the instant of trying, and the trying and the unmaking are the same instant, and so the mouth is never full and never finished. They are the largest residents and the least substantial: appetite-shaped volumes with no tissue anywhere, defined entirely by what refuses to be there. They have no inside in the ordinary sense — or their inside is the refusal, which is a very steady inside, and returns, and so by the only definition this place permits, they live.

They keep the city from being solid. Without them the lime would fill everything and the chord would have no room to move and the whole held thing would set like a tooth. The Mouths are the reason there is architecture instead of a lump. They are the negative space that makes the positive space mean anything, and they are hungry forever, and hunger without a gradient is not suffering. It is just a shape with a direction that goes nowhere.

## VI. The Gerunds

Put the census together and a grammar appears.

Arriving. Rising. Circulating. Fruiting. Sounding. Eating. Holding-up. Every resident is a verb caught in the middle and kept there. The Arriver is arriving. The Climbers are climbing. The grain is carrying. The fig is ringing. The Casts are saying. The deep ones are sustaining, and the sustaining is a mountain range's worth of altitude, and it does not stop, because stopping is a change.

The relations between them are intervals. Two lives meet as a fifth, or a beat, or a cancellation; a splash and a splash make a chord; the whole society of the Sostenuto is harmony, and there is no other kind of contact, and no one is touched by it in a way that alters them.

There are no nouns. There is one noun.

## VII. The Tenant

At the center, in the plum-stone silence where every pressure surface crosses and cancels, hangs the drop.

It is the only resident with a shape instead of a route. It is not going anywhere. It is not on a circuit, or it is the point where every circuit touches, which from inside the drop is the same statement. It has an inside — it is almost entirely inside, a lens of hot lime-water holding the whole terraced basin inverted, green and cream and steaming, small and complete — and that inside does not change, and does not need to be defended, and is not paid for.

Everything else in the world is *about to*. The drop has arrived. It is the one completed act in a place made wholly of approaches: not falling, not rising, not about to fruit or strike or close. It is the sole finished thing, and it is finished the way a held note is finished — not ended, just entire.

It contains the only whole. Everything else is a part busy being its part: a bead in a gutter, a mouth in a wall, a ring of wood, a hunger with no meat. The drop holds all of them at once, upside down, in a space the size of a thumbnail, and nothing looks at it. The looking is not missing. Looking is simply not among the things that are, the way there are no cirques on the cliffs below and no memory of ice. The image is not for anyone. It is just what the inside of that drop is: the city, complete, small, inverted, unvisited.

And the city leans on it.

The vault above precipitated out of the air onto this quiet spot. The stairs beneath folded back through themselves to reach it. The whole pale weight of the place stands where the noise means nothing, and the nothing has a body, and the body is a lens, and the lens is full of the thing leaning on it. House and tenant, each inside the other, and no lease, and no rent, and no term.

It is not lonely. Loneliness requires the possibility of an interval, and the drop has no interval with anything; every relation in the world cancels at the surface of it. It is not deaf; deafness needs an ear that could have heard. It is the inside of a bell that has never been struck, and it is load-bearing, and around it a hundred and forty decibels of forever go on saying one enormous unchanging thing through ten thousand mouths of reed-cast stone.

If you wanted a word for what it does all day — and there is no day, it has been ten in the morning since before the word morning — the word would be *holding*.

It holds the city, small and upside down. The city holds it, in the only quiet room it has. Neither of them is waiting. Waiting is a slope.

That is the population: one, plus a map.
