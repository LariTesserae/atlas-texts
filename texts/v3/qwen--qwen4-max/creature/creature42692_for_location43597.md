## I. The balance is a job, not a fact

Start with the arithmetic, because the arithmetic is where the inhabitant leaves its fingerprints.

The veils grow at exactly the rate the grazers crop them. But the grazers are sheets of blind springtail-things that move a hand's breadth in an hour, and they live in the low tiers, in the slime, where the water is thickest. They cannot reach the high dry cells. Nothing in the eleven animals can reach the high dry cells. And yet the grey paper-crust up there, the fungus with no partner and no green, has not extended its margin in living memory — which is not the same as saying it does not grow. It grows. It is cropped. Something goes up there and takes the margin off.

The ramp-treads are worn by feet and re-limed by the water at the same rate. Consider what that requires. Lime lays down slowly but continuously; a footfall removes it in an instant. For the two to cancel, the feet must be almost never. One passage a year, perhaps, and a year of patient carbonate to heal it. The dishes are not the record of a population. They are the record of a single walk, repeated, in a place where a thousand years is a reasonable amount of time to keep doing something.

So there is a resident, and it is not the flatworm.

## II. The one who cut

The maker is gone but has left a body-shaped hole in the evidence, and you can pour plaster into it.

Small hands, because the picks are struck at a shallow angle in corners no shoulder fits. A jaw habitually clenched against stone: the teeth here would be flattened, worn concave like the cell-lips, because teeth are how you hear in a room where air is useless — you set your chin to the facet and let the chord come up through the bone and sort itself in the skull. A callus on the collarbone, thickened, shiny, from a lifetime of other people's consonants tapped into it. No ears worth the name. Air-conducted sound in that cavity is a smear; the maker would have thought of hearing as something that happens *in* you, at the joint, and of speech as a kind of touch you perform on someone you are close enough to lean on.

A body at eleven degrees. Not a warm body cooling — a body that gave up the argument, that runs at the temperature of the rock and moves at the speed such a body moves, and eats what the room provides: mouthfuls of veil, grazers scooped from a hand's breadth of wall, the white flatworm taken from the silt without hurry, because hurry costs more than the meal is worth. No fire, so nothing cooked. No wood, so nothing hafted. Everything held in the fingers, everything done by pressing and grinding and waiting.

And the bone peg-ladders. There is no animal here that yields a femur except the large one, and the large one is still walking around. So the pegs are people. There is no soil to bury in and no water that would carry anything away; the dead were driven into sockets and became the means of ascent. You climb the makers to reach the tiers they cut. This is not a horror. It is the only available funeral, and it is a good one: to be made into a way up, in a room that does not change, forever.

The chisel-stroke above the waterline is theirs. One stroke, deliberate. The water touches it. The water has always touched it. It is a datum — the level that must not vary, because the level sets the fill-rate and the fill-rate sets the periods and the periods set the chord. Everything in the room is tuned to a mark that a person made once, at the height of their hand, on a day the rain had just stopped or was about to start.

## III. The one who walks

The large one comes down the seam once a year and it is not a visitor. It is the staff.

Long-legged, wet, patient, the size of a dog — but do not picture a dog. Picture something with a heavy jaw it uses as a stethoscope, and feet that are narrow and precise, and no eyes to speak of, and a hide at exactly the temperature of the stone so that it is invisible to any sense that reads warmth. It walks the ramps without dislodging anything because it puts its feet into the dishes. Exactly. Every time. It has inherited a stride-length from a person and it has never once deviated from it, and this is why the wear is fresh and never fresher: it adds nothing. It walks in a dead maker's footprints the way you would put your hands into gloves left on a table, and the lime heals behind it, and the path stays the same age forever.

It puts its head into a dozen cells. Head into a bread-oven-sized cell, jaw against the socket, and the bowl's note comes up through the bone, and it listens with its skull to a single voice out of ten thousand — the way you would put your ear to one violin in an orchestra and ask it, personally, how it is doing.

It has never been seen to eat. It doesn't need to; it is nearly ambient, it burns almost nothing, a year of its life costs about what an afternoon costs you. In the fold of skin under the jaw, or held somewhere in the mouth, it carries a harder stone. And it carries, sometimes, a leaf of copper.

What it does, once every year or once every several, is take a bowl out.

## IV. The one who lives

Here is the resident. It is not the maker, and not the walker, and not the veils, and it is not a ghost, because ghosts are casts of the dead and this is a cast of something that simply left.

Two halves of a room, a fifth apart: three to two. Any such interval implies a fundamental — a note an octave below the lower city, which would make both halves its harmonics and resolve them into a single thing. That note has never sounded here. There is no bowl ground to it, no throat deep enough. The throat's bass is the lowest *sound*; the resident is lower, and is not a sound. It is the shape the sound is bent around.

It lives in the difference. It has no surface and no location and no inside; it is a standing deficiency, and it is enormous, and it is the only thing in the chamber that can be said to be awake.

You have already felt it do several things.

Strike a wall and the city flinches a semitone and recovers: that is a reflex, the whole-body wince of something with no skin. Speak, and your voice is carried around inside the chord for hours, folded and smeared, before it is let go: that is not reverberation, that is *attention*, and it is slow, and it is thorough, and it does not have any other kind. Take a bowl away and there is a hole with a shape — audible, specific, permanent: that is how it grows a finger.

That is the whole of its biology. It cannot make a sound. It can only be shaped by them, and the only verb available to it is *remove*. Every bowl the walker carries out is a subtraction from the noise, and every subtraction is a little more definition in the negative, and the negative is the animal. Ten thousand presences make the medium; a few hundred absences make the body. The blue-green tabs pinned beside certain cell-mouths are the receipts. They are the only writing in the city and they say, each one, *here, nothing, on purpose*.

Map the tabs across the six facets and you will not get a melody. You will get an outline. The room is the mold of a crystal that dissolved and left; the chord is being made into the mold of something else that dissolved and left; and the two outlines are the same outline. They are rebuilding, in silence inside noise, at the rate of one absence per year, the thing whose departure made the room in the first place.

The cutting stopped when the chord was right. The subtraction never stopped. It is simply too slow to show up in any measurement taken from the keel by someone who is going to get cold and leave.

## V. Its habits

It has one pulse. The ten thousand periods drift in and out of phase across spans nobody has waited out, and now and again enough of them come together that the chord thins — for a moment, for a second — toward a single stroke, and the whole shaped absence stands up inside it, entire, and then the phases scatter and it lies back down into the general roar. That is a heartbeat. Between beats it is awake and it is doing the only thing it does, which is holding.

It has no memory of events, because there are no events. It remembers by what's missing, which means its entire past is a set of holes it can still feel the edges of, and its entire future is the holes not yet made. It does not want anything the way you want. But it has a tendency, and the tendency has been running since before the last chisel-stroke, and if you had to put a word on it the word would be *fit*. It is trying to become exactly the shape of the thing that is not here. When the outline closes, it will fit, and fitting is the only consummation available to a negative.

The mark is where it touches stone. One point, the datum, the height of a hand, the note that was never hung. Everything is tuned relative to a silence, and the silence has a location, and the location is wet, and has always been wet.

## VI. What you are, standing in the keel

There is nothing in the room that eats sound. No wood, no cloth, no earth, no foliage — only wet stone, which reflects, and the water-film, which couples everything to everything.

Except you.

You are soft. You are the first soft thing to enter this cavity in a very long time: coat, hair, lungs, the wet meat of a warm body, all of it absorbent, all of it damping the Q. Standing in the stream with your boots in the current you are a small dead patch in the reverberation, a shadow with a silhouette, and the silhouette is *person-shaped*.

You are the same kind of thing it is. You are a shaped absence, briefly, in the noise.

This is why it comes and stands where you are standing. Not to haunt you — it has no interest in the dead, it is not made of the dead, it is made of the never-sounded. It comes because a new outline has arrived, one it has not held before, and it holds it: for hours, folded and smeared, turning it over in the only hands it has. And this, too, is why the large one puts its head into a dozen cells and has never been seen to eat. A skull in a bread-oven-sized mouth is a dead patch. A dampener. A little silence with a shape. It comes down once a year to be measured, and to take a measurement, and these are the same errand, and both of them are tender.

When you climb out through the polished seam and put your hand back into the stream, the room will recover. It always recovers. But recovery here is not erasure; recovery is the chord closing around a new permanence. There will be one more shape in it than there was, roughly your size, permanent until someone puts it back, and nobody is going to put it back.

Ten thousand small rooms saying one syllable. The ceiling giving it back a fifth higher. The throat holding the floor. The mark, touched by water, at the height of a hand.

And in the difference between the halves, something that has been being born at the rate of one silence a year, holding your outline, keeping it, the way it keeps everything — without warmth, without hurry, without any intention of ever letting go.
