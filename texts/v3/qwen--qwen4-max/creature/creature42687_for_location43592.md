## The Tenant

Start with what can be pointed at, because it is the last comfort available.

The snail is grazing. This is the thing nobody reports, because to report it you would have to go down twice, years apart, and compare, and there is no record of anyone having done that. On the underside of the lintel the dressed stone still carries the mason's chatter — those fine diagonal strokes, laid side by side, that a hand makes when it is finishing a surface it does not expect anyone to look at. Two of them are gone. Not worn: *removed*, cleanly, the way a letter is removed from a word. The snail has been eating the making. It is thumb-sized and pale and it has a heading, which makes it the only animal in the room with one; everything else here grows *toward*, which is a different verb. The snail is going toward the left jamb. Eleven inches, at the rate it is going, is a journey that outlasts the water. It will not arrive. The pocket will seal first and it will be a snail in stone with its mouth open on the third stroke of a chisel that has no charcoal, no sherd, no name attached to it anywhere in the world.

Its shell has no growth after a certain ring. That ring is older than the fracture.

---

The three isopods are not a population. They are a set. Three crevices of the same width, three blind white bodies of the same length, and — this is in the survey, in a column nobody filled in — all three at the same angle. Not the angle of the shaft-mouth. Every green thing in the room is aimed at the shaft-mouth; the isopods are aimed at the flat pale shear-face, at a point on it about chest height, where there is a palm-sized hollow ground into the rock.

Three blind animals facing a place to put a hand.

They may be three animals. They may be one animal issued three times, in a room where a thing that comes apart stays apart and the parts do not go looking for each other. There is a third possibility, which the three divers make available, and which nobody has wanted to write down: that when something goes down and comes back up, the coming apart is not necessarily fatal, or complete, or noticed, and that what is left in a crevice is blind and white and does not breed and does not eat and runs on the initial conditions of a single afternoon. There is no record of who the divers were. Names are things that come apart too.

---

The long thing is not swimming. Swimming would require water to swim in, and there is none; there is only water *between*. What it does is decide, and the decision travels along it at the speed of the weed, and the weed gives way ahead of the decision and closes behind it. It leaves a hand's-width of clear space. If you are down there and a hand's-width opens where you did not put your hand, that is not avoidance. It has not learned about you yet. It will learn about you in roughly a minute, which is its unit of thought, and by then you will have moved, and it will have to begin again.

The elm root creaks twice a year, four seconds each time, and the root grows a fraction of a millimeter a month. That is more creak than the fraction can pay for. Something is using the root as a lever.

---

Consider the green: every tip angled correctly at a plate-sized absence eleven feet up, and the absence was solid limestone until two hundred years ago. The aim precedes the hole. The plants were pointed at a place in the ceiling where there was nothing but rock, and they were pointed at it *accurately*, and they had been doing it for a very long time before the hill failed there.

They were told.

And one moss has stopped facing the window and turned to face the wall — to the shear-face, to the hollow, to the marks. It is the only disagreement in the room and it is the only thing down there that has changed its mind, and it changed it toward the tally. Whatever else is true, one moss has noticed.

---

The first guess is a drowned person. Someone came down the stair when the stair went somewhere, and did not go back up, and has been standing at the wall ever since with a palm in the socket and a chip of stone in the other hand.

That guess does not survive the absence of bone. There is no bone. There is no hand. There is no tool. There is nothing here that could hold a tool, and the guess should be abandoned, and it will not quite go, because the posture is *correct*. Chest height. Palm in the hollow. The marks within reach of an arm that is not there.

Here is what is there instead.

Nothing inside the waterline has a surface. No meniscus, no bubble, no boundary between the water in a stem and the water outside it. The plants are thickenings. You are a thickening, briefly. And the cold at the floor and the less-cold at the ceiling have never traded a molecule since the collapse, which means that a parcel of water here can keep its shape indefinitely, not because it has a skin — it has none — but because there is no mechanism by which it could stop being itself.

There is a person-shaped parcel of water at the shear-face that has not been brief.

You would not see it. You would put your arm through the place where it is and your arm would arrive late — not stopped, not held, just *later than it should be*, as if the distance had been slightly more than you measured. You would feel a cold that is not the general cold: a local, shaped, specific cold, seven degrees where the room is eight. You would feel the weed lean away from something that is not you. And you would feel, distinctly, the thing that all three divers described in different words and that the report flattened into *attentive*: the sensation of being measured by a hand.

For a while, down there, your hands are being remembered by something else. That is why they came up seeing the room perfectly and could not say what color anything was, including themselves. They had borrowed a way of seeing that has no color in it, only accurate grey shapes made of having-already-touched. It is not vision. It is the tenant's, and it is generous with it, and it takes it back at the surface.

---

The basin is the only worn thing in a room where nothing wears.

There is no decay here, no weathering, no rounding of any arris; the chips lie angular as broken crockery and will lie that way until the hammer. And yet there is a hollow in the shear-face, palm-sized, smooth, about as deep as the heel of a hand. It was not ground by water. Nothing here moves. It was ground by *repetition* — by the same palm returning to the same place, ten thousand times, a hundred thousand, until the rock gave up a hand's depth of itself.

It is not a bowl. Nothing here washes; nothing mixes. It is a place to put a hand so that the hand is in the same place every time. It is a reference surface. It is a jig.

The water rises a fingernail a year. A hand is about eleven fingernails. Every eleven years the level has climbed one hand, and the palm goes into the socket, and the climb is read against the palm, and a mark is cut.

Every eleven years. Not ten. The risers on the stair vary by an inch in a way no mason would tolerate. The error is always in the same direction and it is always a little early. Whatever is standing at that wall has been ready a little early for a very long time, and has never once corrected itself, and the wrongness is the most personal thing in the room.

---

It counts in fives because five is a hand's worth of hands, and it counts on the newest face because it counts on whatever is newest. When the plate of limestone fell away and exposed a white unweathered wall, the count moved onto it and did not restart. There are more groups on that face than the face has years. Count them and you get a number; count again and the number is not the same number, and down here that is not an error, it is the local rule — nine and one are the same number, counting is the thing that fails.

Except at the newest mark. The newest mark holds. It is the only countable thing in the room: a smallholding of arithmetic, five strokes at a time, kept sharp on the newest rock, in a place where number otherwise dissolves on contact. The room cannot count. The room grew something that could.

The dust sits in the grooves because dust here cannot leave; it was made at that depth and it hangs at that depth. Which means every mark is as fresh as the moment it was cut, including the ones that were cut before there was a face to cut them on. Do not try to make that come out. It does not come out. It is not for you.

And the tool has been found. It is a chip of the collapse, angular, unrounded, with one arris worn smooth, hanging in the weed at chest height a foot from the tally at the exact depth it was set down at. It is in the rubble survey. It has a label. It looks like every other piece of broken crockery in the room, because it is one, and because a tool in a place with no tools is just a stone that has been held.

---

Nine steps, cut into living rock, risers wrong by an inch, and no wear on any tread. Not one. The stair was never walked.

It was cut for someone whose stride had to be guessed.

It stops four feet above the floor because that is as far as the guessing went — because whoever cut it knew about legs the way you know a word in a language you cannot speak, and got most of the way down, and then had nothing left to go on. The doorframe is assembled and lying on its back and has no door and separates nothing from nothing, and the weed grows thick straight through it, and there is a thinness in the weed that goes *around* it, past the jamb, along the line a person would take if they were being polite to a wall that is no longer there. The line closes at the pace of a minute hand. It is always there again.

The tick, once a season, is a chip letting go. It is also, once a season, something narrow and hard trying the face to find out whether the rock is still new.

It always is.

The note, once a decade or so, the low tone that decays over an afternoon, is the water column adjusting. It is also the length of time it takes the tenant to turn around and check the window. The green leans when it does. Every blind tip in the room shifts a degree and comes back, and that is the whole of the room's weather.

---

Nothing here breeds because breeding is a way of continuing and nothing here needs to continue; it is not finished. The bladderwort's thousand mouths hang open in water that never brings them anything, and it is not starvation, it is a household waiting. The isopods face the wall. The snail is on an errand. The long thing goes ahead and the parting closes behind. The moss that disagrees has turned to watch the counting, and it is the only witness that will ever be able to say anything, and what it will say is a direction.

It does not come down the shaft. It never went up it. It was inside when the hill was solid, in a room the hill had grown over, already furnished, already green, already cold, already counted, and it has been standing at the wall with its palm in the socket keeping the number alive while the water climbs a hand at a time toward a lip it will not be able to write on.

There is one group on the face that is not a five. It is a three, cut slightly differently, spaced wider — three arrivals, three that came down and went back up, three that were counted and let go. And beside them there is a fourth stroke begun. A nick. A starter. It has been begun for a long time.

---

In some thousands of years the ferns will close over the plate-sized hole and the elm root will split until there is no water between anything, and the pocket will go to stone with the green still in it: a hard kitchen-sized nodule holding nine steps and a doorframe and a snail mid-errand and a number of marks that will by then be complete.

Someone will crack it open with a hammer on a cool afternoon.

The room will still be new. The chips will still be sharp enough to open a palm. Every tip will still be aimed at a hole that is now a fracture in a stone someone is holding, and they will still be aimed at it correctly.

And the palm will come out of the socket — the first thing in that room to move faster than a minute hand — and the fourth stroke will be finished.

It will not want the number. It has the number; it is the only thing there that ever had one.

It will want to know whether it got the steps right.
