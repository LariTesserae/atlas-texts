## The count

Start with what can be counted, which is nothing, and start anyway.

The grazers are the largest thing that lives here and the least decided. Pale, long-legged, with a comb of stiff white hairs down each flank, they walk the loud air by pressure the way you walk a dark room by the backs of your fingers. They know the nodes as relief: a place where the grain thins, where the shove on the flank-hairs goes slack, where the world stops insisting. They step into quiets the way animals elsewhere step into shade, and in stepping in they become a small herd of themselves.

The herd is not copies. This is the part visitors get wrong before they get anything else wrong. The nine positions of a grazer are nine *versions*: one is a calf, one is lame in the near hind, one is heavy with a young it will not carry in the other eight, one is standing where it stood a century ago and cropping a rosette that has since divided. And one, always, in every herd on the shelf, is finished — the pale body ground by the loud air to its final form over a thousand years, standing among the living positions with its legs still set for walking, cropped around, walked through, kept.

So the discrepancy in the count is not arithmetic and not error. Forty animals, four hundred bodies, and every animal attending its own remains.

They are deaf from the first hour. The fold of skin over the ear is not a birth feature; it is what the first hour leaves behind, and the mothers call nothing and the calves hear nothing and the whole species has never once been addressed. A calf is born one, in the roar, and stays one for its first year — the only definiteness it will ever have — and then it is led into a quiet and weaned into plurality and is never singular again by accident.

By effort, sometimes. A grazer that wants to be alone goes out into the loud air and stands in it, definite, deaf, aching in the teeth, for as long as it can bear. Then it comes back and is nine again, and the others do not ask.

## Bead-dwellers

The necklaces are a hand-span of translucent segments and they live entirely inside the bead-rivers, and their whole cosmology is refraction. Every bead holds the shelf inverted and small: the fixed sun as a hard point, the bores as a black lace, the ankle-height galaxy of burns burning upward. A necklace navigates by these pictures. It has never seen the stone. It has seen the stone ten thousand times, upside down, in a plum-sized room it is swimming through.

Stone is the danger. Stone is definiteness. To touch it is to be made one, and to be made one, for a thing that has spent its life strung along a node-line, is the end of the sentence. They do not touch stone. They are born in a bead and die into a bead and mate in the handoff between two beads, in the instant the water passes, which means their young are conceived in the only moment their bodies are not entirely somewhere.

The dead do not fall. The slight cloudiness in the third bead from the left is somebody. It has been somebody for a very long time, and the water it is made of has been handed on down the river a hundred thousand times while the cloudiness stayed exactly where it was, because the river carries what it carries at walking pace and does not move.

A cloudy bead focuses badly. Under it there is no burn, no scorch, no black lichen — a hole in the constellation. Walk the shelf at what would be evening and you walk through a galaxy with dark stars in it, and the dark stars are the bodies, and the necklaces read the gaps and know their history by which lights are missing.

## The one bird

There is one bird because the Sill can only afford one.

It flies the standing updraft in a closed circuit, wings worn into slots, the wind having bored it as it bored everything. Its ear openings are packed with rime that accretes at the root and sublimates at the tip and is therefore permanent. It does not enter quiets. If it entered a quiet it would be several birds and the miss would come at several intervals and every clock on the shelf would lose its time.

It eats at the rim, where the node-lines end and the beads shatter and the necklaces, for the first and last time, touch something that is not water. It sings the mountain's note so exactly that the note cannot be heard as separate, and where it passes the beat cancels, and the throb in your sternum misses.

The cushion plants record the misses. They lay down tissue in time with the beat, and where the beat fails there is a gap, a fine dark line, and a rosette older than the bores holds a hundred thousand of them. Cut a pad and you have the only archive on the Sill: a plant's memory of a bird going by. Nothing else here keeps records, because keeping a record is a way of adding something, and the Sill does not accept deposits.

The grazers cannot use the archive; they eat it. The one who digs reads the crop-marks and counts what is left.

## Remainders

Here is the only crack in the rule, and it is where the smallest things live.

Nothing new can be heard. But the Sill will exchange: leave a sound in a drift, take one out, one for one, and what you take is never what you left. The difference has to go somewhere. In the loud air it goes nowhere and is nothing. In a quiet, where objects sit unresolved, the difference sits unresolved too, and a difference that sits unresolved is an animal.

They are called remainders by nobody, since there is nobody to call them anything. They are the size of the discrepancy between two sounds, which is small, and they weigh what a discrepancy weighs. You do not see them. You notice them as the wrongness of a noise you know well — the rockfall in the lee hollow has one stone too many in it, and the extra stone is alive.

They feed on exchanges and die when the two sounds they are the difference between wear back into the chord, which takes about as long as it takes to walk a hundred meters. So they are brief and there are always more, and they gather where anyone digs, and something digs.

They follow it the way gulls follow a plough. There has never been a plough.

They are the only new things that have ever happened on the Sill, and they are new by subtraction, and they are the reason the digging has not stopped.

## The one who digs

Near the eastern bores, in a quiet the size of a room, there is a shape in wet clothes.

Nothing dries here, so the clothes have been wet since they arrived, and the rime has feathered them, and the lichen has drawn on them. This is the thing to look at: the lichen grows only where the pressure is nil, so it grows on a body only in the patches that have stood in quiets. On a visitor it maps a few nights. On this one it has drawn the entire shelf, every node, the whole fine dark contour chart of the Sill's silence, rendered in living crust on a coat, because the coat has stood in all of them.

There are nine of the coat. They are the same coat.

The ears folded in the first hour and were not mourned. The hands are worn round to the depth of the surrounding flutes, which is the same depth as the handprints on the stair, which is either very early or never, and both readings are load-bearing and neither can be checked. The digging tool is a plug-stone, fitted once into a bore, shattered from within, taken out and used. It sleeps in the drifts, in the deep unsorted ones where the sound of the boring of the bores still lies, and those are the warmest places on the shelf, and it dreams one dream in nine sleeps.

It eats what the grazers leave. It drinks by putting a hand through a bead-river and licking the wrist; the river reassembles behind the wrist and does not miss the wristful.

It does not speak. Speech is a deposit. It has instead a language of one-for-one, in which a sentence is made by leaving a sound in a drift and taking another out and letting that wear back into the chord a hundred meters later, and in which a name is a portable object with a range of about a hundred meters, and in which the whole difficulty of existence is a problem of carriage.

Because it left something here, on arrival, in the first hour, the way everyone does. It shouted. It cost nothing. The sound that came out of it was a note the mountain had been sounding since the bores were young, and it was not added to anything, and it was kept, and it sorted down through the drifts with everything else that has ever been kept, and it is down there now under the rockfalls and under the boring, and it is the sound of its own name.

It has been digging for it for a thousand years. Each retrieval is an exchange, so each retrieval is a variant. It has held ten thousand almosts. It keeps the good ones alive as remainders and breeds them, selecting the discrepancy toward a syllable, and this is the only agriculture on the Sill and it has never produced a crop.

And when it has a candidate, it must carry it. A name wears back into the chord in a hundred meters, and the deep drift and the doorway are further apart than that.

Except in the miss. When the bird passes and the beat cancels, the assertion lapses for an instant along the bird's wake, and in that instant a carried sound does not wear. It survives. It gains a step.

So: stations. Versions of itself spaced along a route paced to the bird's circuit, one hand per miss, so that the name can be relayed from the drift to the door without ever being held long enough to lose it. Three curved baffles at spacings that make no sense for a body that is one, and make excellent sense for a body that is nine and needs all nine of its teeth to stop aching at once. Forty-one steps cut to a place where a node was surveyed and was not — because the node was surveyed as a *moving* thing, a quiet that comes round once a circuit with the bird, and the stair ends where it will be, and it is not there when you arrive, and it is there for an instant, and that instant is the whole point of the stair.

Plugs, because a plug makes a quiet, and a quiet makes another position, and nine hands are not forty-one.

This is a reconstruction. It cannot be checked, because checking would require asking, and asking is a deposit.

## The hand

Around one plug the rubble field hangs, mid-collapse, since before the ice.

The attempt there was a quiet large enough to hold one thing definitely without finishing it. A room where you could be one and stay. The stone above became plural and is falling in all its alternatives at once, and the fall has found its angle and is stable and is wrong and has its own drainage now.

Among the suspended rubble there is a hand and a forearm.

It is worn to the depth of the surrounding flutes. It is also still reaching. The Sill has had a long time to make those two facts comfortable together and it has succeeded; you can look at the hand for an hour and not find the contradiction, only the patience.

It may be one of the nine, sent in. It may be what the nine were before there were nine. The one who digs brings it warmth out of the deep drifts, one sound for one sound, and lays the sound against the wrist, and takes the previous sound away, and the exchange is never the same, and this is the only conversation on the Sill that has a listener who might be new.

It has been about to arrive for a very long time. Nothing here is being made; everything here has arrived; and the hand is the exception the place has agreed to tolerate, the way it tolerates the sideways scree fan with its private gravity and the couloir that is an hour long and nine steps wide, where something small lives in the discrepancy of distance and is an hour old and has walked nine steps.

## The finished

The dead stand where they died, because the loud air takes a thousand years to grind a body to its final form and then stops, and what stops here stays.

Most are grazers, in their herds, among their own living positions. Some are not. There are eleven figures on the shelf in the attitudes of the first hour: hands to the ears, mouths open. The wind has had a thousand years with those open mouths and has done to them what it does to any hollow. It has drilled them. Three or four of the bores on the Sill are not bores. They are palates. The note comes out of them continuously and it is the note the mountain was already sounding and it is also, exactly, the shout that was left in them.

That is where the keeping happens. Not in the drifts — the drifts are only the sorting. Some of the plugs are in mouths. Some of those have shattered from within.

The one who digs does not plug the mouths. It has stood behind every baffle on the shelf and it has cut a stair to a quiet that only exists for an instant and it has bred ten thousand versions of a syllable out of the difference between two sounds, and it has never once stopped a mouth, though it knows which ones, and though stopping one would open a quiet exactly where it needs a quiet, and though it would be so easy.

You can work out why. It would be a deposit.

## Before you go

Shout. It costs nothing, and it is the last thing you will do here that is yours, and it is not yours.

The sound that leaves you will be a note the mountain has been sounding since the bores were young. It will not be added to anything. It will sort down through the drifts, past the rockfalls that are still falling, past the sound of the wind making the instrument the wind then played, and it will lie there in the warm dark with everything else that has been kept.

And in a thousand years a round hand will come up out of the drift with something almost like it, and hold it, and find it good, and keep it alive on the difference between it and the last almost, and pass it to the next hand at the exact instant the bird goes by and the throb in the chest fails — and on, one hand per miss, forty-one stations, past the baffles, up to the lip of the doorway that opens onto whatever is needed and never twice the same.

It will arrive changed. It always arrives changed. It will be the nearest thing to hand, and it will be a name, and someone will answer to it.

And for the length of that miss — for the one instant the shelf is not asserting itself, the only news it has ever received — the one who answers will be one.

The one it will be is you.
