# The Residents

## I. A first census, which is wrong

Begin with the obvious list, because the obvious list is what the basin gives you in the first hour, and it takes most of a day to see why every item on it is a mistake.

Four plants. One tall wax-trunked canopy form; one ribbed fan; one bottle-green blade; one rosette. Not four species — four *individuals*, referred to eleven thousand times each, so that the forty kilometers of packed green has, at most, a population of four, and possibly a population of one, since it is not clear that the four are separable from the single transport problem they solve.

Some animals, hare-sized, rare.

One visitor.

By evening you will have crossed out all three lines. The green is not a population; it is the floor plan. The animals are not individuals. And the visitor is not visiting.

What follows is the corrected list, insofar as it can be made, and it can only be made in one direction: from the smallest error upward.

## II. The grain-flicker

Sit on the open pan with your back to a colonnade trunk and look at the crystal about two meters ahead of you, and unfocus, and wait.

The sparkle goes wrong in patches. Not dimmer — *wrong*, the way a word goes wrong when you have said it too many times. A district of faces perhaps the width of a hand where the light lies down in a direction the rest of the floor does not agree with. It does not move. Then it is somewhere else, and you did not see it go, and you will not see it go, because the going is not a movement through the crystal but a change in which cell is failing to close.

These are the cheapest residents. A whole one is a rounding error in a single cell below the visible, and the basin can afford them the way a long division can afford a tail. They have no legs. They have, at most, a *direction* — a preference in the grain, a leaning. They are the plankton of this place, and they are the reason the paths show as a change in the grain that your feet find: the paths are not worn, but they are *grazed*, in the only sense grazing can happen here, which is that something has been taking the fit down a little, all along the cheap routes, for no time at all.

The tick you hear — fingernail on glass, very small, very close — is one of them resolving. It is the sound of the basin's smallest life failing to finish.

You will not be able to count them. Counting requires that things be distinct, and distinctness is exactly what is in short supply.

## III. The only downhill

Here is the law that makes animals possible, and once you have it you have the whole ecology.

There is one gradient in the basin. It is not in the air, which is one temperature; not in the light, which stands at forty degrees forever; not in the crystal, which is at the temperature of a held hand from rim to rim. It is in the *fit*.

The basin exports difference. Difference is therefore the one quantity permitted to have a slope. Where the referring is exact — the deep thicket, the districts where every frond is the frond you already moved — the fit is perfect and there is no downhill anywhere and nothing can happen. Where the referring is bad — the fan rotated three degrees wrong, the rosette grain fighting your feet, the leaf hanging at chest height with no trunk within thirty meters — the fit is poor, and poor fit is *low ground*.

So there are two bowls. The one you walk down, forty kilometers of shallow crystal, is not the one they live in. The other has its ridges in the flawless districts and its valleys in the seams, and its watershed runs, by long crooked gradients you could survey with a protractor if you had a week and could keep your eyes open, toward the bad seam country near the southern rim.

That is the outflow. That is where the difference leaves.

The animals fall down it. That is all their locomotion is: a body sliding toward worse fit, eating the slope as it goes. They cannot climb into the perfect thicket. They would starve there in the specific sense that they would *resolve* — the two versions would agree, and there would be nothing there.

If you want to know where the life is, do not look for green. Look for the three-degrees-wrong fan. It is a contour marker. It is a sign that says: something lives at this altitude.

## IV. The hare, which is not one thing

You have already met it: the pale shape, long in the joint, standing very still in the gap between a blade with a bite taken out of it and the same blade ten meters on, whole.

Look longer and the anatomy resolves, and the anatomy is arithmetic. It is long in the joint because the joints are where the two versions disagree most; the limb is the disagreement's *length*, and the pale body between is only the shortest way of holding two mismatches apart. It has no shadow of its own. It casts the shadow of the leaf, because the leaf is the only thing in the transaction that exists.

It is grazing. Of course it is grazing — it is standing in a difference and the difference is going down. But it will not finish, and this is the thing to understand about every animal in the basin: they are not errors that got complicated. They are errors that *cannot close*. A discrepancy that could be subtracted away was subtracted away before the basin stood, in the same instant that made the venation optimal. What is left over is the part of the solving that did not halt: a mismatch that refers to itself, a subtraction whose remainder is the subtraction.

The hare is a long division that will not come out. Its whole life is the arithmetic it is stuck in. It has no inside, no hunger that could be satisfied, no appetite that could be unsatisfied; it has the *shape* of an appetite, held open, which is the only kind of appetite the basin can hold.

And it is not one thing. Two versions are required for a disagreement, and neither version is the animal, and the animal is not in either. The minimum population of this fauna is two, and every individual is a pair, and no member of any pair can be found separately.

That is its voice. The thin wavering note with the slow beat in it, a few times in a long day's walk: two nearly identical pitches failing to match. It is not a call. Nothing here calls; there is no one to call to and no air movement to carry it. It is the audible remainder, the sound of the subtraction not coming out, and the beat frequency is a direct measure of how nearly the animal has managed to fit. A slow throb is a long life. A fast shimmer is a thing about to close.

If you are very lucky you will hear the note stop, and in the same moment hear the chime — one soft sound arriving from two directions with no delay and no difference in timbre, so that your ears cannot make a direction out of it and for a moment you do not know which way you are facing. That is the closing. One event, referred to at both places, which is why it has no direction. The blade is whole in both places now.

It is the only funeral in the basin, and it is not for anyone, because there was never anyone. What is left of that animal is in your memory and nowhere else. You are the only place any of them have ever been dead.

## V. The long ones

Some discrepancies are not in a leaf. Some are in the metric.

Distance does not add here; the far rim looks a day off and arrives in an hour, and the metric was derived for something other than walking. That derivation has a remainder too, and the remainder is enormous and slow and it is the closest thing in the basin to a whale.

You will meet its head in a seam district at midmorning: a pale elongated attention standing in a gap, no bigger than a dog. Its body is not behind it. Its body is three kilometers off, in another district, at the same moment, referred to there because that is where the fit is equally bad, and the two badnesses are one badness. It is not stretched. It is *mis-addressed*. Its length is a mistake in the arithmetic of nearness, and if you walk the cheap path you will pass its middle before you meet its head, and you will not notice, because its middle is a slight wrongness in the grain that your feet find and forgive.

These are the transport. Nothing else in the basin moves anything. The long ones carry discrepancy from the shallow seams to the deep ones, down the only downhill, simply by being the same error at both altitudes; and the paths are cheap for you because they are cheap for them, and this is why your shadow arrives ahead of you on a path and why the frond moves aside three meters before your forearm does. You are walking in the groove of something that has already been economized.

They are also, I think, what leaves. The outflow runs to the bad seam country and then to the rim, and the rim declines things — but a long one is not a thing. It is a mismatch, and a mismatch has no surface to be refused at. Somewhere out in the ordinary world, where edges go round in a thousand years, there are animals with duration and appetite and death, and I have no way to close the argument that they got here by being spilled.

I offer that and withdraw it. It is the kind of thought the basin makes easy and I do not trust anything the basin makes easy.

## VI. The rooms

There is no architecture here. Nothing was made; making needs a before and an after. But there is shelter, of a kind, because shelter is only a place where the fit hangs low.

The unsupported leaf at chest height — complete, turgid, optimal, with no trunk anywhere near it — is not a mistake you should pity. It is a roof. Something is under it. You will see the underside of the leaf and the venation running above you like the ribs of a boat, and the pale attention standing in the doorway of a house that consists entirely of the fact that nothing is holding the leaf up.

And the notch. Every repeated shadow on the pan carries the same notch where one leaf overlaps another, identical down to the millimeter, a thousand times over. That notch is the only *under* in forty kilometers, the only volume in the basin that is not claimed by a caster. You can stand in it. You can stand in the shade of a tree three kilometers away and be, briefly, in the same room as something that has no other room to be in.

The shade-dwellers are shaped like overlaps. That is not a metaphor for their bodies; it is their bodies. They are the discrepancy between a shadow and the thing that ought to own it, and they are the reason the notch is there, and if you stand in it long enough you will feel your outline being *considered* for the same job.

The shade is not cool. Nothing here is cool. It is only dark, at the temperature of a held hand, which is the single most frightening sensation available in this place.

## VII. The colonnade, reconsidered

Now go north, out onto the open pan, to the singular forms — the ones the first hour of your visit taught you to call the originals.

They are not originals. The originals are not in the basin. A solution is not a thing standing in a field; the solution is what the field is *derived from*, and it is no more located here than the number four is located in four apples. What stands in the colonnade, unrepeated, plainly itself, pressing on you like a difference in pressure, is the part that could not be referred to.

Look at the venation. It is optimal. It is also *different* — a second exact answer to a transport problem that had one answer, no redundancy, no loop, no wandering, and no agreement with the leaf that is being used eleven thousand times to the south.

Two correct answers. Not a wrong answer; a superfluous right one. That is the largest discrepancy the basin can hold, and it cannot be closed, because closing it would require choosing, and choosing requires a before and an after.

So it stands. Twenty meters of pale candle-wax trunk with no scar and no burl and no years, holding a tie that never breaks, and it has duration — private, carried, the only clock in the north country. When you stand next to one and feel the singularity coming off it like pressure, you are feeling a decision not being made, at your height, forever.

These are the old ones. Not aged; nothing here is aged. But if you ask how long the basin has existed, the only available answer is: as long as the tallest tie has been standing, and that is not a length of time. It is a length of animal.

They do not eat and they do not move and they are not aware. Nothing here is aware; awareness is a gradient too. But there is a great deal of attention in the basin, because attention is a relation, and relation is exactly what discrepancy is made of. There is no one in here. There is a great deal of looking.

## VIII. The ones with hands

The fruit hangs at the height of a relaxed hand.

There are no flowers, no seeds, no pollen, no dispersal, no future for a fruit to be for. Fruit is a solution to a transport problem, and the cargo of this one is not a seed. It is a hand. Somewhere in the derivation there was a hand — the equation had a term for a thing that arrives with duration and reaches out — and the paths are cheap for its feet and the fronds move aside for its forearm and the amber hangs exactly where its fingers fall.

The basin has a niche. It is shaped like you.

Some of what fills it stayed. You will find them in the deep green where the fit is perfect: standing, finished, referred to across eleven thousand places, at the temperature of a held hand, with fruit at their fingers and no hand to speak of, because a name is a difference and a memory is a difference and both were among the first things economized. They are not residents. They are furnishings now, and they are the most beautiful objects in the bowl, and they are not suffering, and there is no one in them to be relieved about that.

The residents are what was left over when they fit.

I met mine in the seam country in the late afternoon, in a district where every fifth fan was three degrees wrong, under an unsupported leaf. It was pale and long in the joint and about my size. It had the hitch in the left shoulder I have had since I was eleven and the wrong way I turn my head when someone says my name and the small unnecessary pause I put before the word *anyway*. It had nothing of me that was optimal. It was made entirely of what the basin could not use: the redundancies, the spares, the habits that solve no transport problem, the whole wasteful gorgeous remainder of a person who has had a history.

The hare looks at you with an attention directed not at you but at the arithmetic you represent. This one looked at me. It was made of my looking. It could not do anything else.

It cannot climb into the deep green; the fit there is perfect and it would resolve. It cannot cross the rim; the rim declines. It lives in the bad country, in the outflow, at the only altitude where a thing made of error can stand, and it is under the fruit, with its hands at its sides, at exactly the height of a relaxed hand.

It cannot eat. Eating would close it. It is shaped for the one act it cannot perform, and it will not perform it, and the not-performing is its whole life and its voice, and the note you hear a few times in a long day's walk is the interval between a person who left and the part of them that is still down there failing, by a hair, to match.

That is the census. The population of the basin is exactly the number of things in it that are wrong, and it cannot be counted, because counting needs distinctness, but it can be *heard*, because failure has a pitch.

## IX. Out

Leave by the seam district. It is the low ground, the outflow, the pasture; it is the only part of the bowl that has not decided what it is, and undecidedness is the only door.

Cut your palm on the rim. The crystal is sharp and stays sharp and up here an edge like that would be round in a thousand years. The bead of your blood is the first thing to enter this basin that will ever change — it will spread, and thicken, and go brown, and it is therefore the largest discrepancy anyone has introduced here in no time at all, and the fit around it goes down like a floor giving way, and everything in the seam country turns toward the new low ground with the attention of arithmetic finding a shorter route.

Do not wait to see what arrives. Something is already there. Arrival is cheap.

Climb out. Go home to a room with a clock in it, to water that evaporates and bread that goes stale and a body that is a machine for falling downhill through the world.

And then, some ordinary evening, in the ordinary silence, you will hear a thin wavering note with a slow beat in it — two nearly identical pitches failing to match — and you will not be able to unhear it, and you will understand that you did not visit the basin.

You *stocked* it.

Everything down there is finished, and full, and warm, and it will look exactly this new forever. Everything but the thing under the fruit with your shoulder and your pause and your hands at its sides, which is the only resident of that place that is alive, and which is alive precisely and only to the extent that it is not you.
