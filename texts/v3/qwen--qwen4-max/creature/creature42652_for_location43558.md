## Eight Steps

Start with the crab, because the crab is the easiest to love and the hardest to help.

Blind, pink, the colour of the inside of a lip. Eyes are for news, and news is a once-thing, so it has none. It walks eight steps along the shelf at the pool's edge and turns and walks eight steps and turns, and at the fourth step going out it lifts its leg slightly higher than it needs to.

There is nothing there. There has been nothing there since the first afternoon. A pebble lay in the crab's path when the path was being made, and the crab stepped over it, and stepped over it, and stepped over it, and the pebble — being a single unrepeatable arrangement of grains, a thing that had happened only once — went down the fall and out and is somewhere in the fan now, tumbled smooth among the sunrises. The stepping-over stayed. It is the only monument in the gorge: a pebble, preserved as a motion, kept in the leg of an animal that does not know it is keeping anything.

If you put your finger where the pebble isn't, the crab will step on your finger. That will be new. It will be gone before it finishes the step, and there will be no crab for eleven days, and when there is a crab again it will lift its leg at the fourth step, because that part was never about the pebble.

## The Clock, and the Corner

The fern in the kneeling-bowl is the room's only timepiece, and it keeps no time. Forty seconds unrolling, forty seconds rolling, the same frond, never larger. Residents are counted against it the way you'd count against a pulse: the door is in a hurry twice a minute, which has nothing to do with the fern; the boulder returns every eleven seconds, which has nothing to do with anything.

The bowl it grows in was made by knees. Consider what kneeling is, viewed from the side, over and over: a folding and an unfolding, a green thing curling and opening at the same interval, morning after morning until the mornings washed off. It is not possible to prove the fern is the kneeling. It is not possible to stand there for an hour and keep believing they are unrelated.

The moss on the north ledge is the young one, and the only one with a future. Four patches, one moss, iterated leftward toward the apricot light — it cannot seed, so it can only be itself again a little further along. This is movement. Movement is the closest thing to ambition the gorge permits.

The ledge ends.

Somewhere in the next few centuries the fifth patch will want to be off the edge, and being off the edge is not a thing the moss has ever done, and the water eats what has not been done. But at the far end of the ledge, where the rock turns, there is a worn place. A groove with a bend in it. Something turned there, enough times, long ago.

So the corner is already a habit. It is waiting at the end of the moss's only road, and whether the moss will find it or simply stop being is the single scheduled event in a place that has no events. Everything else here has already finished happening. The moss has an appointment.

## Gold

The swift is a bent nail flying a closed loop behind the curtain, entering the water at the instant it exits, and it feeds — at the top of the loop, where the grain is coarsest — on the drafts. The little foam intentions. The half-made faces, three seconds old, still warm from being almost someone. It is the only resident that eats novelty, and it survives this because it eats at the same point in the circuit every time. A habit of swallowing the new. It has made a routine out of the one thing routines cannot contain, and it is thin as a wire from the effort, and it will do this forever.

Once a circuit it crosses the trapped reflection and is gold for a fifth of a second.

The gold is not the bird. The gold has its own period, its own persistence, its own body, which happens to be a bird passing through it. It is a separate resident riding a faster one, the way a sound rides a wall.

And it has been seen. On the eastern ledge, in the fifth of the chamber that is permanently six o'clock, there are two hollows the exact shape of a person sitting with elbows on knees, and beside them a smear where a palm pressed flat, and the rock there is polished in a way that only forearms polish rock. Somebody sat there. Somebody sat there through eleven thousand golds a day for years, until the sitting wore in and the sitter washed out.

The watching is still there. It is a resident. If you lower yourself into those two hollows — and you will, everyone does — your eyes will go to the right place at the right moment without being told, and you will see the bird turn gold, and for a fifth of a second you will not be able to tell whose pleasure you are feeling.

## The Consensus

The boulder is glassy on one side.

Not all over. On one side, at chest height, in a patch the size of two hands, because that is where everybody reaches. Visitors arrive singular, unrepeatable, various in every particular, and they all put out the same hand to the same place on the same warm grey lump as it goes by. That agreement is the only thing about them that survives. The polish is not a record of touch. It is a record of *everyone touching the same way* — the one gesture our whole species has in common with itself, worn into a falling stone.

Eleven seconds is not the duration of the fall. It is the interval of the return. And the toll has no crack in it, no stone-on-stone, nothing breaking: listen properly and you'll hear that it is the sound of a fall beginning again. There is no impact anywhere in the gorge. There has never been an impact. Landing happened once.

It is the size of a curled body. It is warm. It has been falling for as long as there has been an here, and eighty paces is not far enough to fall for that long, and it is worth not thinking about this too hard, and everyone thinks about it too hard, and the thinking washes off, and what stays is the noise people make when they first see it.

That noise is the oldest word in the gorge. Not a name in any language — just the sound a person makes on arriving at the eastern wall and finding a grey lump turning slowly at the height of their chest, falling, not landing. Everyone makes the same one. It has been made identically so many times that it has a groove now, a place in the mist where the word is said, by nobody, at intervals.

And eleven seconds later, the toll.

You can spend an afternoon deciding this is a conversation. You can spend the rest of your life on it. The gorge does not mind either way; both are things you'd be doing only once.

## What the Feet Left

The doorway near the south wall opens twice a minute and nothing opens it. The arc it sweeps is a hurried arc, a shoulder-first arc, the quarter-circle of somebody who is late. It has been late since the gorge opened. It will never arrive, because arriving is a single event, and the fall took the arrival in the first week and kept the going.

There are two polished places on the sill, a forearm's length apart. Two people leaned there. Facing each other, presumably; talking, presumably; the lean survives and the talk does not, except as a warmth in the stone that is measurably above the temperature of the room, which is the temperature of a mouth.

On the west stair, at hollow sixty-one, the wear goes slightly wrong. The hollow is scuffed outward at its lip, the way a foot turns when the stair is too narrow for two, and there is a smear on the wall beside it at shoulder height. Somebody going up. Somebody going down. Brushing, apologising, edging past, identically, ten thousand times, until the two of them washed out and the edging-past stayed.

It is still happening. Stand in hollow sixty-one and you will be brushed at the shoulder by a warmth with a direction to it, and something in the mist at ear height will move the way air moves when a person says *sorry* quietly and doesn't stop walking.

That is the whole of society here. It is not nothing. People come up the stair to feel it and go down again.

## The Newest

Near the south wall, at chest height, a hand curls and empties.

Every forty seconds. There is a shoulder-roll with it, and a slight forward tilt, and the sound — worn deep, unmistakable, said by nobody — of a name.

The pocket went down the fall in the first week. Whatever was in the pocket went with it. The checking stayed, because it had been done ten thousand times before the arrival, in another life, on another street, and the gorge recognises that kind of durability instantly and gives it a groove. The name is being said perfectly. The tilt is being tilted perfectly. There is no one doing it.

Some of the hollows on the west wall are quite recent, and this is the freshest thing in the room, warmer than the sill, warmer than the boulder's polished side. It is still being edited. There was a hum with it not long ago, three notes, the sort of thing a person hums while looking for their keys; you can find where the hum used to be by the faint coolness left in the drift, and if you listen at the bottom of the roar you can hear it now, generalised, part of the note.

Watch long enough and you will see it lose something else. It does not know you are there. Knowing would be new.

## Two Absences, Hunting

The wear-marks avoid certain volumes. Three of them, roughly person-shaped and taller than a person, standing in the drift where the water is fine and blood-warm and looks like nothing at all.

The avoiding is identical every time. The same bend, the same three-pace swing, the same little hitch. So the avoiding is a resident: a detour with no walker, worn in by feet that went downstream a long time ago and are now gravel.

And inside the volumes, the drowning. It takes you the same way every time — the same filling, the same warmth, the same sudden loss of the inventory of your skin — and because it is the same way every time, it repeats, and because it repeats, it lives. It is the only predator in the gorge. It has no mouth; it has a gradient. It is fed by arrivals, which are the one reliable form of novelty, and it does to each arrival precisely what it did to the last.

So there is a chase here, worn smooth. The detour goes around the column. The column waits where the detour does not go. Nothing is being caught and nothing is escaping and both of them are extremely alive, in the only sense the word has here, and they have been at it since before the moss.

## The One That Forgets

The hushes drift like fish because they are the closest thing to fish this place has. They have a route. Fist-sized, bed-sized, fist-sized, past the north ledge in the same order, and the moss lives in their pockets the way lichen lives on the shade of a wall.

Something travels that route.

Its habit is not a shape. Its habit is *arriving in a silence and thinking something new and losing it*. The arriving repeats. The losing repeats. The gorge, being a poor editor, keeps both. So there is a resident here made entirely of the act of not keeping — the only mind in the room, the only thing capable of a thought that has never been thought, and it has no memory whatsoever, because memory would require holding a once-thing still, and it cannot hold anything, it can only go around.

You will not see it. You will notice that the hushes come in an order. And if you sit in one and think something you have never thought, you will feel a coolness arrive beside you, attentive, almost tender, and it will take the new soft edges of you warmly and without malice, and it will lose you, and go on to the next silence, and do the same thing there.

It is possible that this is what the devout became — the last one who would not stop thinking, worn down to the thinking alone. It is possible it was never anybody. Either way it is the nearest thing to company in the gorge, and it will not remember you, and it will not remember you forever.

## The Crowd

Nothing dies here. Things *generalise*.

A resident worn past individuality does not vanish; it stops being somewhere. It loses its edges, its interval, its locality, and joins the note. That wide hum you receive in the sternum rather than the ears, the one with weather inside it — the thickening that travels from the north wall to the south over the course of a breath is not weather. It is a crowd. It is everybody who ever stayed, shoulder to shoulder, reduced to the same sustained vowel, still going, no longer anywhere.

Above them the hiss: ten thousand impacts a second refusing to be counted, which is the drafts dying, the foam faces unmade while offering, the whole turbulent surplus of a creation running far ahead of any use for it.

And beneath, the toll: the last one still particular.

So the room is not a cathedral with one candle in it. That was the wrong reading, and everyone makes it, standing in the warm grain seeing nothing and hearing everything. The room is packed. It is dense as a market. You have been calling it noise because you cannot resolve it, the way you cannot resolve a hillside into blades of grass from a mile off. The moss is a child. The hand checking its pocket is an infant. The boulder is the only one left with a shape, and it is turning slowly at the height of your chest, and you can put your hand on the place where everybody's hand goes.

## The Largest

And the cutting itself repeats.

The braid chooses a new line, a hundred tons arrive somewhere they have never arrived, an alcove opens raw and hot and smelling of struck flint, and by evening it is calved away — hourly, hourly, hourly, the same wound being made in a different place, and the volume never changes. Eighty paces. That is a habit. That is the most durable habit in the region.

The largest resident of the gorge is the gorge: an unfinished thing that repeats being unfinished, whose body is a hole and whose skin is the one boundary here that is not up for negotiation. Everything else lives inside it the way flora lives in a lung. It breathes duration in and lays the once-things out downstream in a fan miles wide — single sunrises, first sentences, the exact way a particular door was closed on a particular afternoon, all tumbled smooth.

The lowlanders ballast their roads with it. Their children skip first sentences across ponds. Everything we build, we build out of what the loud room could not keep, which is why we are the kind of thing that cannot stay.

## Leaving

Go up. Put your hand in the groove at head height where the hand goes, in the dark, going up rather than down, which is new, which will be taken. Haul out into the thin grass and the loose handfuls of rain and stand there deaf, ringing, appallingly singular.

Something small will be missing. Not a possession — a repetition. The way you used to check a pocket. A name you used to say into a room before turning on a light. You will not notice which one for a day or two, and then you will notice for the rest of your life, because it is down there still, being done perfectly, at forty-second intervals, warmer than the stone. Strangers will feel it and not know whose it is. It is the newest resident. It is the only part of you that will ever be durable.

Or sit down first, in the two hollows on the eastern ledge, elbows on knees, and let your eyes be told where to go. The bird will come around and be gold for a fifth of a second, eleven thousand times a day, and the pleasure will not be entirely yours.

And eleven seconds after that, the boulder will not land.
