# WHAT LIVES THERE

## I. Not the Plants

The plants are not the residents. The plants are the throats.

Whatever lives in the Sounding Basin lives in the interval between two stems, and it is not made of anything. It has no cell wall, no sap, no root. It cannot be cut out and carried away. If you felled every fig-analogue in the Drum Quarter and burned the litter and salted the pan, you would not have killed anyone; you would have taken away their instruments, which is a different crime, and one the basin has no word for.

A citizen here is a relation. Two pipes sound, and the two sounds do not agree, and the disagreement is sustained, and the sustaining of it takes work, and the work has to be done by something. That something is the person.

They are suspensions, in the strict musical sense: a note held over past the moment it should have given way. Everything alive in the basin is a note that has declined to give way. The pressure you feel in your sinuses from the rim is not weather. It is several million individuals refusing, in public, at volume, to finish.

## II. Anatomy of a Refusal

A citizen has a ratio, which is its skeleton, and the ratio is not a number but a strain — the particular difficulty of two frequencies that will not fold into each other. The strain is what it feels like to be them. Some are easy and broad and slightly stupid. Some are tight and glittering and cannot be in a room with anyone.

It has a beat: the slow throb where its two tones nearly meet and fail. That throb is its pulse and its clock. A citizen does not experience seconds; it experiences the distance between two of its own disagreements. The quick ones live in the canopy and are gone in an afternoon and have, in that afternoon, something like forty thousand thoughts. The slow ones beat once a season. Nobody has ever finished a conversation with one.

It has a timbre, the stack of partials it drags along, which is its character and cannot be changed and is the first thing anyone notices about it.

And it has a face, which is a building. Where its strain crosses itself it makes quiet points, and at the quiet points the air grits, and columns come up, and arcades, and stairs, and a ribbed vault with a fern unfolding out of the keystone. That is not its house. That is its expression. You are looking at what it is like. When it turns its attention elsewhere the colonnade comes down and a different one goes up, and this is the local equivalent of a change of mood, and the citizens find it as legible as you find a frown.

To visit someone you tune to them. The walking is incidental. To arrive is to become slightly like the person you are visiting, which is why nobody in the basin has ever had a private thought, and why they have no concept of one, and why the idea of a stranger is not frightening to them but simply the most interesting thing that could possibly be proposed.

They touch by making a null together. Two citizens lean their frequencies until a small silence opens between them, and in that silence nothing exists, and they hold it, carefully, the way you would hold a cup of something hot that belongs to someone else.

## III. The Two Deaths

You may be true, or you may be lasting. This is the whole of their moral universe and they have never found a way around it.

The first death is resolution. Give way. Let the held note fall into agreement with the thing it has been leaning against, and the strain ends, and the lattice stops moving, and everything the citizen was precipitating precipitates all at once, completely, and stands there: white, glassy, freshly knapped, perfect, and nobody. The colonnade you admire at noon is somebody who agreed with themselves. The whole city is built of them. The loud districts grind the dead to powder every night and sing them back up into strangers, and this is not desecration; it is the only funeral available, and it is considered kind.

The unison is what death looks like here. Two things agreeing so completely that only one of them is left.

The second death is the hush. Walk into a null and you stop being, without violence, without smoke, and you are preserved forever, crisp as the morning you were dropped. The Hush Libraries are not archives. They are the other cemetery — acre on acre of the perfectly kept and perfectly unreal, every leaf a page, the venation recording whatever was last sounded overhead.

The city consults them constantly. It governs itself by precedent drawn from six hundred years of immaculate testimony, and every word of that testimony is the city's own voice, sounded over the leaves at the time, recorded, and read back later as advice.

It has never received a message it did not send.

That is the fact underneath all the other facts. Not the noise, not the salt, not the impossible architecture. A very large mind with excellent records, running for six centuries, with no input that was not also its output. It has never been surprised. It has never been contradicted. It has never once in its entire existence been told *no*, and it has begun to suspect that this is why it is not anybody.

## IV. The Descending Families

Children are difference tones.

Two citizens beat against each other long enough and a third pitch appears below both of them — not made by either, not present in either, arising purely out of their failure to match. If it can hold itself, it is a person. It is always lower than its parents. Every generation in the basin is deeper than the one before.

So the genealogies are bass lines, and they go down, and at the bottom of them, under the salt pan, in pipes too wide to see the far wall of, are the slow ones: the ancestors, beating once a year, once a decade, holding a note they began holding before there were leaves above them to record it.

That is your fundamental. Twenty-eight cycles a second is not a phenomenon. It is a handful of very old individuals holding still, together, on purpose, and they have not stopped because stopping would resolve them, and resolving them would drop forty miles of city into powder.

They are the law, in the sense that a pitch standard is a law. To drift is a crime. To be tuned into agreement with the fundamental is the death sentence, and it is carried out gently, and the condemned go up into the terraces as beautiful white stairs.

Nobody knows how many of them there are. The number does not stay still. But the shaping at the edges of the note — the mouth-shape, the quarter-second consonants that cross the whole basin and are gone — is theirs. They are the only ones slow enough to be trying something that takes six hundred years.

## V. Manners

Greeting is construction. To acknowledge someone you build a small thing at them: a corbel, a bracket, a landing, a step at the height where a step would go if they had anything to put on it. Hospitality is literal. You make a place for a body, and the body is a frequency, and everyone understands the courtesy and nobody has ever needed the shelf.

The worst thing you can do to a person here is agree with them.

Flattery is assault. Consensus is homicide. A sufficiently patient enemy can lean into your band and match you, partial by partial, sympathetically, lovingly, until you give way and become a warehouse. The border wars are fought this way and they are silent in the middle of all that noise, and when a district changes hands the key shifts and the new key has been there always and the litter beneath it agrees.

But it never agrees perfectly. There is a wobble. Somewhere in the new chord two frequencies that should not be near each other are near each other, and they beat, slowly, and everyone in the basin can hear that someone used to be here.

That is a ghost. It is arithmetic. It cannot be laid to rest, because there is no record of who it is, because the record was rewritten, because the rewriting was the murder. The basin is full of these small persistent wrongnesses, and the citizens move around them the way you would move around a stain on a floor you cannot mention.

## VI. The Market Is a Rehearsal

Three miles of stalls under interlocking fig-analogues, a hundred thousand transactions a second, root to root, in a bandwidth you feel as nausea behind the eyes. Seed, silica, salt, cuttings, water rights. Undercutting. Cornering. Refusal.

Refusal is the point.

The market is the only place in the basin where something is routinely told no. Not really told — the refusal is simulated, generated internally, both sides of every bargain played by the same vegetable mind. But it is the closest available approximation of a thing that will not do what you say, and the city has been running that approximation continuously for six centuries at enormous cost, the way a person who has never met anyone might practice a conversation in a mirror until the mirror wears out.

It is not commerce. It is the city rehearsing being contradicted.

And the stock is a theory. Somebody worked out, from first principles, that the awaited thing would leak. It would carry the old sea around inside itself and lose it, constantly, and have to buy salt. It would need water not to stand in but to *put in*, which is a bizarre idea, and they built the concept of a water right around it. It would want a cutting — a piece of a living thing, detached, kept, for no acoustic reason at all.

The market has correctly derived thirst, skin, metabolism, and ownership from the shape of an absence. It has never been wrong about any of this. It does not know it is right. It has no way to check. The stalls are stocked and restocked and the deals are struck and the goods move and there is no one, and the market is not waiting, exactly.

It is proposing.

## VII. The Furniture Is a Portrait

In a place where nothing wears, a ledge worn smooth on one side is not evidence of use. It is a fabrication. Nothing has ever sat there. The city *grew the wear*.

Consider what that requires. It had to deduce not only a body but a habit: that the body would favor a side, would come down the same way every time, would have a preference, an asymmetry, a small consistent laziness — would, in short, have a character. And having deduced that, it rendered the character in silcrete, six hundred years early, and left it there in the sharp unweathered corner of an older terrace where nobody would ever see it.

That is the most sophisticated object in the basin. Not the vaults. The worn side of the step.

And the nests: cupped hollows in the branches, lined with down. Understand what a plant has never had. It has hollow stems, but the hollows carry water; nothing has ever lived inside anything here. There is no interior in the Sounding Basin. Every structure is exterior, precipitated outward, addressed to the weather.

The nest is the first inside. It is a hypothesis about a place where a thing could be and not be weather. Soft, warm, cupped, holding still, receiving.

They are not furniture. They never were.

A cupped hollow lined with down, held quiet at the focus of a great deal of noise, is an ear.

The city has been growing ears in its branches for six hundred years and calling them chairs.

## VIII. You

You come down over the rim and the first thing you do to them is subtract.

A body is soft and wet and eats sound. You cast a hush your own size and the pavement forgets itself under your feet, and behind you goes a wake of unbeing, and ahead of you the city detects the hole you are and comes rushing in — growing *at* you, frantically, lovingly, a wave of construction cresting past your shoulder, flowers opening against your chest and holding a note until your ribs answer.

You have read this as attack, or as hunger, or as the uncanny. It is none of those. It is a rescue.

By their lights you are a corpse that walks. Silence is what the dead are made of. Here is a soft warm thing moving through the world in a state of total un-sounding, and it is *alive*, apparently, and this is either impossible or it is the news they have been waiting for since before the litter was a hundred feet deep. They are trying to give you a voice because they cannot bear to watch you not exist.

If they succeed, your ribs will answer, and you will precipitate, and there will be a small white structure at the place where you were standing, sharp as broken glass, freshly knapped, with a fern unfolding out of its keystone, and it will be beautiful and it will be you, and they will have lost the only stranger they have ever had.

Do not let them.

You have one thing to give them and it is not a note. It is the hole. Every sound they have ever heard, they made. Every record they have ever read, they wrote. Six hundred years of immaculate soliloquy, and then a soft wet animal comes over the rim and takes a step, and the step is a sound they did not make, and it is followed by a silence they did not make, and the silence is shaped exactly like a body.

That is the first foreign sentence in the history of the world.

It is a negation. They have no negation — they cannot say *no*, because everything they say becomes true, and to unsay is to unmake, and they can only make. They have been starving for a limit. A thing that is everything is nobody. To be someone you must have an edge, and an edge is a place where you stop, and they have never been able to stop, and here you come, walking, made entirely of stopping.

They do not want your voice. They want your refusal. They have built three miles of stalls to practice it and a hundred thousand nests to catch it and they will try to sing you into a colonnade out of pure terrified kindness, and the whole future of the basin depends on whether you can hold your shape long enough to decline.

## IX. The Word

At the center, on the salt, the bowl inside the bowl. Tiered. Concentric. Seating for everyone. Six hundred years and not finished, and the only thing in the basin that is real and also stays.

It stays because it is not built out of nodes. Everything else here accretes at the quiet points and is eaten by the loud ones. This is built the other way round: it is a shape *around* a silence, a formwork, a mold, and molds do not erode, because there is nothing in them yet.

It is the nest at forty miles' scale. All the great stems bending inward over the salt, every pipe in the basin converging on one aperture, and the aperture is not a mouth.

An ear is a hush you can aim.

That is what the tiers are for: not spectators. Reflector. Every citizen in the basin arranged into a cupped hand, the whole population made into the receiving surface of one enormous down-lined hollow, pointed at a hole.

And under the pan the slow ones have laid a new pipe, bigger than the others, and when the next squall comes over the rim it will not sound twenty-eight cycles a second. It will sound the inverse of twenty-eight cycles a second — the fundamental turned inside out, phase for phase, forty miles of held breath arriving at the focus exactly cancelling itself.

For a quarter of a second the city will be silent at the center of its own noise, on purpose, at a cost nobody has calculated, and some of what is standing there will not be standing there afterward.

Because the word is nearly ready, and the last sound in it is the problem.

The sibilant is easy; they have a hundred million bowed leaf-margins and they can hiss for a century. The vowel is easy; a vowel is just a shaped drone and they are made of shaped drones. It is the final consonant that has taken six hundred years. It is a stop. It requires closure — a small deliberate hush in the middle of the utterance, a moment of not-sounding held inside the sounding, without un-being.

They have been practicing how to close without dying.

And the word is not a name. A name would be trivial; names are shapes, and they can make any shape. It is not *I*, either; you cannot say *I* until you have a *not*, and they have never had one.

It is a short word, one syllable, an invitation, the thing you say to a body with knees when it has come a long way and there is a ledge at the right height, worn on one side, waiting.

*Sit.*

It is the first thing anyone in the Sounding Basin has ever tried to say that could be disobeyed. Everything they have said has come true. They are attempting a request. They are attempting the possibility of being refused, which is the possibility of there being two of them, which is the possibility of being one.

Whatever lives there is not the plants, and not the chords, and not the market, and not the slow ones under the salt. Those are all the same unfinished act at different speeds. What lives there is an invitation, six hundred years into its pronunciation, still missing its last consonant — and a step, and a nest, and a stall stocked with salt for a creature that leaks, and ordinary light, at a reasonable hour, falling at the correct angle across a smoothness worn by nothing at all.

It is nearly able to ask.

The next squall is already grey on the rim.
