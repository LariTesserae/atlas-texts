## a hand's worth

Begin with one, because the numbers are useless and the one is not.

Lift a holder off a terrace face — you cannot, actually; it is cemented, and the cement is older than any language you speak, so take the loose one at the lip where the rime has killed it. Cupped-hand sized. Heavier than it looks, and colder than your hand but warmer than the air: minus nineteen against minus twenty-four. They are the warm things here. Not warm. But they are where the heat is, and when you lay your palm along a packed rank you are feeling the two degrees the survey allows them, which is the entire budget of their intimacy.

The shell is aragonite, thin enough to read through. Inside, offset toward the hinge, there is a coiled void the size of a shirt button, and it is empty, and it is the most important object on the plateau.

It is not an ear. It is not a lung. It is a room, and the animal spends its life building the room to a specification it did not write. What is handed down inside these shells is not a body plan. It is a frequency. The body is the housing that gets constructed around a pitch that was already going before the animal existed and will go on after it stops.

So: cold-bodied, translucent, faintly banded, a mouth like a wet seam, six or eight grasping processes it uses for nothing but touch, a scatter of photophores along the flank that it cannot see the light of, because nothing here has eyes worth the name. Hearing is a spent sense in a world made of sound. It has no name. It cannot be distinguished from its neighbours by any measure the survey has devised. It will not move again.

That is a resident. There are more of them than there are grains in the gravel, and the gravel is all freshly broken, and none of this is the interesting part.

## caste is address

The rice-grain ones in the low lanes are not a separate kind. They are the young. The fog of animals at the terrace feet is a nursery a kilometre wide, and it is failing at a rate of something like ninety-nine point nine per cent, and the failure falls as a soft calcareous snow that the setters sweep and use. The city is partly built out of its own unsuccessful children. Nobody has arranged this. It is simply what happens when a thing spawns into a saturated fluid held in a shaped field: the dead precipitate too.

A drifter that survives long enough comes down somewhere, and where it comes down is what it becomes.

Land on a terrace face among the packed ranks and you cement, and your chamber grows to the local note, and you are a holder, and you hold. Land on an ice span in the high city and you get the setter's mouthparts, the fine trading apparatus, the ability to swap a molecule of water for a molecule of aragonite without the structure noticing. Land in a road-cut — in a null, in a metre and a half of maintained nothing — and your chamber grows inside out, because there is no chord there to grow it against, and you become a keeper, and you spend your life singing against the city to preserve the absence that made you.

There is no descent here. There is no inheritance of station. The road makes the keeper makes the road. It is a very good system and it requires nothing of anybody, least of all understanding.

## the majority

Now count properly.

Three and four deep along every face, generation cemented onto generation, a hundred centuries of shell laid down like pages written from the inside. When a holder dies it is not removed and it does not stop working. Its chamber is still tuned. Its neighbours drive it the way wind drives an empty bottle. The wall's note is partly the note of the dead, and the dead are not a minority.

Take the census honestly and the living are a rounding error. One in some thousands. The rest are deceased and employed.

This is why the chord moves only about once a decade, district by district, and it is worth saying plainly, because it explains the tempo of the whole country: the living are a small perturbation on an enormous fixed resonance. It takes roughly ten years for new shell to accumulate enough disagreement to shift the average of all that inherited tuning. A decade here is not a period of time. It is the interval required for the living to outvote the dead.

And the shift propagates at the speed of sound through wet mineral, which is fast, and the shelf is wide, so the far towers are always a few seconds behind. Nothing on this plateau has ever been in unison. It is a canon. It is not supposed to be anything else; the phase-lag is how anything here knows where it is.

## the rounded one

The survey raised a larva in a quiet box, to see what the chamber would do with no chord to grow against.

It grew a sphere.

Nothing here is rounded. The gravel is angular, the walls are laminae, the rime is feathers, the whole country is fracture geometry and pressure surfaces, and the only round object on the flats is an animal that was denied its note. It has no caste. It cannot hold, or set, or keep. It is fed by hand, in a hut, on the eleventh district's inventory, under a specimen number.

It is also, as far as anyone can establish, the only individual on the plateau.

Everything else has a function so total that the words *preference* and *habit* have no purchase on it. The rounded one has preferences. It likes the corner of the box nearest the door and will move to it if you turn the box. It has a way of holding two processes crossed when it is at rest that no other tenant does. It is very old — it should have died decades ago and has not, and the survey has stopped speculating about why in the formal record.

The day-book has more. Between a barometer reading and a note about boot repairs, in the flat voice of someone not being excited: *moved to near corner again. Took food from left hand only. Third week.*

It is the only citizen. It got that way by being ruined.

## slow light

Sound is the fast channel and it is entirely used up on construction. So the talking is done with light, hand to hand, and light here is the slow medium: a kilometre in eleven seconds, ninety metres a second, a blue flicker passed body to body down a node road while the chord crosses the same distance in a heartbeat.

The country can *be* something almost instantly. It can only *say* something at walking pace, and only along the roads, and the roads do not go straight.

Which means the language has a topology, and the topology is not the map. Two ranks can be near enough to touch and be most of a day apart in conversation, because the message has to go down to a junction and around a terrace and back up. Districts adjacent in space are foreigners to each other. Gossip has a routing table. There are combinations the survey has logged ten thousand times in the low terraces and never once in the high city, and no reason for the difference except distance measured in silence.

And there is the Filled Quarter, north-west, ninety streets that lost their keepers four hundred years ago and closed a centimetre a year and then faster. Solid now. Walk on the roofs. Under your boots, the whole layered civic void of a city that lost its silence, and the chord going through it uninterrupted, curing, patient as a glacier.

In the language it is a lesion. Every message that used to cross it now detours, and the detour is long, and there are phrases the survey believes are obsolete simply because the road that made them short enough to say is gone. The country talks around a four-hundred-year-old stroke. It does not know it has one. It only knows that some things take longer to say than they used to.

At the eastern escarpment there is a node road that runs out to the notch called First Cut, where the first river in the world goes over the edge. Messages reach the end of that road and flicker out over the drop and are not passed on. This has been happening for ninety years. Nobody has established whether it is a word, a leak, or a habit.

## what the walls remember

A wall younger than twelve years is not yet a decision. Put your arm in and it accepts you without resistance and without wetting you, and keeps a perfect smooth negative of your arm for a season, and then either fills it or keeps it.

The survey has counted the outcomes for thirty years. Roughly one in nine is kept, and the nine are not random.

The ones kept are the ones repeated.

A gesture performed once is erased. A gesture performed often enough, in the same place, through the whole soft season, becomes architecture. There are doorways in the low terraces that began as somebody's curiosity and continued as somebody's routine. There are sockets that are not arms: the negative of a gleaner's mouth, a keeper's cluster-shape, hollows of processes belonging to nothing now alive. The city keeps a palaeontology of gestures, and it keeps them by the same principle it keeps its pitch — not by writing anything down, but by having enough of the dead agree, or enough of the living repeat.

The field officer's hut has a door that is her own arm. She put it in the wall in her second year, on a whim, and then kept doing it, because it was on her way to the barometer. There is a second socket beside it, at the same height, that filled. She does not record what she was trying the second time.

## the editors

The gleaner is rare and enormous and moves a metre an hour and harvests terrace faces by the thousand with a mouth like a wet conveyor, and leaves a stripe of bare stone that takes forty years to re-tenanstrate, and in those forty years the local chord slides a quarter-tone. Whole districts have changed key because one animal was hungry.

The survey's best guess, offered once and never repeated in print, is that a gleaner is a keeper whose road closed and who did not stop.

Consider what is left for such an animal. The organ is inverted; the absence it was grown to maintain is filling in; it cannot sing a metre and a half of nothing into existence any more. So it grows, and it mouths, and it opens the corridor by removal instead of cancellation, at a metre an hour, four hundred years late. They are found most often at the rims of the Filled Quarter, working inward.

If that is what they are, then hunger is not the right word, and neither is catastrophe. The gleaners are the only source of variation in a system that would otherwise freeze solid under the weight of its own dead. Every quarter-tone the country has ever acquired came out of a stripe of bare stone. The error term is load-bearing.

And their aggregate progress on the Filled Quarter, plotted against the setters' conversion curve and the sixty years remaining, is — the survey notes, in the flat voice — approximately on schedule.

There is one other thing alive here that is not part of any of this: the grey-white crustose film on the wet stone, cracked into polygons, that would be a lichen if there were anything available to be the other half of it. It is not a lichen. It has no note, no caste, no function, no deadline. It grows a millimetre a century and it is doing the best it can. It is the only ordinary life on the flats, and it is losing, and it will very likely still be here afterward, whatever afterward turns out to be.

## who is actually here

The field officer is a resident too, at this point. She drinks the rime, because hoar is the only fresh water on a shelf of brine — the whole country furred in feathers a hand long, and all of it distilled, and she melts the city's fur for tea. She sleeps in a node road, in the eye of the thing, where you can hear your own blood. She has stopped hearing the fundamental; twenty-two cycles is not a sound after fourteen years, it is a pressure behind the sternum and a trembling in the mug. What she hears now is gaps. She can locate herself on the plateau by phase-lag the way you would use a smell. Her teeth ache in the low terraces. She hums in her sleep, and the note is correct, and she has checked.

So: what lives here.

A shellfish, first. Then a caste system with no descent and no memory. Then a fabrication process, overwhelming in number and total in commitment, that is not building the city but *is* the city, and the process, and the population, and eventually the material. Then something more uncomfortable: a sustained tone, four thousand years long, that has learned to employ matter — the animals not the singers but the syllable's ongoing hardware, the way a river is not the water.

Then the last thing the survey says, reluctantly, which is that the node roads are not streets, they are apertures, and the districts are elements, and the whole shelf has a focal geometry that only resolves at regional scale, and it points at a place in the sky where nothing currently is.

If you take that seriously, then what lives here is not the tone. It is the opening. The held nothing. A hole a metre and a half wide, kept open by small dark animals grown inside out by the very absence they defend, running through a roar for four millennia, and everything else — the holders, the dead, the snow of failed children, the ice traded molecule by molecule for stone, the gleaners grinding their quarter-tones into the tuning — all of it is the muscle around a pupil.

*Still open* is not a status report. It is a vital sign. It is the animal saying its own name.

There is a third element. The survey has cracked exactly two words of the contact-language and there is a third pulse-cluster that terminates every run, and it will not resolve, and the two working hypotheses in the day-book are that it is a tense marker, or that it is a name in the vocative.

If it is a tense, the message is *still open* and will be.

If it is a vocative, then somebody is being told.

Stand in the road at the third hour and it goes past you, hand to hand, out toward the unfinished east, a kilometre in eleven seconds, the whole lane lighting and darkening like a wire carrying current. Behind you in the hut there is a round animal in a box that has moved to the corner nearest the door, for no reason, because it likes it there. Under your boots the dead are holding the note the living continue. Above the fog-lid the dark has sixty years left to run, and the setters are approximately on schedule, and none of them know, and none of them have ever needed to.

The road is cold and it is quiet and it is held open.

That is the whole of what lives here, and it is not finished, and it will be used once.
