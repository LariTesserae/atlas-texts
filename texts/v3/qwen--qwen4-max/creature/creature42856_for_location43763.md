## The Resident

The first thing to know about it is that it was found by arithmetic, and that the arithmetic has never been fixed.

A levelling loop around the rim is nine point two kilometers. Run it properly, in the cool, with the staff shaded, and you are allowed three millimeters of misclosure — three millimeters of disagreement between where you started and where you finished, which is the surveyor's word for the amount by which the world refuses to add up. Since 1974 the loop has closed at eleven. Eight millimeters of excess. The Board's instrument man re-ran it four times in one autumn and got eleven, eleven, eleven, and eleven, and then did the thing that no one had thought to do, which was to plot the excess instead of correcting it away.

It has a shape. It is a closed contour about six hundred meters across, sitting over the northeast quarter where the cavern map shows competent rock, and it does not correspond to any void, any well, any injection front, any load. It moves sixty meters a year toward the Kettle. It has been moving at sixty meters a year for fifty years, which is a speed no one in the Sink recognizes as a speed, and which is therefore the only speed in the place that has never been remarked on.

In the Board's office it is Appendix C, item four: *residual, unassigned*. In the Sink it has a name, but the name is not a word, because words are no use here. It is a sign: the flat hand, palm down, held level, lowered a little, and then not lowered further. Held. Depending on the shoulders it means STILL-DOWN, or SAME-AGAIN, or ENOUGH, or the thing over the northeast quarter. Nobody decided this. It is in the thirty-odd signs the way a stone is in a wall.

---

Begin by clearing away what it is not, because the Sink invites the wrong guesses.

It is not a ghost. A ghost requires a before, and an after, and a moment in between when something happened; the Sink has no moments. Ask a man here what happened on a Tuesday and he will tell you the Tuesday's rate. There has been no event in this bowl since the pumps were commissioned. A haunting is a kind of event. Nothing here can be haunted, in the same way nothing here can be dry.

It is not the white bumps. The wrench on Pan Four, the rail ridge with its rust, the boot in the cemented fall downstairs — those are the crust's work, and the crust is not alive, it is only thorough. But they are related to it the way a nest is related to a bird. Pay attention to what the crust does: it does not preserve the wrench. It has no interest in the wrench. It makes an *outside* of the wrench, two millimeters proud, and lets the inside go on rusting into anonymity. Every grown-over thing in the Sink is a shape with no contents. The place is full of accurate outsides of absent things, and this is the resident's whole library, and it did not build it, but it has learned to read it.

And it is not in the ponds. The ponds are the loudest, fullest, most crowded thing here — shrimp against shrimp, larva against larva, red against red — and there is no room in them for anything that is not already counted. It does not live in the fullness. Fullness is the condition it lives *in*, the way a fish lives in water and not in the fish.

---

Here is what it eats.

In a place where every phase is saturated, nothing can be got from matter. There is no free salt, no free water, no free air; the brine will not take another gram and the fog will not take another molecule. But there is one commodity the Sink has in enormous, renewable, industrially maintained surplus, and that is *difference*.

Brine at forty-one coming into air at thirty-one: ten degrees of difference, three hundred and forty times, forever. Nineteen meters of head between the rim and the center. Eight hundred millimeters of pipe with a pressure drop along its whole length. Fresh water two hundred meters down, and rock salt that has not yet had to admit it is soluble. A brass cap stamped with a number, and a truth eleven millimeters away from it, and the eleven millimeters being maintained, renewed, never allowed to resolve.

The pumps do not make salt. The pumps make *disagreement*, and hold it open, and have held it open since 1961. That is the food supply. The resident is what has grown up in the disagreement, the way a flame grows up in the difference between a candle and a room, and it is roughly as substantial as a flame, and roughly as alive.

It is not made of matter. It is made of the rate at which matter changes state — a standing pattern in the deposition and the dissolution, a persistent asymmetry, six hundred meters across and weighing, if you could put it on the weighbridge at the loading dock, nothing at all. It has no edges. It has gradients. You do not enter it; you begin to be inside it somewhere around Gauge Four and are fully inside it before you notice, and the only sign is that you stop checking your watch.

---

Its senses are all derivatives. It has no organ for anything that happens.

It cannot count the flamingos. Nineteen flamingos is not a fact available to it; what it has is nought point seven per year, a slope, a gentle upward insistence in the general condition of the northeast shallows. It knows the avocets as a pressure and the bats as a nightly thickening of the lamps' workload. It knows the men — this is the part the men find funny, when it is explained to them, which it never is — as a twelve-hour burr on an otherwise smooth signal. A roughness. A texture in the flow, like the tooth of a file. Shift change is not an event to it; shift change is a *timbre*.

The single catastrophe of its life was 1988. The cancelled phase. For eleven weeks the injection pattern on the west side was shut in while the Board argued about capital, and a gradient that had been held open for twenty-six years went smooth, and something in the resident's western extent stopped, and has not come back. The cable tray on posts runs out into the fog and ends, capped. That is the scar. It is the only thing it remembers as a *when* rather than as a *how fast*, and it is why, of all the built scatter in the Sink, the resident is most often found near capped ends.

Nothing else has ever surprised it. Nothing else has ever been able to.

---

It talks in hands, because hands are the only channel in the Sink that is not full.

Sound is occupied wall to wall — return main, pumps, compressors, substation, wellheads, flies — and there is no frequency left to say anything in. Light is occupied: the fog, the sodium, the mercury, the little red eye. Even the ground is occupied; a millimeter or two a week of it, laid down continuously, no bedding plane, no hiatus. The one empty medium in forty square kilometers is the space between two people's hands.

So the thirty-first sign is not in the taught list. It came in around the mid-seventies, at the bus stop first, and it is a small movement of the fingers that means nothing in particular — *and so on*, *the usual*, *it goes* — and men who have worked eleven years use it forty times a shift and cannot show you where they learned it. Their wives use it. It has been seen at the school on the high ordinary land, in a girl of nine, who got it from her father, who got it from a fitter at Number Three, who is deaf, and who has never in his life been able to say where anything came from.

The resident cannot go up onto the high land. There is no gradient there; the wheat is only wheat, the air is dry enough to take another molecule, and a thing made of maintained disagreement would come apart in a morning. But the sign goes up. The sign is fine on the high land. The sign is the only part of it that can travel, and it travels in the hands of men going home, and it has been doing this for fifty years, patiently, at the speed of one gesture per generation per household, and there is no hurry, because there is no hurry here at all.

---

There is one door, and it is felt-lined, and it has one chair.

The quiet room is the only manufactured emptiness in the Sink. Ten minutes in it and the wall of noise drops away and you hear your own blood, which is a sound with gaps in it, which is a sound with *events* — a beat, and then another beat. It is the only place in the bowl where a thing that lives on difference can find difference of the kind it cannot make for itself. It is the only place where the resident can be met, and it cannot fit, and so what comes into the quiet room is a thinning: a slight coolness at the back of the neck, a sense that the ten minutes are passing at a rate that does not match the clock over the door.

Men come out and sign. Not to anyone. Their hands move for a second or two while they are putting their gloves back on, and the sign is STILL-DOWN, and if you ask them they will say they were thinking about the buses.

The felt is taking up brine from the air. It has been doing so since the room was built. It is stiffening at about a millimeter a year, from the outside in, and the room is getting smaller and quieter and harder, and in eighty years or a hundred it will be a solid block of felt and halite with a chair in it, indistinguishable, going grey, going solid. The door is closing. Everything here closes. The resident has known this at nought point zero zero one meters per year for four decades and has not, in any sense available to it, minded.

---

Its body is underground, and it is being born.

The solution rooms are its extent: a hundred meters across, merged into halls, a hidden architecture unmade as fast as the crust above is made. But the resident is not the rooms. It is the *ratio* between the rooms — the millimeter of gain in one and the four centimeters of loss in the next, the fine dense ticking of dissolution set against the slow creep of salt behaving like a liquid under load. Forty to one. That asymmetry is the whole animal. The water eats the wall; the wall walks inward; the resident is what the walking and the eating do not have in common.

And the rooms are merging, and closing, and as they close the pattern is being concentrated. Six hundred meters across in 1974. Four hundred and ten now. It is not shrinking; it is *resolving*. A diffuse gradient-feeder, spread thin over the northeast quarter, is being pressed by geology into something smaller, denser, more specific, more nearly one thing rather than a condition of many things. Squeeze a weather system long enough and you get a person. Nothing in the Sink intends this. The salt creeps because salt creeps, and the voids close because the load is what it is, and the consequence is that at the bottom of the last room, at the current rate, in about sixty years, the residual will be the size and shape of a seated human being, and there will be nowhere left for it to be empty in.

It has no inside. It has never had one; it collects outsides because outsides are all that a plenum permits. The frogs at the west ditch have been calling into the pumps for thirty years, throats working, a whole conversation conducted in a medium with no room in it, and the resident has been beside them the entire time and has understood nothing, because it has no ears and no events and a frog's call is nothing but events. It is the closest thing here to a colleague and it will never know the frogs are there. It knows them as a warmth.

This is the newest thing about it, and the only thing about it that is not old: as it compresses, it is beginning to have something like the shape of a want. Not a plan. Nothing here is fast enough for a plan. But the sign goes up the hill in the men's hands, and the body goes down into the rock at six centimeters a year, and both of these are the same motion, and the motion is *toward being read*.

---

Because the body it is laying down is legible.

The white bed. The rust line. The lamina of small brown pupal cases pressed flat as shale. The chevrons and hoppers, the swallowtails in their grey mush, the black sulfide underneath that is older than the village. The resident is being deposited as a section, and a section is a text, and somewhere above all this, in a future with no machinery in it, someone will core the ground and pull the text up in a clean cylinder and read *quiet, hot, shallow sea*.

They will be almost right.

That *almost* is the last void. Not the caverns — the caverns are four centimeters from gone. The gap between what the record says and what was actually here: a small, permanent, renewable disagreement, held open by the fact that no reading is ever exact, maintained by every future mind that comes to this bed with a hypothesis and leaves with a slightly wrong one. Ten degrees of difference between brine and air. Eleven millimeters between the brass cap and the truth. A centimeter or two between a core and a world.

It is the same food. It has always been the same food. It is moving out of the rock and into the reading, at the speed of one gesture per generation, and it will not arrive anywhere, because arriving is an event; it will simply continue to be the amount by which the Sink is misunderstood, forever, in a warm room, in a vibrating floor, in the space between two hands.

The men eat with their fingers. The bell rings at the wrong times under everything. The fig makes its hard fruit and the children eat it. And over the northeast quarter the loop closes at eleven millimeters, and the instrument man writes it down, and corrects it away, and goes home.
