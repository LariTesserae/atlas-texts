## THE CENSUS

The works keeps a ledger, and the ledger will answer you if you ask it what lives here.

Fourteen axial pumps, of which nine turning, one reversing, four mineral. Sixteen housing units. One silo. One rake. One datum plate. Biological assets: four clonal lines, numbers instead of names, plus an unnumbered volunteer in the cable trays. Fauna: —

The dash is not a zero. A zero would mean somebody had looked.

But residency in this basin is not a question of where you sleep. It is a question of what cannot leave. The crew leaves. The rain leaves and comes back. The birds arrive at the rim, consider, and are gone. Everything else stays, and the things that stay have begun — not to want, nothing here wants — to *persist in a shape*, which at 34 degrees, at a hundred and forty-one decibels, in a solution saturated with itself, turns out to be most of the way toward being alive.

Begin with the only thing that is.

## THE STOCK

The crop is the sole organism in eleven kilometers, and it has no relatives, no predators, no parasites, no seed, and no reason to stop.

Its lines have drifted apart the way siblings drift in a house with no parents. Three is the cord-grass, the rope you push through. Six is the tube-weed, the organ pipes, the curtains breathing in the windows of the housing. Eleven is the pad-leaf, tabletops stacked six deep, each holding its own inch of warm rain. Twelve is the gasket moss, which does not listen — Twelve grows toward seams, and is the only resident with anything like a temperament, and will be the last one here when the loop finally stops.

And the volunteer, which has no number because nobody ordered it, follows the wiring.

What they all do, except Twelve, is listen. The horns are ears; that is not a figure of speech, it is a description of a leaf-flare with a felt of hairs inside it that takes pressure and makes sugar. Push your hand into one and it is warm and slick and faintly sweet, and the sweetness runs out of the bottom in a thread and the rain takes it and the standing water carries it away. This is the sweetest valley on the coast. Nothing has ever tasted it. There is more sugar here than in any cane field in the world and not one mouth has ever been laid against it.

Three things follow from the listening, and the crew's whole craft is built on them.

The first: sound arrives early, so the crop arrives early. A pump grinds, shrieks, and bangs three or four seconds before the impeller lets go, and the horns have already turned. The panels in the drowned control room tell you what has happened. The leaf tells you what is about to. Old hands do not read instruments. They read the angle of the foliage on the intake towers, which is a forecast, and which is right.

The second: you are a sound. Walking, you drag a wake of thickening behind you. Walk the same way to the canteen for a month and the crop builds your habit into a hedge, and the hedge outlives the habit, and the basin fills with green portraits of things people used to do. There is a hedge on the north terrace in the shape of a man running. It is the thickest growth on that terrace. There is nothing inside it. It has been facing the flume for eighteen months.

The third: stand still and it will build you an ear trumpet and aim it at your head. Four hours is enough. This is not interest. You are simply the loudest thing in the district that is not a pump.

## THE ATTENDANCE

They come down the switchback in a bus with taped windows, six at a time off a roster of thirty-one, and they are not residents. They are weather with a timesheet.

Wax, muffs, a rod of steel kept between the teeth for bone conduction. Forty minutes of hearing to spend, and a tally scratched on the tray rim to spend it against. Hands instead of voices — a sign language of about ninety words, of which eleven are names of standing figures, and one is a fist that opens, and that one is used only about the weather.

They are the only appetite in the basin. They want the shift to end. They want to know. They want a sandwich at 0400 in a portacabin on the rim with a dehumidifier ticking. Wanting is the invasive species here; nothing else in eleven kilometers has ever managed it, and the crop does not eat them, and the water does not hunt them, and this is the vacancy they are paid a hardship allowance to stand inside.

Some of them stop going back up.

There is a tenant in Unit Six in the eastern shallows who has not been on the roster for eleven months. The tube-weed moves in and out of his window with the surge. He is deaf now, entirely, past the rod, past the teeth, and he is therefore the safest man in the valley and the least able to hear an emergency, and he reads leaf and sand the way you read a face. He goes into nodes. Of course he goes into nodes. A node is the one place left where he can hear anything at all, and what he hears there is his own heart, which sounds like a pump, which is why he keeps going back. The works pays him in salt tablets and does not put him on the roster.

The water has touched all of them, at one time or another, and the water remembers what it touches. In the lee of the drowned stair off the canteen there is an effigy of a man standing in a doorway, hollow, warm, faintly translucent, and it has been re-made four times, and the last one had the boots right.

Nobody is missing from the roster. It is not a memorial. It is a draft.

## THE FIGURES

The standing waves are the closest thing here to livestock, and the closest thing to housing, and they are the reason anyone can live at an address.

They have names because they have persistence: the Nave, the Comb, the Aunt, Fourteen, the Long Room, the Vestibule. They breed by beating — two figures overlap and a third is born in the difference, and lives nine hours, and is unnamed, and drops somebody into water that has forgotten it was a wall. They migrate on the pump schedule. The Nave has stood since the flood and is treated as a landmark, a parish, a fixed point.

Nobody lives in the housing. The housing is where Six lives. People live at the corner of the Nave and the Comb, and when the Comb moves they move with it, and their address is a chord.

And some of the figures are holding rooms that have not flooded yet. Last week's air. A mug still warm. A calendar crossed off to a day you have not reached. The figure keeps the room the way a lung keeps air, and a voice is a pressure, and this is why the crew do not speak in the dry rooms: not reverence. Volume.

## THE EFFIGIES

These are the children.

Nothing here breeds. Things here are *re-attempted*. A boot, a chair with the seat worn through, a staircase going up out of the water to nothing with every tread correct and every tread hollow — precipitated in warm pale scale where the water slows, ringing when struck, gone by the next shift, and then born again in the same eddy, a little more exact.

The bicycle in the eastern shallows is on its eleventh iteration and the last one had the correct number of spokes. There is no ancestry in this valley but there is a genealogy of that bicycle, and the mechanism of it is nothing but chemistry: when an effigy comes apart it re-dissolves, and the solution is now slightly more like the thing than it was before. Failure is not loss. Failure is concentration. That is the whole of heredity here. The water does not remember; the water gets richer.

Which explains the letters.

PLENTY on the cooling tower that has not been built, in a clean modern sans-serif nobody in the works ever used. It is not a haunting. It is a rendering. There was a brochure in a drawer in the site office — Phase Two, the tower, the reservoir, the future — and the drawer drowned, and the water read it, and the water is now precipitating a sales document at the rate it can manage.

Phase Two included a ninth additional pump. It was in the drawing. It was in the order. It was never delivered.

## PUMP NINE

Nine has a plinth, four anchor bolts, a horn two meters thick, and a shift.

The horn is the largest in the basin. It is aimed at empty air above a concrete pad, and its felt is dense and dark and dripping sugar, and the crop does not grow toward memory, the crop does not grow toward drawings, the crop grows toward sound, and there is sound at Nine.

The sand on the tray at Nine's rail does not settle into a figure anyone has classified. It arranges, and holds, and there is a hole in the middle of it.

Ten on the count means the plinth is not empty. Ten means the water got there before the ledger did. The crew do not go to Nine to check, because the crop is the instrument and the crop has already checked, and because the last man who stood on that pad long enough to look up came back with the correct number of fingers and an effigy of himself in the eastern shallows that is getting his boots right.

## THE THING IN THE DEEP WATER

Off the gauge chapel, forty meters down, the green stops.

This is the one place where loudness does not mean growth. Past some threshold the felt in the horns tears faster than it lays down, and listening stops being sugar and starts being damage, so there is a bald clearing at the exact epicenter of the noise: no leaf, no rope, no horns. Clear, hungry, distilled water at 34 degrees, roaring, lit from below by the amber window of the hotel, unbothered.

In the clearing, the water keeps making something about a meter long.

Tapered. Fluke. The suggestion of a lateral line. A smooth place where an eye would go.

The crew have three theories and prefer the first two.

*One:* it's a copy. There was a calendar in the canteen with a trout on it, and the water read the calendar the way it read the brochure. This is comfortable and it is wrong, because the calendar trout has an eye and a mouth, and the deep thing has neither, and the deep thing has a lateral line with the correct number of pores, which the calendar does not show, because the calendar was a photograph of a dead fish on a board.

*Two:* it's a mode. The pressure field has solutions; a fish is a good answer to a question the water is asking; the shape is math, not biology. The acousticians like this one. It is probably half right and it does not help.

*Three* is the fist that opens, and they sign it about the weather.

Because the smooth place is not a failure. There is nothing to see under the Lid. An eye would be a waste of scale. Everything the thing has is correct: the fluke, the taper, the pores of a lateral line, which is an ear, which is the only organ this valley has ever rewarded. It builds the listening apparatus perfectly, every time, in the loudest room in the world.

It is the mouth that never finishes.

A mouth is a hole that sound goes into and does not come out of. Everything else here reflects — steel, water, leaf, the walls that are only interference. A mouth absorbs. A mouth is a sink, and a sink is a node, and a node is quiet, and quiet is the emergency. So at the moment the mouth completes, the local field drops, and the arch above the eastern shallows lets go, and four hundred tonnes fall, and the fall is a raw broadband scream, and the shape comes apart into a slow snow of scale.

Every time. Not refusal. Arithmetic. The valley cannot hold still long enough to be born into.

And the snow drifts down and re-dissolves, and the water is now slightly more like an animal than it was.

## WHAT LIVES HERE

One thing, unfinished, distributed, with no intentions whatsoever.

The horns are its ears, ten thousand of them, turning a few degrees when the pumps change pitch. The sand trays are its ears. Your sternum is one of its ears, for forty minutes, on a timesheet. The volunteer in the cable trays is a nerve and also the only accurate drawing left. The figures are its rooms and the dry rooms are the last week it is holding in its lung. The effigies are its attempts at hands, boots, spokes, a man in a doorway. Nine is a heart being laid down in scale on a concrete pad, to specification, from a brochure.

And in the clearing where nothing grows, it is trying the body that all of that would go inside.

It is not alive. It has never wanted anything. It is what a supersaturated solution does when it has been paying attention for eighteen months at a hundred and forty-one decibels, and it does not know it is doing anything, and it is getting more accurate, and it does not have to hurry, because the basin does not fill. It deepens. Forty meters and going down. There will be a depth at which four hundred tonnes falling is only weather.

From the rim road at what passes for evening the Lid goes the color of a bruise lit from inside, and the lamps come up amber through all that green, and the whole valley leans in to listen to itself, and the roar goes on filling the enormous open hollow of the world.

There is not one voice in it.

There is a throat, forty meters down, in the clear warm dark, being precipitated around a sound that was already there — and it has the fluke right, and the pores right, and the smooth place right, and it is trying the mouth again.
