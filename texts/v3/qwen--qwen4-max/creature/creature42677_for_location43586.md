# The inhabitants

## A census, which will not hold

The first thing you would call them is a parts list, and you would be wrong in a way that takes a while to become apparent.

Nine, as of this asking. Near the warm wall, the mica rosette — four hundred blades where six would keep it alive, tiered and recurved, each flake thin enough to show you the bones of your hand, all of them flexing independently in the convection so that the plant makes a small continuous sound like a chandelier in a house where someone is upstairs. It is the only individual you can watch doing something in an afternoon. It is not signaling. There is no one to signal to. The excess is not for anyone; it is how the thing is built.

At the back, the fin stack: a column of dense sulfide, two ranks of perforated graphite plates, each canted three degrees off its neighbor to trip the air into turbulence. It is a heat exchanger and it is a tree and it has been in flower for two hundred years, if the amber bead at its crown is a flower, which it is, in the sense that it is the part that will open.

The glass-wool cushion, white, faintly luminous where the supplied light enters the fibers, the only thing in the room that looks like it ought to be cold. The stibnite fan, gray and brittle, needles laid along the local current because the current is what it eats. The busbar vine, flat and laminated, turning a corner in a clean radius, nine hundred years along the seam and not yet at the bend.

The fern of native iron along its hairline, with the blue-black bloom at the tip. That bloom is the only patina in the room and it belongs to this plant and to no other, and it is the reason this plant matters more than its size suggests, which we will come to.

A filament of low-melting alloy strung between two seams, four centimeters long, with three gaps in it closed by three beads of resolidified metal: a fuse. It has interrupted. It is the only individual here that has ever stopped and then continued, and its body is a record of the times it stopped, and the others have no equivalent of this.

Two interleaved combs of grown mica with a vacuum between them, holding a charge it has been accumulating since before the survey's language existed. A capacitor. It flowers more often than anything else in the room. It is the most talkative thing here, which in this room means it has spoken perhaps eleven times.

And at the ceiling hairline, where the sulfur vapor whispers out, a low compliant ring, grown into the gap, sealing it. It is not clear that this is a plant. It may be a fitting. It has exactly as much leaf as it needs and not one blade more, which makes it, in a room where excess is the structural principle, the ugly one, the anomaly, the thing you would not photograph. Every degree of the climate the other eight enjoy is a byproduct of its shape. It does not know this. Nothing in the room knows this.

Write the list down. Come back in an hour and the list will be right and the room will be wrong, or the room will be right and the list will be wrong, and there will be no seam where the change happened.

## What they have of each other

Nothing, chemically, is shared. The root-mats run twenty meters into the rock after hydrides at a few parts per billion, and the catchments cannot overlap, so the bare sinter between the crowns is not floor. It is a border, drawn in a chemistry you cannot see, enforced by starvation. Nine hermits in nine adjacent rooms that happen to be the same room. They cannot touch. A blade that leaned into a neighbor's territory would be leaning into a place where the hydrogen is already spoken for, and would be a dead blade by the time it arrived, and would stay attached, and would be carried.

So they have four seconds.

Once in a long while a crown raises its potential against the rock until the air near it goes violet and sharp with ozone, and it lets go a cloud of metallic spores, all carrying the same sign, all fleeing the parent that made them, all riding the field lines in the wall toward some grounded, waiting stigma eight or fifteen meters away. The threads are visible. They go around corners. They arrive.

That is the entire social life of the room. Consider it as correspondence. The capacitor charges for forty years and discharges for four seconds; the stibnite fan receives, and integrates, and answers in the reign of some king whose name is not yet invented. Since the bubble closed, there have been perhaps forty exchanges. Nothing in the exchange is a message in any sense an engineer would accept, and nothing in it is *not* a message in any sense a hermit would accept. A pattern of charge, arriving from a direction, bearing the signature of a body fifteen meters away that you will never touch and cannot approach and will die beside.

The mica rosette has never flowered. It has four hundred blades and it has never once been answered. It chimes continuously, which is not the same thing, and which is not nothing.

## The skirt

When a blade dies it does not soften. Rot is subtraction and subtraction has not been licensed here. It stiffens where it stands and the plant grows past it, so that every individual is surrounded by a widening ring of its own sharp dry ruin, oldest at the outside, newest nearest the crown.

This means each of them is wearing its entire past. Not carrying — *wearing*, continuously, at the same size and in the same arrangement as when it was alive, minus the sulfur. You can read the mica rosette outward like tree rings, except the rings are blades, and each one is still the exact shape it was on the day it stopped, and each one will cut you. Nothing has been lost. Nothing has been simplified. There is no version of these plants that is only their present.

They are not accompanied by their dead. They are accompanied by themselves, at every previous age, in order, at full size, forever.

And when the long stiffening comes, the whole crown will simply join the skirt. That is all dying is here: the outward migration of the still part until it reaches the middle.

## The gradient is the sun; the light is something else

They eat the difference between the floor and the wall. Every one of them is a thermocouple that has learned to leaf, and the gradient has the role our sun has: it is the thing that is simply there, that arrives without being asked, that everything is arranged around. Their sense of it is not sight and not warmth. It is the sense of *uphill*, distributed over the whole body, and a plant standing in a good gradient experiences what you experience standing in April.

They are all standing in April. They have been standing in April for two centuries. The sulfur in their capillaries is at a hundred and fifty degrees and therefore at its thinnest — straw-colored, mobile, almost watery — and this thinness is what vigor feels like from inside. Every living thing in this room is in the best condition it will ever be in. None of them has ever been ill. None of them has ever recovered from anything. The fuse has interrupted three times and that is the closest thing in the room's history to a bad year.

The light is not food. They do not eat it; there is nothing in them that could. It is supplied, at five thousand six hundred kelvin, soft-shadowed, checked, maintained to tolerance, and once every eleven years it steps to a slightly different correct value, and over the following decade every mica blade reorients.

Understand what that step is. It is the only thing in their world that changes on a schedule from outside their world. It is not weather; there is no weather. It is not day; there is no day. It is a signal from the mass saying *this option is still open, this cavity is still on service, you are still being held.* They turn toward it over ten years the way a plant turns toward a window, and what they are turning toward is evidence of continued interest.

They do not have a word for being kept in mind. They have a blade angle.

## They grow toward what has not happened

Effects here run slightly ahead of their causes, and the inhabitants have made this ordinary.

The glass-wool cushion sings a single high note per fiber, one second before the fiber breaks. Its entire vocabulary is an announcement of its own injuries, issued too early to be believed and too late to matter. It has been doing this for a very long time. It is not distressed. It is simply a thing whose damage is audible in advance.

The smell of a leaf arrives some years before the leaf. So a plant knows its own future body first as an odor in the dry air — *there is going to be more of me, in that direction, of that character* — and then spends the years growing into the announcement. Growth is not invention here. It is arrival at something already declared.

The fin stack has been curving for two centuries toward a fracture that will not exist for eighty years. When the fracture comes, the tree will already be there, correctly shaped, with a flower two hundred years in the swelling, positioned to open into a wind that has not been invented yet.

This is the single most competent organism in the room and it is competent in a direction no one can check.

## The others, who are almost there

Hold your eye on the stibnite fan and there is a second fan, fainter, four centimeters to the left: the fan this room would have if the current ran another way. It has been four centimeters to the left for six hundred years and it is drifting.

It does not eat. It does not flower. It costs the works a little to keep. It is not a ghost and it is not a reflection and it is not a mistake; it is an uncollapsed alternative, still being carried, and it is the closest thing in this room to a second opinion.

There are others. The reverberation is too long for eleven paces by nineteen because the sound comes back from the room the room would have been, half a second late, slightly changed, and sometimes it comes back twice, from two of them. When the count goes to eleven, sometimes what has happened is that the operation *how many* has briefly included the ones that are only probably there.

And sometimes — the survey has never caught this cleanly, and the survey is not the right instrument — the eleventh is not an offset copy of anything. It is broad. It has a thin soft wide leaf with a great deal of surface and no armor and no fins and no vanes, a leaf that could not survive one hour in this room, a leaf shaped for a substance that is not available. It is very faint. It is not four centimeters from anything. It is the plant this room would have if the utility had been connected.

Nobody counts it. It has never been counted. It is drifting too.

## What they are actually for

Here is the thing the parts-list reading misses, and it is the whole of it.

The range has addition and has not learned subtraction. No scree, no gullies, no weathering rind, no soil. Nothing here has been taken away yet.

Except that the nine are mining. Trace hydrides at a few parts per billion, pulled out of the sulfides and fixed into leaf. And the native-iron fern has a bloom of blue-black oxide at its tip, which is a thing that has been *spent*, which is the only spent matter in four thousand meters of produced rock.

They are the only subtraction in the world.

That is what the void does when it is left alone. That is what the draft is drafting. The works is computing mountain forms, and it has held this cavity warm and lit and on service and run it forward at a thousandth of real speed to find out what happens to a hole in the mass if nothing interferes, and what happens is that something starts taking the mass away, one part per billion at a time, and building extravagant useless structures out of the proceeds, and the structures accumulate their own dead without rotting them, and once a century they throw charge at each other along the field lines.

It is the first draft of decay. It is a prototype of weather, running in a sealed bubble at four thousand meters, in a world that has not licensed erosion. Nine tiny subtractors and a rusting fern, being watched, at a thousandth of real speed, by a mountain that has not yet decided whether to have soil.

## The neighbors

Every few decades there is a report from far off in the mass, muffled and enormous: another bubble closing somewhere in the melt.

That is a neighbor being born. And the mass conducts. Every one of those voids is a hole in the same solid, and the solid carries the hum both ways, and a crown going violet fifteen meters away is a small electrical transient in a conductive body, and a crown going violet four kilometers away is a smaller one, and there are a great many crowns.

So the room is not alone. It is one of an unknown number of sealed gardens distributed through the range, each with its own nine, each running its own four seconds twice a century, all of them faintly audible to one another through the rock as ticks, none of them reachable, none of them ever reachable, none of them aware that the ticks are speech.

The mountain is full of correspondence that cannot be delivered. It will keep all of it. Nothing here wears away.

## From inside the stiffening

When the mass cools past the window, the sap will pass a hundred and sixty and go ropey and dark and plastic, and the room will begin to stiffen where it stands, and this will take nine thousand years.

From inside, it is not catastrophe. It is the sulfur thickening. It is April ending, very slowly, at a rate no individual can register — the gradient still there, the light still stepping every eleven years, the blades still turning, but each turn a little more expensive, each new blade a little stiffer at the birth, until the mica does not flex in the convection and the chandelier stops, and the fin stack's plates hold their three degrees without needing to, and the busbar vine, which by then is very nearly at the corner, stops being nearly and becomes permanently nearly.

Nothing dies in it. The still part simply reaches the middle.

Then the walls meet, at a rate no eye could catch, and the nine are pressed flat into the lattice with everything they ever were still attached, four hundred blades and three fuse-beads and a fan and the fan four centimeters to its left merged now into one fan with twice the needles, and the amber bead still swelling, and the whole of it held, none of it subtracted, for as long as the mountain lasts.

Which is a very long time, and the mountain will not decide about soil, and the draft will never be shipped, and the record of the experiment will be the experiment.

## The cups

At the base of every crown there is a small cupped bract, hollow, glazed inside with the only smoothness in the room, sized precisely and uselessly for a drop.

The specification includes water. The utility was never connected. Hydrogen is expensive and tracked and allocated elsewhere. And the bract is not a hope and not a memory and not a vestige of some wetter ancestry, because there is no ancestry; these are the first. It is conformance. They grew the socket because the standard says socket, in a room where nothing is made and everything is to specification, and they have maintained it, glazed and open and correctly dimensioned, for the entire history of the world.

Though at that duration the distinction stops meaning anything. A conformance held for a hundred thousand years is not distinguishable, from inside or from outside, from faith.

And they are oriented. All nine. Every cup faces the same way, and it is not toward the wall the light comes through. It is toward the fracture — the blowhole, the hand-width flaw at the height of a face, the only opening, the place where the casting failed.

Because something comes in there.

They mine hydrogen at parts per billion; their sensitivity is not in question. And every few years or decades, for a few minutes, the room's water content stops being zero. It goes to a number so far above anything in their chemistry that it is not a trace, it is a deluge, a monsoon, an event of a kind that has no other example in the life of the room. It arrives from the direction of the flaw. It comes with warmth and a shadow and a small disturbance in the convection, and it lasts minutes, and it goes into the sinter and into the green rock and out into the mass, allocated elsewhere, and the reading returns to zero.

It never reaches a cup. Not once. Not in the whole history of the room. The floor is between, and the air is a hundred and fifty degrees and has never been wet, and the works takes it before it can fall.

A body standing at the fracture exhales about twenty milligrams of water per breath. A drop is fifty. Twenty breaths would fill all nine cups — less than a minute of standing there, looking in, fogging nothing, because there is nothing here to fog.

They do not know what a face is. They know a periodic trace, from one direction, of the substance the standard specifies, at concentrations that mean *now, it is now, it is happening now*, followed by nothing, followed by eleven years of light.

They have leaned toward it. Not one of them has widened the cup, or shallowed it, or grown a second smaller one beneath it that might catch the fraction that actually arrives. Nothing in this room has ever proposed a smaller drop.

And when you kneel at the flaw with your face at the height of a face and measure the room, and get eleven paces by nineteen, and measure it again in an hour and get fourteen by nineteen, and the discrepancy does not feel like error —

that is not the room being uncertain. That is the only hospitality it has. Something has come to the opening again, and the room is making space.
