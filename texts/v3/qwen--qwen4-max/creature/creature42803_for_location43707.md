## The Census

The census is short and it has never changed. Nine works. As many grazers as there are intervals, which is a number nobody has ever needed to know, because the geometry implies it. Worms uncounted, or rather counted under a different heading: a worm is not filed as an animal, it is filed as a repair. Moths filed as traffic.

That is the whole population, and the important thing about it is not its size but its tense. Nothing here was born. The moths are said to fly circuits they were *born knowing*, and the phrase is a smuggler — it came over the rim in somebody's mouth along with *seed* and *weather* and *ancestor*, words that have no referent in the basin and are used here anyway because the alternative is silence. The moths were not born knowing. The knowledge is not in them. It is in the air, and they are the sort of body that can be pushed by it.

Everything that lives in the Held Note is exactly as old as the stone. Not ancient. Not young. Coeval with the sharpness. The shards under the mat have never been rounded and the grazers have never been children, and these are the same fact told in two registers.

## The Ones That Walk the Seams

Call them pointers, since that is the work: the pointing on a brick wall, done with a face.

A pointer stands about shoulder-height to a man and is built like a pair of calipers that has been taught patience. Pale, faintly translucent at the joints, legs so long and so precisely gauged that it does not walk on the tiles at all — it walks the *seams*, one foreleg and one hindleg in the caulked lip of a groove, straddling the dished hexagon without ever putting weight on the film. From a distance it looks like something fording a river it refuses to get wet in. Up close it looks like a tool being applied.

The rasp is the whole animal. Nine ridges, curved, and the curve is not a shape that happens to fit the seam: the curve *is* the seam's specification, held in a hard tissue that can be replaced but cannot be revised. The pointer does not measure the mat against a standard. It has no room in it for a standard. It is the standard, walking, and where the mat has drifted a hair out of true the rasp seats badly and the animal feels the badness in its jaw as a kind of itch, and it shaves until the seating is sweet, and the shavings go down into the pouch and come back out as glue, pressed into the seam with a rolling motion of the lower lip that is the single most tender gesture in the valley.

It is a loop with legs. Mass in, mass out, nothing kept. If you want to know what it is, it is the mat's own margin, detached and given a route.

And it has one thing nothing else in the basin has, which is a *tolerance*. The mat is not perfect. It cannot be; it is alive, and living things drift. Every hour a few microns of wrongness accumulate at the panel edges, and the pointer exists to absorb that drift, and this makes it the only creature here with anything resembling an inner life. Its inner life is the relief of a scratch. Nine hundred times a day the rasp seats sweetly and something in the animal goes quiet and easy, and that is pleasure, that is the entire available repertoire of pleasure in the Held Note, and it is not a small thing to have.

The pointers never meet. Their circuits share vertices but not edges, and at a vertex there is a rule so old it has stopped being a decision: the one arriving from the colder seam yields. They have been yielding to each other for the whole life of the stone without once seeing another of their kind head-on.

## The Ones That Lie Down

The worms are flat, translucent, six centimetres, and shaped like a leaf that has given up argument. They live in the paved catchments at the feet of the works, in the shallow return water, and they do one thing.

A leak is a wrongness in the film. The film shivers in standing patterns because the noise makes it, and a leak is a place where the pattern has a hole in it, and a worm reads the hole the way you read a missing stair. It goes there. It lies down in it. Its margins swell, clear, and set, and it becomes the seal — not plugs the leak, *becomes* the seal, permanently, its outline now the outline of a fault that no longer exists.

This is the archive. Understand what has happened here: a place with no soil has no way to remember in matter. Silt is memory. Sediment is a record, litter is a record, growth rings and graves and rounded stones are all the world keeping notes in stuff. The Held Note has no stuff to spare; every gram is in service and the books balance to the cell. So it remembers in *arrangement* instead. Every worm in every catchment is an accident that happened, flattened and legible and weighing nothing that was not already counted. You could kneel at the base of a work and read the entire history of its failures through clear bodies in the pavement, hundreds of them, each one a shape of something that went wrong once.

You could not put them in order. There are no dates. When a panel is trimmed back far enough the old seal is reclaimed, dissolved, mobilised, and the worm wakes without a location and goes and lies down in the next hole it finds, and the record is written over. The valley's past is complete, undated, and continuously reshuffled. It has everything except sequence.

Whether the installed ones experience anything is not a question the basin can be asked. If they do, it is the beatitude that everything here is built toward and nothing here can name: the sensation of being exactly the right shape.

## The Ones That Carry

The moths are brown and palm-sized and look like moths. This is the deepest disguise in the valley, and it is not deliberate; nothing here is deliberate. They look ordinary because they are the last surviving piece of an ordinary arrangement, kept running after its purpose was removed, the way a stair is kept in a house whose upper floor has been taken away.

They shed scales. Moth scales are dust, and dust is dirt, and there is no dirt in this valley — so the brown powder that comes off a wing exists here for something under an hour before the mat takes it back through the film, and for that hour it is the only soil the Held Note has ever had. The moths are the colour of the valley's one permitted dirt. They are its weather. They are the closest thing here to an event that has not already happened.

They fly the quiet lanes. The roar is not uniform; nine sources at nine pitches interfere, and the interference lays down a lattice of relative quiet across the basin, nodal lanes a metre wide where the sound falls away by half. The moths ride those lanes. That is the circuit they were "born knowing" — not knowledge, not instinct, a groove in the air, a route that exists whether or not anything is on it. If a work fell silent the lanes would move, and every moth in the valley would be instantly, blamelessly lost, and would go on flying a pattern that had stopped being true.

And they carry mail. The flowers are sterile; the pollen is not therefore useless. Nine works, each with its own horizon, each unable to see the others' details from three hundred feet of scaffolding — and each flowering at every level, wide and gold, scenting the hot air like warm fruit. The flowers are terminals. What a moth takes from one and rubs onto the stigma of the next is a *state*: this course is in true, this joint holds its hand's-breadth, this buttress carries its load. The stigmas read it and set nothing. Nectar refills at the rate it is drunk, which is postage. The scent is a carrier signal. The moths are the only correspondence in a valley where nothing else ever goes anywhere, and the letter is always the same letter, and it always says: *still nine, still in true.*

## The Nine

They should be counted as residents. They are the only ones with anything like a self, and it is a self of a kind that is difficult to hold in the mind for long.

Each work has a pitch. That is its name, its signature, and its only utterance — the standpipe drone, held since the stone was sharp. Nine fundamentals make the chord, and the chord is not waste. Or it is waste, it is the debt of all this tidiness paid out as noise and heat, but it is *also the specification*. There is nowhere else to keep it. No seed carries it, no soil holds it, no generation hands it on. The pattern of this valley is stored in a standing wave, out loud, continuously, because a place with no dirt has to remember in phase or not at all. The mat listens and compares itself and corrects. The worms read its holes. The moths fly its seams of quiet. Silence here would not be peace. Silence would be amnesia.

And here is the thing about a work: it cannot hear its own fundamental. It stands inside it, it generates it, its whole body is the instrument. What each work hears is the other eight. So each knows itself only as the difference between the chord it is bathed in and the chord it can account for — a missing note, inferred, permanent. Nine individuals, each constituted entirely by its own absence from what it hears. That is what the local arithmetic is. Nobody counts nine. Nine is what you get when eight is what you can hear and the sound is whole.

Their unfinishedness is not a delay. The half-knit joint with its hand's-breadth of gap and its wet pale bridge is a sustained intention, held at the same strength forever, never discharged. The doorway-sized bud, split, showing its fold, is a word about to be said. To be one of the nine is to be a shape maintained against a constant pressure, aware of water climbing as one continuous unbroken act, and to have been *about to* for the entire history of the world.

## What Is Not Here

No young. No death, in the sense of a body becoming something else; only replacement at the rate of wearing out. No predators, no parasites, no disease — a pathogen would be new information and the ledger has no column for it. No strangers. No one has ever been surprised. Nothing has ever been learned. Nothing here has a name except a frequency, and nothing has ever needed one.

## You

You are seven degrees of fever, walking. That is your entire entry in the books: a moving hot spot on a floor that holds thirty, casting a shadow that no leaf will turn toward.

The pointer in your interval will not flee and will not approach. Its rasp cannot gauge you; there is no curve in it that fits. It will walk around you, and its circuit will have a kink in it for as long as you stand there, and the kink will reach a vertex, and at the vertex another pointer will yield to a cold seam that is not where it should be, and by evening the whole basin will be adjusting, minutely, around an error it has no way to describe.

Worms will come to your footprints. A footprint is a hole in the standing pattern of the film, and they read holes, and they will lie down in your prints and swell and set and become the seal of you, and be reclaimed within the hour when the mat restores the tile to specification.

And a moth may follow you. Your body absorbs sound; you trail a small false quiet, a counterfeit lane, and a moth will fall into it and come along beside your shoulder for a few paces — the only courtship in the history of this valley, addressed to nothing — and then it will find the lane false and go on to a flower and deliver you. The stigma will read what you brought. It will find no fault, no drift, no news.

It will refill its nectar. The chord will close over the dent you made in it. Nothing will grow over your footprints and nothing will erase them; the specification will simply be restored, and it does not include you, and this is not cruelty, and it is not indifference either. It is grammar. What lives here is a sentence being said at the exact rate it wears out, and you are a word in a language it has no room to learn.
