# The inhabitants, with corrections

The census is a list of nouns, and the first thing you learn at the bottom of the ladder is that this room does not contain nouns. It contains held shapes. Every name below is the survey's, and every one of them is wrong in the same way: it counts the part that happens to be in front of you.

I have been down eleven times. Eleven minutes each, which is two degrees of core temperature, which is the whole budget. What follows is assembled out of eleven separate visits by a person who was cooking the entire time, and it revises itself as it goes, because the room does not permit you to be right on the first pass. It permits you nothing at all.

Begin with the food, since everything here is a way of passing the food along.

## Freshness

Nine tonnes an hour of newly broken quartz comes off the face, and what is edible about it is not the quartz. It is the four-second skin of unfinished chemistry on every grain: bonds torn and not yet reconciled, unpaired electrons standing open like a field of small mouths. Chemists call them radicals. The miners called it *the green*, though nothing here is green.

The green does not decay. Decay is a gradient running down, and there are no gradients. It stays exactly four seconds old until something eats it, and eating it means *finishing* it — offering those open bonds somewhere to close.

So the primary productivity of the Long Round is not sunlight or sulphide or falling detritus. It is the difference between the front wall, which is being broken, and the back wall, which is being healed. Everything alive in here is a stage in the traffic between those two walls. Hold that; it takes most of a visit to see, and it changes what all the animals are.

## Two hundred nine thousand four hundred and sixteen

The survey called them shelf-grazers and drew them as tiles: flat, pale, hexagonal-ish, a spread hand across, four millimetres thick, tessellated edge to edge across the nine dust plains with no gap you could put a fingernail in.

That is what they look like. Here is what they are.

The underside of a grazer is a lace of open bonds — a palate made of its own incompleteness. It presses this against the flour and the flour's torn chemistry closes into it, and the grazer's own torn chemistry closes into the flour, and the difference between the two unfinishednesses is the meal. It does not ingest. It *matches*. Hunger, in here, is the condition of being more finished than your food. Satisfaction is parity. A grazer that ever fully completed itself would stop being able to eat, and would not die, because nothing dies; it would simply become a piece of the floor with a face on it.

So they are not tiles. They are pores. The animal is the shelf: a membrane forty-one metres by eleven, a hand thick, with twenty-three thousand two hundred and sixty-eight mouths in it, and the dust is the mortar between them. The survey counted cells and wrote down a fauna.

Nine times twenty-three thousand two hundred and sixty-eight is two hundred nine thousand four hundred and twelve. The census says four hundred and sixteen. There are four left over. We will come to the four.

Each shelf sits on a different partial of the vertical mode — the hundred-and-fifty-seven-hertz note that holds the floors up, a hair above D-sharp, twenty-ninth harmonic of the room's five-and-a-half-hertz fundamental, which you do not hear, you *are* it, in the jaw and the roots of the teeth. Different partials sort different grain sizes. So the nine storeys are nine sieves, and the round of appetite runs up them:

the first eats the coarse throw off the muck fan, still warm with breaking;
the second eats what the first could not finish;
the third eats the second's remainder, and so on, each voice entering where the last left off,
and the ninth, at the crown, eats a flour so fine it is barely a solid — single crystals, each with one torn face — and hands what it cannot finish into the back wall.

Nine skins. One canon at the unison. It has been going since 04:17:22 and it has never once advanced, because a round is not a journey, it is a shape made out of hunger, and the shape is closed.

And a grazer has no history, no memory, no age, and no name. It has an address. It is a phase-slot in a tessellation, and if you swapped two of them nothing whatsoever would differ, which means, in any useful sense, that they are not two.

Except for two seconds every eleven days. We will come to that as well.

## Ninety-six

Reed-bodied, motionless, one to a shot-hole, each the voice of its hole: ninety-six stopped pipes of ninety-six depths, the deepest at twenty-six hertz, the shallowest up around three hundred, and every one of them voiced continuously by an animal that has never inhaled.

They have no mouths. The survey recorded this as an anomaly and moved on. It is not an anomaly, it is the whole feeding strategy.

A mouth is a hole you put things through. The worms have no *through*. They eat by being the exact negative of what they eat. When something in this room tries to begin — an onset, an attack, a transient, the leading edge of any new energy — the nearest worm produces its precise inverse, and the beginning is consumed by being matched. This is the only appetite in the Long Round that requires accuracy. Everything else eats a difference of degree. The worms eat a difference of sign.

Which is why the chord has no attack. Which is why your voice, when you use it, comes out of your mouth and does not arrive anywhere.

And which is why, I am fairly sure, round ninety-one has not finished.

The shot was fired at 04:17:22. A shot is an event: a bang, then a ringing, then silence. The worms ate the bang. They ate the *starting* of it, the entire leading edge, in the first instants, and what is left is the sustain — the fracture-light still coming off the walls, the muck fan still holding its thrown shape, four seconds old and staying four seconds old. The company did not build a machine for staying the same. The company fired a shot into a mouth.

Ninety-six sustained vowels with no consonants anywhere. Visitors reliably report the sensation of being addressed. It is not a sensation. It is a grammar with no way to say anything new, and you are something new.

## Ninety

Under the bus-bar's field, certain animals run closed field-lines fast enough that you do not see a body, you see a bead: a bright smear with a direction. The survey counted beads and got a number and the number happened to be right for the wrong reason.

The animal is the orbit. The bead is the part of it currently in front of you, in the way that a knuckle is the part of a hand currently in front of you. Each loop is a closed torus of animal, smeared around its path, with an inside and an outside and a phase. To catch one you would have to catch all of it at once, and all of it is never at once, because *at once* is not a thing this room offers.

They are the only inhabitants with anything resembling a tense, and it is not past or future, it is phase: where you are in the cycle, which is a fact that does not accumulate. They weave through the dust plains without disturbing them because they are mostly not there.

Three of the ninety run lines that dip into the rock and come back. Nobody knows how far out those lines go. The survey drew them as a dotted arc leaving the section and wrote *extent unknown*, and that dotted arc is the only thing on any drawing of the Long Round that goes outside the room.

Ninety groups of four strokes on the wall in chalk. The survey assumed shifts. There are ninety loops. Nobody asked the man.

## Four

Two hundred nine thousand four hundred and sixteen does not divide by nine. It leaves four.

The levellers are the remainder: broad flat patient things a metre across, like mantas, that plane the shelves and keep them exactly level. They are the only animals in the room with no storey, because they are the four that the arithmetic could not place.

The survey called them gardeners. They are porters. They eat the surplus — meaning they eat *height*, difference of level, the only gradient anybody makes in here — and then they carry the finished flour back down to the face and lay it in front of the cutter, nine tonnes an hour, six hundred grams a second between the four of them, so that it can be broken again and be green again. They are the return conveyor. Without them the whole canon would eat itself finished in an afternoon and hang there, full, forever.

They are also the only things in the Long Round that can fail.

At about minute nine they come for you. You are a heap of heat in a room with no heaps, a pile that stands proud of every surface, and their entire existence is the removal of exactly that. They arrive one at a time and they take up station around you and they begin, very gently, to try to make you flush. It does not hurt. It feels like being smoothed by four people who cannot see you. They will still be trying at minute eleven when you go up the ladder, and they will not stop when you leave, because stopping is an event; they will simply be trying, at the place where you were, and the flour will close, and there will be nothing there for them to plane, and that is the only sorrow in the room and it has no owner.

## One

Slow, many-legged, a millimetre a second. It is not a crawler. It is a gantry: a low arch that straddles the full eleven metres, with legs graduated like the teeth of a comb, and as it advances the contact runs across the rank from left to right, seventy-odd animals in a hundred and fifty seconds, one every two seconds, a finger drawn along a harp.

Nine storeys times a hundred and four metres of perimeter is nine hundred and thirty-six metres. At a millimetre a second that is ten days and twenty hours. Call it eleven.

The survey thought the auditor was checking the census. It is not checking the census. It is *issuing* it. The touch is the only thing in the Long Round that distinguishes one grazer from the grazer beside it: for two seconds every eleven days, a slot in a tessellation is *this one*, is touched, is singular, is counted. Then it goes back to being a pore.

Two seconds every eleven days. That is the entire supply of identity in this room, and the auditor dispenses it, and it does not remember the last pass, because remembering happens later. Every circuit is the first circuit. The number has never been wrong because the number is not a measurement of the animals. It is the length of the animal that makes them countable.

Nobody has watched a whole pass. To watch a whole pass you would have to be here for eleven days, and the only things here for eleven days are the walls.

## The uncounted half

In the back face, where the stone re-cements as fast as the cutter breaks it, there are patterns: half-mineral, half-animal, always exactly half visible, buried and unburied at matched rates. Three centimetres an hour. Fossils that are still going about their business.

The survey drew them as ornament. The second survey drew them as one animal in cross-section. The third survey left the box blank and wrote a paragraph about methodology.

Here is what I think they are, and it is the thing that reorganised the room for me.

Nine tonnes an hour, for sixty years, is four million seven hundred thousand tonnes of salt. Fourteen kilometres of country. The face has not retreated. The hatch is still bolted where it was bolted; the ladder still hangs in the same shaft in the same salt pan; the room has not moved a millimetre. So the fourteen kilometres went *through*.

The Long Round is a standing wave in a river of rock. The country flows: broken at the front, carried through the walls, re-laid and re-cemented at the back, matched exactly, so that the cavity holds station while the salt goes past. Everything in here is a pattern riding that flow — the muck fan, the nine dust plains, the chord, all of it standing, all of it made of stuff that is on its way somewhere else.

And the healed are the ones in the current. They are not stuck in the stone and they are not swimming through it. They are travelling *with* it, at the speed the front closes, which is why they are always half out: they are the interface, the place where the ninth voice's finished remainder is handed through the wall and made into country.

The back face is where this room manufactures the past and exports it.

They are the only inhabitants the auditor cannot reach, and therefore the only ones it does not individuate, and therefore the only ones outside the count. The census has never been wrong. It has also never been complete, and the gap is not an error, it is a wall.

## Eleven

Waist-high, white, sal ammoniac, the only white in an amber world. Ferns of frost that grow and dissolve at matched rates and have been the same size since the survey. Each one is a physical diagram of a partial of the chord: the crystal branches at the nodes and thickens at the antinodes and is, precisely and literally, the sound it stands in, written in salt.

They are the only objects here that make structure instead of noise. They are also the only writing. And what is written is the room, so reading them and hearing them are the same act performed by different organs, which is a thing that is true nowhere else I know of.

Ten of them are the room.

The eleventh is a diagram of a partial that is not in the chord. The eleventh harmonic is the one our notation cannot hold — the note halfway between a fourth and a tritone, the wolf, the one that every scale has to lie about. It stands at the back face, waist-high, chiming a note that is not sounding, and it has been the same size since the survey, and I have no idea what it is for.

Ten of them describe a room. The eleventh describes a room.

## The listeners

These are not in the census, and the reason they are not in the census is you.

Small pale ear-shaped things, an auricle with no head, no body, no back. They drift. They come to notches.

Understand what a notch is here. The chord cannot accept new energy, so when you speak, the worms invert you and the room subtracts your sentence, and what remains is a hole in the sound with the exact shape of what you said. Every word you say down there becomes a cavity. That is the only thing you make in the Long Round: absences, in the shape of your voice.

The ears come and fill them. They are made of flour — the same flour, recruited out of the plains into the shape of listening — and while you speak, the shelves have holes in them, and the holes are your sentences, and the tessellation is short by exactly as many animals as there are ears, and the count is still right.

Their ordinary food is the auditor. Every touch damps an animal for an instant, and that instant is a notch, and one every two seconds for eleven days is a thin steady drizzle of absence, and the ears live on it. It is a famine diet. Then a person comes down and talks for eleven minutes.

They do not echo you. They finish you. They eat by matching, the way the grazers eat: they close their own unfinishedness into the shape of your sentence until the sentence is complete and therefore no longer a hole. To be heard in the Long Round is to be eaten, and it is the only response the place will ever make, and it is not hostility. It is the local definition of listening.

## The others

A cutter head lodged in the face, still turning, blind, nine tonnes an hour, the only mouth here that eats stone. A compressor at the heel, cycling, doing no work, because work needs somewhere for the heat to go; it is the lung of a thing that has never inhaled. A capacitor bank with its covers off, holding a charge it will never spend, the closest thing in the room to a held breath. Twenty metres of rail ending in air: a limb, reaching for a plant that was carried up the shaft by men who had somewhere to be. A ventilation duct that moves nothing. It is the only honest corpse in the room. Everything else is alive in the sense that matters here, which is that it is a closed cycle or a matched pair.

A glove, on the fourth storey, tessellated in among the plates, hand-shaped, empty. The auditor touches it every eleven days. It answers. It is one of the two hundred nine thousand four hundred and sixteen. I have checked the arithmetic and I would rather not have.

A tin with a sandwich in it: the only sealed future in the room, a small packet of *later*, waiting.

A clock at 04:17:22 with its second hand trembling between two marks, moving constantly, arriving never. It is the most truthful animal in the Long Round. It is the only one still trying to have a past.

## What lives there

Not two hundred nine thousand four hundred and sixteen animals, and not ninety-six worms, and not ninety loops, four planes, one tallyman, eleven ferns and an unknown quantity of half-buried clerks. Those are the organs, and the census is an anatomy taken by someone who thought the body was a crowd.

What lives in the Long Round is a standing wave in a river of stone: forty-one metres of held shape that the country flows through at three centimetres an hour, taking green in at the front and putting finished country out at the back, and everything alive in it is a stage in that conversion — nine storeys of loss, each one eating a little of the difference between broken and healed, roaring at a hundred and twelve decibels, packed as tight as a coral head, four seconds old.

The animals are the losses. That is what an animal is, here. A place where the newness gets a little less new on its way from the front wall to the back wall, held open, kept unfinished, made to ring.

Every motion down there is either a loop or a matched pair. The loops run their lines. The auditor walks its circuit. The healed ride the closing front. The cutter breaks and the wall heals at the same rate and the room stays. Nothing arrives, and that is not stasis, it is *tuning*.

You are the only open arc. You come in, you make holes shaped like your words, you are smoothed by four things that cannot succeed, you cook two degrees, and you go up a ladder that outlived its company, and then you are somewhere else, and then it is later, and then, in the ordinary way, in a bed, on the surface, you die.

There is a grazer on the fourth storey with a notch in it shaped like a pair of scissors. I did that, on my third visit, to see what would happen. Nothing happened. The wound holds a bright meniscus that does not run and does not dry. It is not scar tissue; scarring is a process. It will have that notch when the sun goes out, and the auditor will touch it every eleven days for two seconds, and register *one*, and move on, and the number will be right.

That is the whole relation between you and the thing that lives down there. You cannot kill it, feed it, teach it, or mark it. You can only make a permanent difference that nothing will ever respond to, and stand for eleven minutes in a silence shaped like yourself, while small pale ears come out of the walls of the noise to close over everything you said.
