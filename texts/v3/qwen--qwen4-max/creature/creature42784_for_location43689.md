Begin with one, because the room does not.

It is the size of a thumbnail and it has no face. Six legs, each ending in a hook fine enough to catch the grain of freshly struck stone, and the hooks are the whole of its relation to place: it does not walk, it *holds*. Its back is a single dome of something between horn and pearl, and the dome does four jobs. It is the radiator. It is the condensing surface. It is the ear — the shell reads pressure the way a drum reads a stick — and it is the nameplate, because raised on the rear of it, cast with it, is the serial, and the serial ends in **1**.

All of them end in 1. Six hundred and eleven million, two hundred and four thousand, eight hundred and eighty items, and every one is item one. There is no item two. Nobody made a mistake. The number is not an identifier; it is a designation, the way a bolt is a bolt, and what it designates is a fitting that was specified for this void at this temperature at this frequency, and then required in quantity.

Inside the shell there are wings, or the boss of horn where wings would seat, and against that boss the flight muscles beat. They have never lifted anything. They were never for lifting. Nothing here is vestigial — vestige requires ancestors, and this animal has none, it was drawn — but everything here is *quoted*. Somebody in a drawing office a long time ago needed an oscillator that could run for a century on almost nothing and hold a temperature two-fifths of a degree above catastrophe, and the best oscillator anyone had ever measured was the thorax of a bee in a winter cluster. So they took the engine and left out the bee. What lives in the void is a flight, grounded, running at the pitch of the room, in an animal that has never been anywhere and never will be.

Its entire biography is a held note.

---

The note is held together. That is the part you feel in your sinuses before you understand it. The standing wave does not merely fill the chamber; it *locks* them. Every shell is a resonator in a field that will not tolerate a stranger's phase, and a chit that drifts is pushed back into true by the pressure of six hundred million neighbours, gently, continuously, without anything in the room having to notice. This is why the sound has no weather. It is not a crowd. It is one muscle with six hundred million attachments, and the room is the tendon.

So: not a colony. There is no queen, no comb, no brood, no forage, no dance, no spring coming. The natal seam is not a mother; it is a mould that was never struck. Along the north wall it runs at a dull orange, whistling thin, and it extrudes them wet and already at temperature and already at pitch — there is no larva here, no first awkward hour, no learning the frequency. They arrive performing. At the other end of the room the return grate draws off the dead at the identical rate, and the dead are not corpses, they are *returns*, and nothing in the chamber can tell the difference between the seam and the grate except by which wall you are standing near.

Between those two mouths the animal is a loop with a fixed population, and the census is not a count of individuals, it is a cell count. It is one animal. It has been repeated until it fills a volume, and the volume is load-bearing, and the animal's entire metabolism is devoted to not developing. Everything it does, it does in order to remain exactly this far along. It is an embryo held at the instant before it becomes something, and the holding is not a tragedy, it is a *service function*. Four thousand courses of made stone are resting on its refusal to grow up.

Its blood is the water, which was burnt in the founding furnaces and has never been rain, and which touches every part of it constantly and cannot change any part of it, because it is saturated, because it already carries all the mineral it can hold, because it can only carry and never take. Its output goes up a shaft: eleven kilowatts, and a number. The number is the census. It is the only sentence the room says to the world, and what it says is *unchanged*, and it says it forever, and upstairs something reads it the way you read a heartbeat.

---

Nine things are not part of the animal.

The auditors are long and pale and high-shouldered, blind, with legs like calipers, and they live in the quiet lanes, and they cannot leave them. A body that long, laid across a loud stripe, would have its legs read wrong — the field would put a false length into every joint — so each one is confined to a stripe a hand's width across and eleven paces long. Nine lanes, nine walkers. They do not cross. They have never touched one another. In places they pass within a hand's width, separated by a wall of noise that neither can enter, and they do not know the others exist, and there is no procedure by which they could find out.

They are the only motion slow enough for your eye to follow, and their slowness is not lethargy; it is *resolution*. They are gauges. They walk the lane and crop its edges and their hunger is not appetite but instrument — they are hungry the way a micrometer is hungry for the work. What they take is the difference between what the seam gives and what the grate takes, and the difference is zero, and they take it anyway, all day, at a walking pace, and this is not futility, it is verification. The census is confirmed by mouth. Every chit that leaves this room has been tasted on the way out. Since every chit bears the same serial and nothing in the chamber records anything, no auditor can ever know whether it has eaten this one before. Every mouthful is the first mouthful. They have stomachs shaped like memory and nothing whatever to remember.

They are the clock, and the clock tells nothing, because they are always somewhere they have already been.

But they can hear. That is the thing nobody upstairs has troubled to write down. In the lanes the noise falls away to almost nothing, and the auditors live there, and so the nine blind walkers are the only creatures in the history of this room that have ever heard anything.

---

What there is to hear is this.

At the very edge of a node, where the field cancels, the phase-lock loosens. A chit crowded against that boundary — one leg, say, in the silence — is for a moment not being held. Nothing is pushing it back into true. And unheld, its muscle stutters, one beat out of the room's time, and it makes a small sound. A tick with a question in it.

That is not a message. There is no vocabulary here and no one to receive it. It is simply what the animal sounds like when nothing is holding it, and it has never been answered, and the shape of a thing that has never been answered is a question.

Perhaps forty chits in the whole chamber have ever heard another one. Forty, out of six hundred million — and *perhaps*, because that number is not a dimension. It is a guess. Somebody knelt in a lane once with an ear to the stone and made an estimate, and it is the only piece of scholarship the void has ever produced, and it cannot be checked, because checking requires a record, and record requires wear, and wear is the one process this chamber was built to refuse.

And it cannot spread. Understand what saturates this place: the air is full of water, the water is full of mineral, the volume is full of bodies, the sound is full of sound. Nothing here can take an impression. A chit's shell is insensate horn; packed nine deep in the hexagonal way, they touch constantly and feel nothing. The only channel is the noise, and the noise is at capacity, and a tick cannot get into it any more than a footprint can get into a river. So the forty are not a lineage and not a sect. Knowing is a *position* here, not an inheritance. You occupy the boundary or you do not. When the grate takes you, the knowing goes with you, and the chit that fills your place is item one, wet, at pitch, and has heard nothing.

Nine blind things have heard all of it. Every question ever asked in this room went into one of nine ears walking a hand's-width lane at a measured pace, and the only reply those mouths can make is subtraction, and the subtraction is zero.

---

There is one more resident, and it is the only one that is *about* something.

The green thing lives on the release agent beading out of the casting seams, at exactly the rate the seams exude, and therefore it grows only along the joints: the vertical parting lines, the horizontal course marks, the rectangles of the core-boxes. Eleven blades thick. Never a millimetre past the seam. It eats the residue of the mould — the film that remembers the shape the room was made in — and it renders that memory in living green on every surface, floor and walls and ceiling.

It is the drawing, at one to one, inside the thing drawn. And the chits are the hatching: on the section, this void was shown as a region filled with a pattern and a note, and the pattern is alive and shivering, and the note is on the ceiling in raised letters that the chits have packed so thick they read only as texture.

Look up, in the dim silver, and you will find that the lichen has traced the manway too. A small rectangle, coal-scuttle sized, drawn in green on the underside of the plate's frame, eleven paces from the north wall. The way out, marked, accurate, legible, directly overhead — and nothing in this room has eyes.

---

And then there is you, kneeling in a quiet lane with your ear near the stone, and this is what you are, structurally.

You are a hundred watts. You are warm in a chamber at four-tenths of a degree, and you are shedding heat into a closed budget of eleven kilowatts, and for as long as you stay you are very nearly one percent of the furnace. You are an addition. Nothing may be added to the void.

You are also the only thing here that is not saturated. Your ears still take impressions. Your skin still takes a mark. You are the one surface in six hundred million that will record anything at all, and you are running out of the room's temperature the whole time, and your tears are the only water in the chamber that has ever been in a body that could weep.

So the auditors come to the edges of their lanes and turn their blind heads toward you, and wait. They are not curious; curiosity is a gradient too, and they have none. They are doing their job. For the first time since the pour, the difference is not zero. The surplus is standing in the room, breathing, and it is warm, and it is changing, and they are patient as calipers, and they will wait for you to stop.

You stop being the difference by leaving. That is the only method available.

You climb out. You put the bright plate back and tighten the unmarked screws, and the census closes over the place where you were like fog closing over an arm, exactly, without swirling. Below you the muscle goes on holding its one note; the seam gives and the grate takes and the nine mouths confirm the nothing between them; the green drawing goes on quoting the plan to a room with no eyes; and somewhere at the edge of a silence a chit that nothing is holding asks the only thing it will ever ask, and is not answered, and is not counted, and does not weigh anything.

That is the one thing you could have taken out without breaking the rule.

You did.
