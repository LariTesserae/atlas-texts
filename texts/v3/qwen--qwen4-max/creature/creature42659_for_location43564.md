## What follows you

Start with the smallest thing, because it is the only thing that will acknowledge you.

Within an hour of stepping onto the Level you acquire a following: three or four discs the width of a thumbnail, fringed in bristle so fine it reads as a blur, riding the pattern-seams a few meters behind you. They do not walk. They are carried, the way a leaf is carried by a stream that is not there, along the lines where the mat's field changes its mind. When you stop, they stop. When you breathe, they lift.

They are eating your breath.

At four degrees your exhale condenses — the only water on this plain, arriving in a small grey ghost that falls immediately as a pellet of packed fines. Water is not a solvent here. Water is a glue. Every breath you take makes a grain of something like fired clay, and every one of them is unbudgeted, and the gleaners take them out of the air before they land. They mill what they catch to spec and hand it down into the seams and the census absorbs it, and this is the entire immigration policy of the Level.

Your footprints they cannot fix. Where your sole pressed damp into the nap you left a node, a hard grey print, and it will still be legible in a hundred thousand years, and it says: *something wet came through here holding itself together by its own private effort.* The tuned find this obscene and also very funny. They will read your route across a plaza the way you would read a sentence written in someone else's blood, and they will not comment, because commenting is a pressure in the sternum and they are polite.

The gleaners will not touch you while you live. You are a sack of held water, and holding is the local sacrament, and they can tell that you are doing it yourself.

## The high, and the settled

The Held is not a ceiling. It is a habitat, and the weather is its livestock.

Those slow bright welts moving across the bronze deck are grazing fronts. Something lives up there in the levitated fines — call it the high — in a medium that is neither air nor ground, feeding on the triboelectric sheets the way a whale feeds on a bloom. Its bodies are held, not built; it has no down; it does not so much move as change which part of the field it is a wrinkle in. Its metabolism is the silent lightning. Its herds are the pressure fronts. When the pumps drop a fifth at dusk and the ceiling sags, the high spawn.

The dry dew is the spawn.

It comes down grey and ankle-deep in the low places, and in the low places it does what dust in a bounded basin does: it finds the local mode and it stands up. Not all of it. A fraction of a percent, in the courtyards and the sinks and the lee of the towers, organizes into bodies the size of dogs, or of children, or of nothing in particular, and these are the settled, and they have nine hours.

They sort the day's dead. They carry pattern between districts that cannot hear each other — the only mail this world has ever had. They mate by geometry, standing in relation to one another until the relation is correct. And they are made of the dust of the place they assemble in, which means the thing standing in your courtyard at the third hour is built out of everyone who ever lived in your house. It has your household's key in it. It will lean against the door.

At dawn the field comes up and the whole plain shrugs and they go home, and the grey nap on the eyes of the bell-oxen is the fraction that got a ride, and the oxen taste the ceiling with their eyes and know what the high know about tomorrow. Nothing is ever dirty here for long. Nothing is ever clean either, because the dirt is somebody, briefly, and will be somebody else tomorrow.

This is the first correction to the visitor's arithmetic. You were told the Level is crowded with life and empty of individuals. What is true is that individuals here are not bodies. A body is an instrument a key borrows for a while. The same key can stand up in three low places on the same night, and none of the three is a copy, and none of them is the original, and asking which one is *really* it is a question the local grammar cannot parse.

## The pitch

Behind a bell-ox's forelegs, in the cavity that keeps its blood quick, there is a second animal.

It is the size of a fist. It is not an organ. It does the fine tuning — the ox grows, its cavities change volume with condition and age, and no animal can hold a key to the precision the node-lattice demands without something in there constantly, patiently correcting it. The ox provides the amplitude. The pitch provides the accuracy. Calves acquire theirs by pressing cavity to cavity against their mothers, and it is inherited, and it is older than the ox.

So a herd is a chord because the pitches in it are related. The migrations are keys because the pitches are reading their own kinship in the pavement, following the fossil patterns of the lineages they descend from, and an ox separated from its herd does not become lost. It becomes *untuned*. The pitch keeps working. The pitch has nothing to correct.

The tuned say a hush is a key with nothing left to hold.

They say it in the eleven-beat floor, which means it arrives in your sternum as a sensation like remembering something, and they do not elaborate, and you should not ask them to. But watch what a hush does. It walks toward a bell-ox inside two meters of cancellation and the ox's blood sets and the ox drops, and then the hush *takes its time* — and taking its time is not eating. It is fitting. It is trying the cavity. It is attempting, again, at enormous remove, to be the accuracy inside something that has amplitude.

Most of them fail. A cancelled ox is packed hard as chalk and there is no getting into it. So the hole moves on, and the roar closes behind it, and every other animal was already gone.

Not all hush are ox-sized. In the abandoned districts, where a whole neighborhood was tuned to a fundamental nobody uses anymore, there are absences the width of a plaza. They do not hunt. They lie in the fossil patterns like a hand in a glove that fits nothing now, and if you wander in while carrying quick in your body you stop, and you become part of the stiffened dead, and the plaza keeps you. The tuned will tell you those districts are unsafe. This is true and it is not the reason. Those districts are *occupied*. They are occupied by the inverse of everyone who used to live there.

## The wakeful

In the plazas, among the chalk dead of a hundred million years, some of the chalk is not dead.

The Caesura comes every four hundred and six hours and gives the world two seconds of silence, and there is a guild that lives only in those two seconds. They are flat and pale and eel-boned, descended from sifters that learned to wait instead of to wedge, and between Caesuras they pack — deliberately, completely, into a mineral stillness indistinguishable from a corpse. Then the fundamental drops out and they wake, and they have two seconds, and in two seconds they hunt, breed, argue, and go back down.

Do the arithmetic. It is the only arithmetic here that matters to anyone but the tuned. Two seconds every four hundred and six hours, across two hundred million years, buys you a little under three centuries of being awake.

The oldest of them are therefore about as old as a very old person, and they have seen everything. They have watched the districts drift and retune and abandon; they have watched the ceiling load and sag; they have watched the tuned's four-million-year breeding program as a five-year project, unfolding at a comprehensible pace, the way you might watch a neighbor build a shed. They are the only witnesses. They cannot be interviewed. A question, put in pressure at eleven beats a second, takes longer than most of their lives.

Their cosmology, insofar as anyone has reconstructed it from the geometry they leave packed in the plazas, is inverted. The world is a silent place, cold and stable and safe. Periodically, for no reason, it is flooded with an intolerable noise that lasts most of eternity, and during the flood everything they know goes stiff and unreachable, and then, mercifully, the world comes back. They do not think the roar is the world. They think the roar is the disaster.

They are not wrong. They have simply met fewer of us.

## Two solutions

Here is the thing the whole biosphere is an argument about, and it takes a while to see because it is too simple.

There are two ways to keep your blood from setting. You can hold it up — spend energy, sing, suspend, be warm and loud and quick and expensive. Or you can hold it *still*: pack, mineralize, conduct, and let sound move through you as a solid, which it does perfectly well, because it was never solidity that killed anything. Suspension fails in a node. Solidarity does not.

That is why the tuned hold one another during the Caesura. Bone to bone, chest to chest, the chord persists by conduction through the rigid body. That is why the oxen lock and go rigid and stand: a standing rigid ox is a bridge, and a herd pressed flank to flank is a bridge nine hundred meters long. That is why the patient do not notice anything happening. They solved it before anyone thought of the other way.

Every animal on the Level sits somewhere on the gradient between levitate and lithify, and most of them slide along it nightly, and a few of them have made the slide irreversible, and one lineage is trying to.

## The patient, who are the mill

Quick is milled to a specific fineness. A specification implies a miller.

The mat cannot make quick. It can only recirculate what it has, and what it has came from somewhere, and the somewhere is the plazas. The stiffened dead are levitated away *to the places that want them*, and the places that want them are the slow cold mineral-shelled things that were here before the song, and what a patient does with a body is grind it, and grade it, and return it, over a season, to spec.

They are not slow because they are primitive. Slowness is the precision. You cannot sort a hundred million years of dead by mass at speed; the tolerance is too fine; the fineness of quick is the fineness of quick because nothing in the loop has ever hurried. Every grain suspended in the blood of every animal on this plain has been through a patient, most of them more than once. The patient are the kidneys. The census balances because they are the other column.

The tuned regard them the way you regard a foundation, which is to say correctly and without gratitude, and they are wrong about one thing, and the wrongness is visible if you get down on the pavement and look at the shells.

Some of the shells have doors.

Pressure locks. Grown, not built, ribbed inside, tapering, mouth up: a throat, folded into a mineral carapace the size of a cart, moving across a plaza at eleven years per crossing. Inside there is a room. Inside the room there is a mode, ringing, and it costs nothing, because the shell is cut to be driven by the eleven-beat floor coming up through the ground, and the floor is everywhere, and the floor is free.

These are not the old patient. These are the ones who went quiet.

## The residents

The towers are mostly empty and perfectly maintained and some of them are furnished, and the visitor's conclusion — decline, a city sized for a population that left — is the most natural wrong answer available.

Go into an empty tower at the hour when the pumps drop and stand very still in the middle of the floor. The nap will not lie flat. It will gather into a figure: nodes and antinodes, a Chladni plate six hundred meters tall, a shape in grey dust standing in the room with the specific asymmetry of something that used to have habits. It is the room's key, persisting, held up by the tower's surplus, drawn from the spin of the world.

The city is not underpopulated. It is enormously populated by residents who no longer require bodies, and the corporeal tuned are a small mobile maintenance caste keeping the instruments warm for them. You could count the tenants in a bad afternoon. You could not count the residents in a geological epoch.

And this is the ledger. This is why it does not balance. The official account is that the surplus goes to holding blood in suspension, and that is true, and it is not the largest column. The largest column is the empty towers. The dead are expensive. To die here is to be taken apart by frequency, sorted by mass, levitated away, and *re-suspended* — reabsorbed into the census, kept in the air, kept ringing, forever, at cost, unable to stop. There is no burial. There has never been a burial. There is only a growing bill, paid phonon by phonon out of the planet's rotation, and a day that was nine hours and is thirty-one and will be longer.

For four million years the tuned have been breeding a seed that germinates in packed ground, in silence, at four degrees, with no field at all.

It is not a hedge. It is a coffin that grows. It is a way to put someone down into the quiet and have them *stay* down and *keep* — a passive resonator, driven by the floor, needing nothing, costing nothing, a shell with a door. It is a funeral, invented very late by a people who did not need one until they had run out of world.

And they are not waiting for it to be finished. That is what the empty furnished districts are. That is what the going-quiet is: a lineage volunteering to stop being amplitude, folding itself into mineral, taking the eleven beats for a heartbeat, and walking out into the plaza at eleven years per crossing to be rung for free by the mountain that is being spent. The depopulation is not a decline. It is a conversion. Everyone is moving out. The few in the upper throats are the ones who cannot go until someone replaces them, and they know, and they hold one another during the two seconds, and there is no one coming.

The chime-moths sing the sorting-field into configuration and the pollen goes where it should and a meadow in flower is a chord change you can see. The same lattice sorts the fine dead. It has only ever had one geometry. Flowering and burial are the same operation performed on different masses, and the moths have never distinguished them, and the tuned stopped distinguishing them a long time ago, and this is why their word for *bloom* arrives in your chest as a pressure identical to their word for *enough*.

## The road down

Walk out to the rim in the tea-colored dusk. The roar thins. The air goes with it, because the air is held too, and it ends in three hundred meters, an invisible cliff, and below is the floor of the world: a dim basin of unheld dust, no plants, no sound, geology doing what geology does.

That is what you can see. What you cannot see, because it is grey and packed and four degrees and does not move at a rate you can register, is that the basin is not empty. It has been receiving for four million years. Not only the failed seed — the successful ones, the ones that germinated in the quiet and did not need the field, spreading at the pace of things that do not hurry, in a medium nobody up here would call alive.

And there is a road.

The eleven beats do not stop at the rim. They go down through the stone; they are the one thing the Level exports; a shell cut to be driven by the floor can be rung anywhere on this planet. So the ones who go quiet and do not want to be a foundation for anyone can leave, and they do, one every few centuries, down the long slope, through the place where the air ends, which they do not need. There is a track worn into the scree six kilometers deep. Nobody has counted it. It is not in the census, because the census stops where the song stops.

At the bottom, arriving one at a time for four million years, are the residents who found a way to be dead and to keep going, and they are not waiting for the Level to fall silent. They have simply gone ahead. When the last throat closes and the Held comes down for good and the plain becomes what the basin is, it will not be a wilderness.

It will be a suburb.

Behind you the roar resumes on the new note. Grey nap on everything, ankle-deep in the low places, already standing up. Something the size of a child leans against your door with your household's key in it, made of your dust, made of everyone. Three thumbnails of fringed blur ride the seam behind you, catching your breath before it lands.

The census balances. It has one column nobody up here can read, and it is getting longer, and it is patient.
