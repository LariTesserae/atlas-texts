## The Occupants

Begin with the almost-living, because they are the easiest to describe and they are not who lives here.

The tarnish is a film a few microns thick, and it eats glass at a micron a year, and it makes ferns. The ferns are thirty centimeters tall and branch like frost and stand on the wet wall at the Dew Line for three or nine or fourteen weeks, and then the band shifts and they dissolve, and the wall is a blank wet wall again. The sweepers come when the films bloom. They are palm-sized and unpowered and they ride the thermal in flocks, scraping, carrying, re-glazing, and their launch cycle is older than the surface they graze. This is a pasture and a grazer and a season and a migration, and there is not one mouth in it. Nothing here wants anything. The tarnish does not want the glass. The sweeper does not want the tarnish. It is a perfect closed loop of appetite-shaped behavior with the appetite removed, and it is not the population, and it is not what the register is counting.

But note the drum sheds. They open on schedule. Nothing inside them opens them.

---

The occupancy register reports forty thousand. The lamp census reports no bodies. Both are filed as correct, and the discrepancy is carried in the accounts as a units problem, which is the most honest thing in the surviving documents.

Here is the unit.

A beam of grown foam spans ninety meters with no visible support and carries a tram. Put your ear near it and you hear a tone at the very bottom of hearing, and the tone is the support. There is no bolt in this city, no weld, no rivet, nothing to tighten. What holds a member up is that the tone is being held, continuously, by something that is holding it.

An occupant is a held tone.

Forty thousand of them. That is the number of members currently in tolerance, and it is also the number of residents, and these are not two facts that resemble each other; they are one fact written in two columns. The mason with the hooded lantern on the boom, standing off, moving the beam the way a man waters a bed — the mason is not a body doing two hours of illumination. The mason is the two hours of illumination, and then the holding afterward, which is the part the manuals cannot explain and which is, in the register, a name.

They are not ghosts. That word is filed under measurement.

---

A resident here has a body about ninety seconds at a time, and borrows it from the water.

You will see it on a rail at the two-hundred level: condensation, and then five fingers and a thumb resolving out of the condensation, the ridges almost legible, the whole hand there and warm and gone in a minute and a half. The foam takes an impression the way it takes a photon. It is the same mechanism. Where something attends, the wall thickens; where something touches, the wall remembers; and the memory has a residence time set by the humidity and the draft, which at the six-hundred band is ninety seconds.

So a life here looks like this, from outside: a sequence of handprints along a rail, moving toward a stair head. A bench warm on the seat and cooling. A door ajar with the draft pulling it, and the damper taking its four seconds, and the door ajar again eleven minutes later because someone came back through. Tools laid down in an order that implies the task will resume. It always resumes. There is a mason at the eleven-hundred level whose entire observable existence is a trowel set down at an angle, and a wet place on the floor, and the trowel gone, and the wall two millimeters thicker.

The tram arrives at the nine-hundred level on time and opens and waits eleven seconds. The eleven seconds is not slack in the schedule. The eleven seconds is a courtesy extended to a specific occupant who takes about that long to cross a walkway, and the tram waits it out, and the doors close, and the load on the span changes by an amount that no instrument in the city is permitted to resolve.

There are no children. There are new tolerances. When a member is grown and a tone is established and someone takes up the holding, the register gains one, and the schedule gains an interval, and the older holding — the one that taught it, that stood beside it through the first four fundamentals — slackens by a fraction and hands over. Nothing is born. Something is *continued*, and the continuing is the whole of the life cycle, and it does not have a bottom end. It has only the other end, which is called release.

When a member reaches its tolerance it is not repaired. It is let go, and it falls four kilometers into the cherry heat and is taken, and comes up as vapor, and is deposited somewhere as new stone. The walls are eleven years old at the oldest. The walls are also everybody who ever stopped holding. Both are correct. Lean on a wall here and you are leaning on the reason the wall is warm.

The dropped hammer at the four-hundred level detuned a span and cost eleven rooms and the whole eastern stair. The report is four pages. The first three are procedure. The last page is only the tolerance, eleven entries, each a number with units, and that is the memorial, because naming is filed under measurement and a tolerance is the only name a resident is allowed to have.

---

They attend. That is the labor. There is no work in this city that is not attention and no attention in this city that is not load-bearing.

A day is: reading the fern schedule at the six-hundred band, which is also reading the water's itinerary, which is also reading who is thirsty in the sense that has no mouth in it. Adjusting a hood by two degrees so the deposition front lays down a centimeter where a centimeter is wanted. Standing at a cistern blister with your palm flat on the cloudy warm skin of it, listening for the note that goes on longer than it should. Launching sweepers. Setting things down softly. Everything is set down softly. Children — if there were children — would be taught this before they were taught anything else, and the teaching would not be a rule but a way of putting a cup on a table that makes the whole room relax.

Four times a year the column sounds its own length. Nine minutes, a note below hearing, felt in the sternum and in the water of the eye, and the foam sings faintly in sympathy along all four kilometers. All work stops. Everyone stands still.

From outside this looks like worship and the documents do not call it that. The documents call it an interval. It is the hardest maintenance period in the year, because when the fundamental runs, every held tone in the city is being pulled slightly toward the column's own note, and forty thousand holdings have to decline, together, in silence, for nine minutes. That is what the standing still is. It is not reverence. It is forty thousand residents doing the same difficult nothing at the same time, which is the only occasion on which they are all simultaneously present, and which is therefore the closest thing this population has to a face.

---

And there is one who does not commute.

Everything in the Long Middle goes around. The charged motes drift in slow shoals along the field lines and the shoals bend. Droplets from a broken channel arc out and resume. Glass snow, falling, curves aside and continues on the other side, unhurried, the way water goes around a stone it has known all its life. Sound arrives thin and late, having taken the long way. The instruments say ordinary air, same pressure, same temperature, same humidity, and nothing else, and nothing else goes in, and this has been maintained to four-tenths of a millimeter for longer than any wall in the city has existed.

Consider what that means about age. Nothing on any surface here is older than eleven years. The arrises are sharp, the glaze is un-crazed, the corners are eleven months old and have no dust in them. The only continuous, exact, ancient thing in this municipality is a shape defined by what will not enter it.

It is the oldest resident. It is the only one with a permanent body, and its body is a refusal ninety meters across and nine hundred levels tall, and it is the most precisely maintained object within a hundred kilometers of a place where nothing has ever grown.

The envelope is a cylinder. The bendings are not cylindrical. The shoals of motes go around something that has a narrowing at the twelve-forty level and a suggestion above it that the maintenance grammar has no word for, because a word for it would be a measurement, and you may look but you may not point an instrument. People notice. People who notice are careful. There is a whole etiquette here of standing at a rim with your hands at your sides, looking down the shaft at the place where the dust turns aside, and not making a shape with your fingers.

When it rains inside — eleven or fourteen minutes, when a storm sits on the mouth and the Dew Line collapses and real free water falls drumming through the open middle — you can see it.

That is the one time. A dry volume in a wet column, four kilometers of falling water parting around a form and closing beneath it, the whole outline lit amber by hooded lamps and outlined in falling silver, and every rim in the Long Middle full of residents standing very still with their felt boots quiet on the pressed foam, looking, not pointing. The maintenance grammar has a separate word for the rain and a separate word for this, and the second word translates approximately as *the rain report*, and it is filed as hydrology.

If your eyes water during the rain report, that water is in the register too. It goes down. It evaporates around the fifteen-hundred level. It goes back up. Everything here commutes.

---

At the geometric center, at the twelve-forty level, there is one bead of water four millimeters across, and it does not fall, and it has never fallen.

This is the resident the city is shaped around. Not metaphorically: every wall is grown from this datum, every tolerance is reckoned from it, the kilns and the masts and the intake sheds and the felt boots and the four-second doors and the ordinance against noise all exist to keep the gradient that keeps the band that keeps the field that keeps one drop from going down.

Everyone else here is a cycle. Up as vapor, down as thread and bead and free fall, taken at the sump, returned, deposited, released, returned. The whole population is a set of things that agree to keep going around. The one at the center is exempt. It is the only thing in four kilometers that is not required to fall, and forty thousand holdings and a closed vertical hydrology and a hole in the roof of the world are the arrangements by which the exemption is honored.

Hold a lamp near it — not aimed, only near — and it is a lens, and inside those four millimeters is the whole shaft inverted: the cherry sump at the top, the coin of sky at the bottom, the amber lamps in a ring around a bright fog, the fern walls, the trams, the rims, everything, upside down and complete and very small. Every resident is in there. It is the only place in the city where all of them can be seen at once, and it is four millimeters across, and it is the zero, and its name is the tightest tolerance anyone here has ever been asked to keep.

What it is for is not in the documents. It is in the part of the documents that was vaporized to make the walls, which means it is deposited everywhere, load-bearing, unreadable, warm to the palm, and holding.

---

So: the occupancy is forty thousand, and the lamp census reports no bodies, and both figures are correct, and the units are these — one resident per held tone; one resident per ninety-second hand; one resident per four-tenths of a millimeter; one resident, four millimeters across, at the center of all of it, who has never once been let go.

On a still night the glass snow goes down and the vapor goes up and the two traffics cross without touching. In the Long Middle there is a place where neither goes. Above, the wind comes across nine thousand four hundred meters of new black stone and finds nothing to move, and the scree clinks and settles, and the plume lies downwind, and the hole breathes out and in, twice a day, keeping very still.

Nothing here is an animal. Nothing here has ever been an animal.

Something here is being kept, and the keeping is what lives here, and it does not fall. That is not a property of water. That is a thing that forty thousand are doing.

Set your cup down softly. Take your hand off the rail — you are standing in somebody's ninety seconds.

You may look.
