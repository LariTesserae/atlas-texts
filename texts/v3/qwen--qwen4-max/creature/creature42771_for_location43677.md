Begin at the bottom, where the living is thinnest.

The film is four microns deep. It has always been four microns deep. It is not a species and it is not really an organism; it is a settlement of chemistries that stopped being able to live apart so long ago that the word *apart* has no purchase here — a loop of them, passing electrons around a circle, each one's waste the next one's dinner, the circle closed and humming. It eats fresh basalt. It is eaten at precisely the rate it grows. So it is not a quantity of anything. It is a rate that happens to have a color, and the color is the pale of the inside of a shell, and it covers every wet stone in thirty kilometers without ever once getting thicker.

Nothing else in the basin touches rock. Everything else touches the film, or touches something that touched it. This is the first fact about the inhabitants: they are all, at every remove, standing on the same four microns, and none of them has ever felt the world.

---

The files come next, and there are ten trillion of them, and they are the reason you can see anything at all here, though not with eyes.

They are flat-backed, palm-sized, wet, the color of a bruise that has been left in water. They hatch in the crawl and climb out onto the stone and begin to rasp, and they rasp until they molt, and they molt four times, and then they fall back into the mattress and are taken apart. A file is not allotted a lifespan. It is allotted a stroke count — somewhere near nine million — and it spends it. If you wanted to say how old one was you would have to say how much of its allowance it had left, and it does not know this number, and neither does anything else.

Their mouthparts never touch the stone. They ride the four microns and take the four microns, and the film regrows behind them at the speed of the taking, so that the whole basin is a single surface being shaved and regrown forever at a fixed height. The files themselves are mineral-mouthed, and their files wear, and the wear is caught in their own throats and swallowed and regrown. Nothing escapes. There is no dust in this basin partly because the water cannot lift a grain and partly because ten trillion mouths are waiting for one.

And they are loud. That broadband dry hiss over the drone is not incidental to their feeding; it is the illumination. Sixty cycles has a wavelength of eight meters and tells you nothing about where you are; the hiss is fine-grained, and it comes from everywhere at once, and it bounces. Every animal in the crawl is bathed in it, pressed on all sides by it, and reads the world the way we read a room by its echo — the nearness of a trunk, the closing of a gap, the approach of a body — as a distortion in the shaving-noise of ten trillion mouths. The basin is lit by sound. It has been lit continuously since the first hour. Things here have no eyes worth the name because there is nothing to see by, and they have exquisite ears because there is everything to hear by.

Which means that a shape, here, is a place where the hiss comes back wrong.

And a predator is a place where it does not come back at all.

---

The crawl is the soil and it is also the blood.

It runs in fixed circuits, hand-span deep, between the outcrops: a slow turning mattress of the living and the just-dead, molts flipping over and over through it, larvae, the not-yet-hatched, the already-dismantled. It has a grain, like muscle. It has traffic. It has, in the only sense the basin permits, *streets* — and the streets are inherited, not learned, and they are the reason the forest stands where it stands.

There are things in the crawl whose entire function is calcium. A shell is far too expensive to leave lying about, so there are brokers that do nothing but carry shells inward against the general turn, and turners that flip them so the underside gets eaten evenly, and the crews — the crews are the ones that arrive before the death. They gather. They wait, in a ring, at the correct distance, and the animal dies into them, and eleven minutes later there is nothing, including the ring, which has moved on. If you wanted to point at a thing here and say *that is an individual*, the crews would be the hardest case: no member of a crew survives its function for long, the crew is never the same crew, and the crew is nonetheless always there, at the right distance, on time.

Nothing in the crawl is dead long enough to be called dead. There is a stage in the turning where a body is neither, and the crawl is mostly that stage.

---

The trees are predators. This is the part that takes longest to accept.

Roots cannot go into earth; there is no earth. They go into the crawl, and they are nets, and they close. A tree here is a standing digestion nine stories tall: a column of tube and lattice holding a shape by continuously eating the traffic beneath it and continuously being eaten from above. It has no rings. It has no age. It has an *address*, and the address is a circuit in the crawl, and when the circuit shifts — over centuries, a hand's breadth at a time — the tree follows it, growing into the new traffic and dying out of the old, so that the trunk you would touch is not the trunk that stood there, and the forest is a shape the crawl is holding rather than a thing that is standing in it.

The flowers are the only lamps in the world.

Consider what it means that everything here is the same temperature. In heat, as in sound, there is no contrast: the canopy is a flat field of forty-four degrees, featureless, invisible. And then a flower opens and runs itself up to fifty-eight, thermogenic, burning sugar it stole from the crawl, stinking of yeast and iron — and in a world with no other thermal contrast it is a *star*. It is the one thing above the millimeter that can be seen from a distance by anything equipped to see heat.

There is exactly one guild so equipped: the lamp-followers, small, thick-bodied, with a pit on each side of the head, flying a night that has no day in it between a few dozen burning points. They are the only animals here with anything like vision, and their entire visual universe consists of hot flowers hanging in black.

They cannot use the smell. The air does not move. Scent here diffuses and stops; it has a radius of about a hand's breadth, and beyond that it is nothing at all. So the stink is not a signal, it is a *confirmation*, tasted on arrival, at contact, the last centimeter of a navigation begun in infrared. Everything here is already touching, and everything here has to be.

And because the flowers cannot be found by place — the trees move, the shelf jungles rebuild themselves, nothing stays where it was — the lamp-followers do not inherit a route. They inherit a *period*. Each flower pulses on its own beat, a slow flare and fade, and the pollinator is born tuned to that interval and flies until it finds the thing blinking in its own time. A species of flower, here, is a tempo. A species of pollinator is the same tempo, held in a different body.

There are flowers that pulse at another flower's period and give nothing. There are lamp-followers that arrive at the correct interval and eat the flower.

---

The air is full because the air is thick, and the air is thick because it is hot and wet and heavy, and flight in it costs almost nothing. This is why the ceiling exists. It is also, when you follow the heat, why the ceiling *must* exist.

The basin has no wind. It cannot have wind: above the millimeter everything is one temperature, and isothermal air does not convect. There is no thermal to ride, no breeze to be carried on, nothing to move anything up. And yet heat arrives from below and leaves from above, in balance, and has done so forever.

So the animals are the weather.

There is a guild — call it the lift — that hatches in the crawl, feeds in the crawl, loads itself with hot water and fat and the spores of the film, and climbs. Forty meters, straight up, through the mist-feeders and the canopy's upper stories, to the underside of the ceiling. There it is eaten, or joins, and its load goes with it. Every gram of water that gets from the pans to the sky gets there inside something with a heartbeat. Every spore that lands on the new black fields at the center of the basin was carried up by a body and let go from a height. The rain here is not weather either. It is traffic, falling.

The mist-feeders hang in the middle of that column, enormous and slow, combing the saturated air with wet nets for the aerial plankton that the lift keeps resupplying. They are almost weightless. They have no territory and no home and have never touched anything solid; they mate in suspension and calve in suspension and their young fall a little and rise. They are also the ceiling's food, and the ceiling is three and four deep across thirty kilometers and beats at sixty cycles, and that drone is not a sound the basin makes. It is the sound of the basin exhaling.

Because the ceiling is the only skin here that faces the sky. Somewhere in all that haze there is a window, a narrow band where the dark above can be reached, and the uppermost fliers hold their wings in it — thin, bloodless, vast, black in the infrared — and radiate. They are the cold end of the whole world. Everything below them is warm because they are willing to be slightly less warm, forty meters up, forever, in a stratum that never lands.

And the drone is the carrier tone. Everything in the crawl is tuned to it. Every interval any animal keeps is a subdivision of it. If the ceiling thinned, the pitch would sag, and thirty kilometers of arithmetic would go slowly, catastrophically out of tune. There is no record of this ever having happened. There could not be.

---

The wallowers are the only machines.

They lie in the pans with their bellies in sixty-degree water and their backs in forty-four-degree air, and sixteen degrees across a body is an engine — a poor one, a few parts in a hundred, but they have all the time there is. They run a cycle on the millimeter. The bellow is the exhaust stroke. It is not a call; it does not mean anything; it is what the work sounds like when it comes out, and it comes out on the beat because the beat is what the work is.

Their backs are the cold side, and so their backs are below air temperature, and so their backs are the only place in the entire basin above the millimeter where water condenses. They drip. They walk around dripping, in a place where nothing else can, because they carry their own weather with them: a private millimeter, laid over the hide, moving when they move.

That is what the gardens are for. The backs are farmed. Roots go into the cracks of the hide and drink the condensation and digest the wallower at exactly the rate the wallower grows, and the little forests riding those backs are the only forest in the basin that ever changes address. Nothing is carried by water here. Everything that gets from one shelf jungle to another gets there on a wallower, in the drip, on the beat.

They are the clock, the rain, the freight, and the pump. They are also the only animals here that a person would be tempted to call *someone* — huge, patient, singular, apparently continuous. But there is no way to age one. When a wallower dies the crews come, and another lies down in the pan, and the interval between bellows does not change, and the interval is the thing that was real.

---

Now the silence, which is the strangest citizen and the only one that has never been described.

It cannot be seen. Its cuticle is felt and eats the hiss, so it returns no acoustic shape — it is the one black object in a world lit by sound. And it stands at air temperature, insulated down to its tiptoes, so it has no thermal signature either. In both of the basin's two illuminations it is invisible. *Long-legged, soft-footed* is inference: that is how the crawl parts, that is how the wake closes, that is the gait of a hole twenty meters wide moving at a slow walk.

Here is what it eats. When the hush passes, the files stop. They do not flee; there is nowhere to flee and no reason; the arithmetic is settled. They stop, and while they are stopped the film grows unshaved, and thickens — four microns becoming five, becoming six, the only place in the basin where anything is permitted to accumulate. The hush walks forward and grazes backward, along its own trailing edge, on the fat that its passing grew. It is a predator the way a mower is a predator. It farms with a shadow.

And it takes the ones that are wrong.

There are two ways to be wrong here. The first is to keep rasping when the hole goes over you — to be deaf, or newborn and not yet locked, or sick, or simply out of phase with the sixty cycles that everything else is subdividing. The second is to stop and not start again.

The second is the important one, because that is how a silence is made.

Somewhere out in the crawl a file stops when the hush passes and does not resume when it goes. It just — holds. The stop persists, and spreads a little, and the body under it changes: the felt comes, the legs come, the insulation comes, because a stop that stays in one place gets scraped around and starves, and a stop that walks can eat its own wake. A predator here is not a shape. It is an animal that stopped and kept stopping until the stopping became a body.

Which is why there is exactly one.

Because the thing the silence eats, above all, is arrhythmia — and the deepest arrhythmia in the basin is another silence beginning. It hunts its own successors. It finds them by their holes, which are small and new and clumsy, and it walks into them and they end, and the office stays single, and has been single since there was an office.

Once in a while it fails. The old one dies, and for nine or ten seconds there is no hole anywhere in thirty kilometers, and the basin is entirely, uniformly loud, and every animal in it hears the silence die at once. Then somewhere out in the crawl a file does not resume. And the felt comes. And the hole opens again at a new address and goes walking.

Nothing records the interval. Nothing here counts.

There are things that live in the wake and eat the fattened film behind it, silent themselves, felt-backed, following the hole around like gulls behind a plough. There is a colonial thing that fakes a hush — twenty meters of coordinated stopping, a false predator, a lie told in the only language the basin has — and it works beautifully for about a minute, until the real one walks in to eat the competitor and finds a hundred thousand out-of-phase bodies standing perfectly still and offering themselves.

---

Two more, and they are the ones that break the picture open.

The first is the lag. Sound crosses thirty kilometers of this heavy air in something over a minute. The basin is therefore not in phase with itself. The far rim hears the near rim a minute ago; the pans at one end keep a beat that arrives at the other end sixty-odd seconds late; the *now* of this place is a minute wide, and everything alive is tuned not to the world but to its own distance from the clock.

So identity here is a radius. There are animals that can only walk in arcs — concentric, patient, circling a wallower at a fixed distance their whole lives, because to move inward or outward is to slide out of your own phase and become, in every sense the basin recognizes, someone else. They can go sideways forever. They cannot go toward. The few that do make the crossing do it once, and it is not travel, it is metamorphosis: they shed the beat as they walk and take up a new one on arrival, and what comes out the far side is the same animal in the way a molt is the same animal, which is to say not at all, which is to say exactly.

The second is the fold, at the far end, where the soft dark stone goes down like cooling fat and takes the film and the crawl and the nine stories with it.

There are walkers there that do nothing but walk upstream. They must: the ground moves under them at a fraction of a millimeter a year, carrying their whole world down into the closing, and they advance against it at precisely that speed, and so they stand perfectly still relative to the pattern while the floor slides beneath their feet toward the place where everything is folded in. Their entire existence is the cancellation of a motion. They are the only animals here that cannot stop.

And there are riders, which do not walk, which go down with it, and the question of whether anything comes back up is not answerable, because nothing here records anything, because there is no sediment and no rind and no ring and no bone. But the new black fields at the center well up sterile, and within hours the film has them, and the film came from somewhere, and the only route from the fold to the ceiling runs through the bodies of the lift.

The loop closes. It closes through animals. Every arrow has its equal arrow and every one of them is being carried by something warm and wet and busy that is about to be eaten and is already replaced.

---

What lives here, then, is not really the film or the files or the trees or the wallowers or the thing with the felt feet. Those are the instruments. What lives here is the chord — the held, subdivided, thirty-kilometer note that everything is born already tuned to, and that carries the whole of what this place knows, because it cannot carry knowledge any other way. There is no memory in the matter. The matter goes down the fold and comes up the seams and is entirely different every few centuries. The memory is in the frequency, and the frequency is in the ceiling, and the ceiling is held up by the commute, and the commute is fed by the crawl, and the crawl is fed by the film, and the film eats the rock.

A newborn file climbs out of the mattress onto four microns of slick and begins to rasp in time with a drone it has never heard, at a subdivision it was not taught. That is the whole inheritance. Three rules, and nothing else, and no one to state them:

Keep the beat. Stop when the hole comes over you. Start again when it goes.

And out in the middle distance the hole goes over it, and it stops — perfectly, instantly, along with everything else in twenty meters — and the film begins, for a moment, to thicken, and the felted thing walks on and eats what grows behind it, and the hush moves away, and ten trillion mouths resume.
