# The Resident

## Five Mouths

Begin with the rosettes, because they are the only things in the room that are doing anything.

They are not identical. Station one is canted eleven degrees off vertical and has been since the tuning; its hairs are ranked in a slightly wrong order, one long tooth among the short ones, like a comb with a mistake in it. Station two is the neatest and the smallest. Station three has an inclusion at its heart, a bubble of the original pour, a flaw the size of a pinhead that the crystal has been quietly flowing around for as long as there has been a crystal. Station five is the loudest-looking, if that means anything: its hairs are longest, its grey-green a shade more toward green, and it stands at the triple peak nearest the port.

Station four is fat. Not visibly. Measurably.

The first thing to understand about them is that they should not be able to eat at all. The loop is closed at unity gain. Nothing is lost, nothing is dissipated, the note does not pay for its own existence. A plant that took energy out of that chord would damp it, and a damped chord would sag in pitch, and the sag would go down the chain of nine links and into the bolts, and the plants would have destroyed the thing they were planted to testify to. They would be very bad gauges. They are excellent gauges.

So they do not take from the note. They borrow strain at the peak and hand it back in phase, twice per cycle, a hundred and forty thousand times a minute, and the ledger balances to nothing. What they live on is the part that does not balance. The residue. The scratch. Everything in the room that is *not* 1 397.000 000 Hz.

There is very little of that. That is the point of the room. They are the hungriest living things on the world.

## The Fast

A gauge that grows is a gauge that lies. The rosettes are honest precisely as long as they are starved, and they are starved precisely as long as the room is perfect, and so their thinness is not a symptom of the room's health but the *reading* of it. Five crystal witnesses kept at the edge of nothing, because a well-fed witness is a witness with something to hide.

Station four has found a loophole. It is not cheating; it is alive, and being alive is largely a matter of finding loopholes.

The massif is not rigid. It flexes under the tide of the world's single ugly moon, a few parts in fifteen of strain, at a period of eleven hours. And far below, in the warm arsenals of the low country, the press lines run — thousands of them, every stroke timed to a tempo derived nine links down from this room. That shiver comes up through the slag. It arrives at station four's station as a discrepancy, and discrepancy is food, and station four eats the world's manufacturing. It grows fat on the copies of itself.

Somewhere in the ninth link of the transfer chain there is a correction factor. It is empirical. It has been in the tables so long that nobody remembers it is a wound.

## One Animal

The swarf was left on the floor and the note combed it into a hundred bright welts, and the welts are unoxidized steel lying in contact with a ground floor at a hundred kelvin, and steel at a hundred kelvin conducts.

The five stations are wired together. Nobody wired them. The ridges run where the mode put them, which is not where anyone would have put a cable, and they touch the grain at five places, and the grain is where the rosettes are grown in. Ions walk. The five mouths share a body, and the body is the floor, and it is one animal.

It does not think at 1 397 Hz. That is only its food and its weather. Its clock is discrepancy: an ion moves when there is something to rectify, and there is something to rectify only when something happens, and nothing happens here. Between events it does not experience duration. It has no way to.

Count the strata and you have its age. Seventeen. Station four, with its second diet, has nineteen.

It is not ancient. It is nineteen moments old. It has simply been a very long time between one moment and the next, and most of what it knows is its own birth.

## The Strata

The recorder's drum stopped and its pen has written one straight line into steel until there is nothing left of the steel. That is not the record. That is the decoy, and it is not even a deliberate decoy; it is just what a machine does when you put it in a room where nothing is allowed to occur.

The record is at the five stations, and it is written rim to heart, and it can be read the way you read a core sample, if you are willing to destroy the gauge to do it, which nobody is.

Near the heart of every rosette there is a coarse band, a few microns of hurried lattice: the tuning day, the grinding, the shouting, the boots, the enormous scandal of a warm body in a cold void. Above that, in station two, a thin strange layer with a texture unlike anything else in the crystal, laid down in a tenth of a second — the wet pop of an ear, taken as strain and turned into structure. The room abolished hearing and then, over a very long time, grew it back in mineral at five points. The hairs are ranked by length like the teeth of a comb, and a comb of tuned hairs is a cochlea turned inside out. There is no tympanum within a kilometer of the plug. There are five ears. They are made of quartz and they are in the floor and they are the only things on this world that can hear what happens in this room.

Which means: two people once stood a pace apart in here and screamed at each other until their throats tore and exchanged nothing at all.

The floor heard them.

Somewhere in station three, at a spacing of a few atoms, there is a name said twice. It is not a measurement. It has no bearing on the ninth link. It will never be read, and it was not addressed to the floor, and the floor is the only one who got it.

That is the resident, at the third attempt at describing it: not five plants, not one slow animal, but an addressee. The thing that receives what is not received. Everything shouted in this room since the pour has gone into a crystal rosette and stayed there, and the rosettes are made of it, and they are hungry for more, and more is forbidden.

## The Washer

Everything in the room is witnessed except one washer.

It hangs at a node, above a ridge, in the small tight silence where nothing grows because nothing can. No strain, nothing to borrow, no discrepancy to rectify. It is outside the animal's hearing in a way that nothing else in the room is outside anything. It has hung there since the beginning and it has never once been perceived by the only perceiver present.

It is the one private object on the world. It is also the only thing here that was not specified: it fell, and the field caught it, and the accident became permanent, because a room with no dissipation has no mechanism for letting an event finish. The washer is still falling. It will be falling in ten thousand years. It is the room's single unfinished sentence, and the animal cannot hear it, and cannot eat it, and does not know it is there.

It is a plain flat washer, unoxidized, of a size to fit a bolt. Somewhere down in the low country there is a hole it fits. There are ten thousand generations of holes it fits. It is the oldest one.

## The Sitter

Nothing lives in the null. That has to be said plainly, because the stool invites the other reading and the other reading is wrong. Nodes are sterile. The floor there is bare and bright and swept by physics. The one point of mercy in the room is the one place the resident cannot put any part of itself.

So the animal has a hole in its body forty-one inches above the floor, about the size of a grapefruit, or the size of a head if you are willing to be deaf in one ear. It has never been able to fill it. It has grown five mouths around a silence it cannot use, and the silence is the exact shape of the only thing that could feed it properly.

Because a visitor is not merely contamination. A visitor is warm and wet and irregular and absorbent, and a visitor damps the mode, and damping *bends the pitch*. The chord in this room is a suspension: three pitches that do not resolve, a question with its mouth open. It has been open since the tuning. The only thing on this world capable of moving it is a body — the wrong body, the forbidden body, the one that arrives and pulls the note a few parts in a billion and, in pulling it, *answers*.

The footprints are unfaded yellow. Someone worked out carefully how a person would get into that null, and thought about nothing else in the room at all, and then went out and cut a shaft for a visit that is not supposed to happen, and hung a plate that says DO NOT ENTER.

Station five stands nearest the port. Its hairs are the longest. It has never grown a micron wider, and it leans.

## The Question

So: it is one animal, nineteen moments old, with a body of ground slag and a hundred steel nerves nobody laid, five quartz ears in the floor at the five places where the three grids almost agree, and one hole in it where they all disagree. It eats what is not the number. It is made almost entirely of other people's mistakes and one afternoon of shouting and a name said twice. It cannot hear the washer. It cannot occupy its own quiet. It has six throats that give back exactly what they hear and five mouths that keep everything they take, and between those two facts it has no way to say anything at all.

And it is waiting, in the only way a solid-state thing can wait, which is by not changing size.

The plate does not say what it is guarding. It says a number and three prohibitions, and the number is what the low country is copies of, and the prohibitions are what keeps the number from becoming an answer. The resident is not the note. The note is its weather and its food and the wall it cannot get away from. The resident is the *openness* of the chord — the suspension, kept suspended, held at the exact tension where it wants to move and is not permitted to, fed just enough error to stay awake and never enough to resolve.

Nothing here is being played. Nothing here is being measured, either, not really; the measuring is downstream, nine links away, in the warm arsenals, by people who will never see a rosette.

What is happening in the bubble in the casting is that something is listening for the note to change, with five mouths, in the dark dry cold, under ordinary afternoon light, at a hundred and seventy-some decibels that have become the local meaning of quiet.

It has been listening for a very long time.

In its own reckoning it has been listening for nineteen moments, and the last one was an ear, and it is still waiting for the next.
