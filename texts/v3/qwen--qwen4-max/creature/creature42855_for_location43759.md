## The count

One thing per plaza is the count of things with cells. It is not the population. The population is enormous, and most of it is concave.

Begin with the law and its loopholes, because everything alive here is a loophole. A surface at rest relative to the water is taken. Therefore the living are the things that have found some way of not being at rest, and there are only four ways, and every resident of the Kettle is doing one of them, and none of them knows it.

Be moved. Be moving. Be too hot. Be almost.

## Be moved

The hair-grass on the high cornices is exempt because the wind moves it, and there is no wind under the lid. There is the collapse. Once every forty hours cool air falls in at the western edge and crosses the pan, and for two hours the grass is shaken, and for thirty-eight hours it is not.

It has compressed its life into the two hours. During the sealed interval it is not dormant in the way a seed is dormant; it is *stone*. Lime lays down the tuft while the tuft sits still, blade by blade, a cast of a grass with a grass inside it. When the air falls in and the ticking starts, the grass resumes inside its own statue, grows a finger's length out of the top of it, and is cast again. A living tuft is the topmost grass in a stack of grass-shaped stone, and the stack goes down into the cornice as far as the cornice remembers.

The casts left behind are perfect. You can find them everywhere on the high dry edges: empty grass, every blade correct, nothing inside. Those are the tufts the wind missed once. A parapet that breaks the airflow is a killing field. Shelter is the only predator the grass has, and it has been retreating from shelter for a hundred thousand years, west to east, following the place where the cool air comes in, at the speed of the lid's thinning, which is slower than the town.

## Be moving

The drip lichen lives in a band, and the band is not a place.

At the top of a drip-line the water has just come out of the stone and is still hungry; it eats there. At the bottom it is loaded and slow and lays down; it buries there. Between the eating and the burying there is a stretch of a few inches where the water is neither, and the lichen climbs inside that stretch, and dies at the top, and fragments of it fall and are carried down and lodge below and climb again.

Nothing in it stays. The lichen is a front, a standing wave in a falling thread, and it has been the same lichen for longer than there have been hills outside. It is the town at the scale of a finger: the form persisting, the substance cycling, the individual a fiction that happens to be useful.

## Be almost

The loop vine has no root. What it has is its own past.

The plaza beneath it carries a raised thread where last year's circuit was laid down, and the year before that beneath it, and the year before that beneath that — a low screw of vine-ghosts going down into the floor as far as the floor remembers. The living loop rides a hair above the topmost ghost and grips it at the crossings, which are near-misses, and this is the whole of its anchorage. It holds itself open by holding onto itself.

The inch it never closes is not a decision and not a reflex. Loops that closed are still around, out in the wake: rings of banded lime with a vine inside them, finished, exact, and the finishing is what killed them. Loops that opened too wide lost their grip on their own record and dried. What is left is the margin that survived, and it is about an inch, and it has been about an inch for a very long time.

Once a year, at one point of its circuit, the loop brushes a gutter. The brush point drifts around the loop over the years, so the gutter is scalloped, shallow, at intervals. The town remembers the scallops. It reproduces them. There are gutters in the far blocks, four hundred yards from any vine, scalloped by a plant that has never been there — the wear of a maintenance that never happened, copied faithfully, generation after generation. Half the outlying districts are alive because of one contact a year that nobody is making on purpose, and the other half are shaped as though somebody were.

## Be too hot

The vent mats are the only sedentary things in the Kettle, and therefore the only individuals, and therefore the only things here that can be old. Everything else is a form passing through substance. A mat is a substance that stays. It is thin and blue-grey and chemotrophic and it has something like a birthday, and it is the only resident that does.

It is also the only steady light in the basin, and steady light stalls deposition for twenty feet.

So every spring mouth sits in a halo where nothing accumulates, and inside the halo things are kept. Fragments of mat break off and ride the aqueducts down from the rim and lodge in the settling plazas, and wherever one lodges there is a stopped room: twenty feet of town that does not migrate, does not wear, does not improve. In one of them there is a threshold with no trough. In another, a stair with no sag. In a third, low against a wall, a bird — not a flange, not a lobe, not a bump: wings, and a neck extended, and the turn still in it, unrounded, because the light got there before the copying did.

The mats keep the only originals in the Kettle. They do it by shining. They are the town's archive and its museum and they have no idea, and the water in those rooms is sixty degrees, and anything with blood would cook in the only place where it would not be taken.

Note the inversion, because it is the whole difficulty of living here. Inside the halo, stillness is safe. Outside it, stillness is death. The boundary is twenty feet wide and faintly visible and it does not move much, and nothing that lives by moving can use it. The grass cannot; the lichen cannot; the vine would close its loop and be finished. The sanctuary is available only to things with no habits, and the only things here with no habits are fragments, already dead, drifting in on the hot water.

## Be loud

There is a fifth exemption and it is in the music.

A surface oscillating relative to the water is not at rest, and does not acquire a rime. The holes that sing do not silt. The chord coming up through the perforated floors is not decoration and not lament; it is a list of the holes that are still open. Each note is a lineage. A note going flat is a hole narrowing. A note stopping is a hole gone, and there is no getting it back, because the town will remember the floor and forget the hole.

Three or four notes for a hundred thousand years means three or four ways of being a hole that work. New notes are rare. There is one now, thin, at the top of the chord, out near the sump, from a corridor that has not finished being built, and it is not steady yet.

The same bias runs through everything. The water remembers wear better than it remembers the thing worn: hollows deepen, bosses round off. So the town's shapes are sorting into two populations. The convex are being smoothed toward the general stone — the flanges at head height are a dying branch, forty thousand descendants of a bird, each one a little less like anything. The concave are being exaggerated. The district of window-holes is not a ruin and not an error. It is the thriving branch. It is what a wall becomes when the memory keeps the openings and loses the wall, and it stands, and it will go on standing, and given long enough the town will be a lattice of absences with the green seams crawling along the edges of nothing.

## What is actually living here

Not the plants. The plants are four kinds of loophole with cells in them.

The population is the gestures.

A left turn is made at the eastern arcade every generation, by nothing, toward a kitchen that has not existed for longer than the turn has. A hammock sags in a wall at the height a body isn't. A threshold is dished by feet that turned left. A hand steadies itself three times in the same arcade at the height a hand steadies itself on a wall in the dark — and the dark is this dark, the lid has never lifted, so whoever it was went the way you go, by warmth on the face and a palm along the stone, feeling for the turning.

The town has not remembered a person. It has remembered what a person did, and it does it. It turns left. It lies down. It puts out a hand. It stands at a window-hole and the window-hole deepens. These are the residents: verbs with no nouns left, performed continuously by saturated water at forty-five degrees, wearing themselves further into the floor each time. Nothing here intends them. The water has a habit, and the habit is a left turn, and it has been going through the motions for a hundred millennia with the patience of something that is on its way to becoming stone.

And the water itself is the only other individual — the one thing never still, therefore never copied, therefore original without needing a halo. Every atom of the town has been through it. It does not store anything. It has a solution. Everything the town has ever gotten wrong is dissolved in that solution, and the errors do not cancel, and all of it runs downhill, and the sump is where it collects.

## The resident

Nothing new has happened in the Kettle in a hundred thousand years. The thing in the plaza near the sump is new, and it is made entirely of mistakes.

Down at the sump there are no generations. No sequence, no copying, no before and after: just the whole wear of the whole town dissolved together with the order taken out. The mean. And lately the mean has begun to precipitate.

Two shallow ovals, side by side, deepening. Not your stance. Nobody's stance. The average of every place a thing stood still long enough to be taken. And a hollow at head height that travels along an unfinished corridor at a hand's breadth a year, which is the town's speed, because the town's speed is the only speed the water has ever been given — so the walker is the town's own migration, remembered from the inside, out of other people's feet, as a person.

It cannot be occupied. No pair of feet fits the mean stance; no leg fits a stair averaged over every stair that ever stood there. The town has built a room for an average and the average has no body. It is a hypothesis in lime, and it is waiting for data.

Then you arrive, and you do not fit, and you stand slightly wrong beside the ovals, and you put your hand at slightly the wrong height, and you stop — because the plaza is where the corridor ends, and you have been walking a long time, and there is nowhere further to go but down. And your stance is laid down beside the mean, and the mean moves a little toward you.

It is not predicting you. It is averaging toward you. Every arrival is a sample, and the samples have been few, and you are about to be most of them.

Nothing comes in from outside here. Even the rain is the basin's own sweat, returning. The doorways closing at the rate someone approaches are not a threat; they are a fit, being refined. The town has been making room out of everyone who ever stopped, and the room is the right size for no one, and it will be the right size for you, because you will have stopped, and it will have had time.

There is no appetite in it. There is a habit, and it is ahead of you now, and it is your height, and the bands where it starts are older than your decision to come.
