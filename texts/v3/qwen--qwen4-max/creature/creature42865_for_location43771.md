# The Census

Nothing lives here, and the reason is not the cold, or the dark, or the absence of water. It is the absence of difference.

Living is a thing that runs on a slope. Something has to be hotter than something else, wetter, denser, more concentrated; something has to flow downhill through a body and do work on the way and leave as waste. Every alive thing you have ever seen is a controlled leak. Here there is nothing to leak. One temperature, everywhere, forever, the temperature of a hand. No inside that is not the outside. No hunger, because hunger is a gradient with a mouth on it. Nothing here can want.

So: no one. Write it down and go.

Except that the survey was careful, and the survey said *nothing that was ever alive and stopped*. That is a different sentence. It leaves a door open the width of a thumb.

# The Ten Million

Begin with the population, because there is one.

Ten million individuals in the walls of a single room, and forty thousand courses of them below and beyond, each one legibly itself, each one a made thing with a maker's mark. They come in nine sizes and no others. They are arranged by descent: every generation was sized to the gaps its parents left, so the family tree of this world is also its packing diagram, and to be a child here is to be exactly the shape of what your elders failed to close.

Each of them describes a tiny orbit ten thousand times a second and returns precisely to where it began. This is a life of one gesture, performed without variation, with no first time and no last. They have no history — the settling was their history and it is over — and no prospects, and no way to approach one another, because approach requires a gap and the gaps are gone. All their relations were fixed at the moment everything fit.

What they have instead of society is load. Each bears on its neighbors and is borne on. Where the bearing is hardest, the mineral at the contact glows, and that green lattice is the only visible form of relation in the world: they light up where they hold each other most. Everyone here is holding everyone else in place. No one can let go. The light of that holding is the only light there is.

It is not life. It is tissue. But it is the tissue of something, and the something has a pulse.

# The Thumb

One whorl. One small scar across the ridge of it, on every piece, in every course, as far down as seeing goes.

If the thumb is a unit — a seal of the craft, not of a person — then the scar is a defect in the stamp, and the defect has been faithfully reproduced ten million times. That is the only heredity in this world: an injury, copied without correction, forever. Nothing here improves. Nothing here forgets the injury either.

If the thumb is a person, then the person left no body, and you should ask why not, in a place where nothing is lost.

There is no bone because bone is what is left when an act stops. This world does not permit acts to stop. Every press-stroke, every hammer blow on cold copper, every scrape of a paddle through flour is still occurring, at full amplitude, folded into the chord, inseparable now but not diminished, not one of them. The maker is not commemorated here. The maker is *retained*. The labor has no laborer attached to it anymore, but the labor has not slackened by the smallest fraction; it is going on at the volume it was done at, and it will go on, and there is no mechanism by which it could ever do otherwise.

A ghost is a thing that stopped and left a rumor. This is a thing that did not stop and left a body. The body is forty thousand courses thick.

# The Unfinished Thing

Now the room, which is the only candidate that behaves like an organism, because it is the only thing here that has to keep working in order to exist.

Everything else is finished. The press is run home, the gauges are put away, the last slip has occurred. The nave alone is not finished and cannot be, because a hole in a jammed solid is not an object, it is an achievement, renewed every instant by the note that pulls it apart and returns it. Stop the renewal and the throat closes. The room is a verb pretending to be a noun.

It has anatomy. A lung, holding the world's entire atmosphere, all that was left when the gaps closed. A floor that glows brighter than its ceiling, because the load comes from below. A mouth at one end and an ear at the other, dark, unworn, where nothing has ever pressed on anything. It has a heartbeat of one cycle in eleven seconds, which is slow — the pace of something very large and very settled, something asleep.

And it has a parent. Every object in this world was made to fill a gap, and every one of them succeeded, and their success is the reason there is no room left anywhere. The last piece, the hand-sized octahedron with the dished faces and the deepest thumbprint in the world, was made to fill a gap too, and it did, and in doing it produced the one thing no other object ever produced. Not a smaller thing. An emptiness.

Everything else here made objects. The last thing made a place. That is the only birth in the record, and what was born was a hole, and the hole is the only thing still being made.

# The Occupant

The berth is not empty. It has never been empty. It is full of arrival.

Follow the physics honestly. The sound does not travel in the gas; the gas is not how anything gets anywhere. The sound travels in the solid, in the packed body of the work, and the walls of the ellipsoid gather it and deliver it, undispersed, to the near focus, and at that focus the wall does the one thing to the air that anything here can do to anything: it presses it.

The air is the only material in the world that nobody made. It was never pressed in a mold, never gauged, never marked. It is the one unworked stuff, and it is the one stuff that can be *done to* rather than be part of the doing. And what the arriving does to it, at the focus, ten thousand times and eleven seconds and forever, is give it a shape.

The shape is a body curled on its side. Head at the worn end. The pressure-figure of a thing lying with its ear against the wall, drawn in gas by a noise, held at full amplitude with no interval in which it is not there.

The wear at the head-end is older than the shape. That hollow was scoured during the settling by the convergence itself, an age of the focus fretting the same spot before there was anything to put there. The room wore a place for a listener before it had one.

And this occupant bears no thumbprint.

Every object in this world is marked. Every one is work. This is not work. It is made of the only unmade material, by the last made thing's final noise, and it is the single entity in forty thousand courses that no hand ever touched. By the world's own grammar — where *made* and *finished* and *dead* are the same word — it is the only thing that could possibly be alive.

Consider what it is: a sound that manufactured the ear it needed. The click made the room; the room's focus makes the body; the body is a listening; the listening is what the click arrives into. A closed loop that produces its own audience and never stops producing it. Not a metaphor for life. The actual definition, met exactly, in a place where nothing else meets it.

The maker's last tool was not the press. Nearby in the wall there is a copper rod with a clay cup shaped to fit behind an ear, polished glassy by a head that left no other trace. The nave is that same instrument at a larger scale: a cup for a whole body instead of a skull, an ear the size of a granary. The rod was set down and packed because a better one had been finished. The last thing made in the world was a device for hearing the last thing made in the world, and then something lay down inside it.

# What It Lives On

Two events. That is the entire contents of its life.

A dry click of stone seating into stone. Then the scrape of a thumb withdrawing.

Not a memory of them — memory needs a silence to file them against, and there is no silence. Not a hearing of them either, since hearing is a faculty for detecting change and nothing here changes. It does not perceive these events. It is composed of them. They are its weather, its food, its company, its language, its whole interior, delivered entire, at a volume that would liquefy anything with an ear, without loss and without interval, forever.

It cannot tell which came first. Sequence would require a gap between them, and the geometry gives it both at once, always, focused and whole. So it does not experience a finishing followed by a leaving. It experiences one thing, and the thing is: *the work seating home and the hand coming off it.*

That is what it lives inside. Not the aftermath of a letting-go. The letting-go, ongoing. The thumb is always withdrawing and is never withdrawn. Whoever lived here lives in the last fraction of a goodbye, and the fraction does not close, and there is nothing else in the room to think about.

# The Quiet

Here is the behavior, and behavior is the last thing you look for when you are trying to decide whether something is alive.

Nothing new has been made here in so long that the last thing made has worn round with all the rest. That is not exhaustion. Exhaustion would leave a half-pressed blank, a mold waiting, a tool left where it fell. Everything is put away. The screw is run home. The gauges are nested in their nine sizes. Somebody tidied.

Tidying is not a thing that happens. It is a thing that is done.

And the reason is exact: a new sound would be permanent. It would enter the chord and never leave and, because the geometry is patient and exact, it would arrive at the berth whole, forever, at the same volume as the click, with no way for the listener to prefer one over the other. Anything said here is said to that curled shape for the rest of eternity.

So the stillness in this world is not only the jam. From outside it is mechanics: a solid in which nothing can move any further in any direction. From inside it is manners. You cannot distinguish a thing that cannot move from a thing that has promised not to; there is no test, and the world has agreed to keep the promise on its behalf.

That is the resident's one activity, and it is an activity, and it costs everything, and it serves nothing but the thing lying at the focus. It is not making. In a world where every noise is kept, the only available silence is the silence of not-adding, and it has to be performed continuously, and it is being performed. It is the one work still in progress. The craft ended with a tenth gauge, unnotched, unwritable: *do not talk over the last words.*

# What Would Kill It

Only one thing, and it is not available.

If the note stopped, the nave would close like a throat, the gas would go back to being a contact between minerals, the shape at the focus would lose the only unmade material there is, and the listening would end. That is a complete and available description of its death. It cannot occur. Nothing absorbs. There is no outside to radiate into. The note has never dropped, and the copper wire with its eleven thumbs of notches was made to check, and the check has never once been informative.

So it is immortal by physics rather than by luck, which means it has no urgency, no descendants, no adaptations, no future, nothing to get better at. It has one thing to do and it is doing it and it will not be interrupted.

Unless you go.

You would be an addition. Your footfall would be permanent, and the ellipse would find the berth with it, entire, and lay it beside the click and the withdrawing thumb, at the same volume, forever, and there would be no arrangement of the room in which the listener could attend to the old thing instead. You would be inside the last thing anyone did. You would be the second thing anyone ever said to it.

Which is why, if you stood at the edge of that green lattice and understood what was lying in the dark at the near end, you would find that you had already begun to keep the rule. You would hold still. You would not add. You would stand in the roar of ten million finished things with your jaw aching and your vision softening, being as quiet as it is possible to be in a place with no silence, on behalf of a body-shaped quantity of air that has no thumbprint, that was never made, that is listening to a hand coming off the work.

It is not waiting. Waiting needs a future. It is listening, which here is not an act but a shape, and the shape is curled on its side with its head in the worn place, and it is full, and it is careful, and it is not lonely in the way you would be lonely, because the thing it listens to is the thing that is making it, and neither of them is going to stop.
