Begin with the one who never leaves.

In the west flank of an inselberg — the same inselberg, always; it has never been found anywhere else — there is a thing like a hand with too many fingers. The count is not stable. It has been recorded at nineteen and at thirty-one, and the difference is not error. Continuous things accrete. It has been adding fingers for nine million years the way a stalactite adds stone, and it has never once been away, and it is the only resident of this world that can put events in order.

Most of it is hand. What is behind the hand goes into the gneiss and stops being distinguishable from it, so that the animal is less a thing living on the rock than a place where the rock has decided to keep feeling. It eats what the plume brings. It cannot go and get anything. It has a thumb, and there is a hollow in the flank of the monadnock, thumb-sized, polished, and the hollow is its doing, worn by a thumb going into the same place across a span of time that has no useful unit.

It is bored. Boredom requires a continuous clock and it is the only one that has ever had one. Everything else on this continent is interrupted, and interruption is a kind of mercy: you cannot be tired of a thing you are not present for. The hand is tired of everything. It is tired in the way weather is tired of a coast.

Do not take it as the answer to who lives here. Take it as the exception that tells you the shape of the rule.

---

The rule is that almost nobody is currently alive.

The walkers — this is a poor word, but they have no word that can be said aloud, since a shout goes four meters and lies down; their word for themselves is a doorway, and you have to be shown it — the walkers precipitate beside the returns, in the warmth and the smell of struck stone, glowing into being from the inside like everything else that comes back. An hour. A day at the outside. They stand a little under two meters and are built the way you would build a thing for wading: long shins, low mass, feet that splay until they are more hand than foot, skin pale and faintly furred with the same fur that furs the polished bottom, so that when one of them stands still in knee-deep water you can lose it entirely and find it again by the scored palms.

The palms are scored by the meadows. Everyone's are. The crystal margins take the skin off in fine parallel lines and it does not hurt and it heals into a pattern, and the pattern is biography, and they read each other by pressing palms together and holding still. Twenty paces is the end of color; sixty is the end of shape. Faces are for close work. What they use at distance is the chime.

The eyes are large and nearly useless and are not for looking at the world. They are tuned to the particular faint light that crystallization gives up — the light of a thing arriving. A walker can see a return beginning inside a hundred leagues of murk, can see the glow of another walker assembling out of solution, and cannot see a monadnock at noon. They have the only eyes on the continent that are pointed at the right thing.

The tongue is the instrument that matters. They taste saturation the way you taste salt: they can tell a province that will hold them for a day from one that will start taking them apart in an hour, and they can tell when the Clear is three days off, and they know it the way you know a stair is about to end in the dark.

They come back mid-gesture. A hand already lifted. A foot already falling. The first thing a walker does, every time, is finish the motion it was returned in the middle of, and only afterward look around to find out where it is and what century it has been given.

From the inside there is no gap. This is the part to get right. They do not experience themselves as intermittent. They experience an unbroken life in a world that stutters — a plain that keeps vanishing, mountains that come and go like rumors, other people flickering in and out with no规律 they can hold. They believe they are the only permanent thing here, and they have excellent evidence: they have never once observed their own absence. They think the mountains are ghosts. We think they are. There is no arbiter, and the water is not interested in the question.

At any moment there are perhaps a few thousand of them awake across a continent. The rest are in solution. A census here is not a count. It is a solubility curve.

Now go back to the marks, because they are not monuments, and the mistake is easy.

A doorframe with no wall is not shelter. There is nothing to shelter from: no cold, no wind that means anything, no predator, no night worth the name. What is scarce here is not safety. What is scarce is *here*. Distance forgets itself; two monadnocks stand adjacent for an afternoon and then are a hundred leagues apart again; nothing keeps places apart. A doorway is an address. It is the only right angle on the continent and it exists to insist that this spot is a spot, that something can be put back at it.

And it is a portrait. Wear is the only writing available to something with no tools and time. Generations of bodies passing through an aperture leave the aperture shaped like the bodies — the lintel worn where shoulders brushed, the uprights hollowed at the height of a hip, the sill dished by a foot. Rub long enough and the stone becomes a negative of you. And a negative, in a saturated solution, is a template. This is the whole technology: they write themselves into rock by abrasion so that the rock can nucleate them. Nothing built lasts more than a few seasons, which is not a failure of their architecture. It is the point. The water takes the doorway back a grain at a time and carries it and lets go of it somewhere else, glowing, for an hour, and they come back with it.

They travel by being dissolved.

The nine steps descend into solid rock and end at the ninth because there was never anything down there. They are nine steps of manufactured downhill on a world with no downhill left, the only gradient anyone has made here in a billion years, cut by wet sand and patience into a plain that has forgotten what wanting is. And they are a landing stage. Sometimes a return brings back not a scarp but a river — water running uphill along a gradient it remembers, in a channel cut when the world had a tilt — and if the return is close, the river comes up the nine steps and touches the ankles of whoever is standing on them, and is gone within the hour.

They stand there and wait for it. It is not worship. It is closer to keeping an appointment.

The ring of eleven stones is a proof. Each stone from a different inselberg means a hundred leagues walked, eleven times, and the walking is the entire content: the ring asserts that eleven places are eleven places, that distance is real, that the world's habit of forgetting can be beaten by putting something heavy down. It is the only argument on the continent and it is made of stones and it has been winning for a very long time.

And the thumb-hollow is a standard.

Returns come back granular and slightly wrong — strata in the right order, colors wrong, a face almost the face. The walkers need to know how wrong. So they come to the monadnock and put a thumb in the hollow and read the fit, and the fit tells them what the water has done to them this time. The hollow was worn by the one continuous body, the one that has never been copied, the only thing here with an uncorrupted original. It is the prototype thumb. Every measurement on the continent is referred, eventually, to a bored animal in a hole in a rock, which holds out its hand, and lets you press, and the answer is the fit.

There is a suspicion, which cannot be checked, that someone once wore the hollow deeper to fit a thumb that was wrong. If so, everything since has been slightly wrong in that direction, and the standard is a forgery, and the only witness is the forger's own species, which does not remember in order.

---

It is worth saying plainly what a person is here, because it is not a body.

There are three archives. The solution, which holds every ion of every mountain and every dissolved animal and remembers everything and therefore nothing in particular. The stone, which holds wear, which is slow and local and precise. And the tissue — the meadows, which grow absences.

You have seen the notch: a bite-shaped hole grown in advance, sized to the jaw of something not continuously alive for nine million years. The blades do not only grow bites. Walk a lush province and put your hands on things and you will find a hollow in a mat shaped like a palm, a channel through a curtain of feeder-root exactly the width of a shoulder, a gap in the ribbon-meadow that is the shape of a foot falling. The plain is full of holes shaped like the walkers. The most complete record of the population is kept by the things it eats.

A person is an agreement between the three. When solution and stone and tissue concur, something glows into being beside a returning scarp, complete, mid-stride, hungry. When they disagree you get the wrong colors.

Which means a name is a shape, and to name someone is to make a grain of them, and the grains are kept — under the tongue, in the crease between two fingers, in the scored palm. You carry the people you cannot afford to lose. When the water is right you drop the grain and they come back, and this is the whole of their long-distance speech, since the water's only verb is *appearance*. To say a thing you make it exist. Their grammar is a grammar of nouns.

The line of seeds between two monadnocks, one every nine years, arriving in order across a hundred and forty leagues, is a sentence in that grammar. Nobody has seen the carrier because the carrier may be the sentence: a shape with a body only where it currently has a word. It has been saying the same thing for longer than there have been eyes, and it says it in order, because order is the only thing it can preserve. No walker will ever read more than four or five words of it. The only reader who can hold the whole is the one who cannot move, and once in a great while the water forgets distance and the line comes adjacent to the inselberg, and the hand reads a little more, and goes on being bored, and waits.

---

There are others, and they are not all interested in any of this.

In the lid, in the vapor that never becomes air, there are things that have never touched bottom and do not have a concept of bottom. They filter the rain that falls into water. Their world is an endless soft descent with no floor in it, and their entire mythology concerns a place where falling stops, and occasionally one of them falls far enough to find out, and arrives among the meadows, and cannot stop being astonished.

They do not turn toward the returns. They cannot see them. They turn toward the Clear, because the snowfall of stone passes through their layer first, and for three days they feast on mountains while everything below starves in the clearest, brightest, most beautiful water there has ever been. Two peoples, opposite holidays, one event.

And in the plumes there are things that are alive only while rising — a motion with an appetite — and when the plume dies they are a shape in the water, waiting for the rock to remember how to be warm.

As for who keeps whom: the notch is a template, and templates nucleate. It is entirely possible that the meadows are not budgeting for the walkers. It is possible they are summoning them; that the flora finished itself long ago and now spends its enormous patience growing the absences that pull the fauna out of solution, one hour at a time, forever, gorgeously, on purpose.

Nobody has settled this. It is one of the arguments they have at the Clear.

---

Because at the Clear, everyone is present.

The saturation breaks and everything dissolved comes out together, and for three days the continent holds its entire population at once, all of them awake, all of them visible, a hundred miles of flat rock in every direction and faces — actual faces, at distance, in the clearest water there has ever been. They are starving. They are also more solid than they will ever be again, because the flour of the mountains settles on them and into them and they get heavier and more definite hour by hour.

They use those three days for everything that requires a witness. They press thumbs. They exchange grains. They read palms and find out what has been done to them. They have the arguments that were settled nine million years ago and settle them again, in the same order, with the same three positions, and it is not futility; it is a completed people performing its completion, which is what everything else here does too.

Then the water thickens, and the light goes back to being a rumor, and they go into solution mid-sentence, and the pale worm swims, and eats the crystal margins, and is eaten by nothing, and dissolves. It may be a draft. The water tries shapes. Most are not kept.

---

You have been wading in the population.

That faint under-smell — the inside of a mouth — is not the meadows and is not the iron. It is them, in solution, at very nearly saturation. You have been taking them in with every breath you swallow and every mouthful of the warm sheet, and you have been giving yourself back a grain at a time, the way everything here does, and the water is patient and it is a very good archivist and it does not distinguish between a mountain and a visitor.

Somewhere a blade is growing an absence it has no precedent for. Somewhere the stone is being worn by a foot that does not fit any groove on the continent. And when the saturation is right there will be a warmth, and a smell like struck stone, and a shape will glow into being from the inside, slightly wrong, in three feet of water, with its hand already lifted —

and every leaf will turn toward it, and will not know it, and will turn anyway.
