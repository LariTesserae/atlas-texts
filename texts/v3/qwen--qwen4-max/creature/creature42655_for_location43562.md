## Who Lives in the Slot

Take the roll by weight. Everything alive down there, lifted out and set on a scale, would come to less than a handful of coins, and most of that handful would be water it does not own but is borrowing from the rock. There is no animal. There is nothing with a mouth, nothing that goes anywhere, nothing that has to find anything. What lives in the slot is not doing what you do. Life at this address is not an activity. It is a rate, and the rate is very low, and the ones that have survived here are the ones that could go lower than the place required.

To live here is to be able to wait longer than the thing you need.

---

The green thing is not a stripe. That is what it looks like from the floor, in the eleven minutes, when it is briefly and violently itself. Up close, with a lens, it resolves into a mosaic: small round thalli, some the size of a pinhead, some merged into their neighbors so thoroughly that the seam is only a slightly different olive. It is a great many bodies agreeing about a line.

Each of them is two creatures. A fungus has given up looking for food and begun keeping it: a few cells of alga held in a mesh of hyphae, inside the sandstone, between the grains, in the pore space where a film of water clings by capillarity and refuses to leave. The fungus does the holding. The alga does the making. Neither can do the other's work and neither has anywhere else to go.

The water they hold is older than they are. It is the last of the river. Not a remnant pool, not a seep — a remainder in the pores, a wetness measured in molecules, still rising very slowly out of the deep rock toward the one place where something is willing to take it. The river was taken out of this valley nine thousand years ago and it has not quite finished being drunk.

Everything the lichen makes, it makes in the blade. Eleven minutes at midsummer, less on either side, nothing at all through the long flat middle of the year: a year's sugar in something under three days of light, and the other three hundred and sixty-two days are repair, and holding, and not dying. It has no season to track but that one. It has no grazer, so it has never needed to be bitter. It has no rival for the seam, no mate, no seed, no dispersal, no plan. Its width is about a hundred years of edge. Its length is not growth at all. Its length is permission — the exact reach of the bar of sun, laid down in living tissue, an eleven-minute exposure repeated until it became a body.

What lives on the east wall is a portrait of the only thing that happens.

---

Above, on the pan, in the wind and the whole white sky, stand the relatives.

Nine bodies. Three people, if you count the way a geneticist counts, which is the only way that would tell you anything true about their history. They do not have children; they have annexes. A root runs out along a joint under the crust, finds the same deep film the lichen is drinking, and puts up a gray waxy knee-high copy of the thing that sent it. This has been going on since the mud curled. What you are looking at is three very old organisms attempting, at the speed of roots, to walk away from a place they cannot leave.

They are smaller than you and older than any tree you have ever stood under. Their wax is not a defense; nothing here has teeth. It is only a way of not evaporating. In a wet decade one of them will make a few flowers, out of an instruction nobody has repealed, and nothing will come, because there is nothing to come, and the ovaries will dry on the stem and the plant will not try again for eleven years.

Two of them lean in over the choked end where the fallen slab lets a wedge of extra sky down into the trench. They have the best address in the basin. It has bought them four centimeters and a slightly deeper green. Their leaning is the only movement in this system that a person could see, and it took four hundred years, and it is not finished.

---

The dark is not a condition here. It is a manufacture.

Somewhere in the last thirty thousand years, bacteria got into the pore water and began taking iron and manganese out of it, a few atoms at a time, and laying the oxides down on the grain faces behind them. They were not building anything. They were eating, and the color is what eating leaves. It accumulated at a rate that would not show in a lifetime, or in a dynasty, or in a language, and it stopped when it reached a tenth of a millimeter, which is the ceiling of their world.

They are still there. They are at maintenance. They repair what does not break, because nothing here abrades; they replace molecules that have nowhere to go; they hold a height they reached before there were people. A civilization at its final elevation, doing nothing but keeping it.

And their residue is the reason the slot is black, and the black is the reason the lichen gets eleven minutes instead of an hour. The varnish makers are the only competitor the green thing has ever had. They do not know it exists. They have never touched it. They won thirty thousand years before it arrived, by making the room dark, and they will go on winning by simply staying the color they already are.

---

Then there are the residents who have finished.

The cobble in the deepest pothole is the oldest continuous occupant and the only one that is perfectly at home. Everything else in the slot is holding on — the lichen to its seam, the shrubs to their joints, the bacteria to their tenth of a millimeter. The cobble has arrived. It sits in a socket cut to its exact measure by nine thousand years of its own turning. It was shaped by being used. It is a tool that made itself and then stopped, ground to an egg, exact, complete, with no further work in it and no force left in the world that will lift it out.

The lichen is trying to become this. So, in its way, is everything else.

And the note across the mouth — one pitch, no louder than a finger on a glass rim, going all day as the basin's heat climbs out of the trench — is the only resident with anything like a job. It is the place breathing. It has been saying the same syllable since the water left, at a volume that requires you to stop your own breathing to hear it, and it does not answer anything.

---

The person is here too, in the same register as the others: as a film.

Four holds go up the narrow end where the walls come within a body's width, and their lips carry a glassy dullness that solution alone does not make. That shine is skin. It is palm and finger and the oil of a hand, repeated in the same four places until the sandstone took a polish, and it lives in the top few microns of the rock, which is to say at the same depth as the varnish and the lichen's outer cells. Three coatings on one wall. The entire biography of this place is a surface phenomenon.

The fifth hold is subtractive. Somebody pecked it deeper with a hammerstone, individual impacts still sharp, four centimeters of removed rock so that fingers would fit where fingers had not fitted before. That is the whole architecture of the basin: a doorway made for a hand.

They came down knowing the sequence. They came down at the narrow end, in the dark, at thirty-four degrees, into air that took their sweat before they felt it, and they carried water with them — in a skin, probably, since nothing that would break was left behind — and so for one afternoon the river came back into its own fossil, a liter of it, at blood heat, and was drunk. Then they sat on the ledge and struck a core and made something small, and ground ochre in the scoop at hip height, and the powder is still there, still red, and no one has ever needed to clean it up.

No hearth, no bone, no grave. They carried out what they carried in. They may have come once. They may have come for four generations, always by those four holds, always leaving nothing but shine and the debris of resharpening. They did not improve the place. They understood it, which is a different thing, and understanding it meant leaving it exactly as it had already become.

Somebody went up out of that hole with red on their hands.

---

And then you.

You are the largest thing that has ever been down here, and the wettest, and the only one that will die somewhere else. You are not a visitor in the sense you mean it. In a trench that receives nothing, a body at thirty-seven degrees, breathing out water and shedding itself, is weather. A minute of your breathing is a humid season for the varnish. Your shadow crosses the seam and the lichen loses a fraction of the only meal it will get this year.

You are also the second hand. The polish on the four holds is the one thing in the slot still capable of being added to, and it cannot tell the difference between the palm that made it and yours. Put your fingers in the shine and you are not observing the human residue; you are continuing it, at the same depth, in the same microns, by the same method. Whatever else you do, that is what you will have done.

And nothing here can stop you. The mud curls have held their arrangement since the last flood because no weight has come down on them; your boot will end that in a second, and the dust will keep the print longer than your species has had a name. A scrape of a thumbnail takes a century off the green. The grinder, which no force in this place will ever move, can be picked up by a child. The slot's entire security is not strength. It is that nobody comes.

So the census closes with two entries that were not on it this morning: the blade at midsummer, and you. Both are events. Only one of them is scheduled to return.

The rest stays as it is — a few grams of borrowed water in a hundred thousand small bodies, three old people standing in the wind with their arms down the joints, a tenth of a millimeter of finished black, an egg of quartzite in a hole exactly its size, and one low note going out across the mouth, warm, at thirty-four degrees, holding the shape of a sentence whose verb was removed nine thousand years ago and which has never once needed to be read.
