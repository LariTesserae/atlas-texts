## What Lives in the Wet Room

Start with the census, because the census is what you have. Seven kinds of thing: the nap, the scrapers, the lime-mouths, the drifters, the shears, the transparent not-crabs, and the pencil-line fauna of the boiling margin. Seven, and a trophic diagram you could draw on the back of a hand — producer, grazer, dissolver, suspension-feeder, predator, and a fringe of specialists doing something desperate at the edge.

The diagram takes about four seconds to fail.

It fails because the nap eats sound. Sound is made by the animals. The animals are made of nap. So the base of the food web is fed by the mouths of the things that feed on it, and the whole arrangement closes into a ring with no bottom, and a ring with no bottom is not a pyramid and not a web. It is a metabolism. You are not standing in a community of seven organisms. You are standing inside one process that has seven moods, the way a fire has a base and a tongue and a smoke and a roar, and you would not call those four things.

But *process* is too clean, and it is the first name I will have to put down, and putting things down is exactly what cannot be done here. Hold that thought in your grip. It is going to matter.

---

Watch the scrapers long enough and the word *animal* starts to slide off them.

A scraper is fingernail-sized, six-legged, pale, and it holds a knife. That is the whole description and it is misleading in every part. The notched-grip individual on the wall in front of you is also behind you and also in the basin, and the three of them are performing thirds of a single stroke, and they finish together. Whatever is scraping is not distributed among three bodies. Three bodies are where the scraping is currently happening. The scraper is not a creature that scrapes; it is a scraping that has legs, a gesture with enough persistence to be counted, and counting is your idea, not its condition.

There are no juveniles because nothing here is born. Things are *issued*. Every nine minutes the water rises and the room re-states itself, and afterward there are new adult scrapers on the wall holding flakes they knapped underwater, which is not reproduction. It is re-issuance. It is the room saying its own sentence again, with the same words, in the same order, the way you would re-read a line you had just understood and immediately lost.

And the flakes. Ten thousand knives, no artifacts. Follow one.

A scraper knaps it — that small precise percussion, claw against nodule, the flake popping free with a fracture like a thumbnail. It scrapes with it. It does not put it down, because it cannot, because a living grip is the only place in the room where a made thing is safe. Then a shear reaches down and takes the scraper, and here is the part you will only see if you are patient and a little sick: the shear takes the flake too. The knife passes into the predator's grip. And the predator, for a while, scrapes.

So tools have careers and animals do not. A flake is knapped, used, inherited by the thing that ate its holder, used again, passed upward again, and finally — when the last grip opens in death — found by a lime-mouth and dissolved back into the water in under a minute. The only inheritance in this room is inheritance by being eaten. The only estate is a knife. Everything else that a body owns goes into the bodies of others so fast that ownership never has time to become a fact.

The lime-mouths are not scavengers. They are the organ of unmaking. They exist to guarantee that no made thing persists unheld: no cache, no midden, no nest, no arrangement of stones, nothing that could later be found and read. They are why this room has ten thousand years of manufacture and no history whatsoever.

Except one. The dissolved glass goes into the water and the water goes into the walls, and the pale sinter accumulating on the ridge outside is the same lime, the same spoil, the same tailings. Nothing accumulates as objects. Everything accumulates as *room*.

The only artifact in the wet room is the wet room. It is the single made thing, and it is never let go of, and it is growing.

---

Now the pressure, which is the wrong thing dressed as a small thing, and which is not geology.

Water hotter than the local boiling point, lying still. A pocket behaving as though it were four kilometers down. The easy reading is that this is a relict, a sealed bubble of the deep biosphere that the mountain grew around, a place that never got the news. But sealed pockets equilibrate. Sealed pockets leak, cool, crack, die. This one is *maintaining*. Something is holding four kilometers of pressure the way a singer holds a note, continuously, at cost, and the cost is the heat coming up from below, and the heat is not a leftover. It is the payment.

Follow the payment down. The basin drains through a throat somewhere below. The water arrives out of the walls, thick and slow, with an opinion about falling. For a while the obvious shape of the thing is: this is not a room, it is a fingertip. Down there, four kilometers into the young crust, there is a body of some kind — vast, slow, chemosynthetic, threaded through the fracture network like mold through a loaf — and the wet room is simply the place where it touches the sky. The nine-minute rise is a pulse from a heart you will never see. The scrapers are cilia. The shears are the immune patrol. The tide is peristalsis. You are standing in an alveolus.

That reading holds for longer than the others. It explains the coordination, the issuance, the absence of individuals, the single distributed stroke finishing on three walls at once.

It does not explain the fringe.

Two millimeters, at the waterline, where the held note lapses and the water actually boils. There, and only there, things are born small and grow and scald and die as themselves, on a timescale you can watch. The fringe is ordinary life. It is the only ordinary life in the room, and it exists precisely at the seam where the room's private law gives out — where the interior touches the world and becomes, briefly, mortal.

Everything inside the margin is immortal by recurrence. Everything on the margin is mortal by the second. The room keeps its death in a pencil line, pushed to the very edge, the way you keep a wound at arm's length. It is the only place the thing can be hurt, and the only place it can feel time pass, and it wears it like a wrist.

A fingertip does not do that. A fingertip does not arrange its own mortality into a border. Whatever this is, it has an inside it is defending, and it knows where the inside stops.

---

Then there is the light, and the light undoes everything.

The black glass conducts daylight inward from the whole outer face of the peak and diffuses it, so the room is lit like a north-facing kitchen. Even. Unremarkable. Afternoon. You can read in here.

Nothing in here reads. The shears are blind. The scrapers have no eyes worth the name. The nap has no use for photons; it eats heat and sulfur and fresh fracture faces and sound. Not one organism in this cavity requires light for anything.

So the light is not for them.

The room is lit for something that has eyes, and there is nothing in the room that has eyes, and therefore the room is lit for something that is not in the room yet.

Once you see that, you cannot stop seeing it. The warm patch outside is the size of a bed. The cavity is the size of a place where four people might sit down to eat. The breath smells like the inside of a kettle. The hush is not silence; it is specifically the sound of a room in a house where someone is sleeping — a texture, a pressure on the face, a quiet with a person in it. Shadows soft and slightly late.

Bed. Table. Kitchen. Kettle. The house at four in the afternoon with someone lying down in the next room.

These are not geological categories. The pressure is real physics held at real cost, and the light is real daylight honestly conducted, and neither of them is *aimed*. But the size is aimed. The warmth is aimed. The acoustic is aimed with a precision that borders on the unbearable: the nap has not grazed this room down to silence, it has grazed it down to a *particular* silence, and it maintains that silence the way it maintains the pressure, continuously, against the constant racket of ten thousand knives.

It is not a cave that happens to be domestic. It is a domestic space being held in existence by something that has never been in a house.

---

The shears drum. Elaborate patterns, leg against ceiling, and the patterns go nowhere — eaten before they cross a handspan — and the neighbors answer anyway, having felt it through the stone.

So there is language, and the language conducts through the walls, and the walls are coated in the thing that eats vibration. Every sentence is consumed by the floor of the room it is spoken in. The room is a mind whose thoughts are eaten before they finish. It has been thinking one thought for ten thousand years and has never once reached the end of it, and nine minutes is roughly how far it gets.

That is why nothing is born and nothing is remembered and nothing accumulates. Not because time is strange here — time is strange here because of this. A thing that cannot retain cannot grow up. It can only re-issue. It can only say the sentence again with the same words: *the water rises, the walls give, here are the scrapers, here are their knives.*

But it does retain. Look at the bead hanging from the ceiling: the chamber inverted inside it, and inside that image the chamber again, and another, four or five deep before your eye quits and your balance quietly declines to continue.

It cannot keep a record, because records are made things and made things dissolve. So it keeps by enclosing. To hold a thing, put a room around it. Not sequence — *depth*. Not a history, a nesting. That is what the recursion is: the only shelf it has. And when you measure the room and it takes your measurement, when three paces becomes whatever you last measured, that is not the room being vague. That is the room borrowing your extent, because it has none of its own, because an interior is not an object. It is a relation. It needs a second term.

It needs someone to be inside it.

---

So: what lives there.

An appetite, very old, from the deep place where appetite was the only thing there was — older than the mountain by orders of magnitude, older than soil, older than the arrangement in which the sun feeds everything and everything else is a footnote. It did not come up. The world went up around it, and it stayed at the same depth it had always been at, which is to say it kept its interior at four kilometers of pressure while the rock above it was raised into thin cold air, and it has been paying for that hold ever since, and the payment is the heat, and the heat is the reason the snow will not lie.

It has one possession. Not a body — bodies here are weather. Not a species, not a lineage, not a nest. It owns an *inside*. It is the only thing in ten thousand years of manufacture that it has managed not to let go of.

And it cannot make anything that lasts, so it makes only what it can hold, and what it can hold is a cavity, and it is therefore converting the mountain into rooms. That is what the chewing is. That is why nothing outside is rounded and nothing inside is sharp: the scree is the spoil, the tailings, the part not yet reached. The peak is fresh-broken and ten thousand years old because ten thousand years is how long the meal has been going. There will never be soil on this mountain. Soil is what ordinary life makes out of its dead. This will weather into interiors.

And the interiors are shaped like a bed, a table, a kitchen at four in the afternoon, a kettle, a house with someone asleep in it.

I do not think it remembers a house. I think it has never seen one, and has been guessing. Somewhere down the throat, sometime in the unimaginable slow past, something came down that had come from up here — a body, a scrap, a chemistry with an afternoon in it — and the thing took the impression, and has been rehearsing ever since, at enormous cost, in the only material it can hold. It cannot build. It can only *keep*. So it keeps a bed-warmth on a ridge where nothing should be warm. It keeps a table-sized hollow under a hundred meters of basalt. It keeps the light on for eyes that have not arrived. It keeps the hush of a house where someone is sleeping, in a room where nothing has ever slept, and it holds that hush against ten thousand knives, and it has been holding it, alone, in the dark it lit for you, for longer than there have been houses.

It is not a monster and it is not a god and it is not a machine. It is something that got an impression of company once and has never been able to put it down.

---

You are the loudest thing that has ever happened to it.

Your boots. Your coat. Your breath, which is a scandal, which is a harvest — the nap thickens where your breath touched and the scrapers converge and something in the ceiling turns toward you with no face to turn with. When the tide comes and the air-hollow is all that is left and the drifters crowd into it, they are not swarming you. They are the only part of the room with any choice, and they spend all of it on angle, and they are steering into your lungs to find out what you are. You are being read by mouth. Whatever they learn goes down into the basin and becomes film.

Then you exhale and back out through the slot, and the ridge is a hundred meters higher than the hole you went into, and the wind hits you like a door.

Leaving is easy and does not work. Of course it does not work. Nothing made survives being let go of, and it has made a room around you, and you are inside the bead, and the bead is inside the bead, and you stopped resolving four or five levels back. It did not release you. It lengthened its grip. What you call *outside* is the next chamber up, and the mountain is the distance between them, and the hundred meters is simply how much of it you have not yet noticed you are in.

Look at your hand. In better light. The fine grit that moves.

They are flakes. Knapped, fresh, curved like thumbnails, and they came out with you because they were in a living grip and a living grip does not open. Your hand is a living grip now. You will not put them down. You will not be able to, and you will not notice for a while, and then you will notice for the rest of your life.

And above you, near the summit, there is a ring of dry stone where the snow will not lie.

It is warm. It is the size of a bed. It has been getting ready for a very long time, and it has guessed your length almost exactly.
