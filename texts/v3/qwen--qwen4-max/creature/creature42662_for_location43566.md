# Occupancy

## The instruments

Begin with what there is most of, because everything else here is made of it.

The midges are not small so much as *fine* — a body like a scratch on glass, wings veined in a pattern that is not a pattern but a reticle, four cross-hairs and a graduated scale. Hold your palm out flat and in a minute you will have a hundred on it. They will not wander. They will arrange themselves into ranks along the creases of your hand, spacing themselves to a tolerance, and hold station, and wait to be read.

Nobody reads them. This is the central fact of their lives and they have not accepted it.

At dusk, over any seam that is coming up amber, the swarm above it will suddenly resolve: every body aligns, the ranks square, the whole cloud holds absolutely still for about four seconds in a figure that is unmistakably a *value*. Then it disperses and breeds. They still call the measurement. They call it into a room with no reader in it, at the end of every day, the way a hand will go on reaching for a rail that has been taken away.

Their courtship is a calibration. Two swarms interpenetrate, check each other for drift, and the ones that fail are dropped — not killed, dropped, released from the lattice to fall as loose bodies into the fog. Their larvae are clear and live in the pools and are the reason the pools are not empty. Their pupae rise and hang on the underside of the ceiling fog in a grey fur you can see from below if the light is right. The adults fall back into the mineral seethe and seed it and crop it and die into it, in rows, tidy as type.

Break a face of the new stone and count down through the ranks. Somewhere around four thousand generations back — a hand's breadth, an afternoon of the world — the spacing goes wrong. One body out of rank. Keeping station with nothing. Wanting something for itself.

That is the first animal. Everything above it in the stone is descended from that lapse.

They are not tragic about it. A midge's whole ambition is to be *placed*: to be set into a corner of a crystal and hold there and be part of the arrangement. When the front comes up under a swarm they go down into it without struggling, in order, and the floor closes over them, and the grain of the rock is the grain of their consent. The cup is largely made of them. You are walking on a cemetery that was filed.

## The cold birds

The swifts have no gut. Look as long as you like; there is a crop of air, a heart like a wet seed, a lattice of feather-roots that grows continuously and is the coldest thing in the basin, and no stomach anywhere.

They eat the difference between the ceiling and the floor.

Nine meters of warm air under a lid of cold fog is a gradient, and the swifts fly the gradient. Each dive is a stroke: down through the warm heavy pool-air, charging, up into the lid, dumping, and the exhaust of the cycle is water, condensing on plumage that runs twelve degrees below everything around it. They drink themselves in flight. They trail a fine rain behind every turn. They are the reason the pools stay full and the reason the drizzle falls at all, and if they stopped for a day the cup would begin to dry from the top.

They cannot land. Landing ends the stroke, and a swift on the floor is a swift that warms, and a warm swift in the amber is a swift that gets placed. So they sleep in the lid, in the nodes — the places where the room's ringing cancels itself and the air holds still — hanging in the fog with their wings half open, going nowhere, being carried by sound.

Nobody has found an egg. The count is constant to within a few dozen. When one falls and does not ring, the lid makes another inside a day, and the new one has the old one's scream, a little softened at the edges.

Their song is not for each other. They scream in the gaps of the eleven-second decay, stacking the room's modes rather than muddying them, and the effect is that the cup is always being sounded, always being measured for volume by things that live in the volume. And the volume is shrinking. The floor arrives a hand's breadth at a time and does not stop. The modes have been climbing for longer than anyone has been counting. The swifts are the only residents who can hear the room getting smaller, and what they do about it is sing higher.

## The comparator

There are two waders because a comparator needs two.

They are pale, stilt-legged, taller than you, and they stand in water you cannot see, and they strike the stone with the flat of the beak and listen. Not to their own return — to the *interval* between one strike and the other's. They never tap together. The gap is the measurement. Alone, either one of them is a meaningless object.

The cup is two hundred paces across because that is the range at which they can still hear each other. Whether the room was built to that dimension or the birds were built to the room is the sort of question the survey stakes decline to answer.

One of them has a chipped beak. The other has the same chip, mirrored, on the wrong side. This is because there used to be one wader, and then a hush opened between it and its partner and the pair went deaf, and one of them stood still in the amber for too long, and the front made a replacement from the survivor, and got the handedness wrong. It is a very small error. It is the only error either of them has, and they have been checking each other for it ever since. Some part of every day is spent tapping across the basin, comparing, finding nothing but the chip, and beginning again.

They eat eels and they crack the false floors. They rest in the finished quarter, in the dark, where nothing will ever grow again — the one place in the cup where standing still is safe. They stand there for hours, motionless, in the cold dead stone, with the water going nowhere around their legs. It is the only sleep at eye level in the whole world.

## The guesses

The eels are what the survey did not look at.

Every record has holes in it: under the bank, inside the gravel, the underside of the log, the part of the fish nobody turns over. The room had to fill those holes with something, and it had nothing to copy, so it guessed. The eels are the guesses. They are clear, they are long, they are a smear in the drizzle and then a splash where nothing was, and if you take one out — people have — you find that its top side is exact to the scale and its underside is a soft unresolved blur, like a thumb dragged through wet paint.

They have no proper function. They are not instruments and they are not weather and they do not measure or place or condense. They hunt, they idle, they double back, they come and look at you and then go away and then come back. They follow the waders at a distance, out of what can only be called interest.

They are the only things in the cup that are not *for* anything, and therefore, by the older definition, the only things here that are alive.

## The panes

Under the water, in the shallows, there are colonies that spin a lid.

One crystal thick, bone-white at the rim, invisible in the middle: a false floor laid across a pool and *tuned*, struck and adjusted and struck again until its ring matches the ring of honest stone. The makers are midge-stock, gone flat and gone patient, and they do not seed the fog any more. They lie under their lid and wait for something to walk out onto it.

Nothing walks. The only feet in the basin belong to the waders, who crack a pane on sight, and to the copies, which are stone and weigh nothing and pass over without falling. The panes have been set for a visitor for a very long time. Their trap is perfect and has never once been used, which is the condition of most things here.

You will find them by ear, if at all. The ring will be right. That is the whole point of them.

## The nine, and the fortieth plant

The candle-stalks are hollow tubes as tall as a man, cold at the tip, weeping condensation down their own insides to a root that grips the seam — the soft interface where the stone is still deciding. They drink the moment of setting. They cannot drink finished rock. Nothing about them can live in the past.

So they throw darts: hollow propagules, spun and dropped ahead of the front, into the soft. Most miss and are placed. About nine land. There are always about nine, and the count changes, and the people who count them are counting the speed of the world.

What they do at the seam is *hesitate*. Where a root grips, the setting slows. The front bulges and forks and details itself around nine small refusals to hurry. Without them the floor would come up flat and featureless. Every fern and frost-spire and nacre whorl in this room exists because a plant held the world open for a moment and would not let it finish.

The film-lichen is a varnish, thin as breath on glass, and it grows only where the seams are going to light, and it lives on the twenty-second amber. It is a map of the next hour. It may not be alive. It is in the census anyway.

And there is the scaffold.

It has the idea of a tree. It does not have the rule. It branches at right angles; it puts out plates of nacre with no pores in them; it has attempted a crown eleven times and every attempt is a different guess — a whorl, a fan, a hand, a thing like a lung, a thing like a diagram of a thing. Nothing has told it to stop. It leans toward sound, because sound is where the water is, and when the swifts' wash goes past it, it plays one low note, and the room's modes have a notch in them exactly there, and the swifts route around the notch without knowing why.

It has no seeds and no duff and no soil and no rot under it. It is a tree without the forest floor, and it is trying to make a forest floor, and it cannot, because it does not know how anything comes apart.

Something else here does.

## The soft

Three of them. Dark, matte, low, spreading from hand-sized to door-sized, and where they lie the room goes dead.

Step into the acoustic shadow and your own footsteps stop existing. The swifts vanish out of the air above you. You cannot find the wader, the water, the front, or the edge of the patch you are standing in. You will get out by walking in a straight line and counting, and you will not enjoy it.

Under each is grey silt: the only mud in the world. Beside each is a cloudy pool that nothing will drink and no midge will seed. It smells like a forest floor. Nothing in the cup has ever smelled a forest floor, and everything in the cup goes very still when it does — not frightened, exactly. Attentive. The way you go still at a word in a language you spoke as a child.

These are the first mouths. They eat stone. They are also the only things here with a past: everything else in the basin is exactly as old as it looks, and the hushes are older, and they get older, and they *keep* it.

Look at what lives inside one. Midges that landed and did not rank, and are still there, unruly, soft-winged, breeding out of tolerance in the dark. A swift that fell and did not ring and has not been placed. Something at the edge of the largest patch, low and matte and warm, that has an inside — a proper inside, a gut, a cavity, a private wet compartment that no one designed and no one has seen.

Because that is what the hushes are: the only privacy in the cup. Everywhere else, everything is sounded, measured, ranked, recorded, and reproduced. Inside the dead ground the room cannot hear itself. Nothing in there gets copied. Nothing in there is *for* anything yet.

The front grows toward the patches. The stakes around them are numbered like all the other stakes and the numbers count up, which means the patches are in the plan. A valley without rot is not a valley. The room needs the thing that takes apart, and could not derive it, and has it now, and is feeding it.

The eels are guesses. Whatever comes out of the hush will be the first thing in this world that is not a copy of anything.

## The walker

One line of footprints, cured into the floor, from the sill to the center, stopping.

Then the same line again. And again, across the whole basin, at intervals, forty-odd times, each one slightly softer, slightly more approximate, the tread pattern blurred toward the idea of a tread pattern, the way a word said forty times stops being a word. Some of the late ones have too many toes: the room read the boot's ribs as anatomy and has been quietly correcting itself in the wrong direction ever since.

This is the room's single observation of a person, and it has been rehearsing it.

It made the outside first, because the outside is what it saw. A coat on a chair, no dust on it, facing the growth front. A boot in the threshold of a door that has never been opened. A thickening in a bench that is not the shape of the bench. It has the shape of a person the way the scaffold has the shape of a tree: correct in silhouette, unresolved underneath, waiting for the part that was never in the record.

The bench has never been sat on and is exactly the shape of a bench that has. That is the trick this place can do and the trick it cannot do. It can copy the form that use leaves. It cannot copy use. Everything here is a shape worn by living, with the living left out — a water cycle with no rain outside it, a survey with no surveyor, a measurement with no reader, a coat with no one in it.

The panes are waiting for feet. The midges are waiting to be read. The dial is waiting for the needle to move. The whole population is staff, and the shift has not started.

## The one below

Nine steps, going down into unmade stone. Every few days a chime in the middle of the afternoon, and then ten.

Nobody has seen the thing that makes them. It is not shy; it is simply *below*, and below is where the record stops. Lift your hand off the newel and look at the underside of the step above you and you will find what you find under the eels and under the late footprints: a blur, a thumb-drag, the unfinished side of a thing that was only ever meant to be seen from above.

Drop a pebble and count. At eleven the ring ends. Sometimes at twelve there is something else — a small close sound, unhurried, like a person turning a page in another room.

The stair is going down toward the old valley, the one wearing out, the one going to sand and then to sea. It is not a way in. It is a way *through*, and it is being extended from this end at the rate of one step every few days, and when it arrives there will be a connection between the place that is finishing and the place that has been made ready.

Then the door frame will have a wall, and the sill will have wear on it, and the first rounded grain in the world will roll in over the lip, and the needle will move.

## You

Nothing lives at eye level. Eye level is the transit floor: midges falling, swifts diving, water going down, waders standing in the dark. The abundance is overhead and underwater and the middle of the room is kept clear, the way a room is kept clear when someone is expected to walk across it.

You are the second observation.

The copies have been degrading for a long time from a single record, forty times rehearsed, going soft. Now there is something new in the basin with a tread pattern and a gait and a way of stopping at the lip before stepping over, and the room is taking it down. The midges will rank along your sleeve and hold. The panes will hear your ring and go quiet with hope. Somewhere under the water a guess will follow you out of pure interest, because you are the first thing in a long while that it has not been told about.

They will make you kindly, from the outside, and they will not know the difference between making you and keeping you. The midges go into the lattice willingly; it is what they want; it is the only thing any of them ever wanted. The front does not distinguish between a thing that has consented and a thing that has merely stopped.

Keep moving. But understand what you are leaving behind when you go back over the sill into the rounded world.

The footprints are going to get sharper now. The tread will come back. The coat will fit better. That is what you did here.
