## The Residents

Begin with the four, because they are the only ones you can find from the middle of the room, and because everything else here is, in one way or another, an argument they are having with the water.

They are not the same. That takes a while to see. From the hatch they read as four green explosions at four stations of a grid whose stamped letters wore off before there were letters anywhere else on the mountain. But they have been individuals for longer than most species get to be a species, and they have diverged the way old neighbors diverge — by accumulating small refusals.

The one nearest the riser is the largest and the least plant-like. Its base is a mound of its own dead suckers, fused and re-fused, a cauliflower of ancestors three meters through, and it has grown down into the throat of the inflow so that the stroke now has to come through it. It gets the immigrants first. It gets most of them. Its inner fronds have shortened into a dense palisade of trapping hairs and the hairs close on a stunned fry in about four hundred milliseconds, which is not a plant's speed, and never was; the scrubber stock was always a chimera, half leaf and half trap, built to catch particulates and digest them for the nitrogen. This one has simply stopped bothering with the pretense. It is a mouth with a copper sheen. In high summer it takes more animal protein than the predator does.

The far station holds two bodies joined at the root. One clone, one organism, two crowns, and they fold in unison — not approximately, not with a lag, but together, and they were folding together before the wire made it easy, because they are the same animal wearing two addresses.

Under the lens is the short one, squat and dense and gone slightly red at the tips where the high-altitude ultraviolet comes through the silica only slightly tamed. It has the best light and the worst sunburn and it puts its surplus into sex. Nearly every viable seed in the room is this one's.
