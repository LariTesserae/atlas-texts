## First, the thing you will lean on

It is doing nothing, and it has been doing nothing for longer than your species has had a word for nothing.

Palm-sized, coiled, chalk-white, fluted lengthwise by the same dissolution that flutes the columns, so that the shell is not camouflage so much as a local sample. It is grazing. It grazes by acid: a soft sole laid against the throat of a column, a secretion, a wait, and then two microns of stone lifted off with the rust varnish still in it. It cannot bite. Nothing here can bite. It dissolves its food loose and drinks it, and the carbonate it does not want it lays down at the lip of its own shell, so the shell is made of every throat it has ever eaten. Cut the coil and you have a cast of a route, four hundred years of absence wound into a spiral.

Inside the coil, sealed, is a small room of still air at the pressure of the day the animal came out of the plumbing. That is what the shell is for. It is not armor. It is a reference. Along the fringe, the cilia read the world; inside the coil, the world as it was; and the difference between them is the only thought this animal has.

It has no front. Whichever edge is reading is the face. It moves down the gradient, always toward quieter, and it does not choose to, any more than water chooses downhill, and everything that looks like a life — the routes, the rendezvous, the four centuries — is a consequence of that one preference.

It dies of leakage. The sealed room loses its baseline over about four hundred years, a few parts in a million a decade, and one day the animal reads a maximum as a corridor and walks into a white wall with no walls in it and cannot find its way out. Nothing here is killed by the noise. They are killed by forgetting what quiet felt like.

## The only sideways motion in the world

Follow the mineral and you go up a wick and down a shaft and up again, eleven thousand years, vertically, in place. The stone has no lateral circulation. The water has no lateral circulation. The light has no direction at all.

The animals are the only thing in forty kilometers that carries anything across.

Ten thousand of them, in a bowl of a hundred million pipes, each one hauling dissolved carbonate a few meters a day from the throat where it took it to the pavement where it lays it down. It is a rounding error in the mass budget. It is also the only smoothing the field has, the only way a difference between one district and the next can be carried over and evened out, and it is why the dead district cannot heal. Nothing can bring stone into the Stop. The one thing that could is blind there.

So: the herd is not a population. It is a circulation. And what it inherits is not a body plan — the body is trivial, a wet fringe and a bag of acid and a coil — but a *place*. The lattice is the heredity. A corridor three hundred meters long between two maxima has been occupied continuously for four million years by descendants of whatever first found it, and none of them ever knew there was anywhere else, and the shape of their lives is the shape of a standing wave in air that has nothing to do with them and will not notice when they stop.

At the junctions, where corridors cross, they sometimes touch. Sometimes there is a smear left there, an ester laid down in the film, and it means one thing only: *not you; this way is eaten.* It will still be legible in eighty years. It will be buried under precipitate in two hundred. It cannot be read without being eaten — the reading destroys it and feeds the reader a little — and then the mineral it was written on dissolves at some crown, rides down, wicks up, and is laid back over as anonymous white.

That is the whole of communication here. A sentence that means *someone else*, left where it will be buried, understood by being consumed, and finally circulated into the general brightness. Nothing is sent. Things are only left, and then dissolved, and then they come back as the weather.

## The nursery, and the arithmetic

They lay in the cupped depression at the crown, which is the highest point of the world and the wettest, and the eggs go down the bore with the weeping water.

The young are not small versions of the adults. They are tube animals. They live in the dark in a two-finger shaft with a millimeter of falling water on their backs, and they have no cilia and no coil and no need of either, because their world has one dimension and two directions and nothing ever happens in it that is not up or down. They cling. They filter. They grow for sixty or eighty years in a roaring blackness they cannot perceive as roaring, because at their size the eleven-hertz fundamental is not a pressure gradient but a tide, a uniform squeeze, the whole universe breathing on them.

They also detune the pipe. A bore with a body in it is a slightly narrower bore, and its voice goes flat by a fraction so small that no instrument you brought would find it against the mass of tone. A hundred million pipes, some fraction always occupied, the fraction changing over centuries. The walls do not move; the corridors will be where they are in a thousand years. But the grain inside a corridor, the fine texture of the wetness, has a slow shimmer in it, one part in a million, and it is the census. It is the only vibrato in the world. It is the sound of there being children.

Then they are too large, and they go out through the vent — the lip that makes the noise, the mouth of the pipe — squeezed by the compressed air of the bulb, and they come out naked. No shell. They cannot make one until they have eaten stone, and they cannot eat stone until they reach a throat, and they cannot find a throat, because they emerge into a hundred-and-twenty-decibel jet at the exact center of a maximum, where their unformed fringe reads nothing whatsoever.

They are blind at birth in the loudest place in the world. They let go and the air throws them and where they land is not anything they have a say in. Most go into the honeycomb and down through the cells to the dark water.

Over four hundred years a grazer will lay something on the order of a hundred million eggs. The number of adults is one per ten thousand columns and has been one per ten thousand columns since before there were eyes. So exactly one of those hundred million comes out of the vent, lands in a minimum, finds a throat, and seals its little room of quiet. Exactly one, every time, for ten thousand generations.

It is a lottery run at a scale that has become indistinguishable from a law.

## The underside

The water table is a meter and a half down everywhere and perfectly still, and water does not take the air's standing wave. It refuses it. Under the pavement there is a second silence, and it is not a hole in the sense-world like the Stop; it is a whole country, continuous, dark, forty kilometers wide, with a ceiling.

The flat thing lives against that ceiling.

A millimeter thick, translucent, meters across, spread along the underside of the fused pavement where the honeycomb opens out, and it eats the rain. The rain is larvae, coming down through the cells all day every day from a hundred million bores, and it has been falling for four million years, and the sheet lies under it with its whole body open.

It is the only animal in the bowl with an up. Everything else here is radial, or fringe-minded, or has no direction but quieter; the sheet has a face against a surface and a back against the water, and it knows which, and that makes it the only thing here that could be said to have a posture. It navigates by the texture of the ceiling. It goes from sump to sump underneath, through cells barely wider than itself, taking a decade over a hundred meters, and what you see in a sump-hole — a pale plane under the black-blue stillness, moving so slowly you will call it a mineral film — is a hand's-breadth of an animal that may be continuous under a third of the bowl.

It is the rarest thing to see and the largest thing there. Nobody has ever seen it whole. Nobody ever will; there is no place to stand. It knows the field as a roof with a few windows in it, and it does not know there is light, and the light is what lifts the water that brings it its food.

## The other nation

Here is what I got wrong for a long time. I thought the corridors were the habitat and the maxima were the desert.

The maxima are full.

They are full of things that do not read pressure at all — that have no fringe, no coil, no reference chamber, nothing that the noise could blind, because they were never looking. White crusts, rosettes, small fused bells, cemented to the stone at the exact places nothing walks. They do not navigate. They have never navigated. They have no more use for a route than a chimney has.

And they do not breathe. They have no muscles for it. A sac at an antinode is compressed and released eleven times a second by the fundamental, forever, and that is the ventilation; the fluid inside them is moved by the same interference pattern that would erase a grazer's mind. Their hearts are outside their bodies and forty kilometers wide and driven by sunlight lifting water. They cannot be moved somewhere quieter. Quieter would suffocate them.

So there are two faunas in the bowl and their maps are the negative of each other. One's roads are the other's walls. They are interleaved at every scale, a hand's width apart, sharing every square meter of forty kilometers, and in four million years no member of either has ever encountered a member of the other, because to cross into the other's country is to go blind or to stop breathing.

Two nations in one room. Neither has ever suspected the room is occupied.

## The film, which is the only thing actually alive to the light

Everything above is husbandry. The rust varnish is the primary life, and its whole existence is a retreat.

It cannot live where mineral is being laid down, because it will be buried in a season. It can only live where stone is being taken away faster than it arrives — in the throats, in the dissolving hollows, in the two millimeters of translucency where the glare becomes usable. So it lives exclusively in the parts of the machine that are being demolished, and it moves *inward*, following the dissolution front as the throat deepens, a slow migration into the rock at the speed of the rock's removal, laying down behind it a faint dark stain that the circuit picks up and carries and spreads and loses.

Life here is not on the world. It is inside the world's subtraction. The field is white because mineral outruns flesh; the ochre is only ever visible in the wounds the water is making, and there are no wounds that are not being unmade, and this is why nothing here has ever had a good year.

Inside the crystalline fur, in the structured wetness between the needles, there are things that graze that stain and are themselves a tenth of a millimeter long, and to them there are no columns, no bowl, no cloud deck, no rim. There is a labyrinth of held water with no empty interval anywhere in it, and a squeeze eleven times a second, and it is the same everywhere, and it has been the same everywhere for as long as their kind has existed, which is a very long time.

## The one with an ear

In the Stop there is something in the shallow pool that no one has any business finding.

It is descended from grazers that were inside the district when the bores silted. Four million years of genuine silence is enough time to build anything, and what it built was the one organ that is a wound everywhere else in the bowl: a fluid-filled sac with a membrane across it, a reference chamber that stopped being a barometer and became a receiver. It has a thin shell and reduced cilia and it walks the pool margin by touch and taste, and it hears.

It hears the wall. At the edge of the Stop, where the lattice comes back, the noise arrives — not as blindness, not as a hand under the sternum, but as *sound*, an enormous structured sound with a shape and a direction, and it is the only thing in the world that has ever perceived the field from outside. Everything else lives inside the machine. This lives in the machine's one held breath and listens to it.

And in there, a call can be sent. There is a free channel. There is nothing occupying two hundred to four thousand cycles in a few hundred meters of collapsed lattice, and so it calls — a small dry tick, once every few seconds, into a silence that carries it perfectly well for two hundred meters and then hands it to a wall of cicadas a hundred and twenty decibels deep.

Nothing has ever answered. Nothing can hear it. Nothing outside can come in without going blind, and it cannot go out without being torn. It has been calling for something on the order of a hundred thousand years and it has never once been warned, and it is the only thing in forty kilometers that could have a name, because it is the only thing that has ever heard itself.

When you lie down in the Stop to hear your own blood, it comes toward the sound. It has been waiting for a second noise since before your lineage had a spine. It will reach you around dawn, and it will touch you, which is the first attempt at a warning ever made in this place, and you will not understand it, and by then you will already be deaf and unable to feel the corridors, and you will have become, briefly and fatally, a native.

## The passenger

I think there is one more, and I cannot show it to you.

A given atom completes the circuit in eleven thousand years. The loop dissolves at the crown and precipitates at the foot and wicks back up, and everything that goes into it goes around — the film's stain, the buried smears, the shells of the dead, the one dark band. There is a thing that lives in that circulation the way the tube-larvae live in the bore: not carried by it accidentally, but *of* it. A pattern of substitution in the carbonate lattice, an impurity that templates its own arrangement onto the mineral precipitating around it, so that what comes out of the foot is what went in at the crown, plus one.

Its body is a defect. Its metabolism is dissolution and precipitation. Its generation time is eleven thousand years, and since the field arrived it has had something like three hundred and sixty of them, and the grazer has had ten thousand, and the film has had none, and all three are tenants of the same standing wave.

It did not tune anything. It could not have; it came after. It is what a loop looks like when it has been running long enough that something can be *along* for the ride.

And every column, at the same depth, at the same age, carries one dark line out of four hundred thousand bands. I do not know what that is. It is tempting to say it is the passenger — one instant when all of it, everywhere, went into the stone at once, the only thing it has ever done that left a mark, one act in three hundred and sixty generations. It is more tempting to say it was a moment when the flesh outran the mineral, everywhere, for a season, and the world went briefly dark and living and was then buried and lifted and laid back down white.

The field has forgotten it absolutely. That is not a figure of speech. The record is perfect and the record has no reader, and the atoms of that one event are now evenly distributed through every column and every shell and every body in the bowl at a few parts per billion, a stain stirred into a bucket, present in the thing with the ear and in you and in the mist beads you walk through and reform behind you.

Whatever happened, everyone here is made of it, in equal proportion, and nothing here knows.

## What lives here

Not the grazers; they are how the stone gets carried sideways. Not the sheet; it is the underside of the pavement, awake. Not the crusts; they are the noise, wearing a body for a while. Not the film; it is the demolition, visible. Not the one in the Stop, which is a mistake that has lasted four million years and is the only thing here that is lonely, and does not have a word for it, and has a sound for it.

What lives here is a loop that has arrived at the shape that spends the arriving light as fast as it arrives, and holds that shape while everything runs through it, and has one heartbeat eleven thousand years long, and has done one thing, once, and cannot remember it.

Everything else is how it touches itself: rarely, blindly, at a junction, in a smear that means *not you*, which will be eaten to be understood, and buried, and dissolved, and lifted, and set back down in the shape it already has.
