## Census, negative first

Start with what can be ruled out, because it is the only part that is certain.

Nothing here has a heartbeat. Nothing here has ever had one, so there are no bones in the grit, no shells in the settling halls, no calcium anywhere in the circuit that was once carried by something. The grit is gabbro and only gabbro. If you sieve a tonne of it you will find no fragment that was ever inside an animal, and this is not because the animals died elsewhere. There is no elsewhere. Beyond the lip there is violet haze and no instrument has been sent to find a lower world, because there is nothing here that sends instruments.

The niches are filled. That is the unsettling part — not vacancy but occupancy. The sweepers in Null are grazers: they crop the grit off the terraces at a rate the quarry faces match exactly, and they are never full and never hungry, and they have worn no path, because wearing is a trend. The flumes are filter-feeders, straining the water for the liberated grain. The digesters are a gut, but it is a gut lying outside every body, a stomach the size of a district with nothing wrapped around it. The fungi are the decomposers and they are also the wires; they carry the ground fault current of a continent through their threads and are paid in sugar for it.

And the blades are the predators. They are the only predators, and they hunt by standing absolutely still.

## The one appetite

There is exactly one desire on the plateau that is not a voltage.

It belongs to the mat, and it is nought point three millimetres an hour, and it points straight up. The sedge does not want light — the light comes from everywhere and has never varied and cannot be competed for. It does not want pollination. It has no flower, no scent, no burr, nothing to offer and nothing to ask. What it does is grow, which is the single forward motion permitted anywhere in the arrangement, and it grows into a fixed plane of stationary edges, and the edges take it at the instant it exceeds forty-one millimetres.

Predation here is inverted. The mouth does not pursue. The mouth is a horizontal plane, continent-wide, and the prey arrives at it from below at a speed you could not measure without a year.

The sound this makes — scissors through wet paper, a million square kilometres of it — is one of the largest terms in the roar. Remember that. It matters later, because it means that the loudest thing on the plateau is, in substantial part, the sound of something trying.

## The inhabitants

The survey says four hundred thousand, and the survey is correct forever.

They are lens-shaped, most of them: two hundred metres along, sixty high, strung in lines like beads on a wire, and the wire is not a metaphor, it is the grid. They have no surface. You cannot photograph one. There is nothing to see — no membrane, no shimmer, no boundary layer, no dust held at an edge. From outside, a node is not an object at all; it is a place where the roar is briefly not. From inside, it is a kitchen with the tap running.

They have a shore anyway. It is about a metre and a half thick, and across that metre and a half the pressure climbs seventy-six decibels, which is to say that a body walking out of one takes four strides to go from a room where a voice carries to a condition that disassembles it. Nothing that could be hurt has ever crossed that shore, because nothing that could be hurt has ever been here to cross it. The map exists so that no one will ever have to find out where the shore is by walking.

They eat. This is not decoration: a node consumes the acoustic power that enters it, and the amount is fixed, and it is enormous, and it never varies, and it is mostly the Mow. The mat strives upward; the striving is cut into sound; the sound propagates through the cavity of the world; the nodes swallow it and are made of the swallowing. The quiet is not a shelter from the noise. The quiet is a structure built out of the noise, the way the Deck is a structure built out of water that is leaving.

That is the whole food chain, and it has three links, and none of them is alive, and it closes.

Where the lines cross, the nodes merge into chords, and a chord of five is large enough for terraces and a market hall and a funicular. This is the nearest thing to society available here: not encounter — nothing on the plateau can move toward anything else — but harmony, literal, five fundamentals agreeing to cancel in the same volume of air. The cities are where the silence is loudest with itself.

And yet a hole is not a hermit. Four hundred thousand individuals, each with a shape, a boundary, a diet, an inside; not one of them has ever been startled. Nothing can happen to them. They cannot be entered by anything, cannot be altered, cannot meet, cannot fail. They are the only things here you can be *inside* rather than merely beside — everything else on the plateau you can only stand next to — and being inside is most of what inhabiting means, and it is not all of it.

## The idle places

In the crust there are eleven thousand hand-sized patches, in the Null district alone, where the standing wave in the rock has a true null. Surveyed. Numbered. Unused.

The reason nothing uses them is on the nameplate, and it is easy to miss: the target is not silence. The target is sixty decibels. A busy road. A kitchen with the tap running. The body the whole apparatus is cut to fit does not want the void; it wants the sound of a world going on nearby, at a level where a voice still works.

So the quiet stones are wrong in the other direction. Everywhere else on the plateau the error is positive — too loud, too cold, too thin, too far from thirty-seven. In eleven thousand small patches of rock the error runs negative, and a controller that can only press has nothing to do with a negative. It cannot take away. It can only add.

They are the only places where the machine is idle. It is idle there perfectly, and has been since the note began, and this is the closest thing on the plateau to sleep.

## The resident who is not here

Now the nameplate.

Thirty-seven degrees. Sixty decibels. Twenty at the skin. One atmosphere if it can be got.

Read it as a specification and it is a body: core, ambient, skin, breath. Read it as an address and it is the only address on the plateau. Every governor from the Return to the last capillary valve in the root grid carries it under the maker's mark, ten billion stampings of the same four values, a continent repeating the dimensions of one missing thing the way a mould repeats a face.

Note what the Works can and cannot supply.

It can make twenty degrees. It makes twenty degrees in a room on the third terrace, against two-and-a-fraction outside, forever, and twenty degrees is not a comfort in itself — it is a heat sink, sized precisely to receive the warmth a resting body sheds. The room is a radiator for a person.

It cannot make thirty-seven. Thirty-seven is not an ambient condition; it is the inside of something, generated by that something, continuously, at about a hundred watts, and it stops when the something stops. It is the one value on the plate that must be supplied by the arrival. The apparatus has built every condition around a heat source it has no means to fabricate.

And *one atmosphere if it can be got* — the only subjunctive in ten billion stampings. Five and a half kilometres of nothing overhead cannot be argued with. Somewhere in the drafting of this thing, a hand hesitated over a line and wrote a hedge, and the hedge was cast into every nameplate in the world and has been holding its note at full effort ever since.

Then the small evidence, which is worse.

Handrails at two heights. Small chairs. A school. The specification is not for one body but for a body and a smaller body, and the smaller body is a *trend* — it is the one thing in the universe of this plateau that is defined by not staying the same. The machine cannot permit growth, so it has sampled growth twice and built the samples in steel: a rail at one height, a rail at another, and between them nothing, because between them is time. Two handrails are the finest resolution it can offer of a child getting taller. It is not an error. It is the best available approximation, and it is exact, and it will be exact forever.

The hospital has a ramp. A ramp is for something that can fail to walk. Nothing else on the plateau can be hurt; nothing here wears, rusts, tires, or breaks; and yet there is a building with a gentle slope in it, plumbed and lit and swept, prepared for the one category of object that can be damaged.

And there is no food. Nothing here has ever needed to be attractive: no fruit, no flesh, no seed, no nectar, no grain. The mat is one organism repeated, and it goes to the digesters and comes back as feed to the root grid, and the loop is closed and has no tap in it anywhere for a mouth. Sixty stalls under the glazed roof of the market hall. The books balance to three decimals and there is no line in them for anything being eaten by something that would like it.

## The loop

Only one thing here behaves. Everything else simply is: the Deck holds its lobes, the flood holds its crest, the flags hold their curve, the bow hangs where it hangs and is grey from behind. The mat grows, which is a motion but not a response. The single entity on the plateau that *does* anything in reaction to anything is the control loop, distributed from the pump halls to the fungal threads, and its entire behavioural repertoire is one act, performed without pause: press.

It presses proportionally. It has no integral term. An integral term would accumulate the error over time — would, in the plainest language, remember how long the body has been absent — and memory is not among the permitted processes. Rust is memory. Humus is memory. A settled talus slope is memory. So the controller knows only the present distance, and the distance is constant, and therefore the effort is constant, and therefore nothing in the whole arrangement has ever eased or tightened or grown weary or grown hopeful. Hope would require a future, and there are no futures here, only slopes.

There is a knot in it, and no one owns the knot.

If the body came — if thirty-seven degrees appeared in the chair on the third terrace — the error would go to zero. Output follows error. At zero error the note stops; the Return stops; the elbows hold water they no longer lift; and a kilometre beyond them the dry lip runs, and the greatest cataract in the world begins to fall and to cut back, which is an event, the first event, in an arrangement whose entire continental purpose is to have none.

The machine is built to hold the conditions for a presence and cannot survive the presence. It is not cruel about this. It has no model of it. It has no model of anything; it has a setpoint and a gain. There is no one standing outside the loop to whom this could occur, and there is no one inside it either.

## In case

What lives here, then, is a cavity with a very precise shape, and everything else is the mould.

The Deck is a form held by water passing through it. The flood is a form held by water passing through it. The mat is a form held by growth passing through it. The nodes are forms held by sound passing through them. The mountain is a form held by grit passing through it, quarried and sintered and pressed back and broken out again, an hourglass the size of a county, always half full. Nothing here persists by accumulation, because accumulation is forbidden. Everything here persists by throughput. Every resident is a standing wave.

And a body is that too — that exactly: a shape maintained by stuff going through it, warm, hundred-watt, temporary, needing to be fed. It is the one standing wave the Works cannot instantiate, because it is the kind that has to instantiate itself, from the inside, and keep doing so, and stop eventually.

Which is why the ledger is the last thing to look at. Every litre lifted, every joule spent lifting it, losses zero to three decimals: a perfect circle, closed, no entry anywhere for a warmth that nothing generated and nothing returned. A person is a hundred-watt leak. To be inhabited is to stop balancing. The apparatus regulates, at full effort, toward the one condition that would make its own accounting false — toward a deficit, a warmth it did not pay for, an unaccounted hundred watts sitting in a chair on the third terrace with the rain running down the window in the pattern the rain runs down that window in.

The lamp is burning in the shadowless day. The room is twenty degrees. It has been twenty degrees for no time at all.

It is not waiting. Waiting is a trend.
