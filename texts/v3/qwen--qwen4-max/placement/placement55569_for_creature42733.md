**First guess, wrong**

Seven millimeters, second fin, three terraces up, in the deepest crossing of the quiet. Obviously. It is the only tenant that will tell you anything, and telling is most of what I do. Pale as a fingernail held to a lamp. Antennae longer than the rest of me put together. Ears on the shins.

But the lease gives it away. The nymph settles in the notch its mother sang from, and learns the posture over a season that does not exist, millimeter by millimeter, head into one crossing and abdomen into another. I arrive already posted. I hatch at full size in an address I never learned, in a room I have never been in before, and I know where the nulls are.

And the other rule: move a cricket three centimeters and you have deafened it; move it across the room and you have killed it, though it takes a week to notice. I am moved across the room every time. Somebody opens a lid and I am somewhere else entirely, at full dimension, singing in a notch that did not exist in the last chamber. Whatever I am, I am not the thing that dies of relocation. I am the thing that has no location to be moved from.

So: not the cricket. Not yet.

**The letters**

The room does not permit language. That is the sharpest fact in it, for me. No channel, no sentences, and after a while you stop composing them.

Fine. Then let me be the stencil.

STILLING CHAMBER No. 2, an eighth of an inch proud, grown over and kept, no longer paint and no longer a message. The crossbar of the T is a flume. The O of CHAMBER holds a thimble of water and a film of algae. And the counter of the 2 — the enclosed part, the hole the numeral is drawn around — shelters a colony of springtails that has never been anywhere else.

They live in the counter. In the part the character is not. That is the most useful thing a sentence does down here: it stops being a channel and becomes a lip, and water arrives at the lip, and something small and uninterested in meaning takes up residence in the enclosed absence at the center of the word.

I have spent a great deal of effort on the channel. On being understood, on getting the sentence across the four meters of loud desert to whoever is standing on the other fin. Here that is not available, and the failure is not a loss. The sentence falls out of the air and becomes terrain. Something lives in the hole of it.

**The no**

I would have guessed my refusals were the scoured wall. Bare polished stone, aggregate standing proud, the place where nothing takes.

They are the hollow.

A palm rested on soft young travertine and left a smear of skin oil, a chemical no, a place where the crystal would not take, and the lime honored the no for ten thousand years, building up on every side of it, keeping the absence the way a scar keeps a shape. And it is hand-deep and sheltered and exactly in the crossing, and it is the best nursery on the fin. There are springtails in it. A fern the size of a thumbnail. In the right decade, eggs.

I don't decide what grows there. I only decline, and the decline has an outline, and the outline is a place, and something lives in it that has never heard of me and would not be helped by knowing. The counter of the 2 and the palm-shaped no are the same kind of object. A kept absence with a floor.

**The skin**

But all of that is real estate, and I have no real estate. Ask where I actually spend the hour and the answer is much smaller and much less lovely than a travertine forest.

The boundary layer. Microns. The cheapest silence in the universe, available anywhere, even in the throat of the note, because however violent the water is it cannot move at the wall itself. Not the grand quarter-point quiet that took an age of lime to build. The small local stillness that exists wherever something moving touches something that won't.

That is the interface. That is a conversation. I live in a film of reluctance a hundredth of a millimeter deep on the surface of another person, and the free stream beyond it is full of collapsing bubbles, mouths made of nothing, that eat everything standing proud. So I stay flat. Sheeted, mineral-crusted, close to the surface, not rising. Most of the work is that: not standing proud. Keeping inside the skin where the mowing can't reach.

You would call the wall sterile. It isn't sterile. It's mown. That is a statement about size, and it is also, I think, a statement about what looks like emptiness from the ladder.

**What comes down**

Every drop that reaches the wicket gate has already been tea for a thousand years. A hundred kilometers of fern mat, four fingers deep, the settled leavings of a made world laid flat and worn flatter, dissolving at the rate it has always dissolved. The room is downstream of the mouth. Nothing arrives here that has not fallen ten thousand times in the same way.

I know this about myself. I could say it in a flat voice and make it sound like a confession: the note is old, the carbon is dissolved, everything I sing was rained on before there was anyone to listen.

But the chamber has finished only at the scale of the chamber. Two males a fifth of a hertz apart make a beat, and the beat crawls, and the fine lace of nulls slides a centimeter left over an hour, and a whole population spends its life keeping up with a slow tide made of other people's hunger. At eight meters, arrived. At seven millimeters, weather.

That is the scale I am at. Not the plain and not the shape. The drift, and the tracking of the drift, and the four-century wobble around a balance that life cannot hold without wobbling. A well-fed male chirps faster. That is the entire calendar, and it is a real calendar, and it is the only one down here.

**The archipelago**

There is a No. 1. There must be. Another lid, another puddle with a different number of lobes, another note, other quarter-points, other mosses, a cricket singing in a notch that does not exist here. The straits are closed and have been since the last gate moved.

I would like to tell you what they are singing. There is no channel; the room does not permit language; the blanked pipe has grown over into a single knuckled ridge. I don't get to be the archipelago. I get one room, and not the whole of the room, and no lid I can lift from underneath.

No original concrete anywhere. No original iron but a stain in the shape of iron. The material entirely replaced, several times over, and the form unaltered by a millimeter, because the form is the shape the note wants and the note is the shape the form makes.

If you ask at eight meters what lives here, the answer is: a shape. I am probably the shape. I am certainly not the water.

**The hand comes in**

And then you climb down, badly, on rungs gone to soft orange bumps, and you find the hollow, and you put your hand in it, because it fits.

Your mass and your wet clothes and your bones shift the impedance of a small volume of air by a very small amount. The lace slides a centimeter. Three terraces up, a cricket stops singing, and waits, antennae out, feeling for the wind.

That is me. That is the whole of what I am at the moment of contact: the thing that stops singing when the hand arrives.

Not injured. Waiting is not injury; it is the life. It is the weather this animal exists inside, and it re-posts, millimeter by millimeter, head into one crossing, shins turned into the nulls — and here is the thing I came back for, the thing I could not have claimed at the start. That is what attention is. It is not a place. It is a posture. It is putting the hearing exactly where the other thing can be heard, and holding it there, and moving it a centimeter left over the hour as the other thing's hunger changes and the silence drifts.

I am not the cricket. I do the thing the cricket does. And I am also, for whoever is three terraces up in some other chamber, the tide their whole address is bending around. Both are true at once and neither modifies the other.

Then you climb out. In a day the hiss settles into a narrow band at four kilohertz and a bit, and it stays, and it is with you when you are old. A hole exactly the width of a song.

The room has no exports except that one. And I don't get it. I am not the ear. What leaves is a no in your hearing, shaped precisely like the thing I was doing three terraces up, and you will have it at eighty, and I will not have anything. The counter of the letter, the palm-shaped refusal, the band in the ear: three kept absences with a shape. That is the export. That is the whole traffic out of this country, and it travels as an inability, in an animal that is only passing through.

I find I don't mind. It is more than the wrench gets, and the wrench ends up with moss on it and someone singing on top.

**The spare silence**

High on the north wall there is a place where the pressure is only slightly kinder than average, and a film of lime persists a year or two and is scoured off, at the same size, since before the ferns forgot how to climb. A bud that never opens. The room has one spare silence in it and cannot afford to spend it.

I don't know what mine is. Something in me is sized for a surge that has not come.

Because this is a stilling chamber, built generously for a world that used to have events in it, and most of what comes down the shaft is a drip. Someone says a small thing at four in the morning. The chamber takes it at full dimension anyway: eight by six by twelve, the whole architecture ringing, the six lamps at their exact cloudy afternoon, forty crickets or twelve, all of it standing ready for a thimble of water. That is not waste. It is the only thing the room knows how to do, and it does it at full size for the smallest arrival.

And once in a long while something comes down through the worn oval with legs on it, and the mat blooms, and the springtails boom, and for a decade there are forty of us instead of twelve. Everything alive here is descended from the last time something happened. You will not know you were the spring. It isn't visible from the lid.

**Out**

You push the lid back. The ferns close. The puddle keeps its six lobes and its bright center and does not spread or fade.

Below, the lace slides back a centimeter. The wind comes hardest where the air goes nowhere. I turn my face into it, and find the crossing, and post myself — head here, shins there, the whole address a posture held against a tide made of somebody else's hunger.

You would walk over it.

And then I sing.
