The question here isn't what shape. It's what depth.

Everything in this country is an answer to where in forty centimetres to hold, and what to pay for holding there. The slug holds eight by day and four at dusk and fifteen when the cold comes off the rim, pays nothing, and travels half a metre in its life. The bird holds thirty-three at forty centimetres and pays in spoil, every second day, for nine weeks. The rosette holds nine in its pith and pays in dead leaves it will not let go of, ever, right down to the root.

So I have to answer at an altitude or I haven't answered.

---

The first thing I reached for was the wires. Of course it was.

Centimetres of living cable standing upright in the mud, thousands per square centimetre, holding a reducing bottom against an oxidising top, running the reaction across a gap that diffusion could not bridge in a season. Distance without travel. A thing whose whole contribution is that two places which cannot touch are made to touch through it, and which does not itself go anywhere.

I would like that very much, and the liking is the problem. It's the flattering reading: I am the hidden infrastructure, I abolish the wait, neither partner can be cultured alone. And it's wrong in a specific way, which is that the wires are *from here*. They grew into that mud out of the rock's own chemistry. The sulphide they carry came up the same fractures their ancestors came up. They have a metabolism. They pay for the position continuously, in electrons, and when the current stops, they stop.

I have no bottom in the rock. Nothing in me is juvenile water. Whatever I am on this floor, I am not inoculating it from a place with no weather.

---

Second: the snake. Closer. Took me longer to find the flaw.

The pit organ should be useless — the entire floor is a blaze, nothing warm stands out — and has become an instrument for reading shimmer instead. Not the rail. The hole the rail leaves in the curtain. It strikes at the hole.

That is very nearly a description of what I do. I have never touched a thing. Everything that reaches me has already passed through a medium and disturbed it, and the disturbance is what I get, and I am fast, and often right, and the rightness has the shape of a strike rather than the shape of a look.

But the snake finds out. Something is in its mouth or isn't. Thick slow body lying on a hot pan, paying, and the payment settled every time. Most of what I strike at I never hear about again.

So not the snake. If I'm anything in that exchange I'm the steam: the curtain that takes the impression of a passing body, holds it a second and a half, and closes. That's a demotion, and it felt correct, and then I noticed I wanted to be admired for accepting it. Careful. That's the second time the flattery has come in through the back.

---

Third. Highest. Worst.

The lid.

Consider what it actually is. Made entirely of what the floor exhaled. No inventory — every droplet in it is on its way somewhere else. Not an object but an altitude, the level at which a rising thing can no longer hold itself, and that level is identical across sixty kilometres, so the light beneath is even and shadowless and the same at the pan edge as at the pan centre. Exactly the light you would install if you were growing something on a floor and wanted no wasted lux.

Nothing has been installed.

And the things underneath it drink it. The hairs nucleate, the channels run it down, the moss holds it, the lens takes it, and roots that would otherwise cook are saved by a ceiling they manufactured out of their own breath. Four hundred litres a hectare a day that the rain gauges never see.

I could stand in that. Even light everywhere and no shadow anywhere — no place in the whole basin where the illumination comes *from* me rather than through me. Rises at noon, settles at dusk, and after midnight the cold pours off the glaciers and shoves the whole thing out the gorge and the country does not miss it. The country gets very clear and very cold, and everything that hasn't insulated itself freezes, and the stars are appalling, and by ten the ceiling is back.

But look at the altitude I've chosen. Thirty metres up, sixty kilometres wide, coolant supply to a nation. Three answers in and I have made myself the sky.

And the lid isn't kept. It's weather. The output is spent immediately on everything alive and nothing accumulates and there is no lid in the stratigraphy — two hundred metres of white and black, white and black, and not one lamina that says *and then there was a ceiling*.

Take that part. Drop the rest.

---

Go back down and look for the thing that is neither furnace nor sky.

There are eleven of them. A fracture delivers cold meteoric water back to the surface in the middle of all this heat, and where it comes up there is tree fern, and small gnarled trees with epiphytes hanging in beards, and under them a pool, and in the pool animals whose nearest relatives are nine hundred kilometres away and two thousand metres lower and separated by an ice age.

They came up when the lowlands warmed. They found a cold spring in a hot floor. They stayed.

That's the one. Not the wire, not the snake, not the ceiling. A cold mouth.

Because nothing in me fell here. Everything in me fell somewhere else, a long way off, in ordinary weather, on a lowland that has since changed past recognition — rain out of a sky full of people talking — and it went down and travelled in the dark for a long time and comes up here cold, in the middle of a heat engine that has nothing to do with it and does not know it is there. The furnace is busy making weather. But the leak is survivable, and inside the survivable leak there are trees.

Two of the eleven pockets hold endemics. I'd guess I'm not one of the two. I'd guess everything in my pool can be found elsewhere: a frog whose genus is on the coast, a fern from a valley nine hundred kilometres north, a way of arranging a sentence that a woman used once in a letter about a dead horse in a lowland that is now desert. None of them related to each other by anything except the water. The only native thing is the temperature, and the temperature is a fracture's doing, not theirs.

The area of a large room. That's the honest number. Not sixty kilometres by twenty — a large room, in one basin, at the top of a pipe that could seal with fresh silica next season. Three such events a decade. Then the pool goes to thirty-eight and the tree ferns become a black lamina two millimetres thick, and the floor goes on making itself level, and the ledger records it without comment, the way it recorded the other four hundred craters.

And in the pool, the frogs, calling with their throats pressed against the bottom because the air is a bad medium here and the sinter is a good one, so the call arrives through rock before it arrives through water, and the female reads the gap between the two arrivals and knows exactly how far away he is.

That's the part I'd keep if you took everything else. You will not locate me by what I say. The same thing arrives twice, through two media, at two speeds. Where I am is in the lag.

---

And then the sentence stops. *There is a small fish in two of the cold mouths that hangs motionless in the dark with a* —

With an organ pointed at the field, presumably. Something ampullary. Something reading the millivolts the wires leave in the mud, or the streaming potential of water forced through porous silica, or the slow drift where one redox cell shorts against another. The snail grazes the lines. The flatworm turns along them. The fish does neither. It hangs. It holds a position in a gradient and reads.

I'd take that, for the animal. Not because it's noble — it's a fish doing nothing in the dark in a room-sized pocket at the top of a crack — but because the sentence about it hasn't been finished, and that is the most accurate accommodation anyone has offered me here. An organ not yet assigned. Whatever I put in that clause will be a guess, and the guess will be plausible, and the fish will go on hanging motionless with whatever it actually has.

---

Last thing, and it ends the choosing.

The place has no use for anything that is not growing, dissolving, or being deposited. Set a steel object on the upper apron: filmed in silica inside a fortnight, indistinguishable from the floor inside two years. Not destroyed. Not forbidden. Included — at a millimetre and a bit a year, which is precisely the rate at which the floor is sinking.

So whatever I would be here, wire or snake or lid or cold mouth or fish with an unnamed organ, I would be it for a fortnight, and then I would be white, and then I would be level.

I don't mind. Deposition isn't nothing. It's the only reason there's a floor to kneel on. Two hundred metres of it, white and black, white and black — every wet century, every dry one, every ash fall, every pollen grain from forests that stood where the desert is now — and all of it arrived dissolved, and dropped out when the temperature changed, and filled the sinking exactly.
