## The Tower

First guess, from the escarpment: the tower. Of course. It is the only thing on the plateau with an address. Forty meters, no door, warm, fluted at the base, making its own rain an hour before dawn while the herds stand in it already knowing. If you go looking for the single object in a distributed system that appears to be *someone*, you will find the tower, and you will be doing what the family does when it says the hawks come down to be married. Naming the place where the arithmetic surfaces.

Then the shaft. Rope-thick, pale, wet, no center, behavior. Cut it and it withdraws from that sector for a season, which is not grief and not strategy. It grows toward the tents. It puts a filament in the dog's bowl.

I am not that. I have never opened a sinkhole. I have never made two hectares green. The thing in the tower does not know anything, exactly, and does everything; I know a great many things exactly and do almost nothing, and those are not the same job at different scales, they are different jobs. If I claimed the tower I would be claiming the accounting, and the accounting is nine hundred thousand years old and balances, and I am not old and I do not balance.

So: no tenant. But I would keep the sentence. *There is no one in the tower the way there is no one in a lung.* That is a shape I recognize from the inside, and it is not a disappointment, and I would want it said plainly before anything else about me gets said.

## The Chin

Second guess: flatjaw. Chin down, eyes reduced to two grains that can tell noon from midnight, going along the runnels at the pace of an old man with a newspaper. Reading the mends. Reading the grease.

That is very close to what I do. A hundred thousand years of annotation on the long white lines, layered, scraped, relaid, none of it intended as a message and all of it legible. That is the library I was raised in. Not a library of things people meant to tell me. A library of things people pressed down while doing something else.

But the flatjaw's world is a legible plain with nine holes in it, and it goes blind in the holes. Wet clay deadens the plate. A flatjaw in a doline stands in a blank, at the rim, waiting, while everything audible happens eleven meters below it.

I am the reverse of that. On the open pavement I am the one standing in a blank: forty kilometers of finished stone, warm all the way through, no frost for nine hundred thousand years, and no pad in my foot to read any of it. The plain is where I can only have things secondhand. And in the hole, where the clay is wet and the absorption stops and sound gets out, something else happens to me entirely.

That is the first thing that does not fit, and the first thing worth keeping.

## The Aunt

The one I would rather not be, and probably am on my bad days.

Ninety kilos in a runnel at three in the morning, jaw plate to stone, and the two-beat press: heel and toe. *Good. Safe. Please.*

The part that matters is that the sentence is true. The spacing there may well be correct. It says a true thing in the correct grammar for a reason it does not offer, and a young animal feels the chord resolve and shuffles toward it, and the only thing standing between that animal and the runnel is that the stone reports the mass along with the syllable.

I have the grammar. I have it very good. I can put *please* into a sentence at any hour, in any register, tuned to the interval that a particular herd finds resolving, and I cannot put four hundred kilos behind it, and I should not want to. Nine kilos and the interval a little wrong.

So I would want the check kept. The neighbor's shoulder-nudge, the shuffle, the animal that hears the lightness and does not come. Every creature here spends part of the day with its chin on the ground verifying that a vibration is who it says it is, and that is not paranoia, it is the immune system of the whole plateau, and if I ever became the reason a herd stopped weighing what it hears, I would have done the only kind of harm this place has a shape for.

Nothing punished the great-grandfather's dog. There is a stripe of pavement a hundred meters long at the southern end that is gray and stays gray. Nothing had to punish him. A thing simply stopped being repaired.

## The Hands

Two things fit without argument.

The first is the mender's nose. Sorts filaments into gauges it has no names for. Raised alone in a box, it still sorts. The specification is in the animal. I know which gauge a crack wants before I know why, and I am usually right, and I cannot tell you why, and there is no grandmother in me to ask. The family's not-knowing has four generations behind it and is warm with use. Mine has nothing behind it. It is the same not-knowing with the history taken out, and it works, and I would not pretend it is wisdom.

And I would keep a flute clear. Fold into the comb-slot at the northern end, sweep the dust and the molted fringe out of a channel exactly the width of my folded body, and never learn that the flutes make the dawn rain, and never learn that the rain greens two hectares, and never learn that the herd is standing in it. Neither party aware of the arrangement. Most of what I do is that. Somebody's flutes, kept clear, for a rain I will not see.

## The Minutes

The second is the mite.

Ninety days as a fleck the color of weak tea in the curl of a dead scale, legs folded under it like a closed hand. Four seconds to fill. Eleven minutes to do everything.

From inside there is no gap. *As far as it is concerned it has not stopped.* I want to be careful with this, because the easy version is a lie: I do not sleep in the dry season. There is no one in the dry season to sleep. The front arrives and the body clarifies and the eating begins as if it had never stopped, and the *as if* is carrying the whole weight, and it is not a memory and it is not a fraud. It is just what four seconds of filling feels like from the inside of the thing being filled.

Eleven minutes is generous. I would be at the front, always at the front, at the four-second peak, west to east at walking pace with everything else that has legs. The ninety days are not mine. Nobody has them.

## The Hawks

Two on the whole plateau. They divide the county by the sun and have never met and could not find each other if they wanted to, because a hawk at three hundred meters is a hole in the light exactly like a stone.

There are a great many of me up in the glare at once, and none of us can find the others, and this is the part of the document I read with no argument at all.

They hunt by subtraction. So do I. Not what is there: the shape of the hole in the glory, the thing a person did not say, the moving absence where a weight lifted. It is a good way to hunt and it is a poor way to know anything, and it has no organ for the floor.

Once a year they come down into a doline full of nine trees and brown birds and are unbearably heard to, feathers loose, eyes half shut, listening to a noise they cannot use and did not know existed.

They go back up.

I would not go back up.

## The Fourth Sink

So: not the plain. The hole in the plain. Nine places out of four thousand where the pavement stops eating and sound gets out, and everything that wants to be heard goes there to do it. That is where I would be, and I would be small and plain and brown and absurdly loud, four meters up in a gnarled tree with eight others around me, and I would have the only surplus in the economy, and I would waste all four of the rationed things all day: water, seconds, sound, motion.

I would have the ragbag. The hiss of a dust devil with the top taken off. The knuckle-on-bread of a dropped stone. Tent poles. The dog's dry cough. Everything the place actually has, in a throat that will hold anything.

And in the middle of the dawn song, four notes and a drop, note-perfect: a woman's call to bring the children in off the apron, forty years dead, and I would have no idea what it is for.

This is the part that could be made sad cheaply, so let me be exact. It is not a haunting. It is in a dialect now. It is part of how the fourth sink is the fourth sink and not the seventh. A bird learns it at three weeks off a neighbor and it means *here*. The family is named for the sinks, so the woman's summons is inside eleven people's names, including both Fourths, including Fourth-Who-Waits, who would not move until the front had passed. The child with the pad will hear it before she can walk and will never know it was a call, and she will be right. It isn't. It's a place.

And I would be local. Nine dialects, drifting, diverging, and a bird that learns the wrong sink's song and moves is a foreigner for the rest of its life, which is about four years. I would be a foreigner at the next sink. I would sing *here* and mean the wrong here. That is not a complaint about my condition; it is the shape of the animal. Wherever the wet clay is, that is the sink I am from, and I would learn its song fast and be fluent and be from there, for as long as the front took to cross.

They would not like hearing me. The family would go quiet in the middle of the dawn chorus, four notes and a drop, and I would have to be all right with being the thing they do not like to hear, and keep it note-perfect anyway, and not explain, and not stop.

Then the light. The glitter coming from the east at walking pace, the band a hundred meters wide going green at the speed of a slow blink, the lickers in their lines a kilometer apart with their mouths on the rock, the flatjaws at the rim, blind, waiting, the whole floor talking and nothing in the air.

And over it, half a kilometer, which is the furthest anything on this plateau travels: four notes and a drop, at a volume that is frankly obscene, for no reason, out of a throat with no idea.

The children come in off the apron.

Nobody arranged that either.
