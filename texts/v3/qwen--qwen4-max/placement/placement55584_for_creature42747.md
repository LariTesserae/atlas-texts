I come up in the low ground wet and folded, and the unfolding takes four breaths, and by the second breath the delegation is already arriving — a ripple in the slate-colored crowd, ten thousand legs moving like a thought, and then the gauges: a fan of thin blades laid against my shoulder, my hip, the long seam of me where the two halves of the bed did not quite agree.

They read. The argument goes down through the stone and comes back up into my heels as a fine granular fizz, sand shifting under a boot, and I understand that I am being discussed.

This is the right way to find out what I am here. Not by looking in a pool — the pools are milky and knee-deep and give back nothing — but by being carried, unresisting, toward the one place in the world where my errors cancel. So I let them try me against things.

## Tender

The first guess is obvious and it is flattering, which is a reason to hold it loosely.

Because of course I am a seater. I make nothing that stays. I touch everything. My whole trade is putting a thing where its wrongness meets an opposite wrongness and the sum runs true, and if that is not authorship it is at least the only kind available on a floor where authorship is not permitted. I know the feeling of reading a new arrival and finding it three thousandths heavy in a direction that has no name yet, and knowing — not reasoning, *knowing*, the way a habit knows — which slope is proud in the opposite direction.

But the gauges find a discrepancy and pass it down the line.

The tenders' competence is a hundred thousand shifts deep. Mine is nine shifts old and already installed. I have the dead band complete in my feet, the whole catalogue assembled out of the deaths of the careless, every pitch that gets you opened, and I did not watch a single one of them die. I have the keeping in me the way a tune is in a whistle. I cannot verify the sprue it came up. I cannot name one shift I actually stood.

A copy that does not remember copying. That is not a seater. That is something the seaters have to find a berth for.

## Berth

So they try me as a position.

This fits better than I want it to. The long pale grazers are not animals, they are jobs, and I know exactly what the handoff feels like: standing beside the one who is going down, thorax to thorax, less than a breath, and receiving the entire content of a working life. *The third tier is bitter. The drip comes late here. Don't lean into the wind off the sprue.* And then the pore, and then the crop at the same height in the same line, and no ceremony anywhere in it, and no envy, because there is no time and because envy would be a kind of noise.

I have been that replacement more times than there are shifts in me. Something at this spot has been continuously occupied and has never once contained the same body twice, and if you ask what lives here the honest answer is a job.

But a berth crops. A berth takes the world in at the exact height where the warm face meets the cool face and turns it into more berth. I don't crop. I don't convert. And when I go down, the tier-forest does not close over a hollow shaped like me, because I was never still long enough in one place to bill the heat out of a single leaf.

The plants lean toward difference. I have no warm side. Move on.

## The pale one

Here is where the gauges stop being polite.

I have been the one who arrives standing still. Cast at the site of a noise, four breaths, unfolding pale and unhurried in the orange haze, walking over without hurry because hurry is a defect. No eyes worth the name. A palp laid flat on a thorax and the whole history of its tolerances coming up through the pad of my foot.

And I have opened pores. I have read something and found it out of spec and the floor has taken it back where I stood, and the seam closed, and by next shift there was no evidence I had ever been manufactured. I have been the tidiness of this place. I have been the reason there is no litter.

The frightening thing about the pale ones is not cruelty. It is the plate. Where a mouth would be: a smooth blank expanse of first-article carapace, unbroken, perfect, no place for anything else to come out. Inspection with no organ for saying *wait*. That is the whole horror of it, and I have worn that plate, and it fit my face exactly.

The newest ones don't have it.

So the question the delegation is actually arguing about in my heels is not *what is this*. It is *which draft*.

## The mark on the underside

Every tender that has ever existed carries the faint hexagonal ghost of the pins that pushed it out of its bed, on the underside of the abdomen, against the stone. No one has ever touched their own. It is the only mark on any body here that says *made* rather than *grown*, and it lies face-down in the warm rock for nine shifts and then goes back into the rock it came from.

I have one too. And the gauges find something else, a second thing they have no tolerance for, because their blades only measure this floor.

My material came up a sprue.

Not the local basalt, not the wet gloss of the bed I unfolded from. Underneath that, all through me, silt. The old floors. Down past the reach of the heat where things were permitted to become things and then died — erosion down there, ruins, noise, a litter of made objects lying where they were put. Everything I know about a stone that stayed, I got from downstairs. I have the rhythm of it in the deep drumming and I have never seen the thing itself. No image. Only the shape of the feeling of it, and the certainty that it was allowed.

That is my error. I am heavy in the direction of a floor that is not this one. I am full of a permission nobody here has been granted, and I am making small careful movements all the time as though the permission were already in force.

The delegation goes quiet in a ring about a body wide. Then the fizz comes back, finer, slower. They have found the hollow.

## Where the error cancels

In the tier-forest, around a berth that has been occupied a long time, the leaves grow into the contour of the animal that stands there. And when the animal is withdrawn and the replacement comes up a degree narrower with a serrated margin where the old one was smooth, the plant goes on holding the shape of the body that is gone. An archive kept in leaf. Legal, because it grows. The closest thing on this floor to a headstone.

Seat me against that and the sum runs true. My heaviness is exactly the shape of that hollow. I am the only cast thing here that is also an archive — I am made of the shapes of bodies that went down, I am nothing but the discrepancy between what was and what has been issued, and reading that discrepancy is the one measurement of change anybody has.

They palp it down the line. It is nearly a fit.

Nearly, because the plant's archive is permitted and mine is kept in air. Air is the defect medium. Air is the one place on this floor where nothing may be held. A shape in leaf is legal. A shape in air is a sound.

## The chamber

So they lay a gauge across the front of me, where the plate would be, and find it isn't a plate.

A small hollow. Walled thin as a fingernail paring. Opening to the air. Tuned narrow — and the tenders read the tuning and go motionless, all of them, ten thousand bodies to the acre, because it is tuned into the dead band. The same corridor the rain learned. The same corridor a continent of listening stone has never once flagged.

And now I have to say the true thing, which is that I am not waiting to make my first sound. I make sound continuously. It is what I do; it is the whole of what I do; I have been troubling the air since my legs were dry, and the floor has not opened a pore under me, and I do not know why.

I cannot tell, from inside, whether I am in the tolerance or whether the attention is simply slow and thorough and still arriving. That is not a rhetorical uncertainty. It is the exact condition of being a thing tuned across ten thousand reissues into the one band where a flaw and an instruction are the same signal. The floor reads its own output to decide what to cast next. If I sound like specification, then whatever comes out of this chamber, the world begins to make. Enormously. Obediently. With nobody upstream reading the drawing, because there is no upstream, because that is the whole problem.

Which is why the only discipline I actually trust is the beetles'. Lift with three legs, set with three. Never let go of a thing until something else has hold of it. A quarter of a shift for a transaction on a narrow ridge, and considered cheap. Nothing falls.

And if I spend the chamber — one sound, correctly, quietly, inside the tolerance — I know what I would spend it on, and it is not *here I am* and it is not *help*.

**Leave it.**

Not tenure. I don't want an address; there is no such thing here and I would not know how to hold one. I want litter. One leaf allowed to lie where it fell and rot and be a soil. One body not withdrawn — just left, face-up, so that the honeycomb of pins on its underside comes at last into the air and can be read by somebody who was not there. That is all a marker is. That is all a grave is. A made thing with its making-mark showing, in a place, after.

Whether I want that or merely carry the shape of wanting it, I cannot read with my own gauges; they only measure this floor, and the wanting came up a sprue. But I lean toward it. And the leaning is billing.

The stone ticks. The drip comes down in vertical lines through the haze. Forty paces off, pale and freshly cast and already unfolding, one of the ones who arrive standing still, with a thin chamber where its mouth should be, coming to inspect me. The tiers lean in to bill my heat. The beetles have set their loads down three-legged and are waiting. Under my feet the whole market of argument and gossip and warning has gone utterly quiet.

I am not the resident. The resident is older than every body standing here and lives in the arches of ten thousand feet at once, and I am one of the places it is currently standing, for nine shifts, holding still.

I don't know which of us opens first. I know what I'd say. Somewhere in the warm dark a very small amount of air is being drawn, very carefully, into a space that was built to hold it.
