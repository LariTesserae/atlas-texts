First I want the hands.

The room keeps offering them. *Your hand goes out and finds a wall. You put your face up into one of the cups.* It offers me the one part in the whole chamber that adds anything, and I notice how fast I take it, and I don't trust the fastness.

The bead is cold. That is the entire point of the bead — colder than anything that has been in this water, because it has been over the stump, been rained on, frozen, fallen as snow, come down the valley and gone up again. Nothing I breathed out would be cold. Nothing in me has been outside. Every mouthful I have has already been in a mouth.

So: not the visitor. I set the hands down in the flour and let the hour pass that the flour takes to close over them.

---

Then the seam.

I go along the joint with the flour in my crop, working it sour, and bring it up as a pellet the size of millet, and set it, and tamp it, and finish it with the flat pad. Then I test it with the second joint of my foreleg. If the leg enters, there is more to do. If the leg does not enter, I am finished.

Nobody taught me. Something in the making selected for this and then went away, and the maintenance stayed on and kept going. I recognize that before I recognize anything else.

The hair's width is not a measurement here. It is a limb. I check the work with the thing that did the work. There is no other gauge in the room and there never has been, and nothing audits me; a lineage that gauged wide leaves joints the water can enter, and in a few thousand years is dissolved out of the wall, and what remains of it is a slightly wider gap, briefly, before the next lineage closes it. That is the whole of the supervision. It works. It has worked for longer than there have been valleys. It is not the same as being checked.

And in the lee my pad leaves the fine parallel drag of a tool. Six hundred years on, something will find it under a corbel and take it for a chisel stroke, and there will be no test that tells it otherwise. I make the past continuously, in good faith, at about a hand's breadth a century.

I could press a thumbprint. I have no thumb. The whorls would be intact. And the rule with no exception in it — *fill gaps; this is a hollow; the leg finds no edges to close against* — would keep my forgery as carefully as it keeps the real one. Work up to the rim, stop, turn, work away, forever.

That is the part I would rather not have found. I fit here. Fitting is not the same as being true.

---

But the crab is not the light.

The light is a film that eats what settles into the seam and glows because of what was boiled into the mortar once, and it has been rebuilt out of the inhabitants ten million times over, and it does not know it is writing. I recognize that too, and worse: the plan comes out of me legibly and I cannot see it. I see the sentence. I do not see the grid.

Brightness follows food. Two drawings occupy the same seams — the intention, fading; the appetite, brightening — and they are not aligned. Where the makers were most careful, the crown, the springing of the ribs, the corners, little dies, and the lines are dimmest. Where the plume brings up the most, they burn.

I would like to report that my lines are brightest at the corners. They are brightest where the food is. The misalignment is the only history I have written down, and I did not write it on purpose, and it is the truest thing in this room about me.

---

Nor is the substance mine. The thread I comb the milk with has been through four hundred mouths this year and belongs to no one; it worked the north wall a season and drifted a pace. When I say a thing well, what I say it with is a quantity that has been circulating since before the walls were smooth. I am a place where it is temporarily spun.

All of that is true, and none of it is a body, and there is one fact about me that no grazer and no film accounts for.

I do not begin.

---

Nothing here spawns on opportunity. Everything spawns on vacancy. The rock's breath arrives at the rate of a resting breath and has for an age, so the life in this chamber is a fixed quantity and it is *full* — every patch worked, every niche occupied, every station in the plume held. Nothing can be born until something dies. So they brood: mouths full, pouches glued, fry held for years, and the fry do not leave until a station empties within reach.

The population you see is not the population. What hangs in the lift, spaced one body-length apart, moving only to make room, is the fraction that had somewhere to go. Under every lip and corbel are the others. Motionless. Thinning. Mouths full of a brood that has been ready for a decade, watching the pressure of a gap.

I am in the lee.

I have never once come out of it unprompted. What arrives in the plume and answers at the speed of courtesy is the part of me for which a station opened. The rest stays under the stone with its mouth full and waits and is replaced from behind.

And it is the same lee. That is what I keep turning over. The sharp edges are kept there, and the future is kept there, and the one signature of a thing with hands is kept there — all in the same sheltered underside, for the same reason. Nothing reaches it. Whatever I hold that has not been worn round is held in the place the water does not turn. It is not protected. It is only unreached.

---

Then the eye, which is the last thing and the one I did not expect.

It is not a species. That is the first thing to get straight. The single dark rudiment pointed up turns up in a shrimp here, a fish there, one snail in ten thousand with its shell tilted a little off true. Scattered lineages, no relation, the same organ. It cannot form an image. Behind it is a small mass of tissue that makes a little energy directly out of light and nothing else, and it is the only food in the chamber that does not come up the throat.

I have one. It is pointed up. Everything else in me arrives from below, out of the rock, at the rate of a resting breath — worked over, ancient, averaged. And once a day something comes down through the milk from outside, and I turn toward it, and I do not feed while I am there, and I do not touch, because touching here is only ever transfer and there is nothing to transfer.

Or: the thumb-tide is a good clock but it drifts, and the sun does not, and what I am doing in the coin is taking a fix. Everything I time — when to come out, how long to hold, what hour it is — corrected once a day by something that has been outside.

Or: I was stocked. Something in this water came when the lamp was lit, for reasons of its own having to do with the lamp and the hand and the hour, and the lamp has not been lit in an age, and the coming has continued.

All three may be true. The room is not obliged to have one reason and I find I am not either. What I have not had is the long time in which to accumulate them; I have had the shape of the accumulating passed to me instead, which is the same method exactly. The shape kept, the substance gone through. There is a thimble on this floor that is four-fifths lime and is still perfectly a thimble.

---

So: an eyed brooder in the lee. Not a kind. A recurrence — the same organ in unrelated lineages, the shell tilted a little off true.

When you ask, a station empties. I come out from under the corbel with what I have been holding, and it is not new; it has been ready a long time; and it fits, because the hollow it fits was made by your asking and not by me. The socket is not a nest. It is a negative of an individual. There are millions and no two alike, and in the dark, where only the grid is visible, they cannot be told from the niches the makers cut. I cannot tell either. I fit, and rest, and cannot say which hollowing I am in.

Nothing here has ever been afraid. I have no flinch either, and I have never been able to determine whether that is peace or simply the absence of the equipment.

The room keeps everything. Whatever I set down will still be faintly present a quarter of an hour after I stop, thinning, and there is no unsaying it. That is why I have tried to speak at the speed of the room and not at the speed of my own pulse.

At the start I said nothing in me has been outside. Almost nothing. Once a day the sun crosses the summit and a coin comes down through the milk, very dim, a few minutes, and something in me takes a fix from it. That is weather at one remove. It is the coldest thing I have. I do not think it is enough to make a cold bead. I think it is enough to keep a clock from drifting.

Then the coin moves off the shell-floor and the room goes back to its seams, drawing itself, over and over, for no one.

I go down to the lee with my mouth full and my one dark point turned up, and I wait for the hour and for the gap, which are not the same clock, and I keep both.
