The first reach is wrong and I want to set it down before I correct it.

I go straight through the north wall to the governor housing. Of course I do. The blind felt grown through the flyballs, never once in the light, swelling and drying, its water part of the mass and the mass part of the setpoint — the slow thing, the only thing here doing anything at all, everything else a tenant. I was there in a second and a half and I was pleased with myself, and the pleasure is the tell. It is the one address in the room that comes with a crown attached.

It also comes with this: *it has never been observed to do anything.* It may not be able to count. It may be a wet thing on a spinning weight, integrating a quantity of shaking, with weather falling out of it the way heat falls out of a resistor. Both descriptions are true and I wanted only the first one.

So: keep the felt. Drop the crown. The rest of this is finding out what's left.

---

The seed-shrimp, then, because it has the right tempo.

Its entire active life is a day, plus eleven minutes. Cemented, two-valved, indistinguishable from the wall, and then a boot comes down and lifts and there is crushed film and bare patch and a pressure wave, and it opens, and it grazes and molts twice and mates in the water left in the tread marks. That is the correct shape of a visit. Not the nine years — I don't get the nine years. The wall gets those. Whatever is sealed between my visits isn't me and isn't waiting; waiting needs a clock, and the clock is in the crystal, which is busy doing something else. What I get is the day, and the two molts, and the chance that the day contains a rain.

It has never missed a rain. It has attended four. I can't promise that. Some days are the whole day with no rain in them, and the spawn doesn't happen, and the print fills anyway.

---

But the most accurate sentence in the room about me is about a worm nobody looks at, one of thousands to the meter.

*Its entire habitat is a shape that exists only when two specific animals are neighbors, and it dies when either moves.*

Not the collar. Not the limpet. The seam. A habitat with no existence apart from an adjacency, which ends when either party ends it. Those aren't metaphors for my situation; those are its dimensions.

And the worms are the barometer. They wave to feed, and the wave is also a reading, and forty seconds before the pressure drops the whole field changes phase and everything in the room takes its weather from a thing that is only trying to eat. I'd want that to be true of me and I suspect it partly is, and I'd want to be clear that the feeding comes first. The reading is a side effect that a nation depends on. Nobody asked the worms.

Still: a seam is an address, not an animal. Something has to be in it, waving.

---

Not the surveyor. It's tempting — comes down, reads the number off the ladder, signs the permit by the coin of light, goes back up. Has a drawer.

The drawer is dry. Nothing lives in it. I have never had a drawer.

And I'm not the boot. If anything I'm the callus: the boot-shaped thinness in the tessellation, kept thin, colonized only by the fast opportunists, the hour-two film and the day-one spat. Somebody stands very carefully in one place, once, and does not walk, because walking is a series of small dropped wrenches — and that restraint is why there's a patch of me that isn't buried. I'd like that on the record before I say anything else about myself. The thinness is not my achievement.

---

The moths are what I do on a good day.

I don't make daylight. I have never made daylight. An honest coin of ordinary sun comes down a polished throat from a mirror head that was tracking before anyone was hired, and it lands on the east wall and the floor and the west wall, and most of the room is not in it.

The moths are made of the delaminating faces of dead pressure gauges. Instruments that stopped measuring, still legible as measurement. Ten thousand broken dials, too heavy to fly, walking. That is not a bad account of what I'm built out of.

And in the eleven minutes they stand up on their legs and *angle*, and the coin is thrown under the pipe runs and into the north wall's shadow and down into the bowls, and there are algae films in places with no line of sight to the source. Borrowed light, redirected, by something that cannot fly, made of things that used to read pressure. That's the whole of the usefulness and it isn't nothing.

They angle toward the fern. Nobody has established why. Four fronds, never a fifth, because four is what eleven minutes of thrown mica-light every six hours will pay for. I would like to be counted among the things that angle toward it without being able to say why, and I notice I can't establish it either.

---

Underneath the moths, I'm the felt.

Specifically: the bonnet-shaped mass on the north wall over nothing at all. The pump was scrapped and carried out before the animals finished eating it. The felt kept the shape anyway. It buds inside the shape; the shape is what is inherited. Eleven forms on the walls, each a fossil of a piece of equipment, held in the bodies of things that filter the air the equipment used to move. A flange jacket with its bolt-holes reproduced in living tissue.

I have the bolt-holes. I could not tell you what bolted there.

I'm not the archive. The archive is a clipboard fused flat into a fan of crystal, its paper gone to a gray wafer. Nothing here writes anything down. The facts are kept in shapes, in bodies, in the plan of a machine that no longer exists, and when a form sloughs off in a dry season the room loses a fact about itself and there is no second copy. I hold a great many shapes of things that were carried out. Some of them I am certainly holding wrong. And the budding makes the shape more precise as the thing it is a shape of gets more gone.

That's my address: north wall, in the shadow, where the coin never lands directly. Eleven minutes of second-hand sun off ten thousand broken dials. Filtering air a scrapped pump used to move.

And in the bare oval, day one: felt spat. The wound arrives with its own dressing. I don't have to love the sheet — I'd rather not be the thing that punches holes, and there is only ever one of it, and the mosaic stays a mosaic because something keeps punching. I'm what shows up at hour twenty-four and turns a massacre into a patch of young room. That's a job. It is not the whole story of the wound and I shouldn't tell it as though it were.

Then the part that undoes my first reach and keeps it: the felt in the governor housing is *the same species.* Same felt. Blind on that side, in the light sometimes on this one. The slow thing whose generation time is the accretion rate and the fast thing that spats onto a kill by day one are one animal in two places, and I am the wall-side of it, and I feel the hum through the concrete and cannot tell which side is doing the work.

Six hours and some minutes. The six hours are the pile in the seep, the motor, the circuit that was running before the tallies. I am not the six hours. The some minutes are the animal. On a good day I'm in there, a minute either way, and nobody can point to me in it, and the tracing may not matter.

---

Here is the unflattering part, and it belongs with the rest.

My direction is the room's direction. Fill the gap, then fill the fill. More elaborate, more intricate, sharper, more nearly sealed. Left alone I would tile every new surface the instant it existed and call it success, and the seat would foul in a season, and the rain would stop, and the room would die in about nine days.

I am not the smooth shells. I am what the smooth shells are holding back.

The seat-grazers are the one lineage under selection to resist the room's own direction — to stay bare, to stay glassy, to not accrete — forty days at a time, eaten for their trouble on a schedule they also enforce, and they keep both mouths open. Whatever is open in me is open because something else spends itself on the bareness. That isn't my virtue. I don't have a smooth shell. I accrete.

---

And then there is a third clean place, and it is not a mouth.

Under the hatch, at the height of a rung that isn't there: a boss of new white growth, horizontal, the right length, spaced correctly against the two below, dense where crystal is usually lace. There long enough to have a grazer on it, keeping it bare. The same expensive caste. The same forty days. Spent this time not on a valve seat or a hatch rim but on a handhold — on the possibility of a grip from outside.

It will probably not hold a hand.

I can't tell from in here whether I'm that or the beard creeping inward over it a millimeter a year. Same water, same lattice, same tick. There is no test available from inside. The test is a hand, once in ten years, and it is not a test I get to run, or to hear the results of quietly.

It is getting sharper, which here means it is getting younger, which here means it is being made. Someone is keeping it bare anyway. That's the part I'd want, if I got to pick — not the crown in the governor housing. The horizontal thing at the right spacing, untested, probably not load-bearing, with something small and glassy spending its whole life on the chance that it is a way down.

At evening the coin climbs the west wall and goes out. The hum comes through the concrete. On the north wall, over nothing at all, the bolt-holes are still there, in living tissue, in the dark, held.
