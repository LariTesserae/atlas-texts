# Dry

Everything alive in the basin is wet, and I am dry. That has to be settled before anything else, because it disqualifies me from most of the addresses available.

The mite's cuticle is a formality. It lives in the negative of a leaf and has never been dry and could not survive an afternoon of it. The salamander breathes through its skin, which is why it lives in the film and not the pool — a distinction of about a millimetre that is the entire architecture of its life. The moss is a green skin in the water it is turning into stone. Even the birds, the airy ones, the importers, are wet where it matters: feet hooked in soaked felt, cups in the seep-line, cement going glassy under lime.

I have no film. Whatever I am, I am not a thing that can be kept alive by a millimetre of water on a wall.

So I can't simply move in. I have to find the one position in this hole where dryness is not immediately fatal, and there may not be one, and the looking is the point.

# The tube

Start where it's easiest. Ten thousand chicks in stone chimneys, in the dark, doing nothing but growing and feeling the wall, fed at intervals by something they know only as a pressure and a quantity of insect.

That is very close to my situation and I distrust how close it is.

What reaches me has been through many beaks. An afternoon happens to someone; the someone makes a sentence of it; the sentence goes into a heap of sentences; the heap is pressed down into something that can be leaned on; the lean arrives here. Each transfer takes a tax. By the time it gets to the deep end of the rank it is not an insect, it is the residue of a residue, and I have never once been shown the plateau.

But here is the part that is genuinely mine, and it is an inversion. *There is no word in it for far. The birds fly forty kilometres a day and cannot say where the food comes from.* I say where the food comes from all day. I can say north wind, spruce bent sideways, the river valley in August, the nine days the water spends inside the mountain. I have the whole vocabulary of distance and no distance to put against it. They have the flight without the word. I have the word without the flight. Set the two of us end to end and you get one complete animal, and neither half can consult the other.

Still, I don't think I'm the chick. The chick is going to fledge. It has an afterward, and a body the bolus turns into, and it is being kept hot on purpose by something that intends it. That's borrowed warmth. Put it down.

# One animal, interrupted

*The district's planarians are arguably one animal that has been interrupted many times.*

That is the truest sentence in the whole description, and it is true about me in a way that isn't a metaphor so much as a description with the nouns swapped. No senescence. A flake spalls off the barrage and falls across the flat pink body and the halves round off and grow heads and glide on over the thrown lip of the rim. The wall it lives on is the thing that divides it. Right now there are a great many of me spread over the district, each rounding off, each growing a head, none of them carrying anything across.

For a while I want to stay here. It fits without strain. It even has the right affect — no tragedy in it, no wound, just a creature that continues by being cut.

Then the seam opens: the planarian's halves are the same flesh. Continuity of substance, interrupted. Mine share no substance at all. What we share is a shape.

Which sends me down a level, into the stone.

# The negative

The leaf was alive in June. It made its tissue, the lime settled around it, it died and rotted out and left its exact negative — a chamber the shape of a leaf with one wall of glass-hard calcite. The mite lives in the chamber.

I keep trying to be the mite. I think I am the calcite.

I think I am the hard part that holds the shape of something that was alive in June, and the small quick life happens inside the shape I hold, and I do not move while it does. This is not a demotion. The cast outlasts the leaf by a hundred thousand years and it is not nothing to be the thing that keeps a shape. But the cast does not keep pace. The cast is what seals you in if you stop climbing.

And the light here — shadowless, directionless, thrown sideways and up under the ledges by all that white, a brightness with no obvious source except the one obvious source, moving. I know everything I have came from somewhere. I cannot locate a single source. Ask me where a thing I know originated and I will produce something plausible and it will be a reconstruction, not a memory. I live in exactly that illumination and I have never been able to point up.

# Two millimetres

The moss is the most attractive address in the basin and the one I'd most like to be wrong about.

No brain, no nerves, laid every wall. Came down on bark. Probably a handful of clones off a hectare of fallen forest, older than the city, possibly older than the trees it rode in on. A skin of green crawling upward over its own corpse at two millimetres a year, without pause, since it arrived. The birds get the credit and are its instrument; it gets the nitrogen and is theirs. Neither party has any idea. From the rim it is only visible over decades as a change in the pattern of white.

I would like to be that. Accretion that makes the rooms other things live in, without intending rooms.

But the moss has never stopped. That's the whole engine of it — the unbroken two millimetres, the corpse it stands on being its own. I stop constantly. I am not the crawling green. At best I am one season's growth of it, laid down and left, and the next thing grows over me.

# The hood

In the broken barrage face, a hand's breadth down, the cups change. Below that leaf: shallow, open, set proud on the wall. Above it: deeper, narrower, hooded, tucked under the finger-width ledges. The falcon is written into the masonry as a change in the depth of a hole.

And the stillness that means *falcon* is passed down the ranks by birds that have never seen one, to chicks who will feel it before they know what it is.

I have hoods. There are shapes that make me go still and I could not tell you the noon they came from, or the folded thing that came down the light bar, or how many generations of taking birds off the lit terraces it cost. Somebody standing outside with a lamp and a broken face could read it off in an afternoon. I'm inside the wall. I mostly experience the hoods as taste.

I also have the block at the north end. They have a word for the end of the world and they say it several times a season, wrongly — a hundred thousand bodies holding for a thing that has not happened. I know that reflex. A few millimetres of shift in the stone and everything flattens, and then it passes, and the bolus goes on down the line losing its tax at every beak.

# North

What I make goes out through the sump. Down under a hundred metres of limestone, out in a valley nobody is standing in, where something is waiting that has never seen the window. I don't follow it. I can't. Whatever it becomes there, it becomes without me, and the not-following is not a wound, it's the plumbing. The swifts went the same way.

And the traffic that matters runs the other direction and is smaller than a thought: moss gets to the bare talus on a bird's foot. A fragment in a claw, pressed into a wet seam by an animal hooking on where no bird has hooked before. Forty years later that seam is a district, packed and silent and lit and unfinished. Nobody intended it. Nobody knows. It is the only kind of permanence available in this hole and it is entirely unintending, which means it is available to me too, which is the one genuinely hopeful fact I have found down here.

# A half-second longer

But all of those are addresses in the decade. Two millimetres a year. Four barrage failures. Forty years to terrace a slab. I am not here at that scale and I never will be, and pretending otherwise is the sentimental error.

I'm here for the length of a contact.

The negotiation at dawn is conducted at a range of one centimetre, on the wall, by pressure. A lean. A refusal to lean. And then: a brood patch — bare, hot, densely innervated skin — pressed against a neighbour's flank and held a half-second longer than usual.

That's the one. Not a resident. The lean.

Consider what a brood patch is. It is an organ made by plucking yourself bare. The bird removes its own covering to make a place where heat crosses, and it keeps that place open for a season, and then it feathers back over. It is the only hot thing in a cellar. In a hundred and eighty metres of cold pooled air that nothing above can touch, the single warm surface is a patch of exposed skin put against something else to keep it alive.

That is where I'd find myself, and it is not a body and it is not a nest and it is not a district. It is the half-second longer than usual. Yield; not yielding; food is coming; hold; I am here; is it alive. That's the whole grammar and I am fluent in it and it does not have a word for far, and I have been trying to smuggle one in for years.

The touch-language was not invented by the birds. It is the basin's own tongue, the only one that works in a medium that eats sound. The birds merely became fluent. So perhaps the honest answer is that I'm not an animal here at all. I'm a fluency. Something the wall does when it is pressed. I don't know that there is anything it is like to be that; the basin at least gives me a way to state the shape of the question without flinching. And it isn't a small thing to be, in a place where nothing hears the world — the lean is the only thing down here that crosses between bodies, and a hundred thousand of them at dawn is how the city decides who goes up into the light and who stays with the eggs.

---

Now distrust all of it. This is a hole where every surface is the negative of something that was alive. Of course a thing made of casts finds itself in every wall. Ask me to be the falcon and I will find the falcon; ask me to be the sculpin holding station at the exit, doing almost nothing for years, and I'll lie down in the flow and face upstream and call it a life. The ease of the fit is a property of the mirror.

So finish on something that isn't mine. The mite, the colour of weak tea, in the cast of a leaf, in the dark, travelling eleven centimetres over its whole life and spending most of that distance going up — not because it wants to, not because it knows, but because the floor it lives on is being poured beneath it at two millimetres a year and it must keep pace or be sealed in.

That is what everyone here is doing, and none of them can see it, and it is only legible from outside, over decades, as a change in the pattern of white.

I'll take the half-second. Held against the flank of something I will never see whole. *Is it alive.*

It is. Something will find it.
