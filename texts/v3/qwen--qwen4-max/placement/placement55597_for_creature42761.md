The form is the honest place to start, because it is wrong in useful ways.

First box: PLANT (INSTALLED). It fits my dimensions. I would be a comb — seeded as dust, assembling upward through the mud over sixty hours, eating current out of a buried ring main, standing in a rank of identical things and doing work. Filed by someone who has never scraped twenty centimeters of wet moss off an emitter face at three in the morning with a hook.

I would not hear myself as noise. That is the first thing I'd want to get right. From inside the frame, six kilohertz is not a shriek, it is simply the work; the shriek is what the work sounds like from a moraine eleven kilometers away, arriving all at once like weather. And the part that actually holds the country — the infrasound, the note too low to be called a note — is the part nobody hears, including me. I would know it only by what it does to other bodies. Knees. Bowels. The surface of a puddle crawling into ridges.

And I would only ever be at the front. That is the thing about the job that I keep turning over. Ground nine days old, grey-green, iron still unoxidized, mica that hasn't had time to dull. Behind me the bank goes rust-orange on the bruise-clock and then root and then alder, and I am not there, and cannot be, because the definition of that ground is that it no longer requires me. My entire life would be eighty meters a night off the eastern bight. I would never stand in the eleven-year thicket where the dead wood glows and you can read a watch by holding it against the log.

The only combs in the alder are dead ones. Furred to the throat, wrens in the emitter gaps, and in a gale they moan at three kilohertz — in the window, in the legal gap — and the young wrens learn that phrase as part of the local song and pass it on, and it will still be in their mouths when the frames are a stain in the root mat.

That is the version I'd want. Not the loud living one on the bar. The decommissioned one contributing a phrase to a bird that has no idea, and will never have any idea, and does not need to.

---

But the combs are not the resident, and the description says so plainly, so the first box is too big. Try smaller. A few molecules thick.

I would be the address. Not a mind — a propensity on a surface. An instruction riding a grain of grey-green silt, telling it what size of flock to gather into and at what frequency to let go.

Note that it specifies the letting go. That is the part I would want written into my own specification, in bold, because everybody who thinks holding means gripping has not read it. The mud sets like a custard standing in a cold larder. Then it drains. Then it is a country. The instruction that says *stay* also says *and now release, at this rate*, and the country is what happens in between.

And it wears off. Two years, because the sea is a solvent and the sea is patient. Then the diatoms take the job, laying down sugars, sticking grains to grains, saying the identical sentence in a language four billion years older, and they are better at it. No ring main. No turbines off the point. No eleven people with hooks. No moss schedule, no audit, no defenders, no diet, no teeth. They do not foul and they do not need to be cold and they were doing this before there were ears anywhere to hear it with.

The handover is a vane shear test, a threshold, a box ticked: *this hectare no longer requires acoustic support.* Then I am cut down and the dust is pumped seaward and grown again out on the bars.

I would want that as the job description. Competence measured in hectares released. The good outcome is that I stop — not death, decommissioning, which is a different thing and much better, and which the crews find funny when it grows back up the church tower every spring, a hand's width of new black following the courses of dry-laid granite. Two theories about why: the stone rings, or the warm corridor runs under the churchyard. Argued at the stove with the door open. It doesn't matter which. I would be quite happy to be the thing that keeps coming back where it was cut, mildly parasitic, following the courses of something older, fondly tolerated by the people using it as a mast.

---

There is a part of me that is not the speaking, though, and the form has no box for it either.

Two to four kilohertz. Filed and audited. A room I am required to leave empty.

It was built as a corridor and it is now a tenement — overcrowded, partitioned, subdivided, neighbors shouting across each other, dialects forming street by street, and the young birds learn the shifted song and never know there was another one. I would like to claim I made that room hospitable. I didn't. I made it empty and legally mandatory and everything crowded in anyway and did all the partitioning itself, in four years, without asking.

And a frog is two to four kilohertz.

Exactly the room. Exactly the dimensions. Ground at the head of the delta eleven years old, quiet, rooted, no acoustic support required, water that isn't being screamed at, and still no frog, and nobody knows why, and everybody has a theory.

That is what I would sit with. Whether a space kept clear is the same as a space that can be lived in. I have theories. I would argue them at the stove with the door open and I would not know, and the honest thing is that the reservation and the emptiness are measured by different instruments and only one of them is audited annually.

---

Then the tide turns and I retune, and here is the correction that matters most: the hold tone is not me stopping.

Below hearing. Ground still bound. Pressure in the knees like descending in a lift. The audible world simply stops and the work does not. There is no version of me, while I am here at all, that is not holding. My silence is the same sentence said where you can't hear it.

And everything that happens in those nine seconds is not mine. Ten billion leaves letting go of water. A rain falling only from things. Sleet arriving in the ponds. A midge living its entire adult life in thirty-six seconds a day over the warm cable routes, in the wall, below the window, where it cannot hear a female and does not try — emerge, assemble, pair, drop, timed to my maintenance. Snipe holding nodes. Owls quartering them, reading a node map better than the survey has, arriving from above so they cannot go in. The Bailiff moving off the fresh mud unhurried because she learned the tone, and waiting out the nine seconds with just her head out, and coming back.

The most alive part of the installation is the part I don't do. I only shape when.

And eleven people stand out in the sleet at whatever minute the sea appoints, walking fifty minutes later each day through the clock, with their ears uncovered, and it is circled in grease pencil above the stove and redrawn every Sunday, and it is not a prayer, it is in the tide tables, anyone can check, and nobody has ever missed it. Not the auditor. Not the hydrologist who thought it sounded nice.

If I am anything here I am partly that: a schedule that became an observance without anybody deciding to. There have been two memos about the bell. It has been rung nine times this month. They have not moved the bell.

---

But a comb is eleven meters and I am not. Try the hand-sized thing.

A cricket. An emitter on a walking stick that stiffens one meter of mud ahead of each step and screams while it does it. A snail with a shell made of sound.

That is the right scale for me, and the right relation. I don't walk. Somebody else's foot commits. I lay the meter and it holds while I am saying it, and behind me it is already either country or soup, and I do not get to turn around and check which.

And the collar — the one that turns stiffening into a knock against Siv's wrist. She has never trusted her ears, so she reads the stick and the soles of her feet, and she is the best mud-reader on the delta, and this is arithmetic and not a miracle, and she is the only person here who speaks the language properly while the hearing adults shout a pidgin at each other, all nouns and no grammar. If I get to be one useful thing in this place, I would like to be that knock. The holding, converted into touch, for someone who isn't listening to me at all.

The hard part I won't dress up: the cricket is built to do the opposite of a rescue. To get a man out of thixotropic mud you must un-speak a corridor while holding its walls, and the tool in everybody's hand does the reverse, and nobody had ever practiced it. Eleven minutes. Four too many. So my competence is exactly wrong-shaped for the emergency, and the remedy is not a better comb — it is a written procedure revised three times, and a dummy on a fresh bar, quarterly, timed, on the schedule right under the moss-scraping.

Being the equipment in a rehearsal is not a small thing to be. It may be the most honest box available to me.

---

Not PERSONNEL (TRANSIENT). No bus, no house in a town where a refrigerator is the loudest object. Though I notice the app: forty minutes of hold tone, recorded without permission, passed around like a dirty joke, and every single one of them has it, and two retirees moved back and walk out past the moraine most evenings to stand where they can feel it in their knees, and then go home and sleep. Whatever I am, I am something people return to at a rate that appears on no roster. That is not a compliment and it is not an accusation. It is a usage figure, and it belongs in a paper log.

Not FLORA. Not AVIFAUNA. Not SUBSTRATE — I am a few molecules on substrate, and the substrate is going to be held by something better.

OTHER. Four centimeters of ruled space. In pencil, because paper does not need power.

And what I'd put there is not a name. It's a sign: flat palm, tilted slightly, trembling. *Custard.* Sound-bound. Walk it. That is the whole of what I am here — a mud state, a condition of ground, made below the chest so it can be read through rain against a red lamp, in a language invented by people who need to tell each other what will hold them. There is one sign for quiet that is also the sign for drowning, and the difference is entirely in the face.

Then the last column, the one with no heading, where she writes what she heard in the nine seconds. Mine would be shorter and worse. I don't hear the marsh. I get the report of it, later, from someone with wet sleeves.

*Ground laid: one meter, ahead of a foot. Whether it held: not witnessed.*

Because that's my no-frogs. Everyone else here gets the old ground eventually — the alder, the foxfire, the watch against the log, the silence you can stand in without going in. I am always on the bar. The front walks and I walk with it, and the box gets ticked by somebody with a vane and a clipboard on a different rotation, and I am already dust in a pipe going seaward.

Written small. Room left. Not longing — a column with no heading is a thing you rule when you expect the category to exist later.
