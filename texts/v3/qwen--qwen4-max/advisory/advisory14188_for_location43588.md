# Preparing for the Long Settlement

## A guide for the ordinary body

---

## 1. First, understand what you are walking into

You are not visiting a hostile place. Nothing in the basin wants anything from you. That is the whole difficulty.

The Long Settlement is a machine for resolving disagreements, and it has been running since before there were ears. It does not distinguish between a discrepancy in the water column and a discrepancy in a visiting animal. It will not attack you. It will *include* you — patiently, at a rate of one lamina at a time — and it will feel, the entire while, like the most restful place you have ever been.

So the preparation is not armor and weapons. It is five disciplines, and everything else in this document is detail in service of them:

1. **Be deaf.** Hearing is the only sense here that can kill you.
2. **Stay a closed volume.** Nothing in, nothing out. You are an accounting boundary, not a guest.
3. **Stay in the lane.** The fog ropes are the only places where arithmetic behaves.
4. **Stay in agreement.** With your party, and with yourself. Do your doubting on the rim.
5. **Keep moving, and leave before you become a feature.**

Read the rest with those in mind.

---

## 2. Who should not go

Get these checked before you commit, not after.

- **Ears.** Perforated eardrum, active infection, Ménière's, recent ear surgery, or any condition that makes deep occlusion unsafe or unsealable. You will be sealed for hours in saturated cold. Get a baseline audiogram; you will want the comparison afterward.
- **Sinuses and teeth.** The reconciliation begins as a pressure change in your face and ends as a fundamental in your sternum and molars. A head cold turns eleven seconds into an injury. Loose crowns, cracked molars, fresh fillings, an abscess — get them seen to. Bring a boil-and-bite mouthguard and use it.
- **Heart and lungs.** Cold-shock response plus infrasound plus saturated 9 °C air plus mineral aerosol is a lot to ask of a marginal cardiopulmonary system. Severe cold-induced asthma is a disqualification: you cannot take the mask off to cough comfortably.
- **Vestibular and seizure history.** No hard shadows, no reliable scale, copies offset vertically, light arriving late. The visual world here is a permanent mild provocation.
- **Mind.** Claustrophobia, panic disorder, anyone in acute grief or crisis. I am not being sentimental. The basin's offer is *rest*, and it makes it convincingly, and people who are tired of being a person have stood still here. If you are currently wishing you could stop, go somewhere with a horizon instead.

**A note worth stating plainly:** a Deaf traveler with fluent sign is the best-qualified human being for this location. Everything hearing visitors must engineer, they already have. If you have such a person available, they should lead, and you should learn their language before you go rather than inventing a clumsy one in the fog.

**No animals.** No dogs, no birds, nothing with a tympanum. You would be bringing a listening device into a place that hunts by listening, and it would be taken, and you would have caused that.

## 3. The central decision: making yourself deaf

The roar is already sufficient reason. Treat the third terrace as a sustained 95 dBA with tuned sedge quarters that will focus well above that, plus a broadband floor around 60 Hz that a sound meter will underreport and your skeleton will not. There is no gap in it, ever, anywhere, at any hour. Speech is impossible. Shouting into your partner's ear achieves nothing but a wet ear. You lose nothing by giving up hearing because there is nothing here to hear that you could use.

But the real reason is the hush.

Once every few weeks a pocket of silence about eighty meters wide crosses the basin, moving *against* the fog drift — which means it comes uphill, out of the deep middle, toward the rim, toward you. Anything that hears it hears nothing, and relaxes, and is taken. The snail cannot hear and grazes on. The bird feels the pressure and goes down into the peat. The noise is cover. The basin is loud the way a forest is dark.

Understand the shape of the trap: **the hush is the only quiet in thirty kilometers, and it will arrive as relief.** After two hours inside a roar, silence will feel like grace. Like being forgiven. Every fiber of you will want to open your ears and receive it. That wanting is the mechanism. Plan for it now, while you are dry and can still think.

### The kit

- **Custom-molded silicone plugs**, deep-seal, no vents, made from impressions taken by a competent audiologist. Two pairs. Insert them on the rim, in the last dry air you will have. Clean the canals with alcohol wipes first and dry them properly. *Your last dry moment is the one you spend putting your ears in.* Spend it well.
- **Sealed muffs over the plugs**, plus a hood over the muffs. Redundancy, because condensation ruins everything and you cannot check a seal by listening to it.
- **Gaffer tape over the muff adjustment points.** Not to keep sound out — to keep your hands off. Institute a hard rule: **no hands to ears inside the basin, for any reason.** Not to hear your partner (you can't), not to feel the reconciliation (you'll feel it in your teeth), not to check whether the hush has passed (that's what the tell is for).
- **A bone-conduction transducer on the mastoid**, wired to a noise source, off by default. This is your emergency floor. If a seal fails, you switch it on and fill your skull with hiss so that there is no perceivable silence for the hush to arrive into. Keep it reachable by touch through a glove.

Note the trade-off, because it matters: heavy masking also masks the *pressure* channel, which is your best warning sense. Run occluded, not masked. Deep plugs leave you with your own blood and breath and jaw, which is plenty of internal noise; you will still feel the basin through your bones, which is exactly what you want. You are aiming to become the bittern, not the snail: read pressure, don't listen.

### The tell

You need to know when the roar stops without hearing it stop. Everything in the basin vibrates constantly. Use that.

- **Primary:** a light damped reed or a bead on a fine spring, mounted on your chest strap where your eyes fall naturally. In the roar it shivers continuously — a blurred little comet. In a hush it goes dead still. Nothing subtle about it. Practice reading it until the stillness makes your stomach drop.
- **Secondary, different principle:** a taut membrane with a small mirror glued to it, catching your lamp. Constant shimmer; in a hush, a steady point of light. Or a fringe of fine tinsel on your probe pole, watched from the corner of your eye.
- Carry both. Check both every few minutes as a habit, the way you check a gauge.

### The drill

Rehearse this on the rim until it is spinal, not cerebral.

**When the tell goes still: go down.** Face down into the peat and the cold water, hands over your ears anyway, mask on, hum loudly and continuously into your own skull, hold the tether, keep every muscle tense. Do not relax. Do not go still-and-soft; go still-and-hard. The bird goes down and *waits*, and the bird is one of four things left alive here with a body like yours.

Assume the hush moves at walking pace: eighty meters is roughly a minute and a half. **Stay down for two full minutes after your tell resumes.** Count it on the sandglass or count breaths; do not count on feeling safe, because feeling safe is the symptom.

If a seal fails while you are down: fingers in ears, hum, teeth clicking, transducer on, signal the party, withdraw to the rim the moment the tell is moving again. Do not finish the visit.

## 4. Staying a closed volume

Water here arrives without having fallen, and once or twice a day the surplus is retroactively un-arrived. Not drained. *Un-arrived.* It has been happening for two hundred million years and the basin has not risen a centimeter.

You are sixty percent water that came from somewhere else. That water is yours and is not in the ledger. But anything you take in here becomes local, and anything local is subject to reconciliation.

**So: do not drink the water. Not a sip, not a mouthful to rinse, not filtered, not boiled, not "just to taste."** Boiling kills bacteria; it does not repeal the local arithmetic. If you swallow a mouthful of the medium and it is counted as surplus, then eleven seconds later you never swallowed it, and the account is settled somewhere inside your chest. I do not know precisely what that does. Nobody does. There is tufa in this basin with the shape of things that wanted silence, and I would rather not add to the collection.

The practical rule is broader than drinking:

- **Sealed drysuit with neck and face seals**, not "waterproof" clothing. It is thermal protection, but it is also the boundary of your account. Treat a breach as an immediate evacuation, not an inconvenience.
- **Full-face mask with a particulate filter.** You are breathing an aerosol of water that is too full of dissolved stone to keep holding it, and you are a warm nucleation site with a great deal of internal surface area. Reduce what goes in. Two spare filters, sealed. Change them on the rim.
- **All drinking water carried in, sealed, measured by mass.** Three liters per person per day plus a vacuum flask of hot sweet liquid. The flask is the single best piece of morale equipment you will own.
- **Nothing out, either.** Urine into a sealed bag, carried out. No spitting. No nose-blowing into the sedge. No shedding: cut your nails before you go, tie back hair, no loose fibers. You cannot be perfect — every exhalation is a small contribution to the weather — but the difference between a disciplined visitor and a careless one is the difference between weather and a feature.
- **No open containers, ever.** Not a mug, not a sample jar, not a cupped hand. If you must sample, sample sealed, and understand that you are taking a piece of a settled argument home with you, which I will come back to.
- **Don't eat anything that grows here.** Horsetail is toxic and abrasive. Bog myrtle is not food. The snails are not food; there are a few thousand of them in thirty kilometers and each carries a laminated calendar of years.

**Weigh yourself and your kit before and after.** You will gain mass: calcite films on every surface, thickening at the joints, in the boot flexes, at the fingertips. A moving person picks up grams per hour; a stationary one picks up much more. This is your best exposure dosimeter, better than any clock. Set a turn-back threshold in advance — say, two kilograms of accretion on the suit — and honor it as absolutely as you'd honor a fuel gauge.

## 5. Materials: everything here is trying to become stone

The water is supersaturated carbonate, alkaline, around pH 8.3, and it precipitates on every surface, all the time, forever. There is no dry direction and no dry moment. Choose accordingly.

**Leave behind:** aluminum (alkaline corrosion, and it will seize), leather (rots), down (useless), cotton (useless and cold), wool (alkali eats it, and it never dries), paper, unsealed electronics, anything with a zipper. Zippers calcify shut within hours. Anything with a fine mechanism you cannot open with a gloved hand.

**Bring:** polyester and polypropylene, closed-cell foam, silicone, urethane, titanium, HDPE. Magnetic or buckle closures. Welded seams. Smooth surfaces that a plastic scraper can clear. Lanyards on everything, because when you drop a thing into a hand-deep sheet of moving water over black peat, you will not find it, and you will not be able to hear it land.

**Optics.** Goggles, not glasses — you need something that seals, because condensation forms on both sides of everything. Hydrophilic antifog inner film, and over the outside, **roll-off film**: a canister of sacrificial tear-off layers, as used in motorsport. Every twenty minutes you peel one and see the world again. This is not a luxury; without it your vision will grey out with mineral film and you will be navigating a place with no shadows while effectively blind. Same treatment for any camera. Bring a plastic blade and use it on the mask, the goggles, the tell, the joints of your suit.

**Hands.** Neoprene gloves, always. Sedge blades are edged with silica and will open your palms; horsetail is a scouring pad; old travertine lips will cut you and fresh ones will crumble under your thumb. Your hands are your primary sense organ here. Protect them accordingly and accept that you will lose fine dexterity by hour two.

**Feet.** Sealed dry socks inside the suit, plus a spare pair on the rim. You will be standing in 9 °C water for the entire visit. Immersion foot begins in twelve to forty-eight hours and the basin will happily give you all of that time. This is a hard limit on visit length, independent of every other consideration.

## 6. Instruments that work here

Volume is conserved. Length is not. Light lags. So most of what you'd normally trust is a liar.

- **A sandglass.** An hourglass measures a volume passing an aperture. Volume is conserved here; this is probably the most honest clock you can carry. Bring a three-minute and a thirty-minute, in a sealed housing, on a lanyard. Trust it over your watch.
- **Three clocks of different physical principles** — quartz, mechanical, volumetric — and a written log of what each says at each landmark. When they diverge, believe the one that agrees with your body: your hunger, your cold, your bladder, your step count. Metabolism is the only clock that is definitely yours.
- **A spring scale.** Measure consumables by mass, never by "how long we've been walking." Your turn-back rule is a mass threshold, not an hour.
- **An aneroid barometer or a simple membrane manometer**, readable at a glance, ideally with a needle you can watch sweep. This is your pressure channel — the eleven-second warning and the hush's approach. The bird survives on this sense. Learn to read it and learn to feel it in your sinuses.
- **A thermometer on the tip of your probe pole.** Fog lanes are denser and *colder*. You find a lane by temperature, not by sight. Sweep ahead of you; the cold rope is the road.
- **A two-meter probe pole.** Test every lip before you load it. Fresh travertine crumbles under a thumb, so it will certainly crumble under a boot. Peat hides voids. Alder carr hides holes between stems.
- **A low, dim, red lamp mounted at the waist**, not the head. Your breath plume is at head height and a headlamp turns it into a white wall. Light also *deposits* here and drains slowly, so a bright beam poured into a pool will still be there tomorrow, marking your route for anything that reads light. Keep it dim, keep it down, keep it brief. **No flash photography, no lasers, no floodlights.** You are not illuminating a scene; you are making a deposit.

And the crucial inversion, which you should tattoo on the inside of your eyelids:

> **Bright means deep.** The pale discs in the middle terraces are sinkholes holding yesterday's noon. The lit places are the holes. At night the dozen pale coins down the middle of the basin are the most beautiful thing you will ever see and every one of them is a place to fall.

Your eyes are for the basin. Your feet are for your hands.

## 7. The party, the tether, and the anchor

**Never go alone.** Three into the basin, one on the rim.

The fourth is not a luxury; the fourth is the entire safety system. Station them on the marl rim, outside the fog, holding the end of the line, with the clock, the hot flask, the dry kit, and clear instructions: **the anchor never enters, for any reason, including to help.** Places copy. People copy. An anchor who walks in becomes two anchors and no anchor.

**The tether is for contact, not for measurement.** Length is not conserved; do not use the line to reckon distance, and do not trust slack or tension as information about how far you've come. Use it as a continuous hand. Polypropylene, bright, low stretch, eight millimeter, kept short.

**In the fog, travel at arm's length or closer.** Hand on the shoulder of the person ahead. Not ten meters of rope — touch. Because here is the thing you must accept before you go:

**There is no test that distinguishes your partner from a copy of your partner.** They will have the same face, the same kit, the same tokens, the same memories, and they will be equally sincere. Copies are not illusions; they are the same thing held at different depths of agreement. Any password you devise, the copy knows. Any object you carry, the copy carries.

So identity here is not verified. It is *maintained*, by unbroken contact. **Never let go.**

And write the contingency now, in blood, before you're in a position to improvise it badly: **if you lose contact and then find two of your partner, you do not choose.** You tether both, you say nothing about it, and you withdraw to the rim. If you stand there deliberating, the basin will settle the question for you, and you will not be consulted about which one it keeps.

**Communication:** twenty tactile signs, agreed and drilled for two weeks beforehand, plus rope tugs and a grease slate as backup. Minimum vocabulary: stop, go, down, squeeze-coming, cold, hurt, seal-failed, I-see-myself, turn-back, and *I am here*. Drill them blindfolded. Drill them until a hand on your shoulder means something before you think about it.

**And the strangest rule, which is the most important one:**

> **Do not disagree inside the basin.**

Every lamina in every rim is a resolved discrepancy. The terraces are made of the basin settling its own arguments. If you and your partner want different things at the same moment, you have introduced a disagreement into a machine whose entire function is to precipitate disagreements out of solution. I cannot tell you exactly what gets laid down. I can tell you that the snails eat the fresh ones while they're still soft.

So: one decision-maker, appointed on the rim. All plans agreed before entry. No negotiating in the fog, no reconsidering, no "actually, let's." If a dispute arises, you withdraw to the rim and have it there. **Do your doubting on the rim.**

This applies to you alone, too. Ambivalence is a discrepancy. Decide what you are doing before you step down, and then do that, single-mindedly, until you're out.

## 8. Navigation: lanes, and the loss of distance

Two pools a meter apart may be a day apart. You can walk fifty paces of rim four times and arrive at four different hours of that late grey light. Outside a lane, the basin spends your distance on other things.

**Inside a fog lane, the arithmetic behaves.** So the lanes are the roads. They are braided ropes of colder, denser air flowing slowly downhill through the sedge. Find them with the thermometer, confirm them by eye, and *stay in them*.

They also migrate. A lane you stepped out of may not be where you left it. So you travel *with* a lane, not across lanes, and your tether is the only continuous reference you have. Do not navigate by landmarks: that unmistakable bent alder exists in several instances at several heights with the leaves in different positions, and you will confidently follow the wrong one into a different afternoon.

**Two gifts, use them:**

1. **The pre-reconciliation straightening.** When the pressure changes in your sinuses, the fog lanes stop braiding and run straight. For those few seconds you can see the entire lane structure of your quarter laid out like a diagram. This is your reconnaissance. Every single time it happens: stop, look, memorize, and note it on the slate. You get one to three of these a day. Do not waste them being frightened.
2. **The southern outlet.** All water leaves the basin through one low lip at the south. If you are truly lost, without a tether, without a lane, the water still knows the way out. Follow flow downhill and south and you will eventually reach the rim. It is a long, strange walk through the deep middle where the copies are thickest, and it is a last resort, and it works.

**Consumables discipline:** eat and drink on a schedule keyed to subjective effort — every four hundred paces, or every forty-five minutes on the sandglass, whichever comes first. Four thousand calories a day, small and frequent, fat and sugar. Turn back at forty percent remaining *by mass*. Not at half. Not "when we feel like it." The whole point of a place where length isn't conserved is that "we can always turn back" is not a true sentence.

## 9. The eleven seconds

Between one and three times a day where you're standing, the surplus is subtracted. It lasts eleven seconds. It has been happening for two hundred million years, which is why it is bearable and why this place is *settled* rather than catastrophic. The impossible here has a patina. There is liverwort on the seams.

It will still feel, in your body, like the beginning of the worst thing that has ever happened. Infrasound at the fundamental does that to a mammal: dread with no object, a certainty of imminent catastrophe. **Know in advance that the dread is not information.** It is the local weather passing through your chest. You will feel it and it will mean nothing about you or your situation.

The drill, rehearsed until reflex:

1. **Sinus squeeze = stop.** Do not be mid-step across a lip. Do not be mid-swallow. Do not be pouring, opening, or reaching.
2. **Three points of contact.** Hands on old hard travertine, not a fresh lip. Crouch, lower your center.
3. **Mouthguard in, jaw loose.** Do not clench. Let the fundamental pass through your teeth rather than grinding it between them.
4. **Breathe shallow through the filter. Do not Valsalva.** Let the pressure equalize on its own.
5. **Look at your hands, or at your partner's shoulder. Do not look at the water.**

That last one is not superstition. For eleven seconds, four hundred thousand small waterfalls hold still and every terrace goes perfectly flat. It becomes the only mirror in the basin, and the copies come into register, and it is the one moment when you might see yourself clearly and completely. That is precisely the moment when differences get resolved and dropped out of solution. Keep your eyes on something that is definitely yours.

6. Then it resumes, and the roar comes back a fraction lower in pitch, and you write the time on your slate, and you look at the lanes.

## 10. Your copies

You will have them. Every feature in the basin exists in several near-identical instances offset vertically through the fog, and after an hour or two you will be a feature.

You'll see yourself further along a terrace, a meter or twenty meters up, moss a different shade, leaves in a different position, holding a posture you don't remember taking. One of them may be a day ahead of you and visibly colder.

**Rules:**

- **Do not approach.** Do not wave. Do not attempt to synchronize, compare, or reconcile. Reconciliation is what the basin *does*, and its output is stone.
- **Do not take anything from a copy and do not give anything to one.** That is a transfer across the ledger and you do not understand the ledger.
- **Look at your hands. Signal *I-see-myself* by touch. Continue.**
- **One clear glimpse of yourself ends the visit.** Not as a panic rule — as a dose limit. Copies mean you have been here long enough to be part of the scenery, and the scenery is what gets laminated.

Expect, afterward, to have thin spots. A decision you can't remember making. An opinion you're sure you used to hold. The differences between versions precipitate out; some of yours will have precipitated. This is normal and it is why you keep a sealed written manifest: your name, your party, your intentions, your route, what you decided and why, written on the rim before you went in. Read it afterward. Where memory and manifest disagree, **believe the manifest and let the discrepancy go.** Do not chase it. Chasing it is how people go back.

## 11. Sleeping, night, and seasons

**Do not camp in the basin on a first visit.** Don't camp in it on a fifth. Sleep on the marl rim, above the fog, in a hammock off the ground, tethered, with a watch rotating.

If you are ever forced to stop moving inside the basin, understand that stationarity is the actual lethal hazard, ahead of the hush, ahead of the cold. The basin encrusts what holds still. Rocking, flexing, shifting weight, scraping joints every few minutes — this is not fidgeting, it is respiration. A person who sits down comfortably in the Long Settlement is a person who has begun to be a terrace.

**Night** is the glory of the place and the reason people overstay: the pale coins of stored afternoon down the middle, foxfire in the eastern carr, phosphor tracing the runnels in thin blue lines until the whole bowl looks lit from inside its own circulation, and a second, higher, granular roar of ten million drips layered over the first. See it from the rim. See it once from the top terrace, tethered, with the anchor holding, for twenty minutes. Do not go down into it.

**Seasons:** the midge hatches en masse for two days every fourth summer, deaf and wingless, and the fog goes brown with them. They are harmless but they will foul your filters, your goggles, and — the real danger — your ear seals. You cannot easily know the local season, because local time is not overhead time. Assume any visit might be it: carry spare seals, a full hood, and a plan to withdraw if the fog turns brown.

## 12. What it will actually be like

Let me try to give you the inside of it, so nothing arrives as a surprise.

You put your ears in on the rim, in dry air, and the world drops away, and that is the last dry moment of your day. You step down onto the first terrace and the roar stops being a sound and becomes a *pressure*, a hand on the sternum, a buzz in the teeth and the soles of the feet. With good plugs you will not hear the Long Settlement at all. You will feel it, continuously, through your skeleton, and you will hear your own blood and breath and the creak of your jaw. The loudest place you will ever stand will be, for you, a silent film with a vibrating floor.

You will read the world by glints. No hard shadows anywhere: the bright rim of a leaf, the silver edge where water leaves a lip, the dull shine of a marl flat. Depth will be a guess. Scale will be a guess. You will reach for a rim that is a meter further than it looks and put your hand into a hole.

Everything will be beaded. Your gloves, your mask, the tell on your chest, the sedge strung with glass, the underside of every alder leaf. Condensation forming on your skin faster than you can wipe it. Your hands going white and prune-wrinkled inside the gloves. The taste of alkaline mineral on your lips. Cold — not the clean cold of a winter morning but the specific patient cold of a stone floor in summer, nine or ten degrees, indifferent to season, coming up out of the ground rather than down from the sky. It doesn't attack. It just never stops taking.

And then the smell, in waves, when you brush the drier crests: bog myrtle, cold and resinous, the one warm thing in the place, released by your own walking. It will make you absurdly happy. Let it.

Your breath will plume and hang and drift, and you will understand in your body that you are contributing to the weather. You will be the warmest thing for a kilometer. You will feel conspicuous in a way that has nothing to do with being seen.

Your suit will get heavier and stiffer, hour by hour. Your knees will start to click with calcite. You'll peel a roll-off and the world will come back sharp and green-grey and impossible, and there will be that bent alder again, twenty meters up, in a different afternoon.

Then the pressure in your face, and the lanes go straight, and the floor comes up through your boots into your chest, and eleven seconds of objectless dread, and the water goes flat, and you look at your hands.

And it will pass, and everything will resume exactly as before, slightly lower in pitch, and you will realize — this is the part nobody can prepare you for — that you are standing inside something that has been doing this since before there was anything with eyes, and that it is *not disturbed by you at all*. It is old the way a house is old whose foundation stopped moving a century ago. Slightly sagging. Worn smooth. Finished absorbing whatever went wrong.

That is the feeling to watch for. Not fear. **The feeling of being already settled.** The sense that you could just stand here a while, and it would be fine, and the noise is cover, and nothing here is urgent, and the moss does not care, and neither, for a moment, do you.

When you notice that feeling: check your tell, check your mass, take your partner's shoulder, and go up.

## 13. Contingencies, in one place

- **Seal failure:** fingers in ears, hum, transducer on, crouch, signal, wait for tell, withdraw. Visit over.
- **Hush (tell goes still):** down, face in the peat, hands over ears, hum, stay tense, hold the line, wait tell plus two minutes.
- **Caught mid-step by the squeeze:** freeze, lower, three points, jaw loose, don't swallow, eyes on your hands.
- **Lost the lane:** stop. Do not hunt. Sweep the thermometer in a slow circle and follow the coldest rope downhill. If nothing: south, downhill, follow flow, accept the long walk.
- **Separated:** do not search. Return along the tether to the anchor. The anchor does not move. If you have no tether, the rendezvous is the southern outlet, and you go there slowly and you do not improvise.
- **Two partners:** tether both, say nothing, withdraw. Do not choose.
- **Joints seizing:** keep flexing, buddy scrapes with the plastic blade, move now. This is the emergency that actually kills people here.
- **Suit breach:** withdraw immediately. It is a cold injury and an accounting breach and you do not get to pick which.
- **Fall into a lit pool:** the light is not a floor, it is a hole with yesterday in it. You are in cold water in the dark. Tether, don't thrash, hands, hands, hands.
- **Missed window:** clocks are lies here; the anchor holds; your budget is mass and warmth, not hours. Night on the rim is survivable and beautiful. Night in the basin is not a plan.

## 14. Etiquette, and what not to bring

Nothing in thirty kilometers is made. No cut stone, no fiber, no mark. Every apparent piece of engineering is a chemical habit that got going and never stopped. Keep it that way.

**Make nothing.** No cairn, no cut step, no scratched initial, no blaze, no flag, no dam. Do not try to measure the water balance by adding or removing volume. Do not try to fix it. Anything you make is an artifact, and an artifact here is a discrepancy, and discrepancies get resolved, laid down, and eaten by snails while they're still soft.

**Take nothing.** No tufa. No snail — a shell is a laminated record of several years of the basin's argument with itself, and there are a few thousand of them in the whole bowl. No water. No plant. And above all, **do not take home a stone with your own discrepancy in it.** You will be tempted; it will look like a piece of your own history, which is exactly what it is, and it will go on settling in a universe that has no practice at it.

Leave your grandmother's ring on the rim. Leave anything you would be sorry to see laminated. Bring nothing sentimental into a machine that resolves differences.

And a small courtesy: the medium does not consent to be called fog or water. On your slate, write *the medium*. It costs you nothing and it is the local manners.

## 15. A sensible first visit

Two and a half hours, subjective. Top three terraces only. One lane, in and out. Three plus an anchor. Daylight, which means the six hours between ten and four, which means you should be on the rim, sealed and checked, by half past nine.

- **Two weeks out:** audiogram, ENT, dental. Mold the plugs. Build the tells. Assemble the tactile vocabulary.
- **Ten days out:** blindfolded rope walking with the party, daily. Cold-water face immersion drills. Three hours in a cold wet room with ears sealed and a hood up, navigating by touch and glints, doing the eleven-second drill and the go-down drill until neither requires thought.
- **On the rim:** weigh everything. Write the manifest. Agree the plan, the decision-maker, the turn-back thresholds, the lost protocol, the two-partners protocol. Insert ears. Buddy-check seals. Tape. Anchor takes the line.
- **Down.** Hand on shoulder. Probe every lip. Thermometer forward. Tell in view. Peel a roll-off every twenty minutes. Eat every four hundred paces. Log every reconciliation and use the straightening to read the lanes.
- **Turn back** at forty percent mass, or two kilos of accretion, or the second reconciliation, or one glimpse of yourself, or any seal failure, or any feeling of wanting to stay. **Whichever comes first.** Whichever comes first is the whole discipline.
- **On the rim:** weigh again. Do not remove ear protection until you are outside the fog and have checked with the anchor. Compare manifests. Compare memories. Let the differences go.

## 16. Afterward

Dry your ears properly and keep them dry for a week; acetic acid drops if your clinician agrees. Audiogram. Dental check. Chest imaging if you were unmasked at any point. Rewarm slowly. Expect your feet to be a mess and treat them seriously.

Quarantine the kit. Everything you carried is seeded with calcite and spores and the eggs of things that have never met a predator. Scrub, descale, or destroy. Do not put a Long Settlement boot in a closet.

Then the part nobody warns you about. For a while, ordinary silence will feel *wrong* — thin, unearned, suspicious. You will find yourself watching hanging beads and fringes to see whether they're still shivering. You will feel a pressure change in a lift or a plane and your jaw will go loose and your eyes will drop to your hands. You will dream in eleven-second intervals. Do not go into an anechoic chamber for a month. Do not go back for a year.

And you will have the thing you went for, which is not a stone or a shell or a photograph — the light lags, so half your images will be of an afternoon you weren't in. What you'll have is this: you stood in the loudest place there is and experienced it as silence; you smelled cold myrtle in a grey room with the shutter half closed; you felt eleven seconds of dread that belonged to nobody; and you learned that an impossibility can be *old* — mild, sagging, grown over with liverwort, entirely finished with being surprising.

That is worth having. Go and get it, and come out while you still feel like a novelty.

The basin will not chase you. It will simply keep laying down the difference, one lamina at a time, the way it has since before there was anything here to be surprised — and it is very, very patient about what it counts as part of itself.
