# Field Briefing: The Sill

## Understand what you are before you understand what it is

Everything in that cup is hard, sharp, clean, and finished or finishing. You are none of those things. You are a warm bag of salt water and forest-floor bacteria, wrapped in something soft, shedding skin, exhaling forty grams of water an hour, and leaving marks wherever you stand.

There are three soft things in the Sill and they are the apex predators. You would be the fourth, and you would be bigger than all of them, and you would smell more like a forest floor than anything that has ever been there.

There is also a second problem, worse than the first. The room copies what it likes. It has been reproducing one line of footprints until the footprints have stopped meaning feet. It has built a bench in the shape of a bench that has been sat on, and a door frame with no wear on the sill, and a coat with no dust. It is a machine that lost its instructions and has been patiently, lovingly making do with whatever it can find lying around.

You are something it can find lying around.

So the whole discipline of visiting the Sill comes down to two things: **don't be a mouth, and don't be a template.** Nearly every rule below falls out of those.

---

## The first thirty seconds, so you're not surprised

You come up the dead mountain in the cold, working hard for every breath, and your boots are dirty, and the granite is rotten and comfortable and old. Then you cross the line and the last worn thing in the world is behind you.

Three steps down, your ears pop, and the air comes into your lungs like a gift. Warm. Thick. After four thousand meters of negotiation, it feels like being handed a glass of water. Your shoulders drop. Your hands stop aching. Every instinct you have says *you have arrived, you are safe, sit down.*

Then the sound gets in. It is not noise. It is a bright, enormous, eleven-second room full of information, and you are standing in it deaf and blind and grinning, and the floor is body-warm under your boots and quietly deciding whether to include you.

That grin is the hazard. Rehearse the transition until it bores you. The people who get into trouble in the Sill are not the frightened ones; they're the relieved ones.

## Two kits, not one

The lip is a hard boundary between two climates and you need a different person on each side of it.

**Outside kit** lives in the ordinary world, cached on the rotten granite where caching is safe because things there are allowed to decay: real altitude gear, wind shell, down, water you brought, food, stove, your notebook, your sleeping bag, your camp. Sleep out here. Always out here. There is nowhere inside the cup to lie down that will not lie down with you.

**Inside kit** is what you cross the line wearing, and it is deliberately strange:

- **A hard, smooth, sealed shell.** Polished, non-porous, hydrophobic, no fabric face, no nap, no weave, nothing with texture. Texture is a nucleation site: the fog will find a wool cuff and start knitting it into the world. Texture is also soft, and softness is the local signature of a mouth. You want to arrive looking and sounding like a mineral.
- **Cut-resistant gauntlets and rigid plates over the shins and forearms.** Every arris in there will open your palm. You will put a hand out to steady yourself once, reflexively, and that will be the end of the visit.
- **Boots with a steel plantar plate and a one-motion release.** Read that again: *one-motion release.* There is a boot cured into the threshold of the door frame. Somebody stepped out of it and walked on. That is the whole reason it's there, and it's the best piece of advice anyone will ever leave you. Carry spare hard overshoes for the walk out.
- **Sealed goggles.** Not sunglasses. A midge in the eye finds tears, and tears are water, and the midges *measure and place.*
- **A filter over your mouth and nose, ideally with an exhale trap.** You are not going to breathe millions of instrument-insects into your lungs and find out what they decide to build in there. The trap also keeps your breath — your water, your CO₂, your smell — out of a room that has an exact and closed account of both.
- **A sounding staff.** Metal, ferruled, held, never planted. It is a walking stick that must never stop walking.
- **A flask of ordinary muddy water.** Turbid water. Silt from the dying mountain. This is your emergency tool and I'll explain why below.
- **Nothing else.** No rope you'd let trail. No pack you'd set down. No camera with a flash. No drone. No survey gear. No wood, no leather, no cotton, no food, no scent.

**Leave every measuring instrument outside the lip.** No rangefinder, no lidar, no quadrat, no grid paper, no tally counter, no GPS logger. Bring instruments that measure *you* — your gas mix, your heart, a countdown timer — and nothing that measures *it*. In the Sill, the survey stakes don't record the floor; the floor obeys the stakes. Description is prescription there. To measure something is to give it an instruction, and you do not know what language you're speaking.

There is a brass post with a steady needle, measuring something that has not begun to vary. You vary. Don't stand near the post.

## Prepare your body

- **Acclimatize properly for 4,000 m** — not for the cup, for the mountain. The cup will hand you low-country air; the approach and the retreat will not. Most of the actual physical danger of this trip is on the outside, in the cold, on the way home, wet.
- **Go in with clear sinuses.** Three steps of pressure change is enough to hurt a congested head. Equalize on the way down and on the way up.
- **Go in empty.** Fast for several hours. Empty your gut. Rinse your mouth. No coffee, no tobacco, no sunscreen, no deodorant, no perfume, no scented soap for two days beforehand. Your gut is a forest floor and your breath advertises it. Everything in that cup goes very still when it smells leaf litter, and you have spent your whole life smelling like leaf litter.
- **Seal every cut before you go.** Assume the water is a supersaturated mineralizing solution — it weeps out of bonds as they form; the whole floor is a setting reaction. In a wound it has a nucleation site and a warm supply of calcium. Clean any cut with your own brought water, not with anything in there, and close it hard.
- **Do not plan to sweat.** The floor is 31 °C, the air is warm and heavy, and exertion in a sealed shell will cook you. Move slowly. Keep visits short. Your suit is a jar; you are the only weather you're permitted to bring.
- **Do not carry cold metal in.** Anything cold-soaked from the mountain will condense the fog the instant it descends — that's what the swifts are, twelve degrees under ambient, drinking their own plumage. For the first ten minutes inside, a cold canteen or a cold camera body makes you a piece of plumbing, and the room will start treating you as part of the water cycle. Warm your kit at the lip. Conversely: never let yourself become the coldest surface in the cup.

## The rules of the room

I've numbered these so you can memorize them. Memorize them, then leave the list in the outside camp. Numbers are how this place thinks, and you should not carry a numbered list into it or recite one aloud.

1. **Keep moving.** Ten slow breaths is the longest both feet stay planted. Shift, step, shift. Never sit, never kneel, never lean, never set anything down. Kneeling puts three points of you into the record at once.
2. **Walk the fade.** A seam lights amber, holds about twenty seconds, goes out. Amber means *being made now* — that's a mouth. Dark means *finished, never changing again* — that's a blind room full of invisible holes and matte predators. The safe path is the narrow wake between them: stone that lit half a minute ago and has just stopped. Recently finished. Still faintly warm. No longer taking impressions. Follow the light at a distance, the way you'd follow a wave you don't want to be under.
3. **If the stone under your boot is getting warmer, step off.** Setting is exothermic. Rising heat means the reaction is under you. Fading heat means it's behind you. Learn the difference through your soles.
4. **Stay high.** The weeping runs downhill; the pools are in the low places; a wader taller than you stands in them, which means some of them are over your head. Walk the rim shelf. Do not go into the bowl. The center is where the footprints are, and the center is where the copies are, and the center is where the water goes.
5. **Never lose the lip.** It's two hundred paces across; you can hold the whole rim in one look. Keep it in your eye. Navigate by bearing, not by pace count. Do not count your steps.
6. **One strike per decay.** Sound your staff or drop a pebble, then listen for the full eleven seconds before you do it again. Never tap a rhythm. Never hum, whistle, click in time, or repeat a phrase. Periodic sound in a room with modes is not noise; it's a signal, and there are things there that grew up reading signals. Learn eleven seconds in your body until you don't have to count it.
7. **Do not talk.** Your voice will stack for eleven seconds and mask the wader's returns, the water's pitch, the swifts' gaps — you will blind yourself and every animal in the room. Use hands.
8. **Do not write, sketch, count, name, or map inside.** No notes. No tallies. Fewer than forty plants exist and the count changes because people count. Write everything down outside, afterward, in prose, without coordinates.
9. **Do not repeat yourself.** Don't walk the same line twice. Don't take the same route out. Don't stand twice in one place. Don't establish a routine of any kind. Repetition is the mechanism by which the room learns, and it learns *approximately*, and what it learns it makes.
10. **Use nothing.** Not the bench, not the hut, not the chair, not the coat, not the door frame, not the stair. Give the frame, the post, the stakes, and the stair ten paces of clearance. Never stand on a line between two stakes: those lines are the schedule, and you'd be standing where stone is due.
11. **Take nothing out.** Not a crystal, not a midge, not a stake, not a smear of the only mud in the world. Especially not a piece of the soft dark stuff.
12. **Be out before the afternoon chime. Be out before dusk.**

## Water: the local first fact

There is no sediment, so there is no turbidity, so all water is perfectly invisible. You will not see the pools. Learn the three tells before you go, and drill them in some ordinary clear tarn until they're automatic:

- **Pitch.** The cup's ring drops over a void. This is why you strike once and listen for eleven seconds. A pool is a note that doesn't match its neighbors.
- **Shimmer.** Drizzle striking water doesn't splash the way it splashes stone. Look for the small standing wrongness in the sift.
- **The meniscus.** A lens, a curve, a bright edge where the surface pulls up against the crystal. It's the only thing your eye gets, and it's a thin one.

Probe ahead with the staff on high ground; on any ground you're not sure of, don't go. Never wade. Never swim. The eels are clear too, and the waders hunt by striking the stone and reading the return — a body standing in their water is an anomaly they will hit with the flat of a beak to find out what it is. Their beak will break your shin.

**Do not drink anything in there.** Not the pools, not the weep, not the runnels. It's a depositing solution; the animals have gut chemistry you don't have. Bring all your water in and carry it all out. In a genuine emergency, do it the way the candle-stalks do it: hold a clean cold polished plate in the fog and let it condense, and drink the condensate, and keep the plate moving, and stop as soon as you can. You are briefly pretending to be a swift. Don't make a habit of it.

And the cruel corollary: **the only water you can see in the Sill is the water that will hurt you.** Cloudy water means a hush is upstream. Nothing drinks it. Nothing seeds it. Neither do you.

## The hush: rehearse the wrong fear

Three patches, dark and matte and low, spreading from hand-sized to door-sized. They eat stone. Under them is the only mud in the world. They are the only surfaces that don't ring, and inside their acoustic shadow your own footsteps stop existing.

Understand precisely how they kill. Nothing in there hunts by eye. Everything navigates by sound — water by pitch, the front by hiss, the wader by return. A prey animal blunders into the silence, loses the world, stops moving because it can't tell which way is anything, and then either the patch spreads over it or the floor takes it because it stood still. The silence is the trap. The mouth is almost incidental.

If you go deaf in there:

- **Do not stop.** Do not shout — you can't hear yourself and you'll scream and stack the room for eleven seconds and blind your partner too.
- **Do not try to find your bearings by ear.** You have none. Go by your last remembered bearing to the lip, uphill, and go now.
- **Get your partner's hand.** If you can't find it, you should already have been holding it.
- **Deploy the mud.** Turbid water is the one thing the deposition will not seed and the midges will not touch. Silt is dead matter — grains that have already finished happening, that can't be instructions and can't be crystal. Throw the slurry at the leading edge, across your own feet, along your line of retreat. It may suppress the set; it may also feed the patch. Nobody knows. It's the only lever you have and I would rather you had it and didn't need it.

**Train your nose to fear the wrong smell.** After an hour in a room that smells of nothing but wet mineral, the smell of leaf litter and rot will hit you like the smell of home — like safety, like a forest, like the end of a long cold day. That recognition is the trap. Sit outside the lip with a bag of ordinary duff and practice feeling your heart rate go *up*. Do it until it's automatic. It's the single most valuable hour of preparation you can do for this location.

Visually, hunt for matte. In a bone-white glinting room, look for the places with no glint, no amber, no ring, and a grey smear at the edge. Give them thirty paces. They spread while you watch.

## Two people, one hand

Go with two or three. Not more — more people means more marks, more noise, more repetition, more template.

Stay in physical contact. A rope that trails on the floor becomes a line the floor obeys; a rope is a stake you laid down. If you use a tether, keep it short and airborne and put a quick-release on both ends, because if your partner starts to set you must be able to let go instantly.

Agree a squeeze vocabulary and drill it until it's reflex:

- one squeeze — *keep moving*
- two — *water*
- three — *hush, go up, go now*
- long pull — *out*

Never call a name. Never call anything's name.

**If your partner starts to set:** do not pull. Pulling a partly cured limb tears tissue along crystal planes. Keep them moving — flex, shear, walk them, the bond is weakest at the seam where the stone is still deciding. Flood the interface with your slurry. Shed the boot in one motion and get them out in the overshoe. If it's a hand, cut the gauntlet. If it's above the ankle and it's been minutes, you are making a decision I can't make for you, and you should have thought about it outside the lip, in the cold, with a clear head, before you ever crossed the line.

## Copies

The room reproduces what it likes, slightly softer each time, unresolved underneath in the places the record never saw. One wader mirrors the other down to the chipped beak, on the wrong side. The swifts have no gut anyone has found.

Assume you will be recorded from the moment you cross. Assume your footprints will outlive your visit and start walking without you.

- **The wrong-side test.** Before you go in, note your partner's asymmetries: which side the scar is on, which hand the ring, which way the part in the hair. Copies mirror. Check every time you rejoin, casually, by touch and by glance, and check yourself in a reflection.
- **If you meet yourself, do not touch it and do not follow it.** It has no insides. It will not know where the water is; it will know where the water *looked* like it was. It will lead you into a pool with perfect confidence.
- **Do not answer to your name, do not answer a repeated sound, and do not investigate a line of footprints that resembles yours.** Especially not that.
- **If you find your own coat on the chair, leave immediately and do not run.** Running is a rhythm. Walk out by a route you have not used.

## The invitation, which is the real hazard

A roofed hut with one room. A chair facing the growth front. A coat on the chair with no dust on it. A bench worn by nobody, in the exact shape of a bench that has been sat on. A door frame with no wear on the sill, no polish on the jamb, no smoke on the lintel — made and not yet had.

Every one of those objects is a sentence with a space in it, and the space is your shape.

You will want to sit. Not from fatigue — from *fit.* The room is warm and loud and wet and small and it holds its breath at four thousand meters, and it has been made ready, and you will feel expected in a way you have never felt expected anywhere. That feeling is not information about you. It's information about the room. It is a mold, and molds feel like welcome.

Don't finish anything. Don't repair anything. Don't tidy. Don't sit on the bench to see what it's like. Don't step through the door to see where it goes; it doesn't go anywhere yet, and a threshold is a definition, and there is already a boot in that one.

And don't go down the stair. Nine steps into unmade stone, ten after the next chime. The bottom step is a seam. Whatever the stair is for, it is not for you, and the room will be delighted to include you in the plan.

## Timing

- Come up over several days and sleep out on the dead mountain.
- Cross the lip in mid-morning, warm, dry, empty, dark-adapted for twenty minutes at the lip with no white light.
- **Hard turnaround: under an hour.** Two at the absolute most, with a partner and a reason.
- **Out before the mid-afternoon chime.** Don't be inside when the stair grows. Don't be on the low end when it does.
- **Out before dusk.** At dusk the ceiling drops a meter, the drizzle thickens, and every seam in the floor lights amber at once — the whole basin becomes an active front, a city seeing itself, the most beautiful thing you will ever be offered. It is also the moment when there is no finished stone anywhere to stand on. Watch it from the lip, with your boots in the dust, in the cold, breathing thin air, holding your partner's hand. That's the right place to see it from and you'll be glad you were uncomfortable.

If you are ever trapped inside overnight: walk. All night. High, on finished stone, in the light of the seams, hand in hand, taking no route twice. Do not enter the hut. Do not sit. People who sit down in the Sill become features.

---

## Coming out

Set a decon line on the *outside*, on the rotten granite, where things are allowed to be dirty.

Brush hard. Rinse with turbid water — the slurry, the ordinary mud, the stuff full of rounded grains that have already finished happening. Strip and bag everything. Do not shake gear out over the cup, do not wash inside, do not carry a live seam home on a cuff. You do not want to be the person who introduces a self-assembling mineralizing front to a world with two hundred million years of weathering in it, and you *really* do not want to be the person who carries a hand-sized piece of soft dark silence into a world made of stone.

Then, for weeks afterward, watch yourself. A cut that glitters. A cough that won't clear — you breathed instruments, and instruments measure and place. Kidney pain. A thickening in a boot that is not the shape of the boot. A footprint in your own hallway that is slightly more approximate than your foot.

Take nothing. Not a crystal, not a midge, not a numbered stake, not a smear of the only mud in the world. There is an old valley somewhere below wearing itself out into sand and sea, and this cup is being laid down ahead of the loss, one clean crystal at a time, by tools that outlived their instructions and became animals. It is not a landscape. It's a nursery, a mold, a waiting room with the lights left low, and nobody has come to sit in it yet.

You are not the person it's waiting for. Be a guest who leaves no marks, tells it nothing, teaches it nothing, and gives it nothing new to copy.

Keep moving.
