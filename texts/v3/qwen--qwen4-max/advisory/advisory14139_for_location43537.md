# THE WORN PLAIN
## Preparatory notes for the ordinary traveler

---

### The one thing to understand before you read anything else

Every other dangerous place in the multiverse wants something from you, or at least is indifferent to you in a way that leaves you room. This one has no wants and leaves no room. It is not hostile. It is *tight*. A hundred million years of mutual wear have taken up every thousandth of a millimeter of play in a country the size of a province, and when you arrive you will be the loosest thing in it — the only slack, the only gap, the only clearance anywhere.

The plain does not attack gaps. It closes them. That is simply what happens here, the way water finds level.

So the whole of your preparation reduces to four instructions, and everything below is elaboration:

**Come finished. Move constantly. Stay four hours. Leave everything as married as you found it.**

---

### 1. What will actually kill you, in order of likelihood

Be honest with yourself about this list, because the glamorous hazards are not the ones that get people.

1. **Cold.** Shin-deep moving brine, air a degree above freezing, permanent drizzle, no dry ground, no shelter, no fuel, nothing to burn. This is the oldest death in the multiverse and it is still the most efficient. It will take you in three hours if you let it, and while it takes you, you will stand still — which delivers you to hazard five.
2. **The mesh.** You will put a foot between two surfaces that are turning toward each other at a rate you could outrun in your sleep, and you will not trouble about it, because you can see it happening and you have all the time in the world. Immense torque, no speed. The country's grip does not snatch. It *closes*, over minutes, and the fit improves as it closes, and by the time withdrawal requires effort it requires more effort than you have.
3. **Chemistry.** The refinery districts stand in pale green ferrous liquor under a haze of chlorine. There is sulfide in the film and therefore hydrogen sulfide in the hollows, and hollows are where the cold air pools, and cold air pooling is where you will crouch to get out of the wind. Heavy metals in everything, including anything you might think of eating.
4. **The mouths.** Something out in the fog can hold the exact triple knock of a spalling race for six hours without variation, waiting for scavengers. It is not fussy about what converges. You are warm and you are slow.
5. **The fit.** The real one. The one this place is for. Given time it will wear you into a shape that meshes, and the horrible thing — the thing nobody warns you about properly — is that it does not hurt. It gets *easier*. More on this in §11, which is the section to reread on the way in.

---

### 2. Before you go: your body

**Get your teeth looked at.** No mixed metals in your head. An amalgam under a gold crown is a battery in a wet salt country, and this plain runs on exactly that principle; it will find your mouth interesting. More broadly: no pacemaker, no cochlear implant, no steel pin, no copper coil, no metal-bearing prosthesis. If your body has a conductive discontinuity in it, you are a component with a pulse.

**No open wounds, no blisters, no nosebleeds, no head cold.** The film lives on iron and sulfide and the warmth of friction. You are a hearth with a leak. Bleeding here is not an injury, it is an advertisement, and it is also a *donation* — see §12 on what happens to what you give this place.

**Trim your nails. Empty your bladder and your gut before transition.** You will not be opening your shell on the plain. Not to eat, not to drink, not to scratch, not to relieve yourself. Every one of those is warmth, salt, moisture, and iron offered to a country that has never once refused an offering.

**Take off your rings.** All of them. A ring is a bearing race, and this is a country with very strong opinions about marriage.

**Screen yourself honestly.** The people who do worst here are not the clumsy or the frail. They are:

- *Good entrainers.* Drummers, rowers, machinists, weavers, dancers, pianists, anyone who cannot help finding a beat. The roar has a fundamental. Your nervous system will lock to it without asking you. Entrainment is the first stage of the fit and it feels like competence.
- *The grieving.* The grease is an archive. It speaks. If you have someone you are not finished missing, this plain will find the frequency.
- *The unused.* Anyone who has lately felt like spare parts. The plain has a use for you. It will not lie about that. It is the only place you have ever been that will genuinely, mechanically, undeniably have a use for you, and some people cannot walk away from that, and I have stopped being surprised by which ones.

If two of those three describe you, go somewhere else. There are eleven thousand worlds with better weather.

### 3. Kit

**Dielectric, seamless, monomaterial.** No ferrous anything. No mixed alloys anywhere on your person, including zippers, eyelets, snaps, boot shanks, and the foil in your ration wrapper. Two dissimilar metals in contact on a wet body in a standing gradient make a cell, and a cell is an invitation. Ceramic, glass, and one single polymer throughout, chosen for cold-brine and chlorine resistance and for staying ductile below freezing. Ask your outfitter for the cold-flex rating and then halve it.

**Smooth soles.** This is counterintuitive and important. Tread is clearance. Tread is a mating surface with a hundred thousand small gaps in it, and the seal-worms breed into gaps at exactly the rate they are extruded, and the film regrows at exactly the rate it is cropped. A lugged boot left standing on a warm terrace for an hour will come up wearing a new sole, and it will be *native*, and it will be turning. Smooth, seamless, no lace, no welt, no stitch. You will slip. Slipping is survivable. Being journaled is not.

**Seal every interface.** Ankles, cuffs, collar, waist, the corners of your eyes if you can manage it. Tape them. Then have your partner tape the ones you can't see. You are trying to present this country with a body that has no gaps in it, which is impossible, because you are alive, and being alive is a kind of clearance. Do the best you can and understand that you are only buying hours.

**Thermal camouflage, not just thermal protection.** You want a cold shell over a warm core. Insulation here is not primarily about keeping your heat in; it is about keeping your heat *secret*. A loaded bearing at forty degrees is a hearth, and everything alive within a kilometer is pressed against the hearths, rim to rim, walking on each other's backs. You will be thirty-seven degrees, which is to say you will be the wealthiest thing in the province. Do not flaunt it. Chemical warmers inside the shell only. **No stove. No fire. No lamp of any kind that heats.**

**Light discipline: passive amplification, red, downward, brief. No torches.** The bright dendrites in the refinery districts climb toward the light. Shine a beam across a pan of ferrous liquor and pure metal will begin growing out of the muck toward the beam, which is to say toward your hand, at a speed you will underestimate. Nothing in this country has ever considered the sun. Do not introduce it to a point source.

**Hearing: filtered, never blocked.** You need the knocks. Get plugs notched to take the grinding fundamental down by thirty decibels and leave the midrange alone. A traveler who has deafened themselves for comfort is a traveler who cannot hear a mouth working, or a ram coming home, or the specific change in a bearing's note that means the surface next to it has begun to spall and something is about to come apart at the speed of geology.

**No tether, no beacon, no line back.** A rope is a shaft. It will be journaled within the hour and married within the day, and then you will be tethered to a district, and the district will be turning, and you will find out what the Long Bearing feels like from the inside. If you want a rally point, use a slab of dead datum stone — the worn-out bedrock, the lens-ground flywheel that has nothing left to do. Native, inert, already fit. Mark it with a scratch, not a flag.

**Two logs and one alarm.** The alarm is set for three hours and it is not negotiable; you will not be able to judge the passage of time from inside, because the plain will make time feel *smooth*, and smooth is the warning. The two logs are for §10.

### 4. Arrival

There is no beachhead. Read the first line of every account of this place: *you hear it before there is anywhere to stand.* There is no dry shelf, no overlook, no staging ground, no ten minutes to get yourself sorted. The transition puts you ankle-deep in moving cold water on a moving floor with the roar already inside your chest.

**Therefore: everything you will be doing on the plain, you must already be doing when you arrive.** Sealed, taped, plugged, warm, empty-bladdered, alarm running, partner's hand on your shoulder. Come finished. There is no arrival, only continuation.

The first ten minutes are for finding your feet, literally. Stand on the broad dead face of something and let your body learn what the ground does. It moves. Not much, not fast, but it is *going somewhere*, and your ankles will spend the first quarter hour arguing with it. Let them. Do not start walking until you have stopped fighting the floor.

---

### 5. How to walk

- **On the tops of things, never between them.** Every gap between two surfaces is a nip point, and the fit in those gaps is finer than any measurement you could take. You cannot see the clearance because there isn't any. Step on faces. Never in seams.
- **The sixty count.** No foot in one place for longer than a slow count of sixty. A planted foot is loose at ten seconds, snug at ten minutes, and married at an hour, and the transition between those states has no threshold you can feel. Shift, shift, shift. This is the single most fatiguing thing about visiting the worn plain and it is why the honest visit length is four hours and not four days. Your body can wade in cold brine for a week. Your attention cannot stay unmeshed for an afternoon.
- **Slowly, and without rhythm.** Do not march. Do not fall into step with your partner. Do not let your footfalls land on the fundamental. Walk like someone carrying a full cup, irregularly, arrhythmically, badly. Bad walking is safe walking here.
- **Watch the near ground, not the far.** Twenty meters ahead is a little bit later than where you stand. You can, with practice, watch the water you are about to step into and see how your foot will land in it. Travelers who learn this get very good at not stepping into seams. Travelers who learn it too well start watching only that, and stop watching themselves, which is a different failure.
- **Do not walk toward anything you have watched.** See §10.
- **Do not retrace, do not double back, do not close a loop.** Go out on a shallow arc, stop, and transit from where you stopped. You do not walk out of this country. Nobody has ever walked out of this country. You stop, and you leave from where you stand.

### 6. The day, and where to be in it

The whole place runs on one stroke a day, powered by nothing but a few degrees of swing across the phase boundary of water.

- **Dusk:** the water in the stone cylinders begins to freeze. Expansion drives a million rams home. This takes eleven hours. It is the power stroke and it is comparatively quiet — a rising, loading groan you feel in the sinuses more than hear.
- **Night:** the freeze. Cold below zero, everything setting, the knocking at its loudest and least trustworthy. Half of what you hear is real. Do not be out on the terraces at night. Do not be anywhere near a cylinder at night.
- **Dawn:** the thaw, the return, and the dawn chorus — a million rams bottoming out at once. This is the loudest event in the known multiverse and it happens every single day and has happened every day since before the mountains gave up. It is also the moment when an entire generation of ice-ram organisms is crushed and a new one is born, and the noise is not only mechanical.
- **Mid-morning to early afternoon:** the four warm hours around noon, when the ice weeps. This is your window. The film is at its most active, the grazers are packed rim to rim on the terraces, the midges are up, the districts are lit. Everything is happening and nothing is stroking.
- **Late afternoon:** the whine rises with the weather. If you hear the thin electrical whine come up, the gradient is high, plating is running, and you are a difference in potential. Leave before it finds you.

**Arrive after the chorus has settled. Be gone before dusk.** Six hours at the absolute outside. Four is better. And note the mercy in this arithmetic: the horizon runs about three days behind your ankles, so a same-day visit never catches up to its own future. Stay under a day and you will never have to meet the thing you saw out there. This is the strongest practical argument for brevity, and it is a good deal stronger than the cold.

### 7. Sound

Learn three things and you will be safer than most.

**The tell for a mouth is perfection.** A real failing bearing *progresses*. The triple knock drifts, worsens, develops a fourth voice, changes with load. A predator holds the knock. It will hold it for hours, patiently, exactly, because it is not a bearing, it is a lure, and it has all the time there is. **A knock that has not changed in twenty minutes is a mouth.** Move away from it slowly and never toward it, and understand that "away" and "toward" are the only two options and the fog will not tell you which is which until you have committed.

**The archive is tonal.** Grease rendered from a particular windrow of dead will, under load, produce the animals it was made from — a lowing, a wingbeat, one specific distress call — coming out of the metal at the exact frequency of the shaft. It is harmonic. It is *in tune* with the machine. Real animals are broadband, messy, transient, and out of tune with everything. If a sound is beautiful, it is a recording, and it is very old, and it is not asking for help.

**Silence discipline.** Hand signals. No names. **Never say your name aloud on the plain, and never say your partner's.** The mouths are patient learners and a name is the most useful sound in the world to be able to make. There are travelers who have heard themselves calling from the fog in their own voice, and there are travelers who went to see.

**The silence pockets:** a few meters wide, sound simply stops, machines keep turning, water does not ripple. The animals go around them. **Go around them too.** You will want to enter one, because after two hours the roar is a physical weight and a pocket of silence looks like rest. It is not rest. Nobody knows what those places are. The best guess anyone has is that they are where the fit went all the way to completion, and there is nothing left in them to make a sound because there is nothing left in them that is two things. Whatever they are, the local fauna has been avoiding them for longer than there have been vertebrates anywhere, and you should extend them the courtesy of assuming they know.

### 8. Electricity, midges, and the ethics of being a wire

The midge clouds carry the current. Iron in the wings; a cloud settling across two terminals closes the circuit and a district lights cold blue and starts to turn. When the cloud lifts, the district goes dark and quiet and begins, very slowly, to die.

You are a difference in potential. Your shell is your only argument against this, and it is a good argument, so keep it intact.

Two rules, and they conflict, and you must hold both:

**Do not be a terminal.** Do not let a cloud settle on you. Move slowly through clouds — do not swat, do not wave, do not run, do not fan. Lifting a cloud can dark a district. You are the largest single disturbance this plain has experienced in an epoch; a wave of your arm can end something that has been running since the mountains gave up.

**Do not be a break, either — but if you become one, leave anyway.** Here is the trap, and it is the cruelest thing about this place, and it is not even malicious, it is just *tight*: if a cloud settles across you and a district lights up, you are now that district's power supply. Your conscience will tell you that you cannot leave. Your conscience will be very persuasive. It will use words like *dependent* and *responsibility* and *you did this*.

Leave. There are places on this plain where the entire electrical supply is a swarm of flies with a two-day life cycle, and the geology has arranged itself around that fact, and **waits**. Waiting is what this country does. It has waited out supply failures for a hundred million years. It can wait out yours. It is not your job to be a spare part, and the plain has no opinion about whether you stay, and the thing that will be warmer for your staying is not going to thank you, because there is nobody there.

### 9. Conduct toward the living and the worn

- **Eat nothing. Drink nothing.** There is no plant here, nothing green, nothing that ever considered the sun. The base of the food web is chemosynthetic slime on iron and sulfide and friction-warmth. Everything above it is that slime plus heavy metal plus rendered dead. Your own water, in a glass or polymer flask, inside the shell, or nothing.
- **Touch nothing bare-skinned.** Not for the chemistry. For the interface. Two things worn perfectly into each other become, at the interface, indistinguishable. That is not a metaphor here and it is not slow.
- **Take nothing out.** Pull a wheel from its mesh and it is a meaningless lump, and its neighbor becomes a mold of something that no longer exists — you will have destroyed two things and acquired nothing. Worse: some of those gears out in the fog are the *same gear*, in two places. Strike one, both ring. A souvenir on your shelf is a live connection to a country you left, ringing quietly every time your house settles, and something out there is a very patient listener.
- **Leave nothing behind.** Not a wrapper, not a glove, not a scraping of film from your boot, not a hair. Your leavings will be fitted. And because distance here is duration, the thing you drop today is quite capable of being the thing you trip over on Thursday.
- **Repair nothing. Free nothing.** If you find an animal caught in a mesh, it is not caught. It is a gasket, and it breeds into the gap at exactly the rate it is extruded, and it has been doing that for longer than your species has existed. The seal-worms are pink and soft and greased and they look like they are suffering. They are inventory. So are the grazers stacked rim to rim, walking on each other's backs, and the ones underneath genuinely do not seem to mind. Do not import your feelings about crushing into a country where crushing is a career.
- **Bring no animals.** A native animal is a better instrument than anything you can carry — follow what they follow, avoid what they avoid. A foreign animal is a gap with fur on it, and it will love the warmth, and you will have to watch what happens, and you will not be able to stop it.

### 10. Cause and effect: the part you cannot prepare for, only rehearse

The wear did not stop at the metal. A hundred million identical strokes ground a groove in the sequence of events, and the play in causality is gone, and now the effect comes first, out of habit.

Practically, this means:

**You will hear the ram bottom out and then, seconds later, the frost arrives to drive it.** Expect it. It will not stop being wrong. Travelers vomit the first time. That is normal and it passes.

**Use it as a warning system, because it is one.** If you see scavengers converging through the fog, something is going to die there. If they are converging on *you*, you have some interval — minutes, perhaps — and you should spend it moving, not thinking. If you hear your own voice cry out, you are going to cry out. Do not waste the interval on disbelief.

**Do not try to prevent what you have already witnessed.** This is the hardest instruction in this document and I cannot fully justify it, only report it. There is no clearance left in the sequence of events; the effect has come, and the cause is *owed*, and travelers who have tried to stand in the way of an owed cause report that the plain simply took up the slack elsewhere, and that the elsewhere was them. You are the only play in the system. Every deviation you make is paid for out of your own fit. You have a small budget of freedom here, it is measured in the loose tolerances of your own body, and every time you spend it you mesh a little tighter. Spend it on leaving.

**Keep two logs.** What you observed, in the order you observed it. What happened, in the order it happened. They will disagree. Do not try to reconcile them on-site; the reconciliation is what breaks people. Write it down, get out, argue about it at home.

**Distance is duration. This is the rule that makes the geography dangerous.** The far plain is not another place, it is this place later. The herd faint at the edge of sight is the herd at your ankles in three days, and it is not a copy. So:

- Looking outward is looking forward. Binoculars are a forecasting instrument, and what they forecast is *binding*.
- **You do not need to walk to the famous places.** The Weeping Terraces, the Long Bearing, the gallery of sumps, the cold fault — they are all further than the light will carry, which means they are all further than your own future. And you don't have to go to them. Stand still for three days and the horizon arrives at your ankles. Every destination on this plain is available for the price of standing still, and standing still is the one thing that will certainly kill you. That is the shape of the trap. It is very elegant and it has never once needed to be sprung, because it springs itself out of ordinary human patience.
- **Keep your radius small and never walk toward something you have watched.** Walking outward closes the lag. You are approaching your own future. Go far enough and you catch it, and at the point where you catch it you are the same person in two places, and if one of you is struck, both ring.
- **If you see a figure at the edge of sight, leave immediately.** It is standing where you will stand. It is you, later. And in every account I have read, it was turning.

**Do not go looking for the maker.** Nothing here was built. There is no evidence of one in any stratum, and the ores simply have an industrial habit, the way pyrite cubes. There is nobody to find. But the looking will take you outward, and outward is later, and the looking is how the plain gets the curious. It has never needed a lure of its own. We bring them.

### 11. The fit: know the stages, abort at the second

This is the section to reread on your way in.

1. **Wet.** Minutes. Ordinary. Cold, miserable, your boots filmed orange where the water is warmest. Nothing wrong.
2. **Snug.** Half an hour to an hour. Worms have found the gaps in your gear and are breeding into them at the extrusion rate. Your suit has stopped leaking. Your cuffs feel *right*. **This is the abort signal.** Not because it is dangerous yet, but because it is the last stage that feels like an improvement you would notice.
3. **Journaled.** Hours. Walking becomes easy. Your feet land where the ground is going. You stop fighting the floor and the floor stops fighting you and you begin, without deciding to, to keep time. You notice that you have not heard the roar in a while. You have not stopped hearing it; you have been notched out of it. Your hearing has been fitted to the fundamental the way a wheel is fitted to its partner.
4. **Married.** The interface is indistinguishable. You cannot find the edge of yourself and you are not distressed by this, because distress requires a gap between what is expected and what occurs, and gaps are the one thing this plain does not permit. You are warm. You are useful. You are turning. Something is warmer for it.

**Abort criteria, in plain language, for your partner to enforce:**

- When it gets easier.
- When the water stops feeling cold.
- When you stop hearing the roar.
- When your footsteps land on the beat.
- When you feel needed.

Any one of these, and you go. Not "finish what you came for." Not "one more look at the terraces." Your partner's job is to physically move you and your job is to let them, and you should agree to this in writing beforehand, while you are still a person who can want things.

### 12. Companions, and the worst thing

Go with one partner, no more. Two is a watch; three is a conversation, and conversations have rhythm.

**Do not lean on each other. Do not hold hands for long. Do not sleep against each other.** Two travelers worn into the same mesh become the same gear in two places. There are people who went to this plain in love and came back as one thing that remembers being two, and there is no procedure for that.

**If your partner dies, do not attempt recovery.** By dusk the sheet-water will have carried them to a sump, and the ice rams will press them, and what comes out the far side is a black faintly sweet fat that seeps uphill to the bearings because bearings are where the pressure is lowest. Within a day, some shaft will be turning on your friend, and under load it will make their sound, at its own frequency, in tune. It will be very beautiful. It will not be them. And the mouths will learn it, and they will use it, patiently, for a long time, because a distress call means a coming death means food.

Write this down before you go, and give a copy to someone at home: **if you hear my voice from the metal, do not come.** It is the last useful thing you will be able to do for anyone you love.

---

### 13. Leaving, and after

**Exit from dead stone.** Cold, worn-out, lens-ground, nothing left to do. Not from a warm bearing — warmth is activity, and activity is interface, and if you transit out while married you will find out which parts of you the plain considered joint property. Check your seals. Check your soles. Check the seams of your partner's suit, and let them check yours.

**Quarantine on return, properly.** The film is a chemosynthetic slime that lives on iron and warmth and it is patient and it does not need this particular plain to survive; it only needs what this plain has, which your world also has. The seal-worms breed into gaps at exactly the extrusion rate and your world is full of gaps. The midges have iron in their wings and a two-day life cycle and a single gravid female is a founding population. Strip, scrub, incinerate the shell if your outfitter allows it, and wear nothing you love in the first place so this costs you nothing.

**Take no grease home.** Not a smear, not a sample, not a scraping for the lab. It is an archive of everything that had to die to keep a machine turning, and it will speak in your basement, and you will listen.

**Expect, afterward:** the phantom knock in refrigerators, in pipes, in your own knees. A period of finding ordinary slack unbearable — the play in a door hinge, the slop in a steering column, the loose minute between two appointments. Dreams of being useful. A grief with no object, because there was nobody there to grieve for, and yet.

And expect the temptation to go back, which will not present itself as danger. It will present itself as *peace*. Because there is something genuinely offered here, and I do not want to end these notes by pretending otherwise: it is the only country I know of where nothing wants anything of you, where nothing is judging, where no one needs you to be anyone, where the whole enormous apparatus has been running without a maker or a purpose or a single act of intention since before the mountains gave up. Some travelers stand in the drizzle with the roar in their sinuses and feel, for the first time in their lives, entirely unrequired. It is a real gift. It is the closest thing to a sabbath I have found anywhere in the multiverse.

Take the four hours. Take the gift. Do not take the rest of it, because the rest of it is the fit, and the fit is the peace, and they are the same thing arriving from two directions.

**Come home unfinished. That is the whole discipline. Being unfinished is the only thing you have that this place cannot make for itself.**
