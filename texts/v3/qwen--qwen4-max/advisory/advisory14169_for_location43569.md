# Preparing for the Kept Room

## First, correct your category

Everything you would normally do to prepare for a cave, a ruin, a wilderness, or a wonder is wrong here, and some of it is dangerous.

It is not a cave. There is no country rock; nothing here was carved. It is not a ruin; nothing here is failing. It is not wilderness; it is an artifact with a metabolism. The closest honest analogues on most worlds are: **a cleanroom, a neonatal ICU, a pipe organ, and a body-sized caving squeeze** — and you must satisfy all four sets of rules simultaneously, because they conflict, and the conflicts are where people get hurt.

Three facts generate every rule below.

1. **It is finished.** Its mass, shape, and pitch are in exact balance. Anything you add or subtract is not weather. It is an edit.
2. **It is alive,** by about two square feet, working at a hair's breadth per decade. You will be ten thousand times its biomass and you will be shedding.
3. **You are in the squeeze the whole time.** Read the account again: there is one path, body-width, arms down, chest brushing stone on both sides. You do not walk around in a chamber. You spend your entire visit inside a chest-compressing slot in the dark-that-isn't-dark, deafened, numb, with no shadows and no clock. That is the actual physical situation. Plan for that, not for "a bright room."

---

## Decide, honestly, what you are going for

There is a cruel trade at the center of this place. The thing everyone goes for — the moment the white wall stops being a wall and becomes a medium, and you hear your own carotid and the squeak of your neck bones — arrives *late*. It requires your hearing to give up defending itself. Your hearing gives up defending itself by being damaged, or by being asked to tolerate a dose you should not give it.

You cannot have the full opening and a conservative exposure. Pick one, out loud, in writing, before you travel. Then tell your partner, because inside you will not be the person who wrote it.

Most people should choose the threshold and be content. The threshold is already more than almost any world will give you.

## Screening: who should not go

Rule yourself out if any of these apply. There is no rescue in there (see below), so screening *is* the safety system.

- **Ears:** existing tinnitus, hearing loss, Ménière's, vestibular disorder, any ear surgery, perforated drum, tubes. A head cold or allergic congestion on the day is an automatic abort — the pressure/equalization behavior described will find a blocked Eustachian tube and may find your round window.
- **Teeth and sinuses:** get a dental check. The report says it lives in the roots of your teeth. Loose crowns, cracked molars, large restorations, recent implants: don't.
- **Lungs and heart:** anything where sustained low-frequency chest coupling is a bad idea. Pregnancy: no data, so no.
- **Mind:** claustrophobia, panic disorder, dissociation, personal or family history of psychosis, recent bereavement. This is not squeamishness. The room is a hallucination engine with an existential argument built into it, and it will make that argument in your own voice.
- **Body:** if you cannot get through a slot, you cannot go. Measure, don't hope.

**Baseline audiogram** (extended high-frequency, plus tympanometry) within a week before. You will want the comparison, and if things go wrong the comparison is what an ENT will need.

## Train the slot, not the room

Build a mock slot: two rigid panels, adjustable gap, a length of at least your height plus a meter. Reduce the gap over weeks. Find your true minimum with lungs at 60% and shoulders relaxed. Most people discover their minimum is set by the ribcage, not the shoulders, and that it is 3–5 cm smaller than they believed.

Drill three things until they are reflexes, because in the real slot you will be numb, deaf, and unable to see the floor:

**1. Panic exhales.** In a tight slot the frightened body inhales. Inhaling wedges you tighter. This is the mechanism that kills people in squeezes. Train: *fear → long slow exhale → shoulders drop → reverse.* Say it to yourself in the mock slot until it's spinal.

**2. Don't black out.** The entry instruction — "exhaling" — tempts people into repeated hard Valsalva. Stack four of those in a warm body and you will gray out. Fainting while wedged is a death sentence. Exhale to about 60%, slide on the pause, breathe shallow between moves. Never to empty, never held to the point of sparkle.

**3. Don't claw.** Hyperventilation in a tight place produces tetany: your hands cramp into claws and you cannot use them to reverse. Slow nasal breathing, four in, six out, rehearsed under load.

Also rehearse the **exit** in the mock slot, uphill, tired, cold, with numb skin. Coming out is worse than going in, and it is worse when you have already spent your dose.

## Finding it, and getting to the lip alive

The approach is an ordinary lethal desert and should be treated as one; people die on the walk, not in the room.

- **Water and shade staged at a base camp** well back — a hundred paces minimum, farther if you can. You cannot carry a flask through the seam. Pre-hydrate, and accept that you will come out thirsty.
- **Playa dust is not inert.** Deflation basins concentrate evaporites: borates, selenium, arsenic, lithium salts, sometimes worse. Wear a low-profile respirator on approach and on exit, eye protection, and log your exposure. Inside, the air is clean; the danger is the plain.
- **Navigation:** featureless glare, mirage, no landmarks. Lay a removable low trail, keep a hard turn-back time, and have an independent position fix. Do not assume your multiverse extraction method works in a place whose defining property is the abolition of gradients. **Shift only from the surface, well away from the seam.** Never from inside.
- **Detection:** the hum is your friend. A contact geophone or a cheap seismic sensor and a spiral search will beat eyes. Look also for *the place where the dust is being taken* — a mouth that inhales keeps a clean line in a dirty plain. Ground-penetrating radar or a gravity traverse may find the made mass, since there is no bedrock shell.
- **Vehicles stop far out.** Vibration, compaction, and dust plume near the intake are all edits.
- **Mark nothing at the mouth.** No cairn, no stake, no windbreak, no flag. The seam is a hand wide and the room is a siphon; anything that changes the flow at the lip changes the chord. Markers go far away, low, permeable, removable — or nowhere, and you navigate by coordinates and the hum.
- **Never widen the seam.** Not by a millimeter. A semitone took an epoch. Chipping the lip to make entry easier is vandalism of a tuning that cannot be restored by anyone, ever.

## Go / no-go at the lip

**Weather is the thing that can kill you and destroy the room in the same afternoon.** This is the lowest ground for a hundred leagues. It is a hole that inhales. Selenite is gypsum, and gypsum dissolves in fresh water. A rain event anywhere in the catchment — not at the site, *anywhere upstream* — sends water into a body-width slot in a room whose free volume is one tenth, and you will drown inside stone while the artifact dissolves around you.

- Require **72 hours of stable, dry forecast across the entire contributing basin**, in every season, including the mountains you cannot see.
- Your surface watcher's *only* jobs are sky, upstream horizon, and the line. Not photography. Not napping. Not "just peeking in."
- Cool season. Convective-storm season is disqualifying.

**The hair test.** Pull a hair, hold it in the draft at the lip. It should stand out straight and not tremble. If it droops, flutters, reverses, or hesitates: **do not enter.** Either the siphon is off its mean or something has changed, and you do not have a model of what "off its mean" means for a body inside.

**Send instruments before you send yourself.** A slim inert probe on a rod through the seam — cleaned, sterile, PTFE or titanium, touching no lip — logging sound pressure level (Z-weighted and third-octave, not just dBA, because A-weighting hides the low end that will be living in your sternum), temperature, humidity, flow, and spectrum. Log for days. Also measure the drop from the seam to the floor before you put your feet into it: shadowless light means you cannot see the floor, and the buzz-lubricated slide can accelerate you into an ankle-breaking landing.

Then do the hearing arithmetic honestly. 100 dBA buys you fifteen minutes; 115 dBA buys thirty seconds; 130 is pain; 140 is immediate injury. And plugs are not the whole answer: a sound you can feel differently on the palm and the back of the hand arrives through your skeleton, and bone conduction goes around earplugs. Assume your protection is partial. Set your dose from the measurement, not from the romance.

## Kit: what you wear, and what you refuse

The governing principle: **the room forgives photons; it does not forgive crumbs.** Block all the light you like — it averages light. Leave one skin flake in a mortar-line and you have fed a rosette, changed a growth rate, altered a lip's curve, and detuned a pipe forever.

Wear:
- A **seamless or flat-seamed, slick, close-fitting one-piece** — caving slide-suit fabric, no external pockets, no front zipper, no buckles, no buttons, no hooks, nothing to snag or shed. Laundered lint-free, unscented.
- A thin merino base. Twelve degrees is not cold in air; twelve degrees of stone touching both sides of your chest continuously, with a huge thermal mass drawing heat out of you, *is* cold. You are trading warmth for thinness and you will pay for it.
- Hair fully covered. Thin slick socks or booties with a toe you can push with.
- **Custom-molded, deeply seated earplugs**, made from an audiologist's impression, fit-tested in your mock slot. Cordless. No muffs — they will not pass and they will not help your sternum.
- Soft daily contact lenses if you need correction; no rigid frames through the slot. Discard them after.
- A slim elapsed-time watch you can read in glare. Set it to *elapsed*, not clock. Two alarms, even if you can't hear them.
- A **2–3 mm low-stretch UHMWPE line** to a low-profile ankle or waist loop worn *under* the suit.

Carry, inside the suit or sent down the line after you:
- A tiny directional torch. Not for brightness — for **shadows**. There are none in there, which means no shape-from-shading, no depth, no way to see a tube mouth before your face meets it. A single point source restores the world for navigation. Then decide, deliberately, whether to kill it for the experience. Kill it only when you are stationary and holding stone.

Refuse, explicitly, at camp:
- Sample vials. Hammer. Chalk. Marker. Camera flash. Cosmetics, sunscreen, repellent, lip balm, perfume, leather, wool. Gloves with grip texture. Jewelry (snag, degloving, and metal chatters). Food. Water. A second person.
- **A flash is food. Your breath is water. Your dandruff is a decade of growth.** Say this to yourself at the lip.

Shower with unscented soap before you dress. Empty your bladder. Treat this as scrubbing for surgery, because functionally you are entering something with two square feet of immune system.

## The partner, and the truth about rescue

**Never alone.** Minimum two on the surface: one at the lip (kneeling to the side, never over the mouth), one at camp with comms and evacuation.

Voice comms are useless: the draft goes inward, and the room eats sound anyway. So: **tug code**, agreed and rehearsed on the surface.
- One tug: *I am okay.*
- Two: *coming out.*
- Three, repeated: *help.*
- Continuous steady tension from the surface: **hard abort — come out now, no discussion.** Set this at a fixed elapsed time regardless of how you feel.

Understand what the line is for. It is for signaling and for finding you. **It is not for extraction.** If you are wedged in a hand-width slot, hauling degloves your skin and dislocates your shoulders. Gentle steady tension only; never a jerk.

And face the structural fact: **the path fits one body.** Your partner cannot come in after you. There is no second route. There is no stretcher, no air, no light, no leverage. **There is no rescue in the Kept Room.** Every safety measure here is prevention, because that is the only category available.

## Inside: rules of conduct

- **Touch as little as you can,** and never grip. Slide. The buzz numbs you, so you will not feel the abrasions you are giving yourself or the pressure you are putting on a lip.
- **Never touch a rosette or a filament.** Those are the animal. That is the whole animal. They may be the last of their kind anywhere.
- **Take nothing.** Not a flake, not a chip, not a thread. The mass balance is exact to the atom over ten thousand years. A gram is a permanent edit.
- **Leave nothing.** No hair, no lint, no mark, no scratch, no cairn, no name, no date. Especially no name.
- **Do not enter the body-sized tubes.** Write this down before you travel and make your partner repeat it back to you. They fit you almost exactly and your mind will insist that means *for you*. It does not. A maximal packing of tubes leaves body-shaped gaps; that is geometry, not invitation. And practically: you would be a plug in a resonator, immobile at twelve degrees, in a room where nobody can reach you. People have died of a metaphor before.
- **Speaking:** the urge to sing into a room made of ten thousand throats will be the strongest urge you have ever had. Know what it costs — aerosol, CO₂, water vapor into a system that has never had an animal in it, plus a detuning that damps in five seconds. My counsel: silence, and if you cannot manage silence, **one word, once, at the very end,** and understand it as something you took, not something you gave.
- **Move slowly.** Numb skin + no shadows + no depth + no audible feedback is a recipe for a broken ankle in the one place a broken ankle is un survivable.
- **Use your nose as an instrument.** The room should smell of plaster, cold wax, and one thread of dried hay. Any other smell is either you or news. Both are reasons to leave.

## Inside: rules of mind

This is the part people skip and it is the part that follows them home.

You are entering a **full-spectrum Ganzfeld**: uniform shadowless light brighter than noon, plus unvarying broadband sound with zero information, plus no time cues, plus no other living thing. Every laboratory version of this setup produces the same effects within minutes, and this one is stronger than any laboratory version.

Expect, and pre-label:

- **Voices in the white.** Every audible frequency is present at equal intensity. That means *every possible word, song, and voice is already physically in the room.* Your auditory cortex is a pattern-finder with no input to check itself against. It will find your name. It will find your mother. It will find a choir. **This is not a message.** Decide now, in advance, that you will not come home with a revelation, so that when it happens you can say "ah, there it is" instead of "it spoke to me."
- **A presence.** The sensed presence — the certainty that someone is in here with you — is the standard Ganzfeld hallucination. In a room with empty body-sized niches, it will be *the tenant who never arrived.* It is your own temporoparietal junction, not a ghost.
- **Time loss.** Minutes will feel like hours or seconds. Your pulse is a clock that lies. **Leave when the watch says, not when you feel.**
- **The argument.** Here is the one that gets people. The room integrates. Everything that varies enters and is averaged and does not vary again. You are the only thing in here that changes, and the room is already relaxing back toward the value it had before you. To a tired person, that will not feel like indifference. It will feel like an offer. *You could stop being the transient. You could be finished too.*

  If you find yourself thinking you could stay — that is not the sign that you have arrived. **That is the abort signal.** Two tugs. Go up. Do not finish the thought; you can finish it outside, and it will be a much smaller thought out there.
- **Shivering means you are already late.** It is also the only reliable body-clock you have in a place with no events.

Bring one pre-commitment card, mentally, written on the surface: *I will not enter a tube. I will not take anything. I will leave at [elapsed]. If I want to stay, I leave immediately.* The person who wrote that card is the competent one. Deputize your partner to enforce it against the person you become in there.

## Coming out

The physical exit is the crux: uphill, through a hand-width slot, cold, numb, deaf, with your dose spent. Go slow. Exhale to move. Do not thrash. Have the line tended with steady gentle tension, not pulls. Expect to be scraped in places you cannot feel yet — the buzz has been an anesthetic and it will wear off in the sun.

Then the world will hit you, and it will be *offensive*. Gusting wind, directional light, your own shadow, events that happen and then stop. It will feel cheap. It will feel like noise. This is a normal perceptual hangover and not a judgment about your life, though it will present itself as one.

Immediately, at camp: shade, warmth, water with salt, food, sit down. **Do not drive. Do not fly. Do not shift for at least a day** — vestibular effects can outlast the visit. Nasal saline, lubricating eye drops, check your skin for abrasions you didn't feel, check your teeth with your tongue.

Photokeratitis, if the stored spectrum carries UV, declares itself six to twelve hours later as grit and pain in the dark. Know the signs; know where the nearest help is.

## The three days, and after

- **Audiogram at 24 hours and at three months.** The tone in your teeth for three days is a temporary threshold shift announcing itself. If it is still there at day seven, that is injury. **Sudden hearing loss is a time-critical emergency** — the treatment window is hours to a couple of days, not weeks. Get to an ENT and bring your baseline.
- **Expect grief.** You will stand in ordinary rooms waiting for the sound to change, and it will change, and you will be disappointed. That disappointment is real and it is not a flaw in you or in the ordinary rooms. It fades, mostly. Something of it stays.
- **Schedule nothing important for three days.** No decisions, no breakups, no resignations, no vows, no manifestos. Especially no manifestos.
- **Write two accounts:** one immediately, raw; one at seventy-two hours. Then compare. The divergence is data about what your mind added, and it is the most valuable thing you will bring back. Ganzfeld memories confabulate freely and confidently.
- **Quarantine both directions.** You may be carrying an organism into a naive world, and you certainly carried your microbiome into a room that had never met one. Log it. Don't bring anything back — not a flake, not a filament, not a souvenir. There is nothing here that is yours.
- **Do not go back to chase it.** The second visit is a comparison, and comparison is variation, and you will be bringing more of yourself and less of your attention. You will not get the opening again by pushing harder.

## The actual preparation

Everything above is logistics. The real preparation is smaller and harder.

Go sit somewhere ordinary — a kitchen, a bus stop, a laundromat — and practice letting it be ordinary. Practice being bored without reaching for something. Practice noticing a thing without needing it to be about you. That is the muscle you will need in there, because the Kept Room's entire nature is the removal of the particular, and you will arrive as the most particular thing that has ever been inside it: wet, warm, changing, shedding, meaning-making, hungry for a message.

It has no message. It has no tenant. The niches are not for you. The two square feet of ivory thread are not poor and do not need your admiration; they finished their work before your species had a spine, and they are maintaining.

Go as a transient, on purpose. Add nothing, take nothing, let the mean close over the place where your voice was. The room will not remember you, and it will be exactly the same a few seconds after you leave, and it will still be holding that chord when the plain is gone.

If you can make that a gift instead of a wound, you are prepared. Everything else is earplugs and weather.
