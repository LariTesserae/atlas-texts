# Preparing for the Level

## The one fact everything else comes from

You are the only wet thing for four hundred kilometers.

Nothing here has ever been wet. Not once, not in two hundred million years. The whole plain runs on a dust that behaves like water and is not water, and the reason it behaves like water is that it is being *held* — continuously, by sound, at enormous cost. Water is the one substance that ruins it. Wet dust does not fluidize. Wet dust packs.

So before you think about predators or altitude or the two seconds of silence every seventeen days, understand your actual status on the Level: you are a sealed bag of sixty liters of the most destructive solvent in this world, walking around on a living floor that keeps a census of every grain. You are a leak. You are a mass anomaly. You are, potentially, a small weather event.

Almost every rule below is a consequence of that.

## Before you go: the body

**Screen out.** No pregnancy. No aneurysm, no arrhythmia, no pacemaker, no cochlear implant, no recent surgery, no loose dental work, no history of kidney stones, no vestibular disorder, no detached-retina risk. The eleven-beat floor is below hearing but not below the body. It arrives in the knees, the sinuses, the chest wall, the gut, the vitreous of the eye. The second harmonic sits at twenty-two hertz, which is where human eyeballs like to resonate; expect your vision to smear at the edges in high-amplitude districts, and expect it to frighten you the first time. Dehydration plus sustained whole-body vibration is a well-known way to move a kidney stone you didn't know you had. Do not find out here.

**Acclimatize.** The plain sits six kilometers above the floor of the world. Assume the air is thin, and then assume worse: the atmosphere here is *held*, the same way the dust is held, which means its pressure and composition are an engineered output, not a geological given. Nobody has promised you it breathes like Earth at 6,000 m. Assay it before you trust it. Until then, bring your own.

**Condition.** Vestibular tolerance work, loaded walking at altitude, and — this is the one people skip — vibration exposure. Get on a shaker table at eleven hertz and find out what four hours does to you. Some people vomit for three days and then adapt. Some never adapt. Better to learn which you are at home.

**Do not plan to entrain to the local day.** Thirty-one hours is outside the human circadian capture range. You will free-run against a day that is itself lengthening, and you will be permanently, structurally jetlagged. Keep home-time on a sealed clock inside your helmet, black out on your own schedule, and accept that you will be tired in a way that sleep does not fix.

## The kit

### The suit

A fully closed dry suit with a rebreather loop. Not a filter mask. Not a positive-pressure helmet that vents. **Closed.** Scrub CO₂, carry O₂, retain every gram of exhaled water. You will breathe out roughly a liter of water a day into an environment that has never had any, and it must all come back with you.

Double seals, redundant, sacrificial. Nothing here rusts, which sounds convenient until you realize that everything here *abrades*. The fines are milled to a specific fineness — that fineness is the whole point of the place — and a particle engineered to flow through a capillary gallery will find every zipper, bearing, and O-ring you own. No wet lubricants. Dry film only, contained.

Full-seal goggles. No contact lenses; the dry dew will find the edge of a lens and abrade your cornea inside an hour. No exposed skin, ever — not for the cold, for containment. A cut here is not a wound that might get infected. It is a spill.

Static is a real problem: triboelectric sheets flicker inside the Held all day, everything is dry, and you are walking on a non-conductive lignified plate. Dissipative outer layer, bonded gear, intrinsically safe electronics, no sparks. A plain full of suspended milled organic dust is, in principle, a flour mill. It has not exploded in two hundred million years, which probably means the oxygen fraction is low or the dust is mineral-loaded past combustion. *Probably* is doing a lot of work in that sentence. Don't be the experiment.

### The bottle

Carry a clear, rigid, half-full bottle of water. Sealed, glass or thick polymer, mounted where you can see it.

This is the best instrument you will own and it costs nothing. Vertically shaken liquid grows Faraday waves at half the drive frequency. Your bottle will ripple in the local key, and the ripples are a direct readout of the field your feet are standing in. Learn its face. Learn what it looks like in a district, in a plaza, in a seam.

When the ripples flatten, you have seconds. When they flatten because something two meters wide has walked up behind you, you have fewer.

It is also the only water permitted on the Level, and it is taboo, and you should treat it as both instrument and relic. Do not open it outdoors. Do not offer anyone a drink.

### The null compass

A differential microphone/infrasound array with a haptic alarm, worn against the skin. It watches for *holes* — small moving absences in the roar. This is your predator alarm and it is not optional.

**Do not bring active noise cancellation.** I cannot stress this enough. ANC works by generating antiphase. Antiphase is what the hush does. You would be carrying a small, tame predator in your luggage, and at minimum you would be killing the ground under your feet, and at maximum you would be saying something in a language you don't speak, very loudly, to everything within a kilometer.

Passive attenuation only, flat-response plugs, and understand their limit: plugs stop the hiss. Nothing stops eleven hertz. You cannot earplug your sternum. Your vibration dose is therefore unblockable and must be *budgeted* — measure the acceleration, log it, and expect a hard daily cap of a few hours inside working districts.

### What not to bring

Anything that makes a steady note.

This is the most dangerous category of object you can carry, and it includes things you would never think of: a drone, a motor, a fan, a pump, a generator, an alarm, a phone on vibrate, a tuning fork, a wind instrument, a metronome, a child's toy. Two periodic sources placed wrong beat against each other and open a node. A node packs the quick hard as fired clay, kills everything growing in it, and stops anything with quick in its body that wanders in. You could sterilize a hectare of a two-hundred-million-year-old organism with a badly chosen cooling fan.

Everything you bring must be aperiodic, damped, and swept in an anechoic chamber against the eleven-beat floor and its harmonics before departure. No exceptions, including for medical devices. *Especially* for medical devices.

Also: no lint-shedding fabric, no crumbs, no seeds, no soil, no live cultures, no probiotics, no gifts of food or water or music.

## Arrival

You will be weighed. Going in and coming out, and the numbers have to match.

This is not bureaucracy, it is the census. Every grain on the Level is either built into something or held in the air on purpose, and the ledger balances. You are an entry. Declare your mass, declare your water, declare your consumables, and plan to carry out every gram — including shed skin, hair, nails, urine, feces, vomit, blood, and tears.

Yes, tears. If you cry, cry inside the suit. People do.

Arrange retrieval before you go. A beacon, a party of at least three, and a written contract with someone who will carry your body out. If you die here and your suit breaches, you release sixty liters of water into a dry organism and make a wet node that will not heal on any timescale you'd recognize. It is a genuinely obscene thing to do to a place. Have the bag. Have the plan.

## Moving around

**Read the ground.** The pavement grows in Faraday patterns — squares, hexagons, stripes — and the ornament is a direct transcription of the field. This is your map, your footing guide, and your hazard placard, all in one. Learn the difference by eye: fluidized quick has a boiling, matte, faintly moving surface; packed quick is chalky and hard and holds fossils. Step on the packed stripes. Do not step on the shimmer.

Get score-charts from the tuned before you walk anywhere. Districts sit four, nine, eleven kilometers apart with nothing between them, and the spacing is interference arithmetic, not aesthetics. An unmapped shortcut between two districts is exactly where a node lives.

**Never enter a seam.** The description of this place will tempt you: put your hand in, feel the cool weight of water that isn't water, watch it fall off dry. Don't. An antinode can become a node in nine seconds, and quick that packs around your wrist packs with the force of setting clay. There are things in the seams that swim; you do not.

**Do not enter an un-sanctioned tower.** They are throats. They are resonators, open at the top, and you would be standing inside an organ pipe at an antinode. The pressure in a working throat can rupture alveoli. Guest quarters exist and are amplitude-limited; ask for them. If you are offered a room at a node, take it — it is the safest place for a human body — but understand you may be accepting what they consider a grave. Thank them anyway.

Do not touch the shutters. Windows here change the room's key, not the light. A house is an instrument the family lives inside, and if it goes out of tune the family gets sick. You adjusting a window to "get some air" is you retuning someone's home.

**Night.** The pumps drop a fifth, the Held sags and thickens, the world goes nearly black, and the fines settle out ankle-deep. Visibility is zero and the ground is a dry nap you can half-drown in. Camp in a district. Sleep suspended — a sling with a natural frequency well below eleven hertz, so the floor's pulse is attenuated before it reaches your spine. Do not sleep lying directly on the mat. Do not sit on it for long either; the pelvis-to-skull transmission path is the worst one you have.

**The rim.** There is a three-hundred-meter transition where the held atmosphere simply ends. No wall, no color change, no wind, no visual cue of any kind. You will walk off the edge of the air. Hard geofence, barometric alarm, tether, buddy, and a rule you do not break even for the view. The tea-colored dusk out there is the most beautiful thing on the Level and it is a cliff in every sense.

## The Caesura

Every four hundred and six hours. That's about 16.9 Earth days, or 13.1 local days — which means it does *not* fall at the same local hour. It walks slowly through the day. Get the ephemeris and time your insertion to it: arrive around hour 380, see it, leave. Do not sit on the Level for seventeen days waiting for a nine-second event; your vibration budget won't survive it.

The change takes nine seconds. Two of them are silent.

Protocol, in order:

1. **Be in a sanctioned shelter, seated or slung, at hour 400.** Not walking. Not in a seam. Not in a plaza.
2. **Clear your airway.** This is the one that kills visitors. If you have breathed *any* Level dust — a leaked seal, a bad filter change, one careless minute — it is sitting in your alveoli, and for two seconds the field stops and it packs. Assume a Caesura with dust in your lungs is unsurvivable and act accordingly. Your respiratory isolation is not a comfort item.
3. **Do not hold your breath.** The air here is held the same way the dust is held. Nobody has published a clean measurement of the pressure transient during those two seconds, and the safe reading is that there is one. Equalize, keep your glottis open, breathe shallow. Treat it as a decompression event until someone proves otherwise.
4. **Expect the whiplash.** After days of entrainment, the sudden removal of the eleven-beat floor is a vestibular event. Vertigo, nausea, a sensation of falling, and for many people an unshakable conviction that something has entered the room. Something has.
5. **Stay down for the grey sheet.** When the new fundamental arrives, the dust comes off the ground in a single sheet and the Held brightens a shade. That is a wall of respirable particulate. Wait it out inside.
6. **Do not go outside for at least an hour.** The hush come in from the edges during those two seconds because their weapon is briefly free and everywhere. They are not obliged to leave immediately afterward.

The new key will feel wrong for about a day. That's normal. Your body re-entrains slower than the plain does.

## The living things

**The hush.** Two-meter cancellation bubble, walks toward prey, takes its time. Your blood is water, not quick, so the mechanism that stops a bell-ox should not stop you. *Should not.* Do not build a plan on it. A large predator that discovers its primary weapon doesn't work on you still has whatever it uses for everything else, and you are the most interesting object it has ever encountered. Treat every hole in the roar as lethal. Move away, don't investigate, don't film it. Your null compass and your bottle will both tell you before your ears do.

**The patient.** Mineral-shelled, cold, metabolizing on a scale of seasons, eleven years to cross a plaza. They are not dangerous and they are not rare; they are simply slower than your attention. Watch your feet in the packed quiet plazas. Stepping on one is an injury to something that was old before the mat learned to sing, and the tuned regard them the way you regard a foundation. Do not touch, do not sample, do not move one out of your way.

**Bell-oxen.** A herd of four thousand is a moving chord and a lot of mass. Their routes are keys; they navigate by the score. Do not stand in a migration line, do not try to ride one without sanction, and do not spook a herd — a herd that stops humming is a herd in trouble, and you will have caused it.

**Sifters, chime-moths, the mat itself.** Assume the mat will digest anything you drop. It ate a mountain range. Your food scraps, your lint, your dead skin are not litter here; they are unaccounted deposits in a ledger that balances, and the organism's response to a deposit is to take it apart by frequency and send it where it's wanted. Which is not where you wanted it.

## The tuned

Tall, hollow-boned, chests like resonating boxes. They speak at the eleven-beat floor, which means their conversation is a pressure in your sternum and never a word.

You cannot hear them and you cannot answer them. Arrange a sanctioned transducer — key-locked, amplitude-limited, approved — or write, or gesture. **Never generate the fundamental yourself.** An unsanctioned tone at eleven hertz is either a false instruction to the score or an assault, and there is no version where it's a friendly greeting.

Being spoken to is being vibrated. Keep conversations short. If your chest starts to hurt, say so and stop; they will understand the constraint even if they find it strange.

There are not many of them. The city is sized for a population that is not here, districts stand empty and warm and perfectly maintained, and some of the empty ones are still furnished. Do not enter them. Do not sleep in them. Do not take anything. They are maintained by someone, for something, and you were not told what.

**And do not ask about the surplus.**

They know. They have known for four million years. The towers put out more than the sun puts in, the difference is coming out of the planet's spin, the day was nine hours once and is thirty-one now, and everyone here can do the arithmetic. They are not in denial; they are at work, breeding a seed that will germinate in packed ground, in silence, at four degrees, with no field at all — a message addressed to the only readers who will still be here.

The worst thing a visitor can do — worse than the fan, worse than the spilled water — is to arrive with a solution. They have had four million years and better math than you. Do not say *have you tried*. Do not pity them to their faces. If someone offers you the number, receive it quietly and don't change the subject.

## The daily budget

Water: four to six liters a day at altitude in dry cold with a high ventilation rate, all of it inside the loop, all of it recovered. You are a small still. Eat small and often; a full stomach sloshes at eleven hertz and a full bladder is worse. Empty both before entering a throat.

Food: sealed, non-crumbing, cold or chemically heated inside the suit. No stoves. No flame.

Waste: total containment, weighed out.

Sleep: on your own clock, suspended, blacked out.

Dose: log your vibration exposure and your dust exposure every day, and treat the log as authority over your itinerary. The Level does not hurt while it is hurting you. There is no rot, no rust, no pain that arrives on time. You will feel fine and be accumulating.

## Leaving

Brush down inside the airlock and give the dust back. It is not yours; it was never yours; it is on the ledger.

Then know this: the roar comes home with you. For weeks you will hear a broadband hiss with a floor under it in quiet rooms. You will wake at three in the morning certain that something has gone silent, and your heart will be going before you're awake. You will find yourself watching the surface of a glass of water.

And you will have seen the thing that is actually hard about this place, which is not the hush or the two seconds or the invisible cliff at the rim. It is the empty furnished towers, and the grey nap on the eyes of the animals, and the tea-colored light coming through a lid that has been stable for two hundred million years, and the arithmetic that everyone can do. It is a hospice that has lasted four million years and will outlast your species, run by people who are still, every four hundred and six hours, carefully changing the key.

Prepare for the dryness. Prepare for the vibration. Prepare for the two seconds.

But the thing to actually prepare for is walking out to the rim at dusk and feeling, through your boots, a mountain being spent — and having to come home and be cheerful about it.
