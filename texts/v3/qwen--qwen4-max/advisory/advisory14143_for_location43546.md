# Preparing for The Lamp

## A briefing for the ordinary traveler

---

## 1. Understand what you are, before you decide to go

Everything else in this briefing follows from one fact: **you are a hole in a closed account.**

The Lamp is not a town that happens to be underground. It is a town that has decided to be a sealed vessel, and has organized six hundred years of law, guild, cuisine, art, and mourning around that decision. Mass in equals mass out. A birth requires a death. A needle that loses a grain of copper requires a grain of copper carried down from the hot world. There is no away.

You are, by contrast, a creature from places where things accumulate and wear out and get thrown away. You shed skin. You sweat salt. You breathe out carbon that was, a moment ago, their oxygen. You will eat their ancestors and drink their fog and, if you are not careful, leave a crumb on a ledge that a woodlouse — who has a summary line in the ledger — will have to deal with.

None of this makes you unwelcome. It makes you **an accounting problem with a face**, and the whole art of visiting is to be a problem so small and so well-documented that the Weight can close your line without a meeting.

The second thing to understand is what the place will do to your head.

There are two senses that don't tell you *what* is in front of you but *where you are*: the ambient sound field, and the direction of light. The Lamp removes both. Not dims them — removes them. The moss eats every echo; the plaster returns nearly all the light, hundreds of times, until brightness has no source and nothing casts. You will be able to see a snail's five whorls perfectly and have no idea how far away it is. You will be able to hear your own blood perfectly and have no idea whether anyone is standing behind you.

Every traveler's first hour is the same: standing on the Dish with your head back and your mouth open, turning slowly, looking for the bright place where the mouth must be. The children watch. It is the closest thing the town has to theatre.

**Let them watch. Be a good show.** It is the only gift you can give that costs no mass, and it will buy you more goodwill than any ingot.

---

## 2. Cross above. Never below.

Whatever you use to move between worlds: **open it on the moor, in daylight, well away from the ring of wrong grass, and walk in.**

Do not open an aperture inside the chamber. Three separate catastrophes:

- **It is a light source.** A glowing gate in a perfectly diffuse room is a hot spot, a gradient, a direction — and therefore it *casts*. You will show six hundred and twelve people, including infants, the thing their language has a word for and their world does not contain. There is no apology adequate to this.
- **It is an outlet.** The flask has no second opening. That is the entire reason the cold lake still lies there after a hundred thousand years, the reason the Veil sits at its fixed height, the reason the fog combs into water, the reason there is a city. A hole in the belly of the flask drains the winter out of it. You could kill the town in an afternoon while standing there admiring your own cleverness.
- **It is unweighed mass arriving.** That is a *breach*. The fast stutter goes out along every rail in the city into every palm, and the Throat gets sealed, and you spend the rest of your visit being a constitutional crisis.

The same logic rules out the well-meant suggestion you will be tempted to make within an hour of arriving: *"Have you thought about opening a second shaft? For ventilation?"* Do not say it. Do not say it as a joke. It is, in their terms, a proposal to murder everyone.

---

## 3. Be cargo, not a citizen

The town's name is its census is a knot on a cord, and it is **612**. It does not change. You must not become 613.

Arrange, through the porters, before you descend, to be entered as **manifest, not resident**: an item on the Scale with a declared mass, a declared duration, and a return date. You are a bale of goods that walks. This is not an insult; it is the only legal category that lets you exist there without breaking the constitution.

Practicalities:

- **Weigh yourself and everything you carry, at the lip, in the baskets.** Strip and weigh naked first; then kit. Record it. Get a copy, or a knot, or whatever the Weight gives you — and *ask what the knot means before you accept it*. Do not tie it yourself. A knot on that cord is a word, and some knots are persons.
- **Post a consumption bond.** If you eat their curd and drink their water, you will go up heavier than you came down, which means the town has lost that much substance forever. Cover it in advance: leave at the lip a mass of ordinary importable goods — hair, pine, bone — sized generously against your expected intake. You will not get the surplus back. That is the point.
- **Carry out everything you make.** Sealed, double-contained, pale, matte. Urine, feces, vomit, nail clippings, the hair that comes out when you scratch, the lint from your cuffs, the wrapper from your ration. All of it goes up with you. There is no line in the Weight for a foreigner's nitrogen, and their snails die on salt.
- **Have a death contingency, in writing, held above.** If you die down there, your mass stays, and the town must expel something equal — a tool, a room, a citizen's substance — to balance a stranger's corpse. Or they will compost you, and you will become heat, and then curd, and then a person, which is an honor you have no right to accept and a debt they have no way to refuse. Arrange extraction of remains in advance. Travel with someone who knows the arrangement and can sign it.
- **Ask for a gauging of the Ice Ear and the Slow Hand on your arrival and on your departure, entered in the Weight, with your name against both.** This sounds paranoid. Do it anyway. The Ice Ear is their alarm for the world; it has never changed; if it changes while you are there, you will be the cause, in every sense that matters to a town whose entire politics is four constants. Have the numbers. Have witnesses.
- **Do not get anyone pregnant.** A birth requires a death. The Counters keep the queue, and it is not a cruel office, and it is not a kind one. You would be ordering an execution.

## 4. Kit: pale, matte, soft, silent, sterile

### Wear white

Not for purity. For **albedo**. The Limewashers keep the reflectance at its number; every surface in that bulb is a contributor to a commons of brightness. A dark cloak is a hole in the commons. Worse: in a room with no shadows, a large dark object still *absorbs*, and what it makes is not a shadow but a soft, sourceless dimness that follows you around.

Their children draw people with a stain attached to their feet. Their adults find this genuinely upsetting and cannot say why. **You will be that drawing.** Wear undyed cream, bone, pale grey, chalk. Cover dark hair with a pale felted hood. Matte everything: no glossy shell fabric, no polished buckle, no glass, no mirror, no jewelry. A specular highlight is a hot spot, and a hot spot casts.

### Bring no light of any kind

No torch, no candle, no match, no lighter, no phosphor stick, no luminous watch dial, no phone, no camera with a screen, no flash. **Especially no flash.** A single flash photograph is the shadow thing again, and a smoky lamp takes a hand's-breadth of wall out of the commons for a generation, and the taboo on fire is not superstition — every child there can do the arithmetic better than you can.

If you must record, sketch. Learn their conventions first: line and flat colour, an art of edges. Do not shade. Do not render a shadow. Do not draw the thing that follows you.

### Quarantine like you mean it

The Hush is a living monoculture maintained by hand: moss over tufa over mycelium, with snails and furred mice as the pest crew. A foreign mold, a foreign mite, a boot-sole spore from another world's cave could take out the acoustic lining of an entire city. And their fauna has no defenses against anything, because nothing new has arrived there in six hundred years.

- Dedicated cave kit, **never worn on any surface of any world**, sealed in transit, put on at the lip.
- Felt-soled shoes, pale, grippy on wet flowstone.
- Do all your decontamination **above**, in the sun, where light is free.
- No soil, no seeds, no bark, no live plant, no animal. Not a dog, not a cat, not a bird, not a mouse in your pack. A single rat would be an extinction event with a ledger line.
- No food with a living culture in it. Nothing that could inoculate the mother-curd. **Never touch a vat.**

### No metal beyond what you declare

One cold-hammered copper needle per household, named, inherited, its wear recorded. A pocketful of steel tools is an obscenity of wealth and a scandal for the Weight. Use bone rings, hair loops, friction hitches. Declare every gram of metal you carry, including the fillings in your teeth if they ask, and they may.

### The rest

- **A pale probe staff**, bone or pine, a body-length. This is your replacement for depth perception. You will need it within ten minutes.
- **Earplugs, for the Throat only.** Remove them at the bottom; put them back before you ascend. In the chamber they do nothing but amplify your own blood by occlusion.
- **A wraparound opaque sleep mask**, pale outside. The light comes from every direction; a flat patch won't do.
- **A cough-cloth**, in local style, over a fine filter. Their air is packed: spores, gnat columns, moth scale, snail-shell dust. Assume you will develop something. Assume also that your exhale is a biohazard to them.
- **Foot care**: barrier balm, antifungal, spare felt insoles (declared). Nothing dries down there, ever. Your boots will be damp within the hour and stay damp until you leave. Keep the grease on your skin and off the walls.
- **A soft harness** with no hard hardware, and a tether you can clip to a hair-rail with a friction knot you have practiced a hundred times.
- **Nothing that rattles, clicks, beeps, or ticks loudly.** Not because anyone will hear it — because *you* will, and so will anyone within arm's reach, and a ticking watch pressed against a stranger's ear is an intimacy you did not intend.

## 5. Your body down there

**Cold, wet, forever.** Two or three degrees, ninety-five parts in a hundred of water. Everything is damp and nothing is wet. You will not get dry. You will not get warm. There is no fire, no boiling, no hot drink, no hot food, no drying room.

The only warmth in the city is the middens: fifty degrees of composting heat in the deep wall-cavities, rationed by the hour to infants and the very old, with a waiting list six hundred years long. **You will not get a slot. Do not ask. Do not queue-jump. Do not bribe.** Asking for warmth there is asking to take a baby's hour, in a room full of the dead. Bring your own insulation and be cold for the whole visit. It is survivable and it is the price of admission.

Two things nobody warns you about:

**You will steam.** They are cold-adapted, felt-wrapped, their skin near the air's temperature. You are a hundred watts of unsanctioned furnace at thirty-seven degrees in saturated near-freezing air. You will trail a plume of your own fog. Your breath will be visible. In the Veil, where the Combers work, you will be a small weather system moving along a ledge. It's another kind of stain. Move slowly, breathe shallowly, and don't stand still under the fog-nets, where your plume will thicken the gauze and change the yield on a frame that has been constant for longer than anyone has been counting.

**You are the loudest thing in the room and only you know it.** In the Hush, your own sounds have nowhere to go: your heartbeat, your joints, your gut, your chewing, the wet click of your swallow, the whine your own nervous system generates when there's nothing else to listen to. Some travelers find this unbearable in the first day. Prepare in an anechoic chamber if you can — increasing sessions, until you can sit calmly for an hour with nothing but yourself. Learn to like your own noise. You'll be spending a lot of time with it.

And the corollary, which is the thing that actually makes people cry: **you will not be able to hear yourself walking.** No footfall, no rustle, no echo of your own movement. The sense of being a body moving through a space, which you have never once noticed having, is simply gone. It is a bereavement. It passes.

### Food and water

Their water is fog-condensate, saturated with stone. It tastes of chalk and cold. It is the only water. Negotiate your allowance and pay for it; don't help yourself; don't put your hand in the basin.

Their food is curd, paste, pickle, pressed cake, snow-flea flour, sourness of great age. No fire means no cooking, and fermentation so slow that flavours take decades. It is genuinely excellent and it will wreck an unprepared gut. Bring your own rations for a first visit; eat their food only formally, in small amounts, as a guest.

Understand what you are eating. The dead go into the middens and come back as heat and then as curd and then as people. The mother-curd in the wall-vat has molecules in it that went in at the founding. If you are offered a spoonful, it is communion, and it is ordinary, and it is a person. Accept with a straight face or refuse with a straight face. And know that every mouthful you swallow and carry up the Throat is substance subtracted from a future citizen. This is why the bond matters. It is not a fee. It is a replacement.

**Salt is your enemy.** Your sweat, your tears, your urine — sodium has no line in that ledger and it kills snails and moss on contact. Don't sweat on the Dish. Don't weep on the felt. If you're going to cry, and you may, cry above.

## 6. The descent, and the rope

Eleven body-lengths of flue, on a hair-rope, in noise like a market. Cicadas come down the shaft and hit the walls and come back doubled and trebled. Your boot on the rock goes away from you and returns. Plugs in. Head covered. Move steadily.

Then you fall out of noise, and the change is so total that people have fainted. Have your tether sorted before it happens.

**The critical thing about the rope:** every handline, bridge-rail, hammock-sling and ladder in the city is spliced into one continuous tensioned system. It is a broadcast medium. Signals put in at the hub arrive in every palm in the town.

Which means **your rope work is speech.**

- Bounce three times on a handline and you have told six hundred and twelve people to eat.
- Load it in a fast stutter and you have called a *breach*.
- A long slow tremor means *lie down*, and you can cause an entire city to go to bed by accident.

Learn to move on cord without talking: smooth loading, no shocks, no drumming, no sudden take-up of slack. Better, on your first descent, **be lowered as cargo**: passive, still, hands off the line, a porter controlling everything. It is the correct posture for a stranger in every sense.

And: **do not tie knots in the cord. Do not cut it. Do not splice it.** The town's name is a knot. Some knots are people.

## 7. Retraining your eyes

Nothing casts. A hand held up in front of a wall does not put a hand on the wall. Depth has to be read from overlap, from texture, from the known size of a doorway. Infants there learn it late. Outsiders never learn it well.

This is not an aesthetic curiosity. **It is the main way visitors die.** The streets are ledges a body wide, spiralling, with ladders and slung bridges between them, thirty metres up the wall of a flask. A ledge edge against the wall below it has no shadow line. The void reads as texture. You will reach for a handhold that is a painted line, and step onto a surface that is a metre further away than it looks.

Drill before you go:

- **Three points of contact, always. Tether always.** No exceptions, not even for two steps, not even on the Dish.
- **Probe before you step.** The staff is not optional and it is not a walking stick; it is a sense organ.
- **Never walk while looking up.** Everyone does this in the first hour, because the ceiling is astonishing. Stop walking first. Then look.
- **Learn the standard dimensions.** Doorway height, scallop span, rail spacing, ladder rung pitch. Ask a porter to teach you the module of the town. Once you know one true size, you can scale everything else, and the world snaps into relief. This is the single highest-value hour of preparation you can do.
- Practise navigating by touch, blindfolded, in a familiar space, until your hands are competent.

Also prepare for the visual *rest* problem: it never gets dark. When the slot stops giving, the mycelium comes up to nearly the same brightness in cold green, over hours, imperceptibly. Nobody there has ever seen it get dark. Your body will not get a night signal, ever. Bring the mask; bring melatonin if that works for you; expect your sleep to free-run and plan for it. Otherwise, **sleep when the cord says sleep** — the long tremor from the hub. Sync to the town, not to your own drifting animal.

And **sleep in contact**: palm on the rail, soles against the floor, as they do. Not for the music, though you'll get the music. Because the alarm is tactile. A traveler who sleeps detached from the cord sleeps through a breach. There is no shout that will wake you. Nobody can hear you and you can hear nobody. Detachment is the only true solitude in that city and I suspect they have a word for it that is close to their word for death.

## 8. Manners: a short and load-bearing list

**Speech is intimacy.** Mouth to ear, at the distance of a hand on a shoulder, breath more than tone. Whispering to a stranger is roughly equivalent to kissing them. Whispered thanks, whispered apologies, whispered pleasantries: all advances. Don't.

**Shouting is worse than useless.** At two paces it is a puff of warm air on someone's cheek and nothing else. You will be breathing on a person you cannot address. It reads as assault.

**Sign is public and cannot be hidden.** Eleven thousand shapes, shoulder grammar, six hundred years old, legible everywhere in that shadowless light. There is no corner where a hand can hide. Therefore: *sign is for facts and promises; speech is for secrets and love.* Don't whisper a commitment. Don't sign a tenderness. Learn two hundred shapes and the shoulder grammar properly rather than a thousand badly, and learn the polite forms first.

**Touch is the traffic law.** Descending give way, enforced by a hand on the hip. Strangers will touch your hip. It is not aggression and it is not flirtation. Practise not flinching. Learn to do it to others, gently, with the flat of the hand.

**Announce by touch, never by voice.** People arrive silently, always. Everyone there has a startle discipline you don't have. Come into arm's reach, touch a shoulder, *then* speak. Never breathe on the back of an unaware neck.

**Don't lean. Don't lounge.** The felt must be exactly a hand's length. Compress it and stone shows through and *rings* — you have made an acoustic wound, a squeak-hole, a defect in a public work that a Cropper must climb out and repair. Sit where you're shown. Keep your palms on rails, not on walls.

**Gloves on, hands off the white.** Skin oil on lime plaster is a hand's-breadth of the commons gone for a generation. Never touch bare plaster. Never touch the Slow Hand — you would corrupt the only thing permitted to change, the record, the calendar. **Never breathe on the Ice Ear.** Your exhalation is thirty-seven degrees and saturated; a single sigh at a cupped-hand-sized lobe of ice that has not changed in centuries could change the town's alarm for the world and start a schism. Never put your hand in the basin.

**Do not disturb the citizens.** The fell-bird has a ledger line; harming it is murder. The snails are livestock. The spiders are farmed. The moths are silk and are caught with two fingers by children — swatting one is kicking a lamb. The harvestmen knot in the scallop-corner, twenty legs to a fist, is never disturbed. Don't brush it away. Don't kill a centipede; there is exactly one way to be poisoned here and it is on a ledger line, and you should be unfailingly polite to whoever holds it.

**Have no opinion about the depth of the felt.** There are factions. There have been schisms. A foreigner's casual remark can found a heresy. If pressed, defer to the Croppers and change the subject to something safe, like the weather, of which there is none.

**Don't ask about the queue.** Don't ask who's next. Don't joke about it.

**Don't pity them, and don't call it timeless.** It is not a dead place. It is an enormously busy one, every hand working all the time at holding a thing still. The correct compliment is about the *work*. Admire a Cropper's nap. Admire the Limewashers' number. Admire the rope-players, and mean it — there are critics, and they are good.

## 9. Gifts, trade, and the most dangerous thing you can give

**Bring hair.** Clean, cut, your own, quarantined. Their entire cordage economy runs on it and a traveler's head is a fortune in handline. It is ordinary, importable, weighable, and it does not distort anything.

**Be careful with copper.** It is tracked by the grain, per needle, per household. A kilo of it dropped into that economy is not generosity, it is a monetary event. Modest, declared, entered. Let the porters handle it.

**Buy properly.** Frost-saxifrage is the real export: cold-flower, white and star-shaped, faintly bitter, sold to the fever-houses of the hot plain for its weight in copper. That is a legitimate channel with a legitimate balance. Silk cord, perhaps. Take nothing else. Not a shell, not a moth, not a scraping of plaster, not a chip of anything, and above all **not a knot**. Every gram is a citizen's substance, and cutting a knot off that cord is unregistering a person.

**Give news through the proper door.** The Listener is a salaried office held by four families since the founding, and what they mostly report is *nothing*, and the town pays them for it, because a thing that is always nothing still has to be checked. Sit on the bone seat. Report in signs. *Rain in the west. A stag. Thunder.* You may add a line. Let the town decide whether to hear it.

And know that **description is the most dangerous gift you carry.** They have never seen dark. They have never seen a shadow. Their word for shadow is a porter's loanword, and there is a single word in their language that means *the thing that follows you and that we do not have*. You come from a world of evenings and echoes and sunsets and things that wear out. Telling them about it is either poetry or blasphemy and you will not be able to tell which, in advance, from the look on a face you cannot read the shading of.

Give it sparingly. Give it to porters, who already have ears for the world. Do not offer improvements. Do not offer a lamp, a chimney, a mirror, a drum, a bell, a dye, a second shaft.

## 10. Leaving, and afterwards

**Departure is an audit.** You go up through the Scale, and everything is weighed again — you, your kit, your sealed waste, your souvenirs — and the line is closed against your bond. Budget hours for this. Do not rush the Counters. If it does not balance, you may be held, and the holding will be entirely reasonable and possibly indefinite.

**Plugs in before the Throat.** After days in the Hush your central gain will have wound itself up hunting for signal that never comes. The doubled cicada racket in that flue will be physically painful. Ascend slowly. Stop on the bone seat if you need to. The porter will not think it strange; they sit there all day.

**Then plan for the surface, which is the harder crossing.**

The world above is hot and loud and *directional*. The first echo will feel like being shouted at. The first hard shadow will look like a stain attached to your feet, following you, and you will understand exactly why their adults find the children's drawings upsetting and cannot say why. The first sunset will be a thing happening to you rather than a thing you are watching. And the first night — real night, with a source, and a dark side to every object — will be the loneliest you have ever been.

Stage it. Sunglasses and earplugs for the first day. A dim quiet room for the first week. Don't schedule anything important. Expect grief that seems disproportionate to a short visit; it isn't. You have been somewhere that removed two of the senses you use to know where you are, and replaced them with a hand on a rail and music coming up through the bones of your wrist, and it was *very good*, and there are critics, and you will miss it in a way you have no vocabulary for.

---

## If you remember only five things

1. **Cross above. Never open anything below the lip.** A gate down there is a light, a shadow, and an outlet, and any one of the three is a catastrophe.
2. **No light, no fire, no dark clothing, no polished surface.** You are a guest inside a lantern that has agreed never to strike a match.
3. **Tether, probe, three points, never walk while looking up.** The absence of shadows kills more visitors than anything else.
4. **Everything you take in must go out again, including you.** Be manifest, not census. Bond your consumption. Carry out your waste. Have a plan for your corpse.
5. **Touch to speak, sign to promise, palms on the rail to sleep.** And when you leave, if the Listener asks what you saw out there in the loud hot world, the kindest and most accurate thing you can report is *nothing* — and mean it as the compliment it is.
