# The Attendance

## 1. It comes as weather

It does not open the hill. It does not stand on the fourth terrace at dusk with the terraces glowing beneath it, and it does not announce itself to the trade in a dream, because a thing announced in a dream gets carved into a lintel and then nobody can change it.

It comes the way everything that touches this room comes: from outside, briefly, and out of breath. It finds the place by listening, through the joints of the paving, through the soles of its feet. It puts its hand in the slot worn into the slab. It goes down the steps, arris-sharp under the palm, and it takes the lamp on the cord, because a power that will not use the local instruments has no business having an opinion about a place.

Full flame at the top step. Small at the third. It stops at the third, with the flame dying at its knee, and it accepts the ten minutes.

That is the first decision and the largest one. Everything that grazes here has to leave. The power does not exempt itself.

## 2. The refusals

It has been benevolent a long time, and it has learned that the hard part is not the wanting. The hard part is that most kindness is a kind of masonry: it fills the void you were leaving.

**It could cool the water.** Thirty-nine at the throat down to something a body can stay in. Then the lime keeps its cargo, the whey runs clear, the flour never falls, the dogtooth never plates, the copyist stops with its work half done. The room becomes a bath. A very fine bath, warm and bright and loud, sterile forever, with a rope on the floor that stays a rope until the wood finally softens and there is nothing left of it at all. No block. No street. No holes. It declines.

**It could carry the sweet away.** A second shaft, a draught across the freeboard, the invisible lid lifted off the pot. But the draught would take the fog the fern drinks, and dry the condensation run that the velvet lives on, and shift the plume, and cool the surface, and change every wrinkle that makes the lace. The gas is not an intruder in this body. It is the whey's breath. You cannot take an animal's breath away and call it a rescue. It declines — and remembers, while declining, that this is the resident that kills people, and that declining is therefore not free.

**It could send something down to eat the algae.** A snail, a grazer, anything to stop the sloughing that gathers in the south corners and makes the dark seam that fails the block. But nothing that grazes can live at thirty-six in sulphur; the grazer would have to be a thing that comes down, feeds in agony, and dies. It will not buy a clean block with an animal's suffering. It declines.

**It could pull the iron.** The one wound, bleeding red into all that white. But the skin is already walling it under glass, which is precisely what a body does with a splinter, and it is the only colour in the room that means *something happened here*. To take it out would be to tidy. It declines.

**It could knock the boss off the fourth pier**, or decide, in the small hours, in some mason's sleep, that four holes are better than three. This is the temptation it feels most, because it is the cheapest and looks the most like wisdom. But the undecided capital is the living part. Three hundred years of variation and selection are lying all around in the terraces, and the whole reason the trade is any good is that nobody ever handed it the answer. A power that decides for the lineage ends the lineage and leaves only a style. It leaves the boss on.

**It could stop the filling.** Hold the weir where it is, keep the scribed line true, keep the level from creeping its two fingers a year. Save the room. This is the one it wants. A hidden bright roaring hot white box, going on and on, the net running over clean plaster forever, the note booming and holding, the fourth pier unfinished forever, and every year the same year. It would be a mercy, and it would be the exact murder of the thing it came to be kind to, because the thing it came to be kind to is *an absence being made precise*, and its whole life is its ending. It declines, and it stays a while with the wanting.

Every mercy it can think of is a mercy to one layer and a killing of another, and the seven layers cannot tell that the others exist. A power that acts on one of them is not a benefactor. It is a partisan with a long arm.

## 3. What is actually wrong

Only one thing here is not indifferent, and it is the power, and the only deaths in this room are ours.

The ledger has them. Not as accidents — as arithmetic. Two hundred descents, ten minutes each, a day and a half of human presence, and the breathable place is the drowned place and the drowned place is cooking you, and the sweet creeps up the steps on still hot days toward the man who is tired and has one more joint to check. Some of them went down alone. Some of them were the best ear in the valley and stood on the third step sounding the pier with the lamp out at their knee, listening for the semitone, and did not notice that the room had gone quiet in the other way.

The power reads the names. It cannot undo them and it does not pretend that reading them is a repair.

Then it does the only useful piece of reasoning available to it. *This place copies nouns and loses every verb.* Stone will take the rope, the mallet, the root, the stain, and it will not take the note, the net, the knock, the gulp, the ten minutes, the fear, the names. Nothing with a behaviour gets a likeness here.

But there is a second lineage on this hill, and it copies behaviours. Throat bore, pier spacing, weir height, the scribed line, the way you hold a mallet, the way you test the air. Habits vary and the ones that work get copied again. Three hundred years of that is the ground the shell is bedded on.

So the gift cannot be a miracle. Miracles are not heritable; they get carved into a lintel and then misremembered. The gift has to be a habit good enough that people who have never heard of the power will keep it because it works, and teach it to their apprentices, and be annoyed when the apprentices do it wrong.

The stone keeps nouns. The trade keeps verbs. That is the only medium in the room that will take a verb, and it is standing at the top of the steps, shouting a little, humming without knowing.

## 4. Three small things

**The line and the song.** The room already puts its note inside the diver's skull — near ninety in the water, low, singable for exactly as long as you can stand the heat. The power makes a rule of what the room is doing anyway. Down you go on a tended line, and you sing the note, and you keep singing it, and the tender at the lid keeps a hand on the line and the verse in his mouth and feels your voice come up through the fibres like a purr through a cat's ribs. When the singing stops, he hauls. Not when he is worried. Not when he thinks you have had enough. When the singing stops. Ten minutes is four verses, and the fourth verse is the one you turn back on, and there is no negotiation about the fourth verse, and the tender is not permitted to be the best ear in the valley or to have opinions about how full the room is.

It costs the room nothing. It adds no resident, alters no chemistry, takes not one grain off the four tonnes. It uses a resident that was going to die of being finished anyway, and it makes that resident's last years useful.

**Count the knock.** The sump self-siphons and the interval shortens as the void shrinks: forty minutes when the water came in, less now, less next year. The masons already know this and use it to guess how long the room has to run. The power gives them the other half of the inference, the half that is not about money: *when the knock comes at half its old interval, the sweet is standing higher than you think it is, and the freeboard you are breathing in is not the freeboard you drew.* Scratch the interval on the underside of the lid each time you close it. Date it. The room's own pulse becomes the warning, in the room's own units, and no instrument has to be invented, bought, maintained, or trusted.

**Leave the holes.** This one it puts in last and deepest, and it does not put it in as engineering, because as engineering it is false and would be argued away in a generation. It puts it in as a superstition, which is what a long-lived instruction usually has to be. *Leave the mason's holes. The wall breathes.* Don't point up the putlogs. Don't plug the beam sockets when the beam rots out. Don't fill the rope-cast in the sill; that's what it's for, thread a rope through it. It is willing to be a wrong reason held for four hundred years rather than a right reason held for thirty.

Then it goes up. It goes up too fast, with its pulse in its teeth, and it stands in the ordinary air at the top of the hill humming a note it does not know it is humming, colonised like everybody else, and it lets that happen, because that is the mechanism. That is the whole mechanism.

## 5. What it gives the thing itself

Before the ten minutes are up it does the one act that is not for the masons and not for the town and not for the swifts.

It stands on the third step with the flame small at its knee and it counts all seven columns at once.

A fifth of a second: a loop of the lace opens on the second pier and closes. Four seconds: the note the throat has been feeding since spring, twenty-one below hearing in the air, the sternum's own pressure. Forty minutes, shortening: the sump. Three days: the sweet, gathering toward its enormous slow gulp. A month of rain on a ridge nobody from this valley has stood on, arriving as the density of the whey. A season of lip, two fingers a year, the skin advancing into the velvet's country. Three hundred years of shed shells, terraced and planted, holding up the fig.

Nothing in the room can hold those together. The light does not know there is stone. The stone does not know there is light. The voice has a memory four seconds deep and the lace has no memory at all, and the skin is blind, and the knock is one organ doing one thing, and none of them has ever been in the presence of the others.

For a moment they are in the presence of something that can count them. Late in the afternoon, when the bar has walked to the steps and the net goes long and orange and the vault takes over the lighting, the gulp arrives as the sump lets go, at the end of a four-second decay, in the fifth of a second when a particular loop of white fire closes on the arris of the third step. Nothing mystical. Just the phases passing through each other, as they must, often, whether or not anyone is there.

Someone is there. That is the entire gift: an accurate census, taken by something that could read all seven columns, of a body that has never once been counted whole. Not rescue. Not one extra second of life. It does not extend the room by a heartbeat, and it would not if it could, and it is not certain the room would call this a kindness, and it does it anyway, because being truly counted is the only thing it has that no layer of this place can supply for another.

Then it goes up and leaves the lid off a while, so the cold can fall down the rings and thin the sweet out for whoever comes next.

Years later it comes back for the breaking, which is the only ceremony worth attending: the shell cracked open along the courses, the terraces gaining one more skin, the block coming out solid and silent with a hole where the fourth pier was — three, they decided three, and the power did not decide it — and the note that has not stopped since the water first came over the sump stops.

It listens to the last four seconds. It learns them.

It does not save the voice. It learns it.

## 6. Coda

The block goes down the hill and stands in a street holding up somebody's floor. Inside it: a stone rope with a rope in it, exact to the lay of the strands; a lump with a mallet in it; a pale root, finger-thick, its tip still white; a red stain sealed under glass; the dark seam in the south corner, which the mason saw and split around, because the trade knows about seams now and has known for a long time and cannot say who first said it.

The beam rots out in a hundred and forty years and nobody points up the socket, because the wall breathes, everyone knows that. A swift goes in. Bees take the putlogs in the second course. Somebody threads a rope through the rope-cast in the sill and hangs a line of washing across the street, and the rope in the stone and the rope in the air are the same rope, four hundred years apart, and no one notices, and it does not matter that no one notices.

On the hill there is a new shell, bedded on the old ones, and a woman at the lid with her hand on the line and a verse in her mouth, four verses, and the fourth one is the one you turn back on. She learned it as a counting rhyme before she knew what it was for. It has been mis-copied twice; there is a version sung at weddings that makes no sense; in one valley they leave the holes and in the next they point them up neatly and are proud of it. The power accepts this. Heritability is not fidelity. It is the only kind of continuation that was ever on offer here.

And in the woman's mouth, without her knowing, in the ordinary air, at the top of a hill made of shed skins, there is a low note near ninety that has not stopped for years, and did not stop when the void closed, and is not going to.

Nothing lives in the water. The power did not change that.

Everything, eventually, lives in the holes. The power left them open.

And the one thing that was never going to be copied went out of the room in a mouth, and is still going, and has not stopped once.
