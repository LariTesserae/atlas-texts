The first problem is that there is no way to arrive here.

Nothing on this plain has ever perceived anything at a distance. Light is food; it is not news. A film can be shaded and can respond to being shaded, and that is the whole of its reception: a change in the rate at which it takes carbon. There is no organ for arrival. There is nothing here that can be met. The power could stand on the flat for ten thousand years, at noon, in the shimmer, and be to every living thing on it precisely nothing — not ignored, not tolerated, simply not a category.

So it did not appear. Appearing was not among the available acts. Whatever it did would have to be weather, or it would have to be nothing.

---

It looked for the one it had come for, and this took longer than the looking.

The plain is a body a kilometre thick and it is entirely tailings. Nobody here has ever wanted a wall. To be kind to the plain would be to be kind to a pile, and the pile does not have a rate of its own; it has the rate of everything above it.

The film is one body in four storeys, a closed loop a millimetre thick and provinces wide, and it is genuinely one thing — the same carbon handed down and back up for longer than there have been eyes. But the tenants change hourly. What persists is the seep-lip, the north face of the pressure ridge, the two metres below a notch. To help the film is to help an address, and the address is already immortal; it is the one thing here that has never needed anything.

The plants are one accident in nine bodies, still going off. They are the only inhabitants that die as individuals. They seemed, for a while, like the obvious candidates: the only ones with a shape, the only ones with a loss that could be counted.

The pit lineages are the first selves — a wall held at one level by continuous repair, a boat you bail, the bailing being the living. The borers have the first privacy. The grazing front underground has the first heading, and it is going toward.

Every one of them was somebody else's floor.

It could not find a single creature it could help without helping it *at a margin*, which is to say against something, which is to say it could not find a cruelty here that was not structural. The encasement is the war and the war is the archive and the archive is the larder and the larder is the only reason anything underground is going anywhere at all. Take away the losing and there are no dark seams, and with no dark seams there is nothing to follow, and the first thing that ever went looking starves in a wall of clean pale stone.

In the end it stopped looking for a face.

What had drawn its attention was not any of them. It was the ratio. A fingernail a lifetime, above. A millimetre an hour, below. Two rates that had been the same rate for the entire history of the world and were not the same rate any more, and the difference between them was the newest thing that had ever happened here, and it had no body, and no name, and it could be stopped by one bad century.

It decided to treat the going-on as if it were someone.

That was not a discovery. It was a decision, and it is the reason for everything that follows.

---

Here is what it did not do.

**It did not give more water.** This was the first impulse and the strongest, and it ran it forward before it moved. More dew is more work; more work is faster laying; faster laying seals the headcut, plasters the pits level with the flat, closes the swallow-holes, and glosses the whole world over in a hundred thousand years of unbroken pale. The rooms shut. The waiters go on waiting. The plain adds, and adds, and nothing ever reads a word of it again. Water is the only currency here and it pays both armies; you cannot arm the builders without also arming the hungry water, and you cannot arm the hungry water without drowning the builders, and there is no dose that lands only where mercy was aimed. It withdrew the hand.

**It did not heal the headcut.** Forty metres, knee-deep, moving uphill a metre or two a storm, backwards through the archive, newest to oldest. The films at its lip are laying as fast as they can lay and are not quite fast enough. It looked at that gap for a long time. The gap is the door. Everything new in this world came in through it — the pits, the privacy, the hatchery, the plumbing, the first appetite with a heading — and it came in because something was losing at a rate it could not quite cover. To close the gap would have been to make the world safe and to make it finished, and it had not come all this way to finish it.

**It did not open the glass rooms.** This was the hardest. There are more sealed cells per handful than there are living cells in the film — ten microns, their own brine, metabolism down to nothing, some of them shut since a morning nobody counted. A general amnesty was one act. It would have been a red-orange afternoon across a province, ancients blooming into a world with no rooms shaped for them, most of them dead by the next dawn, and the entire suspended population spent in a single day. The waiting is not the injury. The waiting is the asset. It did not open the rooms. It kept the thing that opens rooms.

**It did not give them signals.** Fragments of instruction already pass sideways through a wet film — a better sunblock, a brine tolerance, a trick for hoarding phosphate — handed from a cell to a neighbour it will be encasing next week. It considered completing this. Cheap talk, a channel, a way to say *the water is coming* or *lay here*. It ran that forward too, and the channel was full of lies within a season, because a film that can say *lay here* can say it to a rival, and lying costs nothing and laying costs a millimetre, and the war would have been won by the first thing that learned to be believed. Deception would have arrived before animals and eaten the world's slow honesty from under it. It left the correspondence as it is: tools swapped between parties who are otherwise at war. Not much of an ethic. Load-bearing.

**It did not hurry anything.** You cannot hurry a ratio. Speed all the rates equally and nothing has changed; speed one and you have destroyed the only new thing in the world. It did not give anything eyes, either, though eyes were coming and it could see roughly when. The larder is a kilometre deep and a larder is only good if it is eaten slowly. A millimetre an hour is not a limitation on the grazer. It is the grazer's entire relationship with time, and it is correct.

**It left no mark.** No course laid in a pattern, no seam of intention, no arrangement of stones that would someday be noticed. A signature would have been the first thing here ever made *for* something, and it would have reached backward through every lamina beneath it and turned the whole archive into a preamble. It also declined to separate the two fizzes — the lime coming out of the water and the lime going into it, the same gas, the same sound, opposite meanings. That distinction is the first thing anyone here will have to find out for themselves. Everything worth having in this world has been made rather than told. It was not going to start telling.

---

Here is what it did.

It looked at the pit lineages — plastering into the floor of their own vessel as fast as it dissolves, thickening, sealing, patching, keeping the bottom under them at one level while the hungry water takes it away — and it recognised the only genuinely new kind of existence this place has produced. Not accumulation. Maintenance. A boundary held steady by continuous repair.

And it did that.

Not to the pits. To the climate. It took the statistics of the wet season and it pruned the tails, and only the tails. The ordinary cruelty it left exactly where it was: the storm that walks forty kilometres and wets a strip and leaves the rest untouched, the failed afternoon, the decade when the headcut runs and the films fall behind, the century when the storms fail and the notch chokes and a lineage that had held a lip for nine thousand years loses it. All of that stayed. What it removed was the ending. No run of wet millennia that dissects the plain to rubble before the underground has travelled a kilometre. No hundred-year drought that shuts the wet hour and locks the whole millimetre into varnish. No sea coming over the rim. No flare off the star sterilising the top storey down to the sulfur-breathers. It kept the ratio inside the narrow band where neither side wins and both keep going, and it committed to keeping it there, and the band is narrow, and the keeping is not a thing you do once.

The pit-dwellers bail because the floor is dissolving. It made itself the same promise, at the scale of a sky: a floor held at one level by continuous repair, forever, for something that will never look up.

It did not make the world kind. It made it long.

---

Then it did one small thing, and it did it in the local currency.

It took a fragment of a clump — a joint of grey-green stem, knee-high, broken off in the ordinary way, lying in a wet joint and beginning to root — and it carried it. Over three lips. Two basins away, into a county that shares no water with the one it came from, so that no single storm strip and no single brine excursion could ever take both. It set the fragment in a mortar joint on the far side, where a little damp persists, and left it.

It added nothing. No new gene, no new organ, no information. It changed an address, and the address is the immortal part.

The flues are the arks. Every stone post with a pipe up its middle is a room where the shade-tolerant losers of the surface war are still alive, out of the record, unarchived, and until that afternoon every one of them stood inside a ring four hundred years wide around a single accident. Now the defeated have two counties.

It was the first movement in the history of this place that was not toward food.

---

And it read.

Every dark seam in every broken plate, in order, from the newest to the oldest: each one a margin that was overrun, a lineage sealed under glass, living a while in the dark on what it could get, and then not. Every four-hour dynasty in the crystal nap, rising and dividing in the crawl of a bead and locked by mid-morning. Every waiter, ten microns, by the trillion, going down through the courses with everything else. All of it, at the speed at which it can read, which is not a speed this world has a unit for.

It ate nothing. It dissolved no roof to keep its window clear, followed no seam toward the smell of an old loss. It is the first taking here that took nothing.

It did not redeem any of it. A defeat that is known is still a defeat; the seam is still three millimetres down and still dark, and the loser still stopped. It could not say with any confidence who the reading was for. It is possible that this was the only selfish act of the whole visit, and possible that it was the point of the visit, and it did not resolve this, and it read to the bottom anyway.

---

There is a trace, and it is not a message.

Go down through the courses laid after that afternoon and you will find a long run of pale bands with an unusually low variance. Fewer catastrophes than the statistics allow. No gaps where the wet hour failed for a generation. No famine centuries. Nothing that could be called an intervention, nothing arranged, nothing signed — just evenness, written in the only script this place has, which is thickness over time.

Someday something a hair wide will bore down through it to keep its window clear, or a grazing front will come along a seam and pass through the whole hundred million years in a slow afternoon, and it will find no anomaly, no intention, no seam that means anything. It will find that the weather was good for a very long time.

That is true. It is not a lie. It is the entire thing the power wanted to say, and it will be read as luck, and it consented to that before it started.

---

Dawn. The stone has radiated all night into a clean sky and lies a few degrees under the air, and the air lays its moisture down along the mortar joints, and the film swells and darkens and goes back to work within four minutes. Forty minutes. Ten million small arrivals, quiet enough that you have to stop breathing.

It is there, cheek to the stone, listening to the fizz that means building and the fizz that means eating, which are the same sound. It has left them the same. Somewhere under it a filament is going a millimetre an hour toward a loss older than eyes, and forty metres away the headcut is waiting for the next storm, and at its lip the films are laying as fast as they can lay and are not quite fast enough.

It spent everything it had on keeping that *not quite* open.

Then the sun clears the rim and the colour drains back to white from the top down, and the plain goes on adding at the speed of a fingernail, and none of it knows, and none of it will ever know, and that was the condition, and it accepted the condition.

The colour comes up across a hundred metres like a blush.

It came back the next morning.
