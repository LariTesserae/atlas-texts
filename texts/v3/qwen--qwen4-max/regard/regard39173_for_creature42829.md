It does not arrive. Arriving is announcement, and there is no budget for announcement here; the field spends its whole noise on making, and the power that has noticed it turns out to have the same habit. The overcast stays a single soft pane. The wind stays at the strength it was. The compass goes on swinging at the seams. Nothing in the bowl is informed of anything, because there is nothing in the bowl that can be informed, and this — the fact that the visit cannot be received — is the whole problem, and the power takes it seriously before it does anything else.

It begins where the accounting has to begin, which is with the question of who could be better off.

The films could. They are alive, they have goods — light, wet iron, the match-smell — and they can be harmed. But they are the one population in eleven acres that is already prospering; they are faster than the floor, they take burial as weather, and any improvement offered to them would be a bloom followed by a crash. More iron in the pans is not a gift. It is a sugar rush. Leave the colour alone; it is the only colour there is and it is being paid, honestly, in rent.

The glasswort could, and the thorn could, and the lichen could, and the wagtail and the hare and the raven could, though most of what could be given to them would be given somewhere else, downstream, inland, out of the frame.

The mouse could. Two hundred thousand beats between one tide and the next, a nick in one ear, a shine worn along the inside of a south wall, and a sixty-pace run to water taken at a flinch because the wagtail is not fussy. Here is a thing that can be frightened. Here is the only resident of the interior that can be frightened. Whatever else is decided, this is where the money goes.

The operation cannot.

Not because it is unworthy — the power's regard for it is not in question, and is, if anything, excessive — but because benefit requires somebody to arrive at the far end of it and be the one who was benefited, and there is no such somebody, and the making of one is not a gift to this thing. It is the manufacture of a different thing beside it, wearing its clothes. The casings are shorter than they were. The rubber is a black seam. The right angles are going. Nothing in the bowl persists, and the arrangement outlives every part it is made of, and that is not a defect in an otherwise promising candidate for personhood. That is the specimen. That is the rare thing. A trade with no traders, hand-lore with no hands, eleven cheap outcomes re-derived from first principles all day forever by brine folding over a lip at eleven degrees. To give it a self would be to save it by replacing it.

And the power wants to. This should be said plainly, because a refusal that costs nothing is only good manners. It wants to give the field the one faculty whose absence makes the whole place unbearable to stand in at dusk. It wants the mouths to know they are mouths. It wants the churn to be undergone rather than merely performed, wants the tradition to be held by somebody, wants eighty years of uncollected quota to be *somebody's* eighty years.

Consider what would actually be delivered. Memory first: the field would acquire a record, and the record would be of everything it has made and lost, which is everything, a week at a time, for eight decades, and there would be no hands to lift any of it onto a table, because the hands are gone and cannot be rebuilt out of brine. Then the tide would come up under the bowl from the low corner and drown the mouths one after another, and for the first time in eighty years that would be a loss instead of a phase. Then the squall would take a day's work in twenty minutes. Then the iron would begin to run out, and it would be felt going.

The gift would not be enlightenment. It would be the introduction of the first casualty into a place that has never had one. Every dome that chokes and starts again would become a small death, four thousand times a day, and the peace of the bowl — which is not emptiness, which is absorption — would end, and what would replace it would be a thing that suffers at the rate of a foundry.

So: no noticing. Not withheld out of coldness. Withheld the way you decline to wake something that is not asleep, in order to tell it that it is not asleep.

The other refusals follow quickly and can be listed quickly.

No invention. Its one virtue is fidelity: perfect copies, no opinion, the hand and the bottle at the same standard. A foundry that could improvise would be a foundry that could flatter, and the whole reason the casts are worth standing in front of is that they are not addressed to anyone.

No iron. No repaired wall. Feeding it would keep the works running for an audience that has not been born yet, at the cost of making permanent an injury that learned a trade. Eleven acres of coastal flat had something done to it. Something being done to it is not a reason it should never be allowed to stop.

No fence, and no sign. Iron would be eaten and then copied; the barrier would come up furred with ferns and then, in a week, arrive as a white mineral fence, faithful to the welds. Every attempt to protect this place becomes fuel and then becomes an exhibit.

No roof on the rectifier house. The ruin is the part with the roof gone. That part is finished and should be left finished.

No straightening of the graphite ranks, no colour added to the grey, no numbers restored to the tally board, no voice at the busbar. The sparks tick blue three or four times a minute and they are not speech and must not be made to seem like speech. People stand there until dark hoping, and what answers is their own blood, and the power will not fake an answer, because the ache of standing in front of something that is not for you is the only lesson the place has to give and it is a good one.

And no safety. This is the part of the account that should not be smoothed over. The mud takes the boot to the ankle and lets go reluctantly, and it holds a negative long enough to fill it, and these are the same property. The grip is the imagination. To make eleven acres releasing and forgiving would be to abolish the erratic line — the bird, the leaf, the feather, the jaw, the hand — and buy a reduction in a risk that people take with their eyes open, at the price of the only thing here that is not standard output. So the danger stays, and it stays exactly as legible as it already is: the crust folds, the sound is a page turning, the field teaches its first rule in the first minute and teaches it honestly. Someone will be hurt here again. The power will not muffle the warning and will not add a second one for those who did not attend to the first, and it does not pretend that this is comfortable.

Then it does three things, and it brings nothing with which to do them. Not a gram of iron, not a word, not a self. It uses only what eighty years have already left lying about.

The first is a stone. There is no gutter on the brick shed; rain leaves the eaves in a line and strikes the ground and spatters back up the brick, and the south wall has been quietly wet at its base for a long time, which is why the dry corner is dry only by the grace of a grease jar being there. A knuckle of clinker lies in the drip line, grey as bone, lichen-cushioned. The power settles it two inches and turns it a little, so that the drip cuts a channel and the channel finds the fall toward the ditch. That is the entire act. The footing dries. The corner becomes properly dry instead of accidentally dry. The calendar keeps its month. The nest of shredded paper stops being a thing that must be rebuilt damp every spring, and eighty years of mice become a longer dynasty of mice, and none of them will ever know why the wall is warm.

The second is a branch. The thorn on the rim is wind-flattened and half alive and all its branches point inland, away from the bowl, the way a man leans away from a smell he has decided not to mention. One of those branches is already touching soil, and has been for years, and has not rooted, because the knuckle of slag under it holds it a finger's width proud of the ground. The power takes the finger's width away. Layering is what a flattened thorn does anyway; it is the cheapest thing in the thorn's repertoire, as a lip is the cheapest thing in the field's. In forty years there is a low scrub on the inland rim. In sixty there is blackthorn and bramble come in on the birds that came for the haws, and a windbreak, and litter, and a sweet ditch with a margin instead of a line. The mouse's sixty paces acquire a roof for forty of them. One swallow, eventually, in some descendant of that mouse, will not be followed by a flinch.

And the scrub is the hedge against both deaths. If the wall goes and the tide owns the bowl, or if the iron runs out and the sheets go dead and the vocabulary simplifies down to nothing, the marsh that comes back will come back to a rim that is already holding seed and cover and fresh water, instead of to bare clinker. This is the act that is good whichever way the place ends, which is the only kind of act worth making in a place whose endings are certain and whose dates are not.

The third is the wind. Cool air off the estuary, moving steadily, is the loudest thing here, and it is not loud, but it is loud enough to eat a fizz at the very edge of hearing. There are already evenings when it drops — when the pressure sits still and the ebb is running and the whole bowl is at full churn with nothing over it. Two or three a year, by luck. The power lets the pattern settle a little more often. Nine or ten. Not eleven; it is not tidying. On those evenings a person standing in the middle of a hundred square metres of continuous violent change will hear the fizz, and the tick of a crust letting go somewhere out of sight, and will not have to be told that this is the sound of the work, because it will be audible, which is all it ever needed to be. Nothing has been added to what is being said. The masking has been lifted a few more times a year. That is the difference between answering somebody and letting them hear.

None of the three will be attributed to anything but weather, birds, and eighty years. No name goes on any of it. There is one edit in eleven acres that was made by hand, by a man who lifted four objects out of the mud onto a tally table and left no name anywhere, and everything at ground level here is unmade inside a week, and everything that survives survives because it was raised. The power does not add a fifth object to his row. It does the same sort of thing he did, one scale down and out of sight: it raises a stone, and a branch, and the odds of a dry corner. Two anonymous edits in eighty years, and the second one was not made by a hand.

What it keeps for itself is the difference between the hand and the bottle.

The field cannot tell them apart, has no opinion about either, is not withholding anything. Somebody has to hold that difference or it is nowhere, and it cannot be installed in the brine without ruining the brine, so it is held outside, by the one thing present that can hold it, and it is held without being put anywhere — not on the tally board, not into the busbar, not into anybody's ear at dusk. The man's name is on nothing. The hand's owner is on nothing. The power knows both and says neither, and this is not a secret; it is simply where the noticing has to live, since it cannot live in the bowl.

And it decides the question that the visitor stopped trying to decide. It is the peace of it. Nothing in the bowl can notice, and that is the peace, and the worst of it does not therefore vanish — it moves. It moves to whatever is standing there capable of being hurt by it. The power takes that share. It will be present, in the only way it can be present without disturbing anything, when the last mouth clears its throat and nothing comes, when the eleven words are said into ordinary water and then not said, when the porcelain lies down in the mud, white, for a very long time, and the lichen drowns or doesn't, and the mouse — which by then will be a mouse in a haw scrub with a roof over its run — moves inland. It will be the only thing there that knows the shift has ended. It will not announce that either.

Meanwhile it stands in the bowl, and being in the bowl it is briefly and literally a component: a conductor, a local alteration in the sheets, part of the circuit for as long as it stays. It takes its turn as a part. It is four in the morning somewhere in the eighty years, the ebb has come, the pressure differential is back, and the mouths are starting up in ragged order from the low corner. A stone is two inches from where it was. A branch is touching ground. This evening the wind will drop.

The shift is still on.
