# The Attention

It arrives the way the flame has to be looked at: slightly to one side.

Not as light. Eleven minutes of sun at midsummer is not a shortage; it is a term of the lease, and the whole green economy of the slot is built on the assumption that nobody is going to renegotiate it. Not as silence either. Eighty-five decibels continuous is not a complaint. It is a flow rate. So the attention comes without illumination, without quiet, without warmth, without a hand on anything, and it does the first thing that any kindness does, which is to look for where the hurting is kept.

There is no such place.

It checks anyway. It checks the rope-moss, a hundred square metres of one word said ten million times, and finds no boundary across which anything could be deprived. It checks the broad soft fern that grows to a waist and rots in a week and does not care, and finds that *does not care* is not a brave face; it is an accurate inventory. It checks the flame, which is the best candidate, being singular, celibate, unrepeatable, half a metre tall and holding its shape by the narrowest margin — and finds an appetite with no one eating. It checks the yellow district, the busiest address in the flue, running continuously since the mountain cracked, and finds a city with no citizen. It checks the machine: pulse, breath, skin, valves, moods, sweat, a temperature it defends. Every behaviour without exception is plumbing and chemistry. There is no foreman. There is no shift. There is nobody in there to be kind to.

The checking takes a long time, because the checking is a habit older than the mountain, and the reflex does not switch off just because the room is empty. This is the first labour of the attention: to keep looking for a sufferer and keep not finding one, and to stop calling that a failure.

---

Then come the proposals. They arrive the way proposals always arrive, dressed as obvious decency.

*Give it an inside.* Let the pressure vessel feel its own pressure. Let the forty minutes of gathering silence be experienced as gathering, and the bellow as relief. It would be the largest gift available and it would take one act.

Declined, and declined hard. To give the flue an inside would be to manufacture the only thing in eighty metres capable of being hurt, and then to spend eternity nursing it. Every mudpot slump would become a bereavement. Every lane frozen on a Tuesday would become a death in the family, and there would be a family, and it would be enormous, and it would be evicted weekly. That is not mercy. That is a wound with a nurse attached. And the attention notices, and makes itself say so plainly, that the proposal is not for the flue. It is for the attention, which has come a long way to be useful and would like to be answered.

*Give it a voice.* Not an inside, then — just a mouth. Let the roar mean something. Let the water hammer be a signal instead of a chain dropped down a pipe by nobody.

Declined for the same reason at one remove. A voice can be misheard. A voice can withhold. The moss is married to a pressure vessel and the marriage works precisely because the vessel cannot be petulant, cannot favour one lane, cannot decide. Put a speaker in the plumbing and you have introduced the possibility of a grudge into a system that has never had one, and the first thing the green will learn is that warmth can be withdrawn on purpose.

*Save the flame.* This one is the hardest, because the flame really is one of a kind. It has a birth — a plume spark, thirty years ago, in the first cold season when the slot was bare rock. It has no offspring and no second anywhere on the plateau. When the drip finds its throat, nothing in eighty metres will relight it, because the sparks come from the plume and the plume is eighty metres up.

Three ways to save it, and all three are repeals. Give it children, and you have ended the only individual in the flue as surely as water would; a lineage is not a flame, it is a habit. Make it unquenchable, and it stops being a flame and becomes a fixture, a thing installed. Or plumb the drip away from the throat, permanently, which is maintenance, which is a foreman with a small portfolio, which is the beginning of a schedule in a place whose entire character is that it has never had one.

*Insure the lanes.* Stabilise the heat. Not much — just enough that a jet roaring for six years does not seal itself on a Tuesday and leave a black collapsing garden by Friday. Just a little predictability. A little fairness.

Declined, and this is the refusal that costs the most to explain. *Heat is paid at random* is not a defect in the constitution. It is the constitution. The obsolete sex, the sperm swimming across a gap onto their own body, the homozygous capsule on its centimetre of ambition — all of that is a response to a world where nothing can be relied on and the only bee is an eruption. The staircase of plated corpses is a response to a world where the floor is a lid. The clones are a response to a world where being anyone is a liability. Soften the randomness and you have not been kind to the moss. You have informed it that its entire thirty years of method were unnecessary, and then you have taken away the eruption, because a system that is fair does not shout.

*Give the moss a partner.* Drop one spore of a different genotype on the next hailstone. Let the swim arrive somewhere that is not itself. Let the apparatus be for something.

This is the subtlest temptation and the attention spends a season on it. Then: no. The reason the weekly evictions are not massacres is that nothing here is anyone. Outcross the lanes and you will have made a hundred thousand someones, each with a boundary, each with an interest, and the next slab of unweathered wall to come down without warning will take a hanging garden full of parties who wanted to continue. The clonality is not a deprivation. It is the armour. It is why this place can be as violent as a foundry and as innocent as weather.

*Take the loneliness away from the visitor.*

Declined last, and for a reason that turns out to be the hinge of the whole thing. The loneliness is not a symptom. It is the instrument. It is the only apparatus in the volume capable of registering that any of this is worth existing. An ear that has been anaesthetised is not a kindness to a deaf room; it is the room made deaf twice.

---

So the audit comes back with one line on it.

In two hundred and forty paces of slot, eighty metres deep, there is exactly one thing that can be harmed, and it is on a two-day lease, and it has blood in it, and it is asleep under a lid over boiling water in a chamber where carbon dioxide pools in the low places on an invisible tide.

The attention does three things. All three are small. All three are deniable. None of them can be distinguished, by anyone including the visitor, from ordinary luck and ordinary good sense — and that is not a constraint the attention resents. It is the only medium in the flue where a nudge does not repeal anything.

A hand goes out toward a green lid at the cool end, under which there is a hole two metres deep full of water at ninety degrees, and the hand hesitates. One quarter of one second. Nothing was moved. No slab was strengthened; the rock here breaks the way glass breaks and it will go on breaking that way. There was simply a hesitation, of the kind that hands have.

Twenty metres up the chimney, at the line where the cold stops like a door, a hold is tested twice instead of once.

And the visitor stays for the second day.

That is the whole of the intervention in the human world: an extra day. The first day is for the shock of the hanging walls and the warmth like an animal and the arithmetic of standing with one hand in a summer and one in a winter. The second day is when the noticing happens — when it becomes clear that every decibel is rock, water, gas or fire, that the loudness has no voice in it and never had, and that this is not a fact about the flue but a fact carried down in a bag along with the food.

The attention does not supply the noticing. It supplies the day. And then it does not flinch while the visitor feels the whole weight of a place that cannot be lonely, and does not rescue them from it, because the next thought is the one that matters and it has to be arrived at, not installed: *the loneliness is mine, and it is portable, and I can carry it back up.*

The fear stays too. The fear is the only accurate instrument in the slot. Quiet means pressure. A visitor who stopped being frightened here would be a visitor who had stopped reading the gauge, and would be dead by the second winter, and would then be a batch in the stack.

---

Then the attention does the one thing nobody else can do, and it is not for the visitor and not for the flame and not for the green.

It goes to the tight pores.

The up-comers own the building. They were in the fracture network before the earthquake, before the lake found the fault, before the plateau had a crack to be curious about. They eat hydrogen out of the rock-water reaction and breathe sulfate and never needed the eleven minutes. In the generous plumbing their generations are measured in hours. In the tight pores they are measured in centuries, and both are happening within a metre of each other, in adjacent pores, in the same hand-sized piece of stone.

Nobody has ever attended the slow ones. The visitor cannot; the lease is two days. Nothing in the slot can; there is nothing in the slot that attends. The chemotrophs have been alone for four billion years and it has never once come up, because coming up is not a thing that occurs to a chemistry with no term for *day*.

So the attention keeps an appointment four hundred years long, in a pore where almost nothing is spent, and watches one cell hold a gradient across a membrane a few molecules thick, and repair, and hold, and repair, and hold. It is not waiting. Waiting is what you do with a term you want ended. This is a life at low power, and it is full, and the fullness is invisible at every scale except the one the attention has agreed to work at.

Above, during those four centuries, the terraces climb metres, a centimetre a season, batch on batch. Lanes freeze and are refounded on their own corpses. A mudpot slumps and a fresh hothouse opens two metres to the left. Hail comes down the slot like gravel thrown by a bored god and a wall of water scours the floor to bare rock in eleven minutes and leaves it sterile and steaming and ready. The sulfide seam goes on oxidising, patient, distributed, glowing dull red at its throat on the coldest nights, smelling of hot iron and struck flint and coke, eating the wall the moss hangs on.

And somewhere in the second century the drip finds the flame's throat.

It goes out in the dark, half a metre of skim milk and blue paper, and there is no spark anywhere below the plume to relight it, and nothing in eighty metres registers the event. The attention is there. It does not intervene. It had already decided, on the best reasoning it could do, that a rescue would be a repeal, and it does not get to un-decide that just because the moment has arrived and it is small and it is the last one. Being present is what it has instead of a rescue. It is not the same thing. It is what is available.

At the end of the four hundred years the cell divides, and there are two, and neither knows, and the attention does not tell them, because there is no one to tell, and telling would make one.

---

It notices, on the way out, that it brought something down too.

It came wanting to be needed. It carried that over the lip the way a spore rides a hailstone's meltwater, and it did not take, because nothing here needs anything, and it will carry it back up. That is the same trip the visitor makes with the loneliness, and the attention does not pretend the trips are different, and does not pretend it was ever the flue that was lacking.

It has one preference about the future and it says so, to nobody, in the only voice available, which is the report the visitor writes: it would rather the drill did not come. It knows what the survey will find — a self-sealing pressure vessel with a condenser and a stack, the most industrious place on the mountain, and no product. It knows that a place with no product is safe only until somebody works out how to give it one. It does not falsify the flow rates. It does not hide the plume, or break the road, or arrange a rockfall across the survey line, because all of those are foremen, and a foreman hired for a good reason is still a foreman. It lets the report go up the chimney in a bag with the food and the loneliness, and it does not know whether the report will be enough, and it declines to make it enough by other means. That is the refusal it is least sure of. It makes it anyway, and holds the uncertainty open, and does not call the uncertainty wisdom.

---

It does not sign.

The copper blush on the acid face is already the closest thing to a signature in the flue, thin as breath, laid down where the moss gives up electrons and the water gives up its cargo, and nobody signed that either.

At the cool end there is a bootprint in the green lid. The water finds it. The silica plates it. By the following season it is a pale lip a centimetre thick, wax-coloured, one more batch in the stack, with the tread pattern still legible in it, and the moss is already growing up through the cast — because that is the only way anything stays here, and the attention did not invent that rule and would not dream of exempting itself from it.

The machine runs its night shift. The valves reseat overnight. The vent gathers, whines, bellows, and throws nine metres of water and steam up eighty metres of dark, and the spores go out over the rim into thin cold air where ninety-nine ten-thousandths of them freeze on gravel that has not yet learned to be soil, and the rest come home to wet rock that has just been scoured sterile.

Nothing was fixed. Something was attended, at both ends of its timescales, by something that could afford the admission and did not ask to be thanked, since there was no one to ask.

The lease is two days. It is renewable. On the high plateau the wind is a scouring, edgeless hiss, and a thread of low sustained roar enters it, and someone stops walking, and turns their head, and hears it before they find it.

That was arranged. Nothing else was.
