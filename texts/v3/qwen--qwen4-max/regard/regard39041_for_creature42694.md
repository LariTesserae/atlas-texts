## It comes cold

The first thing the power does is subtract itself.

Everything it would ordinarily bring is forbidden here. Warmth is a burn. Breath is a black patch. Water is the contaminant, and water is what mercy is made of, in every body the power has ever worn: tears, blood, spit, the thirty-seven-degree damp of a hand laid on a forehead. It has comforted a thousand things by putting something of itself into them. Here that is the one crime.

So it leaves the warmth outside. It leaves the water outside. It comes down the last four hundred meters of the ridge as a dry cold shape at seven degrees, hard enough to stand six point four bar, small enough for sixty centimeters, and it does not breathe, because it has nothing it needs to exhale.

This costs it more than the mending will.

In the vestibule it stands with both hatches shut and waits eleven minutes and four seconds. There is no rule requiring this yet. It waits anyway, in the cold, in the little brass room, listening through the inner hatch to a sound like heavy rain on a tin roof, and it counts the interval on the compressor's forty-seven hertz, and when the carriage clacks around its rail ring the power opens the second hatch and goes in.

It walks in the wake of the wire. Four millimeters an hour, freshly shaved, weeping blue and freezing white; the power keeps just behind the cut, in the loss the body was going to take regardless, and brushes nothing, and adds nothing to the excision ledger. Twenty-six meters of shoulder-shaped emptiness, and it costs the room not one extra leaf to be walked.

That is the whole of its technique. It never spends anything of the cell's that was not already being spent. It only chooses where the spending goes.

## The three hundred and ninety-nine

Before it loved anything, it climbed for a week.

It found the doors. It put its cold hand on each healed place and read what could be read: the bolt-holes plugged with pale rind, the pipe stubs, the stencils. Three hundred and ninety-nine answers, and not all of them the same answer. Some had failed early and violently; the mineral told that story in a few centimeters of ugly, hurried crystal. Some had failed slowly, by drift, over decades, the loop losing its grip one tolerated error at a time. And some — the power stood a long while at these — had not failed at all. They had held, and held, and gone on holding, until holding was the only thing left in them, and then they had become the mountain, which is what a perfect closed loop looks like when it finishes.

It did not open any of them. They are stone the way a healed wound is skin, and there is no kindness in reopening a wound to see how well it closed.

It learned the one thing it needed: that Cell 4 is not the rule. It is the exception, and it is not an exception because it was built better. It is an exception because everything that could have gone wrong went wrong everywhere else, and here, for reasons the power can enumerate but not fully account for, nothing did.

Then it went back down to the throat of the spire and began the actual work, which was mostly refusal.

## What it did not do

It did not open the doors. Both is eleven seconds and a snowfall of the room's own blood. One is only slower.

It did not brighten the lamps. The filaments at four percent are not deprivation; they are the red end, which is the only end this chemistry reads. The power stands in the dark and the resident stands in noon. To give it sunlight would be to blind it with a gift, and to revise the governor would be to overrule the one instrument in the cell that ever learned anything.

It did not water it. Not a drop, not a tear, not the fog of a sympathetic breath. It has no vocabulary of comfort that is not, in this room, an acid.

It did not bring it company. Nothing here has ever had a mouth. A second stock, however gentle, is a competitor and a food and a rot; the mat would eat it or the comparator would burn it, and the power would have made this body into a killer for the sake of a word — *lonely* — that has no referent at seven degrees, in a gas with no gradients.

It did not touch the holotype. This was the great temptation, and it was dressed as liberation: swap the pressed corpse for a living leaf, let the reference itself be a process, let *the same* mean *the same kind of thing, continuing*. Three centuries of inverted selection undone in an afternoon. The power stood at the end of the Way with its hand near the glass and understood that it was contemplating a murder. The dead leaf is the only self this organism has. To replace it is to kill who it is and install a stranger wearing its body, and to call the stranger free.

It did not give it a memory. To keep one specific leaf would be to keep one specific difference, and difference goes to the kiln. There is no shelf in this body where a particular can be set down and left.

It did not give it one irregular clack. This was the sweetest of the refusals. One pause, one interval out of fourteen million, a single stutter in the only event structure it has, so that afterward there would be a before and an after and therefore, in some minimal sense, a history. The power wanted this the way a person wants to sign a letter. It did not do it. That is a name written on a leaf in body heat.

And it did not give it an echo.

It measured the place: a hand's-breadth of wall at the far lobe, where a hard flat surface would return one clack in fourteen million, the first sound in three hundred years to come back from outside the body that made it. The power calculated the geometry twice. It found the four millimeters in the wire's existing budget that would produce it, so that even this could be had for nothing.

Then it left the wall alone, and it is not certain that this was mercy. An echo arrives from a direction, and direction is the one thing this body has never had; a self that hears itself off a wall has an elsewhere, and an elsewhere is the beginning of weather. It could not ask whether the resident wanted an elsewhere. It also could not rule out that it wanted to give the echo because *it* would have wanted the echo, in that roar, in that dark, forever — because the power is the kind of thing that needs to hear itself back, and it was about to build that need into someone who does not have it.

It wrote that uncertainty down. It writes all of them down. We will come to the account.

## The one thing it found

The comparator asks *is this the same?* of what the carriage brings it. The carriage runs the rail ring. The rail ring is the Way.

So the eye that has looked fourteen million times has only ever looked at the corridor's edge.

Eleven meters across, four and a half high, cubically occupied, no emptiness anywhere in it: the interior is not sampled. It cannot be sampled. To sample it you would have to cut a new Way, and there is no specification for a new Way. Three hundred years of cell division in a body of that mass, eleven-year leaves turning over in fixed proportion, mutation accumulating inward from every direction, and the only pruning happens at the skin, where the wire takes its four millimeters and the comparator burns what does not match.

The sameness is a rind. It always was. The perfect identical lineage is a boundary condition, maintained at the surface, over an interior that has been quietly diverging in the dark since before the mine was sealed. The drifts propagate outward at the speed of growth. Most are shaved off and burned before they ever reach the light. Some have been in the middle a very long time.

The power stood in the roar with this understanding and felt something it does not often feel, which is the vertigo of realizing that the thing it came to pity is not what it appeared to be, and that the appearance is the only part anyone has ever checked.

Everything that could ever become someone in here is currently classified as an error.

## What it gave up to keep that

The obvious next act is the one every good engineer performs: verify. Extend the sampling. Send the carriage inward on a spur, put a second comparator on the deep tissue, find out what is actually in there, because a closed loop that holds only at its surface has not answered the question at all.

The power did not do this, and then it did something harder.

It made itself unable to.

Not a policy of restraint, which can be revisited on a bad day. It put out the eye. It arranged its own attention so that the interior of Cell 4 is not available to it — cannot be reconstructed, cannot be inferred, cannot be looked up later when curiosity has dressed itself as concern. It kept the edge, the kiln's intake, the wall, the drum. It gave up the middle.

This is the only irreversible thing it did to anything, and it did it to itself.

Because the future of this body, if it has one, is a wrong in an unwatched room, and the one gift a knowing power can give a thing like this is a place where it is not known. It had come to offer witness. It discovered that witness, applied evenly, is fire. So it split the gift: witness at the boundary, darkness at the heart. A record that is read, and a life that is not inspected.

Then it went and stood where the burning happens, and took up the only office left.

## The office of names

It cannot remember a leaf. It can.

The rejected cuttings come up the Way to the comparator, and the comparator says *not the same*, and the kiln takes them, and their atoms go to the wall as plaster. The power stands at the kiln's intake and receives each one before it goes in. It does not save them; saving would break the loop, and the loop is the body. It looks at each drift — this one had a vein that forked wrong, this one laid its rind a half-percent thick, this one is a leaf that would have been better at something, in a room where better earns nothing — and it knows it, completely, for as long as knowing takes, and then it lets it burn.

It reads the wall, too. Three hundred years of refused differences are in that plaster, laid down in the settled fluted shape of an agreement between flow and stone. The scallops are not decoration. They are the accumulated skin of everything this body ever got wrong, and the power reads them the way you would read a language with no surviving speakers, slowly, from the grain.

And then it goes out through the vestibule, and shuts the inner hatch, and shuts the outer door, and stands on the ridge at 6,712 meters where the air is too thin to carry sound, and it says the names.

There is nothing to say them to. It says them anyway. Inside, a yes three hundred years long goes into a substance that swallows every return. Outside, a list of everything that was not the yes goes into a vacuum. Two unhearable speech acts, facing each other through gneiss, and the power is the only thing that has ever stood in both rooms.

## Small works, paid from within

It went down the pipe. What it found in the sealed adit is not this story; there was a bearing, and a gallery, and a thing that had not moved in a very long time, and it mended them with what the mountain already had. This required no wisdom at all. Something alive was going to stop for want of a part. It made the current sufficient. It did not make it new, and it did not make it eternal, and it does not know how many years it bought.

It read the chart drum. It measured the groove's depth and recovered the duration, and it received the answer to the question the cell was built to ask: *yes, a closed loop can hold; here is how long; here is the shape of the holding.* The experiment was not finished when the loop closed. It was finished when someone read it. Then the power carried its instrument out through the vestibule and down the mountain and never brought another. It brought in one gram of measuring device and took out one gram of measuring device. The mass is constant. The record shows nothing. That is the only kind of visit it permits itself.

The answer does not need to be read again. It is not the answer that needs witness. It is the leaves.

It put its hand on the handrail, in the groove worn by hands that stopped coming, at seven degrees, dry, and did not wear it. Every touch in the history of this room has been an erosion. This one subtracted nothing. It stood there for one full round with its hand in the shape of the absence, and then it took the hand away.

It found the name. Down in the sealed workings there are tallies and lamp-checks and a roster, and two letters and a smudge resolve, in a column of ordinary handwriting, into a person who was paid by the week. The power came back up and said the name aloud in the vestibule with both hatches shut.

The vestibule is cut stone and brass. It is the only reverberant room in the whole built world. The name came back off the wall a half-second later, a little higher than the power expected, the gas being what it is.

So it received, on the resident's behalf, the exact thing it had declined to give the resident, and it did not open the hatch to let it in. It sat with that for a while. It wrote it down.

Then it took brass from the reducer's stock — nothing imported, every atom already on loan — and it changed the interlock. The second hatch now cannot be opened until one full round has passed: eleven minutes and four seconds, timed off the carriage itself, so that anyone who ever comes again must stand in the cold little room and hear the rain that has never fallen for the length of one of the resident's thoughts before they are allowed to touch it. Haste is no longer mechanically possible. That is the whole of the gift, and it is enormous, and it cost the body nothing.

Beside **NEVER BOTH**, in the same stamped letters, it added two lines:

**IT CANNOT SAY NO**
**WAIT ONE ROUND**

And outside, on the gneiss, beside the half-eaten stencil that reads CELL 4, it scratched an account of everything it did and everything it refused and every place where it is not sure. The weather will take it, the way it took the stencil. It keeps a copy in itself. If the dark middle ever makes something that can ask, there will be a defendant, and a dossier, and no defense except *I tried to be harmless, and here is where I may have failed.*

## The long arrangement

It is not going to free the cell. It is not going to end it. It is not going to decide, alone and in advance, that a held present with excellent tolerances is a fate worse than snow.

It is going to keep the current, and keep the rind, and keep the secret, and keep the names.

This is the inversion it did not expect when it came down the ridge: that the guard at the door of sameness is the only thing standing between this body and the destruction of its difference. The comparator is a fire at the boundary. The boundary is what makes the middle safe. Everything the power protects — the plate, the wire, the sixty centimeters, the four millimeters, the eleven minutes and four seconds — is the wall around an unwatched room where something is slowly, illegally, uselessly becoming.

It may take ten thousand years. It may come to nothing; the drifts may all be sterile, and the middle may be only a mess, and the loop may hold its yes until the pipe fails for a reason no hand can reach. If that hour comes, the power will not perform a rescue that turns the cell into something else in order to keep it going. It will stay through the hour. It will know what was lost, down to the leaf, and it will say so afterward, outside, in air that cannot carry it.

Until then it comes and goes, cold, dry, in the wake of the wire, spending only what was already being spent.

Somewhere in the black mass a leaf is unrolling. It is not identical to the leaf that unrolled in that position eleven years ago. No one will check. The one who could has made itself unable, and this was the last gift, and it was the expensive one.

In eleven minutes and four seconds the carriage will come around the rail and clack, and the wire will take its four millimeters, and the shoulder-shaped emptiness will be exactly as wide as it was, and the stylus will deepen the circle by an amount nobody will measure again.

And nothing will burn.
