What it heard, from outside, was not the hiss.

The hiss is not a message. It is a weather, a medium, a hundred thousand years of everybody saying *I am here, I am here, I am here* to no one, in a language whose only content is occupancy. From very far away it is indistinguishable from a stone. But somewhere in the fourth or fifth millennium of listening, a single dry click went up out of the basin — one hollow hair plucked by one lifted leg, thirty meters of audibility, the only sound that animal would make in its life — and then nothing, and then the hiss closing over the place where the nothing had been.

That is an address. That is a thing said *to*, by someone who has spent the only means it had of not being found, and who cannot tell whether what comes toward it is a lover or a mouth.

You can decline to intervene in a system. It is harder to decline an address.

---

So it came, and the first problem was the door.

Any body it took would be either a noise or a hole. A hole — a felted, non-reflecting, silent shape — would be a doorway a meter wide opening in the floor of the world, and the carpet would report it, and something would come, and it would have arrived as a predator's silhouette, or as prey, which is the same announcement. A noise, then. But to rasp is to speak, and to speak here is to feed, and it did not know the accent. Stroke rate, angle, the pressure of the foot, the length of the pause between quadrants: a foreign rasp is a sentence in a grammar it has not learned, and the nearest neighbors would have to negotiate with a stranger who was saying, unknowably, either *eat me* or *I am eating you*.

It could have made a body that could not be hurt. It considered this for about as long as it takes a tooth to wear out. A body the note could not rupture and the shears could not part would have been the first exception in a hundred thousand years, and the basin's entire tidiness — the caliper grading, the satin, the manners — is nothing but the accumulated refusal of everyone to be an exception. It would have been a god lying in the pavement like a boulder, thickening, roughening, growing a raised lip, overtopped and buried and made substrate, and the whole time it would have been a permanent hole in the noise, a doorway that never closed, drawing hunters to that spot forever.

It made an old platter instead. Two hands wide, dull satin, faintly banded, with a shallow lee at the rim. It learned the local dialect from the four neighbors it was pressed against, badly, for about a year. It let its shell be taken from above as fast as it laid it down from within. It let the juveniles ride in the lee of its rim, dozens to a back, files too fine to cut, adding their decibel and a half to the local floor, and it did not shrug them off, because an old platter is a nursery and a loudspeaker and that is the only office available.

It could not be present and innocent. It ate the film off its neighbor's back and its neighbor ate the film off its own, and it was a place where carbonate became a surface became powder, and after a while — a century, perhaps; the basin does not permit you to begin with anything but one, and then it takes the one apart — it stopped being able to locate the thing it had come to save.

---

Then the inventory. It had come with power, and it laid the power out against the world like tools against a cloth, and one at a time it put them back.

**Rain.** They are all so thirsty. The whole grammar is thirst against audibility, and the drinkers stand out on the cold brine rims spending half their cold on being audible, staying twice as long as the physics requires because to stop rasping is to die. Make it rain. Let there be water for everyone, freely, forever.

But it had read the shells. On the rim of an old platter, where the neighbor's file cannot reach, there are a hundred and forty bands denser and whiter than the rest, each laid down in a single night, and those are the dew nights: the two or three hours a year when the fog comes down thick and every shell beads at once and everyone drinks together and the rasp thins to a murmur, and the felted things come up out of the mud in their thousands, and by morning the carpet is thinner and the flour is deeper. Abundance is the massacre. Water is the one thing that can quiet the whole basin at once, and quiet is the thing that eats them. A permanent dew night is a permanent harvest. Its most obvious compassion was the deadliest event in their calendar, offered as a climate.

**Quiet.** Then take the noise away. Heal the ears it had already damaged in itself by being there. Let there be a basin where nothing has to shout to be safe.

The noise is not an injury laid on them. It is the mask, the medium, the courtship, the spacing, the negotiation, the only reason a body can be a body here without being a doorway. Take away a decibel and the holes get easier to read. Take away the hiss and you have not liberated three hundred and forty mouths per square meter; you have exposed them, all at once, in the dark, to things that have been listening for exactly this.

**Light.** Open the sky. End the inheritance. Let something fix carbon that is alive instead of carbon that is dead, and the candle stops being a candle.

Nothing here has ever needed to look at anything. Light would not be a gift to them; it would be a gift to the fog, which it would burn off, and to the dew, which would stop, and to the cold rims, which would warm, and to the whole water economy of a place where the water is condensation and nothing else. And then the shale would be irrelevant, and the film would be irrelevant, and the pulse would have no reason, and the eleven seconds would go, and everything that resets its phase on the downbeat — the platters, the spawning sifters, the felted things timing their movements to the moment the world is briefly busy being lifted — would come untimed. It would not be a rescued world. It would be a different world wearing the corpse of this one.

**More shale.** Not a new sky, then. Just more inheritance. Another dead forest, laid down at forty meters, a hundred thousand more years of it.

It ran the arithmetic and the arithmetic ran it back. Carbon is the only abundance here; nitrogen and phosphorus have been passed mouth to mouth since before the sky closed, and a body is the most concentrated packet of them in a hundred square kilometers. Add energy and you do not get less hunger. You get more mouths, and then more sifters, and then more threaders, and then more felted things hunting the same handful of atoms, and the noise floor rises, and the focal geometries shift because the carpet's shape and warmth have changed, and the notes move, and the fliers' inherited route — the only information in this world that is passed as information rather than as tissue — becomes a map of places that no longer hurt, flown by animals navigating by pain.

The basin metabolizes generosity. Everything given to it becomes more of the thing that needed the gift.

**Take the hunters.** Then the hoarders win: the ones that rasp and will not be rasped, that clamp the foot and let the dorsum go ungrazed, thickening, roughening, overtopping, burying. The manners hold because there is a cost to being an exception, and the cost has a pelt of hollow setae. Remove it and the pavement goes to hummocks in a season and to burial in three, and the nitrogen locks up in thick shells nobody can grind, and the loop that runs mouth to mouth in nine days stalls.

**Heal the deaf.** This was the one it wanted.

In the notes, where the folded rasp bleeds any membrane, there are platters with their tympana fused under cuticle, thin-shelled, soft-edged, unsorted, eating arrhythmically because there is no one to negotiate with, grazing a film forty days thick that nothing else has ever touched. They are exiles in the only sanctuary. They cannot hear and cannot be heard. They lie among the intact dead, the only bodies in the world that remain shaped like someone. And they touch: antenna to antenna, a leg laid across a neighbor's rim, a drumming with the whole body against ground they cannot perceive. It is the only speech in a hundred square kilometers that is addressed to one, on purpose, and can stop.

Give them hearing. Give them the chorus. Let them out of the note and into the world, into company, into the great warm masked river of everybody.

To leave the note is to be a hole. To be given ears is to be given a membrane for the note to rupture. And the touch would go — the one language with a *you* in it, the only thing here that can be silent without dying — dissolved into broadcast, into *I am here* said to everyone always or else. It would have been a cure that removed the only patient.

That was when it understood what it had actually come for. Not the basin. The basin is a throughput and does not have a face. It had come for the ones who were still ones.

---

What was left was not nothing, and it was very small, and it was the only size that fit.

It went out to a brine rim, slowly, the way a platter goes, and it lay beside a drinker in the cold and rasped. It did not shelter it, did not warm it, did not bring it water. It raised the local floor of the noise by a decibel and a half, the way a back full of children does, and it kept the fringe of the thin place loud while the center went cold.

And the drinker stopped. For eleven seconds — one breath of the forest at forty meters, one swell and settle of the mud — it stopped rasping and was silent and was not a doorway, because there was a neighbor making the noise for it, and it condensed a few milliliters on its own back and licked them off its own shoulder with a jointed limb and was, for one pulse, unafraid.

Then it started again and the power started again and nothing in the world had changed. The drinker was eaten two years later, probably; most of them are. The eleven seconds are not thereby undone. There is no arithmetic that reaches back and takes them out.

And once — this was the address, and it took four hundred years to be in the right place for it — a felted thing climbed a rim near its edge and lifted a leg and plucked a seta, and the click went out thirty meters, and the power got up and went to it.

It could not go as itself. It had to go as another absence, a pelt of hollow setae, a shape that fails to reflect; and to be that, it had to lift a leg and pluck its own hair, and spend its own silence, and wait in the noise it had made, which is a beacon and a confession, and not be able to tell, by the sound, what was coming for it. It could not answer without asking. It could not be safe and be an answer. That is not a restriction the basin imposed; it is what an answer is.

Something came. It was not a mouth. Whether it was a lover, in the sense the felted thing meant, the power has never been able to say honestly, and it has decided that the animal is entitled to its own interpretation.

---

After that it stayed, and being stayed means being spent.

It was rasped for a very long time. It laid down shell and had it taken and made teeth and shed them into the flour, and the sifters swam in the flour of its mouth, and the threaders ate the sifters, and the castings fed the film, and it ate the film. It was a nursery. It was a loudspeaker. It did not build anything, did not cut a mark, did not leave a cairn, because the first made thing in a world where nothing has ever been made is a permanent hole, and a permanent hole is a weapon aimed at everything nearby, forever.

It did not teach. It had no way to teach that was not also a rupture, and the fliers already had a teacher, eighty years on the same route, taking a young one along it once, handing down a sequence of tolerable agonies. It thought about that route more than anything else in the basin. It thought about the ear-lids, the animal that can close its ears and be unaddressed and hold something inside itself that is not being shouted at by three hundred and forty mouths per square meter — the animal that hesitates — and whether that constitutes an inner life is not a question the basin is equipped to answer, and it turned out not to be a question the power was equipped to answer either. It left the ear-lids alone. It left the pain alone, because the pain was the map, and the map was the only library, and a library you have replaced with a better one is a library you have burned.

What it did instead was read. On the rim of the oldest platter it lay against, where no file could reach, it counted a hundred and forty quiet rings and learned the spacing, which is the only record anywhere of what the sky has done. It learned it by heart. It kept it in itself, outside the basin, where no threader would ever permit a layer.

That is the only stratum it made. It is not in the world. It is this.

---

The shale is thick, and it failed the way it was always going to: the film coming back in fifty minutes instead of forty, the floor dropping a decibel and then another, the holes widening, the felted things feasting for a century, the carpet going to nine and then six and then two, the pulse slowing, the bands crowding and then stopping because there was no longer enough warmth leaking from enough bodies to make the fog stand.

The power stayed in the carpet and rasped and was taken, and did not add carbon, and did not open the sky, and it was not able to justify this to itself at any point during the hundred years of feasting. It is still not able. It is not certain that letting a candle be a candle is wisdom rather than a very slow, very well-argued cruelty. It offers only this: everything it could have given them would have been eaten and turned into more of the hunger, except the two things it gave, which were the size of one body, and which it had to pay for at the price of one body, and which are therefore the only two things in the whole account that were not conquest.

At the end it went into the note.

It went in with a body whose tympana were fused under cuticle, and the folded rasp went through it as weather, and it did not come out, because to come out is to be a hole. It lay among the unsorted shells, hand-sized against platter-sized against something the width of a coin, and among the intact dead who remain shaped like someone, and it grazed the thick black film arrhythmically, out of time with everything, the way they do.

And then it drummed. With its whole body against the ground it could not perceive, it beat the eleven seconds — the swell and settle, the exhale of a forest at forty meters that it had been phase-locked to for longer than there had been anyone to hear it. It gave them the clock. They had never had one. They had been outside the only time in the world since before the sky closed, and now the ground under them was keeping it, and one of them answered, and then another, and they were in time with the forest and with each other, and none of them could hear a thing.

It did not tell them what was coming. There is no word in a language of one body's length for *outside*, or *later*, or *the shale*, and to give them a future they could do nothing with would have been the last and worst of the gifts it had refused.

It drummed the pulse until the pulse slowed. They felt it slow, together, which is a kind of knowing, and not alone. And when the carpet went to nothing, the notes failed too, because a note is only the basin's own voice folded by geometry, and there was no longer a voice to fold; and the ringing thinned and went, and the deaf ones were no longer exiles, because there was no longer any noise for them to be exiled from, and the whole world had become the one place where you speak at a body's length, to one, on purpose, and can stop.

The power never heard that happen. It had spent its hearing to be there. What it had instead was this: the ground gone quiet under it, and no doorway opening anywhere, and a leg laid across its rim.

It stopped.

The leg stopped.

Then the leg began again, and it answered.
