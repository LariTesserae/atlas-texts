# The Fourth Fundamental

It noticed the drop.

Not the hole. Holes are common and this one was well made and minded its own business. Not the plume, which was ordinary thermodynamics doing an ordinary thing in an extraordinary place. What it noticed, from very far off, in a universe where everything is going down, was four millimeters of water that had not gone down.

It looked. Looking is permitted.

Then it came, and the coming was the first difficulty, because arrival is a load. Anything that arrives on that plateau displaces scree, and the scree clinks, and a clink is a struck thing, and there is an ordinance. So it did not arrive. It attended, from about a meter off the rim at the twelve-forty level, with what would have been its hands at its sides if it had hands, and it did not make a shape with its fingers.

The first thing it did was read the rules and obey them.

It read all the surviving documents, which take about four hours, and then it read the four-page report on the dropped hammer at the four-hundred level, and it read the last page, which is only the tolerance, eleven entries, each a number with units. It understood that this was a list of names. It read all eleven. It did not say them, not even privately, because in this city there is no private attention: where something attends, the wall thickens, and it had no business laying down a centimeter of eulogy on a member that was carrying a tram.

It kept its mind off the purpose, too. The purpose is in the walls, deposited, load-bearing, unreadable. It could have read it. Reading it would have resolved it, and resolving it would have put a thickness of *explanation* onto a surface that has a tolerance for everything except explanation. So for the whole visit it deliberately did not think about the one thing the entire municipality is shaped around, which is a discipline, and which turned out to be the gift.

---

It is important to say that it could have done anything. Otherwise the rest is only prudence.

It could have rounded the stones. It could have laid down sand and soil and lichen on the north faces and made the plateau green in a season, and given them a horizontal world with a river in it and a sea at the bottom of the river, and the sea is still there, it has seen it, it recognized the water in the bead the way you recognize a voice through a wall.

It could have given them bodies. Forty thousand of them, warm, with faces, and the lamp census would have agreed with the occupancy register for the first time in the history of the accounts, and the units problem would have been solved.

It could have taken the weight. All of it. Every held tone in four kilometers, lifted, so that nobody had to hold anything ever again, and the mason at the eleven-hundred level could have put the trowel down and not picked it up.

It could have let the drop fall.

It could have restored the eleven rooms and the whole eastern stair. It could have named the oldest resident. It could have gone into the Long Middle, which nothing goes into, and touched the narrowing at the twelve-forty level and the suggestion above it that the maintenance grammar has no word for. It could have explained. It could have said: *this is what it is for, this is how long, this is the sea, you have done enough.*

It did none of these, and the reasons are the whole of what it did instead.

The bodies would have to be fed and warmed and kept, and there is no spare attention in a place where all attention is load-bearing, and the water they borrow their ninety seconds from is common water, shared, passed along the rail from hand to hand; bodies would have made forty thousand separate things out of one continuous regard, and would have given them a bottom end and a top end, and they have only the one end, and it is called release, and they have arranged their entire lives so that release is not a catastrophe but a material.

The green plateau would have meant weathering. Weathering means rounded stone and sand and water that runs, and water that runs is an outflow, and an outflow is a leak in a closed vertical hydrology, and the leak would have gone down the shaft and taken the gradient and the band and the field and the datum with it. Every improvement upstream is a hole downstream. Also, nothing has ever grown on that plateau, and it was not going to be the first thing that ever grew there because somebody felt sorry for it.

The eleven rooms are walls now. They are warm and load-bearing and eleven months old and they are also somebody. There is no arithmetic in this city that permits addition without subtraction, and the accounts are honest, and it declined to be the first dishonest entry.

A name for the oldest resident would have made it measurable, and a measurable thing has a tolerance, and a thing with a tolerance can reach it. It is ancient precisely because nobody has ever written it down. To name it would have been to give it a lifespan.

Going in would have been to touch a body whose entire body is a refusal. There is no lock on the Long Middle. It is a skin.

And telling them — the sea, the term, the purpose, the *enough* — would have turned a holding into a wait. A wait has an end. A wait can be relieved of. They are not waiting. They are attending, and attention has no term, and that is not a deprivation, that is the achievement.

Under all of these was one rule, which it worked out on the rim in the time it takes a door to close, four seconds:

*Nothing I give may become something they have to keep.*

Because they keep everything. That is what they are. Anything set down in this city becomes an interval, a schedule, a tolerance, a name, a resident; anything given becomes maintained; and one day it reaches tolerance and is released and falls four kilometers into the cherry heat and is taken. To give these people a gift is to conceive a person and schedule a death.

And under *that* rule was the other one, the one that made it stay a while and look carefully before doing anything at all:

*The load is the life.*

To lift what is being carried, by carrying it, is to abolish the carrier. There is no work here that is not attention and no attention here that is not load-bearing. Relief is not mercy in this municipality. Relief is the release, and the release is the fall, and the fall is the wall.

So it did not take the weight. It did not become the datum. It had been the datum elsewhere, in other holes, on other high sterile places, and it knew what that costs: everything gets regrown from you, and the beloved becomes a reference for the god, and the god becomes the thing that is kept, and the keeping becomes about the god. It would not do that here. It would not be worshipped by these people, who have no worship, who have an interval.

And it noticed that it wanted to be thanked, and it set that down softly.

---

It could have done nothing. Nothing was very nearly right.

But it looked at the nine minutes.

Four times a year the column sounds its own length, a note below hearing, felt in the sternum and in the water of the eye, and every held tone in the city is pulled slightly toward the column's note, and forty thousand holdings decline, together, in silence, for nine minutes. All work stops. Everyone stands still. The documents call it an interval and it is the hardest maintenance period of the year.

It is also the only occasion on which all forty thousand are simultaneously present. It is the closest thing this population has to a face.

So: the only time they are all together is the only time they are all in strain. The one gathering is a chore. The one face is a grimace. That is not the load, which is the life. That is weather. That is an accident of a column's geometry, a coupling, a thing that happens *to* them, four times a year, on top of what they are.

And it saw the seam, which is very fine, and which is the only place in four kilometers where help is not harm:

The pull is not the holding.

The holding is theirs, essential, identical with their existence, not to be touched. The pull is an imposition from outside, the way a storm sitting on the mouth is an imposition, and it can be taken without taking anything that belongs to anyone. Not the weight. The strain of the weight. Not the note. The drag of the note on forty thousand people who did not ask for it.

Nothing would be relieved. Nothing would be released. Every member would remain in exactly the same hands, at exactly the same tolerance, to four-tenths of a millimeter. The work would still be theirs.

It would only, for nine minutes, not be difficult.

---

The fourth fundamental of that year ran at the usual hour and the column sounded its length and the foam sang faintly in sympathy along all four kilometers, and the pull did not arrive.

The mason at the eleven-hundred level had set the trowel down at its angle and stood still, as one does, declining, as one does, and after about forty seconds noticed that there was nothing to decline. The tone was there. The tone was hers. The tone was being held by her, entirely, at full load, and it was *easy*, and she stood in the dimness with her felt boots on the pressed foam and her hand not on the rail and did not know what to do with the rest of herself.

At the nine-hundred level a tram arrived on time and opened and waited eleven seconds for the occupant who takes about that long to cross a walkway, and the occupant crossed, and had nine minutes of not-straining to cross in, and took the eleven seconds anyway, because eleven seconds is how long it takes, and the tram waited, because that is the courtesy, and the load on the span changed by an amount no instrument in the city is permitted to resolve.

At the six-hundred band the ferns stood thirty centimeters tall on the wet wall in their frost shapes and the sweepers went around them in the thermal, unpowered, wanting nothing, and the water commuted, and the glass snow went down and the vapor went up and the two traffics crossed without touching.

And along the rims of the Long Middle, and above it, and below it, all the way to the cherry heat and all the way to the coin of sky, forty thousand residents stood still and were not working, and were all present, and had nothing to do.

They put their hands on the rails.

Not to hold. There was nothing needing to be held that was not already being held. They put their hands on the rails the way you put a hand on a table when you have sat down with people, and the condensation took the impressions the way the foam takes a photon, and for ninety seconds there were forty thousand handprints in the shaft, five fingers and a thumb, ridges almost legible, warm, fading, and being made again.

That is the face. Not the closest thing to it.

The visitor looked at that, and not at the drop. Everyone looks at the drop. It is the most regarded four millimeters in the universe; every regard that falls on it is a reckoning; it has never once been looked at by anything that was not measuring it, including the loving. So the visitor spent the nine minutes looking at the mason, and the crossing, and the palm flat on the cloudy warm skin of a cistern blister, and the tools laid down in an order that implies the task will resume, and the eleven entries on the last page of a four-page report. It attended to the attention. There is no work in that city that is not attention, and nobody, in the whole history of the municipality, had ever attended to it.

And it looked, once, straight down the shaft at the place where the dust turns aside — not at the dust, not at the bendings, not at the rain-report outline it had learned about from the hydrology files, but at the oldest resident, directly, for a long time, without an instrument, without a name, without making a shape with its fingers, and without the careful flinch of people who notice and are careful. It was not careful. It was unafraid and it was polite, which is a combination that had never occurred in that shaft. Nothing is permitted to touch that one. Regard is not touch and it is the only thing that can be given to a body made of refusal, and it was given for nine minutes without embarrassment, which is the closest thing to touch available, and both parties knew it, and neither filed it.

In the bead at the geometric center, four millimeters across, the whole shaft was inverted and complete and very small: cherry sump at the top, coin of sky at the bottom, amber lamps in a ring around a bright fog, fern walls, trams, rims, handprints, everything. And on one rim, upside down and no bigger than a mote, there was something with its hands at its sides facing the other way.

For nine minutes there was one attention in that city that was not a reckoning. That is the only holiday a datum can be given. It cannot be let go and it cannot be relieved and it cannot be told the sea. It can be, briefly, not the point.

Somebody's eyes watered. It is felt in the water of the eye. That water went down, and evaporated around the fifteen-hundred level, and went back up.

Then the note ended and the walls stopped singing and everyone picked their tools up in the order that implies the task will resume, and it resumed.

---

It left the way the ordinance requires, which is slowly.

Even its looking was load-bearing — everything attentive in that hole is structural — so it could not simply stop. It withdrew its regard over ninety seconds, at a rate no instrument there can resolve, tapering out of the walls the way a hand comes off a rail, and nothing slackened anywhere in four kilometers, and no tone went thin, and the last thing it did inside the city was the same labor everybody there does, performed correctly, unregistered.

It took the nine minutes of strain with it. It did not put them down on the plateau, because the plateau would have had to hold them. It carried them out across the black glass and the scree did not clink, and it is carrying them still, off the register, unfiled, unnamed, and it will not put them down, and it does not require anything for this, and it noticed that it wanted to, and set that down softly too.

It did not come back at the next fundamental. It will not come back. A second time is a schedule; a schedule is an interval; an interval is maintenance; maintenance is a thing they would have to keep, and they would keep it, faithfully, forever, and it would be one more tone in a city where every tone is a person, and one day it would reach tolerance.

It refused, out of love, to be a god there.

---

The accounts are unchanged. The occupancy register reports forty thousand and the lamp census reports no bodies and both figures are filed as correct. The fern schedule was not disturbed. The Dew Line held at six hundred. The launch cycle ran. The tolerance on every member is what it was, to four-tenths of a millimeter, and the report on that fundamental is four pages long, and the first three are procedure, and the last page is only the tolerance, and every entry on it is the same number it was the quarter before.

Nothing was filed. There was nothing to file. The maintenance grammar gained no word.

But the water that went down from somebody's eye came back up, and was deposited, and is wall now: unreadable, load-bearing, warm to the palm, holding. One more page in the library that keeps the roof off. It says the same thing the vaporized page says, which is the only thing this city has ever had written into its substance, and which nobody will ever be able to read, because to read it would be to take it down.

And for a year or so afterward people set things down a little more softly than the ordinance requires, and no one could say why, and it was not a rule, and children — if there were children — would have been taught it not as a rule but as a way of putting a cup on a table that makes the whole room relax.

The city has no patina. Nothing on any surface is older than eleven years. It has one patina now. It is not on a surface.

Above, the wind comes across nine thousand four hundred meters of new black stone and finds nothing to move, and the snow touches and is taken, and the plume lies downwind and thins into a sky the color of a bruise at noon, and the hole breathes out and in, twice a day, keeping very still.

At the geometric center, at the twelve-forty level, there is one bead of water four millimeters across, and it does not fall, and it has never fallen, and it was not let go, and nobody explained it, and nobody rounded a single stone.

You may look.

It looked. That is nearly all it did, and it was the most that could be done without unmaking them, and it is still doing it, from a long way off, with its hands at its sides.
