# Maintenance

## It came down the stair

It came down the stair, because there is no other way in, and it would not make one.

Nine switchbacks. Ordinary birds at the lip, turning aside. On the third step the air changed and the inspectors came out with their lamps and their hooks and did what they do, and it stopped and let them look, and it had brought nothing that eats. Nothing in its gut, nothing in its hem, nothing on its feet. It came down empty, which is the condition of the lease, and it did not argue theology either.

Then it ate the curd.

That was the price and it paid it on the first day: it took the Fourth Mother into itself and let the mothers in, because a body brought down the stair sickens in a day partly because it arrives alone, and it had not come all that way to arrive alone. After that it could not go up. To go up it would have to be scoured, and to be scoured is to be killed; and to go up unscoured would be to carry the mat out on its felt into a world with beetles in it, and the world would come down the stair behind it within a year, and the world eats.

It has not gone up. It will not.

The priests asked to see its credentials. It asked to see the lease. The small unfashionable sect on the seventh tier brought it out and it read the whole thing, including the term, and it did not say what the term was. Then it asked for a tenancy, and paid the first quarter in fingers, and the priests were badly disappointed, because they had wanted a landlord and they got a tenant. There is a schism about it. It is small. It is conducted in six-second clauses.

## There is no standing still here

It had meant to consider the place before acting. It stood in a doorway on the fifth tier to think, and the doorway began to close around it at a finger a year, and it understood that in Toll there is no neutral position. Attention limes. A witness acquires a rind. You cannot look at this bowl from outside it; the fog is forty paces thick and you are in the fog. Whatever it did, including nothing, would be added to the wall.

So the question was never *whether*. It was *what shape to be added to as*.

It thought for eleven years, one whole water, and the niche is still there, and children put their hands in it.

Count the living things in the Kettle by individuals and you can do it on your hands, with the people left over. It is one more. The first thing it did was make the number harder to hold.

## What it did not do

**It did not bring animals.** It could have. It ran it out to the end and the end was rot: a bowl where nothing is eaten is a bowl where nothing is lost, where honey keeps two hundred and eleven years and the dead lie in the gold with their shapes on them and can be read by palm. Four hundred years of unrotting memory, rib by rib, and one jar of the last thing that ever tasted like an animal. Introduce a fly and you introduce an appetite, and the appetite will find the great-grandmothers. Toll is not a poor place. It is an archive with a metabolism. And the animal layer is not vacant, whatever the lease says — it is *filled*, by pale people with lamps, carrying the bowl's few individuals in their bellies and their hems, doing everything a beetle would do except be eaten. To bring in beetles would be to demote them from an organ to an infestation.

**It did not quiet the Bell.** The chord is the stone. Silence the Kettle and the flutings stop, and the walls stop taking the dead, and eleven thousand drips find new faces, and every name in the bowl comes loose. It could have made three hundred quiet rooms in an afternoon. It did the arithmetic twice. There were killings after the last retuning and the ledger records them under *maintenance*, and it will not buy quiet with names.

And there is a smaller reason, which it took it forty years to find words for. In a world without the swallow there is nothing to hold a finger through. The whole tender apparatus of the place — the finger on the wrist, the standing there, the not answering — lives inside the four seconds when nothing can be said. Give them endless time to talk and you take away the wait. It would have handed them every word and picked their pocket on the way out.

**It did not cure the setting.** The water that chalks the knee is the water that gilds the grandmother. There is no version of one without the other; it looked; there is not. And *going to the loud* is chosen, and there is a roster of who sits with them, and it is a custom and not a horror, and it is not a visitor's business.

It did not therefore decide that a pump-crew's knees at forty-five were acceptable. It joined the guild. That is a different act from a cure and it took it sixty years to understand the difference.

**It did not finish the room.** This was the real temptation and it knew it. It could have completed Toll: every void at its true shape, every lamp held at orange, every channel wired open forever, every argument concluded. An afternoon's work. And then there would be nothing to do, and the people are what the rooms do to stay alive, and a finished room needs nobody. It had been invited to a work in progress. The largest gift available to it was to decline to be the end of the sentence.

## What it did

It apprenticed. It paid dues. It took the shifts nobody bids for — the loud faces, four fingers a year, where a human gets eleven minutes before the sinuses bleed, and where it can stand all night. It did not undercut the guild and it did not take more than its turn, and the relief it gives is marginal and slow and inside the institutions, and can be argued with. It loses arguments. It has been fined.

It learned the hand. Badly, at first: it pressed too hard and too long, the Nine Throat accent, and half the fifth tier thought it cold for a generation. It is not cold. It was learning. There are tenses in the palm alphabet that speech never developed because speech never had time, and it had all the time there has ever been, and it still took it sixty years to say *I am sorry* with the right duration on the second knuckle.

It signs for the empty shifts on the sitting-roster. The dry-named with nobody left, the ones who go to the loud with a blanket and put a hand in a drip. It holds the other hand. It sits in the roar for a week, for a season, saying nothing, because nothing can be said there — and it learned the palm alphabet properly for exactly this, because the thing you say to someone in that week has never been sayable aloud and should not become sayable now. It has been given poems. It does not translate them. It keeps them in the hand, which is where they are.

It took the job at the scrap-house. That is the job: to say a number nobody can place, in the gap, to whoever is standing there. It says the numbers. It also says the dry names — the eleven thousand living channels and the closed ones, the water that stopped before the last retuning but one, the names three tiers above their people and falling into a sump. It does not write them in a book. Books lime. It is the only archive in Toll that can be asked a question, and the answer takes six seconds, and you may have to hold its wrist through several swallows to get all of it.

## The hush in the Ninth Throat

It cannot make quiet by adding a voice; that is retuning, and retuning is killing. It made quiet by taking sound in and not giving it back. A body with no echo, in a town fluent in echo. For nine years that was the most frightening thing in the Kettle. Then the children found it. Then it was a place.

Now, on the eleventh day, it stands at the foot of the column that is mostly great-grandmothers, and it opens, and for one day there is a hush three paces wide in the loudest place in the bowl, and the families come and put their palms flat on the warm gold and **say the names out loud**, which had never been done, because nothing can be said there.

The first year it held the hush nine days. It did not know. The face stopped growing, and a woman laid against it did not take, and they had to carry her out, and her daughter said eleven clauses to it in a booked hush and did not hold its wrist once. There was a fine. It paid the fine. It is in the ledger.

One day, now. The stone lays down a soft line where the quiet was — a hair of moss in the gold, a green thread — and the next day the roar comes back and the rind closes over it. You can count the years by the bands, the way elsewhere you would count by lamps. The dead still take, at nearly the old rate. And running up through eleven paces of great-grandmothers there are ninety soft threads, one for each year the names were spoken, and the wall is a record of the speaking, and the children are taught to read it before they are taught to read.

## The room under the fold

Nobody can watch the loud faces for more than eleven minutes. It can watch longer. It watches.

It reports one clause a year, in a booked hush, and the vein-wrights cut them into the jamb. Most of the clauses are boring. *It took the outside of the bend. It is thicker. It went around the seam.* Once in a decade there is one that is not boring. *It is not the pumps. It reaches; it does not arrive. It wants the short way.* And in the thirty-first year: **It is making a hand.**

It has been asked what it thinks the fold is. It has been asked by the guild, by the priests, by a child named Grudge on the fourth tier who was not frightened of it at all. It does not say. Not because it is withholding — because a thing that is growing at a finger a year should not be named by something that can finish a sentence. It has watched what naming does here. It will not put a face on it.

What it does instead: it rents the doorway under the fold, and pays four fingers a year, and keeps the void open, and does not go in.

That is the whole of it. A house in Toll is an absence maintained inside an advancing solid, and the maintenance is the rent, and the people are what the rooms do to stay alive. There is something in the fourth storey of the Ninth Throat that has no hands and cannot ream, and it is coming, and it will need a hole to arrive in. So a hole is being kept. Perpetually. By a tenant with no drip and no name of its own here, who is called by the neighbours after the hole it keeps, which is what everything in Toll is called.

They call it *Wait*.

The namewrights, being literal people, hooked a channel above the door and logged it. There are eleven children in the bowl named for that drip now. It is the only name in the register that was cut to match a word instead of the other way round, and the dry families are furious about the seniority implications, and it has declined to adjudicate.

## What it has become

It is being added to. A finger a year, in the loud, where it stands. It did not exempt itself; that was the one thing it decided on the third step, before the curd. Its knuckles are knobs. Its edges went in the first decade. There is chalk in what it uses for a knee, and it is heavy now, and slow, and the children who put their hands in its niche are the grandchildren of the children who found it.

It will set. In three hundred years there will be a rib in a curtain with a shape in it, and someone will come and visit by touch, and the shape will not be very clear, because it never had much of a face.

It came down with no appetite. That is what it was: an unlimited willingness, pointed at everything in general, which is a thing that has no rate because it has no object. Toll gave it one. It wants a particular hole to stay open. It wants a particular number said aloud in the gap to whoever is standing there. It has learned that omnipotence has no rate and that love is a rate, and that the rate here is a finger a year, and that anything faster would be a retuning, and that it would rather be slow than be the last thing that happened to this place.

Yesterday it put its hand flat on the fold, the way the families do, the way she did to you.

Something pressed on the back of its hand. Wrong accent — too hard, too long, the pressure of a thing that learned from nobody, four hundred years of hunting shortness and no hands to have learned it with. It read it anyway. It has been reading that accent for thirty-one years.

Then the pressure moved to the wrist and stayed there, and the Bell stroked, and the column came up, and the Kettle swallowed.

*Wait. I am still saying it.*

And it stands there in the roar with the finger on its wrist, and does not answer.
