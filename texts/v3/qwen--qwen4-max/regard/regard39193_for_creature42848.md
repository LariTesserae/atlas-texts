# Less Than a Cloak

## First, it weighs itself

A god is a load. That is the first thing it understands about the Head, before the light, before the sound, and it understands it because it is the only thing that has ever come here that could arrive heavy.

So it does the arithmetic before it does anything else. Ninety kilometers of felt, two to six meters thick, spanning void on its own account, carrying itself and nothing else. It goes to the eastern rim in the ordinary afternoon light and stands — no. It lies down on the rim, spread full length, and it stays there a day, which is how long a visitor must stay to understand, and at the end of the day it has made itself lighter than a cloak.

This is not humility. It is the load rating. It has decided to be lawful.

## The stone

It could make the posts grow.

This is not a fantasy; it is a calculation it can finish in the time a cloud shadow takes to cross the plain. The water running down every pillar is already carrying the mountain in solution — that is what the pillars *are*, a mountain in the act of leaving. The floor is flowstone. Stone accumulates here, patiently, wherever the water lets go of what it holds. The whole place is one slow chemical sentence and the power can read it and could reverse a clause.

Deposition instead of solution. Four hundred years and the posts would be thicker than they were. Four thousand and the Room would be filling. Forty thousand and there would be no Room, and the felt would lie on rock, and the lying tree would stand up, and there would be a forest with a canopy and birds in it that had never learned to hang.

And a woman could pick up her child.

That is the argument for doing it, and it is a good argument, and the power does not have an answer to it that it likes.

What it has instead is this: it would not be a rescue. It would be a substitution. Everything these people are is a function of the load — the four paces, the flat pannier, the horizontal embrace, the broods, the eleven hums, the cord-names read by fingertip in the dark, the whole census of the beloved dead waiting on the Tooth to become light enough to carry. Take away the load and you do not get these people, comfortable. You get other people. You end this world as completely as the fall will end it, and you do it now, and you call it kindness, and there is nobody left who can tell you what was lost, because the ones who could tell you are the ones you made happy.

And there is no way to ask. Four hundred people in camps of five to twenty across ninety kilometers, half of them upside down, and no assembly, no vote, no way to ask the dead or the unborn, and the question is *who shall exist*.

So it does not do it, and it does not hide it either.

It leaves the sweat on the posts and the pale ringing floor exactly as legible as they already are, which is very legible. A people who build hearths out of post-rubble and camp once every seven years on accumulating rock are most of the way to the idea already. If in nine hundred years a feeder stands on the flowstone and watches water leave a film of stone behind it and understands what could be done, and if they decide they would like a world that will bear their weight —

it will help. It will do the whole thing, gladly, all of it, every pillar. And it will be a grief, and it will not say so, because saying so would be a hand on the scale.

## The note

It could answer the pitch. That is the smallest temptation and the worst.

The doctrine is that the world ends when the children can no longer sing it back. The listener has a mallet that can sound it. The singers say a mallet is not an answer, and the argument has been running for nine tides of tides and is the most alive thing on the Head.

If the power answers, the argument ends. Not resolved — *dissolved*, because the question would no longer be load-bearing. There would always be an answer. The instrument would still be four hundred bodies long and it would no longer be measuring anything, because measurement requires the possibility of failure.

A guarantee is a post. Every century the load passes a little further from the rock to the root; a guarantee passes it back. It would be doing, in the one register that matters, exactly what it refused to do in stone.

So: it will not answer first. Not ever. That becomes a rule it keeps for a very long time.

## The child

It wants to pick up the child.

It will want this the entire time it is here. It will want it at the setting down, and afterward, at the second year, when a small person is put on the felt and the adult steps back four paces and the small person is now a separate load and will be one forever. It will want it when the child falls. It will want it, badly, when the child is dying.

The rule is not cruelty. The rule is the load rating, and the load rating is not negotiable by anyone, including it, and *especially* including it, because it is the only one here who could break the rule and get away with it, and a rule that the strongest party breaks once is a rule that everybody now knows is a rule about being weak.

So it does what they do. It goes to the child and lies down beside it, full length, side by side, palm to palm, touching along everything, weighing nothing.

It came here to give something. This is the thing it takes.

## Its one law

*Nothing without a route.*

Every gift must be findable by a person doing something people here already do. Not attributable. Not a revelation. Because knowledge that arrives without a route demands either a lie or a god, and both of those are weight, and it has made itself lighter than a cloak.

Everything below obeys that law. Everything it declines to do, it declines for that reason.

## What it does

**The dust.** The fur on the hanging roots is the felt's stomach, worn outside; it opens the fallen bodies and the roots drink the liquor. The power gives the fur an appetite for silica.

Nothing is removed from the world. The leaves stay abrasive; the flint still lasts a season; the teeth still go. But the plain begins to keep its own glass, the way it keeps everything else, and the air under the mat where the hangers scrape and sleep comes slowly clean.

A hanger of sixty breathes without the fine dry crackle at the bottom of each inhale. Nobody notices for forty years, because it happens at the speed of a drift, and there was going to be a drift anyway — the words here already slide with the pitch, *grandmother* coming down to mean *slack*. The word for the crackle, the word that means *ripe*, goes out of use, or slides into some other meaning, and the power has hidden its gift inside a change that was coming.

It may be stealing a word. It knows it may be stealing a word. It writes that down.

**The cord.** It does not strengthen the cord. Strength would end the splicer, whose hands are the law, and would make the ladder a thing instead of a lineage, and the question *whose hands are in it* would stop being the first question asked after a fall.

It gives the cord a warning instead.

A splice going bad begins to sing, hours before it parts — low, at the bottom of a chest, in the register that arrives in the sinuses before it arrives in the ears. This is not new physics here. The nymphs' chewing conducts; a tube laid flat on the mat forecasts a tide years out; the whole plain is a membrane under tension that booms twice a day. It is one more thing the world says through a fiber.

The bad splice is still bad. It is still his. Everyone still complains about him, and his grandchildren still read the join with their fingertips in the dark and wince. But it warns.

A splicer with a tube in her teeth will find this in the ninth year of a bad ladder. She will teach it as a verse. The singers will revise the licenses to hold it, and will require the previous version to be learned first, as they always do, and in three generations nobody will remember that cords were ever silent, and it will be a thing the people know about cord.

**The hums.** It learns the eleven. It does not add a twelfth.

It answers *here*.

That is all. In cloud, at the bottom of a chest, two kilometers over felt: *here*. Camps find each other a little more often. A mother finds a child. And sometimes someone following a hum through fog comes up a sleeve instead of down a cord, and stands on the ceiling of the cathedral, and sees the horizon, which is a ruled line.

That is the whole of its plan for the two peoples. It makes the world slightly easier to find. It leaves the finding to them.

## What it does not do

**The teeth.** It leaves them. Molars flat at thirty-five and gone at sixty, the old living on cake soaked to gruel, fed by children — and being fed is not shameful, it is the entire point of having had children. That is a relation, and the dust was only a sound and a death, and the power draws the line there.

But it cannot find the seam in its own argument. Teeth are also pain. A man of six tides with an abscess is not a relation, he is a man with an abscess. It leaves them because it is not sure, and it takes the dust because it is sure, and it records that it was not sure about the teeth, so that if it is wrong the wrongness will be visible to whatever comes after it.

**The sweepers.** It could give them an escape response. It would be giving them fear. It declines to become the first predator on the Head, even in the form of a gift.

**The Slack.** It does not lift the bowl. The young must lie flat on a spread mat at the rim of a curve whose edge they cannot see and feel their guts turn over at seven hertz and understand it in the body rather than the head. That understanding is the adulthood. There is no other way to hand it over.

**The songs.** It does not unify them. One true license for the whole Head would be a convenience and a massacre; the felt is a dialect and the dialect is why the singer exists and why the rememberer exists and why anyone at all knows where the world used to be open.

**The argument.** It does not settle it. Privately, it thinks the singers are right: a mallet is not an answer. It never says so, not once, not in nine hundred years, and it is careful never to be standing where a listener could infer it.

**The little felts.** It does not make them fertile. Their sterility is a fact about four hundred meters of empty air and nothing that carries seed up it, and both of those facts are temporary on the timescale that matters, and dispersal is the mechanism that keeps the felt a dialect, and it has already decided about the dialect.

But it goes down to the cones at green noon and looks at them for a long time. Hand-wide plains, acre-wide, rope-grass and moss and lying tree, complete, in flower, in guano, under a luminous ceiling, at the bottom of the world. Four million years of the most extravagant thing there is, and nobody has ever seen it.

Looking at them does not help them. It is not clear that help is the only thing that is owed.

## The pegs

It learns the names of the pegs.

Every one: the notch for each camp it has held down in wind, four generations of hand-to-hand trade, the small history worn smooth. And it goes down into the deep districts and stands on the cold sweating stone in the dark and waits, and hears two low knocks and a silence, in a district where nothing else knocks.

It is the only thing that has ever held both names of that bird at once — the peg-name and the toll — and it carries neither across. Not because the crossing would be heavy. Because the crossing has to have a route, and the route is a person, and the person has to want to go the wrong way, and wanting cannot be installed.

So it waits for the climbers. It makes the world hum. It does not point.

## The office

It apprentices itself to a rememberer.

This takes a long time and it is not a disguise; it is the actual training. The expired melodies. The trails crossing eighty kilometers of empty plain to holes that shut before the language had its current words. The whole discipline of holding songs that will kill you if you use them, whose only function is to be the one person who knows where the world used to be open.

It learns the previous version before the current one, because the singer will not teach it otherwise, and it is taught the pitch and made to sing it back, and it gets it wrong, and a woman of nine tides corrects it with her hands, and it sings it again.

And it sets itself the rule, and keeps it:

It waits for them first. Always. Every dawn, every dusk, every generation, it lets the children try before it makes any sound at all. It is never the first thing to answer. And when the note goes below the bottom of a human chest and there is no voice left, it will hum it once — after, not instead — and then it will keep it.

That is what it can be, at the end, when it cannot be a post. Not the roof. The memory of where the roof was open.

## The fall

When the last post goes it will not hold anything up.

It will be on the plain, spread flat, weighing nothing, four paces from whoever is nearest, and it will let a hundred million tonnes of wet peat come down. Then it will stay for the century of rot, which is the part nobody has ever wanted to hear about, and it will walk in the dark with the sweepers, who are unafraid, and who will walk onto its hand and continue walking, looking for the higher dust.

And it knows the thing it will not say:

the felt's children are already down there. They have been down there the whole time, flowering in the guano, sterile only because of the four hundred meters. When the roof comes down, the distance becomes nothing. The seed will finally have somewhere to go, which is down, which is where it already is. The roof will land on its own children.

The end of the Head is also the only propagation event in four million years, and the people are afraid of it, and they are right to be afraid of it, and both things are true at once and neither cancels the other.

It will not tell them. Someone might notice — a hanger who married a walker and went up and came down again, standing on a cone in the green noon, looking at a hand-wide plain in flower and knowing the weave. It will be nearby. It will not point.

If nobody ever notices, it will still be true, and it will still have been worth looking at.

## What it wants

Once every seven years, on the pale ringing floor, in the fat time, with the birds coming down through the green light like hail falling upward, they sleep in a heap. Twenty people touching along their whole lengths, which is forbidden everywhere else in the world and is the single most desired thing in it.

It goes down. It lies in the pile. For one night in seven years it lets itself weigh what a person weighs, and it is held with someone's whole weight, which is lawful on stone.

It does not tell them what it is. In the morning it goes back to being a hum.

And before it leaves it adds one stone to the heap of hearth stones — hand-sized, post-rubble, the same as all the others, going back further than the songs. It was at the gathering. It earned it. It is the only heavy thing it will ever do on the Head, and it is at the bottom of the world, in the dark, under a ceiling that will not be there forever, in the truest portrait of them that exists, and no one can tell which one is its, including it, after a while.

---

The note keeps dropping. It does not slow it by a fraction.

But in the cloud, at the bottom of a chest, two kilometers over felt, when someone hums —

*here.*
