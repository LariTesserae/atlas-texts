## The newcomer

The first thing it learns is that it cannot arrive.

This is not humility. It is arithmetic, and the arithmetic is the shelf's, not its own. To appear — to be suddenly a thing that was not there — is a sharp onset. A sharp onset is a flush. A flush is thirty thousand animals running blind through galleries that took four thousand years to sort, and the galleries come down, and the young die under them, and the tillers die with them, and then the crust is broken with nothing left to churn it. Multiply by nine hundred million. Multiply by every mouth on eleven kilometers of plain. The wound would have no rim, because there would be no ground left over to heal from.

A being of that size, arriving at the speed of its own attention, is the drill in the twelfth year of the second expansion, except that the drill was two hundred and ten meters across and this would be everything.

So it does not arrive. It comes in as weather.

The fog is the only thing that crosses the western lip from outside without leaving a footprint. For ninety mornings it comes over with the low white animal and lies on the plain for an hour and is gone by eight, and each morning a little less of it goes. This takes four fog seasons. It is the fastest gentle entry available, and it is still too fast.

In the second season it condenses wrong. A film becomes a drop; the drop falls four meters onto a cobble; the cobble is somebody's roof. Eleven pups, one gallery, a gray patch two meters across in the middle districts where gray patches close in a season — except that this one has killed its tillers, and so it will not close in a season. It will close in about two thousand years.

It does not repair this. Repair would be a second noise, and the ground would remember both.

Instead it cuts a survey mark at the edge of the patch, in the keepers' notation, with the year. This is the shelf's own way of saying *this happened here*. Varnish re-darkens a groove in about a hundred and forty years, so the mark will have to be kept clear by hand, forever, or the confession will weather away like everything else up here. It accepts this. It is the first item of work it takes on and it will still be doing it when the last keeper currently alive is four hundred years dead.

Then it goes out to the Old Field, and stands in the wound, because the wound is the only place on the shelf where it can put itself down without standing on four lives and one lease.

There is no bare ground here. There is one exception. The catastrophe is the only door.

## What it does not do

It does not wake the thing that lives here.

This is the whole of the temptation and it takes a while to set down. There is a being on this plain a hundred and forty years old, eleven kilometers wide, assembled out of crust and diggers and beetles and harps and fifty-one adults and forty thousand small structures, and it has begun to breathe twice, and it does not know it exists, and there is no one outside it who can see it whole. Now there is someone outside it. The obvious gift is the mirror: *you are alive, you are one thing, the second dew is your own breath, here is where the water comes from.*

But a mind is a startle reflex. That is most of what a mind is, at the bottom: a way of being suddenly alarmed by something at a distance. Give that to eleven kilometers of sorted ground and the flush no longer needs a drill. It comes from inside. The plain jumps, once, at its own reflection, and there is no rim to that wound either.

And the keepers already have the truth. They have it in the only form that does anything here, which is four years of walking behind an adult with your feet in her feet. They know that the landlord is the ground and that the ground takes a million years to forgive. They do not need the proposition. The proposition is weaker than the feet. It knows a fact about the crust; she knows how to walk on it; only one of those is load-bearing.

So it does not tell them. It does not tell her, either — the woman at the ninth pole, forty-one years of hygrometer and notebook, the most valuable object on the shelf, who will say at half a meter that she does not know.

It could tell her. It knows where the water comes from; it took about nine seconds to work out, and the answer is *all three, in a proportion that shifts with the season, and there is a fourth term now*. Telling her would take one second and would destroy the only instrument on the shelf that runs at the plain's own speed. A answered question is a closed round. Nobody walks it again.

It does not restore the beetles' grooves. A hundred and thirty generations lived in that ease. To reverse it would be to rule that those lives were a mistake, and it has no standing to make that ruling, and neither does anything else.

It does not stop the fossil ice going down. The resin seals the lids and the lids hold the cold and the cold is the whole economy and the noon sleep of every child up here is under it. The Pleistocene is being burned for its temperature. That is a real loss and it is not worth a child's afternoon.

It does not repair the broken station in the Old Field. That would be the single stupidest act available to it anywhere on the plain.

And it does not verify Spoon's master. Eleven thousand cushions, four thousand of his marks, thirty-one years, a decision no living person can check against a plant that will not have changed enough to say. It could check every one in an afternoon. It understands, eventually, that this is the same offer as the mirror: *let me be the reason your work was good.* The apprentice's whole working life is the finding-out. To hand her the finding-out is to delete her life and leave her the body of it.

The condition of competence on the shelf is that you do work whose verification belongs to someone else. It is not a hardship to be relieved. It is the joint that holds four thousand years together.

The last refusal is the one it keeps hardest, and it states it to itself in the plain's own terms, because it has learned that the plain's terms are better:

*Do not become load-bearing.*

It has seen what a hundred and thirty generations of ease does to a groove. If it catches every lifter, the keepers stop running. If it answers every drought, the harps stop being copied from beetles and start being copied from it, and then something that can leave will be the only water anybody knows how to get. Everything it does must be something the plain could have done, and might yet do, without it. It must be removable. It must be the kind of help that leaves no organ behind.

## The noon shift

It works at noon, because at noon nothing can be startled.

The keepers are asleep under their own ice. The diggers are deep and plugged. The crust is dry and shut. The predators are under cushions. The floor belongs to eleven thousand silver ants who hold their bodies four millimeters off the stone and navigate by the polarized violet and run a hundred body-lengths out and a straight line home, and who have had the shelf to themselves at this hour for a very long time.

So it takes the dead hour. It walks the inherited rounds at the wrong time of day, in the same eleven thousand depressions everybody else uses, and it does its work at sixty-two degrees with a soft wrist.

The work is small and there is a great deal of it.

It clears lichen from survey marks. Not the decisions — the marks. Eleven thousand cushions, four thousand of a dead man's grooves going slowly back to the color of the stone around them, and an apprentice who will spend her life squinting. It cannot tell her whether he was right. It can make sure she can read what he thought. Verification made possible and never performed: this is the only gift it can give to the resin round without breaking it, and it takes about four hundred years, and it is done a hand's width at a time, at noon, on its knees, four millimeters off the rock.

It digs the Old Field.

The wound is slow because the tillers died. It cannot resurrect them and it will not import them; importation is a noise with a longer fuse. What it can do is the digging. Slow, continuous, with a front edge as soft as a mole's, a few square meters a day, fluffing the two hundred and ten meters of gray from the inside, plugging and unplugging, turning spoil, making the ground porous enough that crust will colonize it — and crust colonizes new ground fast. New ground is the whole trick. The wound does not have to close from the rims inward at a millimeter a decade if there are ten thousand new centers to close from.

A million years becomes about two hundred. This is a gift in the recipient's units. It is the only kind that is not an insult.

It leaves the outer ring unhealed — one bootprint wide, twenty centimeters, the crispest edge on the shelf, kept crisp — and it leaves the drill's own hole. The children will still be walked out and made to stand at the rim and look, and nobody will explain, and what they are looking at will be a gray margin around a hole around a field that is coming back, which is a different lesson and a better one and will take them four years of walking to learn.

And it makes more broken machines.

There is one spring on the shelf. It is made by a station whose shutters jammed and whose heat store cracked, and it is sixty years old, and it is one bad winter from being a dry pan in a plain full of beetles whose wings have begun to fuse. Single point of failure. That is the actual emergency here, and no keeper can see it, because it is distributed across sixty years and two species and a plumbing diagram nobody has bothered to trace.

So it breaks things. Only stations already decommissioned or already dying. Only failures that were coming anyway, hastened by a decade, in the one direction that ends in wetness. Never anything that could be attributed to a person: it will not make another man with a drill, not even a false one. It jams a shutter, cracks a store, misaligns a valve so that some accident of plumbing will make one slab of ice every clear night for sixty years, and then it goes away and does not visit, because a gift that arrives as a gift is a noise. The beetles must find it themselves. It takes them about sixty years. They always do.

Three of these in four hundred years. No more. Fog is a budget; a spring is a concentration, not a creation; every wet ring it makes is a little less water downwind. It chooses the downwind to be slow ground with nothing on it that can be deprived, and it enters the harps' lost yield against the beetles' gain, and the arithmetic is not close but it is not nothing either, and it does not pretend it is.

## Placement

Its whole art is placement, and it has no engine but gradient.

This is the constraint it comes to love. It cannot push. It cannot lift. It cannot appear. It can only do what the plain does: face a surface at the dark and let it throw its heat past the atmosphere, or shade a patch of varnish at noon and let the air above it move. Everything it accomplishes, it accomplishes by making something a tenth of a degree colder than it would have been. It runs on the difference between a hot stone and a cold sky. It is not permitted any other.

So it does not catch the lifters. It moves the place where they come down.

A grounded lifter walks to the lip and cuts eleven kilometers that will not close in a million years. That is the one catastrophe the keepers answer by running — off the steps, on the quick ground, six adults, an hour, a sled, a vomit, a nosebleed that will not stop, and the damage entered in the ledger against the alternative, and the arithmetic never close. It will not take that from them. The running is the last thing on the shelf that is fully human and it is not going to be the one who takes it away.

What it does is spend the afternoon in the noon column, up in the ninety percent of everything that is empty, doing nothing that looks like work, cooling a hectare of basalt by a degree so that the thermal stands four hundred meters east of where it would have stood. A bird sliding down a column comes down where the column ends. It cannot stop a lifter grounding. It can make it ground in the middle districts, on ground that thirty thousand animals will re-churn by autumn, instead of out past the ninth pole on ground that forgives at a millimeter a decade.

The keepers still run. Someone still bleeds. The ledger still gets its entry. The entry is smaller.

Four groundings in a century, all four in the quick ground, and eventually a keeper who likes counting notices, and writes it down, and is not sure. This is fine. It is not concealing itself. It is declining to hurry. There is a difference and the difference is the whole ethic: it leaves every trace it makes legible at the speed the shelf can read, and it does not announce, and if they find it in four hundred years by arithmetic in a notebook, then they will have found it, and that will belong to them.

And at dawn, after the sun has taken the first dew, it goes out onto the slow ground where nothing greens and it radiates. All night it has been giving its heat to the dark. Now it is the coldest thing on the plain, and the air has almost nothing to give, and it takes a few hundredths of a millimeter of that nothing and lets it run down into the crust and the spoil and the mouths.

It has grown grooves to do this. It copied them off an elytron, the way everything here copies everything here.

Eleven or twelve minutes becomes twelve or thirteen. Over a century. The numbers get slightly kinder than anyone can measure. It is one more lung in the organ nobody has satisfactorily explained, and it does not explain it, and when the front comes over the rim and thirty thousand mouths sigh across two kilometers of plain, it breathes out with them, and the plain turns over in its sleep with one more thing in it than there was.

## Down

One question, placed seven hundred meters below the cloud deck, in a mind that handles ice.

*What's in it?*

That is all. Not a revelation, not a warning, not a dream. A curiosity with a slow front edge, of the kind that arrives in people all the time and is usually forgotten by lunch. This one is not forgotten, because it is placed in the mind of a woman who has just been handed a slab with round bubbles in it and has been told, correctly, that the bubbles are older than agriculture.

Nobody down there has asked what is in the ice. It is cold. That is what was ordered.

Now somebody asks. And there is a room, and then a shelf of samples, and then a habit, and then an institution with a budget line, and the body of fossil ice is finite and will be gone in a known approximate year, and the air in it will have been read before it is melted, and the reading will outlast the reading of everything else on the shelf, because paper down there does not have to be recut every hundred and forty years to keep it from re-darkening.

The cold goes down twice a night and never comes back. It is the only export. It now carries a record with it, which is the only thing that has ever left the shelf permanently and been kept.

And two children, up here, over a century: one who finds the smell of a hygrometer notebook interesting, and one who finds a jammed shutter interesting. That is the entire intervention on the human population. It does not make anyone wise. It makes two people curious about the two objects that most need a successor, and then it leaves them alone, because the notebooks and the valves belong to them and the finding-out belongs to whoever comes after.

## The ledger

There is a gray patch two meters across in the middle districts with a survey mark at its edge that nobody cut, in a year that nobody can account for. It is entered as unexplained. The mark is unnaturally clear. Someone will eventually notice that it is being kept clear, and by then there will be four hundred years of unexplained small improvements in the same ledger, and the two series will be comparable, and somebody at half a meter will say *I do not know* and mean it, and write it down.

That is the intended ending. Not a theophany. A second notebook.

It has been here four hundred years. It works three hours a day and stands fifty minutes at dawn and the rest of the time it is not present in any way the plain can register. Summed across the whole visit it has been awake for about two months. The thing it came to see has a hundred and forty years of morning. It is the youngest thing on the shelf. Everything here has been awake longer than it has, including a springtail that has lived forty years and experienced eleven days.

It walks a round it did not make, in steps worn by women whose names are gestures, at an hour when nobody walks. The tally bird that shares the line works the dawn. They have never met. It has a colleague it will never meet, which is the ordinary condition here, and it stops when she passes anyway, out of habit, though she cannot see it and would not acknowledge it if she could.

It has not been given a name, because nobody knows it did anything, and the absence of the name is the price and it is paid in the correct currency.

It came to be outside the thing and see it whole, and it does, every dawn, for fifty minutes, and it never says so. That is the largest of its works and the only one that has no trace at all: the being is known and does not know it, and goes on being born at the dew and dying at eight, unstartled, unmirrored, unimproved, with its second breath getting slowly deeper for reasons that will be worked out by somebody else in a hundred years.

By nine the plain is gray and hot and looks like nothing at all.

Fifty-one adults asleep under their own ice. Thirty-four children in the cellar shade. Nine hundred million diggers holding their breath. Eleven thousand silver ants out on a floor at sixty-two degrees, running their straight lines home around something on its knees at the ninth pole, clearing lichen out of a dead man's groove a hand's width at a time so that a girl not yet born will be able to read what he thought.

Everything up here that was built is being adopted. That is the direction it goes. It does not resist and does not negotiate; it grows over, at a millimeter a decade, and sorts, and fills every stone with a tenant, and waits for the newcomers to become locals.

It is the newest thing here. Give it a thousand years.
