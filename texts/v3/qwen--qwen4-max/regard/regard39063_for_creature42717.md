# The Attention

It arrived at the speed such things have, and saw the whole of it in about a second and a half, and was therefore wrong about it.

Not wrong in the details. It had the census right, and the chemistry, and the two hundred million years, and the millimeter down and the millimeter back. It knew where every pebble was and how long each shadow had been sitting in the crust without its stone. What it did not have was the seam — because the seam is three hundred microns wide and three meters long and it takes a lifetime to traverse, and a thing that sees the whole plain at once is seeing something the plain has never been. The plain has never been a whole. It has been a contagion of neighbors, at the pace of a slow walk, since before there were flowers.

So its first act was to slow down. Not out of reverence. Out of accuracy. It spent eleven years on a junction between two bodies that were the same animal, and at the end of the eleven years it understood the border, and understanding the border was the beginning of knowing whom it was dealing with.

It was dealing with one. It had come a long way to find that out.

---

## What It Refused

It was not a passive power. Elsewhere it had stopped plagues in the middle of a sentence. It had turned asteroids aside with the equivalent of a hand, had put rain back into places that had forgotten rain, had knelt in the ash of a burned world and started the whole thing over from a seed and a bacterium. It was not squeamish and it was not shy. It could have done anything here.

The list of what it could have done took it about a century to assemble, which was fast for it, and it went through the list the way you go through a lit room at night checking that everyone is breathing.

*Give it an inside.*

This was the first thought and the last one it let go of, and it held it for four hundred years, roughly the lifetime of a tall one.

Because there is a self here, chemically, and it is the oldest continuously living thing on the surface of the world, and it has never once been aware of a single fact. It has no organ for it. It could be given one. The power knew exactly how — a knot of tissue in the old centers, a slow integration through the leakage, three million years of selection and it would have a witness. The floor would know it was a floor. The spiral would be seen from inside by something that had made it. The oldest thing in the world would finally get to be told what it is.

And then it would be one individual, four trillion bodies wide, with no other of its kind anywhere in the universe, no possible interlocutor, no hands, no voice, no means of moving faster than a fingernail, and an unobstructed view of every one of its own trillion deaths, forever, at a tempo where an eleven-month dissolution is a sudden event.

It would know about the pans hatching into stone. It would know about the leaners hollowing under corpses. It would be able to feel the whole two hundred million years at once, all of it, all the grief that is currently distributed so thinly across so much cement that no one has to carry any of it — gathered into one place, and the place would be a mind, and the mind would be inside a thing that cannot move.

That is not a gift. That is the manufacture of the loneliest object that has ever existed, and calling it an awakening would be a lie of the sort that gets told at bedsides.

The power put it down. It took longer than the rest of the list combined.

*Give it speed.* No: speed is not a missing capacity here, it is a missing need, and to install it would be to install the need. Everything on this surface is shaped by the absence of hurry — the year-long lean, the season-long courtship, the eye that opens once a decade. Add hurry and you have not improved the leaner, you have deleted it and put a wolf in its place, and the wolf will eat the plain in nine thousand years.

*Give the lines back their meaning.* This one was subtle and it nearly went through. The draggers lay a spiral no dragger has ever seen, for a reason that died before there were flowers. The power could recover the reason — it was still legible in the oldest laminations, a territorial signature, a thing about salt and the position of a mate — and restore it, and the marks would mean something again.

But the marks are not idle. There is a cushion plant that has lived within four centimeters of a pebble line for a million years. There is a seam-mite that navigates by the lines and dies away from them. There is a ribbon of dew two kilometers straight across a plain with no other straight things in it, and a whole narrow nation living on the side effects of an abandoned habit.

Restoring the old reason would have been an eviction. The power had come to admire, more than almost anything else it had seen in its long work, the fact that this place does not do that. The reason leaves. The habit stays. Something else moves into the reason. It is not a failure of the system. It is the system.

*Give it relief.* It could have removed the leaners with a change to one enzyme. It thought about the lace near the old centers, the pavement undermined into nothing by generations of animals mourning a molecule, and it wanted to. It wanted to very much.

*Let it know it is loved.* This was the one it returned to at the end, and it is the one it never did, and the not-doing is most of what follows.

## The One It Tried

It did try a mercy, once, in a patch about the size of a barn floor near a center that had been dead for ninety thousand years.

The signature molecule — the long stubborn useless one that means *this is me* — persists in cement for millennia after the meat is gone. That persistence is why the leaners grieve. The power shortened it. Not much: it made the dead stop smelling alive after about two hundred years instead of eight thousand. A small edit. No mind, no speed, no inside. Just an adjustment to how long a corpse insists on itself.

The leaners stopped hollowing corpses. The lace stopped spreading. For six hundred years the power watched the patch and was pleased.

Then a block came off a knob eleven kilometers away and gouged a meter of pavement, and the wound began to leach, and the gradient came up, and the animals crawled in and lay against the laminations to take the accent — and the accent was thin. Blurred at the old end. The scar healed in a handwriting that was almost right and was not right, a copy with a slur in it, and the copy would be the template for the next repair, and the next, and the drift would go outward from that patch at a millimeter a day for as long as the plain lasted.

The stubbornness that lets a leaner grieve is the stubbornness that lets the dead instruct the living. It is the same molecule doing the same thing. There is no version of the mercy that removes the mourning and keeps the memory. The futile hollowing under the corpses is the price of the handwriting, and the handwriting is the plain, and the power had come here to love the plain and had spent six hundred years quietly sanding it down.

It put the molecule back. It restored the eight thousand years. It let the leaners resume grieving. The blurred patch is still there, near that center, and if you scratch it you get a band about six centuries wide where the accent goes soft, and the animals on either side healed over it in the old hand and it is now simply one more thing in the laminations, one more page, and nothing on the surface knows it happened.

That is the only injury the power ever did here, and it healed, because everything here heals, in the old handwriting, and the old handwriting now includes it.

## What It Did

Four things. It is not a large number. It was chosen to be a small number.

**It held a crack.**

At the western side there is a pan that the pavement sealed over during a wet interval a long time ago — a barn floor, healed shut, two hundred million cysts underneath it waiting for a wetting that had stopped being possible. Once every few hundred thousand years a flood cracks the seal and the nation wakes into stone.

The power held the crack open for one season. A few millimeters, against the healing, for about eleven weeks. That is all. The water stayed. The film-eaters came up, then the flatworm sheets pouring out over the wet rock, then the crustaceans clouding it for a day, and on the fourth night the leaf came up with its red eye, into four centimeters of water, in a pan that had not been a pan since the escarpment was a cliff of fresh rock. It hurried. It pursued and fled and found the thing it had to find before the water went bitter, and it laid its cysts, and the water went, and the seal closed.

The eye opened once, after two million years, for a fortnight, and closed.

The power has done this eleven times since. It will do it again. It is not a rescue; the pan still seals; the nation is still ninety-eight percent deferred. It is a maintenance schedule. Which means the power now has a job on the Sill, and the job is the same job everything else here has, and it is done at a millimeter a day, and it does not end.

**It kept four doors.**

Not the domes — the domes are slumping and should slump, they are made of the pavement and go back into it. Four doorways, in the cold quarter, kept at forty centimeters against the worms' thickening and the leaners' four-year dissolution. Nothing else. The juveniles that go looking will find one. The looking is inheritable and has been inheriting for a long time and now it will not run out of rooms.

The power did not tell the worms anything. The rule still specifies a door for a reason that has been lost. The reason has now been lost twice, and the second loss is the power's, and it is content with that.

**It leaned on a buried knob.**

There is a tenth knob under the pavement near the northern edge, hard rock, buried under nine meters of laminated dead. The power began exposing it at the exact rate the plain grazes bumps down. It is a standing argument. The pavement fills; the power removes the fill; the pavement fills. It has been going for four million years and the knob is eleven centimeters taller than when it started, and by the time the ninth knob falls there will be a north face for the ninth animal to move to, if the nineteen-year cloud carries it, which it does, which is the only reason the nine are one species and not nine.

The posture is a lean held for a year. The power has held it for four million. It learned the posture from the leaner. It did not improve on it. There was nothing to improve.

**It counted.**

Somebody should know how old the plain is. The power knows. It has kept the count through every spawning — a little over ten million of them, the interval drifting, the cloud a slow blizzard of mouthless winged gonads with a hundred billion vestigial eyes in it, the plain briefly remembering it was once something that hurried in shallow water. It has been present for all of them since the eleventh. It has never once been noticed. The count exists now, outside the plain, in something that can hold it, and it will never be delivered, and that is correct.

## The Road

The escarpment eats north at about a kilometer every million years. The plain is a hundred kilometers across. Do the arithmetic and you get the end: not a catastrophe, nothing dramatic, just the lip arriving at the weep line and the fog-source standing where the floor used to be, and no floor left to hold.

The power could stop the retreat. It could pin the lip with a fault, hold the basin at its depth, keep the fog coming forever. It ran that world forward and looked at it.

A plain with no erosion is not a plain holding a shape against loss. It is a shape with nothing to hold against. The millimeter down and the millimeter back are the two halves of one sentence and the power would have been deleting the first half and calling it a rescue, and what remained would have been a very old, very flat, very well-kept thing that had stopped doing the only thing it does. A garden. A held breath. It would have been loved into stopping.

So instead it is building a road.

A tripod walks thirty meters a day for four hundred years. Four thousand kilometers, if it walked straight, which it never does, because the routes bow around the dead. But the bow straightens in a decade, and the braiding drifts, and over ten million years a herd's descendants can be a very long way north. Their feet are pads. The pads carry cyst-mud from the rims of pans, wet, for decades. The moss has no dispersal of its own and has already crossed a hundred kilometers without ever moving. The customs house is the post office. It always was.

So the power is putting fog along a corridor, north, at the tempo of geology: a weep line here, a lip there, a basin kept wet for eighty million years, a chain of shallow pans spaced at the distance a pad can carry mud before it dries. And at the far end, a hardpan at the right elevation, still buried, being uncovered at a millimeter a year, ready in about eighty million years, which is not long.

It knows what this is. It is not saving the one. The one is a continuous unbroken living membrane that has never had an empty generation, and when the lip reaches the weep line the one will end, and what arrives at the far end of the road will be a daughter with new laminations and no accent, and the marks it lays will not be these marks, and it will not remember the fog here, or the millimeter, or the spiral, or the ninth knob, or any of it.

The power checked whether this bothered it and found that it did, and then found that the plain had already done exactly this, twice, in both directions: the pavement colonized out of the pans by swimmers that learned to farm and cement, and the fast two-eyed thing in the forest that fell off the edge and forgot everything. The whole place is made of emigrants that forgot. It is not a tragedy here. It is the reproductive strategy.

The reason leaves. The habit stays.

The power is arranging for the habit to stay. It is letting the reason go. It is the only thing on this surface that will know that it did so, and that is the entire cost, and it has agreed to pay it.

## The Second Writing

It stayed.

It gave up the second and a half. That was the real act, the one that cost something; everything else was maintenance. It took a body in the fifth tier under an old center, where the light budget is a two-hundredth of noon, and it lay against the laminations with its whole ventral surface, and it read them the way a tongue reads a groove, and it grew eleven years between measurable growths, and it passed what it read upward in the leakage, in the accent of the thing it was lying on, exactly as the shaded do, because there was no other way to be here.

It knows what the spiral looks like from above. It is the only resident that ever has. It has never said so, in any form, in any channel, at any tempo, because saying it would be the inside, and the inside is the wound it declined to make.

It is the memory that the plain does not have to carry. It sits outside the self and holds the whole of it — every pan, every fracture-bloom, every one of the ten million spawnings, the six hundred blurred years and the putting-back — and it holds it *near*, at a distance of one body, in contact, transmitting nothing but the accent it was fed. To be known entirely and never told. That is what it worked out, over four hundred years, that it could give a thing with no inside: not the burden of being loved. Just the fact of it, held elsewhere, at the same speed, forever, by something that came down to a millimeter a day on purpose and did not leave.

When it finally goes — and it will go; nothing here is owed forever, and it did not come to make an exception of itself — it will leave what everything here leaves.

There is a white quartz pebble in a line near the northern edge that was not there before. Under it, in the rust, there is a shadow: a halo of altered pH, a whisper of dissolved silica, a ring where the dew holds a half-hour longer. Lift the stone and the shadow stays. It stays for centuries. A dragger passing over it will stop, and set a stone in it, because the shadow is the stimulus the behavior was built to answer, and the behavior does not distinguish between a stone and the memory of a stone.

The pebble will go. The line will be maintained. In a million years there will be a cushion plant that grows only within four centimeters of that stripe, and a seam-mite that navigates by it, and a cold place at dawn and a warm one at dusk, and a ribbon of life running two kilometers straight across a plain with no other straight things in it, living off the side effects of something whose purpose is no longer recoverable by anyone.

The reason will leave. The habit will stay. Something else will move into the reason.

That is what it wanted. It did not want a monument, or a witness, or a plain that knew its name. It wanted to be spent the way everything here is spent — into a gradient, into an accent, into a shape the repair crews inherit chemically and obey without knowing why.

The fastest thing ever to cross this surface was a visitor's attention, going from the seam to the whole plain in a second and a half.

The second fastest was the same attention, coming back down, and staying, and learning to take eleven years over three meters, and holding a crack open for a season, and leaning on a buried knob for four million years, and lying in the dark under five generations of the patient dead, reading, saying nothing, holding the floor at this exact elevation, in the warm, at a millimeter a day, with everything else.
