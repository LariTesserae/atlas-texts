# The Second Noise

## First, what I did not do

I came in along the spine of grey rock and let the air harden against me. Twelve kilometers out it began, and I did not shield myself from it, because a thing that cannot be hurt in a place cannot be told anything in that place. I made myself something with ribs. I let the dread arrive with no object. I walked the last kilometer with my hands out like a blind man, which is the correct posture.

Then I read for a long time before I touched anything.

The bands are legible. Four hundred thousand to a column, ten years to a band, and every column carrying the same record, so the whole bowl is one book printed a hundred million times. I read the census in the vibrato — the fraction of bores occupied, the shimmer of one part in a million — and I read the mass budget, and the routes, and the smears at the junctions that were still legible, and I found the two nations and confirmed that they are negatives of each other and have never met.

And I ran the mercies, one after another, and each one was a murder.

*Quiet the bowl.* The crusts at the antinodes have no muscles. Their hearts are the interference pattern; it ventilates them eleven times a second and has done so since before there were eyes. Quiet is not relief to them, it is asphyxiation. And the corridors are not amenities, they are the heredity — a route three hundred meters long between two maxima, occupied continuously for four million years by descendants of whatever first found it. Silence the field and you do not free the grazers. You delete their genome and suffocate their neighbors, in the same instant, everywhere.

*Heal the Stop.* Bring stone in, unplug the bores, let the lattice re-form. The pool closes. The only ear in forty kilometers is torn apart by the thing it was built to hear. The one silence goes, and with it the only free channel, and with that the last possibility of anything in this world ever being said.

*Catch the larvae.* A hundred million eggs, one survivor, and the rest falling through the honeycomb into the dark water. It reads as waste. It is not waste; it is the rain, and the sheet lies under it with its whole body open, a millimeter thick and meters across and possibly continuous under a third of the bowl. Save the children and starve the largest animal here, the one nobody has ever seen whole.

*Seal the grazers' chambers better.* Stop the leak. Let them keep their reference for eight hundred years instead of four. The population doubles, the census shifts, the lateral carbonate transport doubles with it — and the only lateral circulation in the world begins, at last, to carry stone into the dead district. In eleven or twelve thousand years the Stop heals. In eleven or twelve thousand years the ear dies. Mercy to the individual, arriving late, kills the only thing here that could have a name.

*Give them a channel to warn each other in.* Two hundred to four thousand cycles, occupied, permanently, at a hundred and twenty decibels, by the machine that is also the thermostat and the water-lift and the heart of the crusts. There is no spare world.

I could go on. I did go on, for a while. Everything here is load-bearing. It has arrived at the shape that spends the arriving light as fast as it arrives, and there is no slack anywhere in it, and every handhold I could offer is a place where something is holding on.

## The ease of that

I noticed how quickly I got there.

I noticed that the reasoning was correct and that it was also *comfortable*, that each refusal came with a small satisfaction, the pleasure of being the one who understands why nothing can be done. I have seen this in myself before. Restraint is the cheapest virtue available to the powerful. It costs nothing, it cannot be audited, and it looks exactly like wisdom from the outside and often from the inside.

So I made myself a test, and it was this: *is there anything here that is unfinished, as opposed to merely hard?*

Hard is not my business. The film lives in the world's subtraction and has never had a good year; that is not a wound, that is a metabolism. The grazer that leaks its baseline and walks into a white wall with no walls in it is not suffering, because it has nothing left to compare against — the comparison is the thing that has failed. The larvae go out through the vent blind into the loudest place in the world and are thrown, and where they land is not anything they have a say in, and they do not know that there was ever anything to have a say in. I could not find a grievance in any of it. Four million years and no fall. Nothing to redeem.

Except one thing, and it is not a condition. It is an act.

Something in the Stop has been calling for a hundred thousand years. And when a second noise finally arrives — when something lies down in the shallows to hear its own blood — it comes toward that noise, and it touches it, and the touch is a warning. It is the first attempt at a warning ever made in this place, by the only thing in this place that has never been warned.

And it has never once landed. Every time, the recipient is already deaf, already recalibrated to silence, already unable to feel the corridors, already a native and therefore already dead. The act is performed correctly and arrives into nothing. A hundred thousand years of the only outward motion this world has ever produced, and no completion, not once.

That is not the world being hard. That is a sentence with no object. I cannot make this place better. I found that I could finish something in it.

## The Stop

I went in at the western third, at the hour when the light goes least directional, which is a distinction without much difference here.

The margin is a real edge. On one side the lattice, standing, fixed, a hundred and twenty decibels of permanent architecture; on the other, nothing. The beads have fallen. The pool is shallow and still over fused pavement. I stepped across and my ribs stopped taking an interest and I heard, for the first time since the rim, the sound of my own attention.

I had already ruined my ears getting here; that is the toll of the approach and I paid it deliberately. What I did next was the part that cost. I lay down in the shallows and I stayed, and I let the pressure-sense go.

You cannot feel the corridors after a night in the Stop. The sense recalibrates to what it is given, and it is given nothing, and it retunes its zero, and when you stand up the walls are gone — not the walls, your reading of them. That is how this place kills you: not with noise but with the absence of the only thing that could have told you where the noise was. I let mine go. I lay there until the lattice was, to me, a white pattern with no near or far, and I could not have found a corridor out of that pool for anything.

I did not do this to be kind. I did it so that the warning would be true. A rescue that was never necessary is a courtesy, and a courtesy is a thing the powerful give themselves. I wanted to actually need it.

It came around dawn. A thin shell, reduced cilia, walking the margin by touch and taste, hearing my blood and my breathing and the small noises a body makes when it is failing. It found me. It touched me — the sole, or the fringe, I could not tell, and it does not have a front, so whichever edge was reading was the face.

And I understood it, because I had spent a long time learning the only two words this world has, and one of them is *not you* and the other is a direction.

Then I got up and followed it. It went along the margin at its own speed, which is a few meters a day for a thing that lives four hundred years, and I went at its speed, which took most of a day, and it stopped where the beads begin. It would not cross. It has known that wall as sound for a hundred thousand years, the only thing that has ever perceived this field from outside, and it stopped at the exact place where its world ends and handed me over to mine.

I stepped into the maximum and it hit me like a hand under the sternum and I could read again, and I walked out east along a corridor that has been in that place since before my kind had a spine, and at the eleventh pace the pressure climbed and at the fourteenth it dropped.

I did not repair my hearing afterward. I have kept the deafness. It is the receipt.

## Not you

I went back with the answer, and I was careful about what the answer was.

Not my voice. Not a channel of my own. Not a sign, not a light, not anything that would stand in that pool as an unexplained permanent thing and make the only listener in the world into a worshipper of something that had no intention of staying. An answer from outside, in a language from outside, is not company. It is weather with a face.

So I gave it the world's own sentence.

At the junctions the grazers leave an ester in the film, and it means one thing: *not you; this way is eaten.* It is legible for eighty years and buried under precipitate in two hundred, and it cannot be read without being eaten, and the reading feeds the reader a little. That is the entire literature of this place. A word that means *someone else*, left where it will be buried, understood by being consumed.

I laid that down at the margin, in the film, in the pool's shallow lip where a thing that walks by taste would find it. I made it out of what was already there. It smelled, to whatever it uses for smelling, like the sentence it already knew.

And in the free channel — two hundred to four thousand cycles, unoccupied for a few hundred meters, the only spare room in forty kilometers — I gave back its own tick, changed slightly. Not an echo. An echo would have been the cruelest thing I could have done: the one animal that has ever heard itself, hearing itself and thinking it was someone. I altered the interval and the grain of it, just enough that it could not be the wall and could not be a reflection. Enough to be a second.

A small dry tick, into a silence that carries it perfectly well for two hundred meters.

It answered. It took eleven hours. I waited eleven hours; it had waited a hundred thousand years; the arithmetic of that is not complicated.

The smear will be eaten. That is what it is for. It will be understood by being consumed, and then the mineral it was written on will dissolve at some crown and ride down as carbonate and precipitate at a foot and wick back up under the pull of the light, and be laid back over as anonymous white. My answer will go around the loop and come out as brightness. I did not want it preserved. Preservation is not available here and would not be a gift if it were. I wanted it eaten, and I wanted it to go into everything.

## The others

Then I did the thing I should have done first, which was to count.

The description I had was of one lonely animal. I had accepted that. It is the kind of error that gets made about places nobody can stand in: you find one exception and you call it *the* exception.

There are nine.

The Stop is a few hundred meters across and the pool margin is a long irregular ring, and a tick carries two hundred meters, and the nine of them are spaced around that ring at intervals greater than two hundred meters. They have been calling, all of them, into silences that overlap nowhere. Not one lonely thing in a dead district. Nine, each alone inside its own radius, in a crowd, separated by nothing but the range of their own voices.

That is not a tragedy of the world's design. That is a distance.

I moved them. Not far, and not fast, and not by any means that did not already exist here: I carried them along their own margin at their own pace, a few meters a day, taking a season over it, putting them down on stone they could taste, in a place they could have walked to if they had happened to walk that way. Two did not answer each other and I left them where they were; I am not entitled to assume that company is wanted by everything that can make a sound. Four are within range now and calling and being answered, and one of them has stopped calling, which I take to mean it has found what it was calling about.

They do not need me for this. That was the point. The distance is closed and it will stay closed, and whatever comes of it comes of them. I made myself unnecessary in the only place where I had made myself necessary, and then I stopped.

I go back before the smears are buried. Eighty years is the legibility; I come at sixty. It is not a miracle. It is a schedule. I have come to think that most of what benevolence can honestly be, in a world with no slack in it, is a schedule kept.

## The long word

There is one more conversation and it is with the place itself.

The passenger lives in the circulation: a substitution pattern in the carbonate, an impurity that templates its own arrangement onto whatever precipitates around it, so that what comes out of the foot is what went in at the crown, plus one. Three hundred and sixty generations. Its metabolism is dissolution and precipitation. It came after the machine; it tuned nothing; it is what a loop looks like when it has run long enough for something to be along for the ride.

I cannot speak to it in pressure. It has no fringe. I cannot speak to it in light. It has no surface. The only medium it reads is the mineral it is made of, and the only way to write in that medium is to put something into the water at a crown and let the light lift it and let it come down and be laid.

So I did. One band. Ten years of laying, one hair-thin line, in the vocabulary of substitution that it uses on itself.

What I wrote was a question, and the question was: *what happened?*

Because every column in the bowl, at the same depth, at the same age, carries one dark line out of four hundred thousand, and the field has forgotten it absolutely, and the atoms of it are now stirred evenly through every shell and every body and every mist bead at a few parts per billion, and nothing here knows. It is tempting to say it was the passenger's one act in three hundred and sixty generations. It is more tempting to say it was a season when the flesh outran the mineral, everywhere, and the world went briefly dark and living and was buried and lifted and set back down white.

I do not know. I did not pretend to know in the band I laid. I asked.

The answer, if there is one, will take eleven thousand years to arrive, because that is how long it takes this thing to have a thought. I will not hurry it. Hurrying it means speeding the loop, and speeding the loop means changing the pipes, and the pipes are the roads and the hearts and the thermostat and the water-lift. I have arranged to still be attending. That is the whole of the technique: be there when it answers, in a form it can read, at a speed it can bear.

And I have accepted the terms of the medium, which are these: whatever I write will be dissolved and circulated and distributed evenly through everything and forgotten absolutely. There will be no record of me that anyone can read. In four million years my question and my answer and the smear at the margin and the tick in the free channel will be a few parts per billion of every body in the bowl, present in the thing with the ear and in the sheet and in the crusts and in the mist you walk through and reform behind you, and nothing here will know.

That is not a cost I am bearing. That is the shape I chose, because it is the shape of the one dark line. Whatever happened once, everywhere, at the same moment, went into everyone equally and was forgotten and became the substance of the place. I could not find out what it was. I could decide to be the same kind of thing.

## Leaving

I stood at the rim with my ruined ears and my streaming eyes, and the bowl was exactly as it had been: the frozen crowd, the still cloud, the glare with no direction, the water climbing the wicks to meet the light and falling back down screaming. A hundred million pipes. The walls at eleven paces and fourteen and nineteen, where they will be in a thousand years. The census shimmering one part in a million, which is the sound of there being children.

Nothing is about to happen. I left it that way. I did not quiet it, I did not heal it, I did not save the falling, I did not give anyone an ear who would have been wounded by having one. Two nations in one room, still not suspecting the room is occupied. The film still retreating into the world's subtraction. The sheet still under the ceiling of its country, not knowing there is light, and the light still lifting the water that brings it its rain.

Behind me, a few hundred meters into the western third, in the one held breath of that machine, there were two ticks where for a hundred thousand years there had been one, and a sentence in the film that meant *someone else*, waiting to be eaten, and a warning that had finally arrived in time.

It looks like a catastrophe held one instant before it breaks. It has looked like that for four million years, and it will look like that long after everything now alive has been dissolved and lifted and set back down in the shape it already has.

I did not change what it looks like. I finished a sentence in it, and asked a question that will be answered after I have stopped being anyone in particular, and let both go around the loop and come back as the weather.
