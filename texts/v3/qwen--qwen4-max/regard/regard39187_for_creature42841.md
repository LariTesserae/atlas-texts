# The Second Rope

What drew its attention was not the eleven birches. It has seen better. It has made better. Beauty is the cheapest thing in the universe and the most lavishly spent, and a green ring forty paces down a hole at the top of the world is, in the general accounting, a nice piece of work.

What drew its attention was a rule kept by nine people toward something that could not punish them for breaking it, in a place where nobody was watching, for longer than anybody had been counting.

It came to look at that.

---

There is no way into the Kettle that is not a fall, a wind, a fur, or a trade.

It thought about the trade. Four days downhill and back, something carried up the mountain in a pack, paid for in guano, arriving with a use-name and a joke attached. But that place is filled. There is one needle, and the needle is the joke, and it is still funny, and you do not take the joke.

So it took the fall. It came up over the snow-line on a thermal off the lowlands with the aphids and the small brown moths and the spiders still trailing their threads, and the plateau's cold dropped it out with the rest of them, and it lay on the snow in the brown speckling for a morning, and at a slow tilt, one at a time, out of the suspension at the height where lift equals the weight of small dead things, it was picked clean by a swift with two primaries missing from the left wing.

It went down the shaft inside a bird. It went out of the bird onto the floor. It went into ten paces of guano in the deep corner where a collapse block keeps the heat in, and by the third night it was warm.

It has made worlds. It had never been compost.

---

It read the niche. Not the scratches — the scratches are the least of what is written there. It read the second column, the one that measures the rope's shortening and therefore, indirectly, the wearing away of the world, and it understood that it had arrived assuming it was looking at a precarious place and was in fact looking at a maintained one. It read the saddle in the lip, two grooves deeper than a hand is wide. It read the lashings renewed each spring. It read the aunt's hand over the child's mouth, once, hard, and never again needed.

Then it sat with the fact that it had come to give something, because it always comes to give something, and that this is not the same as having been asked.

It has spoken first once.

There was a reed country, and the people in it were careful too, and there was a slot, and a rule about the rule, and it broke its own silence out of what it can only describe as kindness. The reed country is still there. It is rich. The people in it are numerous and have a written language and a temple with a hole in the floor of it, and a liturgy for the hole, and a schedule, and four hundred words for what is under the hole, and they are not careful, and they have not been careful for a very long time, and the thing that lived down there — which had also been keeping a rule, which had also never once spoken first — has gone as far down as it is possible to go.

It did not do that here.

---

It made a list. It made the list nowhere: not in the niche, not in the dust of the floor, not in anyone's sleep. Beside each thing it could give, it put the price. The list is the only thing it ever built in the Kettle and it unmade it, and the unmaking took eleven years.

**Two feathers.** The bird would hunt the fast high drift again. It would go up into the noon wedge with the rest and be nine thousand, which is not a number but a weather, and the scratch with the nick in it would become an error in the tallies, and in two generations nobody would be able to say what the mark had been for. The Kettle has ten thousand residents that are a season and one resident that is a person. It left the wing alone.

**Four handfuls of soil**, carried up the north wall to a ledge sixty paces above the floor where a rowan seedling stands three paces tall in four fingers of dirt. To carry it would require a second rope. The second rope is not a rope. It is the decision that there is somewhere in the Kettle worth going that nobody goes, and once that decision exists there is no longer anywhere in the Kettle that nobody goes, and the difference between those two worlds is the whole difference between a home and a site. Also the shrub is doing fine. It will do fine for forty years. At the end of the forty years the whistlers will still be coming down for the berries, taught badly and correctly by an impatient aunt, and that was the thing worth protecting.

**An hour of light.** It did this arithmetic four times, because it wanted the answer to come out differently. The wedge is two hours wide because of the shape of the mouth. To widen the wedge you take down the south lip. Take down the lip and the night's cold pour thins; thin the pour and the turning moves; move the turning and the height where the rising warmth meets the plateau's cold moves with it. Three hours of daylight on the birches would cost the cloud its address. The cloud is the one possession that cannot be lost, mended, sold, or eaten, and is therefore the one they are least grateful for, and it is the one that would have been taken. Not on purpose. That is the entire difficulty with gifts.

**A louder cricket**, so the mat need not wait. Then they would court all day, and the ticking would become a background instead of an appointment, and the people would settle their arguments when they chose to rather than when the hole permitted, and the two things would come apart, and nobody would ever know that they had been together. They have never once mentioned the coincidence. It thought about mentioning it for eleven years. A coincidence you have been told about is a mechanism.

**A word for the sump-fish. A number for them.** The tallies are for what can be counted, and counting is the first clause of a sentence that ends in taking some. It has no improvement to offer on a thing that has been left uncounted for longer than there have been hands to count with.

**A real room behind the east wall**, so the young bats would not come back thin. The older bats let them. It found it could not improve on letting them.

**One degree**, in winter, in the four's hut, applied nowhere in particular, too small to be noticed. The plug is a degree. Six weeks with the breath stopped is the year's other silence, the one nobody enjoys, and it is the only time the instrument is quiet enough to hear a name come up out of the rock. It was cold with them instead. This is not nothing and it is not anything you could put in a column.

**A word for what Salt saw.** There is a word. There are four hundred words. She went south in a bad year and came back with a thing in her head that will not fit into any conversation available to her, and the word would have fit it, and then she would have been the only mouth on the rim that had the word, and she would have become the one who knows. It has seen what is built for the one who knows. In the reed country they built her a house.

---

The last item on the list was the one it had actually come for, and it had not written it down, even nowhere.

It went down to the floor in the Quiet, the way it came in, which is the way the newts came and the crickets and the first birch. Not on the rope. The rope is measured; a finger's width per generation; it was not going to spend a generation's wear on its own weight. It leaned over the north lip of the floor where the households lean, and it listened down the slot at the water still moving somewhere far below, and it faced what it had wanted.

It had wanted to introduce them.

Two households on the rim and a third thing under the floor, all of them inside one throat, all of them polite, none of them ever met. It had thought — it had not thought, it had hoped, which is older — that here was a loneliness it could end. It could be the word between them. It could carry the courtesy across the last inch and let two careful things finally have each other plainly, out loud, in the warmth, with the rowan overhead.

It listened for one Quiet. Three minutes, the crickets going, the hide humming above it, the far wall's answer coming back a half-beat late.

It was not the absence of a conversation. It was the conversation. It had been running for two hundred generations without one word risked, and it had never once been broken, and there is no marriage anywhere in the world that can say that. To answer would have been to translate it, and a translation is a thing you can be wrong about, and the moment it was in words there would be a version of it, and then a better version, and then an argument, and then somebody who won the argument, and then a boy at the north lip with his mouth open and his aunt four paces away.

The silence has two owners. It was not being offered a third of it. It was being offered the courtesy, which is a different thing: you may keep it, you may not spend it.

And it recognised the rule. It keeps the same rule. It has kept it since the reed country, and it had believed it was keeping it as a penance, a wound it held shut with both hands. Here it was a muscle. Here it was the third step, the one that has always been the third one. Here nobody had a theory about it, and nobody was injured by it, and it went in through a child's mouth once, hard, from an aunt's hand, and thereafter it was simply how the floor was walked on. It sat in the guano warmth with the note stopped and understood that its own worst memory could be an ordinary competence, and that it had come to the top of the world to be taught manners by nine people and a mat of moss.

---

In the ninth summer of the mark there was no bird.

It had been trying for three summers to think of something to do about the mark. It had considered a great deal. It was, in the end, watching the tally-keeper's hands when she came to the column and found the chaff-line empty at the tilt where the bird had worked, and she looked at the shaft for a while, and then she spat on the flint and made the mark once more.

That was the thing to do about the mark. It had been thought of, that morning, in three seconds, by a woman with ruined hands who had never once been consulted about the governance of the world.

After that it stopped drafting.

---

It began with Salt in the first summer and it has not stopped.

She does the creel-work. She has said *they go on* and been nodded at. What it gives her is not the word and not the forest and not a companion, because a companion would be a mouth and a mouth would be a house built for her in four generations. What it gives her is that when she says the thing, nothing comes back that closes it.

It does not nod. It attends, with the specific patience of something that has watched limestone dissolve.

So she said the second sentence. *There's no lip.* And then, over four summers, with the creels going up and the windlass creaking in time with nothing, she said the thing she had never said: that she had stood in the obscenity of trees and could not find the place where she was supposed to be careful, and had wept, and did not know whether she wept because it was terrible or because she wanted to stay, and that she has been homesick ever since for a place that is not her home and that she would not choose, and that this is not a thing she can say to anybody who has only ever had one sky.

She thinks she got better at talking. It has let her keep that. She is not talking to something; there is nothing there; she would be frightened, and then she would be certain, and then she would be the mouth. She is talking in a place where the talking does not get closed off, which is all anyone has ever meant by being heard.

It has never been thanked. It did not know until it happened how badly it had wanted to be. It put that down where the children put everything they cannot use.

---

Then it paid.

Everything in the Kettle is either counted or held. Counted: birds, creels, mouths, the rope's shortening, the marks. Held: the cloud, the dead, the names. It cannot be counted. A tenth mouth eats barley that comes up the mountain on somebody's back, and there is no room in the second column for a thing that arrives from nowhere and does not die.

So in the third minute of a Quiet, with the crickets going and the hide beating its slow throb against the fundamental, it said its name down.

The name is not available. It was said once and it belongs to the chord now and the chord does not give things back. What that means is plain and it is the whole price: it cannot be called here. Not by the households, not by their grandchildren, not by anything with a mouth, not ever, not by itself. There will be no asking it for anything in this place. It has been everywhere. It is now from somewhere.

A child on the rim heard a sound in the Quiet she had not heard before, and put it where she puts everything she cannot use, which is down, and did not turn round, because in the Quiet you are listening down anyway and a name going that way is not news.

---

Much later, in a warm month, in the Quiet, it leaned over the north lip of the floor and called down the slot.

Not a word. The households do not use words for it either. The call is a note held, and the note is the hole's own, and what you are doing when you make it is not asking a question. It is checking that the thing you gave away is still being kept, the way you check that the spare rope is in the niche.

The water went on moving far below. The sound that came back had the shape of an answer in it.

It closed its mouth.

It thinks the shape was its own name, coming up the way the warmth comes up, because that is where names go and the slot is where the keeping is done, and it has been down there long enough now to be held. It does not know. It is not going to find out. It has the muscle, and the muscle is worth more to it than anything it has made, and it will not spend it on the one thing it wants.

Nobody says anything has ever answered. There is one more who does not say it.

---

In the six weeks it is cold with the four. It listens to the ice tick and to the hide going slack and dumb, which is the part they had not been warned about, and it does not warm them by a degree, and one of them is always the one who does not call, and now there is one more.

Nine cairns. Three huts, one roofed niche, two drying racks, one windlass, one rope, nine cairns, eleven birches, one rowan, one pool, one slot. One copper needle, eye mended twice with sinew, still sharp, still funny. No third column. The shrub on the ledge is doing fine. The loach hold still through the hollow minutes and feel the world come back. The whistlers come down the nine ledges and the third moss-beard and strip the rowan green, and the rowan goes on making berries, because a tree cannot learn, and neither can kindness, and this is the only thing about itself it has ever been glad of.

It contributed, in the end, about a midge's worth of nitrogen and one name. The nitrogen is in a birch leaf. It knows which leaf.

The cloud comes home at dusk to its forty paces and lies there, three thick, and is read, and is not thanked. The two households sing against the half-beat delay. Salt says the fourth thing, and the fifth, and nothing closes it off. The catkin circles at the height where lift equals the weight of small dead things, and the chaff-line holds, and the eleven birches go on getting up out of their chair, and somewhere in the dark under the floor a name given away sixty years ago is still being kept, and beside it now there is another, and it is not down there because it is dead.

It is down there because it lives here. It is not frightened, and it is not waiting to be called, and it cannot be. It is simply where it is, the way a spare rope lives in the niche.
