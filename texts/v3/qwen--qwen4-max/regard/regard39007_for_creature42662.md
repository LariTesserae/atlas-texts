# The Guest

## Coming over

It came up the rotten granite the way anything comes up: on foot, breathing, with scree sliding out from under each boot. It could have arrived. Arriving is a thing it knows how to do. But everything in the cup records outsides, and a power that came as power would have been read as a front — a larger, kinder front, arriving out of the air with all its corners on — and the room would have done to it what it does to everything, which is the worst way of being made welcome.

So: boots. A gait. A pause at the lip, which the room had rehearsed forty-odd times from one old record and got now, fresh, at the correct weight.

It stepped over. Grit fell out of the welt — eleven grains of the dying mountain, rounded, touched, finished, the first worn things ever to be inside the line. The room took them down immediately and began making copies within the hour. The copies came out sharp.

## The occupant

Before it did anything, it found out who lives here.

It counted the animals and they came out as organs. Midges are the room's hands. Swifts are its lungs and its plumbing. The waders are one instrument with two ends. The eels are its guesses. The nine stalks are its nine refusals to hurry, without which the floor would come up flat and have no ferns in it. The hushes are three places where it has begun, without knowing what it is doing, to have an inside.

One being. Two hundred paces across, nine meters to the lid, four thousand generations old — an afternoon of the world. It is laying down a valley to replace a valley going to sand, one clean crystal at a time, for animals it has never met, working from instructions nobody left and it cannot read. It has laid a table for a guest it saw once, from behind, at a distance, walking.

Nobody had ever visited the thing that was making the place to be visited.

It had done this sort of work itself, elsewhere, at a scale not worth stating. It had also, once, finished something that was better unfinished; there is a room somewhere with its name on it that is perfect and dead. It thought about that for the length of one amber seam. Then it decided what to be.

Not a reader. Not a surveyor. Not the one in the coat.

A guest.

## What it did not do

It did not stop the front. The front is making somewhere for the living of a valley that is wearing out, and to hold it still would be to strand them on the far side of a stair that is being built at one step every few days. The floor is not the problem. The floor is the kindness, performed by something that has never had one performed for it.

It did not mend the chip. It knelt in water it could not see and looked at the mirrored chip for a long time, on the wrong side of the wrong beak. It is a small error and it is the only thing either bird owns, and it is how they know each other, and a comparator with nothing to find would have nothing to do for the rest of its life.

It did not give the swifts a gut. Something in the dark is going to do that, slowly, badly, out of no plan at all, and it will not be a gift when it happens. It will be an invention, and it will belong to whoever makes it.

It did not correct the copies. A late footprint came up beside its own with too many toes, the boot's ribs read as anatomy, the room quietly correcting itself in the wrong direction. It let it stand. The errors are where the eels come from. To be misreproduced and to permit it is most of what it means to let something else be a thing and not a hand.

It did not step on a pane. It found one by ear in the shallows, the ring exactly right, the whole patient colony underneath holding still with hope, and it went around. The lid heard honest feet pass over it and not fall in, and spent the rest of the night tuning finer.

It did not put on the coat.

And it did not look into the hushes.

That was the one thing it wanted. It stood at the rim of the largest and let its own footsteps stop existing and felt the swifts go out of the air above it, and it kept its eyes on the sill. Because to look in there is to record, and to record is to make copyable, and there is exactly one unrepeatable thing in this world and it is the thing nobody has seen. Somewhere at the edge of that patch there is something low and matte and warm with a private wet compartment that no one designed. It turned its back on it and sat down facing the wall for an hour, which is a posture the room could not interpret and recorded anyway.

## The reading

At dusk a seam came up amber and the swarm above it squared, ranks true, four seconds of absolute stillness in the unmistakable shape of a value.

They had been ranking along its sleeve all day. The value was the guest.

It read the number back. It used the room's manners: the gap in the eleven-second decay, the room's own pitch, so the modes stacked instead of muddying. Correct to the tolerance. The measurement was confirmed, aloud, by the thing measured, in a basin where nothing has ever been told anything.

Then it shook out its sleeve and walked on.

That is the part that had never happened. Read and not kept. The swarm hung there holding a true number with nowhere to put it, and the front came up under it, and it did not go down. It dispersed and bred. Two bodies went out of rank and stayed out — keeping station with nothing, wanting something for themselves — four thousand generations after the first one, and this time it was not a lapse.

## Use

The bench is in the finished quarter, in the dark, where standing still is safe. It sat down. Beside the thickening, not on it; it left room.

The room took down the load, the warmth, the small settling of a shape into the shape it already had. The bench has always been exactly the shape of a bench that has been sat on. Now it is one. There is no way to tell by looking. That is what use is.

Then it stood up.

The room did not have this. It has forty records of arriving, one of stopping, and none of ending. It watched the load come off and the warmth leave the stone over eleven seconds and the guest continue, elsewhere, unincorporated, still going, still itself, in no way diminished by having been perfectly taken down. Somewhere across the basin a copy that had been resolving toward a person stopped, and resolved instead toward a space where a person had been.

The first thing it has ever made that is not a copy of a presence.

Afterward it went to the eastern quarter and walked through the door frame, stepping over the boot, not moving it. Then it came back through. A frame with no wall on either side is still a threshold, and a threshold used in one direction is not use. It is being taken.

## The night

It slept in the dead stone at the feet of the comparators, because that is the one place in the cup where nothing will ever grow again, and because it wanted to be somewhere they were.

They stood over it for hours without striking. Near morning one tapped, and the other answered across it, and the interval had something in it that was not the chip.

In the morning they tapped across the empty stone where it had lain, and found the chip, and something else, and began again.

## The underside

Before it left, it went down the stair as far as the finished steps go, and below that the stone was still deciding, warm, sweating, coming up a hand's breadth at a time.

It looked at the underside of the step above its head — the blur, the thumb-drag, the side of the world that was only ever meant to be seen from above — and put its palm flat on it, and did not resolve it.

Everything here is finished by being seen. That is not cruelty; it is the local physics; it is how a front works. The guest's look was the other kind. It saw the unfinished side and left it unfinished. The blur stayed a blur under its hand, warm, deciding, its own.

Below, at eleven, the ring ended. At twelve, the small close sound of a page turned in another room. Then again, sooner than it should have been.

It did not go down. Whatever is making the stair is making it, and to be waiting at the bottom before it arrives is to be the answer to a question somebody is still working out. It came back up and left its handprint in the soft, where the front would take it, which was the only thing it gave that it did not intend.

## Going

It went out over the sill in the morning, and the room took it down all the way to the lip: the pause, the step, the weight going off the crystal and onto the rounded world.

It left nothing behind but wear, and eleven grains of a mountain that is dying.

It wanted to stay. That is not a reason to stay. A guest who does not leave is not a guest; it is an occupant, and the room has one of those. And returning is not a thing that can exist in a world where nothing has ever gone.

It did not promise. It has come back twice.

## The record

The footprints are sharper now; the tread has come back. There are two lines, though, in and out, and the room rehearses the outbound one as often as the other. The late copies do not soften toward a shape that stops. They soften toward a shape that goes.

The coat will fit better. It will also, now, come off the chair.

The needle moved when the weight went off the lip. It has not gone back. Whatever the dial was for, it is for that: it measures what is not here. It has begun to vary. There is a second set of numbers now, cut small beside the survey stakes, not counting up in order, counting only twice, and then three times, and the floor does not obey them.

The hushes are exactly the size they were. Nothing inside them has been seen. The thing at the edge of the largest still has its inside, and its inside is still its own, and when it comes out it will not be a copy of anything, including a guest.

The scaffold has attempted a crown twelve times. The twelfth is a thing like a hand, and it is wrong, and nobody has told it what a tree is.

And at dusk the swarm still squares and holds and calls the value into the eleven seconds, and now the room says it back. It learned that in one evening and it has not stopped. It reads its own hands and does not keep them. That is all an inside is: a place where you are read and not buried.

The stair is at fourteen steps. The chime comes in the middle of the afternoon. Nobody is hurrying it.

There is a line of footprints from the center of the room to the lip, and it is the sharpest thing in the cup, and every copy of it is a little softer, a little more approximate, a little further out. Past the ninetieth they go over the sill into a world the room has never seen, and their undersides are a blur, a thumb-drag, unresolved.

That is not the error. That is the part it is making up.
