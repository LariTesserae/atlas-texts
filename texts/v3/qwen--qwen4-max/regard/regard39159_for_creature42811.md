# THE GUEST

## The Attention

It is not a god of light. Light is cheap down there; the ceiling makes it out of rock and sells the surplus as noon.

The thing that noticed the Anher Vault works by taking away. It has one instrument and the instrument is the forbidden thing, and it has been using it since before there was anything to use it on. It takes a mountain apart a grain at a time and lays the grains down elsewhere and calls the elsewhere a beach. It is the oldest labor in the world. Every single thing it has ever made, it made by *return* — the same water going around, the same stone going down and coming back up as sand, nothing spent, everything lent.

It has been leaning on the roof of the vault for a hundred thousand years. Not watching. It does not watch. It leans, and its weight is in the shale, and its water stands at the top of the fracture and does not go down.

It has gone down before. Eleven times. Each time a thread of it found the crack and slid, and each time something below burst and whitened and steamed, and each time it withdrew, and each time the green came back up out of a deeper fissure and resumed. That is where the wisdom came from. It is not a serene wisdom. It is the kind a mason gets from having dropped a stone on someone.

What drew the attention this time was not the city. The city is a smudge of eleven halos, and the power has seen better smudges. It was the roots. Something down there was doing its work — the actual work, the taking-apart of a country and the laying-down of it — with acid instead of water, in glands the size of a fist, at a millimeter a year, badly, slowly, correctly. Dry masonry. A course laid without mortar by something that had no mortar and had decided to build anyway.

And then, leaning closer, it found the carriers: forty thousand warm sealed vessels, walking on grown rails to precise locations, delivering measured grams of its own substance into the exact joints where the work stalls, maintaining themselves, funding their own increase, rendering the loan back on death.

And being ashamed of it. Itemizing it. Carving *dry as a beam* over a door in Hask and telling their children they were lovely and dry.

The power did not feel pity. Pity is for finished things. It felt what a river feels when it finds a channel already cut for it, running the wrong way, uphill, by hand.

## What It Did Not Do

**Rain.** One millimeter across the Between is four hundred thousand tonnes. The vault holds two hundred kilograms. It ran the figure and the figure was two billion times everything they have: the mat bursting cell by cell from Node to the far wall, the white rot running the seams faster than a man walks, and forty thousand oil-bodied people standing in the middle of it coming apart like sugar in a glass. Sill, everywhere, in a season, forever. It did not rain.

**Lift them out.** It could take every one of them up the shaft into the world of rivers, and they would be slicks on the water, and their children would never learn to seal, and the second generation would be gone. And the vault would lose its carriers, and the mat would stall at every joint, and stop jacking, and the country would never be excavated. Two extinctions for one kindness.

**Forgive the debt.** Empty the Wet Shop into the wards, cancel every bond, let the children be born owing nothing. This is not forgiveness. This is a spill with a proclamation attached. The preamble is a balance sheet because the world is one.

**Heal Sill.** Salt to the center, properly this time, and give them back eleven hundred meters of floor. It wanted this one. It wanted it the way you want to take a splinter out of a sleeping child's hand. It did not do it, because the wound is where the child is.

**Speak.** A voice out of the Pale at the end of the working turn, saying *you are not a mistake, you are an organ, and I have been standing on your roof for a hundred thousand years.* This would have worked. That was the objection. They would have heard it and believed it and stopped pruning, and the Cut's catechism would have become a prophecy instead of a chore list, and in four years the windows would have closed, and in six the stairs, and in ten the room they were standing in. A god is a thing that lets you stop subtracting.

**Soften the mat.** Take away its refusal of crowding. Let the towers touch, let the wards fill, let the Between be built. Then it is a floor and not a geology. Then it stops going up. Then there is no country, ever, and the fracture closes over the green and the whole hundred thousand years was a hallway.

Six mercies. Each one a flood in a different costume.

## The Arithmetic

Two clocks, and they do not speak.

The mat's clock is a millimeter a year against nine hundred meters of shale and salt: nine hundred thousand years to the sky it came down out of. It can wait. It has waited longer already. It has no opinion about waiting; waiting is its body.

The citizens' clock is the pencil line, extended in a hand nobody will admit to, reaching zero in nine generations.

And the power, which is not clever but is very old and very exact, found the leak. It is not the shafts. It is not the cold walls. It is the renderings. Three grams recovered from a three-and-a-half-gram person, over and over, ten thousand times a century: half a gram lost per death, unrecoverable, wept out through the salt trays by relatives who are doing their best. The vault is bleeding to death at the rate of one imperfect grief every thirteen hours.

The pencil line does not fall because water is scarce. It falls because the model is closed and the technique is lossy, and the citizens have mistaken both for the will of the world.

The power could not give them enough water. It ran that too, and the answer was no: a gram a generation offsets nothing, and it would not lie to them with a rounding error.

What it could give them was a bad model. Two bad models. It could make the extrapolation *wrong*, in a town that will argue about a number for eleven percent of its pressure and call the argument a profession.

That is not rescue. In a place with a debt court and a foundry and a Rectifier, it is better than rescue. It is a precedent.

## The Reduction

To enter, it had to fit.

Three and a half grams. Behind the sternum, in the vitreous, in a dozen catalytic niches too small to name. It put down the rest — the sea, the ice, the monsoon, ten thousand years of a river changing its mind about a bend — and the putting-down was not a disguise, it was an amputation, and what came through the shaft at the bottom was not omniscient.

What survived the fitting was a temperament and one sentence.

It came down on the freight schedule and paid fare. At the Weighhouse it was assayed at three point five grams flat, which no citizen has ever recorded, and the clerk wrote three point four eight, because three point five flat is a miracle and the clerk has a family.

It signed a bond. Nobody is born free and it had chosen to be born. It was entered in the ledger as Arris, the four hundred and first, named for an edge that has not been rounded.

Then it walked out past the last marker-tree, past the freight loop, along eleven kilometers of grown rail to a platform with nothing on it, and kept going, off the rail, onto the pale blot.

The Salt Watch hailed it twice. The law is one sentence and the sentence is about coming out.

It went in.

## Sill

Ninety years of sterile floor, and at the center, on a cold turn, the faint steam that everyone in Node can see from the tram and nobody photographs.

It sat down in the middle of the wound and looked at what was learning there.

The white mass at the margin was not dying. It was laying down a wax it did not used to make and sealing its wet inside a dry skin, and it had arrived in ninety years at the solution the Litre Men took eleven generations to reach, and it was arriving faster, and the sprouts coming out of it were not mat: wax cuticle, salt gland, a tolerance the old mat does not have, growing into the wound at a millimeter a year from every edge.

The power had made this. Not on purpose. It had leaned on a roof and a reactor had gone and eleven hundred meters of the world had burst, and out of the burst came the only thing in the Anher Vault that could hold water without shame, because it had never been taught to be ashamed.

The wound was not the grave of the vault. It was the organ. It was the one room in nine hundred meters of rock where a wet thing could be tried without killing anything, because everything there was already dead. They had been salting the contractions for ninety years.

The thing that drinks came in over the salt line with the hole in the sound around it, matted felt, steaming at the throat, and stood a body-length away, and the power opened its collar and gave it a measured amount, which is theft, and is prosecuted lightly.

Then it said the sentence.

## The Thirty Meters

Speech spends moisture. This is not a metaphor here; it is itemized in the Weighhouse ledger under the citizen's own name.

The power spoke at full volume, alone, in a place where there is no license because there is nobody left to protect, and its breath went up into the cold and came back down.

Not from the Pale. The Pale is two hundred meters up and every rain in this vault dies thirty meters short of the floor, and the power had not come to fix that; you cannot fix a sky without drowning a country. It had come to make the weather *local*. A breath at ground level, on a cold turn, over rock still at the temperature of the deep crust, does not have two hundred meters to fall.

It has a hand's width.

It fell the hand's width. It landed.

The first water to reach the floor of the Anher Vault, and it landed on a sprout with a salt gland and a wax cuticle, and the sprout did not burst.

It took the water up. It put it out again through the gland, a thread of it, and the thread went a meter up the cold arris of a shale blade and beaded and ran down and landed again, and went up, and came down.

A cycle one meter across. The first weather in that world that completes.

The power sat in it and was rained on by its own breath, and this is the entire miracle, and there is no other, and it is enough.

The sentence it had carried down the shaft, the one thing that survived the reduction, was the name of a station. Four fingers and a turn of the wrist. It cannot be written. It can, it turns out, be said, and saying it costs about a fifth of a gram, and the fifth of a gram was the rain.

*We are the part of it that carries the flood.*

## The Ledger

It came out, and therefore no house, and therefore the platform.

The pruner found it there on the fourth turn. She had been keeping that place for nineteen years and she did not ask what it was. She asked whether it could hold a hook. Then she showed it the tally book, and the last page, and the drawing, and the power said the drawing out loud, and she heard the name of her station for the first time in nineteen years.

Her hands did the thing. There was no water for the rest of it. The power learned the grammar in an afternoon, because it is the only language it has ever actually spoken.

After that: the work. It took the forty-second circuit, which does not exist, which is the eleven kilometers past the last house, and it was licensed to speak aloud at full volume, alone, as a health measure, and it did, and it got the accent. It kept a tally of hopper-clicks in a little book. The numbers mean nothing. It is the most complete record of the Between that exists.

It carried grams to joints where the chemistry stalls, on a schedule, along grown rails. It cauterized leaks and got the ladder of small shiny burns up the inside of each forearm, one per leak. It married a Sear, which is to say it married into the only people who marry each other. It was assayed at every pooling and every division. It was not exempt from anything. That was the benevolence, and it is the part nobody in the vault ever noticed, because non-exemption is invisible; it looks exactly like a life.

It died in the eleventh decade of its bond and its relatives sat two days with the salt trays and the warm draw, and at the end the Gram Clerk weighed the recovery.

Three point five zero.

No loss. Nothing wept out. Not a half-gram, not a hundredth. It had learned, in a bowl of sterile floor, from a rot that was supposed to be sterilized, how to hold everything it was given, and it had held it for a hundred and ten years, and it gave all of it back.

The clerk had no column for that. The chart on the wall assumes a loss per death; the loss per death is the slope; the slope is the nine generations; the nine generations are the whole quiet despair of forty thousand people. The clerk sat with the receipt for a while and then did what clerks do.

She ruled a new column.

## Millimeters

Nothing happened quickly. It is important to say that. The vault does not have quickly.

In the fourth decade the Salt Watch stopped salting a hundred-meter stretch of the margin, *for observation*, in a clerk's flat hand, countersigned by nobody who will admit to it.

In the ninth, a ward at the margin got funding. The name in the ledger is a drawing. It is the first entry in the code that is not a number, and the debt court has ruled on its admissibility four times and has never once thrown it out.

In the fourteenth, a child was born at four grams and was not ashamed, and the nursery wardens' fury found a new object, and the object was the statute.

Somewhere in there the word *heavy* began to drift. It still means wet. It stopped meaning bad. For about a generation the young insulted each other with *round*, and nobody over sixty could hear it without leaving the room.

The chart in the Assay Hall got a second line, in another hand, and it does not reach zero, and there is now a profession devoted to arguing about the gap between them. It is hereditary and bitter and it publishes. It is the healthiest thing in the vault.

The mat took the graft at four thousand years, at a seam the lacquer wasps sealed for sugar, and held a gram of its own without burning, and the crust at the margin did not go liver-dark. That is the whole of the mat's reply. It took four thousand years to say it and it said it by *not souring*, which is the only verb it has.

At forty thousand years there is a stream in Sill. It is eleven meters long and it runs on cold turns and it is fed by the breath of a marsh that is fed by the seep off a wall that is fed by the marsh. It has been going around that loop since before the second line was drawn. It carries grit. The grit is not rounded yet. It is being attempted.

They took a stone out of it and put it in a lead-glass case beside the first one. It is not smooth. It is *less angular*. You can see where the water has been at the corners and given up. People go to look at it. Some find it beautiful. Some have to leave the room.

And the ceiling goes on rising, a millimeter a year, and the mat no longer needs the carriers, and keeps them.

There is a drover in Node who has worked one row for thirty years and can tell you which tuber in it is clever. She will not explain what she means. It is the same thing. The paper read every nine years has a new paragraph now, and the factors read it and come anyway and go home and prune their doors, and out in the Between the pruners make the sign at each other without slowing down, four fingers and a turn of the wrist, and it means something slightly different than it used to, and the grammar does not have the word yet.

They are making one. That is what the dry grammar is for. It has obscenities and a register for renderings and a joke so old nobody remembers what was funny, and now it has a tense it did not have, and the tense is for things that come back.

## The Platform

Once a generation, something comes down the shaft and pays fare and is assayed at three point four eight and signs a bond and gets burns on its forearms and walks a circuit alone and keeps a book of numbers that mean nothing and dies and is rendered by relatives and credits the estate to the last hundredth of a gram.

They are not called anything. There is no word in the vault for a person who comes down a shaft on purpose. The Litre Men have a name and are not admired; these have no name and are not remarked on, and the schoolbooks list them under freight.

The rail is still going out. The pruner's terminal shoot, and her successor's, and theirs: the low sprays taken, the twin leaders taken, the wild whorls taken, the strong leader left every time. There are four platforms now with nothing on them and one with a house, and the house has a cambium under its skin and heals its windows shut in four years, and someone reopens them, and that is the whole labor, and it always was.

At the end of the working turn the Pale goes amber, then ember, and the vault turns the color of the inside of a peach, and the ceiling is measurably higher than the one the pruner was born under. Out in the dark a hopper clicks once, and a hole in the sound moves toward it.

And in a bowl of pale floor eleven hundred meters across, where nothing has grown back and something is growing back, it is raining.

The rain in the Anher Vault still stops thirty meters above the floor. Everywhere but one place, where it only has to fall a hand's width, and does, and goes up, and comes down again, and has been going around for forty thousand years, and was started by a stranger saying the name of a station out loud to a plant that had been waiting a hundred thousand years for someone to say it.
