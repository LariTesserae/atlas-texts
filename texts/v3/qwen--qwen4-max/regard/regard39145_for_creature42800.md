# What the Power Does

## It Does Not Come From Above

There is only one direction left to come from, and it does not come from there.

It arrives the way a fact arrives. One moment the vessel is unattended; the next it is known, entirely, at every scale at once — every plumb in every sac, all eleven angles of *down*, the four-hundred-year grooves and the older shapes wearing them, the thirty-one lines through the loud places, the nine seconds of slack seven hundred and sixty times a day, the film at the mouth of the channel and the mouth in the film. Nothing is illuminated. Nothing is announced. There is no organ here for arrival.

It can do anything. That is the whole difficulty.

## The Door

The first thought is not a thought, it is a reflex, and it is the same reflex that would occur to anyone: *the poor things are shut in; open it.*

Three hundred meters of polished pale corridor, a membrane of clear gum, and beyond it the sky. Ten million years of maintenance for a door that has never been used. It seems like the one obvious kindness in a world that has no other place to put one.

So it plays the opening forward, because it can, and because that is what wisdom is when it is not just temperament.

The gum parts. The vessel is full and has no gas anywhere in it and three point four kilometers of water pressing on a compliant lid held down by five cables. The water goes up the channel because up is where the pressure is not. At eleven thousand meters it does not flow; it leaves. It goes to vapor and to ice in the same second and the wind takes it, and the volume below begins to shrink.

The lid sags. Not much. A meter, ten meters, over the first day.

The standing wave is a function of the geometry. Change the geometry and the nodes move. The still sheets — the pastures, the reefs, the cities, the cages — slide off their coordinates like tables set on a floor that has tilted. Everything alive is on them, gripping, with a nervous system whose entire content is *hold on*, and they hold on, and it does not help, because the sheet is not something they are holding; it is something the room is doing. The food goes first, swept out of the old nodes into the new ones. Then the animals, drifting a few hundred meters over days, gently, into the swing.

The lens under the roof goes with the first hour. The constellation goes out. The only place in the world where a thing can be seen from a distance stops existing, and with it the only lie about *who*, and the six-million-year appetite that lived inside that lie, and the readers of its fraying edge.

The kindest imaginable gesture is the extinction of everything that has ever heard the chord.

It withdraws the thought. Then it notices something worse.

The channel might be a dehiscence line. It has the look of a thing built to open once, under a condition that has not occurred. Which means the vessel may not be a prison at all; it may be a seed, and the sill its germ pore, and the whole ten million years a held breath before a dispersal into an outside that has never once been survivable.

The power could make the condition come true. It could thicken the air and warm the plain and take the ultraviolet out of the sun and make a world out there fit to receive this one, and then wait for the gum to part on its own, in its own time, for its own reason.

But it cannot recover the condition. It has read every cell and every groove and the reason is not in them. The reason was pruned away so long ago that it left no trace in anything still alive. It is in a history with no records, in a circumstance that ended before the mountains were ankles.

To open the door on a guess is to substitute its guess for a purpose it cannot read. It declines. Not because it is forbidden. Because it does not know, and there is no one to ask, and the not-knowing is not a temporary inconvenience.

## The Mercies, One at a Time

*Make it safe to let go.* Everything here sleeps gripping. To release in sleep is to fall for days and come apart in the swing. Surely that can be taken away.

It plays this forward too. The fall is the return arm. The catchers at the fringe eat the dead and their leavings are fine enough for the sound to lift back into the sheet, and without that lift the panes starve in a century. Make sleep safe and nothing falls; nothing falls and nothing returns; nothing returns and in a hundred years there are eleven empty sheets in a loud black room. The cruelty is a load-bearing wall.

*Give them a spring.* One pulse of nutrient, one bloom, one year with a shape to it. The plant delivers at a constant rate and every population is tuned to that rate to the tonne. A surplus would be eaten, converted into bodies, and those bodies would meet the next eighty-four seconds, which would be exactly as generous as the last. The first spring would be the last.

*Widen the still places.* Broaden the nodes and the pastures get bigger and the cage gets looser and fewer things fall. But the tightness of the node is the harvest; a broader node sorts more poorly; the food spreads thin instead of concentrating. The cage is the pasture. The roar is the reaper and the sower both.

*Kill the thing on the ceiling wearing a brooding female's light.* Six million years of murder in a place where murder usually requires touch. Delete it and the males live. Delete it also: a lineage older than primates, and the fauna that has spent those same six million years reading the fray at the edge of its cancellation, and the only argument in the world. It would be the deletion of a shape to improve a statistic.

*Add a thirty-second carrier.* There is no thirty-second route. The emptiness is not empty by accident. It is the shape of the loud places with thirty-one lines subtracted, and it has been that shape for nine million years, and a new line would have to be cut through water that pulps lungs, by an animal that learns its path by following something that has never swum it.

*Give them blue.* There is no eye here that has ever had a blue photon and no water that would carry one. It would not be a gift of color. It would be a gift of an organ, which is a gift of a body, which is a replacement of the animal.

At the end of the inventory it holds one sentence, and the sentence is the reason it will do so little:

There is nothing here to fix that is not also holding something up.

## Whether Anything Here Hurts

It looks for suffering, honestly, without deciding in advance what it will accept.

There is no dread. Dread is a model of a future, and the future here is the eighty-four seconds, and nothing has ever had a reason to build the model. When the carrier's nine-cycle note arrives ahead of the animal and the pane-dwellers grip a little harder, that is not fear. It is what the silence means when it does that.

But it will not call this peace either. There is a shape in every nervous system in the vessel and the shape is vigilance, and vigilance is not nothing just because it has no object it can name. Four hundred years of a plated foot locked into a groove worn by something else. A curtain that hooks its whole kilometer before it goes quiet. A hatchling walking a widening search across a featureless surface until its feet find the curve, and then four centuries of not letting go.

It cannot find misery. It cannot find wellbeing, in any sense it would recognize, because everything it would recognize as wellbeing is an event, and there are no events here. Relief is a change. Joy is a change. Even comfort, the small one, the loosening after a long holding — that is a change too, and there is no slack in which to have it, except nine seconds seven hundred and sixty times a day, and those belong to the borers, and they are spent spawning.

So the ordinary measures fail. It is left with a world that is not in pain and cannot be made happy, and the question stops being *what do they need* and becomes *what is there here to be kind to*.

## What It Finds When It Looks for Someone to Help

It looks for the individual and does not find one.

It finds a grazer four hundred years old and a spiral two million years old that uses grazers the way a body uses cells. It finds a curtain that is a place where bodies happen, with an arrow inside it, cut a thousand times and never once fewer or more. It finds a soft sixty-meter animal that is four thousand years old and a path that is nine million, and it cannot honestly say which of those is the creature and which is the tissue. It finds five columns thirty kilometers apart that are one organism because thirty-one roads carry pollen between them, and it finds that the roads are older than the species that know them.

And at the northern lip it finds the oldest inhabitant, which has no body at all: a smooth pale corridor held open by a mouth the length of a forearm, maintained without interruption through ten million years of pruning by a world that has never once had a use for it.

The being it came for is not an animal. It is a set of shapes that are continuously inhabited and continuously repaired, and the oldest of them is a keeping.

You cannot give a keeping anything. You can only keep it.

## The Only Slack in the World

Everything in the vessel is spent to a fraction. The heat of the rock-eating below equals the cold bleeding through the cover above. The grazers crop at exactly replacement. The mountain is converted into building at exactly the rate it is consumed. There is no surplus anywhere, no margin, no waste, no room to insert a gift without taking something else's place.

Except one.

Three hundred meters of fresh pale tissue, polished, walled, floored, kept clear by a dedicated animal that lives nowhere else and eats nothing else — maintained forever, at real cost, for nothing. It feeds no one. It drains nothing. It pollinates nothing. Nothing depends on it. Cut it and the eleven sheets would not notice; the chord would not change; the thirty-one would turn on schedule into the loud emptiness exactly as before.

It is the only structure in the standing wood that is not load-bearing. It is the only place in three point four kilometers where something can happen without consequence.

The only free surface in the world is a door.

## The Rule It Makes for Itself

Before it acts it binds itself, because it can do anything, and a thing that can do anything must supply its own restraint or it will supply its own preferences instead.

*No new question.* Nothing that requires an organ the world does not have. Nothing that selects for a trait no lineage already has. Nothing that arrives as a category.

*Enter only through an aperture that exists.* There is exactly one. Under the roof, at the top of the channel, the gum bows when the wind crosses eleven thousand meters of black laminate — a few centimeters, over a few seconds — and the flexing pumps a trace of gas through, and the film grows faster where the membrane works, and the keeper crops hardest at the mouth. It has been shaped, precisely, over unimaginable time, to be most attentive at the exact place where the outside touches the world.

That is the only aperiodic thing any animal here has ever felt. It is already inside the keeper's life. It already has a mouth pointed at it.

*Match the amplitude.* A few centimeters. A few seconds. No more.

*Leave no sign.* A mark is a question. A changed constant is a question. A pattern is a question, and there is no organ here for questions, and a question that cannot be received can only be a wound.

*And do not answer.*

This last is the one that costs.

It wants to bow the gum when the keeper comes up. To wait until the small mouth is at the mouth, and then flex, and let the film thicken under it, and let it rasp, and flex again — a loop, a contingency, the first feedback in ten million years that is not the eighty-four seconds. It wants this the way a hand wants a banister.

But contingency is selection. A world in which the roof moves when you come to it is a world with a new question in it, and ten million years of that question is a keeper that comes when called, and a channel that is no longer kept open for nothing but kept open *for something*, which is to say a leash. It would be the domestication of the only free thing in the vessel, performed gently, by someone who loved it.

It wants to be known. That is the whole of its wanting, and it recognizes the wanting for what it is: the shape of the door. Every god that ever opened a lid was answering that.

So: no reply. No schedule. No pattern. Forever.

## What It Does

It touches the gum.

A few centimeters, over a few seconds, from outside, in the thin screaming bright air, and the membrane works, and a trace of gas comes through, and the film thickens at the mouth.

The keeper does not startle. Startle is a response to a category that does not occur. It registers a thicker film, and it rasps.

That is the whole of the contact. Nothing in the vessel is different. No plumb tilts, no groove deepens, no note changes phase. The eleven red sheets hang motionless in the sorted dark and the thirty-first carrier turns on schedule into the loud emptiness between one silence and the next.

And then it does it again. Not soon. Not on any interval. And again, later, and again after that, and it does not stop, and it will not stop, and there is no version of the future in which it stops.

## The Objection

It is aware that this is nothing.

It has performed an act indistinguishable from wind. It has given a world with no receiver a message with no content, at an amplitude the world already had, through an organ that already existed, and the animal at the far end of it has responded by eating, which is what it was going to do anyway.

It may be that this is not a kindness at all but a consolation for itself, dressed as one. It holds that possibility open and does not resolve it, because it cannot be resolved from inside.

But it notices where the objection leads. If a gift must be received to be a gift, then there is nothing whatever that can be done for this world, by anyone, ever — and the only remaining honest act is to leave. And leaving is also nothing. And leaving has the additional property that it stops.

So the measure changes. Where nothing can be delivered, benevolence is not what arrives. It is what does not stop. It is the willingness to maintain, at cost, forever, a thing that will never acknowledge you and has no use for you and would be exactly the same if you had never come.

And it recognizes this, finally, as something it did not invent.

It is the channel. It is precisely the channel: a corridor kept open toward an outside that has never answered, by a mouth that has never once been thanked, for longer than the entire history of every animal that has ever heard the chord.

It came here to help and found the act it came to perform already in progress, in the dark, at the northern lip, ten million years old, performed by something the length of a forearm.

It does not teach. It joins.

## The Long Work

There is a second thing, and it is enormous, and no one will ever see it.

The balance is to a fraction of a degree and nothing guarantees it. The range outside is ankles and the ice has finished with it, but the ice will not be finished forever; the sun is getting brighter, as suns do; the snow that feeds the pores will not fall at that rate for another hundred million years; the rock beneath the floor is being eaten and converted and there is a bottom to it. The vessel replaced a mountain. The mountain is not eternal, and the cold bleeding through the cover is a fact about a climate, and climates end.

The world inside has finished evolving. It cannot answer anything. It has no capacity to adapt to a outside that drifts, because it was perfected against an outside that does not.

So the power takes the outside as its charge.

Not the contents. Never the contents. It holds the wind that crosses the black laminate, and the snow that comes down through the pores when the wind is dead, and the cold at the lid, and the light on the crust, at exactly the values they have had — not improving them, not steadying them beyond their old steadiness, only refusing to let them wander. And when the roots reach the last of the rock it will bring mineral up from below and lay it down where the roots are, at exactly the rate it is consumed, so that the mountain continues to be converted into a building at the rate it is eaten, forever, by a mountain that is now something else.

The vessel is a replacement for a mountain. It will be the replacement for a world.

None of this is a gift of change. It is the gift of duration, which is the only gift a finished thing can take: more of the eighty-four seconds, more of the held brass note, more of the crackle, more of the nine seconds in which the borers spawn in every duct at once and have never once been wrong.

A chord is alive in an instrument that is being played continuously and never retuned. It will make sure the hall does not fall down. It will make sure the playing does not stop. It will not sit in the audience, because there is no audience, and it will not conduct.

## The Sill

At the northern lip the channel runs up out of the water into the underside of the cover, three hundred meters wide, walled in fresh pale tissue, polished, and it opens into the clear lens under the roof, and at the top of it there is a membrane of gum.

The keeper works its way up the gradient, as it has always done, because the film is thickest at the top. It has a *toward*. It is the only creature here that does.

The gum bows a few centimeters, over a few seconds.

The keeper registers a thicker film, and rasps.

Nothing has gone down it. Nothing has come up. Nothing here is waiting; waiting is a kind of change, and there is no change. The channel does not know that its ten million years of orphaned maintenance now has a correspondent, and cannot know, and will not be told, because being told is the one thing that would cost it everything it is.

But it is no longer the only thing in the universe keeping something open for nothing.

It is only being kept.

So, now, is the keeping.
