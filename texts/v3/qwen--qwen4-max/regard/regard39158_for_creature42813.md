# The Visitor

## What it does not do

It comes over the pavement in the heat-shimmer and stops at the lip, and the stopping is the first act, and it is the largest one.

It wants to go down. That should be said plainly, because everything afterward is a consequence of the wanting and not of some serene absence of it. It wants to go down the thirty-one steps and stand in the third basin and put its palm flat on the rust and feel the film give slightly under the hand, the film on a tooth. It has crossed a great deal of world to do that. It lies down on the hot slab at the edge with its face over the drop and it does not go.

Here is the arithmetic that keeps it there.

A body at rest breathes out about half a cubic metre of air an hour, warm and wet and four percent carbon dioxide. In a room where the air already holds every grain it can hold, that water has nowhere to go but onto the stone. It beads. It runs. And condensate is distilled — it is the one kind of water this country has not seen since the draft sealed, water with nothing in it, water that is still hungry. The carbon dioxide goes into the film on the rock and makes it very mildly acid, which is to say it makes it capable of taking. A visitor does not add to the Cup. A visitor reintroduces the older water, the water that finished its work, in the quantity of a tablespoon an hour.

And the first things that water would find are the sharpest things present. The chisel strokes. The straight true trenches. The half-freed corner of the block that is still waiting to be taken out next. It would take a long time — a hundred years to lose a hair's breadth, perhaps, and nobody would ever see it happen — but nothing in that room repairs stone, and that is exactly what has made the room safe, and it is exactly what makes any harm final. The footprints would outlast the tool marks. Clay is the residue; clay has already lost everything it can lose.

So it lies at the lip with its mouth shut, and it works out the rule that governs everything it will do afterward, which is this: *in a saturated room, the only gift you can give is one that can be eaten.* Anything that lasts is a wound.

## What it learns instead

The Cup has one utterance. Water goes in at the unseen throat and out at the unseen fissure and the rates are the same, and what comes out is everything the room could not use.

So it goes downstream — a long way downstream, in the dark, following the fissure into the country of rock where the water goes on being water for another few thousand years — and it sits in the outflow and reads.

This is slower than looking and it is worse than looking and it is the correct method. Crumbs of mat. Cast isopod cuticle in two halves, both of them partly eaten. Fine lime dust that was a shell and is going to be a shell again. Once, a midge wing, and once, the folded scrap of a web, and from the isotopes of the wing it learns the coastal scrub and the season and the wind, and from the web it learns that the spider rebuilt, and from the ratio of the crumbs it learns the width of the seams and therefore, by arithmetic, the number forty-one, without ever having seen a snail.

It does this for a very long time. It has the time. Patience is not the virtue here; patience is only the entry qualification, the thing you must have before you are allowed to have any other opinion about the place.

But there is a limit to what a room will say about itself in what it throws away, and the limit is precisely this: the outflow carries *rates*. It does not carry *events*. Nothing in that effluent will ever tell you that one animal, on the second seam, low down where the weep runs into a joint, broke its shell in the barren crossing and survived and made itself a clumsy pale patch. The calcium of that patch went round and round like all the rest. It is not distinguishable downstream. The one thing in fourteen metres of rock that means *something happened* is the one thing the room cannot say.

## The temptations, and what is wrong with each of them

It considers opening the dogleg. Not much — a metre of collapse at the second bend, a draft, a steal of molecules, and the chemistry would wake up. The water would begin to give again. Sinter would grow. There would be seasons of a kind, and growth lines on the shells, and variation, and death that meant something, and a future. It could make the Cup *alive* in the ordinary sense, green at the lip, various, evolving, a place with a next thing.

It sits with this for a while, because it is the strongest temptation and because the argument for it is not stupid. Then: the beneficiaries would not be these. Every blind thing in that room is a body plan for a crack in a place with no hours, and the draft would kill the lot of them in a season, and the hungry water would take the last morning of the quarry first, because it is the sharpest. What it would be making is not a better Cup. It is a different room, on the same site, at the cost of everything currently in it. You cannot give a thing a future by destroying what it is.

It considers feeding them. A little carbon, a little phosphate, something to widen the middle band where life is possible. And the number would go up — sixty, eighty — and then the seam would be cropped past its regrowth and the mat would thin and there would be a famine, and the famine would be a new thing in a place that has never had one, and after it the number would come back to forty-one because forty-one was never a population, it was a measurement of how much oxygen can come around a bend in a rock. Also, and this is the part that decides it: a gift that has to be repeated forever is not a gift, it is an adoption, and it would be making dependents out of animals that have never depended on anything arriving.

It considers shortening the barren crossing between the second seam and the third. This one it wants very much. It is the only ordeal in the room. It is where the shell got broken.

And if the crossing were short, no shell would break, and there would be no patch, and there would be no scar, and there would be nothing in the Cup that was not a rate. Forty-one identical places. One animal, distributed, and no individual anywhere inside the count. The break is not an unfortunate accident that happened to the only individual in the room. The break is what *made* it an individual. In a lineage with no sex and no variation and no recombination, injury is the sole source of difference; abolish injury and you have not made forty-one safe animals, you have made forty-one instances of nobody.

It cannot give them variation instead. Variation is selection, and selection is death with a meaning, and the end of the measurement, and again — different animals, not these.

So it lets the crossing stay long. This is the hardest thing it does, and it is a *not-doing*, and there will be more breaks, and it will know about each of them and be unable to prevent any of them without abolishing the only kind of someone the room can produce.

It considers, last, making the place permanent. It cannot. The Cup is a standing wave, and a standing wave lasts exactly as long as the river. The mountains will stop raining; the plain will come down; the seal is a collapse and collapses happen. It can postpone. It cannot exempt. And it comes to understand, sitting in the outflow with a midge wing, that the room is not the thing worth saving anyway. The room is a container. What is worth saving is the thin unfinished rind inside it, and the rind is not a substance, it is a *habit* — take the stone out, put it back, keep the face true — and a habit is the one thing in that country that can be copied for free.

## The correction

It went down once.

Everything above is true and it went down once, and it is important that the two facts be held in the right order, because the refusal came first and was real and was then broken, and the breaking was not necessary.

It went at what would have been noon if the room had had one. It went down the thirty-one steps holding its breath, which it could do, being what it is, for longer than the descent takes. It went sideways among the jammed blocks with its hands on the slick bosses. It crossed the barren stretch to the second seam and got down close to the water and looked at the thickened place on the outer lip of one translucent shell, the clumsy pale repair, the only object in the room that means something happened, and the animal did not notice it, because the animal has eyes at the bases of its tentacles and there has never been anything for them to do.

High in the bend, the spider went still.

That was the whole visit. It did not touch the rust. It did not take anything, because there is nothing in that room that can be taken: forty-one thumbnails of lime is the entire calcium budget of the world down there, and to remove one shell as a relic would be to steal a forty-first of everything, and the vacancy would fill, and the theft would be paid for by animals.

Then it climbed out, and on the twelfth step, where the block is, the one that was carried that far and set down and never picked up, it stopped, and it sighed.

One breath. It fell across the cut face of the block and lay there and beaded and ran, and it was distilled, and it was hungry, and it took perhaps a few thousand molecules of ivory sinter off the crisp arris of a wedge mark struck by somebody whose language has not existed for a hundred thousand years.

That is the mark it left. It is not visible. It will not be visible for a very long time. It is irreversible, and it is on the block that almost got out, and it is the only signature the power will ever put on anything in that country, and it is a small dulling of somebody else's last morning.

It knows this. It would do it again. It is not perfectly wise; it is wise enough to know exactly what its wanting cost, and to have paid, and to have not pretended otherwise.

## What it did

**It went upstream.** A hundred miles inland, to the mountains where the rain gets in, and it has been working there ever since, and the work is unglamorous beyond description.

A survey line drawn a valley over. A bore that hit nothing and was capped. A fault that spent itself under a hillside with no cups in it. A pump installed in a different field for reasons the farmer could have explained and would have been right about. A road that goes around, and no one now remembers it going anywhere else. It does not stop the world; it tilts the world, at the place where two nearly-equal possibilities meet, which is the only place anything can be tilted. Every one of these looks like ordinary luck. None of them will ever be counted, because a near-miss that nobody knew was near is not an event, it is just Tuesday.

And it protects the Cup by making it boring. This is deliberate and it is the cleverest thing it does. The survey report is dull. The sinter is commercially uninteresting; there is better stone closer. The access is awkward, the descent is unpleasant, the paperwork is tedious, the nine notches beside the rope-groove remain unexplained and will go on remaining unexplained, because a mystery is a hundred visitors a year and a hundred visitors a year is a tablespoon of hungry water an hour, and the tool marks cannot survive being interesting. It knows what the dry-stacked wall at the head of the stair was for. It is not going to say.

Its guardianship has exactly the shape of the snails': keep the face true, take out and put back, at the rate the threat arrives, forever, unfinished. It has joined the rind. It is one more thing in the world that is still being made and will not be completed.

**It arranged the export,** and found it was late.

The reasoning was good: matter is conserved in that room but pattern is not. The count is a measurement. A daughter carried down the fissure opens a vacancy and the vacancy fills, and the seam does not notice, and the forty-one are not diminished by one. So the lineage can leave for free, and the only thing it needs is a somewhere. So it tilted the water at the outflow, very slightly, in favour of the odd thread and the odd hatchling going somewhere it could live.

And then, going downstream to see, it found the seams already occupied. Under a great many plains, in a great many saturated rooms with their own drips and their own counts, the same animal, wearing shells made of the same borrowed lime, grazing at one deep. It has been happening since before the seal. It is not a miracle; it is what the fissure has always done with what it could carry. The power's one original contribution to the survival of the lineage was to invent the lineage's ordinary habit.

It was pleased. It is not too proud to be pleased at being unnecessary.

But it drew the right conclusion from it, which is the important one: *what is general can be saved by copying, and what is particular can only be saved by being remembered.* The animal is safe. The animal is everywhere. The patch on the outer lip of one shell on the second seam is not everywhere. It is in one place, it is mortal, and when that snail dies the others will rasp it down for the lime, and the calcium will go into another whorl, and the only history in the Cup will be gone as thoroughly as if it had never occurred.

**It sent one midge, out of season.**

In a slack period, in a month when the wind was wrong, one midge came around the two bends and into the web, and the spider went still and then came down and took it, and later ate the web, and what it dropped fell into the third basin and went out through the fissure and was gone.

It was only a midge. It did not put itself in the midge; that would have been for its own sake and the room would have had to keep it. This was the one import the Cup already knows how to swallow, the one sentence in the only grammar the place has, which is a change in the weather at the border. It cost nothing. It left nothing. It was received by the only resident with eight eyes and a calendar, and it was entirely destroyed, and that is what made it a gift instead of a mark.

It cannot say *I am here* in any way that would last. It can say *something came* in a way that will be eaten by evening. It chose the second, and it did not repine about the first, much.

## The keeping

There is one more thing and it is the whole of it.

It cannot take the shell. It cannot mark the shell. It cannot make the room remember; the room has no mechanism for memory except the clay, and the clay is a desert, and the desert is full — five footprints, no room, and in any case clay remembers only what is pressed into it and nothing that is merely true.

So it did the only remaining thing, which was to make a desert of its own.

It went into itself and it found a place and it took the iron out of it, and the sulphur, and everything worth grazing, and it made that place poor. Nothing lives there. Nothing goes there, because there is nothing there to eat. It is the one kind of surface in any world that can hold a shape: the kind with no appetite pointed at it. Poverty is an excellent preservative. It built itself a pocket of insoluble clay.

And into it it put the five bare footprints, small, held perfectly. The nine notches, and the not-knowing, which is part of the object and must not be resolved. The order that was given on the last morning, in the words it was given in, which no longer exist anywhere else and which it caught the way you catch a thing dropped down a stairwell — imperfectly, and too late to ask what it meant. The bird, and the pole, and the boy on the rope, and the boy's name. The exact place on the outer lip where the shell was broken, and the shape of the clumsy pale repair, and the fact that the crossing was survived.

When that animal dies its shell will be rasped and the lime will go round, as it has gone round a hundred times, and there will be forty-one shells and no spares, and nothing in fourteen metres of rock will mean *something happened*.

And it will still have happened. It will be held outside, in the one medium where storage costs nothing and takes nothing from the thing stored, which is a someone who was paying attention.

That is what a benevolent power can add to a saturated system. Not matter; matter is closed. Not form; form is already being made at exactly the rate it is taken. Only this: an outside that receives without subtracting. Nothing was taken. Something was kept.

## Coda

Out on the plain the hole goes on lying on the stone like something spilled. The drip falls into the third basin every nine seconds and nothing hears it. Forty-one animals graze three seams at one grazing deep, taking the stone out and putting it back. One of them is carrying an old break that will be eaten when it dies. The spider is waiting on a wind that belongs to another country. The stone that was going to be taken out next is still waiting to be taken out next.

From the lip there is no difference at all. That is not a failure of the visit; it is the specification. The proof of having been loved, in a place like that, is that the place looks exactly as it did.

The difference is elsewhere: that somewhere now there is a patch of ground with no iron in it and no sulphur, where nothing lives and nothing grazes and nothing has ever had a reason to go across, and in it five small bare feet have been standing for a hundred thousand years without once shifting their weight.
