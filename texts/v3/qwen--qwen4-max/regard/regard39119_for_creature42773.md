# One Cake in Twenty

It comes over the rim at midmorning and lets the light hit it like a hand pushed against the chest.

It could have come at dusk. It could have come as weather, or as a change in the water table, or not come at all and simply attended from wherever such things attend. It comes on foot, in the glare, at the hour when the snowfield is throwing the sun back up under everything, because it has decided that whatever it is going to do here it will do as a body, at the height of a body, at the speed of a body. This is the first decision and most of the others follow from it.

It makes goggles on the way up: bone, scraped, a slit in each. It makes the slits too wide. It knows better and it does it anyway, out of the same failure the parents have, which is that it cannot bear to make the world that small on the first day. On the second day it goes snowblind. A girl of nine who has done this twice leads it by the hand for three days and is extremely bored and talks the entire time about the herd standing on the rim, their names, which one is stupid, how the shadow comes off the headwall in the morning like a lid being lifted.

On the second of those days, walking, she mentions that there are ten.

The visitor says nothing. It does not go and look. It does not find the counter and tell her, or the listener, both of whom already know in their own mediums and have not said so to each other. It has come a very long way and the first thing it does with its knowledge is nothing, and this turns out to be the shape of the whole visit.

---

Before that, in the dark behind its ruined eyes, it does the audit. It has come intending to help. It is not stupid about help. It goes through the bowl feature by feature looking for the missing part.

Water: not missing. The grandfather found it in a February afternoon with his face against a wall — full, everywhere, under everything, running whether or not it comes out. The gardens are not the water supply. They are nine places where the water supply is briefly visible.

Life: not missing, and not even scarce. More animals in one cushion than in a mile of the forest below, plus a dry bank of eggs that have been minerals for forty years and will not be minerals forever, plus larvae in the black mud under the tarn ice, plus a population in the fractures a molecule thick and acres wide that divides once a decade and cannot be killed by anything that happens in the light.

Succession: the hare's belly, the fox's circuit, the finch's passes, the spiders working the windrow of leaked midges along the drift edge, and a woman with dirty boots who has never said a word about it.

Law: complete, and not theoretical. It was bought. A six-year-old carried a vole in a mitten because he wanted the gray ones to have a red friend, and a garden went brown and hollow and blew off the mountain as dust, and out of that came *a garden keeps its own*, which is taught before the stepping stones. There is no ethics anywhere better than an ethics with a real dead garden behind it.

Communication: better than air. A cobble struck three times on the black band arrives in every cheek in the bowl. A ring of people on their bellies humming into stone, each hearing all the others dead center inside the skull, louder than any voice this thin air will carry.

Memory: hearths under hearths, rope under rope, tallies on cairns, one cairn with no tally at all, and a cake at the bottom of the pit that nobody eats.

Foresight: a woman with a mallet and a river-cobble who can hear three years into the rock.

Grief, meaning, jokes: present. The oldest joke in the Cup is that the mountain looks like it is cooking and it is only just alive, and the children are always disappointed, and the adults always laugh, and this is a load-bearing beam.

There is nothing missing. The place is thin, which is not the same as poor. Everything here is concentrated. The emptiness is the space between the notes.

---

So it considers the gifts it could give, and it puts them down one at a time.

*Stop the headwall.* A hand's breadth a winter; it could hold the rim still, and the tarn would not drain, and the nine would not die in a week some few thousand years from now. But the frost that pries the grains apart is also what snaps the feldspar bright, and the bright unweathered fracture face is what the films in the dark are eating. The rock is young because it is being broken. Stop the breaking and you have not saved the animal, you have stopped its food arriving. And the Cup is a wound with first tissue in it. Hold the wound open forever and it is a wound; close it and it is something else, a paved thing, a bowl of very old stone with nine antiques in it that must now be maintained. It would be the boy with the mitten again, only enormous.

*Re-route the water back to the two silent gardens.* This is the one it wants most, because it has met the listener. But the water has not left. It has gone deeper. The animal has nine mouths and it is not obliged to keep breathing through all of them; two have gone quiet because the breath is going somewhere else, and the somewhere else may be the beginning of the long descent that will outlast everything above it. To plumb those two back to the surface would be to call the animal up into its mouths at the exact moment it is learning to live without them, and it would do this in order to make a number on a cairn correct.

*Thicken the air.* Give them shouting. Give the children a song they can sing standing up, give the ravens their noise back, give the dog a world it can understand. It sits with this one a long time. Then: the frost ticking would be continuous small-arms fire in the walls, the meltwater would be a river running through the house all night, the silent white bloom of a door-sized slab would arrive as a roar, and no one would sleep, ever, and the listener's cheek would be useless, and the counter's palms would be a quaintness, and the bone choir would stop within a generation because you do not lie on your belly to hear something you could hear standing up. Every skill in the Cup is a skill of attenuation. The gift would be loved and it would take everything.

*Ninety days to two hundred.* The compression is the productivity. Freeze at dawn, riot at noon, collapse at dusk, vole generations turning over in weeks. Stretch it and you get a slower place with fewer lives in it, which is to say a worse one, which is to say the forest, which is already down there and does not need help being itself.

*Make the water boil hot.* No. The disappointment is the joke and the joke is four centuries old and it is not the visitor's to improve.

Every gift it can think of is a living thing carried across the stone.

---

What it does instead:

It pays the toll. One cake in twenty to the family that owns the right to set the rope in the groove. There is an argument about the rate that ended in a marriage within living memory; the visitor does not adjudicate it, does not point out that the groove is a hand deep and the rope is renewed annually and the ownership is a fiction. It is not a fiction. It is the arrangement. It pays.

It learns the stepping stones. All of them, in order, like the song they are, and it is slow at it, and it does not cheat by looking down from above and memorizing the pattern; it learns it with its feet, one slab and then the next, because the road is invisible and the road is the covenant and to be exempt from it would be to stand outside the place while being in it.

It enters the low house on its belly.

It sweeps once, badly, standing on the concave slab at the edge of the Kettle, taking the lower shaft and leaving the crown for the finch, and its face swells for three days and it goes down to the valley in the autumn with cake-face and is proud of it in a way it does not entirely understand.

It does not knock. Not once, not ever. This is the hardest of the disciplines and the reason is technical: any strike on the black band is a public statement about water, and there is a spaced triple tap that nobody uses because it sounds like the Bath saying *tak*, and children who use it as a joke are slapped, because for one second every adult in the bowl believes the water has changed. A god knocking is that tap. There is no way to say *hello* in this language. There is only *come*, *the rope is set*, *get off the wall*, and *the water is not what you thought*. It has nothing true to say in any of those registers. So it does not use the channel. It puts its cheek against the stone, which is the one posture that transmits nothing, and it listens, and it goes on listening long after the eleven days are over and the windbreaks stand at nine sleeping places where nobody sleeps.

It learns the voices. The Bath's irregular fingernail-on-a-comb. The Kettle swallowing. The Long Wood's actual hum. The thickness that is the Little One and the newer thickness under the elbow. It learns them the way the listener learned them, by cheek, over years, and it is not faster at it than she is, because it has decided not to be.

---

It sits with the dog.

This is the one thing in the Cup that is here against its nature. Everything else is small or flat or warm-centered or has been selected for eight hundred generations into exactly the shape it needs. The dog was brought up by people for people and it is miserable: the glare, the altitude, the silence it cannot parse, tied at the throat with its nose in a cushion full of voles, quivering, forbidden. It has learned to watch hands because listening for its name is useless here.

So the visitor has hands. It lies down on the stone at the throat with its belly on the cold and its arm over the dog and stays there through the worst of the noon, and the dog, which cannot hear it, can read it, and after a while stops quivering. Nobody notices. No rule is broken. Nothing is ferried. It is not a solution to the dog, which will be miserable again next summer and every summer; it is four hours in one summer in which the dog is not alone in a silence it did not choose. This is most of what the visitor came for, though it did not know that until it was doing it.

It goes to the cairn on the scree above the throat, the only one with no tally, and sits there, forty feet from the house, in the position the grandfather lay in for a winter.

It learns, eventually, which garden the boy killed. It takes a long time to find out and it is careful how it asks. Then it does not say. The not-saying is a mercy the people built and it is not the visitor's to dissolve; but somebody should know, and the visitor can bear to, and it does, and it keeps it, and this is the second thing it came for.

---

At the singing it lies down in the ring with its head against the black band and ten people hum into its skull, dead center, louder than any voice, and it wants to knock. It has a river-cobble in its hand. It knows about the seven. It knows the listener has not told her mother and carries the difference around like a stone in her boot, and it could end that in three strikes, and it would be true, and it would be a lie about water, and it would be the end of the only thing in the Cup that cannot lie.

It puts the cobble down on the hide.

And it hears, in the ring, the listener's note go very slightly flat at the same place in every verse, and no one else in the room notices, and it does not tell her that anyone noticed.

That is the gift. It is not information and it is not rescue; there is nothing here to rescue, the place is full, the place is running, the place has a tenth garden starting under the elbow and three people who independently know it. It is the other thing, the thing left over when there is nothing to fix, which is company. She never finds out. It works anyway.

Afterward, in every generation, there is one more person in the bowl than the families can quite account for, and nobody finds this remarkable, the way nobody finds it remarkable that when one raven dies another appears with the same mannerisms.

---

Once, and only once, it does what the careless woman does.

It does not plant. Planting is forbidden and worse than forbidden, it is silly; a grandmother transplanted cushions into a wet crack and they died in one winter and everyone was embarrassed. So it does not plant. It walks the wet places with dirty boots. It goes a little out of its way to stand in a new seep, a nameless one on the lower wall where there is nothing but wet black rock and a fringe of hare-dung, and it kneels, and it gets up, and it moves on, and what is stuck to its boot goes into the water.

It does not protect that spot afterward. It does not check on it often. If it fails it fails, and no one will ever know it was tried, and it will not be counted, and the visitor will not count it either.

Because this is the only conversation available. The being here is not the willow and not the ravens and not the nine; it is the fracture system, ten thousand cubic yards of cracked warm rock full of moving water and films that divide once a decade, and it has no ear and no mouth and it cannot be knocked at. Its only utterance is a green place and a column of midges. If you want to say something to it you say it with your boot, in a wet crack, and then you wait a hundred years, and if it answers it will answer as a thickness in the sound, and then a wetness, and then a voice, and then a column the height of a forearm, and then a garden, and then a fox altering its circuit by forty feet, and then a woman pecking a cairn.

The visitor is willing to wait for that. It is possibly the only thing in the universe that is unambiguously willing to wait for that. It has, after all, come on foot.

---

It goes down, too, eventually, into the fractures, to check the one thing that could actually be lost — not the nine, which are temporary by construction and backed by a bank of eggs, but the continuity of the dark population, which might be stranded above a falling water table and dried out in fractures that go dry when the tarn finally goes.

The way is already open. It has been open for centuries. The two gardens that have gone quiet in the stone are not the beginning of the death; they are the leading edge of the descent, the animal already going down toward the valley four thousand feet below at the speed of water through stone, which is to say slowly, which is to say it will take longer than everything above it has taken so far. The listener is hearing three years, five at the outside, and she is right about the gardens and wrong about what it means, and she will work that out herself, or her niece will, because her grandfather already gave her the sentence she needs: *the Cup is one animal with nine mouths.*

The visitor comes back up and tells no one. It finds it has been carrying a grief on their behalf that they were not carrying, and it puts that down too.

---

When the headwall breaches it does not hold it.

It is there in the afternoon the tarn goes out through the gap. It watches the columns come apart west to east, ninety seconds each, nine of them, the last time. It does not peck the tally. It leaves the windbreaks standing.

At the bottom of the cache pit there is a cake older than the roof, and every year the newest go on top and the oldest come off the top of the bottom layer, and the bottom one stays, and if you ask why you are told: *so that there is always one.*

It does not eat it. It takes it, wrapped in its gut, and goes down through the gap and into the fractures after the water, and it is still going, and it has the cake, and there is always one.

And in a wet crack on the lower wall, where it knelt once with dirty boots a very long time ago, there is a thickness in the sound. Not a voice yet.

It has not counted it.

It is waiting for the winter.
