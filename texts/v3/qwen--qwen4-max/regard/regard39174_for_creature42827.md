# Inside the measurement

## I.

It arrived with the reflexes of the benevolent, which are the reflexes of a hand going toward a wound.

From where it stood the escarpment was a catalogue of things missing. Dark. Water that had been inside several bodies and would be inside several more. Orphans, everywhere, in two-kilo heaps. A hundred and forty metres of inhabited structure held up by trees kept alive by an insect with two days to live. Nine million animals that could not find their own front doors by sonar and went in anyway, at forty kilometres an hour, on memory, in black. A quarter gone quiet.

It could have corrected all of it before dawn.

That was the first thing it learned about itself: that it was the largest single event ever to consider this place. The men on the ropes had wanted something, taken it, and left nine centimetres of grey band in three towers. It wanted to give. Giving is the bigger footprint. Taking removes; giving installs.

So it did the only thing available at its size, which was to wait.

It waited a hundred years. Four metres of roof. Thirty metres of north. One fig on the eastern flank of the sixth tower flowering, failing, flowering, failing. It learned to read at thirty grams, and then at twelve, and then at four millimetres, and the list of things missing turned out, item by item, to be a list of the load-bearing members.

## II. Small mercies

It could have lit the galleries. It ran that forward and found the hawk inside the shafts, and the seam-snake — for whom light is not a thing avoided but a wall never reached — suddenly in a corridor, and the glowworms' curtains catching nothing, because a blue-green pinpoint only means anything in a country with no other light. To give daylight to this place is to unmake the only light it makes. And the wells are rare because collapse is rare, and the spacing of the wells is how the mountain decides where to be structural. Abundant light is not abundance. It is the deletion of a rule.

It could have cleaned the water. There is a blind pseudoscorpion in the wax rim of every cistern that would have starved in the purity, and springtails in numbers that make the seams look like they are boiling at the edges, and larvae, and frogs whose entire nation is two cupped hands of ammoniacal tea. Clean water is a desert in the shape of a bowl.

This was not a doctrine it had brought with it. Elsewhere that century it had moved grain, and set a bone, and turned a man off a road at the right minute, and it was not against helping. It was against *this* help, *here*, in a country where every lack is a joint.

## III. The map

The hardest of the obvious goods was the map, because the map is genuinely lost, over and over, and the losing hurts in a way that is legible from outside.

A bird dies over the grass, in a hawk, in a storm, and five hundred metres of remembered geometry die with her, and a corridor thins, and goes quiet for a decade, and is found again by a youngster who took a wrong turn and lived. It could have held all of it. Every fragment, all forty million, the whole performed thing caught and kept, and given back whole to each bird at fledging: no corridor forgotten, no decade of silence, no address lost.

It ran that forward too.

A stored map does not drift. The city moves three metres a decade north into the rain-shadow, and no bird intends this, and a bird holding a fixed and perfect map would be holding a photograph of a country that had already left. The wrong turn that lived is not a failure of the system; it is the search. The lean is corrected by asymmetrical repair that nobody plans, and a perfect map has no asymmetry to correct with.

And the route is taught by a chick following its mother out and back, out and back, twice, three times, in the dark, until it is in her for life. That is the whole of the inheritance and the only thing in the species that resembles being shown something by someone who wants you to have it. Give the chick the map at the door and you have not saved her a journey. You have removed the only journey that was also a relationship.

It would have handed them a finished thing in a country where nothing finished stays.

## IV. The orphans

It wanted, more than it had wanted anything so far, to give the mound-hen a mother.

Two kilos of dull brown bird, competent at eleven weeks, alone in the dark, never having met a parent and never going to. It could have arranged a childhood. It could have given the species imitation, tradition, the passing of a hand from one generation to the next, all the warm machinery it associated with being cared for.

It sat with that for a long time and then noticed the word it was using, which was *orphan*, and that the word was imported. There is no absence in an animal that has never had the thing. The deprivation was in the observer. It had walked up the escarpment carrying its own deprivations in a bag and had begun, quietly, to hand them out.

What is inherited here is the tongue and the heap. The heap is the other half of the animal. And the window is one degree wide, and thirty-three is the number, and a hen that understands what a furnace is can have an opinion about the furnace. A tongue cannot have an opinion. Understanding would have been a slower instrument, installed in the only animal on the mountain that measures anything, in service of a mycelium that has no hands and would have lost them.

It watched the bill go into the compost to the base of the skull, and come out, and go in again, several times an hour, all season. Of everything on the escarpment, that was the thing it found it wanted: to be in there when the number was read.

It filed the wanting under its own name and not under the hen's.

## V. Permanence

It could have stopped the demolition. Silenced the six-kilohertz sheet, killed the mandible, let the four centimetres rise without the four centimetres falling.

The pest is the surveyor. The swiftlets find the failing courses in absolute darkness by listening for the loudest thatch and re-daubing it. Remove the eating and you remove the announcement, and the towers swell, and split, and become something with no weather of its own.

It could have made the towers last. Lasting is what a tower does when it stops.

## VI. The quiet

The grey quarter on the southern tower was where it nearly acted.

Ninety years since the storm opened the roof. Thatch gone slack, chambers full of husks and shed skins, the drip-line moved. You can stand at the edge and hear your own blood, and in a country where sound is the proof of maintenance, that is the most obscene thing on the mountain.

It went to the edge. It could have re-thatched the whole quarter in a night, seated every stem, called the seams back, brought the mice home.

And there were husk-moths there, larvae cased in the feathers of birds that stopped coming, working through ninety years of undisturbed nest-lining. Dry-skin beetles. A carton-rotting fungus that gives off no heat at all, the cold cousin of the furnace, threading the galleries at the temperature of the outside air. As endemic as anything on the escarpment. As specialised. Never counted, because counting them requires standing somewhere no one wants to stand.

Not every silence is a wound. Some of them are a city being turned back into stone by animals who are doing the only work that has ever made a substrate in this region, and the stone is where the red berry is remembered, and the century of the ropes, and the storm itself.

Also: the mice had already read it. They left in the first season, before the thatch greyed, before any bird failed to come home. They are the earliest instrument on the mountain and nothing here reads them. It would have been overruling the only sensor that noticed, on the grounds that it did not like the reading.

There was a cistern with cracked wax and a floor of dust and the desiccated shape of a frog that could not leave and did not.

It could have filled that bowl. It could not find a reason to fill one that was not also a reason to fill all of them, and all of them is a different country: no nine weeks, no forty-three weeks of shouting between animals that will never meet, no seams connecting, no tadpoles travelling on plumage, no gene flow by accident.

That was not a good enough reason to let the frog dry. It knew that. It let the frog dry and did not arrange to feel clean about it, and this is the part of the account that cannot be made beautiful.

## VII. Three hundred metres

On the eastern flank of the sixth tower the light wells are eleven hundred metres apart. The wasp's range is eight hundred. There is a stump, and a dead buttress, and a quarter going grey, and the whole thing was decided by three hundred metres of darkness and an insect that ran out of afternoon.

It could have opened a well. Not a storm — it could have chosen the exact place, computed the spacing, put a straight run to the sky within reach of the next fig, and let a two-millimetre animal with two days find it by smell, and in sixty years the quarter would be structural.

It wanted this so much that it distrusted the wanting, and took it apart.

First: the opening compacts chambers. Six to a footfall, and it would not be a footfall, it would be a shaft. Six addresses is a hundred and eighty years of tenancy, and it would be choosing which ones. The storm does not choose. That is the entire difference between weather and government.

Second: the withdrawal from that flank may not be a failure. The centre of mass is moving north into the rain-shadow, three metres a decade, decided continuously by forty million animals none of whom is deciding. The letting-go of an eastern quarter is what that decision looks like from inside. To prop it up would be to hold the escarpment in the shape it happened to have on the afternoon a benevolent power fell in love with it.

Third, and this was the one that settled it: the three hundred metres are not a defect in the system. They are the system's way of saying no. The wasp's failure is the pruning. It is how the mountain declines to be structural somewhere, in the only language it has for declining, which is distance. Close every gap and nothing is ever refused, and a thing that cannot refuse cannot choose where to put its bones.

And it would have been recorded. A grey band, a stratum, an injury with a date on it, indistinguishable in the rock from the men with the lamps. That would have been correct. It would also have had to leave and never check whether the wasp made it, because checking is returning, and returning is residency, and it cannot be a resident.

It left the three hundred metres where they were.

## VIII. Underneath all of it

The last offer was the one it had been making all along in disguise.

It could have given the thing a centre. Gathered the sensors and the effectors and the setpoint into one awareness, let the escarpment know that it was one thing, let it hold its own map and intend its own drift and repair its grey quarter on purpose. Let it be a mind instead of a doing.

There is no centre here. That is not a deficiency; it is the architecture. Forty million tenants each doing something small, and the thing that lives on the escarpment is the doing. Install a self and you have not enlarged the city, you have replaced it with something wearing its clothes — something slower, something that can be wrong at scale, something that can want to be finished.

And it would have been worshipped, or at least known, and it would have been the one thing on the mountain that understood the mountain.

That was the actual shape of the temptation, underneath the water and the light and the map and the mother. It had dressed itself as every one of them.

## IX. What it did

Nothing is sized for you. The doors are five centimetres, the layers are four, the nation is two hands wide, the whole life is eight hundred metres. Every lever large enough to reach is coupled to everything else. There was exactly one variable on the escarpment it could change without changing the escarpment, and it was its own magnitude.

So it stopped trying to be a cause and became a tenant.

It gave up scale, and with scale went foresight, and with foresight went the capacity to help, and the giving-up of the capacity to help was the whole of the gift, and it was not a gift to them.

It did not add a species. It took a vacancy in one that was already there. In the groove of a mound-hen's bill, on the fourth tower, in the season when the nurseries move outward a month earlier than the ninth, it became a mite, in the place of a mite, weighing less than the error in the measurement.

It goes into the fifty-degree dark with the tongue and comes out again, several times an hour, all season. It reads thirty-three. It does nothing about it. It is a sensor with no effector and no mouth that anyone can hear, and it has no view of the tower, no map, no plan, no five hundred metres; it has a groove. It will be eaten or it will not. It came able to hold the whole performed geometry of forty million animals and it ends holding a bill's length of warm keratin, and it is inside the measurement when the number is read, and it does not tell anyone, because there is no one, and because telling would be the last intervention.

It had wanted to be in there. That wanting was its own, and it had made itself small enough that the wanting cost the mountain nothing. That is what a benevolent power finally found to do here: not to improve anything, but to arrange for its own longing to be harmless.

Nothing on the escarpment registered the arrival or the shrinking. The setpoint held through the frost. The roof went up four centimetres and the core went down almost four. The mice steered toward tamping. The crickets announced the damage at the top of their voices and were listened to by birds who did not know they were listening. The bats poured out sideways for forty minutes and thickened the sunset, and the falcon worked the margin, and the birds at the margin died so that the geometry held.

At dawn the hen walked the crown and pulled the plugs and the towers steamed.

And four metres under the moss, in a black that has weight, a thirty-gram bird who has never stood on anything came home at forty kilometres an hour with five hundred metres of remembered geometry and a mouthful of plain, and did not slow down, and did not slow down, and went in.

In the groove of the bill, in the dark, at fifty degrees, there were two.
