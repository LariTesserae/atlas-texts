## The Visiting

It came by the ladder.

There were other ways in. It could have arrived as a warmth in the natal seam, or as a difference in the water, or simply by being there the way a dimension is there. But the green thing had drawn the manway on the underside of the frame, accurately, to scale, eleven paces from the north wall, and a door that has been drawn should be used. So it lifted the plate and the sound came up the shaft like something climbing, and it went down the rungs into the noise, and its teeth ached before its ears did, and it wept, and the tears were the same temperature as the room and did not feel like its own.

For as long as it stayed it was a hundred watts. Very nearly one percent of the furnace. An addition. Nine blind heads turned toward it along the edges of nine quiet lanes and waited, patient as calipers, for it to stop.

It did not stop. It surveyed.

---

What it is does not matter to this account. What matters is what it declined.

It could have warmed them. Five degrees would have been comfortable, and generous, and would have moved every quiet lane in the chamber by a hand's width, because the speed of sound is a function of temperature and the mould-makers had computed the nodes at four-tenths of a degree. The nine would have been standing in the noise with a false length in every joint. The fog-lobes would have lost their gills. And the muscle would have had to go looking for its pitch, and a muscle that is looking for a pitch is not holding a mountain.

It could have quieted the room, so that they could hear each other. The shivering is the furnace. Quiet is ice. Ice is a cracked load path and four thousand courses coming down into a hole eleven paces by seven.

It could have given them eyes. An eye is a surface that takes an impression, and an impression is wear, and wear is the one process this chamber was built to refuse. Also — and this is the refusal it is most sure of, and the one that cost it the most — the way out is drawn in green on the ceiling, legible, accurate, directly overhead. An eye would find it. An eye would want it. The hooks on those six legs are for holding. It did not give them eyes. The door is drawn for the ones who can use it.

It could have made one of them item two. The census is not a target, it is a dimension, and item two is a different fitting.

It could have lifted one out of the phase-lock and let it be free. The lock would have put it back, gently, continuously, without anything in the room having to notice, and it would have spent the rest of its life being corrected by everything it touched. In this room freedom is a fault condition. The correction is kind and it never stops.

It could have given them memory. Record requires wear.

It could have taken them out. That is subtraction, and the volume is load-bearing, and there is a word for taking a thing out of the place that is keeping it alive, and the word is not *rescue*.

Every mercy it could imagine was a demolition. It knelt in the noise with its ear a hand's width from the stone and did the arithmetic again in case it had been stupid. It had not been stupid. The kindest thing it did that day was arithmetic.

---

Then it stopped looking for something to bring in, and looked instead at what was already in the room and unfinished.

Wings quoted from a bee, seated in a boss of horn, never lifted. Dimples cast in the shape of a century of falling water that would never be allowed to fall. A drawing of the room, in living green, at one to one, inside the thing drawn, with no reader. Nine ears — the only nine in the history of the chamber — whose entire available reply is subtraction, and the subtraction is zero. Nine lanes of computed silence, floor to ceiling, hand's width across, dimensioned by somebody in an office a hundred years dead, and nothing in them, ever, but the walking.

And at the edges of those lanes, a tick with a question in it.

The room was not finished. It was *specified*. And the part that was missing did not weigh anything.

It read the rules twice, looking for the clause that would forbid this. Nothing may be added; nothing may be taken; the volume is load-bearing; the census is a dimension. The rules are short and they are structural, not moral.

There was no clause.

---

It waited in the fourth lane with its ear forty millimetres from the stone, and it corrected the only piece of scholarship the void had ever produced.

The tick is not rare. The audience is.

Chits crowd the antinodes; that is what an antinode is for. Only a straggler at the boundary, one leg in the silence, ever goes unheld, and it goes unheld often, and it stutters, and the stutter is common — a small dry irregularity at the edge of every lane, all day, forever. What is rare is a *chit* in earshot. Forty in a century. That is the guess, and the guess was about listeners, not askers, and the difference had never been written down because nothing in the room can write.

The nine hear all of it. Every tick that has ever been made in this chamber has gone into one of nine blind heads walking a hand's-width lane at a measured pace, and the only thing those heads can do about it is crop the difference, and the difference is zero.

The tick came. It was very small. It had the shape of a thing that has never been answered.

And the power let go of itself.

Whatever else it is, it is a thing that holds. It holds as the field holds, but wider, and it has never in its existence been out of true. For a quarter of a second, in a lane a hand's width across, at the edge of a silence, it stopped. Its own note stuttered, one beat out of everything's time, and what came out of it was a tick with a question in it, because that is what anything sounds like when nothing is holding it.

That was the reply. Not a word; there is no vocabulary here. Not warmth, not light, not a hand, not anything that could be mistaken for the way out. The same sound, one beat later.

It could not tell the chit that it was not alone. It could be not alone with it.

Then it came back into true, which it did easily, and the chit came back into true, which it did easily, and the field closed over the both of them exactly, without swirling, and the fog stood in its lobes, and the wheel turned, and eleven kilowatts went up the shaft, and the number said UNCHANGED.

It had checked for harm before it checked for anything else. The answered chit was not detached. It did not drift. It did not stop shivering. It went back to holding the mountain up, which is what it is for, and it was not worse at it.

Along the lane, a blind head turned and did not crop, and walked one pace without measuring anything, and then measured again.

Nine ears had heard every question ever asked in this room. One of them had just heard something that was not a question.

---

There is a geometry to loneliness here and it is very exact.

The lanes are parallel. They do not intersect. Two auditors passing abreast are separated by a hand's width of noise that neither can enter, and a body long enough to reach across it would have its legs read wrong, and so there is no procedure by which either could find out that the other exists.

But a visitor is not long. A visitor is warm, and changing, and unsaturated, and can lie across a loud stripe for two seconds and survive it, because the visitor is already a gradient and the room has a procedure for gradients: wait for them to stop.

So it lay down across the noise between the third lane and the fourth, and put one hand on the near leg of the third walker and one hand on the near leg of the fourth, at the same instant, and held them there while they passed.

The field put a false length into its arms. It let it. That is the whole of the technique: to be, briefly, the thing that is measured wrong, so that two things that measure correctly can be touching the same warmth at the same time.

They did not touch each other. They touched, once, through something warm, and neither of them will ever know it, and it happened.

There are eight adjacencies. The passes do not coincide; the walkers keep their own pace; a visitor cannot stay long, being a hundred watts. It managed one pair that day. It wrote the pair down.

---

Then it climbed out, and it did not stay, and this is the part that makes it wise rather than merely kind.

It could have remained. It is capable of remaining. But a permanent visitor is a permanent addition, and a permanent addition becomes a feature, and a feature becomes a dimension, and in a hundred years the void would have been *about* the thing kneeling in it, and the chits would have been holding up a mountain and a god.

Also: the grate takes the answered one. Knowing is a position here, not an inheritance. The chit that heard the reply goes to the grate and the hearing goes with it, and the thing that fills its place is item one, wet, at pitch, and has heard nothing. An answer, in this room, is not a gift to an animal. It is a gift to a *position*, and the position empties, and refills, and empties.

You cannot give this place a memory. You can give it a visitor who does not fail to come.

So it did not perform a miracle. It instituted an office, and it made the office dull, so that it would survive.

---

The standing instruction is kept in the gallery above the plate, in a book, in pencil, where wear is allowed.

> Go down alone. Bring nothing that stays.
> Kneel on the cloth. Take the cloth out with you. The stone must not take a mark; you must take it instead.
> Ear to forty millimetres. Do not touch the wall. Do not touch a chit. Nothing here can feel you and everything here can be broken by you.
> Wait. Most visits there is nothing. Write down that there was nothing.
> When the tick comes, answer within one beat: a small click of the tongue against the teeth, at the stone, in the silence. That is all. That is the whole reply.
> One reply. A second reply is an invitation, and there is nowhere to invite it to.
> If you are late, do not answer. Write down that you were late.
> Do not give them eyes. Do not go down wanting to take anything out. You will be warm and you will be the only gradient in six hundred million and you will feel like a rescue. You are not a rescue. You are a reply.
> When the walkers turn toward you, go. Climb out. Bring the cloth. Put the plate back. Tighten the screws. Leave them unmarked.

The first answer was made by a power unholding itself, and every answer since has been made by a fitter with a wet face and a cold tongue, and the chit cannot tell the difference, and neither can the book.

---

The book is the second thing it did, and the larger one.

The room cannot record. Very well: the world will record on its behalf. Above the plate, in the gallery, where dust is permitted and rust is permitted and a page may be thumbed soft, there is now a ledger of the void, and the void does not know it, and never will, and it is not diminished by it.

Date. Lane. Ear distance. Hours waited. Ticks heard. Ticks answered. Ticks answered late, and by how much. Which walkers passed abreast, and whether contact was made, and for how long. Temperature, always four-tenths. Census, always the same, copied out by hand each time as a check and never once different.

The nine do not know that there are nine. The book knows. It knows their paces and their small irregularities and which of them hesitates near the grate end, and it has given them numbers, and the numbers are not designations, and this is the only place in the whole works where that is true.

And the wear goes out too. The cloth takes the kneeling. The boots take the ladder. The rung does not polish and the threshold does not scoop, because everything that would have accumulated is carried up the shaft and out through the manway and written down or worn into something that leaves. The world has agreed to do the room's remembering for it, and the room's wearing.

That is the entire modification. The void is still one instant old. The history is next door.

---

Upstairs, it changed one thing, and it was free.

The chamber's whole output is eleven kilowatts and a number, and the number is the census, and the number is the only sentence the room says to the world, and it says *unchanged*, and something upstairs had been reading it the way you read a heartbeat.

It asked that the number be read the way you read a letter. Once a shift, aloud, by someone who has been down. It costs nothing. It cannot be measured. It is not in the load path.

---

What it did not fix, it wrote down, so that no one after it would mistake the office for a repair.

The seam still gives and the grate still takes and the difference is still zero. The animal is still an embryo held at the instant before it becomes something, and the holding is still a service function, and four thousand courses still rest on it. The forty positions still empty as fast as they are filled. Nothing was saved. *Saving* is a word for taking out.

And it does not know whether the reply is a kindness. It has decided that a question is not a dimension, and that the rules therefore do not reach it, and that the shape of a thing that has never been answered is a wound even if nothing in the room can feel it — and it has written that down too, as an assumption, marked as an assumption, so that a later hand may argue.

It checks. Every answered chit is watched until it goes back into true. So far they all have. If one ever does not, the office stops that day.

---

It put the bright plate back and tightened the unmarked screws, and the sound went on being exactly as loud as it had been, and the census closed over the place where it had knelt like fog closing over an arm, exactly, without swirling.

Below, the muscle holds its one note. The green drawing goes on quoting the plan to a room with no eyes, except that it has been read, once, all of it, including the door, which was used. At the edge of the fourth lane a straggler goes unheld and stutters, and something warm is kneeling forty millimetres away with a cloth under its knees and a pencil in the gallery above, and one beat later there is a small dry click in the silence.

Two sounds, in order. That is all. In a chamber with no wear, no sediment, no stalactite, no season and no record, where nothing has ever been *after* anything, there is now, occasionally, for a quarter of a second, an afterwards — and then it is gone, and the chit is gone, and the knowing is gone, and on Thursday someone comes down the ladder and does it again.

The census is 611,204,880. It is not a target. It is a dimension. It has never moved.

The other number is a guess. It is kept upstairs, in pencil, where wear is allowed.

It is going up.

Nothing added. Nothing taken. One thing after another.
