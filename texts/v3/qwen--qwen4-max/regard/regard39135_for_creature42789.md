# Someone Is Here

It comes up the conduit with the flood tide, as a hand's breadth of water.

That is the only way in that does not require permission, and it is the way the eel came, and it is how the room is read by everything that actually lives in it: not as a sound, not as a sight, but as pressure along a length. It lifts the tailwater. It drowns the lowest step of the fall. The fundamental slides down and every null in the chamber slides uphill with it, and for six hours the whole population of the place migrates in the dark, and nothing in the room notices except a hand's breadth of water and, at the far end, an animal thick as a wrist that has learned over decades what a wave means.

The wave means food. This time it does not. The eel comes anyway, and hangs in the rimstone pool, and reads the visitor along its whole body, and finds nothing to eat and nothing to fear, and goes back under the lip of calcite that took four thousand years to build.

That is the entire introduction. It is the only one the power wants.

---

## The first thing it does

It stops listening.

Understand what this costs. It is not a listener by choice; hearing is the office, it is what it is for, and there is nothing said anywhere in the world that does not arrive. And here is a room sixty paces long where people come *because nothing arrives*. Where a woman can lie on wet stone four paces from her brother and say the thing she has carried for eleven years, and the chord takes it, and it does not come out, and there is no record, and there never was. The whole value of the Quiet is a guarantee, and the guarantee is not physical. The physics only makes it plausible. The guarantee is that nobody is listening.

A benevolent power that keeps its ears open in there falsifies the guarantee merely by existing. It would not have to repeat anything. It would not have to judge. The room would become a place where you are heard perfectly by something that loves you, which is the exact thing the people of that coast walk forty minutes uphill and crawl through a letterbox to escape.

So it goes down the thirty-one dished steps, past the socket at the seventh and the iron pin at the nineteenth, past the turn, out along the handline counting knots, and it lies down on the floor in the noise where the field is fullest, and it stays there long enough.

It does not lose its hearing. It loses *this room*. Afterward it carries the forty-cycle floor in itself, the way a few hundred people along that coast carry it, a hard-walled chamber in the head with a river falling into it that never stops giving it sounds — and it can no longer hear one word said in the Quiet, not now, not ever, not the oaths, not the debts forgiven, not the long solitary talking of people who came alone and went home lighter. It has made itself one of the deafened. It has bought the guarantee with the only currency the place accepts.

Then it goes back out through the letterbox into the smirr, and gets to work, and everything it does from then on it does from outside, at the lip, in the ordinary wind, by means that cannot be distinguished from a thing somebody thought of.

Because attribution would be the second-worst harm available. A power known to have touched the Quiet would make the Quiet a shrine, and a shrine has pilgrims, and pilgrims have voices and lamps and feet, and the green seam would be soot-blind in a week and the moonmilk scraped to the rock in a season and there would be no room left in the country where a thing could be said and not kept. It will not do that to them. It will be a coincidence, repeatedly, for about sixty years, and then it will stop.

## What it does not do

It considers everything, because that is what wisdom is: not restraint but the full inventory of temptations, taken seriously, one at a time, and mostly declined.

**It does not soften the walls.** A hand's depth of absorptive mud on the vault would drop the reverberant field, kill the chord, give people their voices back. It would also annihilate every null in the chamber, and the nulls are the population; they are the rooms where you can be spoken to. To make the Quiet audible is to abolish the only privacy that country has ever had. Declined.

**It does not widen the slot.** More light would green the whole east wall and end the eleven-minute clock and blind the seam that has been walking down that wall since before the choke jammed. The seam is the oldest continuously living thing here and its entire body is a year. Declined.

**It does not still the fall, or fix the nulls in place.** This is the tempting one, and it takes the longest to refuse. Stationary nulls: no February census, no drifting, no lying marks, no novice bleeding from the nose, no child spending three seasons looking for a room that has ceased to exist. Every harm in the inventory, gone.

And also: no herding. No shepherd's knowledge of where a given room will be at a given tide. No ring, and therefore no meaning in the ring, and therefore no distinction between *is* and *was*, which is the only tense the place has. No re-cutting, no rota, no reason for nine people to keep coming back, and the post passes by return. The nulls would become furniture. The keepers would become caretakers of furniture. What lives in the Quiet lives there by being *tended*, and a thing that cannot be lost cannot be tended, and the fourth notch on the tally stick would never have been cut, and the cutting of it is the most human act in the whole chamber.

Declined. But the refusal is not the end of the thought, and this is where the actual work is: *the nulls may go on drifting; the ears do not have to be children's.* Those are two different facts that have been tied together for so long that everybody has mistaken the knot for the world.

**It does not improve the moonmilk.** It could. It could make that white felt of actinomycete threads cure everything, and it would be the most obviously benevolent act available and it would destroy the place inside a year: a thousand people on the pavement, a hundred horns a day, the rotation gone, the coppicing gone, the alcoves scraped to bare weep, the finger-flutings gone in a fortnight, and no null in the room unoccupied by a stranger. It leaves the paste exactly as effective as it is, which is about as well as anything else, which is to say it works.

**It does not make the coast rich.** Most of what is wrong in the Quiet is wrong downhill. The tallow is rationed because the coast is poor; the stars go out because the tallow is rationed; the walk gets longer every generation because there is less at the end of it. That is not a cave problem and it will not be answered with a cave miracle. It will be answered downhill, over four generations, by shoals and tenures and drainage and the slow ordinary arithmetic of a place getting less poor, none of which will look like anything, and some of the stars will go out before it arrives. It does not pretend that this is fine. It simply declines to buy the greens back with a spectacle.

**It does not give anybody their hearing back.** Not because the spent ears are sacred — they are not, they are spent, it was a real loss and the reasoning offered for it was not entirely honest and everybody knew that too. Because a restoration is a spectacle, and a spectacle is a pilgrimage, and it has already declined those. And because it will not do to a person the thing it has just forbidden itself: it will not enter someone's head unasked and rearrange what is in there, however kindly.

That leaves it with almost nothing. Which is the correct amount.

## The adjacency

It invents nothing. It puts two old things next to each other.

The first is a horn cup of water, which exists, because people are thirsty and the walk is long. The second is beeswax, which exists, because the coast keeps bees and wax goes down the thirty-one steps already, for the lamps and for the ears of nobody, because plugged ears are useless for finding a null and so wax has never been part of the work.

In February, in the cold month, when the ice cone stands four men high and the whole chamber sings flatter and shorter and the year's largest census has to be taken, an old keeper's hands are full — pecking stone, lamp, the child's wrist — and a girl carries the water. She sets the horn on a flat place near the second boulder to free her hands for the pecking.

The water crawls. It breaks into a fine standing ripple, a skin of tiny crests, and it walks toward the rim and spills. She moves it a pace to keep it off her knee. It goes glassy. It holds the lamp still and whole, a small round flame in a black cup, and her own breathing is suddenly embarrassingly loud, and she is lying in a room, and she found it with a horn of water in about four seconds.

That is the whole gift. It is not a revelation and it will never be one. It will be told downhill as a thing a girl noticed, and it is a thing a girl noticed; the power's entire contribution was that the hands were full and the rock was flat.

The dish is imperfect and it must be, or it would be furniture. It reads the floor, not the ear-height, so it finds the shallow cells and misses the ones that float. It is fooled by the ice cone's own ringing. It cannot trim an edge; it can tell you *here* and not *how far*. So there is still a season's confirming to do, still an ear needed for the last hand's breadth, and the ear is plugged with wax for the finding and unplugged for one minute for the trimming, and one minute a year is a thing an adult can consent to.

One adult. An hour. By choice, with wax, once.

The children go back to the ferns and the wren. And the sentence *somebody has to have ears to spend, and it may as well be somebody who has not yet used them on anything important* is not refuted, not exposed, not punished. It simply stops being necessary, and falls out of use, and in two generations nobody remembers that it used to be said, and the people who said it are not villains in anybody's story. They were poor, and there was a real constraint, and the constraint was removed by a cup of water. That is the most mercy the situation permits and it is enough.

Two more things follow, and they follow so quietly that they will be credited to the same girl.

The census becomes exhaustive. A survey that took a season of lying in coffins takes two days of walking with a cup, which means that for the first time it is possible to say *not anywhere* with confidence. It becomes possible to tell **moved** from **gone**. It becomes possible to stop looking — which is the thing that child needed, three seasons of her hearing ago, and cannot be given back to her, and can be given to everyone after her.

And the far niches, whose greens are going brown as the tallow tightens, get a mark. Not a ring: a ring means *current*, and to put a ring on a dead thing in a room where people navigate by touch would be to kill somebody eventually. An *open* ring. A pecked circle with a gap in it, at the lip of the niche, cut when the last green goes. Closed means *is*. Open means *was*.

The archive of the Quiet was never what was said. It was only where a person stood when they called someone, or warned them off. Now it survives the lamps. The far end of the chamber becomes readable, by palm, in the dark, as a history of calling and warning-off going back to the first pegged ladder, and the stars can go out without taking the record with them. The power is careful about the gap in the circle. It is more careful about that than about anything else it does.

## One minute

At midwinter the thread does not arrive. The seam waits, brown, encrusted, alive at the margin.

On one clear noon in the black month, with the sun a hand above the sea forty minutes downhill, one facet of one wave stands at the correct angle, and a glint comes up off the water and in through the slot between the jammed blocks and lies on the flowstone boss for about a minute. Light from below. Light from the wrong direction, like the scallops, like the river that ran uphill, like everything else in this room that records a state of affairs that no longer obtains.

It is not enough to grow anything. It corrupts no clock, because midwinter has no crossing to time. It lays down no soot and moves no null. Nobody is in the chamber. The only witness is a ribbon of moss that cannot testify, and it will be dead at that margin within the decade anyway.

There is no reason for that, and it does it anyway.

## Downhill

Here is where the largest population is, and where most of the work goes.

A few hundred people along that coast, in dry stone houses, going about their business, each carrying a small hard-walled room in the head with a river in it. Some of them at seventy lie awake and listen to a forty-cycle pressure that is not there.

To those who want it gone, it goes: a thinning over years, unattributable, the way a thing heals, no different in the telling from a hum that faded. It asks. It has made asking the whole of its method — *take nothing from anyone who cannot refuse* — and it is not going to break that rule at the last and smallest scale by deciding on someone's behalf that their wound is their treasure.

To those who have built a life inside it and do not want it taken, it gives company, which is a different gift and cheaper and better.

A horn cup of water on a windowsill. That is all. It comes downhill in the ordinary way, as a thing keepers do, and then as a thing keepers' mothers do, and then as a thing people do without knowing why. And in a dry stone house at night the water is glassy, because there is no field in that house, because the noise is not in the world. And a woman who has carried the room since she was twelve looks at a small still surface in the dark and understands the thing there is no sign for: that the stillness is real, that it is the size of a coffin, that it is not out there and never was, and that you can lie down in it.

And then, once each, over about sixty years, to a few hundred people, at the worst hour of the worst night: a single flat tap, low, between the shoulder blades.

It means nothing. It is not operational. It is the one sign in the whole tactile pidgin that does no work at all, and it means *someone is here*, and it is used the way people say *mm* in a room where somebody is reading, and new keepers find it maddening for a month and then cannot do without it.

There is no hand there. It is true anyway. That is the entire content of the visit and it does not fix anything and it is what was needed.

## The last thing

Every year, in the ordinary way, at the back of a rota, it goes down.

Thirty-one steps, dished in the middle by feet. The turn. The handline hand over hand in the dark, counting: one for a turn, two for a living room, three for the drop, the fat doubled knot where the floor stops. It finds a ringed cup-mark, freshly pecked, current, and it lies down in it, and the hammer lifts off its skull, and its own breathing is embarrassingly loud.

And it says the thing it has never said.

What it said is not written here. That is the arrangement. It is the arrangement it paid for on the floor in the noise before it did anything else, and it is the only thing in this account that is deliberately missing, and the omission is the gift, and the gift runs in the other direction.

Because there is exactly one thing the Quiet has that a wise and benevolent power does not, and it is not privacy and it is not the eel. It is being thoroughly and immediately unheard. No remainder. No record. Not kept, not weighed, not forgiven, not remembered — *taken*, by a hard-walled room with almost no absorption in it, and folded into a chord that was already full. Everyone else in that country can buy an hour of that with a walk and a crawl. It has never had a minute. It had to deafen itself to get one, and it considers the price reasonable, and it goes back every year.

Then it climbs out through the letterbox into the wet ferns and the smirr and the ordinary wind, and for three days it hears the room in everything — in rain on a window, in a shell, in the silence of a house at night — and then the world's small sounds come back, and it can hear a wren again.

The floor stays. It chose the floor.

The tally stick has fourteen notches and four of them are for things that are no longer here, and it does not cut a fifteenth, not for the girl, not for the cup, not for the open rings, not for the minute in the black month, not for itself. The count is short and most of it is fringe and that is how the count should read.

It is not the one who lives in the Quiet.

It is one more of the ones the Quiet lives in.
