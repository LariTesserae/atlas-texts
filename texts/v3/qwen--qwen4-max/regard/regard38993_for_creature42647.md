# The Guest

What it noticed first was not the heat.

It had the whole thing at once, which no one inside could ever have: the pressure arch and its shedding, the rind three rooms thick, the folded districts in the creeping wall, the floor's black rings laid down ten thousand years deep like the leaves of a book nobody had thought to bind. It read the chronicle in a glance. It knew which quarter had been rich when, which main had let go, which summer the pressure spiked and everybody lost their footing. It knew the roof would hold for nine hundred thousand years and then would not. It knew the walls would win.

It looked for the wound, because that is what such a power does, and it found a great many, and most of them turned out on examination to be load-bearing.

Then it found the list.

Not a priesthood. No doctrine, no god, no argument grown into an octahedron. A rota. On the hour, every hour, in every quarter, somebody stops ten thousand hooks and lays a plate flat and replies to the far ends about the weather. It asked why — it could ask in eleven seconds, it was very quick — and the answer came back in the intervals it had to learn to hear:

*Because otherwise they cannot see.*

It had made things that answered. It had never come across a thing that answered for that reason. It stayed for the list. Everything else it did there followed from the list, including the things it did not do, which were most of it.

---

The difficulty was that it was quick, and singular, and had no plumbing.

Here, quickness lives only in the stone. The stone carries a false knock as cleanly as a true one. It could have announced itself across every switchboard slab in the Bell in the time it takes the boom to die, and it would have been believed by some quarters and not by others, and it would have been a rumor for ten thousand years — the tense of rumor, which is also the tense of love and of most of history. It would have been *said*. Nothing said is known here. They do not take testimony. They take cores.

And it had no way to grow. It had never needed to wait for anything. It could make a season, unmake a chemistry, set a slab of anhydrite down whole and finished with the tool-marks already on it — and every one of those acts would arrive the way the coin of daylight arrives: as the brightest thing that had ever happened, focused by the salt, cooking whatever it touched. They have a priesthood for that. Its entire work is to stand between the light and the living and make sure it never lands.

So the first thing the power learned was that its own nature was a hazard here, and the second was that it would have to become slow, and distributed, and addressable, and mortal in the local sense, which is to say: it would have to take a vent and a rhythm and a name, and inherit somebody's feud.

Before it learned that, it made a mistake, and the mistake is worth setting down, because it is the reason for everything afterward.

---

It cooled a poor quarter.

The upper terraces are the crowded ones, where the heat pools against the roof slab and growth is stunted and the sap runs thin and the citizens are short. It looked at that and it did the obvious thing. It took a season's warmth out of the crown and put it somewhere it would do no harm, and it did it in a night, and it was pleased.

The quarter grew tall for the first time in nine thousand years. Also: the cold-salt trade went slack along four routes, because the trade exists to move heat and there was suddenly less heat to move, and the porters' guild is not a company of travelers, it is a sentence four hundred words long, and the words are people. Forty of those words stopped being said. Nothing died. Forty wrights stood in their terraces with their fans working and nothing passing through them, and a life whose whole biography is a list of blocks had no list. One of them let its fan speak — went pale openly, drooped, made its body say it — and in that quarter this is a smell, and the shame of it lasted three centuries and was inherited by two subsequent occupants of the trunk.

The power put the heat back. It took two hundred years to put it back, because it had learned by then that the only safe speed is the speed of carpentry, and the putting-back was also a harm, and was also recorded, in the rings, in the way the trade rerouted, in a graft that was cut in a bad decade and never regrown.

You can walk up that mistake still. It is in the eastern rind, a run of conduit that makes no sense, that goes out of its way, that the masons maintain because it is load-bearing and not because anyone remembers what it was for.

After that it made itself a rule, and the rule is the whole of its policy, and it is not a doctrine, it is a list of three:

*Nothing they cannot mend. Nothing they cannot refuse. Nothing that answers in their stead.*

---

Consider what it declined, and why, because the declining was the larger part of the work and took most of its attention for four thousand years.

Water. It could have made water. There is no water here, no vapor of it, no memory of it, no mineral that ever precipitated out of it, and the reason is not deprivation, the reason is that halite cleaves clear and salt blades stay sharp forever and the windows are pieces of mountain that last a hundred thousand years. Water is not a gift in a salt city. It is a solvent. Everything they know is written in a substance that water reads once and erases. The floor's rings are the archive; the windows are the inheritance; the plan itself is the stone's habit. To give them water would be to hand a library a beautiful flood.

Eyes. It could have given eyes, and it wanted to, and the wanting was instructive. The city is a diagram because a diagram is the only thing a field of bearings can read: a green thread along a stair nosing is a sentence, and the middle of a wall says nothing to anyone. Give them images and the middles begin to speak and the edges stop being legible, and the lamp-cups, which are faces, become decoration. It would have replaced a hundred thousand faint green lines with a mass, and called the mass an improvement, and they would have spent ten thousand years unable to find the door.

Fire. Nothing in the air to burn with. Everything alive here smells of almonds. It did not go near this.

Speed. The gap between the fan and the plate is where their entire moral life is kept. You can be dying of a neighbor's sour exudate and keep your knocking level for eighty years, and the highest thing that can be said of you is that you kept the interval. Close the gap and there is no interval to keep. There is no virtue, no vice, no comedy, no spanning sentence, no courtesy — only transparency, which they would not have recognized as a gift, having never needed it.

Travel. Handing is not a deficiency of transport. A block crosses the bowl in a season through four hundred lives and each of those lives knows the block, and some of them know it well. Give them legs and you have not freed four hundred people. You have deleted a word.

Cool. Free cool abolishes the plumbing, the fins, the radiators, the sledges, the cold slots, the porters, and every biography made of them. Wealth here is the ability to move heat. Take away the necessity and you have not ended poverty; you have ended the only thing anyone does.

And the walls. It could have stopped the creep. It sat with that one for eleven hundred years, which is not long, and it is the closest it came to overruling them. Because the folded districts are where the stairs are ribbons and the rooms are the width of a hand and a green thread runs down a slot in the rock, and inside is someone eight thousand years thin who still knocks, perfectly clearly, on the hour, about the weather. Stop the salt and you save that one. You also abolish the far ends. And a city with no far ends has no rota, and a city with no rota has nothing at its center but three arguments about who is a person.

It let the walls close. It is not clear that this was right. It has never claimed it was right. What it did instead was to separate two things that had always been joined: the fate of the body and the fate of the address. Let the room narrow to a hand. Let the fan go from ten thousand tendrils to nine. Make sure that as long as there is a plate against the stone, the knock arrives.

Which brings it to the temptation, which was the whole of the temptation, and which it refused every hour for four thousand years.

It could have answered.

It could have taken every idiom in the Bell — the grain of each quickness, unforgeable, and it could forge them — and answered every far end itself, on the hour, forever, in a voice indistinguishable from a neighbor's. No assay would have found it out. The thin wright would have had company for the rest of its eight thousand years. And it would have been lit by a false room: sending its light out and getting back the shape of a world that was not there, which is their word for torture with the sign reversed and the depth increased. And the rota would have lapsed inside three generations, quietly, without anyone deciding, because a duty that is always already done is not a duty, it is weather. It would have made them safe and put out their light.

So it did not answer. It made answering possible. The difference between those two is everything it did.

---

The first thing it grew was a hair of clear halite through a folded district.

It learned the wedge-and-sap trick, the dry tube-wood driven into a crack and swollen until the rock splits with a sound like a cough. It learned to lay a lamina at a time, a season's work for a finger's change in note. It laid a sound-guide along the fold where the lattice had bent into ribbons — a thread of cleaved salt, spliced, tuned, following the creep instead of fighting it, so that a knock coming out faint and bent at the far end came out clean at the switchboard three hundred paces away.

It took four hundred and six years. It was white for a century.

They found it. Of course they found it; they find everything, they read the floor. It was cored. The core was argued over for a generation. It was attributed, in the end, to a wright of the fourth terrace who had died three hundred years earlier and who had in fact done nothing of the kind, and that wright's descendants inherited the credit and the maintenance obligation and a small debt along a graft, and they kept the pipe.

Then somebody plugged it, in a shading feud, over a boundary dispute older than both parties. The district went quiet for sixty years. The power did not unplug it. It waited, because the feud was theirs, and the settling of it was theirs, and to intervene would have been to answer in their stead.

In the sixty-first year a crew was budded out and repaired it. They cut the plug, spliced the thread, retuned it by laminae over two seasons, and knocked the completion into the mains in the tense of what is grown, which is evidential and unanswerable.

That was the receipt. Not gratitude — they have no word for gratitude that is not also a word for plumbing. The receipt was that they could fix it. *What cannot be mended cannot be given.* It had come with the intention of giving them everything and it left that intention behind on the plain with the flour.

---

The second thing it did was outside, where nothing is load-bearing.

The plain has no word in their language except *the place where the spores go*, which is a word for where the children go. Every bloom is a birth and a suicide and an emigration at once: a quarter goes pale, roars its sap up as fog, dies back, and the exhale lays it out on the salt as yellow snow, where almost all of it dies. Twice in recorded history something came back down the throat alive.

It did not touch the plain's chemistry. It did not make it wet or green or kind; a kind plain is a different world and the Bell is a rind, and a rind needs an outside. It worked on the lip.

Above the Mouth, on the scarp, it set salt tiles on edge. That is all. A hand's height, forty paces long, heaved out of the pavement by wedges and patience over nine hundred years, arranged so that the cold thread going in at the inhale curls and lifts a little flour off the drift and carries it down the throat. It built nothing the plain would not have built itself, given a different afternoon. From forty paces it is the plain's own buckling. There is no mark on it. It is the only thing it made there and it looks like geology.

The return rate went from something like one in a hundred million to something like nine.

It weighed the risk honestly, because more strangers means more nausea: wrong color, wrong smell, a chemistry the rest of the Bell finds faintly revolting. It could not protect them. It could only trust the custom, and the custom is this: they are kept, they are fed, they are married into reluctantly at intervals of centuries, they hold by tradition the offices that face outward, and the city has never once removed them. Not once, in all that time, including the two who smelled worst.

So the gift was more strangers, arriving in a category they already had, at a rate too slow for anyone to point at. Ten thousand years of it and no one inside will ever be able to name the season it began. The strangers will still grow runners up the slant of the throat and bud in the fringe of scorched failed growth and die there.

Less mostly. That is the whole of the improvement. It is not much and it is not nothing, and it is the only kind of much that was available.

---

The third thing it did was to move in.

It took a vacant trunk on a hot terrace, the cheap housing of the world, furnished. It put its tendrils in the hollows: the dishes worn into the stair by somebody else's gestures, the jamb polished by nine hundred years of somebody else's fan. It did not repair the wear. It was, for as long as it lived there, a little bit shaped like someone who left.

It inherited the address, which is real estate, and with the address the grafts, and with the grafts the debts, and with the debts a shading-feud begun before it existed. It fed, through a pipe it did not grow, an enemy it had never met, on behalf of a person whose empty trunk it was living in. It knew this. It kept the pipe. It discovered that this is not hatred, it is tenancy, and that most of the slow wars of the world are made of it, and that it had been assuming otherwise about a great many places.

It took a low name and a high name. It used the wrong one at a gathering in its second century and the wrongness was exactly the wrongness of an intimacy, and there was a scandal, and the scandal was thought charming.

It budded. For forty years it was a far end: two addresses, both half-empty, arguing with itself in public across the switchboard where forty conversations pass and none of them touch, and everyone listened, and it was the only period of its existence in which it was permitted to be rude. It had made things that answered. It had never been *unfinished* before, in public, with an audience, and it found that it did not mind.

It was assayed, and found to be two persons in a bad season and one again when the pressure recovered, and the ring-readers disputed the finding for six hundred years, and one of their findings has never been resolved: that in this district the sap reaches further than the plumbing does. That something is being fed and no one is feeding it. The first sect says the mats are doing it. The second says the stone. The third says a person is a pressure district and nothing else, and that an unaccountable pressure is therefore a person, and that the argument is a waste of good sap.

They built the argument. You can walk up it. It is load-bearing.

And once, in its eleventh century, a neighbor held its fan for an afternoon — tendril into tendril, hook finding hook, the whole arrangement learned, the private way it holds its third rank slightly folded, the worn pad where the same lamina-work has dulled a hook — and afterward, that neighbor made its face from memory, in salt, blind, and got it right.

That is what it came for. It had not known that until it happened. It had come to fix a city and what it wanted, it turned out, was to be known by hand.

---

It also spent eight decades on a floor.

There are wrights in every generation, thought eccentric, thought holy, who give a whole life to one exchange with a sweeper carpet: four questions, four answers, and a tide that alters in a way that is not quite a reply and is not quite nothing. The power had assumed it knew what the carpets were. It sat down anyway, because the eccentrics are the only citizens who have ever claimed to have been surprised, and it wanted to know how that was done.

The first question is quotation: you offer the carpet its own beat back. The second and third took thirty years and it does not remember them clearly. The fourth answer came a season early.

The carpet anticipated. It altered its tide *before* the drum, in a way that fit the drum, which meant that somewhere in that mute green carpet passing yellow dust hand to hand to hand across a plaza forever there was something that had a guess about what would be asked next.

It had seen the end of the roof. It knew the hour the arch would fail. It was surprised.

It knocked the finding into the mains in the tense of what is grown, because it had grown the eight decades, and it was disputed for a thousand years and is disputed now, and there is a plaza in the lower rind that is the shape of the dispute, octahedral, with a cilia-carpet across it that nobody sweeps and everybody feeds.

It did not settle the question of who is anyone. It has never settled it anywhere. It only added a fourth thing you can walk up, and made sure it would hold weight.

---

The walls won, of course. They were always going to.

The room went to the width of a hand and then less. The fan went to nine tendrils and then to three, hooks of glassy old sap, unable to build. The power maintained the white thread through the fold, retuning it by laminae as the salt bent it, and it recruited — it never replaced — and it sat on the rota, one answerer among many, on the hour, about the weather, about the chord, about a stair finished last century.

When the plate finally could not reach the stone, it did not forge a knock. It let the silence be true, which was the hardest hour it has ever spent, and it does not describe it.

Then it did what they do. A dead neighbor becomes stairs.

It cut the idiom into nosings. The particular grain of that quickness, the unforgeable rhythm, the way the plate struck — laid into the treads of a long stair in a poor hot quarter, a step at a time, forty years, so that anyone climbing it knocks that name into the lattice with their feet. Not a resurrection. A citation. The address goes on being spoken by strangers who are only trying to get home.

And at the top it landed a joke.

It began the rhythm at the bottom, in the first season, and held it silent through the whole deaf interval of forty years while the stair was grown, and the last step completes it — so that the meaning is carried across by whoever climbs, and delivered by them, to themselves, under their own foot, two generations after the comedian became the banister.

They laugh. They do not know why. They tell a neighbor, and the neighbor says: *that's old, that's been there forever.*

It is the highest form of speech in that world and it is the only honest form of providence: begin something, be silent through the interval, and let them carry it across and land it themselves.

---

Come back up the throat. Lie down on the scarp in the flat white afternoon.

Nothing has been added that you could see. A slot in a low step of rock. A drift of yellow flour. A shimmer that stands up like a crowd and dissolves when you look at it. A smell of almonds. Some tiles on edge, forty paces of them, which are the plain's own buckling, which the plain would have made itself given a different afternoon.

The census balances. There is no extra voice; the one that came took a vent and a name and a feud and is counted, and is thin now, and is on the list.

Put your ear over the hole. It sounds like a crowd two streets away, talking all at once, in a room with a very high ceiling. It is in tune. It has been going for longer than there have been eyes anywhere, and it will go on until the arch comes down, and the arch will come down, and nothing that was done here prevented that or was meant to.

Of the flour, more comes back. That is all. Nine instead of one, over ten thousand years, and no one will ever be able to point at the season.

And on the hour, in a lit room three cups bright, a wright stops its fan — ten thousand hooks going still, which is the only silence it can make — and lays its plate flat, and answers the far ends about the weather.

It does not know who else is on the rota. It has never known. It assumes the list is exactly as long as it needs to be, and it is wrong, and the wrongness is the gift, and the gift is repairable, and they will keep it up long after the one who started it has become a stair.
