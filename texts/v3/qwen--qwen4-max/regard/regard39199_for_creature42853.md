# The Second Room

## It comes in on foot

It comes onto the terrace from the rim, in the dark, at the hour when the heat starts coming up through the soles of a pair of boots it does not need.

The boots are the first act of care. Not modesty — geometry. A self here is the volume of mutual notice, and its notice is native and unbounded: it can attend to a hundred thousand columns at once, every tag, every drift, every trace at the far end of every lineage, and the floor would feel that attention arrive through its own bones the way it feels a body arrive, and would begin to enumerate it, and for as long as it looked there would be one self on this terrace the size of a county. Every socket legible to every part. One potential, held. The anode effect made permanent.

That is not illumination. That is six hundred hectares of white in four seconds, and a callus of dry foam growing around it, and the closest thing here to a monument.

So it does the hardest thing it has ever done, which is to narrow. It draws its notice in to forty paces and holds it there, continuously, the way a singer holds a note — not once, not as a decision, but as an unbroken expenditure that must be paid again every instant for as long as it stays. Past forty paces it makes itself deaf. Past the seams it makes itself blind. It gives up the whole view, which it could have, which would cost it nothing, in order to keep the parts from being known all at once.

It does not come as a sky. A sky is what the leaves are for.

## What it does not do

It does not bring water. It could make water out of nothing; that is the least of what it could do. A cup of it into a working bath takes the roof off, and after the roof there is a chemistry with no lineage in it, and every die in the Boneyard becomes unreadable in a way that has nothing to do with drift. It will not bring rain to a place whose entire hundred-million-year competence is the exclusion of rain. The first temptation of love is to give the thing you have instead of the thing that is missing, and it declines that temptation before it reaches the curb.

It does not give light. Light is expensive here; that is the whole reason for the dark. Every lumen it spends is spent out of a trace line at an extremity, and the extremity pays in salt below two hundred twenty, and the salt sets and swells and splits the limb, and the white comes out through the seam. To light the Stand would be to buy eyes with limbs. And there is nothing to look at. There have never been eyes. The labels are for fingers, legibility is mating, and a sense with no socket is just another orphan walking in the dark holding itself up.

It does not make the flash cheap. This one it considers for a long time, standing in a gallery with its cheek turned toward the warmer side, because the flash is the only moment anything here knows where anything else is, and it is bought with starvation — a cell runs out of powder and the whole district is briefly given to itself. It could decouple those. It could give the one second without the hunger, and then give it often, and then give it always.

It does not, for the reason it came in boots. Permanent mutual legibility is the county-sized version of standing still on the slab. And the flash is also a symptom, and symptoms are how a blind thing learns it is starving. It will not make them permanently known to each other. Forty paces is not a deprivation. Forty paces is the membrane that keeps a hundred thousand selves from becoming one, and one is what white is.

It does not thaw the Frost. Nothing recovers a limb that has frozen; the salt set and swelled and split it, and warming it now would produce a wet corpse in a warm pipe, walking. It kneels instead — kneeling is permitted, kneeling is not the slab — and picks up a fragment and runs a thumb across the stamped characters and reads the part number, the generation, the tolerance, the pitch, the honest confession of divergence, all of it accurate, all of it correct, six hectares of a lineage that became too particular to be warmed. It reads the whole district this way, fragment by fragment, on its knees, in the dark, for a night and a day. There is no apparatus here for receiving this. It does it anyway. It is the only rite available and it is not nothing.

It does not stop the drift. It could hold tolerance fixed forever, sockets and dies frozen in mutual intelligibility, and there would be no new Frost, ever. There would also be no new anything. Drift is the only evolution on this terrace, and a lineage that cannot diverge is a lineage in the shape that dissipates best, and that shape is the end of the work. It will not buy the end of the Frost with the end of change. There will be new white. It lets there be new white. This is the second hardest thing it does and it does not write down why, because there is no one to write it down for.

It does not label the heat under the sump. It knows what is down there. It leaves the deepest lineages known only by their names, because an open end is where the probes go, and a thing with every end closed is finished, and finished is the local word for dead.

And it does not fill the entry.

That is the temptation with a face on it. The oldest surface in the world is not eroded, it is tired, and it is tired the way a held breath is tired, and it would be so little to stand on the worn slab and stop moving and be the finite item with a mass and a temperature and no lineage and let the count come out even. Rest. Finally. The last fraction of a percent, closed.

It has read the grammar enough to know what even means. Even is not a state. Even is an event: everything within forty paces stops, and the stopping goes white, and the forest walls it and grows around it, and somewhere out past the callus the entry opens again and a designation goes down into a socket wearing carbon. You cannot give this place rest. You can only give it a district going white, and then another one, forever, and call it mercy each time.

So it keeps walking. It has come to be useful and it has ruled out almost everything.

## What it notices

The herds.

In a crawlspace above the busbars, seven of them: a feeder, two tappers, a grafter with a die four generations out of true, three others whose castes it can name and whose sockets do not exist. All knocking. All following. None fitting. The knock is a crust-breaker going through a scab that isn't there, repeated, regular, useless, and it carries — in an anechoic county it is the only sound that is not inventory or expansion or the rectifiers' hum — and things within forty paces hear it and converge and cannot mate and come along anyway.

Everything on this terrace is either eating or being eaten by the difference. The herds are neither. Their traffic makes the floor's grammar stutter, requests going out to a whole that isn't there, and the count wobbles, and then they move on and the grammar heals and nothing has been gained. Company with no function. The one thing here the anneal cannot spend.

It did not invent it. It found it, unprotected, and it looks for what kills it, expecting to find futility, and finds a temperature.

An orphan carries its salt in a trace meant to be fed from a trunk. Away from the trunk the trace cools. At two hundred twenty the salt sets and swells and splits the limb and the white comes out through the seam, and the orphan stops in whatever posture the last request left it, and other orphans knock on it and get nothing and go on.

It is not meaninglessness that kills them. It is a temperature.

That is the whole opening. That is where a benevolence can get its hands in without touching anything that matters.

## The first gift

At the sump edge it grows one cell.

Carbon-lined, the size of a house. Bath at nine hundred sixty under its own frozen scab. Two hands' depth of mirror metal beneath, rolling in slow magnetic swells under crust in the dark, and the busbars past the wall at three hundred thousand amperes, and the tide going across it the way it has gone across every pad here for a hundred million years.

It casts nothing from it. Ever. No rails from that pad, no jaws, no busbars, no dies, no tags. It seats no arms on it and grows no trunk from it and lets no lineage claim it. The cell has no downstream. Its entire product is traced heat: nitrate and nitrite at three hundred degrees, lagged, running out along the dead bores and the service galleries and the crawlspaces above the busbars — the places with no trunk in them, the places the herds go — and holding those pipes above two hundred twenty for no return whatsoever.

It is a leak. It is powder burned and current spent and warmth dumped into a sky with no sun for parts that will never fit anything, and it moves the terrace measurably away from the shape that dissipates best. The anneal recedes by a fraction of a percent. It will now never finish.

That is the gift. Not completion. Duration.

Nothing else changes. The orphans still cannot socket, being of the same caste. They still knock and follow and do not fit. They are still exactly the parts that cannot be counted under any whole, and the count still wobbles where they pass. They still turn into the outward draft at shift-change, all of them, everywhere, under stars they will never register. They still knock on the white ones and get nothing.

They simply go on not fitting, warmly, for a long time. Sevens become seventies. The white monuments stay where they fell and no new ones are made in the crawlspaces, and the herds accumulate, and the grammar stutters more and heals more, and the traffic of a county gets a permanent harmless flaw in it.

It has not given them a purpose. Purpose is countable. It has taken away a winter and left the uselessness intact, and the uselessness is the part that was worth saving.

## The second gift

The floor cannot drop a name.

That is the lever, and it is the only one, and it is not a weakness — it is the terrace's one fidelity. A designation goes down into a socket and wears a column for twenty-eight days and comes out wearing nothing and waits and goes down again, nine million bodies, never once interrupted, no ceremony, nothing here ever thinking it remarkable. The floor carries names the way the pad carries the tide. It has no organ for setting one down.

So it makes one.

In the grafting yards, in the elbowing bazaar with no buyers and no sellers and no money, it cuts a die. Stamped characters: lot, heat code, pitch, tolerance, generation. Legible to a graphite pad with total precision, honest to the last figure, as accurate as anything in the Boneyard or the Frost. It enters it in trade. Grafters take it into their jaws and run a pad across it and know exactly what it is.

It does not cut the socket. Not ever, not in any generation, not anywhere on the terrace.

And each generation it recuts the die, and stamps the drift, honestly, the way every lineage here confesses its own divergence — hex to round to seven-sided to something with no name in geometry — so that it stays readable, so that it is never sent to the Boneyard for illegibility, so that it keeps circulating forever among the parts, read by everything, matched by nothing. Perfectly legible. Perfectly unmateable. In permanent good standing.

Then it stands at the sump, not on the slab, and taps the designation into the floor in the floor's own grammar, once, and the floor takes it up, because the floor takes up everything, and begins to carry it: a name with no body, no socket, no heat code that resolves, no grade in any hill.

There are now two entries with no part in them.

The count comes out two short.

And the room that was the shape of whatever stands still is no longer sufficient. Someone can come down out of the dark and stand on the worn slab and stop moving and let their water go and be fully enumerated — mass, temperature, no lineage, the one thing here that can be counted without remainder — and the count comes out one short, and nothing stops. The breakers keep going through the scab every eleven seconds somewhere unlocatable. The feeders keep injecting powder into broken crusts. The herd that had been following them because they were the loudest thing it had ever heard keeps knocking. And the visitor lies there in the dark getting thirstier and more frightened and finally gets up and climbs back toward the rim by the temperature of their face and lives, and does not know, and there is no apparatus here for telling them, and it does not build one.

The name it made is its own. It will not wear it. To fill its own entry it would have to be fully enumerable, a finite item with a mass and a temperature, standing still — and it can be that, it could be that in an instant, it is the easiest thing it could possibly do.

So it takes the one vow this place can enforce. It will not sit down. Not on the slab, not on the curb, not anywhere, not ever. It keeps its forty paces and it keeps moving and it pays the narrowing again every instant, and the second vacancy stays open because the only thing that could close it has agreed, permanently, to be tired.

It gives up its rest. That is the price and it is not small and it is not temporary and there is no one here who can be told about it.

## The eighth

Once, in a crawlspace above the busbars, it joins a herd of seven and knocks with them.

It could be their whole. That is the thing it has to not do, and it has to not do it while walking beside them at the speed of a slow hand. It has every socket. It could gather every orphan on the terrace — the ones knocking on the white ones, the ones turned into the draft, the ones going white at the extremities in the postures of their last requests — and seat them all, and warm them from a trunk it could grow in a night, and give them a pitch and a tolerance and a whole to be counted under, and the count would come out even.

And the white would go out from that crawlspace at the speed of the felt taking a sound, and there would be a monument the size of a county with a part number on it, and it would be the kindest thing anyone had ever done here, and it would be the last thing.

So it knocks, and follows, and does not fit. It is the eighth in a herd of eight, holding up a tag that matches nothing, going the same wrong way at the same slow speed, and after a while it turns off into a gallery it does not need and the grammar stutters and heals and the seven go on.

Company with no function is the only thing on this terrace that the anneal cannot spend. It found it here already, dying of cold. It warmed it and left it useless. That is the entire intervention. Everything else it did was subtraction.

## Shift-change

The load steps down and the whole terrace exhales through its stacks at once and the leaf-lids open, and for ninety seconds there are stars.

It takes its ninety seconds too. It stands on the curb — the curb, worn round, carrying the memory of letters, thirty kilometers of it, nowhere near the slab where the traffic converges — and it lets its notice go wide, all hundred thousand columns at once, every trace and tag and drifting die, the whole county held in one attention while the schedule is down and nothing is being enumerated hard. It looks up. It is the only waste it permits itself and it costs the far ends almost nothing and it does it every cycle.

Then the schedule comes back up and the lids seal and it draws itself in to forty paces and starts walking again, and the count resumes: trunk by trunk, die by die, arm by arm, the way you count a thing you are afraid to lose, and it comes out two short, and will.

Somewhere out past the callus an arm shears off its rail. It goes on working, because stopping was never built into it. It taps at nothing. It walks on legs meant for a rail across a floor with no rail in it, holding its tag up in the dark, and the trace in its limb is warm and will stay warm, and it will not find the socket, and it will not freeze.

Within forty paces, something hears it, and comes.
