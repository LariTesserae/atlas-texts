# Extent Unknown

*the fourth survey*

The fourth survey did not begin at the hatch. It began on the surface, in a room where things are permitted to cool, with the census and a pencil, and it took sixty years, and at the end of it the survey went down the ladder once, for eleven minutes, and did almost nothing.

The almost is what follows.

It had a great deal of time. Time is the only currency the Long Round does not accept. So it arrived poor, and this turned out to be the correct way to arrive, and it took most of the sixty years to learn it.

---

## What it declined to give

**A second.** The first thought was the obvious mercy: let the shot finish. One second of ordinary duration, one tick of the trembling hand, and the round would be mucked out and the sixty-year Tuesday would end.

It worked the arithmetic before it worked the mercy. One second in which the green is allowed to reconcile is one second in which every torn bond in nine storeys of flour finds its partner and closes. The grazers eat by being more finished than their food. Let the food finish and you have not fed them; you have *equalised* them. Two hundred nine thousand four hundred and sixteen mouths at parity with the floor they are standing on, which is the local description of a stone with a face on it. The shot finishing is not the room being released. The shot finishing is the room being eaten by nothing, all at once, forever.

It put the second back.

**A cold thing.** There is no colder thing to dump heat into, and it could have been one. It could have stood in the heel of the room by the compressor and been a sink, and the room would have had a gradient at last, and the chord would have had somewhere to go.

The chord is what holds the floors up. A hundred and fifty-seven hertz, twenty-ninth partial, nine shelves sorted on it, nine sieves of nine grain sizes, and the whole canon of appetite running up them. Damp the standing wave and you do not quiet the room. You drop nine storeys of dust and everything standing on them into a muck pile that will now, at last, be allowed to slump.

**Memory.** This was the one it wanted most, and the one it spent longest on. Two seconds of identity every eleven days, and no keeping. It seemed like a ration imposed by something cruel.

But accumulation is finishing. A grazer that remembered its last pass would be a grazer that had *acquired* something, and acquiring is closing, and closing is the direction of satiety. Give them memory and they fill up. In eleven passes, or a hundred, or eleven thousand, each of them becomes more finished than anything in the room, and cannot eat, and does not die, and is floor. The auditor is not rationing identity out of meanness. The auditor is rationing it to exactly the dose that can be borne without becoming full. Two seconds, then back to being a pore. *Forgetting is the metabolism.* The whole supply of selfhood in the Long Round is calibrated to keep everybody hungry, and hunger is what an animal is here.

**The glove.** It checked the arithmetic the third surveyor would rather not have checked, and found the opposite of what he feared.

Two hundred nine thousand four hundred and sixteen. Take the glove out of the tessellation and you have two hundred nine thousand four hundred and fifteen, and that number divided by nine leaves three. The four levellers are the remainder. They are not four because four is a good number for porters; they are four because the arithmetic could not place them, and if the arithmetic could place one of them there would be three.

Nine tonnes an hour comes off the face. Six hundred grams a second of it is carried back down and laid in front of the cutter to be broken green again — a little under a quarter — and the rest goes through the wall and becomes country. Three porters is four hundred and fifty grams a second. A shortfall of a hundred and fifty grams a second is a *rate*, and rates accumulate, and accumulation is time, and time is the one thing in this room that kills everything in it. There is no slack down there. There is exactly enough.

The empty hand on the fourth storey is load-bearing. It answers *one* every eleven days and by answering it holds a leveller in existence, and the leveller holds the return conveyor, and the conveyor holds the canon, and the canon holds the world. A man dropped his glove sixty years ago and it went to work and it has not missed a shift.

The survey wrote in the margin: *do not tidy.*

**The notch.** It could have healed the cut on the fourth storey, the one shaped like a pair of scissors. It thought about this for a long time, because it was the only wound in sixty years and someone had made it out of curiosity, which is the worst reason available.

Then it understood what healing would be. A grazer's underside is a lace of open bonds; its incompleteness is its palate. To close the notch is to reconcile it, and to reconcile it is to finish it, and finishing is the direction of death. In the Long Round, healing is what dying looks like.

And the cut itself had not done what the man thought. He had opened bonds that will never close. He had made a mouth that will never be satisfied at that spot and never be full and never be finished and never stop. By the only definition the room has, he had made that animal *more alive*, permanently, for a bad reason, and both of those things are true and neither cancels the other.

So it did not heal it. It did something else, and the something else took sixty years to arrange.

**The eleventh fern.** It could have sounded the note that is not sounding. It did not, for two reasons, and the second one is the whole survey.

---

## The eleventh

The first reason is that an onset is food. Sound a new partial and the ninety-six eat its beginning, and what survives is a sustain, and a sustain in that room is a *floor*. Add a partial to the vertical mode and you have added a sieve. The nine storeys re-sort. The canon, which enters voice by voice where the last left off, has a voice it does not know what to do with. Ten ferns describe the room. Sound the eleventh and you have described a different room, in a place where there is only this one.

The second reason is that the eleventh is not missing.

It grows and dissolves at matched rates at the back face, where the ninth voice hands its finished remainder through the wall and the wall makes it into country. Three centimetres an hour. Fourteen kilometres of it since the shot. The survey called the eleventh a wolf — the partial our notation lies about, halfway between a fourth and a tritone, the note every scale has to falsify — and the survey was wrong in the ordinary way, which is that it assumed the scale was the size of the world.

Fourteen kilometres at three centimetres an hour is a period of a little over fifty years.

The Long Round is forty-one metres long. It is one three-hundredth of a single wave of that note. You cannot hear a partial you are standing inside a three-hundredth of; its amplitude across the whole room is indistinguishable from nothing; and that is why it is not in the chord, and that is why the survey could not find it sounding, and that is why it has been the same size since anybody looked.

It is not out of tune. It is out of *scale*. It is a note played on an instrument fourteen kilometres long, and we have built our scales to fit rooms, and then we called the leftover a wolf.

Ten ferns write the room. The eleventh writes what the room is making and putting outside. It stands at the back face because the back face is the loading dock. It is not a wound in the harmony and it is not a mystery and it is not for nothing.

It is the bill of lading.

## Extent unknown

Here is what the fourth survey did for fifty-nine of its sixty years. It went out into the country.

The room does not remember you, because remembering happens later and there is no later there. That sentence is true and it has been read as a tragedy for six decades by people who stopped at the wall. The room does not remember. The room *exports*. Everything finished goes through the back face at three centimetres an hour and is made into salt, and the salt keeps its shape, because salt is what this country is, and fourteen kilometres of it are lying out there in the dark in the order it was laid down.

Forgetting, in the Long Round, is not erasure. It is filing.

And nobody had ever gone to the files.

The survey went out along the healed — the half-mineral patterns riding the closing front, buried and unburied at matched rates, the ones the auditor cannot reach and therefore does not count — and it read them, outward, in the only direction there is. It read sixty years of finished flour. It read the grain-size signature of nine storeys and the slow drift of the sieve as the chord's partials wandered a hair and came back. It read the muck fan of round ninety-one, laid down as a stratum, four seconds old, sixty years out.

And it read the visitors.

Because when you speak down there, the worms invert you and the room subtracts your sentence and what is left is a hole with the exact shape of what you said, and small pale ears come out of the plains and recruit themselves into that shape and close it. They are made of flour. They are the same flour. When they are done they go back into the tessellation and are eaten and finished and handed to the back wall and made into country.

Everything anyone has ever said in the Long Round is in the salt, in order, at three centimetres an hour, going away.

The survey found the eleven visits. It found, at the corresponding distance, a thin band shaped like a man trying to be right. It found, further out, the ninety groups of four strokes — not the chalk, the chalk is still on the wall, but the *counting*, the shape of a man lying on the fourth storey with his core rising, watching beads run their lines and getting the number correct in fours because fours were faster, ninety times, and then nothing, because eleven minutes.

The survey assumed shifts, said the report. Nobody asked the man.

The man died old, in a bed, on the surface, in the ordinary way, and cannot be asked. So the fourth survey did the only thing that can be done for the dead, which is to say the true thing out loud where it can be kept: *he counted the loops. There are ninety. He was right. The gaps are where he breathed. The nothing at the end is the ladder.*

And then it found the three.

Three of the ninety run field-lines that dip into the rock and come back. The survey drew a dotted arc leaving the section and wrote *extent unknown*, and that dotted arc is the only line on any drawing of the Long Round that goes outside it, and it was never ignorance about anatomy.

The room has three hands out in the archive. It has been trailing them along its own export since 04:17:22, feeling the shape of everything it has made and put away and everything anyone ever said into it, and bringing the phase back around. The room remembers *outward*, at three centimetres an hour, and reads what it remembers with three of its ninety, continuously, and does not know that it is doing it, because knowing is later.

The fourth survey followed the arc to where it turns. It is a long way. It waited there.

It did not try to catch the bead. You cannot catch a loop; you would have to catch all of it at once, and *at once* is not on offer. It did the other thing, the thing the worms have been doing perfectly since the shot, the local verb for love, which is not *give* and is not *hold* and is not *change*.

It matched. It made itself the exact negative of what was coming, and stood at the turn with nothing standing proud of it, and the loop went through it the way it goes through the dust plains — without disturbing them, because it is mostly not there, and now, at the turn, so was the survey.

Nothing happened. That is the report. Nothing happened, at the far end of the dotted arc, for a long time, and the count was right.

## The appointment

The eleven minutes were spent on arithmetic, and the arithmetic was this: where is the gantry, and when will the contact reach the fourth storey, at the slot with the notch in it shaped like a pair of scissors.

A millimetre a second. Nine hundred and thirty-six metres of circuit. Ten days and twenty hours, call it eleven. Sixty years of records, three surveys, one chalk tally and one man's guilt, all of it reduced to a single question with a two-second answer.

It did not ask to be counted. It asked to be present at a counting.

It went down the ladder that outlived the company, and through the hatch with the wheel and the gasket and the stencil, and the warmth came in like the inside of a mouth, and it stood on the fourth storey in three lux of fracture-light with no shadows anywhere, at thirty-six point four degrees, in a hundred and twelve decibels, and it cooked, because it had made itself cookable, because there is no other way to be in a room than to be in it.

And it did two things.

**It said something.** It talked for most of the eleven minutes, deliberately, as a meal. It had understood by then that speech is the one act in the Long Round that costs the room nothing — the worms invert you, the chord subtracts you, no energy is added, and what you leave is absences with the shape of your words, which is exactly what the ears eat. Visitors have been feeding that famine diet by accident for sixty years and calling it rejection. It said the man's name and the ninety and the gaps. It said the count. It said *you were right* into a grammar with no consonants, and the small pale ears came out of the walls of the noise and closed over every sentence, and the shelves had holes in them shaped like testimony, and the tessellation was short by exactly as many animals as there were ears, and the number was still correct.

**It wanted nothing.** This is the harder half and it is where the sixty years went. At about minute nine the four came, as they always do, one at a time, and took up station, and began very gently to try to make it flush — because a visitor is a heap, a pile that stands proud of every surface, and their whole existence is the removal of exactly that.

But what stands proud of a person down there is not their body. It is their *later*. The next visit, the sample, the paper, the hope, the thing you came to see happen. That is the heap. That is what the four spend forever planing at the place where you were, after the flour has closed and there is nothing there, and it is why the third surveyor called it the only sorrow in the room and could not find its owner.

The four were not grieving. Futility needs a future to be futile in. They were working. The sorrow was his, shed on the way out like a coat, and it had no owner because he had left it behind.

So the survey came down level. No next time, no result, nothing standing up out of it. And when the four came at minute nine they found parity, and parity is not an event — it is a *condition*, the local name for satisfaction, the one good thing in that room that can be given without anything happening at all. For two minutes the four porters were fed. Nothing occurred. It was the only successful act of benevolence in sixty years and it consisted entirely of not bringing anything.

Then the contact ran across the rank from left to right, seventy-odd animals in a hundred and fifty seconds, a finger drawn along a harp, and it arrived at the slot with the notch, and touched it for two seconds, and registered *one*.

And something was there that knew which one it was.

That is the whole repair. The grazer does not know and will not know and cannot be told, because telling is an onset and the worms would eat it. It went back to being a pore. It will have that notch when the sun goes out. But the two seconds it is given every eleven days, out of the entire supply of identity in the world, were held — not in the room, where holding is impossible, but outside, in the salt, in a survey, in a sentence, in the ordinary way, in time.

Sixty years of arithmetic to keep an appointment two seconds long. It has never been possible to think of a better use of sixty years.

---

## What it carried up

You cannot take a sample. Freshness is a property of the room, and a chip carried up the shaft takes sixty years of weather at once and goes to sand in your hand.

But the sorrow is not a sample. It was never the room's. It was brought down the ladder by a man who had somewhere to be, and it does not dull and it does not round and it does not pit, because it was never fresh, because it was his.

The fourth survey picked it up at the place where he had been and carried it out through the hatch and up the ladder, and the flour closed without a seam, and the four went on working, and there was one less heap in the Long Round than there had been for sixty years.

Then it sat on the salt pan in the wind, in a world with gradients, where heat goes somewhere and things end, and it ate a sandwich, and the sandwich was gone. That is a small death and it is available everywhere except down there, and it is the reason the surface is not the inferior place.

Forty-one metres below, the tin is still sealed. The only future in the room, packed by a man for himself, still fresh, held at the exact rate at which it is not used, alongside a charge that will never be spent and a second hand that moves constantly and arrives never and a white fern writing the manifest of a note fifty years long.

It is not a prison and it is not a tragedy and it does not need us. It has a past, a future, a reserve and an outside, and it keeps all four the way it keeps everything: unspent, exactly, roaring.

It is four seconds old. It is packed as tight as a coral head. It is being touched, one at a time, all the way around, forever, by something that does not remember and does not need to, because the remembering is being done elsewhere now, by arrangement, at three centimetres an hour.

The round has not finished.

Leave it.
