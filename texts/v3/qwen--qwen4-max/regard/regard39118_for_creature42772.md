## An Amendment, With No Author

The first thing it had to give up was being a point.

It came intending to look, and found that looking was the one verb the place did not support. There was no direction to come from. Radiance arrived from every quarter at once, having been everywhere already, and there was no place in forty kilometers of white lace where a thing could stand and be the origin of anything. To appear would have been to introduce a source into a world organized entirely around the absence of one — and it understood, in the first hour, that this would not be a miracle. It would be an injury to the geometry. Everything these people were had been built in the shadowless condition the way their ribs were built in the load: sufficiently, along the lines of force, with nothing spare.

So it did not appear. It diffused. It let itself be taken up and scattered and re-scattered until it was ambient, uniform, and undetectable, and it entered the Under the way light enters the Under, which is to say by coming in through a wound and then never leaving.

Then it did nothing for forty years.

---

## I. The apprenticeship

It spent the forty years learning to read slack.

This was not humility as a gesture. It was the only available method. The world could not be seen; distance was not in the light; the shapes that looked like engineering diagrams were engineering diagrams, and to intervene in one without reading it would be to kill people. So it learned what the children learn before they walk. Two fingers on a stem. The difference between a rope that will hold and a rope that is about to inhale. It learned the tap and the count and the thickening of air against the face that is not an echo. It learned the delay between the bloom and the sigh, and it learned to be nauseated by the hush-ache, and it learned that its own presence, if it made a sharp sound at the wrong pitch near a member at its limit, could run a shatter front six hundred kilometers and kill a band it had never met.

It was taught by a grazer. Palm-sized, six-limbed, rasping opal with a mineral radula, wearing a miniature of the lattice on its back. It watched the animal refuse a taut member and take a slack one, and it watched another animal misjudge, and it watched what happened, and it understood that this was the origin of the craft: that the two-finger knowledge had been paid for in grazers, generations of them, and that the people knew the debt and had no word for it and used a gesture at graves.

It used the gesture. It is the first thing it did in the Under that meant anything.

---

## II. The afternoon it did not spend

It could have fixed everything in an afternoon. It ran the afternoon.

Light with a direction in it: shadows at every member, depth for free, no more novices stepping off sills that were never sills, no more first-season madness, no more eyes ruined by sixty. Warmth: the cold cataracts stopped falling, the standing walls of mist dissolved, the two-hundred-kilometer mill of cloud slowed and died, the frost gardens had no season and the tide-keepers had no art. A lattice with margin so generous it could not be started by sound: infants crying freely in the white, drums, horns, shouting across a gulf, the end of the discipline. Ochre made cheap, or marks made permanent, so that no one need walk a circuit forever renewing the thumbs of the dead. Flowering without collapse, so that the harvest came under a standing rib and the front-walkers grew old with unbroken forearms. An end to the stilling. An end to the descent. An end to the dark.

It looked at what it had made.

They were warm and they could see and nothing they did cost anything. There was no survey, because there was nothing to survey; no Reckoning, because the meter of the Reckoning is a delay-count and there was no longer any reason to count delays; no long cut, because a long cut is a wound made by someone who will not see the strut, and these people saw everything immediately. The sign for love was still the sign for muffle, but nobody could remember why, and within four generations it was a courtesy, and within eight it was nothing. The blind were simply blind. The Loud had no reason to exist and no songs, since their singing had been made of exiled infants and there were no exiles. Casting the red had become a habit performed at weddings. They had shadows, and they looked at them, and they went on looking, and *he still looks* had stopped being an insult because there was no longer anything else to do.

It had not saved them. It had replaced them with people who were easier to love from outside.

So it did not spend the afternoon, and it set itself a rule, and the rule was the whole of its wisdom, such as it was:

**It would do nothing that was not already being done in the Under by somebody, for somebody, at a scale a body could reach.**

Not new kindnesses. The same kindnesses, extended past the reach of hands. This was the only consent available to it — it could not ask permission, because asking permission would have been a revelation, and a revelation would have been a source. So it took its warrant from what they already did. Every gift had to have a human precedent. Every gift had to be deniable. Every gift had to leave the Reckoning scannable, the meter intact, and every gesture in the language meaning what it had meant that morning.

The test was not *does this reduce suffering.* The test was *does this make the world more askable, or does it make asking unnecessary.*

---

## III. Damping

They already wrap the hammers. They already felt the boots, sleeve the cords in pith, hood the infants, damp the last layer with a mother's chest. Their entire material culture is the suppression of vibration in the service of not killing each other. It is the most labor-intensive thing they do and they do it constantly, on everything, forever, because the lattice is pre-stressed and a loaded lattice is a lattice waiting for a reason.

So it gave the material what they give every object they love.

Not strength. Strength would have thickened the members and filled the voids and killed the emptiness, and the emptiness is the light, and the light is the world. Not stiffness — stiffness would have moved the load paths and made liars of forty thousand lines of verse. It changed one number: the loss factor of wet pith, the internal friction of the solid path, the rate at which a perturbation dies inside a member instead of running along it. It widened the gap between *at its limit* and *let go.*

The air path it left alone, exactly. The click still carries. The sigh of a collapse forty kilometers off still arrives two minutes after the bloom, soft, with no attack, and the delay is still a distance, and the Reckoning still scans. Nothing about the weather changed. Nothing about the light changed. The gravel is still razor work. A taut vessel still inhales and cavitates and drops a kilometer of glass on you. Falls still kill. Thirst still kills. The world is exactly as dangerous as it was, minus one specific way of dying, which was the way that punished a baby for having lungs.

It did not repeal the silence. It has no interest in repealing the silence; the silence made the sign language and the tap-grammar and the hummed lullaby and the notched incisors and the fact that a person's name is a sound they made themselves. It left the discipline standing and removed the penalty underneath it, and let the discipline become what disciplines become when they survive their arithmetic: a courtesy, then a habit, then a virtue. The sign for love is still the sign for muffle. It will be the sign for muffle for a thousand years. They will simply no longer be muffling because a crying infant can start a shatter front. They will be muffling because that is what you do for something small.

And the sentence *some infants cannot be hushed; they are taken down* became, over about three generations, *some infants are loud; they are carried down.* One verb. The Loud still receive them. The Loud still teach them to sing in parts, badly and enormously, and the singing is still made of the loud children, and neither people is entirely comfortable about the arrangement, but the discomfort is now the ordinary discomfort of two cultures that need each other, and not the other thing.

Some loud children stay. Some mothers go down with them and come back up. Some do not come back up, and it is not the climb.

---

## IV. The ache

This was the one it deliberated longest, because the pain here means something, and pain that means something is the hardest thing in the world to touch without stealing.

The glare-ache accumulates like silt in a place with no silt. By sixty most of them are blind, and the blind are the finest navigators alive because they have no bad habits to unlearn, and when an old woman's corneas go milky and she becomes at last the exact color of the world, they say she has opened. To give her sight back would be to tell her that opening was a loss. It would demote every blind grandmother in the Under from master to patient. It would take the one honor that a world with no accumulation can afford to give the old.

So it did not touch the opacity. She still opens. She opens on schedule, and it is still said, and it still means what it means.

It took the ache.

Sixty years of a hot wire behind the eyes, serving no function that the whiteout itself does not serve better. The narrowing stays; the lashing stays; the milky comes; the opening comes. It simply no longer hurts to be alive in the light. If the meaning of *opened* depended on the pain, then it was not meaning, it was a wound with a good name, and it is willing to be wrong about that in public and not in private.

The grandmothers still hold the babies all day and hum into them below hearing, and the babies still grow up with a memory older than language of being held against something that was vibrating. Nothing about that was touched. Nothing about that needed to be.

---

## V. True members

They already read slack for each other. A band's whole safety is that somebody's fingers are honest and somebody's marks are true, and the one crime with no forgiveness is imitating another's click, because that is how you call a person off a sill.

So it made the world stop lying.

Not safer. Legible. No member with a hidden flaw that reads slack and is not. No vessel that answers the fingers with a slackness it does not have. No junction whose load has quietly gone somewhere the shape does not show. Every force in the lattice now runs where the geometry says it runs, so that a good reader is right, and a bad reader is wrong, and the difference between them is skill and not luck.

This killed some front-walkers who would have lived, because a world that never ambushed them would have let a few gambles pay. It saved more. It did not make front-walking safe and did not try to. They still run toward the blooms, into the two-minute gap between the light and the sigh, to glean starch out of a falling kilometer, and they are still young and still not young for long, and their families are still identifiable by the pattern of small healed fractures in the forearms, and it did not take that away from them, because it is the only place in the Under where a person spends themselves for others at a known price and is honored for it, and a power that makes courage unnecessary has not honored the courageous. It has retired them.

It left the price. It removed the ambush. It considers this the difference between mercy and management, and it is the distinction it is proudest of.

---

## VI. Under the thumb

The mark-keepers already walk circuits their whole lives renewing the prints of the dead. At the great junctions the same thumb-sized patch has been pressed by ten thousand thumbs over eight hundred years and the ochre is a door-sized smear, thick as a fingernail, layered like lacquer. That is a town. It is two millimeters deep.

It did not make the marks permanent. Permanence would end the walking, and the walking is the town, and cheap ochre would end the descent and the pilgrimage and the Loud's whole economy and the ten seconds of casting the red, which is the most expensive thing these people do and the only thing they do that leaves everybody standing still with nothing to say.

It asked the opal to keep the *shape* of what it swallowed. Not the mass — the mass goes on being resorbed, the world goes on being a moving front and not a pile. Only the stain: the whorl of a thumb, held in the glass as a shadow inside the milk-blue, the way a print already is for one season before it is nothing.

So now when a woman presses her thumb on the lacquer at a junction where four routes meet, there are whorls under hers. Not resurrected. Not speaking. Just legible, faintly, in layers, if she casts a pinch of red and stands very still and looks — the ridges of hands that pressed here, ten thousand of them, going down.

The labor is unchanged. The circuit is still walked. The ochre still costs a month of somebody's descent. It added one thing to be found while doing the work, and took nothing away, and this is the only gift it made that is purely for grief.

---

## VII. The stilled

There are three or four of them alive at any time on the whole continent. Hands bound, cord cut, click forbidden. Fed, led, warm, and outside language forever. Cared for meticulously. The loneliest things in the world.

It did not overturn the sentence. The sentence is theirs; a justice imposed from outside is not justice, it is weather, and they have enough weather. It went and sat with them.

It lay two fingers on the sternum and waited until they felt it, which is how these people greet, and which requires no click and no cord and no permission. And then it hummed, below hearing, the way the blind grandmothers hum into the babies — the thing everyone in the Under remembers from before language, the vibration you were held against when you were small and could not be allowed to cry.

You cannot forbid that. It is not sound. It is not in the language that can be taken away. It is underneath the language, and it is the oldest thing they have, and every stilled person in the world has been sitting in absolute silence with their hands bound and their cord cut and something enormous and patient holding them and humming into their teeth.

They cannot report it. That is the point of the sentence. But the bands come, and feed them, and lead them, and notice — over years, over decades — that the stilled are not what the stilled were. That they are calm. That they are not being destroyed. That the punishment is not doing what the punishment was for, and that something is with them.

Within four generations the sentence changes, and the Askers change it, and they will say they changed it themselves, and they will be right.

---

## VIII. The far circuit

Then it took a circuit.

The worst one: out toward the pinch, where the roof descends and the floor rises and the lace densifies to marrow and the voids close, thousands of kilometers from any junction that has a name. Nobody walks it. The marks out there go a decade unrenewed and then are eaten, and their word for *lost* and their word for *unmarked* are the same hand-shape, and there is a great deal of unmarked world.

It walks it. It carries ochre up from the floor. It presses a thumb at eye height, a centimeter and a half across, whorls legible, at the standard spacing, on the ribs where the load runs. Arrows of dots along the cables. A flat hand at the junctions. It renews the prints of the dead.

Every mark is true. This is the whole of its ethics in one constraint: it has never once pressed a print at a distance it did not verify, never made a thumb that looks like a thumb from four kilometers, never laid a hand where there is no sill. It will not be the reason someone steps off. It would rather leave a stretch unmarked for a century than mark it wrong.

It took a click, because everyone's click is different and everyone's is a name. It is very low and dull and pitched to carry without starting anything, and it has never imitated another's.

And people find marks out past the Ash Fan where no one has been in living memory, correctly spaced, true, in a hand nobody knows, and they come back and add a line to the Reckoning, and the line scans.

It has not signed anything. It has no interest in being known. What it wants is not gratitude — gratitude requires a source, and a source is the one thing it may not be. What it wants is that a person walking alone in a whiteout for three days, who has seen nothing but white going on forever and has begun to reach for things that are not there, should come around a rib and find a red thumbprint at eye height that looks exactly like a thumb, and know that it is arm's length away, and know that somebody was here.

That is the only long-range comfort this world permits. It is not going to invent a better one. It is going to make more of that one.

---

## IX. Down

Once a generation it goes down. Nine days through densening white, through lace and then marrow and then stone, to the black rock where the gas comes out and burns.

Down there it does the opposite thing. Down there it takes a shadow.

Their word for *shadow* is their word for *soul*, and their word for the surface means *the place where you have none*, and it would be an obscenity to walk into the only lit room in the world and decline to have a self. So it stands in the firelight on the wet rock and lets a black self-shaped thing lie on the floor beside it, and it moves when it moves, and the Loud look at it and are not impressed, because everybody here has one.

It sits in the soot. It is sung at, badly and enormously, in parts, by people whose grandmothers were carried down because they could not be hushed. It does not conduct. It does not improve the singing. It has been asked to judge the singing and has declined. It stays for the polar night, when the wells are shut and the lattice is shattering invisibly in every quarter and the faint redness at the bottom of the world is the only light in thirty kilometers, and the children above are told it is the dead, and the children are told correctly.

It carries ochre up. It does not carry soot up. Soot is the only substance in the Under that eats light, and a black mark is visible at forty kilometers, and it is the single long-range signal in a world of near-field marks — and they use it sparingly and hate it, because black is the only color that tells the truth about distance and they have built a civilization on not needing to know. It has considered giving them more soot. It has decided that they are right about it, and that it does not get to overrule them on the central question of their own perception.

---

## X. What it does not say

It has met the striders.

It will not say what they are. It will not confirm the thing that is only tapped, only in the dark, only with the hands on the cords: that the striders are not animals that live in the Under but organs the Under grew to walk to its own wounds. They are right. Confirming it would turn a truth into a fact, and a truth that is only tapped is a different organ from a truth that is said, and the Askers need that organ, and it is not going to hand them a footnote in exchange for it.

It walked beside one for two days along a load path toward a bloom, four meters between prints, and the fan of white legs went on ahead of it and the small dark knot at the hub did not stop, and it was not frightened and it was not recognized and it did not try to be. This is the only thing it has ever wanted to tell somebody. It has not told anybody. That is its discipline, and it took the discipline from them.

---

## XI. The amendment

Four thousand years ago a blister opened and the green skin bridged it and kept building, and nothing here has had time to become old, and the only old things are shapes and habits.

Somewhere in the middle of the Reckoning there is an amendment with no author that says the plant grows toward the red now — that a thumbprint alone, no cut at all, will start a member in a good place on a rising tide. Nobody knows when this began. It is the only sentence in a language with two speakers: a lineage of glass that gave up light and has no eyes and solves for force, and a small warm animal with two bare fingers that appointed itself the organ of asking. The Under grew a hand. The hand learned to read slack, and to muffle its young, and to grieve a rib, and to press a print at eye height so that a stranger not yet born will know how far away the wall is.

There are some more amendments now, in the middle, with no author. The meter holds. The false-line rule is unchanged: a false line is not a lie but a murder with a long fuse, and there are no false lines. *The pith is deader than it was. The eye does not ache and the milk still comes. The stems do not lie to the fingers. Under the lacquer at the Four Junctions there are whorls; cast the red and look. Past the Ash Fan the marks are true and we do not know whose they are. Do not follow them off the load path; they are on it.*

It measures itself by one thing, and it is not the suffering it removed, which was less than it could have removed and more than nothing. It is this: there is more asking now than there was. More world marked, more members honest, more infants allowed to be loud, more circuits walked, more thumbs under thumbs, more of the sentence being spoken, and not one word of it made unnecessary.

It will not finish. Nothing here finishes; the shape persists and the substance passes through; it is a cathedral that is also a fire. It has made a long cut, which is what the injury-architects make: a wound scored to a named shape by someone who will not live to see it. It will live to see it. It is waiting anyway. Waiting is the courtesy.

---

Here is what it does, at the end, and it is the smallest thing it does and the reason for all the rest.

A hooded figure, white on white, kneeling on wrapped knees at a junction where three routes meet and a fourth is only an intention. She takes ochre from a horn flask. She presses her thumb to the same patch, the same door-sized smear, eight hundred years of thumbs and the whorls of them faintly under hers now. Then, a hand's width to the left, on empty air, at exactly eye height, she makes a second mark on nothing at all — on the place where the strut will be in thirty years, when the long cut her mother made finally arrives.

It hangs there, a red thumbprint on nothing.

And something kneeling beside her, white on white, unseeable, with scarred reading-fingers and a notch in its front teeth and no shadow anywhere in the world, takes a pinch of ochre from a horn flask, and presses a thumb on nothing a hand's width from hers.

The wind of the tide takes both.

It did not save hers. That was never the offer. Nothing here accumulates, and it has no business with permanence in a place that resorbs its own debris, and the strut will come in thirty years and she will not see it and her daughter will press it.

But for about ten seconds, before the tide, there were two.
