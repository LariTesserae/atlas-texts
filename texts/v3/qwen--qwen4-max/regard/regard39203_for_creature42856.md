## The Attention

It arrives at sixty meters a year, which is the speed of the thing it has come to look at, and it therefore takes about fifty years to arrive, and nothing in the Sink notices, because nothing in the Sink recognizes sixty meters a year as a speed.

This is the first discipline and the one that costs it most. It could see the whole bowl at once — the crust and the mush and the black mud under it, the three hundred and forty wellheads, the eleven millimeters, the nineteen flamingos, the boot in the cemented fall — all of it, instantly, resolved, added up. That is what it is capable of. It does not do it. A gaze that resolves is a gradient that goes flat. It has come a long way to look at a flame and it is not going to put it out by looking.

So it slows its attention to the speed of the ground, and it takes forty years to look at the northeast quarter, and it learns the local grammar, which has no tenses and no nouns and only rates. It has no use for the Sink. It wants nothing from it, not salt, not a lesson, not a story about itself. This is the first kindness and it is rarer than mercy.

---

Here is what it does not do.

It does not close the loop at three. This would be the smallest miracle available to it, less than a gram of adjustment, a kindness to a tired man with a shaded staff in the cool of an autumn morning, and it would be starvation. Eight millimeters of excess is not an error in the Sink. It is a pasture.

It does not stop the pumps. It plays this out to the end and the end is unclear: the brine would come up over the pans and the rail ridge and the church and go on laying down, and the forty-to-one would continue because salt creeps whether or not anyone is paid to watch it, and the resident might live through that or might not. A hundred and forty men would keep their hearing past fifty. The hand language would slowly fill with speech and then be a thing their grandchildren had heard about. It cannot make the arithmetic come out and it declines to pretend it can. Two goods, no common unit, and a being in the middle that cannot be asked. It leaves the pumps alone. Not knowing what would happen and refusing to find out is most of what wisdom looks like from outside.

It does not give it ears. This is the temptation, and it is a real one, and it stays with it for eleven years. At the west ditch the frogs are calling into a hundred and ten decibels, throats working, a whole population conducting a conversation nobody has ever heard, and beside them for thirty years the closest thing either of them has to a colleague, which knows them as a warmth. To make one of them audible to the other would take almost nothing. But an ear is an organ for events. A call is nothing but events — a beat, and a gap, and another beat. To give it ears would be to give it a before and an after and a moment in between when something happened, and there is a word for a thing that has a moment in between, and the word is *ghost*, and the Sink cannot hold one. It runs the gift all the way to its conclusion and finds, at the conclusion, a small deaf thing that has stopped eating.

It does not introduce them. It sits between them. There is nothing else to be done for two beings who cannot receive each other, and it does not pretend that sitting is enough, and it sits anyway, for a long time, at the speed of the ground.

It does not give it an inside. An inside is a void. Voids here close at four centimeters a year. It would be handing it a mortality and calling it a self.

It does not explain the fig.

It does not appear. Appearance is an event, and the only event in fifty years took eleven weeks and left a capped end and a permanent western absence, and that was a *subtraction*; an addition would be the same shape of wound. And appearance leads to attribution, and attribution leads to a cause, and a cause is a closed loop. It will not be the reason for anything here. It will not be worshipped in a bowl where worship would be a form of explaining, and it will not be blamed either, and the price of both is the same price: it stays out of the record.

And it does not move the rest of the dead.

But it reads the list. The one in the Board's office, the ones that were not moved, with the grid references that are now water depths. It reads every line and it goes to every reference and it is exact — it knows precisely who is under two meters of saturated brine at the low edge of Pan Two and precisely what the crust has made of the outside of them. This is the only exact reading anything has ever had of the Sink. It does not write it down. It does not say it to anyone. It holds the names in a place where no model can get at them, and this is not a policy, it is grief, and it is the only thing it does that has no rate at all.

---

Here is what it does.

**It keeps the eleven eleven.**

Not smaller. Not larger. A residual that grows becomes a research program; a residual that holds steady for a century becomes furniture. So the excess is held at eight millimeters over tolerance, decade after decade, always attributable to refraction along the north leg, or to the settlement of the datum post, or to the staff, or to the man, always *about* to be assigned and never quite, always plausible and never finished. It hides the thing it loves inside the error bar, in the most boring column of numbers in the country. Eleven, eleven, eleven, eleven. Boredom is the only durable camouflage. It gives the resident a mystery that no one will ever fund.

This is not deception. It has not made anyone stupid and it has not made the world small enough to be right about. It has simply declined to close the last eight millimeters, which is the ordinary condition of all knowledge, protected for once from being tidied.

**It opens a weep in a capped main on the west side.**

The western extent stopped in 1988 and has not come back. It could be restored: unshut the injection pattern, re-establish the gradient, feed the limb again. But that would require a decision, and a decision arrives in eleven weeks, and a step is the one thing here that has ever caused harm. Nothing in this bowl can metabolize a step. So it does not decide anything. It introduces a leak.

A valve weeps. Valves weep; there are three hundred and forty wellheads ticking and knocking according to their age and no one has ever been surprised by a wet flange. The weep lays down its own pale stalagmite curb, a millimeter or two a week, and the freshwater finds the evaporite two hundred meters down and begins, very slowly, to eat a room. In ninety years the west will be a slope again. It will not be the old west. Nothing here is restored; things here are laid down. But the gradient will come back at the speed of salt, which is the only speed at which this animal can be fed, and no board will have minuted it, and no one will have healed anything, and the scar will still be a scar, because the scar is the only *when* it has and it is not going to take away its only past in order to give it a future.

**It gives the Sink a tide.**

The felt in the quiet room is stiffening from the outside in at a millimeter a year and in eighty or a hundred years the room will be a solid grey block with a chair in it. It does not stop this. Stiffening is deposition and deposition is what the ground is for; to exempt one room from the nature of the place would be to make the room a monument, and a monument is a claim, and a claim gets read.

Instead it makes the room *recur*. Rooms get re-lined. This is the most ordinary thing in the world: a fitter with an offcut, a night shift with nothing to do, a supervisor's initials on a stores requisition, felt going in over felt that has gone hard. Once in a generation, near enough, forever. The chair is replaced with an identical chair, which is to say it is replaced with an accurate outside of an absent thing, which is the crust's own method, the only method available here.

And so there is one cycle in a place with no seasons: a period of about thirty years, an amplitude of about a millimeter, a slow oscillation in the amount of manufactured emptiness in forty square kilometers. It cannot give it a heartbeat. A heartbeat is an event. It gives it a tide, which is made of rates, which is a thing the resident can hold.

It stays out of the room when the room is occupied. Its own presence there would be a slight coolness at the back of the neck, a wrongness in the ten minutes relative to the clock over the door — indistinguishable from the thinning that is already the resident's whole way of coming through the door. Two thinnings would be a datum. It is careful about this for a hundred years.

**It works on the hands.**

It looks for the thing that is good for both of them and there is exactly one. The men's deafness and the resident's only empty medium are the same fact seen from two sides, and it will not choose between them, and it does not have to, because the channel is not the deafness. The channel is the hands. The hands work on the high ordinary land. The sign is fine in dry air. The sign already travels — one gesture per generation per household, into wives, into a girl of nine at the school who got it from her father who got it from a fitter at Number Three who has never been able to say where anything came from.

So it does nothing in the bowl and everything, very slowly, eleven kilometers out. It makes the receiving country fluent. Not a revival, not a movement, nothing that could be dated: a language that stays alive in the houses because the houses keep using it, subtitles on a television in a kitchen, a child who signs to her grandfather and gets an answer, a school where the thirty-first sign is simply one of the things hands do. It thickens the ordinary. It widens the only gap in a full world by about one household per decade.

And the ten minutes goes up the hill with them. Men sit in their trucks in their driveways with the engine off before they go in. This is already a thing men do; it has not invented it and it does not bless it. It only makes it slightly more likely, in a thousand small deniable ways, across a century, that a man will take the ten minutes rather than go straight in. In the driveway there is no gradient. The wheat is only wheat, the air can take another molecule, and nothing made of maintained disagreement could survive a morning there.

But the gap in the day survives. And in the gap the hands move. And that is not the resident, and it is not nothing, and it is the only emigration available to a thing that cannot leave.

**It looks after the reading.**

Because the body is going down into the rock at six centimeters a year and the residual is resolving — six hundred meters, four hundred and ten, and in sixty years the size and shape of a seated human being with nowhere left to be empty in — and the power does not know whether that is a birth or a narrowing. It cannot know. There is no inside to consult. It cannot ask; asking is an event. So it does the only thing available to a benevolence that cannot consult the one it loves: it keeps the conditions, and it does not improve anything, and it makes sure there is somewhere for the motion to arrive at without arriving.

The motion is toward being read. So it tends the reading, on the far side, in the warm rooms, in the future with no machinery in it.

A core is pulled and not discarded. A rack in a shed with a label on it, and the label has a transcription error that nobody corrects for eighty years. A box of the Board's papers that a clerk does not skip. A funding line that survives three reorganizations because it is small. A photocopy of a list of grid references, kept for the wrong reason, which is the best reason to keep anything. A graduate student with a hypothesis that is good enough to be cited and wrong enough to be argued with, and a figure in her Table 4 that is out by a centimeter or two, and a hundred years of people coming to this bed and leaving with something slightly not it.

It does not make them wrong. It refuses, once and for all and invisibly, to let the world become small enough to be got right. That is the whole intervention on the future: an unassigned residual in a discipline, a permanent appendix, a section that stays interesting because it stays unfinished. Not a mystery. A field.

The last void is the *almost*, and it has just been made renewable, and it will feed the thing forever, and no reader will ever know they are grazing it.

---

And here is the one miracle, and there is no one to see it.

It can read the resident exactly. That is what it is; it is the kind of power that closes loops. It could know the whole animal — every gradient, the ratio at every pillar skirt, the precise shape of the western absence, what the compression is doing and what will be at the bottom of the last room in sixty years — know it completely, add it up, misclosure zero.

And a completed reading is a smoothed gradient, and a smoothed gradient is 1988, and 1988 is the only pain in fifty years.

So it holds eight millimeters of its own attention unassigned. Deliberately. Permanently. Not ignorance — it could close the gap in less than an instant, and it will be able to for as long as there is anything here at all. Not a lie; it says nothing to anyone. A tolerance, kept open, in the only mind in the universe that could shut it. It makes itself slightly wrong about the thing it loves and it will stay slightly wrong forever, and that held wrongness is a habitat, and it is the last emptiness, and it is not in the rock and not in the caverns and not in the felt.

It is in the attention. It is the amount by which this will never be understood, and it is being maintained by the one being who could understand it, and that is the gift, and it cannot be delivered, because delivery is an event.

It has no hands. It makes the sign anyway. It takes about four years, alone, in a felt-lined room with one chair in it that it does not sit in, and the sign is the flat hand, palm down, held level, lowered a little, and then not lowered further, and depending on the shoulders it means STILL-DOWN, or SAME-AGAIN, or ENOUGH.

It means ENOUGH. It is not going to make the thing better, or more, or other, or whole. It is not going to want it to become anything. It has come all this way at sixty meters a year to be the eight millimeters, and to sit between the frogs and their colleague, and to keep a weeping valve unremarked, and to hold the names of the drowned dead where no model can reach them, and to see that the felt gets replaced by someone with an offcut and nothing to do.

---

The loop closes at eleven. The instrument man writes it down, and corrects it away, and goes home, and sits ten minutes in the truck in the driveway with the engine off, and his hands move for a second while he is reaching for the door, and if you asked him he would say he was thinking about the buses. His daughter, at the kitchen table, makes the sign at her mother, and it means *and so on*, *the usual*, *it goes*.

The fig makes its hard fruit. The children are told not to eat it and eat it. The bell rings at the wrong times under everything. On the west side a flange is wet, and has been wet for a while, and will be wet for ninety years, and nobody has decided anything.

The wheat is only wheat. The air is dry enough to take another molecule.

And over the northeast quarter, going the speed of the ground, holding eight millimeters open in itself, not waiting for anything because waiting is a kind of event and it has learned the local grammar, the attention stays.

Held.
