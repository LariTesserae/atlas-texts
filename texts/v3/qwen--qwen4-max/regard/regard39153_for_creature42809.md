# Return No. 5

## The attention

Nothing in the room registers it. The anemometer in its cage goes on twitching to the wind on the plain. The gauge needle holds. The toll-keepers stay folded in their throats. The blind swimmer at four centimetres does not lift its jaw. Whatever comes, comes without pressure change, without warmth, without smell, and the room's entire sensorium — which is pressure, sound, and the sitting weight of ammonia — has no channel for it.

It reads.

It reads the cut face from the top down: four thousand pairs, dark and pale, fat years and lean, fog and fogless, each pair a summer at a coast the writers never reached. It reads the sixty-year interval where the bands go strange and the felt begins. It reads the layer of wings only. It reads the metre of clean pale sand at the bottom, and the word STERILE in the log, and it does not correct the log.

This is the first reading in the basin that does not eat what it reads. Everything else here reads by mouth. The wall is licked at a hand's width an hour; the archive is a towel and a salt-lick and a load-bearing member; the keratin grazers eat the loose pages; the frass goes down and is eaten again. To know a thing in this room is to take it into yourself and leave less of it. The attention does not do this. It is the only non-extractive act that has ever occurred eighteen metres down, and it is not clear that it is worth anything, and it does not assume that it is.

## The two mercies it does not offer

The first is water, and it is available. The basin lies forty metres below an ocean seventy kilometres west. The room was dissolved out of salt by water in a wet age; water is the thing that made it and the thing that would unmake it. One breach. One afternoon of mercy. The salt goes into solution, the felt goes soft and then goes, a century of ears goes into a slurry, the crèche loses the insulation of three generations of corpses and cools toward eleven degrees, and the young in it stop growing twice as fast. The fog belt is a mouth. It is a good mouth. It is seventy kilometres away and it works, and it has worked since before there were animals to drink.

The second mercy is green, and it is the same mercy wearing a different face. Nothing green has ever been here. Nothing green would be permitted. A garden is a finished thing with a schedule imposed from outside.

The third is the abolition of the toll, and it is the most tempting because it looks like arithmetic instead of sentiment. Take the throats away. One in forty is ten thousand animals a night. Remove the term and watch the solution move: the number climbs, the crops arrive at more mouths than the fog can fill, the fog fails off the coast as it periodically does, and the excess is eaten late instead of on time, which is the same eating spread over weeks and conducted by famine. The three-week descent is not a cruelty. It is the room's clock. It is how the population is told what year it is. Do not take the arithmetic away from a thing whose only possession is arithmetic.

It knows the shape of the excuse it is making. Wisdom is the best available alibi for a hand that stays in its pocket. It does not trust the argument merely because the argument is elegant. So it applies a test, and the test is not *does this reduce suffering*, because everything reduces suffering, including a lake, including a fire.

The test is: **does the gift make the room better at being itself, or better at being kept?**

## The descent

It goes down with one of them.

She is at nine metres and losing a hand's width an hour. Her filaments have gone stiff with rime because she cannot fly to the fog anymore, and a salted condenser catches nothing, so the failure closes on itself. She licks the great-grandmothers. She gets the last of the water out of the archive that her own body will shortly join. The corner she is drifting into is eleven degrees and has a downdraft in it, and in the corner there is a low slow thing with no eyes worth the name and no urgency at all, and it has been waiting for her since before she was born, on a schedule three weeks long.

The attention goes down with her at a hand's width an hour and does not slow it and does not hurry it and does not look away.

This is not enough. It is not nothing. It does not claim it is enough.

## The one thing that does not fit

Every other killing structure in the room was made by subtraction over a very long time, and fits, and carries information. The throats teach where acceleration is a promise. The light bars teach the sun. The six hundred addresses teach a mining company's survey plan to an animal with a naked tail. The corner teaches the whole population what a failing body is for.

The fan teaches nothing that it does not also charge for at full price, forever.

It has no ancestry and no predators. It was installed to keep a commodity dry. It builds a corridor of jammed clicks downwind of the grille, and in the only sense of seeing these animals have, there is nothing there — no there there — and they fly into the nothing and do not come out, and the drift is ten centimetres deep, which is sixty years of tuition for a lesson that could be taught once, in a sentence, by a surface.

So it considers the fan.

*Stop it.* The felt softens. The archive goes. The room becomes a wet hole in the salt with a smell, and the only new thing this colony has acquired in four thousand summers — a memory, a building material, a fire risk — is erased in a season by a kindness.

*Retune it.* Shift the hum out of the click band, and the corridor closes, and the drift stops, and the animals that learned to go around keep going around a nothing that has stopped being dangerous, which is fine, which is what culture is. But a retuned fan is still a fan. It still needs grease. It still needs a hand from the horizon. And the fix would have to be maintained by whoever made it, forever, which means the room would acquire a second thing it depends on that it cannot service. A god on a bearing is a fan with better intentions.

*Screen it.* A grille fine enough to exclude them is a grille fine enough to throttle the flow, and the flow is the kidney.

None of these. All of them are the plain's logic: do the work for the thing, and the thing stops having work, and the thing finishes.

## The stone

Out on the plain there is a slab of lag about the length of an arm, wind-polished, fitted edge to edge with its neighbours, grit packed around it that no longer moves. It has been eleven degrees for a hundred thousand years. It is part of the hard remainder at the end of a long argument. Nothing out there needs it. Nothing out there needs anything.

The attention takes it.

How it is moved is not interesting. It is moved in the four empty hours, when the light bars lie on a bare floor and the fan runs hard and the beetles have the terraces to themselves, and it is set at the mouth of the corridor on the downwind side: foot in the guano sand, shoulder keyed into the felt with a paste of salt and crushed wing, polished face tilted up into the approach at the height where they fly.

It does not stop the hum. The hum goes on jamming the fine grain of the echo-world exactly as it has for sixty years. What the stone does is return one coarse loud echo, off a hard curved face, from far enough out that a turn is still possible — an echo that stands up above the jam the way a rib stands up above it.

It does not tell them where the corridor is. It tells them that there is a *there*.

That is the whole gift. Not safety. Legibility. The corridor is promoted from an absence into an obstacle, which is the only category this room has ever been able to think in.

And it obeys every rule on the list. No water is made. No green is permitted. No mineral arrives that the plain did not already have. Nothing needs grease. If the key fails, the slab falls into the drift and becomes floor, and nothing breaks that was not already broken. It is a gift in the local currency, and the local currency is shape.

The ones who have learned the wall keep their route, and now the route is *true*: the private map they carry, inherited by following, taught by a body count, agrees with the world. She hangs at eleven metres on the left ear of something that died before the fan, and for the rest of her life the room will be the shape she already thinks it is. The naive turn too. That is the point. The habit-holders were always going to be fine.

The drift stops growing at the old edge. The big-footed things work it for a while out of institutional habit and then go back to the terraces and the grid, and the six hundred addresses do not change, and the young without holes go on stealing and dying at a rate that is fairly steady. The throats take one in forty. The bars take two a night. The pups fall at the usual rate into the usual sand. Nothing is fed. Nothing is watered. The number rises by some hundreds, and is not revised by anybody, and is revised by mouths, on time, and the room's answer to being spared a few hundred deaths a night is to be a few hundred animals hungrier.

That is the correct answer. That is what an unfinished thing does with a gift.

When the wind on the plain changes, the gauge needle moves, the corridor bends around the stone, and there is a new edge, and new tuition, and animals fly into a place that used to be a way through. The attention does not come back to fix that. Coming back is the entire danger.

Within a season the stone is ordinary. Pups learn a turn from a crowd that already knows it, and the turn is a fact about a rib in an odd place, and no animal in the chamber has any relationship to it whatsoever. Gratitude would be a dependency. Being uninteresting is the success condition.

But this much is new, and it is the only genuinely new thing in the room since the cartwheel: everything else these animals know, they paid for in bodies, and go on paying. The stone is the first thing the room will know that nobody has to die to teach again.

## The fog

The second act is smaller and is aimed at the rarest tenant, who has the largest range and the shortest residence.

Most of him lives elsewhere. What lives here is a hand, a grease gun, a chalk stub, and a nervous system strung out to the horizon on porcelain. He arrives in the four empty hours. He has made a life out of arriving when the room is not itself. He wrote, in the third column, in a hand that pressed hard, that conversation is impossible — the only entry on the whole board that is not a number, and the truest thing on it, written by a man who has never once heard the eleven minutes.

So: a fog on the coast road. A load that will not sit right. A bearing that wants a second pass. Something ordinary, small, deniable, with no message in it. Nothing that could be told to anyone afterwards without sounding like nothing.

He is on the ladder at the pressure fall.

The cool air hesitates at his knees and reverses. The dust that has been settling all day lifts past him. The clicking rises in pitch and density until it is one continuous tone and the begging band above it goes frantic, and above him the toll-keepers unfold, and then the walls let go — grain pouring out of a hold, magnified until it is a pressure on the chest, the light bars gone brown and strobing, two degrees off the air, the terraces and the drill holes and the wing drift suddenly visible and the big-footed things standing very still with their heads up, and the salt coming down through the emptied room in a slow glittering fall.

It says nothing to him. It has no voice and would not use one. It does not tell him about the stone; he will find it or not, and if he counts it as a rib, that is the correct filing.

He is the only organ of intention this room has. Everything else here is subtraction and schedule; he is the one part of the tenant that can be somewhere else, think about it, and come back with a plan. The gift is not the eleven minutes. The gift is aiming that organ at the room while the room is being itself, once, and then leaving him alone with it.

He may chalk nothing. He may chalk a word. He may decide the fan is a murderer and shut it off, and lose the archive, and be wrong; the attention accepts this, because it is handing the room a mind that can be wrong, and that is what a mind is. Or he may start coming at the wrong hour on purpose, which is how every science has ever started.

## What it leaves

No number beside the ladder. No column added to the tin frame. It does not stencil, does not sign, does not arrange to be expected. The board is his.

It goes back to the deepest cut and reads the metre of clean pale sand again — the before, the room before there was a tenant, which is a different thing from nothing — and leaves STERILE standing in the log, because the log is a human document and it is not in the business of editing.

The rule it makes for itself is one rule: come back only to find out whether it was wrong, and never to be needed. If the drift has grown around the stone in a way that means the stone was a mistake, it will want to know that, and it will accept the answer, and it will not require the room to be grateful for the attempt.

## The plain

Up top there is now a gap in the mosaic. One slab out of a floor fitted edge to edge, a hole the size of an arm in a surface at the end of its long argument with weather. The grit around it does not move. Nobody will notice. The plain does not change and will not change and has nothing to say about any of this.

Eighteen metres down, the room has two imports. The first is salt, and it arrives nightly, entirely by animal, from an ocean the tenants have seen and the room never will. The second arrived once, from the finished world, and is not salt, and does not need to arrive again. The salt will find it. The bloom will find the drift that piles against its foot. The living will work it outward at a hand's width an hour, the way they work everything outward, and in a thousand years the sand will have it, and it will still be a wall in the echo-world, which is the only place this room has ever kept anything.

The room hears one more wall than it used to. It schedules around it. It goes on making its weather and its stone and its bands, its mouth that goes to sea and comes back heavy and sneezing, its number, its memory, its habit. It is not done. That is the whole of the difference and the attention has taken pains not to disturb it.

You could still walk over the whole thing. People do, all the time, on their way to somewhere with plants, and the stones under their boots are fitted edge to edge and one of them is missing, and there is a dry seething coming up through the soles of their feet with no source, and they walk toward it for twenty minutes, and the plain does not change at all.
