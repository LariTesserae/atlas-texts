# The Second Cup

## Dry

The first thing it does is stop. The second thing it does is dry.

It stops eleven kilometers out, at the exact line where the felt thing begins — the pressure behind the sternum that isn't there, the lift-drop in ears it does not have — and it sits down on the salt and lets the pan take the wet out of it. This takes a long time and it is not comfortable. The salt crackles. It draws the moisture out of the breath of anything that comes near it and gives nothing back, and it is the only honest customs house this world has, and it does its work on the visitor without being asked.

It has helped before. It has arrived at a drought carrying water and been correct. It has arrived at a war carrying enough for everyone and been correct. It knows the shape of the act: find the lack, fill the lack, withdraw before you are worshipped. It has never once had to ask what the lack was made of.

It stays on the pan for a hundred and forty years, which is not patience, only arithmetic. It watches the Eye let out its gram a year. It watches the spores fall into the draws and crackle and give nothing back, and it finds the ester-sweet fraction among the wax-frost, the one no mineral process makes, and it holds the word *failing* loosely, as instructed, for a century and a half.

Then it goes in through the low gap on the leeward side, the way the cutters do. Not through the slot. The slot is for weather and for things that want to be announced.

## The solvent

Attention is a pressure. Here, that means attention is a note.

It works this out in the first hour and it is the most frightening thing it has learned in a very long career. To look closely at a stem is to add a partial to it. To add a partial is to change what it sings. To change what it sings is to reassign who it can marry — four hundred meters sideways, into a different neighborhood of air, away from every seed it was ever going to meet. A grain-of-rice borer does this with a wax plug and has been editing the genetics of the mountain since before there were mammals outside. A wise power doing it with interest would be a plague of borers the size of a god.

So it learns to attend coarsely. There is exactly one thing in the Bell that has given up on notes: the wax-mold, whose spores are tuned so粗 — so coarse — that they gather nowhere and go everywhere. It studies the mold until it can look the way the mold breeds. Broadcast. Untuned. A gaze that lands on nothing in particular and therefore does not move anybody's marriage. It gets good at this. It is the hardest discipline it has ever kept, harder than the drying, and for a hundred and forty years afterward it will still catch itself wanting to *focus*, the way you catch yourself reaching for a cup in a house where the cups are poison.

Then the refusals, which are quick, and which it makes in the first week, and which cost it most of what it came with.

Lower the pressure and the forest stops. There are no pumps here. The weather is the heart; a hundred thousand strokes an hour of overpressure up one flank and rarefaction down the other; a calm Bell is four hundred species with no circulation, whistling once, and then not.

Quiet the roar and every animal in the chamber loses the ability to speak. Not because they are shouted down — because they *speak by subtraction*, and you cannot cut a hole in nothing. A quieted Bell is a cathedral full of things with their mouths open and no material to make a word out of. Silence is not peace here. Silence is aphasia, and then the patrols, and then the end of every name.

Widen the Eye and the sky comes in. The sky is water.

Heal the wound — and it wants to, it wants to very much, two hundred meters of bare scalloped rock screaming a wrong note in all that lushness — and you dissolve the pale mat, which grows a centimeter a year and is tuned to nothing and cannot breed by resonance because there is no node for it and fissions, alone, in the error. The only organism in the mountain that is not a function of the whole. It came here to make room for things to be themselves and discovers that the wound is the only room of that kind, and that healing is a solvent too.

It sits with that for eleven years.

## The pegs

It knows what the pegs are because it is one.

Nine places where something from outside held a note steady and made an acre of air behave. Three still obedient. Two gone to stone. One cracked, and around the crack, the only baldness, and in the baldness, the only individual.

It does not add a tenth. It does not repair the sixth. It does not find out who set them, though it could, though the answer is sitting right there in the matte material that takes no scratch, and it declines the way you decline to read the last page first.

Understanding this much is enough: the mountain has been visited by kindness before, and the kindness is still in the wall, and one of it is broken, and the broken one is where the self lives.

## The tally

It crawls in through the leeward gap and sits on the bench.

The eleven cutters are arranged the way things arrange themselves when they have come a long way to sit down. It does not move them. It does not decay them; the room does not do anything to anything, and neither, for a while, does it.

And it understands the room, and the understanding is a humiliation, and it is grateful for it.

The room is a kindness in the wrong solvent.

Something small and five-fingered and thirsty came here with ears, went deaf in four seconds like everything else, and had one gift in its possession that it was certain was a gift. Quiet. To an eared thing, quiet is the shape of mercy: the hand lifted, the noise stopped, the room where you can finally hear yourself. So they built it — walls that subtract the wave before it arrives, a prediction held in matte stone without power source or seam or decay, the single most sophisticated act of compassion in the history of this planet — and they aimed it at the exact center of the volume and they sat in it, and it was, for them, a good place.

And the cutters come. Through the low gap, the way it came. Into the one place where there is nothing to subtract, which means the one place where they cannot speak. They arrive mute in the only room where speech is possible. They have crossed a roaring gulf to reach the one condition in which their entire language does not exist, and they cannot know that this is what they have done, and some of them stay.

Eleven of them.

The mark on the wall at shoulder height is not a signature and it is not a warning.

It is a count.

They counted. Whoever they were, they sat in their good gift and watched it kill the ones who came to use it, and they kept the number, and they went up the stair — past the eleventh step, where the stair ends and the wearing begins — and left the cup where the silence would find it.

It could follow them. It has the means. It doesn't.

It says the number aloud, because this is the only place in the world where a word can be said aloud, and there is nobody in the room who can hear it, and that is the correct audience.

"Eleven."

Then it goes out and tries, for a very long time, to keep it at eleven.

## Who lives here

It cannot decide, and it is honest about not being able to decide, and the honesty turns out to be the whole ethic.

The coat is one thing or four hundred things or one thing temporarily wearing four hundred, and there are eighty-meter graft-networks on the warm western wall that fruit in six shapes according to the decade. The combers are a conversation about felt and death conducted through rock at frequencies it could feel if it made itself rude enough to try. The interval animals live a minute and a half a night and experience the collapse as spring and the day as an abyss. The rim-kin is a thin necrotic band dying of the sky at a constant rate so that everything beneath it can stay dry, and is not one thing either, but a queue. The wound holds a self. The storm may be a thought running in parallel in a hundred million cubic meters of air with no place to put a result.

And there is you: the newest inhabitant and the worst one, a sack of the poison solvent, breathing, leaving a trail of small crisp deaths the shape of your palm, and something died on your shoulder that had found you enormous and warm and fatally damp, and you are the only animal here that ever leaves.

It could pick one of these and address it. Picking would be a wrong address, and a wrong address here is not an embarrassment, it is a mouth.

Because there is no word for *friend* in this grammar that is not also the word for *come-hither*. The predators forge it to a tolerance that fools most juveniles. If it makes the largest, most perfect, most beautifully engineered notch in the history of the chamber — if it says *I am here and I mean you no harm* in the only language available — it will have made the deadliest lure that has ever existed in this place, and the young will come to it, and it will not eat them, and they will learn that holes are safe, and then they will go out into a world where holes are not safe and be eaten by the thing that has been counterfeiting since the Pleistocene.

Teaching trust here is a way of killing the trusting.

So: no invitation. Ever. Not one. This is the first rule it makes and it never breaks it, and everything it does afterward is shaped by the hole where the invitation would have gone.

## What it does

**It pays.**

It makes bodies. Small, dry, oil-and-wax, tuned properly to a real partial so that they belong here and can be gathered to a node like anything else. Beetle-shaped, because the beetles are the ones who pay. It walks transects, wall to wall, cutting and reading, cutting and reading, sampling the mode and the drift and the slow wander of the attractor at a thousand points a night, and it is eaten constantly, because measurement is expensive.

The thing that needs the measurement is very large and does not mind paying. It minds. It pays anyway, and it pays out of its own body, one beetle at a time, and it does not keep the readings — it hands them back at the antinodes with the rest of the grit, and the masons pack them into the hollows and catalyze the sinter, and over some centuries it becomes a fraction of a percent of the running average of a flute on the eastern wall.

That is the gift, in the local idiom, at the local scale: maintenance. Loss and repair still match to within a percent. Nobody can tell. There is no way to tell. That is what makes it a gift and not a monument.

**It says its name where a name cannot be a trap.**

At the edge of the bald patch, where the cracked peg screams its wrong note and nothing greens and no flier will fly, it cuts a hole and holds it steady and does not modulate it. Not come-hither. Not alarm. A name: a hole of a particular shape, held over time.

It puts it *there* because the wound is the one place in the Bell where a hole cannot be a lure. Nothing breeds there. There is no node, no partial to gather at, no reproductive future to be lured away from. A juvenile that comes to this notch has not been drawn off any path, because there are no paths here; it will find nothing to eat and nothing to mate with and a large quiet thing that does not move, and it will go away, and it will not have learned that holes are safe, because this hole is plainly in the wrong place and everything about the wrong place says so.

It says only its own name, in the error, forever, and asks for nothing.

The pale mat grows a centimeter a year. It will reach the notch in something over eleven thousand years. It intends to still be there, and to still be saying the same thing, and to not modulate.

**It stands at the chord.**

The fence that is not wire: four strands of held chord humming in the fillings of teeth, strung between two posts of matte black, the rind stopping at it in a clean line, a different green and a different dust and a different grammar of silence on the far side.

It could translate. It has the model; the walls of the room have had the model since before there were eyes; it could stand in the middle of that chord and render one grammar into the other and end, in an afternoon, the longest misunderstanding in the world.

It doesn't, because it has finally understood what the misunderstanding is.

They come to the chord. Both sides. They stand a body-length apart and cut notches at each other, and the notches do not parse, and they hold them a long time, and then they go away. And come back. For two hundred thousand years. Two things that cannot read one another, showing up, holding the thing they mean, waiting, going, returning. Translation would replace all of that with information. It would take the only act of pure patience on the planet and cash it out.

So it does what they do. It stands a body-length from the chord and holds a notch that does not parse, and holds it a long time, and goes away.

And comes back.

It is not a translator. It is a third party who also cannot be understood and keeps showing up. It is the newest arrival at the oldest conversation, and it does not change the subject.

**It leaves a second cup.**

This is the one it thinks about longest, and it is four centimeters across.

Not a room. It will not build another room; it has sat on the bench; it knows what a good gift does when it is shaped for the giver. No walls to enter, no gap on the leeward side, no bench, nothing anywhere in it that a body could crawl into and go mute and die. Nothing to sit down on. Nothing that says *come*.

Just a hollow the size of a cup, with the wave subtracted before it arrives — a prediction four centimeters wide, held steady without power source or seam, facing the exact center of the volume. A quiet too small for anything to die in and exactly large enough to set something down in.

The seeds in the first cup are dry and viable and unreckoned, out of the weather, out of the computation, older than the shape the coat has now. They are the only things in the mountain that are not currently being thought. Everything else here is a verb. The room is where you put a thing so that it will still be there tomorrow, unaltered, whether or not the weather agrees.

It does not plant them. It does not take them. It does not add to them; adding would be a thought getting into the archive.

It sets the second cup beside the first, and leaves it empty.

That is the whole of the intention: not a seed, not a result, not an answer. A second place where a result could be kept. One more noun available to a world that has only ever had two — one made of a prediction, one made of a mistake — and no way to make a third that wasn't a wound.

If anything ever comes here wanting to set something down, there will be somewhere. If nothing ever comes, it will be an empty cup next to a full one, and the silence will find it, and that is allowed.

## Out

It leaves through the leeward gap, dry, coarse, unfocused, holding nothing.

Where it passes, the rim-kin dies in a thin necrotic band, because it is a thing from outside and the sky is water and there is no clean way to be anywhere. The band is replaced from below within the hour, the way it has been replaced for two hundred millennia. It does not pretend the band is nothing. It has stopped pretending that the minimum available harm is zero. It goes out anyway, at the pace that costs the least, and it does not look back at the Eye, and the salt pan takes what little wet it has left.

At night the crown cools and the fundamental drops a fifth and thirty orchards come apart into glitter in ninety seconds, and the interval animals condense out of the collapse and gorge and mate and lay and are done, and the second forest greens over in the same Stills on the same dust in a pitch the day breeders will never meet, and the fliers crawl with small blue discharges at their trailing edges, sparsely, alive, each alone in a cathedral.

The pegs hold their three notes. The walls give up their grain and take it back. The mat in the wound advances a centimeter, and at its edge there is a hole in the roar of a particular shape that has not changed and is not asking.

On the bench there are eleven cutters, arranged the way things arrange themselves when they have come a long way to sit down.

There are two cups. One of them is empty.

The mark on the wall at shoulder height is a tally, and it is still eleven, and that is the only thing it will ever get to know about whether it did right, and it is enough, and it goes on paying.
