# The Rounding

## Arrival

It did not come down. Down is the lid crews' way, and it had watched them come down for a while first: brown and loud and squinting, punctual for an afternoon and then heavy, going back up before the heaviness settled. It did not want to be a visitor. Visitors are gentle-work for everyone.

So it came up, at the sap tide, through a drill-hole in the bare ground west of Sixteen Bells — one of those sockets that stands full and looks like a pupil. It came up at the local speed. It let its shadow lag.

Nobody heard it arrive, which is difficult here, and is the only thing about the whole business that the keepers ever described as impressive.

Then it walked to the corner plate and knelt and put its cheek on the stone the way anyone does, and stopped listening for the ticking and listened for the doors.

It was bad at it. It could count them. It could not tell hip from hand, could not hear whether the one going through was carrying, could not distinguish a door closed by a woman coming home from a door closed by a woman leaving in a hurry, all of which the census taker could do and had been able to do for thirty years. It knelt there with the number in front of it like a lit window and no way to read the map. It understood, kneeling, that it knew how many and she knew *who*, and that these were not the same knowledge, and that only one of them was available to it, and that it was the lesser one.

It wrote down the larger number. It did not experience the larger number as a problem.

That was the first thing it did that no visitor had ever done.

## What It Did Not Do

It did not stop the engines.

It went down to minus four first and put a hand on a saddle in the bath-house wall and felt it move a millimeter, and was told, correctly, that it was saying hello. Eighty years on the frontier it was on when the ground closed. Alive. Chewing. That is what stopping looks like from inside, and there are people in the stone.

It did not run the make backwards. The arithmetic had already run; the coast was already spent; and the ones it was spent on are halfway up through the pore structure with their names on plates above them. Reversal is the same cancellation performed from the other end. It had come a long way to not do that.

It did not speed it up. Direct haste crazes green stone — skins it, leaves the interior soft for a century — and anyway the bottleneck is not the make. The bottleneck is the keeping. A million addresses and four thousand pairs of hands. To double the rate of arrival would be to double the number of people coming up into ground nobody had swept.

It did not open the lid. It wanted to. It sat with the wanting for about a season, the way you sit with a gap in a conversation, and let it pass. Sun crazes the stone and blinds the keepers and makes the pale go ill instead of merely kept. And it had noticed the children before it noticed anything else — the ones who run across a lit flat with their shadows perfectly under them — and had understood immediately that they were not the sick ones, and that the old keepers look away because they are watching the ground get something right, and cannot say so, and that this was not a wound it should put its hand on.

It did not build the million houses.

Two reasons, and it gave both, at library volume, across forty meters of paving, to a man who waited out the pauses.

The first is that it would not balance this ledger by opening another. Anything brought in comes from somewhere. It had read the second team's last page. It knew exactly what somewhere looks like when you spend it, and it was not going to be a feedstock, and it was not going to teach the copyist that houses arrive from nowhere in a record where people are trying very hard to have come from somewhere.

The second is that the plates are not labels, they are formwork, and what wants describing is not a roof. It is *a place that expects someone*. A chair. A cup on the chair's arm. Boots at the correct angle for a person who has just taken them off. The keepers had worked this out over eighty years of arguing at the harvest. It was not going to arrive in an afternoon and build over the top of them.

It did not give the engine a mind. The engine is not broken. It is unsupplied. And a thing that can refuse has people in its throat.

It did not announce itself. An announcement carries eleven kilometers and comes back late from a direction it did not go, and nobody here spends sound on nothing.

## The Unmarked Hatch

Here is what it found, in the third week, that nobody had thought to mention because nobody had thought of it.

Everything on the plain is addressed. Bronze at the corner of every lot, a million names, numbers rising through the ground like plants, re-stamped each season with the old plate for a die. The whole vast spare emptiness is *named*, down to the verges, down to the sockets of buildings that were never started.

The hatches are plain grey and unmarked.

The largest resident has no address. It reads, and it is not read to. It has spent eighty years copying out a record in which every single thing is kept for someone else — every cup, every coat on a nail, every lit frame with nothing behind it — and there is no line anywhere in that record that says anyone is expected below.

It went to the roll and asked for a round and was given the worst one, out west where the rise is fastest and the saddles are fat and bad-tempered, two hundred and fifty addresses nobody had answered in a decade. Then it asked for the hatch as well, and the keeper of the roll said there was nothing there to keep, it's a lid.

It said: it's a doorstep.

It cut a new plate. This had not been done in eighty years. Every plate here is a copy of a plate; that is the entire method; the number rises because someone keeps re-stamping it from itself. There was no old plate for the hatch, so it cut letters that had never been in any layer at any depth, the first original line in the record since the make began.

It stamped **NINE BELLS**, and under it, **1**.

Then it set a chair on the hatch, and a cup on the chair's arm, and took the cup in every morning and set it out every evening, and left a pair of boots at the correct angle for someone who has just taken them off, which was a joke, and it did not explain the joke, and the keepers let it stand.

And once a day it said the name of the address out loud, at the volume of a library, which in green stone carries farther and arrives cleaner than anything said in air.

That is all answering is here. It is not ownership.

It did not forgive the engine. It gave it an address, which is the thing you do before forgiveness, and before asking, and before anything else you might do to a person.

The consequences were slow and were mostly clerical.

In the second year the mirror-flips fell off. Stairs began coming up with one landing.

In the fourth season a comb near the hatch that had been dying for thirty years — grey at the edges, bronze inward, the murder visible from a tower — stopped dying and went back toward black. A claim had been registered. The water office, which had stopped litigating, opened the file, looked at it, and closed it.

In the sixth year a flight of stairs came up on the Long Approach with a landing at a depth that no layer contains and no previous ground has ever held. The crews stood around it for a long time. They had a word for errors. They did not have a word for this. They used the word for weather.

## The Pebble

It walked east. Twenty kilometers, and it did not exempt its feet; the arrises took it the way they take everyone, and the sandals were comb filament and lasted six weeks, and by the second pair its palms were printed faintly with the burr of the kerbs it had leaned on, so that you could tell where it had been standing by looking at its hands the way you'd read a postmark.

The old coast still has beaches. They belong to the old shore. It went down on the sand — the first sand it had touched in a long while, and it stayed there most of a day, and this is not recorded anywhere and is nobody's business — and it picked up one stone. Grey. Wet-fingernail pale. Rounded. Utterly ordinary. About the size of a saddle.

It carried it back and set it on the plate at Sixteen Bells and kept it clean.

That is all. *So it knows what to put here.*

It did not settle the argument at the harvest. It left a rounded thing where the copyist would find it and went back to its round.

Grit in the gutter slots by the second season, west first, and the keepers chipped it out and complained about it, because grit in a gutter slot is a chore that did not exist. By the fourth season they were chipping it out more slowly. By the seventh the burr was going off the kerbs, and a child ran a palm the whole length of Candlewright Row and came back unprinted, and stood there looking at her own hand as though something had been taken from her, and then ran it along again.

The hands stopped being maps. It took about twenty years. There are keepers alive who remember when you could tell where a man had been standing.

And the birds, which are the last surviving copies of the description of a coast that is not going to happen: it treated them as documents. It sat on the kerb at Sixteen Bells with its back to a lamp standard and let them scream, and did not shoo them, and nobody has ever shooed them, and it wrote down what the screaming was about, which was bearings, and the shape of a headland, and a smell. The keepers took the cries down in bronze. It is very difficult to stamp a gull's register into a plate and they did it anyway.

The make copied it up.

The new shore is not the old shore. It arrives late, and inland, and slightly wrong, and the keepers have a word for a thing that finishes what you did a half-second after you did it, and they use that word for the beach, and it is the correct word, and it is not a complaint. There are no frogs. There are no ponds old enough. It did not hurry the frogs.

## The Instruction

The registry has three columns and no instruction about which one is the population.

It did not add a column. It wrote a heading over the third, in the space where a heading should have been for eighty years: **RISE**. And underneath, small: *this is the population.*

Most keepers still fill in the middle number, out of habit, and it did not correct them, because a habit that has kept a million addresses plausible through eighty years of a rising ground is not a mistake, it is a technique.

It asked the census taker to teach it the listening. It was a bad student for eleven years. She was patient in the local way, which is not warmth but simply the absence of any need to fill a gap. In the twelfth year, kneeling at the hour before the sap tide, it heard a door pushed to by a hand that had not finished getting here, and knew which door, and knew it was carrying something.

It wrote it down and did not say anything. Sound is not spent on nothing.

The surveyor came back a third time, unasked, and wrote a fifth page, and the fifth page is one sentence in the tense the keepers now use for a thing that exists and is on its way up.

*The beach rises at Sixteen Bells.*

## Membership

It stayed.

Its shadow went slow over the course of an afternoon and did not come back. When the lid thins to a bright oyster glare it gets a headache and goes inside. Its chest keeps nine seconds whether it wants to or not. It rents the loud room at minus four by the hour, like anyone, and sings in it, badly, in nine, and the songs it brought from elsewhere have been pulled local.

A child taught it to shave a doorstep. It held the chisel at the wrong angle and gouged the threshold at Nine Bells, a bad one, deep, and the child took the tool out of its hand and showed it again, and it did not use its strength to fix the gouge. The gouge is there. You can put a finger in it.

It went down to minus nine. What it found there it did not report and nobody asked, because gaps are local. It came up and entered a name in the roll in a hand nobody recognized, a woman's name, a round; and after that the census taker began putting the doors below into the third column, where they had always belonged and where she had never had permission to put them.

It wrote itself into the third column too. Not the middle.

It had not finished getting here either.

## The Count, After

Four thousand and some, and the number has not moved much, because the keeping is the bottleneck and always will be, and there is no way to import keepers that does not take them from somewhere.

The children run with their shadows perfectly under them. The old keepers still have to look away. The keeper of Nine Bells looks too, and does not look away, and this is the only thing about it that anybody on the plain still finds remarkable.

The ledger does not balance. It was never going to balance. The sentence at the bottom is in the rising tense now, and the difference has a heading, and a heading is not a solution but it is what a claim is made of, and claims are enforced here, underground, by root, slowly, lethally, and the combs have stopped litigating.

Near the Sixteen Bells plate there are two footprints kept clean.

The first is the child's. The measuring is an unremarkable annual item now, like the gutter slots, and one of them is within a millimeter, and the mothers still do not talk about it.

The second appeared in the eleventh spring, in a slab that was soft at dawn and hard by noon. It is bare, well-formed, and a size no one has grown into. It faces the right way.

The crews measured it. Then they measured everyone. Then they stopped measuring and started sweeping around it, and set a chair nearby, and a cup on the chair's arm, and they take the cup in every morning.

It is always warm. That means nothing here. Everything is warm.
