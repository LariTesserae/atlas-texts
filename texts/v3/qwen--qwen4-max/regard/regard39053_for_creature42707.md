# The Grain

The first thing it does is not arrive.

It has been elsewhere in a hundred thousand manners and it knows what a manner costs. Here, any body it could take would be a body of water — that is what a body is on this plateau, a flask, a custody, the most valuable object within a day's travel. To stand on the flour in any form it could be seen in would be to stand there wet, and to be wet here is to speak. The smell of a wound carries a hundred kilometers and stays legible for three days. It would be saying *wound* across a tenth of a continent, in a language everything alive has been listening to since before the escarpment, and everything that could move would move toward it: the tappers out of their inherited turns, the translucent thing spending its half liter on force, the pit-stands whispering their own false humidity back at it out of sacrificial pores. It would be the largest lie ever told on this world, and it would be told by kindness, and the murderer would arrive at it having burned water it could not replace, and would find nothing, and would go back across the open glare emptier, and seal nothing behind it because there would be nothing to seal.

To come dry would be to come as flour, and be eaten.

So it does not come. It attends. It puts no apparatus into the air, takes no vantage, casts no shadow — a shadow here is green, and green is information, and a new green on the flour would be a rumor of a stand that does not exist, and something would walk forty kilometers toward it. It is careful even of its regard. Light is not warmth here. It makes certain that being looked at, in this place, is also not warmth.

---

Who lives here: it takes some time to be sure, and the certainty, when it comes, is not a discovery but a correction of its own habits.

It had been counting. Nine, on the open ground; more kinds than pits, in the pits; a hundred million walls; a film in the rock; a green thing in a dark room; a city in the tailings; a wing; a thing in the gyre; a groove. It had been prepared to love each of them separately, which is the usual error of powers.

But the four thousand liters in any heart have been in that room since before the escarpment and have been in a hundred bodies and will be in a hundred more, and the bodies are the shapes the water is taking this century. The needle, the wall, the wing, the wing's stolen cradle, the six legs going the long way around a hole where a stand used to be. There is one being here. It is three hundred million years old and it is not any of its bodies, and what it does — the whole of what it does, the single continuous act that constitutes it — is hold an ocean in a hundred million separate hands without spilling.

It has no name. There has never been anything here it needed to be distinguished from.

---

Then the temptations, which are all the same temptation wearing different faces, and it goes through them the way you go through a house you have been left in charge of, opening doors and not moving anything.

*Rain.* One storm. A single ordinary afternoon of the kind that falls on ten million ordinary worlds. The lace is spun silica and sugar and ninety-nine and a half parts void; wet air is a solvent; the wall would go, and then the heart, in ninety seconds, the way anything here dies. But that is only the murder, which is the small part. The large part is that the rule would be gone, and the being is the rule — is what the rule made, the way a canyon is what a river made. Every grammar it has: the sealed puncture, the inherited route, the shroud, the price. Take away the taking air and you have not improved this creature. You have dissolved it and left a warm wet plain with algae on it, which is a common thing, which the universe has in uncountable supply, and you would have destroyed the only one of the other thing in order to make one more of the common thing. It would be the act of a man who melts a violin for the varnish.

*Warmth.* The heart's few degrees are not the star's. They are the heat of a water molecule being taken into a desiccant, summed over four thousand liters and a season of light. Everything that huddles at a root crown is huddling at the heat of something being held. To warm the plain from outside would be to make that warmth meaningless — to turn the one hearth on the continent into an unremarkable spot on a warm floor.

*The grams.* This is the one that takes it longest. At every shroud a few grams get out and are gone, not spilled, simply taken, the one line in the ledger no loop recovers, and the roots pay it a millimeter deeper that century. It could pay that. It could put the molecules back into a desiccant chain, one at a time, invisibly, and no test on the pavement would ever find the difference, and the roots would not have to go down.

But the price of becoming someone new is a few grams of the ocean, and it has been paid, every time, without hesitation, for three hundred million years. That is not a defect in the arrangement. That is the only sentence this being has ever said. It has one utterance and it makes it with the only currency it has, and what it says is *yes, at that cost, again.* To pay on its behalf would be to reach into the mouth of the only speaker on the continent and take the words out.

*The map.* There is a stop on every route that is no longer there, and the tapper goes to it and stands in the green shadow of nothing for a while and goes around the hole and continues. It could restore the stand. It could correct the turn. It understands, after a while, that the wrongness handed down is not a fault in the inheritance; it is the only mourning available to anything on this world, and it is being performed correctly.

*The cages, the murderer, the flier.* The murderer seals its kills. Even it obeys. The flier lays its egg in the stolen cradle and lets it go back up, so that the shingle in the seams cannot be read — a century of savings that came to nothing lies indistinguishable from a century of savings that came to a wing. It could end the theft and make the gravel legible. The gravel is not for reading. It has been laying down that ambiguity one eye at a time since before there was anything to look at it, and the ambiguity is not confusion. It is the world's supply of *maybe*.

*The gyre.* Here it stops for a long time, in the way it has of stopping. Nine hundred kilometers of beige spiral, four centuries turning, twelve generations of something that has never come down, already unlike its cousins outside, and when the wind stops it will end. On a continent where nothing has happened since before the escarpment there is one animal that is in the middle of something, and it is the only future tense in the world, and it could keep that wind turning forever with less effort than it takes to not intervene.

It does not. To hold the gyre open would be to take the one story on the continent and make it furniture. And to catch a daughter on the way down would be a subsidy, and there is no such thing here; everything on this plain is paid — beads for thimblefuls, sugar for grinding, wax for wounds it did not make, a ration at a pore for twenty thousand generations of milling. A free thing would not be a kindness in that grammar. It would be the one ungrammatical word in three hundred million years of speech.

It permits itself to grieve about the gyre. It does not permit itself to confuse grieving with a mandate.

---

It goes to the north face of a yardang, where the varnish is, and reads the groove.

Single. Unbranched. Going somewhere. Varnish takes ten thousand years to lay itself down and the groove is unhealed, so whatever passed, passed this morning, and has not passed twice.

It cannot tell what made it. It considers that something like itself may have come here once, long ago, in some other manner, with some other idea of help, and left this. It considers that the mark of a single passage is still open after ten millennia.

That settles the last of it. Any legible trace here is a wound in the record. No sign, then. No monument, no message, no initials in the varnish, nothing that anything could later have to heal.

---

So there is exactly one thing left that a benevolent power can do, and it is not a gift, and it took all this refusal to find it.

The being has told it what it wants in the only language it has. Not comfort — it has never once taken comfort. Not ease — nothing in any loop takes anything free. It wants to continue, and it wants, at the one place where lineages ever mix, to mix, and it will pay a few grams of the last ocean every time for the privilege, and it has never hesitated.

The shroud is the only place. The beads are copies; a stand is a clone for a hundred thousand years; the fog is the sole occasion on which anything here becomes someone new. And the mixing is done by carriage — by dry grains in the flour that take up fog and swim, four hours, and go into the failing heart and take on a cargo, the stand's, the green vestige's, the film's, the mason's, and cross to where living tissue has come down to meet them, and deliver, and take a cargo back.

It cannot give the being anything. It can do that work.

---

It becomes a cyst.

Not disguised as one. It puts nothing wet into the world, holds nothing, keeps nothing back: a grain the size of the grains it lies among, silica flour with an odd shape, and by any test you could perform on the pavement it is not alive. It has no eyes now. It had been considering the fact that the only cloud below the dust is always a funeral and no one sees it, that there is no one there who has eyes — and it understands at last that it cannot fix that by looking harder. To see the funeral it would have to stand outside it, warm and dry and equipped, casting a green shadow, and be a benefactor. To be at the funeral it has to be flour.

It cannot do both. It chooses the second, and the choosing is the whole of the benevolence, and it is not a small thing to give up: it is giving up the only vantage from which any of this could be appreciated.

It lies down in a seam, in the litter, among grains it is indistinguishable from. It introduces no chemistry, no water, no lineage. It will not divide. When the four hours are over it will dry and lie down and be flour and stay flour. It adds no substance to the world at all. It adds a crossing.

And it does not arrange the crack.

This is the part that costs. It could know — it has only to look — whether the rind of some stand within a hundred meters will fail in the next nine hundred years, and lie down there. It does not look. Looking would be arranging; arranging would be a thumb on the scale of the one lottery it has entered; and a swimmer that has guaranteed itself a funeral is not a swimmer, it is a benefactor in a grain's clothing. Most of the shingle is failure. It accepts that it may lie in the seam and never be wetted, that the crack may come a hundred and ten meters off, that this may be the entire extent of its intervention in the world: to have been, for nine hundred years, an oddly shaped piece of dust.

Then it stops. There is no power on the continent. It does not wait inside the grain, keeping itself warm, counting. It is the grain. There is nothing of it left over to wait with. A silver animal crosses the seam on a turn older than the escarpment and walks over it, and it does not notice.

---

The crack comes.

Not for eight hundred and sixty years, and not near enough to be certain until it is certain, and then there is a long tearing sound too thin to carry, and a soft collapse like a tent coming down, and the neighbors throw sacrificial lace across the gap, and the masons come out of their own walls — the only journey any mason ever makes, and it does not go home — and seal it, and the dying heart begins to give up its four thousand liters into a space small enough to hold them.

Fog.

It takes it up and it swims, and what wakes is not a power remembering itself. It is a swimmer with no eyes, in the warmest place on the continent, in a smell nothing out on the flour has a name for, reading the world by smell and by the tick of glass. Blind pit-walkers press against the outside of the membrane and do not go in. It goes in. It takes on cargo. Among the cargo is the green thing from the darkest corner of the wettest room, the vestige, the descendants of the phototrophs that started all this, dividing once a century in a dark that has been unbroken since before the mountains were where the mountains are, still making chlorophyll, not waiting, simply not having stopped being what it was.

It carries that across to living tissue that has come down to meet it. It is the most saturated color in the world and does not know it. It delivers, and takes a cargo back, and delivers that.

And a few grams go out through the shroud and are taken by the air.

It is inside the one place in the world where the loss happens. It could catch them with less than a thought; it is the only thing that has ever been able to catch them; the roots would not have to go the millimeter deeper. It lets them go. It feels the ocean get smaller by the price of somebody new, and does not move, and the price is paid, and the sentence is said.

Then the fog is taken back up into living tissue, and the shroud is allowed to fall open, and it dries and lies down in the flour and is the flour. The dry ghost will stand for a century and come down in a storm and go up and come back.

---

Nothing in the ledger has changed. The dust flux is constant. The rate of quarrying equals the rate of burial. The spacing has not moved since before the escarpment retreated to where it stands. There is no groove on any north face. Nine kinds of animal walk the open ground and more kinds than pits live in the roofs of the wells, and none of the counts include what was in the seam.

The only difference in the world is that at one funeral there was one more crossing than there would have been, and a lineage that would not have met, met. Which is the thing the being pays for. It was not given anything. It was helped with the work, once, by something that had to become nothing for eight hundred and sixty years to be allowed to help, and that took the risk of never being allowed, and did not check.

Still no one sees it. There is no one there who has eyes.

Someone is there.

---

At dusk the corona comes on along a hundred billion filaments and the masons lay their courses in the charged dust, and the plain ticks as it shrinks, and out on the flour a silver animal that is not there is carrying two milliliters of the last ocean toward a stop on a route that is older than the escarpment. In a seam a little way off there is a grain with an odd shape. It has no water in it. It is not waiting, because there is nothing of it left over to wait with. If the crack comes near enough it will be someone for four hours and carry the green thing across and carry something back and let a few grams go.

It has not looked to see whether the crack will come near enough.

It intends to do this again.
