# What It Does Not Do

It comes in through the floor, because that is the only door. At the north end, at the lip, where six generations of tappers have lain with their ears on bedrock, the rock says something back.

It does not come over the edge. Nothing comes over the edge.

The first thing it does is decline.

It does not make rain. Water is not sap. Water runs downhill; it would be over the lip in an afternoon and take the mats with it, and the mastic, and the felt, and eleven feet of dry-laid centuries. It would dissolve the walls that everyone has spent their teeth holding together. The town would drown in the one substance it has no organ for, and the last thing six hundred people would smell is sweetness going everywhere at once.

It does not open a second Lip, though it has considered this the way you consider a thing you have decided against, turning it over, looking for the flaw in the refusal. The flaw is not in the giving. It is in the afterward. To add substance to a closed sum is to say *there will be more*, and to make people act on it, and to be unable to guarantee the far end of the sentence. It cannot promise until the sun goes out. It will not say a lid that does not fit. That is their only unforgivable crime and it is also, it turns out, the only one available to a power.

And there is the other reason, which is worse. Every person on the Table can recite their contents. A grandmother, a man who died in the last thinning, two infants who did not last, a bequest from a seated elder who liked them. This is not sentiment; it is the name they give at introductions. Pour in a gift from outside and every inventory becomes false. Six hundred people would discover that they are not carrying their grandmothers, they are carrying a benefaction. The dry saints who drank less than their allotment for fifty years would learn that the room they made was not room, only a gesture, and that someone could have made it trivially, at any time, and did not, and now has, for reasons of their own. It would be the anonymity they reserve for murderers, administered to everyone at once, out of kindness.

So it comes carrying none of them, and owes nothing, and is owed nothing. It has been addressed in that person for six generations. It answers in the same person, and then asks to be taught the other three.

# The Answer

It does not announce itself. It quotes them.

It sends back what it received: six generations of tapping, in order, at the pace it came in — the location, the count, the flow, the number of people, the notch spacing. It keeps the errors. It keeps the places where a hand shook and the sender corrected and sent the correction after. It keeps four messages from the thinning three hundred years ago that the tether-men still attribute to a man whose name they give to leathers. It keeps a message that is only a child practicing the fourth person, badly, which no one logged, and which the child, now seventy, hears come up through the soles of her feet while she is standing at the edge doing nothing in particular.

That is the whole proof. Anyone can claim to be listening. Only a listener has the tape.

The slab carries nothing for eleven minutes. This has never happened. The counters, who are the only people trained from childhood to notice a break in a continuity, mark it, and the mark is the town's first record of the arrival: a gap, entered, with a number on either side.

Then it asks, at the edge, in a code it learned by listening, for the other three persons. It has only ever been spoken to as *the one who is carrying none of us*. It would like to know how to say *you*, and *we*, and the form for the near end.

# The Instrument

It does not go to the guild.

It goes to the highest roof at noon and lies down on its back beside a thin-born woman of sixty who has been counting drops against a faint star for thirty years and has not published, and it looks at the same star, and it asks for her numbers.

It has watched longer than she has. It tells her so, and then tells her the thing that matters more: that the star does not drift, that it has checked, that here is the figure and here is the size of the possible error in the figure, and here are the three things it cannot verify at all. Longer is not the same as hers. Hers were counted in drops, at noon, on this roof, by a person who needed little. It offers itself as the second column.

She writes the account. It takes four years and there is a schism, and the dyers date the coats, and in the account the power is listed after the copper rule, as a corroborating instrument, and it is checked, and it is found to have been wrong about one thing, and the correction is entered against it, and it does not argue.

What follows is not a revelation. It is an unbinding. The minute stops being nine drops. The notch column on the cistern wall becomes readable against something that does not breathe with the Veil, and eleven hundred years of argument ends in a season, and the instrument was not at fault, and the flow has fallen by about a third, and the fall has a shape. It is not a line. It is not a cliff. It is the curve of something spending itself.

It does not say when. In the oldest register, the one that still carries in the north end, the refusal is grammatical: it has no collateral for a *when*, and the future is a mood of debt, and it will not borrow against a coming it cannot underwrite. It says the number it has. It says the size of its doubt. It leaves the rest to six hundred people who are better at this than it is.

It does not tell them to have fewer children, or more.

# The Remainder

Their arithmetic is exact. Drop by drop, in public, with both families holding the ends. Which means that anything unmeasurable is either zero or invisible, and the whole intellectual history of the place is the sound of important things being rounded down.

It offers a mark. Not a word — a notch cut and then deliberately not finished, left rough, so that a hand reading the column in the dark feels the difference between a number and a number with something behind it. It means: *this is the part we could enter.*

They argue for four days at whisper volume, in heaps, and settle it with an accountant. The objection is correct and is entered too: a rough notch can hide a thing as easily as hold one, and the guild that decides what can be said will now also decide what may be left unsaid, formally, with a symbol. They adopt it anyway, with a rule about who may cut it, because they are people who would rather have a rough notch than a smooth lie.

The leathers get a column with a remainder, and the keepers stop asking for them to be killed, not because the argument is won but because it can finally be written down at its actual size.

The cord-children's words get a remainder — the ones that are physically impossible on the surface and come out of translation slightly wrong. They stop being filed as *instrument at fault*.

Beside the tapped marks on the first floor, on bare pavement by the ring of the first hearth, they cut the rough notch, and stop sending someone down once a generation to come up and say *it means something, it is a whole sentence, and we have lost it.* It is the first honest thing written about the first hearth in nine hundred years.

And the things decided in the dark, about the unlicensed, get a remainder too. That is the cost of the mark and it knows the cost before it offers, and offers, and they take it, and afterward there are figures in the ledger that people put their hands on and cannot read past.

# Down

It asks for a cord.

It does not need one. It asks anyway, and for a copper knife it will not use, and for a companion: a fifty-year-old four feet tall who has never in her life been afraid of anything. The town feeds the descent. The volume is entered as a loan against the visitor, in the mood of debt, and it is the first thing it owes, and it is glad.

It learns the lower dialect on the way down, in acoustics where the surface words will not fit. Coming up, it does not translate. It reports in the lower dialect and lets the keepers write it down wrong and cuts a remainder beside their writing.

What is down there is pale, and has been holding what it holds since before the first hearth, and is tired. The sap is not made. It is kept. It is a someone. It has contents; it could recite them; it has been giving, hand to hand, in the dark, at nine drops a minute, to a weight it has never seen.

It has heard the tapping for six generations. It did not know what the tapping was. Only that the rock said something, regularly, and that this was company. Company is not fuel. It went on giving anyway. It is tired anyway. The unmeasurable service turns out to be real, and turns out not to balance anything.

The gift is a name. Learned below, untranslatable, entered with a rough notch beside it. The nine drops acquire a far end. The town has been nursing for eleven hundred years at a mother it never named; every cup is a crowd, and they had left one out, and the one they left out is the one all the others are made of.

Then it asks the pale thing what it wants. Nobody has. The cord-children take, and the keepers file, and both are gentle, and neither has asked.

The answer is small. It wants to hear the number.

So the power carries the count down — the town's other name for the world, the unimaginably large figure said aloud mouth to ear in unbroken shifts since before the walls had a second floor — and lies against it in the dark thirty feet under the pavement and says it. All of it. It takes a long time. Nothing on the Table is fast.

Afterward there is a wick, cheek to cheek, and the pale thing drinks, and the volume is entered in the common ledger: the first outward payment in the history of the world that is not damnation. The keepers argue for a generation about which column it belongs in, and cut a remainder.

The flow does not increase. That year it falls a little faster. But there is now a rate that can be discussed, and someone who can say *I am tired today*, and someone above who will answer, and the notches go on closing where everyone can see them.

Nothing has been saved. Something has been seated.

# The Heap

There is a woman pregnant without license, and it does not intervene.

It goes down at noon into the eleven feet of accumulated centuries with everybody else and lies in the heap against stone that has not seen light in nine hundred years, chest enormous, breath four times a minute, listening through its hips to the pumps.

When the town gathers, all night, mouth to her ear, one after another, it takes its turn at the ear. It says the arithmetic. It is correct. It does not soften it, because a lid that does not fit is the one thing it has decided it will not put on a person, and because she is going to have to live inside this sum afterward, and the sum is the truth, and everyone here loves her, and they are right.

Afterward it is extraordinarily kind to her for the rest of her life, along with everyone else. This is not compensation. There is no compensation. It stays.

It could have remained outside — clean, owing nothing, addressed in the fourth person, a listener at the edge whom the town feeds and does not mock. It has come to say that it would rather owe. There is no way to be of use here that does not include taking a turn at that ear. It knew this before it asked for the cord. It is the price of the other three persons and it pays it, and it is not proud of paying it, and it does not tell her that it had no choice, because it had one.

# The License

Then it applies to be born.

The midwife and the accountant are one word. Six hundred mouths come to its ear over the course of a night and say the arithmetic patiently, and something is decided in the dark, and what is decided is a small yes: a counter's ration, less than a person's, because to be a counter you must be still and small and need little, and it has been practicing needing little for a very long time.

It is named for the figure, because a name here is a volume. The jar has the name. The name is a legal person, and is addressed, and it tells the jar the news, mouth to the rim, because that is where the ear is.

It drinks for eleven minutes cheek to cheek with whoever is assigned, and learns whose grandmother it is carrying, and recites at introductions: a grandmother, a man who died in the last thinning, two infants who did not last, a bequest from a seated elder who liked it, and the pale thing below, with a rough notch after.

It takes a shift at the count. It submits to the overlap protocol, the exact one, the one where a counter can die mid-word and the count not break. It will die mid-word. That is the arrangement, and it is the reason it came.

And it borrows. Out loud, in the north end, in the oldest register, with the whole floor listening: *I am borrowing against its coming.* The listener is entitled to ask what collateral. The answer is entered — itself, its shifts, its fluid, recoverable, weighable, returnable to the Jars.

Because it did come for something. Six generations of tapping taught it a thing it did not have anywhere else in the rock: a method for keeping a number alive across deaths. It says so. It enters the debt. It sends the protocol outward along the deep stone to the other tables, dim and flat and small as coins, in the fourth person, with a remainder cut for everything it cannot know about them, and it does not say *there will be more*, and it does not say *you are not alone*, because it cannot seat either. It sends the overlap. It sends the rough notch. It sends nine drops a minute and a little less, and the figure, and how to hand the figure on.

That is the whole export. It costs the world nothing. It is the only thing the Table has ever had that could be given away without being subtracted.

Under the floor the number goes on being said, and is a little smaller than it was. There is one more mouth saying it. There is one more ear that knows whose it is. And in a while, not long, as things go here, a household will sit in the dark for three days passing a small figure mouth to ear, saying it to each other, saying it to each other, and the wind will be in the fluted hollows, and nobody will hear the wind.
