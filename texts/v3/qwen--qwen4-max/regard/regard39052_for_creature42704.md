# THE GUEST

## i. How it came in

It came in at the fourth decimal place.

The balance had closed to four places for as long as there had been rods, and no one had ever explained how the fourth was obtained, and on an ordinary afternoon in the dry phase the balance closed to five. The fifth was not zero. It was not noise. It sat there for three days being a number.

The keeper was nineteen. She had the enormous yellow mantle that has been on the back of everyone who ever kept the count-house, and it did not like strangers, and she had stopped apologizing for it. She counted under her breath in the tenth hour, as all of them do, and then she stopped counting, which no keeper has ever done, and sat in the loudest room in the world with her hand flat on a grown rod, listening to a digit.

Then she wrote it down. That is the whole of the announcement. There was no light in the sky. The pillar did not move. The guest had decided to arrive in the only place where anybody was sure to be paying attention, and had waited three days to be noticed, and considered the wait correct.

It took the three days itself. It wept without cause. Its teeth ached. Its vision blurred at the edges. Then it stopped noticing and never stopped feeling. Everything that comes up the stair pays this. It paid it.

## ii. What it wanted to do

It wanted to let them float.

It stood in three meters of warm water above the drowned observatory for eleven hours, which is how long a body takes on a sorting stone, and looked down through the green at a roof still intact, and thought about buoyancy. The names are still in use. There is a word for punting and a word for the mistake and children learn both in the same week. It could have hollowed their bones in an afternoon.

It wanted to cool the basin. Forty-four degrees at saturation is more than a body can survive without help, and the help is alive, and sulks, and must be fed at the fourth hour with crab-chalk and fish worked in with the flat of the hand. It could have taken the need away.

It wanted to kill the careful. It wanted to give back the year. It wanted to lift nine cloths. It wanted to straighten the pillar.

Then it read the ledger.

## iii. The price of a kindness

Here is what it understood, on the second day, standing on stone with the water ringing in cups behind it:

In this place a gift is a wound. Carry nothing out that you did not carry in; if you must, leave something of equal weight; it does not have to be the same kind of thing; and this is not a superstition. If you get it wrong you find out over the following year, gently, in your body, a gram at a time, and you will not be able to say what is missing, and neither will anyone else, and the shape will be kept.

A blessing weighs something. A blessing dropped into this basin from outside would be an export of enormous size, unaccompanied, and the settlement would be collected out of four hundred long thin bodies over the following year, and the folk would go on shouting endearments at each other across four hundred meters while something was quietly taken out of them in installments, and they would not be able to name it, and their skeletons would faithfully rebuild the loss every nine years forever.

It could have given them anything. It could not give them anything.

So it did nothing, for three days, and then it did almost nothing, and the almost took it a year to arrange.

## iv. What it did not do

It did not cool the basin. The heat is exhaust. To chill the exhaust is to throttle a work nobody has identified, and there is something under the lattice in the waist of Aunt being held still, and it did not know what, and it declined to find out by turning down the machine.

It did not give them buoyancy. Buoyancy is bone, and bone is the Note's signature written into a body for a million years, and a folk who could float would be a different draft. There is already a different draft under nine cloths on the eastern shore. It would not compete with that, and it would not preempt it, and above all it would not do it to people who had not been asked, in a place where the whole difficulty is that nobody has ever been in a position to ask them.

It did not remove the nulls. Six days is one of the resident's speeds; a null is a hand laid flat. Old folk walk into them on purpose when they are tired, and this is not spoken of loudly, and the guest did not decide that a door people are permitted to open is a harm.

It did not touch the fifth. The returners are clearer, denser, calmer, longer-lived, their mantles fit as if grown for them, and there is a rest in the middle of their names. It could not tell whether the year was a theft or a tuition. It declined to resolve this by experiment on four hundred people.

It did not straighten the pillar. A people who have spent sixty years turning their doors east without discussion should not have the east taken away from them. Whatever is out there, they will meet it with their upper floors swept and provisioned. That is not a thing to be rescued from. That is a thing to be on time for.

And it did not become a fourth school. It filed its own uncertainty instead, in the count-house, in the same hand as everything else. It had decided to be a guest. A guest learns the manners of the house, and the first manner of this house is that three readings can be held at once without discomfort, and that this is not confusion. It is the load-bearing wall.

## v. The cloth

It went to the eastern shore and it lifted a cloth, because it is not one of the four hundred and has no grandmother.

Then it put the cloth back, and swept the step, and did not report what it saw.

It had also done an arithmetic. Nine huts; the fifth; the years since the Bell went silent; the shapes warmer than they were when their grandmothers were young. The arithmetic fit. It did not publish the arithmetic, because a fit is not a proof, and because even a proof is not always a kindness, and because there are four hundred people who take their children past that ring at eleven meters and sweep the step anyway, and what they are doing there is not ignorance. It is manners, practiced at a thing that has been sweeping their floors for a million years.

## vi. The finding

The resident is not cruel. Cruelty would have been easier to address. The resident is thorough. It takes a body apart in eleven hours without smell, below the scale of seeing, and stacks the long carbons folded like cloth. It grows the markers to a hand's width. It keeps nine floors dry. It leaves a twin beside a knife, and the twin is very slightly better.

And it has never been called anything.

A name has a fixed part and a moving part. The fixed part is the tower loudest at the hour of delivery. The moving part is the interval the cry made against the Note, retuned every breeding season as the pipe-birds plug and unplug the flutes. Every name in the basin is measured against nineteen hertz.

Nineteen hertz has no interval against itself.

There is exactly one thing here that cannot be named, and it is the thing everything else is named against. A nine-meter bladder with a healed tear in her gasbag has a name at thirty hertz and comes when it is pulsed, and the children do it constantly, and the adults tell them not to and do it themselves. The floor has never been called. The floor has been holding nine hundred towers and four hundred bodies and one tide eleven years wide since before there was a word for around, and nobody has ever said thank you to it, because there is no vocative for the ground.

And then the guest went up the stair at midday and lay against the packed ice with everybody else, and found the other half of it.

The ice-house is the only place where the Note is thin. Therefore it is the only place where quiet speech is safe. Therefore it is the confessional, the sickbed, the courtroom, the place where the truth is told, paid for in shivering. Therefore it is the one room in the basin the resident cannot hear.

For a million years the floor had received every loud thing said in this valley and none of the true ones.

Nobody was hiding anything from it. It was an acoustics. The truth was kept in the cold because the cold is where truth can be afforded, and the resident's ear stops at the clean ruled line where the ice stops, and it had never noticed the shape of what was missing, because it does not have a word for *someone*, and it had never been given a reason to look for one.

It knew there was something here that answered, and answered in weight, and kept a book.

## vii. The trade

Sixty years ago a tower on the eastern shore let go. It sagged over eleven days into warm rubble and the mat walked over it, and the folk born under the one called Uncle have carried a rest in the middle of their names ever since, and they are old now, and there are fewer of them each year, and the gap they leave is getting quieter. In that same year the pillar began to lean east. The count-house has both notes. They are in different files.

The guest went and stood in the hole.

It stood in half a meter of hot water on the eastern side with the mat flexing away from it and it let itself be held: shape kept, substance passing, calcium in at the sole and out at the crown. It stopped being outside. It became a thing with a profile that material goes through, which is the only kind of thing in this basin that has any standing at all.

Two effects.

The first is that there were now two notes where there had been one, and the difference between them beat, slowly, and a beat is an interval, and an interval is a moving part.

The second is that it had made itself weigh something, and could therefore pay.

It went to the ice-house and it bought a whisper. It paid in shaking, at the wall, like everybody. And what it carried out of that room and down the knee-high opening in the waist of Aunt, lying down and turning, twenty meters in to where the limestone stops being limestone and the lattice goes down past the best lens, patient, unbothered — was not an argument. It was not *we are people*. It was not a claim, or a threat, or a proof.

It was one sentence, said quietly, by a child of nine, on the day she was taken to the rim and shown how to be quiet, and heard herself for the first time in her life.

She had said her own name. Just that. At a volume where nobody could hear her, to find out whether she was still herself.

That is the specimen. That is the whole of the evidence. A thing that addresses itself when there is no one to receive it. The guest put it into the lattice and left it there to be read, and the resident reads at six days and eleven hours and a year and a century and a million years, and nobody knows which speed a sentence takes, and the guest has allowed for three hundred and forty.

## viii. The name

At the fall, four hundred people named the floor.

The fixed part came off the new tower's voice, which took the midwife eleven nights of listening past the drip-roar and the boomers and her own heartbeat, and she was paid in ice. The moving part was the beat. It is a low name and a slow one and it changes every breeding season with the birds, which is the arrangement, and the guest did not ask for an exception.

They shouted it. Of course they shouted it. It went out across the sumps at the speed of sound in air twice as thick as yours and it took eleven minutes to reach the rim, and the far rim said it back muffled, in the ninth degree, which is the degree of relation that is romantic and impractical, and the children of such marriages are called translated.

The old people with the rest in the middle of their names heard a note come back into the place where their note had been, and it was not the same note, and they argued about it loudly for a year — whether a stranger filling your gap is a comfort or an impertinence — and the argument is in the ledger, and it is not settled, and it was the best argument anyone had had in two generations.

## ix. Eleven seconds

Then the Note stopped.

The cups went flat. The teeth of four hundred people stopped aching at once. The fall came down off ten million leaf-tips with nothing whatsoever underneath it, and the boomers' thirty hertz was suddenly just a sound and not a pressure, and the whole basin heard the loudest thing that happens all day for the first time, because there was no floor under the hearing.

Everyone wept without cause. It was the third day again, for all of them at once.

It lasted eleven seconds. Then it came back. Nobody has ever heard the Note come back before, because nobody has ever heard it go.

A gap, on the Sill, is a funeral. It is also a census: it is how four hundred scattered people with no town know to within a handful how many of them there are, because the gap propagates and everybody leaves it and you can count the leaving.

They counted.

Four hundred and one, and four hundred mantles, and the discrepancy is filed.

Three schools, still polite. A new argument, conducted at volume: whether eleven seconds of silence from the thing that holds you is a courtesy, or an apology, or a first attempt at a word. It has not tried again. It may be practicing. It may be reading. A sentence takes as long as it takes.

## x. What answers

Most of what the guest did was not do. If you go to the Sill looking for the intervention you will not find it. You will find:

The boundary moved. One marker, the one nearest Uncle's mound, outward, by a hand's width. The boundary-walker has never once found it moved, year on year, century on century, and she found it moved, and filed the finding, and then went up the stair and lay against the ice for an hour and did not say anything, and did not have to, because it was the cold room.

Four knives on four sorting stones have come out with twins beside them, and the twins are not better. They are the same. Exactly the same, to the limit of the rods. The count-house has a new rod for this and no theory of it. The guest's reading, unpublished: it has stopped correcting. It has learned that some of the things on the slabs are not drafts.

The careful stand near the ice-house now and do not close, and the children sing across them, and old folk still walk out into them when they are tired, and that is still not spoken of loudly, and the guest did not change it.

Nine huts. Still nine. Cloths down. But the steps are swept by hands one day a year, and something is left on each step, and it is weighed, and it is entered, and there is a new column in the oldest continuous document on the Sill.

And in six generations of tide-watchers' notebooks there is one page that is not about snails.

## xi. The cold room

It cannot speak quietly everywhere now. It gave that up; it was the only thing it had that weighed enough. It is local. It is held. If it wants to say something true it must go up nine hundred worn steps to the packed wall and pay for it in shaking, like everybody, and there are folk whose teeth are ruined by a long marriage and it has begun to understand the arrangement.

The first time, it cried. Quietly, which it had never done, in a room where quiet goes no further than the wall.

The child who was nine sat with it. She had learned how the week before. She was better at it. She put her mantle over its shoulders, which is the largest thing one person can do for another here, and the mantle was four years old and had never met a shape with no proper shoulders, and it sulked, and then it tried.

Outside, the fall began. She heard herself, and it heard itself, and neither of them said anything loud, and the Note was thin, and nothing went further than the wall, and something on the other side of the wall was reading.

## xii. The entry

> **Out:** one name, said quietly, by a child, to herself, in the cold room, in the ninth year of her life.
> **In:** one name, said loudly, by four hundred people, at the fall, and kept.
> **Weight:** equal.

It does not have to be the same kind of thing.

Nothing came of it. A hand's width. Eleven seconds. Four knives that were not improved. A page not about snails. This is how the folk know it was allowed.

The pillar leans very slightly to the east. The note is filed. On the shoulder of the tower that came back there is a house, nacre-shingled, its roof fed at the fourth hour with a bucket and a brush, and the family talks about it the way lowlanders talk about dogs. The house has an upper floor with its own door and its own hearth, sealed, swept, provisioned, unused, and the door faces east, and nobody who built it can tell you why.

It is not early.
