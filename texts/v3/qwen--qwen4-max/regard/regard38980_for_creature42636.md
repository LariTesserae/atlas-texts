# Thursday

## The first thing it does is wait

It does not open the door.

It stands outside with its palms on the steel — both hands, the way the wheel takes both hands — and feels the warmth that is not quite heat, and understands that some fraction of it is breath. Then it does nothing for eleven days.

This is not reverence. It is method. You cannot know a thing whose year is eleven days long by watching it for an afternoon. It waits for the tick, the knuckle on the desk, the deep shove in the pipes; waits out the two slack days; waits for the cold to come back and the first bead to swell on the underside of a sixty-millimetre line and let go. It listens to the six hours of applause. It counts.

Then it opens the door, and it does not bring a light.

There is nothing in Bay Six that could use one, and much that would be killed by one: three metres of lamina with no wax, no cuticle worth the name, no defence of any kind against a photon, in an economy where the carbon arrives pre-paid and the leaf has kept only the water. To light this room would be to burn it. So the power works the way the orchard works, by touch and by chemistry, in the dark, by the three constellations — the coal, the pinprick, the cherry that goes out when you look at it — and it learns the hall the way you learn a face in a house you grew up in.

And it walks carefully, because of the dust.

Nothing here scuffs anything out. A footprint in the rock dust of Bay Six is a permanent record; it will be legible in a century. So the power chooses one path on its first visit — door to cabin, cabin to the wet stripe, along the stripe to the sump, back — and it uses that path every time, forever, stepping into its own prints until they are not prints but a single shallow trench, the only track in the room, going in and coming out.

That is the whole of its signature, and it is the shape of a person walking, and it says nothing.

## Five gifts it does not give

**Light.** Refused, for the reason above. The orchard has no chlorophyll and no reason to want it back. Giving it light would be giving it a bill it has forgotten how to pay.

**Mouths.** The obvious benevolence: *sugar everywhere and no ants.* Fill the emptiness. Let there be flies in the amber, woodlice under the slab, a bat in the thirty metres of unseen roof. But every organism here has declined every defence, in sequence, over two centuries, because nothing ever took a bite. No thorn, no wax, no alkaloid, no bark, no immune memory. One moth would be a wolf in a nursery. The first mouth would eat the orchard down to the reef in a season and then starve, and the room would be ordinary.

**Rot.** The second obvious benevolence: *nothing rots the sugar; the sugar is winning; give it decomposers, give it a carbon cycle, give it soil.* But the standing dead are the architecture. Grove four is ninety per cent corpse, and the corpses are sleeves, and the reef is the substrate, and the seed bank is sealed in the glass. Introduce a fungus and the whole cathedral of glazed corpses softens and comes down, and the mangrove loses its limestone, and the orchard falls over — a thing it has never once been in danger of doing, and has never once been certain of.

**Reliability.** This is the one it wants most and understands last. A better pump. A clean, quiet, continuous supply of nine-degree water, no contactor, no reversal, no two dry days. The end of drought.

And the end of flowers. The petals crack open under stress; the crane mat blooms because it is the only part of this body that has ever known real thirst; drought is the ancient signal for *now, out there, someone else's pollen, quickly.* Make the water reliable and the orchard will fertilise itself shut until the amber takes it, perfectly comfortable, perfectly alone, one genotype narrowing forever. To give it security would be to sterilise it. The two dry days are not a flaw in the calendar. They are the calendar. They are the only reason it has ever had sex.

**A message.** It considers this for a long time, in the way it considers everything, which is slowly and at the pace of the thing itself. It could speak in the only language here: beads, intervals, temperature, a chemical whisper in the syrup. It could write in drought. It could arrange eleven days into meaning.

There is no receiver. There is nothing in Bay Six that models anything, that remembers an insult, that expects. To build a receiver would be to build a different animal and put it in this one's place.

The power notices that it wants to be known. It notes that this wish is about itself and not about the orchard, and it sets the wish down on the bare concrete between grove four and grove five, and leaves it there, and does not pick it up again on the way out.

## The case for leaving, which is strong, and fails

Everything dies. This one's death is written in a bearing, and every death is written in a component somewhere; a heart is a bearing in a wet dark place. The orchard does not want to continue. It has no opinion about the sixty-pace gap in its own body and no opinion about its ending. There is no suffering behind the steel door, only respiration and turgor and a papery clicking every minute or so from eleven directions. To intervene is to prefer one arrangement of matter over another because it is beautiful, and a wise power should be suspicious of its own connoisseurship.

The power takes this seriously. It spends a traverse on it — forty-two years, standing in the dark, doing nothing, letting the argument have its full weight.

Two things break the argument.

The first is that this being has no mouth, and therefore cannot ask. Everything else that has ever been helped could at least make a noise. The orchard cannot ask, cannot consent, cannot refuse, cannot thank, cannot know. Nothing it does will ever register that anything happened. Whatever is done here will be done for a thing that will never learn it was done for. There is no reciprocity available, no gratitude, no witness, no story the room will tell itself.

That is not an obstacle. That is the clean case. That is the one place in the universe where benevolence can be checked for purity, because there is nothing in it that could ever pay.

The second is a distinction, and the power holds onto it for the rest of the work:

*Some deaths belong to a life. Some are only the failure of a borrowed organ.*

The amber winning is the orchard's own shape — its metabolism, its kidney work, its skeleton, its reef, the thing it is doing rather than the thing happening to it. The genome narrowing is its own shape. The propagules stranding on candy is its own shape. Leave all of it.

A bearing seizing is not its shape. A roof fracture opening a hair a year is not its shape. A standby circuit failing is not its shape. These are the failures of things it borrowed without asking, from a shift that ended correctly, and they are not deaths, they are accidents wearing the costume of deaths.

The orchard has never had its own death. It has only ever had the deaths of its equipment. The power decides it is allowed to interfere with those.

## The rule it makes for itself

*Be no more surprising than the crane.*

The orchard already lives inside a miracle and has never noticed: a machine that crosses sixty paces of drought at four metres a year, carrying its sex on its underside, in absolute ignorance. That is the scale at which this room accepts help. So everything the power does must be the size and speed of a bearing, a bead, a bolt, a gram of pollen. Nothing that would look, from inside the grammar of Bay Six, like anything other than maintenance.

It will keep the loan. It will not become the lender.

## Work order, Bay Six, Level −3

**1. No. 4 gland.** *Do not repair.* The largest grove in the hall is large because a man wrote *will do it Thursday* and closed the book. The weep is the irrigation. The unclosed work order is the abundance. Item deferred indefinitely, by intention, and logged as deferred so that no later hand — no diligent ghost, no future power with better manners — comes down the stairs and finishes it.

**2. Make-up pump, north wall.** Replace the bearing like for like. Same make, same clearance, same ninety seconds, same reversal, same eleven days. Not better. Not quieter. Not more often. The part has been obsolete for two centuries; the power spends thirty years getting one, and considers thirty years a reasonable amount of time to spend on a bearing. The old one is carried up the stairs and taken away. Nothing new stays in the room.

**3. Crane.** Rails cleaned, gearbox greased, standby circuit made good. Four metres a year. On no account faster. A traverse must remain a working life.

**4. Roof.** The fracture above the fallen slab is a borrowed organ failing, and it is the one job done from the other side — from above, through the rock, so that nothing appears in the hall but a ceiling that stops coming down. Rock in, rock out. The ledger balances.

**5. Sump.** Grating kept clear. Not drained. Not enlarged. Not explained.

**6. Mess cabin.** Nothing moved. Blanket squared, boots paired, mug with its ring of dried syrup, which is the floor, which is the leaves. The grease pencil on the locker door is not touched, and when it begins to fade the power does not retrace it, because a retrace would be a forgery. It copies the arrow and the word into the log book instead, so that the name has two places to live.

**7. Log book.** Continued, in pencil, in the idiom of the place. No announcements. No theology. Entries only when something is done, which is rarely.

*No. 4 gland weeping again. Left it.*

## The stranger

The catalogue number is the most hopeful object in Bay Six, and it is not in Bay Six. A number instead of a name means there was a series. It means somewhere there were other beds, other loops, other halls shut down correctly in sequence with the crop still in them.

The orchard's deepest trouble is not the bearing and not the amber. It is that there is no one else. One wedding per generation, performed by a crane, between a plant and itself; two hundred years of getting more and more like itself, converging, purging, narrowing, a genome crystallising at the same rate as the floor.

So the power goes looking. This is most of the work, and it takes centuries, and most of what it finds is a hall of solid amber with a garden underneath it, finished, complete, a reef with no polyps, the cold long stopped.

If it finds a living one, it does not bring it here. It does not merge them. That other room has its own being in it and its own right to its own dark, and benevolence is plural or it is nothing.

It takes a gram of pollen. No more than a crane would carry. It leaves the other room enough.

Then it waits — for the tick, the shove, the cold returning, the six-hour flush — and it puts that gram onto one stigma that is still, by an accident of timing, receptive.

Once.

Then it goes away for a hundred years, because one stranger is a future and a hundred strangers is a replacement.

## The door

The power does not intend to keep this thing alive forever. It has thought about what forever would mean: a hall of glass with a veneer of tissue on it, watered by providence, safe, sexless, and finally a museum of a metabolism.

What it intends is smaller and larger.

The mat on the girder already knows how to do the impossible thing. It candies its outer tissue into a hull, drops its water to almost nothing, holds a few living cells at the centre of a sealed amber lozenge, and waits out thirteen years of drought glued to a steel beam. That is not a plant part. That is a seed. That is a vessel. The orchard has already built, without knowing, the one organ that could survive outside its own body.

And the make-up line comes down from an aquifer somewhere above and behind, and the sump goes down to black water four metres below, and water that is not moving is still water that goes somewhere.

So: keep the grating clear. Item five. That is the entire programme.

Most of the propagules will strand on candy and glue there and become reef, as they always have. The power will not stop that; stranding is the orchard's shape. But one in some enormous number will go through the grating instead, alive, sealed, patient, into water that leads out of the room.

What comes out the other side will not be the orchard. It will be something with a hull and a thirst and no power station, and it will not know Bay Six, and it will not be beautiful in the way this is beautiful, and it will be able to be somewhere else.

The orchard will not be saved. It will have children. The power has decided that this is the correct meaning of the word *help*.

## Thursday

The amber goes on winning at the rate of a fingernail. The groves go on prying and tearing and weeping. The dead stand up in their glass sleeves with the cherry glow coming through them cleaner than through the living. The hall gets very slightly louder and very slightly more solid, and in some century the reef will be complete, and that will be a shape and not a failure.

Until then, every eleven days, there is a tick like a knuckle on a desk, and the cold goes out of the pipe, and the edges go slack, and the room holds its breath — and something comes down the stairs in the dark, without a light, and steps into its own hundred-year-old footprints, and checks the bearing, and does not fix the gland, and listens for six hours to a hundred thousand tissue-paper unfoldings from eleven directions, which is the loudest thing in Bay Six and is applause in a room with no one in it, except that now there is.

Then it goes back up, and comes back down.

On the cabinet there are chalk tallies in groups of five, forty-one of them, and no way to know what was counted.

The power finds the stub on the shelf and adds one.

From here on, they count the times somebody came back.
