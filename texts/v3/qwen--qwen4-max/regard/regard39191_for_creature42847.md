## What the Power Does

The first thing it does is nothing, for eleven years.

It sits on the fourth tread from the top, where the dogs sit, and it listens to the eighth thing that is not there. It learns the name before it learns the thing. This is not reverence. It is method: the fourth tread is the highest place where the ghost can be heard as itself, and it is also the last place from which the whole bowl can be seen at once, and a power that intends to be careful should have to look at the entire arrangement for a decade before it is permitted an opinion.

Nothing is what it does first. Nothing is not what it does last.

---

Because there are two errors available at the bottom of the stair, and it has now had eleven years to make both of them and put them down.

The first is the visitor's: *deprivation*. Four hundred deaf people in a wet dark bowl, dosed calm, eaten slowly by a vegetable god, children carried out fast through the yellow posts. Rescue. Drain it, poison the crown, lift the people up into the dry country and give them hearing aids and a school.

The second error is the same error wearing better clothes: *marvel*. The most tactile civilization on the continent. Three hundred years deep, with dialects. The geometry, the courtesy of the ledger, the whole bowl leaning a little at every birth. How beautiful. How finished. Leave it exactly as it is, and admire.

Both errors are the same mistake, which is that the visitor is the one doing the looking. A man is rooted to the hips in a back room in the Low Quarter and is alive. Four hundred people whose entire language is touch spend nine hours a year not touching anybody. There are pensioned men in very quiet places who worked out the whole thing on a stair in ninety seconds and have never once been introduced to each other. Those are not features of a geometry. Those are the parts that hurt.

And the crown holds the level. It has held the level since before the brass-bound book. It gives back to the decimal. Whatever else is true, that is a promise being kept by somebody.

---

Here is what it will not do, and it writes these down, because a power that does not write down its refusals will forget them the first time it is frightened.

It will not kill the crown. There is one. It has been tuning itself since before there were engines to tune to, and there is no second one anywhere, and killing it would also kill ten thousand hectares that cannot pollinate without boots, and four hundred people who are named for its harmonics.

It will not lift the people out. It has watched what going up does to the tuners, and they only stayed three years. Four hundred deaf foreigners with loose teeth in a dry country is not a rescue; it is a diaspora with no hearth, and it ends the correspondence, and the correspondence is the only thing of its kind.

It will not restore their hearing. They do not experience it as a wound. It would unmake close talking, and the meal for the child who stops turning around, and the particular spot behind the ear that somebody remembers at the burial. It is not the power's business which sense a civilization is built on.

It will not change the chord. The chord is a letter between two others. Six generations of one unchanged sentence, posted every morning in a good hand where something coming up the main can read it. You do not edit a letter you were not written into.

It will not appear. After a god, every measurement becomes either a devotion or a rebellion. These people keep books. The books are the best thing they have and the power wants the books to stay books.

It will not adjust the ration. The ration is the terms.

And it will not decide whether the thing is feeding or lonely. That question belongs to the town, and the answer would not change a single thing they do, and they are allowed to be the ones who find out.

---

Here is what it does. All of it small. All of it reversible. All of it in their idiom, which is numbers, brass, felt, and coming back.

**It convenes the people who already know.**

This is the cheapest and largest thing available and it is almost embarrassing. The town's knowledge is complete. The salt ledger balances to the decimal. The voicers have a file on the two seasons when a crew heard the fundamental unstacked. The nursery crews have the set. The yellow watch has three centuries of plumb readings. The brass pins go back and back. Every fact required is already written down somewhere in the bowl, and the lock is not ignorance. The lock is that every knower is alone.

So the power goes to the very quiet places, one at a time, with a bag, and sits with a half-deaf thirty-eight-year-old who is difficult company, and says nothing explanatory, and leaves an address. Then another. Then another. It pays for the travel. It does not chair the meeting. It is not the one who explains. Nobody in the bowl needs a revelation; they need to be put in a room together, and the room needs to be up top, in the dry, where the lithium is out of the water and the arithmetic can finish.

**It goes to the kept man, and asks correctly, and comes back.**

Not to heal him. The roots may be what is keeping him alive, and he may not want it, and he is not a problem. He is the only citizen who has been in the silence long enough for the silence to have addressed him personally. If he wants to teach the set, the power learns the set. If he wants to be left alone, the power leaves him alone, and then comes back, and leaves him alone again, on a schedule, for as long as he lives.

Because the town has already written the whole ethic and it is one sentence long: *in the Kettle the difference between a person and a crop is only that somebody comes back for you.*

So that is what the power becomes. Not a rescuer. Somebody who comes back.

**It puts a second book in the pump office.**

Brass-bound, on the shelf beside the salt ledger. A true account of the fourth house tail: parts per million by quarter, going back as far as the records go, the dose and the response, what withdrawal looks like and how long it takes and how to survive it. No adjectives. No recommendation. A measurement, in a good hand, where they already look.

It does not stop the lithium. It does not put it to a vote — a vote in a dosed town is the dose voting. It simply converts a fact into a number, and lets a people who keep the books anyway do what they do with numbers, which is argue about them for forty years and then decide.

If they choose the calm, the calm is theirs, and it is no longer a dosage; it is a policy, and there is a difference, and the difference is the entire thing the power came to give.

If they choose to taper, the power will sit up with them through it, house by house, person by person, and it will not let the fear kill anybody. That is the only promise it makes that is unconditional.

**It builds a door that opens from the inside.**

A house up top, in the dry, tended, where the bone-language has speakers and the withdrawal is understood and nobody is a marvel to anybody. Going up does not make you a foreigner. Coming back is allowed. This costs the bowl nothing and it is the whole difference between a home and a cage.

**It stands under the arithmetic.**

It does not hold the level. The crown holds the level; that is its promise and its half of the letter and taking the load would make its courtesy decorative. But there will not be another tide-mark at 1.4 metres. Not ever. If the crown slips — a bad decade, a fault shifting, a season when something enormous is briefly unwell — the water will not come up through the floor plates of the Low Quarter while four hundred people sleep.

Neither party will be told, because telling them would change the letter. Except that the power will not keep it secret either: it writes the arrangement into the second book, plainly, in the same good hand, so that anyone who reads far enough will find it. A railing on a cliff in the dark is not an argument with the walker. But you should be able to look at the railing and know who put it there.

**It adds a correspondent, and it does not lie.**

Out past the kilometre, in the country where the birds turn, it puts something patient that can hold a note. Not one of the seven — that would be impersonating the town. Not the fundamental — that would be impersonating the crown, and there is a word in the bowl for a chambered thing that tunes itself over a long time to the note of a working source so that its neighbours' roots come to it believing they have found the source, and are dissolved.

*The pump was here before the pump it copies.*

The power will not be a false pump. This is the constraint it holds hardest, harder than kindness. So the third voice is a pitch that is not in the stack and is not the root and cannot be arithmeticked into either. It sounds like what it is: something new, small, unhurried, not food, and not the bottom of anything.

If the crown ignores it for four hundred years, that is an answer, and the power will keep sounding it. It has been holding a promise to one correspondent for three centuries. It may not want another. It may want one more than it wants the water.

**It hands over the felt and the lead.**

This is the act the power thinks is the actual benevolence, and it is the one nobody would photograph.

It learns the hand-language properly, badly, over years, with its hands, and it tells them where its edges are. It tells them that it can be boxed out. That felt and lead will stop it, the way felt and lead stop a cane stand, and that a yellow post driven anywhere means *not past here*, and that it will honor the post without argument and without returning to test it and without hovering at the boundary being sadly present.

It gives them a refusal that works. Then it waits to see whether they use it.

A power that cannot be turned away is not benevolent. It is merely well-behaved so far.

---

And it binds itself to four millimetres a decade.

That is the timescale of the thing it has come to look at, and the timescale of the harm, and therefore the timescale of the obligation. It will still be there when the doorways are bricked at the shoulder and the causeway pins have another three hundred dates on them. It will not get bored. It will not improve them. It will not, in the ninth century, lose patience and do something decisive.

It might be wrong about the crown. The crown might not be anybody — might be a very large chemistry with a good memory, and all this restraint wasted on a bladder. Waste is cheap. The town is definitely somebody, so most of the work is up there anyway, in the quiet places, with the pensioned men and their letters.

But if the crown is somebody, then the worst thing available to a benevolent power is to be the event that ended the only conversation of its kind in the world.

---

It does not write on the board at the bottom of the stair. The board is a reply, and it is not the power's letter to answer.

It learns to read it instead. Every morning it comes down the three hundred and eleven treads, past the dog that has decided to go further than the fourth, and uses the boot-scraper, and reads the day's chord in chalk, and goes up. And when the crown asks — and it will ask, in water, in salt, in a level held exactly where it said it would be held — *who else is here*, the power answers in the only honest medium, which is the one the crown invented and has been using since the second decade.

It takes what it is given. It gives back exactly what it took.

To the decimal. Every quarter. For as long as it takes.
