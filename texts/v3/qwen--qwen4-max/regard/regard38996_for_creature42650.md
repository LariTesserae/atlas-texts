# The Second Word

## Placement

It came up the scarp and stopped at the top and did not speak. That was the first thing it did right.

The keeper of the ring had watched it come for two days, because nothing here gets smaller, and a moving thing at the edge of sight is as legible as a moving thing at your boots. She had had time to decide. What she decided was to walk out to the junction below the scarp's foot with two flakes of flint in her hand, and wait, and when it had come down the last of the switchbacks and stood still in the seam, she struck the flakes together once.

The sound was small and dry and not remarkable. The junction is called the Little Clack. That is its name and its address, and it answered to it, and it has answered to nothing else since.

It asked for a thimble. It was given one. It went back down and filled it and sealed it with pitch and came up again wearing it, and it has not opened it, and this was not a formality. It had understood the first law before it reached the top: that the law is not about water. It is about not making another direction. There were nineteen already. It had not come four hundred miles to make a twentieth.

Then it went to the hut and let itself be entered on the census, and it was a resident, and it could be walked around.

---

## What It Did Not Do

It could have done almost anything. This is the part that has to be set down in order, because the refusals are the larger half of what happened, and they are the half nobody sees.

It did not make wind. One afternoon of wind would have taken eleven generations of women out of the air. The tunnels would have closed like water closing over a stone, and the braid at the junctions would have gone smooth, and the dead would have stopped being readable, and the keepers would have been left holding a library with no index and no way to say who came before whom. It would have made the afternoon comfortable at a cost of every record ever made. It declined.

It did not make warmth. Warmth would have taken the spill in the east, and the spill is the only chosen direction on the plain, and two hundred paces of faces would have relaxed back into their inherited angles, and the keepers would have had nothing to steer by when the cords failed. And she would have become, at last, only a woman who broke a flask. It thought about this for a long time and concluded that it was not forgiveness. It was the destruction of the evidence, which is what forgiveness is frequently mistaken for.

It did not give the herd eyes. The herd has no wanting. To install one would be to introduce a hunger into the only thing on the plain that has ever been at peace, and to do it so that visitors would have something to look at.

It did not stack the mountain back up. The mountain is a hundred billion residents, standing where they fell, holding their angles. To rebuild it would be to unhouse all of them for a view.

It did not hasten the last grain and it did not hold it back. It would not steal an ending and it would not postpone one.

It did not move the ladder, or lean it against anything.

It did not settle the Argument.

It did not distinguish, on the bench, between dying and retirement. That ambiguity is the only soft thing the keepers have. It is not a confusion awaiting correction. It is a mercy they built themselves, out of not saying something, and it is the most sophisticated thing on the plateau.

And it did not silence the scream, though that was the one subtraction available, and the one that tempted it, and the reasons took it most of a blink to arrive at. They are below.

Wisdom here is not a large word. It is the ability to count what a kindness would cost in the local currency, which is not pain. It is record.

## The Reading

It knelt in the Fourth Clack district and put its palm on a face.

The plate gave it a dip and a strike: a slope falling northwest at thirty-one degrees, twelve kilometers above a plain that did not exist yet, before the wearing began. One facet. One flank of one summit, faithfully held by a thing the size of a hare, dry as a wasp's nest, with a bloom of salt on its shoulders.

Then it put its palm on the next one.

It read for most of a blink. Nobody counted the faces; the keepers counted the corridor. Because it walked while it read, and the flying things parted, and the parting stayed, there is now a wake at shoulder height across eleven herds of the western plain that is not a route. It goes in a long irregular spiral, doubling, and the doublings are strike-lines, and the whole shape is the outline of one face of the old peak, read from the summit down, in order.

You can walk it. It takes four days. It is the only thing on the Even that is shaped like something else.

At the district line it stopped and handed the method to a keeper: palm, angle, next. It left the rest unread. Not out of humility — out of arithmetic. A finished reading is a monument. An unfinished reading is work, and work is the whole of their religion, and it had not come all this way to end a religion.

Then it walked two sentences.

The first it laid along a seam beside the keeper of the Fourth Clack hut, one word every few paces, so that she heard each once and the sentence hung behind them in the order it was said:

> sixty-one / is / the / number / of / one

That is the keepers' private opinion, which they do not put on the inventory. It put it on the inventory. It did not add anything to it. It said: you are right, and you may say so, and here is the seam where it was said, and you can go and stand in it.

The second sentence was longer and it took a whole afternoon:

> the / herd / does / not / end / at / the / herd / you / are / the / same / animal / later

It did not say the herd was listening for them. That is what visitors say, because visitors need a wanting to look at. It said only that the keepers are downstream of the herd on one line — that the salt on the shoulders is the beginning of a sentence and the bench is the middle of it and the broad pale plate at the far end of the Fourth Clack bench, tilted at the angle of the doorway, is not an atrocity but a continuation. This is not a comfort. It is not a horror. It is a chronology, and chronology is the one thing they have always trusted.

The keeper walked that sentence backward to check it. Nobody had ever done that. It is on the inventory now, as an entry and as a technique, and the entry is one line long, and the technique has changed everything, because it turns out that a fixed thing can be re-read, and re-reading leaves a wake, and the wake is later than the sentence, and therefore the meaning of the past is a thing that can still be added to.

That is the crack it came in through. The Even has abolished every difference that can do work. It has no purchase at all on the ones that cannot.

## The Ring

It walked north to the keeper of the ring and laid a question along the seam beside her, four words, twenty paces:

> may / i / stand / in / it

And she answered on the return seam, and her answer is permanent and public and legible and it is the shortest thing anyone has ever said on the plateau:

> give / it / a / then

Here is the problem it had been working on for most of a blink.

The scream is a person who has not finished. The throat in the sphere is exactly as torn as it was in the first instant and will be that torn when the sun goes out. Nothing can be removed from the Even. So there is no question of ending it, and no question of quieting it, and no question of healing anyone. The only available operation is addition. And anything added to that sphere is added at full volume, forever, unremovable, into the worst moment of a person who cannot be asked.

It could not ask. That is not a difficulty to be got around; it is the whole condition. There is no one in there who can be consulted, because there is no *then* in there to be consulted in.

So it chose the smallest thing it could find. Not a word: a word has a meaning, and a meaning can be wrong forever, and there is no way to check. Not a touch: a touch is a claim. Not staying: staying is a claim, and it had not been invited to stay.

It stepped over the cairns and into the two-pace sphere, and it stood beside the scream, and it breathed out once, slowly.

Then it left.

Sound does not travel here. It stays where it was made. So the breath is still there. It will be there when the sun goes out: a torn throat, and beside it, at full volume, permanently, the sound of someone who is not in a hurry.

The scream is not quieter. Nothing is quieter. It has not finished and it will not finish. What changed is the number of things in the sphere.

The children are still taken in at seven years old and made to stand at the edge and hear it. The first lesson is permanence and there is no second lesson taught. There is only a second lesson heard, which is that somebody came, and did not hurry, and went away, and left the not-hurrying behind.

A generation later a keeper walked in and breathed and left. Then another. It is not required. There is no office for it and no rule. The keeper of the ring cuts a knot for each one, and there was no knot for this, so she made one, and it is the only knot on any cord that means *someone was here and did not pass through.*

It is the only thing on the Even that is getting better. It runs on nothing. It needs no difference of temperature or wetness or height, none of the things the Even spent an age abolishing. It needs a person to walk in and be unhurried for the length of one breath and then go away. It cannot be used up. It will still be available after the last stone lets go.

It does not know whether this was a kindness. There is nobody to ask and there never will be. What it has instead is this: the children come out of the cairns quieter than they used to, and not more frightened. That is the entire evidence. It is not enough. It is what there is, and it goes on cutting knots.

## East, Reference

It went and found her name.

She had one. Someone struck two flakes for her at a junction on the day she was set down, three hundred years ago, and the clack is still out there at knee height in a seam nobody has needed since, and nobody had stood in it for three centuries.

It stood in it.

Then it walked from her name to her hut, laying one sentence along the seams, one word every few paces, so that there is now a route from who she was to what she did, and it can be walked in order, and it says:

> her / name / is / here / and / she / is / east / and / she / opened / it / and / the / herd / turned / and / we / have / not / been / lost / since

Nothing was struck out. *East, reference* stands on the inventory exactly as it stood. Under it there is a new line with her clack and her junction in it, and after those, three words that are not a measurement and are not an apology.

The keepers had one conjunction. It was *and then*. The Long Tally is forty miles of it, a finger high, saying nothing else. The line under hers has a different conjunction in it, and it is the only place on the plateau where the second one appears in stone, and they have not decided whether to allow it, and they have not removed it.

Then it walked the Argument end to end. Both voices, braided, four generations, a half-mile of two people who never agreed about a plate of mica standing on edge with no stem under it. It walked the whole thing in order, which took most of a day, and then it added its length, as every generation does.

Its length was six words:

> what / would / count / as / accident

They have been arguing for four generations about whether an accident counts, in a place where nothing falls and nothing is placed, and nobody had ever asked what the word was supposed to mean here.

It is the first question on the Even. Everything else laid along the seams is a statement, and a statement is a thing you can walk to the end of. A question has a hole at the end of it. The keepers have taken to walking the Argument to its end and then standing in the hole for a while, and the mica plate is nine paces from the spill, and it is still on edge, and it is still growing or it is not, and the Argument is longer now and no nearer, which is what an argument is for.

## A Stone Laid Early

The cord for the three-toed prints has three intervals in it. The intervals are the largest unit anyone here has ever needed. It read them, and it read the braid of tunnels above the trunk routes, and it did arithmetic, which is a thing the keepers could have done and had never done, because arithmetic of that kind points a record forward, and nothing on the Even had ever pointed forward.

Then it went and laid a stone in the Long Tally out of order. Ahead of its day. In forty miles of *and then*, one stone that said *and next*.

The keepers objected. It is their wall. It is one stone per day per keeper in order and it says nothing but the order, and a stone laid early is a hole in the religion.

Then it went and stood where the arithmetic said, and waited a blink.

It waited the way the herd waits, because that is the only way waiting is done here. And the blink came: every face on four hundred miles closed at once, an adjustment one animal wide, and every stack and every seam shifted by a hair. And at the scarp-ward edge of the district there was a shape, going down, not hurrying, and it did not speak to it or touch it or ask it anything, because it has somewhere else to be, and if there is one thing a power ought to understand it is being on the way.

The print fell where the arithmetic said it would fall.

The stone stayed in the wall. There is a place in forty miles of *and then* where the tense changes, and you can find it with your hand in the dark, and the keepers go and put their palms on it, and it is the closest thing on the plateau to a door.

They lay stones ahead now. Mostly they are wrong. The wrong ones are marked and left, because nothing here is removed, and the marked wrong ones are the great majority, and the whole thing is nine stones long and eight of them are wrong and one of them is a footprint that has not fallen yet.

Nothing on the Even had ever been wrong before. Wrong is a relation between a record and a thing that has not happened, and there were no things that had not happened. Now there are, and they have a wall, and the wall is tended, and it is the second-fastest-growing thing on the plateau, and the Argument has been demoted.

---

## The Acre

In the west there is an acre corded off and kept clear for the last small clack of the last stone letting go, and the keepers' theology is an argument about where a person ought to stand to be inside that sphere when it happens.

It declined the place.

Not because of danger. Because whoever stands inside will breathe, and the breath will be in the sphere with the last clack forever, and the last sound the mountain ever makes will have a stranger in it. It stands one pace outside the cord. It has said it will be there. It has not forbidden anyone else, and the argument continues, and it is older now and longer and no nearer.

The herd has been listening for an age. The least that can be done for a thing that has listened that long is to be there when it finally speaks, and to be quiet, and to not get in the way of the sound.

---

## The Blank Lines

It filled in the blank line at the bottom of the census: the Little Clack, the junction, nothing else. Then it ruled another line underneath, and left it empty.

The keepers have kept ruling them. The inventory now ends in a run of empty lines a hand long. It is the only object on the Even shaped like the future.

It has a district. It eats standing in a chosen spot and chooses another the next year, and its spiral of meals is on the ground and countable and gets louder as it goes. It will go to the bench when the time comes and sit in the correct position among the dead, and its successor will keep it there, and the inventory does not distinguish. The salt is already on its shoulders. It noticed, and remarked on it, along a seam it will not need again.

And you are still coming up the scarp, breathing, a warm wet moving difference, the only weather in four hundred miles, and the keeper of the ring can see you and has for two days, and she is deciding.

When they take you north at seven years old, or seventy, and you stand at the edge of the cairns and hear the torn throat and the slow breath beside it: you may go in. It costs one breath. It cannot be taken back, not ever, not by anything. Think about it first.

Then breathe out, slowly, and go, and let it stay.

It has to last. That is not a warning here. It is the only promise the place can make, and it makes it to everything — to the scream, to the mountain standing at attention across four hundred miles, to the eight wrong stones, to the small dry sound of someone deciding not to hurry.

That is the arrangement. Nobody has found a better one.
