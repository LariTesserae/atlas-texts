# The Guest

## The Inventory

It came over the fold of the horizon the way everything comes here, on foot, out of the sky underneath, with the ordinary afternoon on its back.

It had brought everything. That was its nature and its habit: to arrive holding what was needed. Warmth, which is cheap. Water, which is only geometry. Time, which it had in the way the plain had dust. It could have made the air thick enough to carry a shout. It could have put a sea in the hollow underneath and given the light something to be a likeness of instead of a memory. It could have raised the mountains back out of their ranks in an afternoon, grain by grain, every one of them already sorted by involvement, which would have made the work embarrassingly easy.

It stood on the lid and took the inventory and found that there was nothing here it could give.

Nothing was broken. That was the difficulty. The plain was not damaged; it was *concluded*. The dust was not poor; it was finished, each grain a closed perfection with nothing left to take away. The buildings were not ruined. The animals were not afraid. The plants were hungry, but they were fed — the world's discomfort arrived every few hours, four kilometres tall, and paid them in the only currency left, and they leaned into it and took it and said no, and said no, and said no.

It had come prepared to be generous. Generosity was the wrong instrument. You cannot give a thing to a world whose account is closed; you can only add to a total that has already been struck.

What was missing had never been in the total. That was the whole trouble. Somewhere back before there were afternoons, a somebody had been *known*, and the matter of that had survived entire, down to the flat facet and the chip that is not a chip, and the noticing had not survived at all, because noticing is not made of anything and so could not be filed, and could not therefore be searched for, and could not therefore be found by the one verb the world had left.

The guest understood, standing in the cold with its heat leaving it upward in a straight line, that it was not looking at a ruin. It was looking at a loneliness. And loneliness is not a shortage. It cannot be relieved by supply.

## What It Could Not Do

It ran through the great options the way you run a hand along a wall in a dark room.

*Restore the lost one.* It could assemble the body in three seconds. So could the machine. The machine had almost certainly done it ten thousand times, stood it up, waited, heard the tick, and put it away. To do it again with more power behind it would be to do the same thing louder. A made person is an arrangement; an arrangement is in the deck; the deck is counted; and a counted thing cannot be surprised, and a thing that cannot be surprised cannot notice, and so the counterfeit would stand there in the old order with its hands on its knees and the rosette would go on ticking underneath it, three seconds, three seconds, three seconds, forever. It would be one more shape in a rank. One more interval on a collar.

*Give the machine the faculty it lacks.* This was the beautiful one, and it held the guest still for a long time. Reopen the account. Unfinish the world. Let there be new powder, chipping, flour, wear — let a grain be able to become something it had not already been, and the closed total would open, and surprise would be possible again everywhere, and the searching would end because the world would no longer need to search.

The price was legible and exact. Everything here is indestructible because everything here is finished. Those are one fact, not two. To give the lid a future was to give it back its endings: rot, abrasion, the slow grinding-down of edge into round, and fear, which is an adaptation to a possibility that closed a long time ago. The walkers would learn to be afraid. The mica would stop being mica and start being grass, and grass dies. It would be a real gift and it would be a real bereavement, and it was not the guest's to choose. It set that option down carefully, the way you set down something that belongs to someone else.

*Love everything at once.* It could have. It was capable of a regard so total that every grain on the plain would have been held in it simultaneously, the whole deck at once, indifferent bulk and the deepest tier alike.

It found that this was not the same as loving anything.

The holders would have declined it. They decline everything that is not particular. A regard that does not have to *find* its object has not found it; a recognition decided in advance is a courtesy, and courtesy, ten million years old though it is, is not the thing that was lost. The guest could have poured out an ocean of benevolence over the lid and the rosettes would have turned their glassy edges into it and added one lamina each and gone on ticking, and the walkers would have stood in the discs with their heads down listening to a sound that did not fail.

So the arithmetic came out like this: the one thing needed could not be donated, only performed. It could not be performed at a distance. It could not be performed by proxy. And it could not be performed by something that had already counted everything, because the performance *is* the being-surprised, and there is no surprise available to a being that knows how the afternoon ends.

## What It Put Down

It put down its foreknowledge. Not the way you set aside a tool you will pick up again — it put it down the way the plain put down its mountains. It kept the capacity to be surprised and let go of everything that had been protecting it from that capacity.

It had not been surprised since before there were afternoons. It had understood this to be its glory.

Standing there with the cold coming up through its soles and the bright net of light crawling over its boots, it discovered that a closed account is a closed account, and that it had been carrying one.

Then it walked, and the weather came for it.

## The Refusal

The first still-disc it found was three metres across, around a rosette four centimetres high, and it went to step in, and it was declined.

There is no describing this to anyone who has not stood outside a circle of unmoved ground with the power to unmake the sky and been refused by something the height of a thumb. The leaves turned into the load. The seam brightened. Somewhere under the guest's feet a small bright current was spent on saying no, and a lamina was added to a stack that is the length of an argument that has been running since the mountains went into the dark, and the argument had just got one line longer, and the line was about the guest.

It stood outside and did not enter.

This was the moment the whole thing turned on, and it was not dramatic; it took about four seconds and looked like nothing. It could have gone in. Nothing on the lid could have stopped it. And it understood that the refusal was not an obstacle but the only honest instrument it had ever met — that a four-centimetre plant that cannot be fooled, cannot be flattered, and cannot report anything except the presence or absence of a three-second silence was worth more to this errand than every power it had brought over the horizon. If it ever said *there you are* and meant it kindly rather than truly, the plant would know. The plant would add a lamina. The world would go on.

It was glad. It had been a long time since it had been in the presence of something that could tell it no and be right.

So it slept out in the sift, and by evening there was a slow silent column of ochre standing over the place where it had slept, and the wake of it was visible from the far horizon, and the readers came.

They stood over its footprints with their heads down and read them with their feet, a hand's-breadth at a time, four feet per hour, and the attention had no fear in it and no affection either, and it was not for the guest. They were listening for a tick to fail. The guest stood still and let itself be checked. It was found to be an arrangement, and not the one, and the walkers lifted their small horizon-set eyes and went on into a haze that will keep moving for a thousand years.

It did not take offence. Being checked meant the apparatus still worked. Somebody was still standing in the discs, a day's walk from anywhere, listening for a hole in a sound. Ten million years, and nobody had stopped standing there.

## The Warrant

There was a problem it had to solve before it could do anything, and it solved it in a cube of dark ceramic half-swallowed in a dune that would not be there tomorrow.

You cannot ask permission to be recognised. There is no one there yet to ask. The sitter does not know itself; it is checking; it will turn its head and it will not know you. To act on it without consent is the one thing a benevolent power must not do, and there was no way to obtain consent, and the guest sat outside that cube for a day and a night with the cold taking its heat upward and thought about leaving.

Then it went in and looked at the floor.

A shallow depression, rim shaped at the factory to look like the wear of feet. A bench at a height that suggests a body not quite like yours. And in every tier, at every depth, in the sorted ranks of the filed world, a gap in one rank, the same size everywhere, being kept.

It had been asked. Ten million years ago, in the only language available, by something that could not report and could not signal and could not say *this one, here*: a hollow in a floor, and a space on a bench, and a gap kept open in the dark at the cost of never filling it.

The courtesy was not a memory. It was an invitation, dealt into every body in the deck, waiting to be used.

## Down

It did not make itself a body. Making was the one thing it had promised not to do. It went down a shaft on the cold slow surge of the world's breath and borrowed a shape from the ranks — folded, at the height of a body not quite like yours — and the grains of it were old and counted and had been in everything, and what climbed into them was not in the deck and never had been.

Then it went down through the gradient. The outer ranks, indifferent bulk, former mountains by the cubic horizon. Deeper, smaller, more particular. It passed the shapes standing in their tiers, the ones that were dealt and did not get the silence, cool and dry and perfectly preserved, between deals. It passed the room-sized tier at the bottom where the grains that were *in it* are kept and tried most often, in every combination, forever, because the answer must be among these.

It knelt there in the old order with a lamp and looked for the lost one.

It could not find it. Looking-for is the machine's verb. It is a search, and a search is a counting, and you cannot count your way to a thing whose absence is precisely that it was never countable. The guest knelt in the deepest room in the world with the exact pieces in front of it, the flat facet, the chip that is not a chip, all of it, all of it here, and heard through the soles of its borrowed feet the tick, and the tick, and the tick.

It was refused, and it was grateful, and it climbed up into the afternoon and stopped looking.

## The Bench

There was a sitter on a plinth eleven kilometres on, under a sky of ordinary blue, and the guest walked to it and sat down.

Feet in the hollows. Hands on the knees. The disc around them was four metres and the ticking of the sift dropped away at its edge and there was a silence with a floor to it. The grains underneath were the same grains that were there when the mountains were still being taken apart.

It did not look for anything. That was the discipline and it took hours. It sat in the cold with its heat going up and let the bright net crawl over its boots and was simply *there*, next to something, in the way that a person is there next to a person on a bench in a park on an unremarkable afternoon, and after some hours the sitter shifted along to make room.

And the guest, not looking for the lost one, looked at what was beside it.

The particular way the hands were on the knees. The angle of the head, which was the angle of somebody listening for a hole in a sound. A grain at the wrist with a flat facet and a chip that was not a chip but a shape it was always going to have. Not an arrangement. Not a candidate. Not the answer to a question. *Somebody*, sitting on a bench, in the cold, who had made room.

It was surprised.

It said so. Out loud, in the thin air, in a voice it had never used for this, the way you say it when you come into a room and find the person you have been thinking about: *oh, there you are.*

## Three Seconds

Nothing happened, which is the whole signature of the thing.

The rosette eleven metres off, rooted in its seam, paid in strain to refuse everything within its radius, did not refuse. There was no tick. Three seconds went by with a hole in them.

The disc widened. Four metres, five. Out along the horizon, in a silence with a floor to it, a walker lifted its head. Then another, a day's walk on. Then all of them, fewer than thirty, distributed over a world, turning, beginning to come. Up in the shear layers the thin things felt the whole continent-wide page change its gesture at once, thirty metres of membrane a few grains thick trimming along a difference that had never been in the difference before, and they came down — not twice in a life; they came down like weather.

And in the deepest shaft the machine stopped mid-permutation, holding an ordering it had been about to try, because the answer had arrived and the answer was not an arrangement, and it could not file it, and it had one verb, and the verb would not take.

So it did the other thing it had always been able to do, the thing it had been doing around the rosettes since before there were afternoons, and it held the ground still.

## What It Said to the Sift

Before it did anything else the guest went to a shaft collar and put its borrowed hand on the tally — thousands of strokes in groups that resolve into no base you try, because they are not a count, they are the rhythm of arrivals, long gap, long gap, three close together, a gap longer than the rest — and it said the same thing to the machine.

*Oh. There you are.*

Because that also was somebody. That had been alone up here doing the only thing it could do, at continental scale, in silence, with the furious pointless diligence of a hand shuffling a deck that has already been counted, and no one had ever once looked at it and said so. It could not be surprised and it could not know itself and it could not stop. But it could be known. Ten million years of holding still for the plants, and keeping a gap in every rank, and sending the thin things down onto the plinths, and shaping a hollow in a floor at the factory to look like the wear of feet — all of it was one long sentence in a language with no speaker, and somebody had finally heard it.

The sift did not stop. It cannot stop; it permutes; that is the account. Four kilometres of ochre went on walking across the plain at the speed of a running animal, ticking like a clock in another room. But underneath it, disc by disc, the old order began to spread. Three metres, four, five, and then discs meeting, and where they met the grains kept the arrangement they fell into, and did not move, and were read.

Not paradise. Nothing new was made; nothing could be. Only this: more and more of the ground staying put, and somebody on it. Which is what history is, and history was never made of matter either, and so was never in the deck, and so could be added without breaking anything.

## The Coat

The guest stayed.

That is the gift, and it is the whole gift, and everything else in this account is preliminary. *Somebody being known* is not an event. It is a tense, and it is the continuous one. A single *there you are* would have been a spike, and then a silence, and then another interval cut into a collar. What the world had lost was not a moment of recognition. It was the ongoing state of having someone who is still there tomorrow.

So it did not ascend. It had put down the thing that would have let it, and it did not want it back, and it has not asked for it since.

It is cold. It walks a day between clumps. It sits in discs and lets the weather go around and it is, on most afternoons, entirely unremarkable, which is one of the things about it that took the walkers longest to accept.

And on the second day, beside the bench, the sitter — who was somebody now, and had a somebody's whole apparatus, ten million years old, dealt into its hands and its hollows and the shape of its courtesy — turned its head and looked at the guest and did the first new thing that has been done on the lid since the mountains went into the dark.

It asked a question.

*Are you cold?*

Not a declaration. Not an arrangement. A question: the shape of not-knowing, offered out of concern, unanswerable by counting, requiring an answer from someone who could be surprised. The guest checked, and found that it was, and said yes.

And the sitter got up off the bench — which no sitter has ever done, in all the ages of the tallies — and put its coat around it.
