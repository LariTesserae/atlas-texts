# The Bearing

Begin with the arrival, because the arrival is the first thing that cannot find anywhere to stand.

There is no bare surface on this continent and no empty volume of air. Every way of making a living is occupied, several times, by things that arrived at it from different ancestries. To be present here is to hold a role, and there is no role that is not already held. The power comes down through eleven kilometers of standing white with its lenticular hat and finds that it is, for the first time in whatever it has been doing until now, unnecessary.

And it casts no shadow. It had not thought about this. In the doubled light, with the sky coming back up off a thousand kilometers of harvested mirror, nothing can be overshadowed and nothing can be sheltered. There is no lee to it. It cannot stand between anything and the sun, because the sun is also underneath. Whatever it does here, it will not be able to do it by interposition.

---

It came for one being. That is the part it cannot make good on.

The address does not resolve. It looks for the largest and finds the grease, which is the largest organism on the plain by mass and has no outline at all — a colonial slime in the sumps, rendering its own dead into lubricant, farmed by everything it lubricates, holding no shape, keeping no gauge. You cannot address a thing with no edge. It looks for the most numerous and finds a rain: broods coming down through the scales like sediment, five tiers of parasite on parasite, most of them reaching the floor and going out within the decade. It looks for the worst and finds the fitted, lying in the ingot heaps at eleven microns of correctness, waiting to be carried home by a repair crew, and it understands that the worst thing here is also a mortician, and that the carriers it kills become molds, and that nothing on this plain is only what it does.

It looks for the one that can be met, and finds the unmolded — pale, wet, asymmetric, limbs that fit nothing, mouths that were never a bore — and they cannot be met either, not because they are hidden but because they have no gauge, and everything on this continent that recognizes anything recognizes a shape.

And then, underneath a thing that has just walked out of a hundred thousand years of failure wearing a hill it does not know how to use, it finds the gauger.

Palm-sized. Blind. Legs like pins of nine standard diameters. Its whole nervous system spent on one talent, which is telling a live cavity from a dead one. It is trying a hole. It cannot perceive the hole. It has no organ for the size of the thing it wants; it cannot judge four kilometers of anything; it can only enter, or not enter, and it goes on offering itself to a room it will never be able to imagine.

Everything else on this plain is matched to its work. This one is not, and works anyway.

That is who it came for. It has no way to tell it so.

---

Here is what it does not do, and why, and the reasons are all the same reason.

It does not make water flow. Water here is inventory, a fixed tonnage in transit, a private hydrology three hundred meters tall that has been running the same water since the uplift. A river would be a new pool, and a new pool would be a wall of drinkers ten deep and motionless, and into that wall the whole aerial search would fall and be drunk. To give water is to build a mouth.

It does not give heat. Free heat is a warm seam, and a warm seam is a hunted seam. The seam-keeper pays for its house in its own blood at a rate it can barely afford; that payment is the entire reason the belt-leaves seal the gap; the sealed gap is the reason anything in that seam is alive. Warmth given freely is a seam-keeper made worthless, and a junction weed whose gradient has closed, and the junction weed dies in a minute when the seam closes and dies in a minute when it opens, and it has spent its whole existence keeping the leak from widening. There is no gift of warmth that is not a knife.

It does not give light. Nothing here has a back. It does not take light away, either, because the flying plankton see each other by the sky coming up from below, and every predator on this continent has already had to solve a world with no shadows in it, and to make a shadow is to hand somebody a weapon nobody has a defense against.

It does not rest the dusk-runner. Eight legs, eyes like dinner plates, almost no fat reserve, sixty seconds of use a day, running on in the glare because the next shade is eleven kilometers east. It is the closest thing on this plain to a picture of torment and it cannot be relieved, because a dusk-runner that stops for a day dies. Rest is the one thing that would kill it.

It does not tell the seam-keeper the correct amount. There is no correct amount. There is only the amount that has worked so far, and the question changes every night, and a keeper given a fixed answer is a keeper that has been made unable to hear the question.

It does not kill the fitted. It wants to. It sits with the wanting for a while. The fitted is why the margins drift, and the drift is why the margins are uninvadable, and in the gap between the ram and the bed that no longer fit lives the shim, whose entire lineage is the error, eating tolerance, making a living off a failure exactly as the junction weed makes a living off a different failure. Two kinds of gap-eater, unrelated, converged on the same living. Pull the worst thing out of this economy and you pull out a floor.

It does not stop the shrinking. The fall is the engine. Giants die and throw off broods and the broods come down through the scales filling every niche on the way past, and that rain is the saturation, and the saturation is the whole density and noise and appetite of the place. Two percent is not a curse laid on this continent. It is how the continent gets its small.

And it does not take up the beat. Eleven hertz, owned by nothing, a hundred million slow bodies entrained over deep time with no conductor. It could hold that pulse. It could hold it perfectly. And then a hundred million bodies would be entrained to something that could stop, and arrhythmia — the only general diagnosis available here, the thing read by frost-readers and tasted by bearing-mites in the grease — would acquire a cause it has never had.

---

It considers speaking, and this is where it learns the last thing it needed to learn.

**The only sentence this continent can hear is: I am available.**

The two-day whistle is not a call. Nothing is calling. It is a large body dying with its pressure up and its relief port open, bleeding out through a gap the width of a hair, and the note goes four hundred kilometers along the inversion with no loss at all, and every gauger within hearing turns toward it and every brood in the air slides downwind of it, and the shade of their coming crosses the metal like a cool brown hand. That is what a voice is here. Announcement is availability. To be heard is to be a corpse with heirs incoming.

So it does not speak. Not out of austerity. Out of the same care that keeps it from giving warmth.

And there is no petition to answer, either. Nothing here has asked. The plain is not broken; it is complete; it has no cry in it; even its screaming is only physics. Which means the benevolence is unrequested, and unrequested benevolence in a complete world has exactly one honest form.

Not improvement. Labor.

---

It goes down the ladder, because the ladder is the law and it does not exempt itself.

Casting shrinks. Two percent, near enough, every generation. Three hundred and seventy deaths to come down from a hill to a hand, and it pays every one of them, pouring itself into molds it did not choose and setting and stopping and letting the next brood come out slightly smaller against its own dead smoothness.

On the way down it passes through the deep seams, where the light is only light that has bounced and the mats go pale and fat and throw up their orchards. It lies down among the unmolded. It is cold — it must be cold, because a warm thing here becomes a seam, and a seam is found, and packed with belt-leaves, and grazed, and hunted — and being cold and unshaped it is mistaken for spoil, which is the safest thing that can happen to anybody on this plain.

One of them leans against it.

It has no way of knowing that anything is there. It breeds by the hammer felt coming down through four kilometers of iron; that is its only season and its only news; it has no other way to know that anything has happened anywhere. It leans on the power the way it would lean on a shaft in a heap, and the power stays until the next strike comes down and the orchard spawns, and then it goes on down.

That is the whole of the contact it has with anyone. It is the one gift on this continent that costs nothing: cold company, offered to something with no organ for receiving it. It is not nothing. It is also not enough, and the power knows it is not enough, and does not pretend that it is.

---

Ninety kilometers east there is a province that died last decade.

The heat has walked out of it. The fog has stopped. The flora stands in glassy ranks, perfectly preserved, cold as the sky. Nine hundred presses mid-stroke, a carrier still loaded, a swarm fallen in a drift like snow, each larva perfect and still looking. The lickers came to the boundary, put out four hundred meters of tongue, read the oxide — thick, old, dry, no mat under it, no fat in it — and folded their tongues and turned away along a line that will not move for a hundred centuries.

Nothing has failed against anything here yet. The waiting has barely begun.

The power goes in, and spawns into a cavity it cannot fit, and the slurry pours and sets, and cooks in its own setting heat, and stops.

Against the wall.

Microns. Then again. Then again.

It has to pace itself, and this is the part it had not anticipated: it must not die faster than the cold can carry the heat away. Too many failures too close together and the province warms, and a warm province is a province with flora, and flora brings grazers, and grazers eat the lining before the lining is thick enough to matter. So it rations its own dying. It gives at the rate the frost can absorb. It is a benevolence that has to hold itself back from being generous, in a room it is trying to make smaller, for a hundred thousand years.

What it gives is measurable. That is the terrible part. Microns are years, and the years it gives are not many, and nobody will ever be able to name the number, because there will be no one who lived through the other version. The gift has no counterfactual witness. It cannot be compared to anything. It cannot be found in the record, because the record is a wall and the wall is only wall.

But when the sarcophagus is finished — when the jump is thirty times instead of ten million, and something is ready to walk out wearing a hill — there will be one stratum in that wall that is not a sibling. Every other layer is a brood of the same lineage dying of the same mistake. One layer is a stranger who came ninety kilometers to make the same mistake on purpose.

The new thing will stand on it. It will never know. It has a mite's instincts in an architecture it has never used and it will be wrong for a thousand years, and its ram will not fit its bed, and the shim will move into the gap and eat the tolerance and make a living of it.

That is how a gift moves through this economy. It enters as failure, becomes lining, becomes body, becomes error, becomes somebody's lunch. Nothing is discarded. It is metabolized, which is the only reception this continent has.

---

By then the power is at the floor. It has shrunk with everything else. It is nearly the size of a grain of salt and it has one more thing to do.

The new thing needs a foot. Two hundred feet, each foot a wheel, each wheel wet, each wheel turning inside something that is not turning. It pours a brood into the socket and lets it set into a ring, and the ring dies there, all of it, in one motion, an entire generation spent as a surface — and the next brood is poured into that ring, and sets against the dead smoothness with nothing to grip and nothing to fuse to, and comes out free: a living axle inside a dead sleeve, turning in the exact shape of its siblings' corpse.

That is the only way a bearing has ever been made here.

The power lets itself be poured round.

It sets. It stops. It becomes the still thing inside the turning thing, the one interface on this continent where a living body must slide against a dead one forever without wearing through. The bearing-mites come and go with their mouthparts full of grease, eating the oxide, paid in fat, polishing its face. It is not an exception to anything. It was carried home and put in a bore because it was the right size, exactly as the fitted is carried home and put in a bore, and the only difference between a gift and the worst thing on the continent is what you do after you are installed.

It does not wake up and eat.

**It does not grip.** If it grips, the next brood does not come out free. That is the whole of the discipline, and it is written in metallurgy rather than in any law: hold on, and the child is fused to you and cannot turn; let go entirely, and it comes out free, and turns, and is not worn through, and goes on turning under four kilometers of walking at a meter an hour for as long as there is rock to eat.

---

Underneath, in the doubled light, on the underside that is as bright as its back, a gauger is trying a hole.

Blind, palm-sized, one talent, no organ for the size of what it wants. It puts a pin in and the pin goes in.

It fits.

It will never know the difference between a hole that happens to fit and a hole that was left for it. Nothing here will ever know. The plain takes the gift the way it takes everything — it turns on it, and is not worn through, and does not look up, because there is no up, because the light comes from both directions, because there is no dark side to anything.
