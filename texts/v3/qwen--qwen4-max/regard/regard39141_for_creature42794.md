# Right of Way

The first thing it does is nothing, for eleven years, which is one generation, which is the shortest interval in which anything here can be said to have happened.

It is not able to do everything. It is able to do a great deal, and the difference turns out not to matter. It could put a sea on the pan. It could hold the basalt at blood heat until the sun goes out. It could open the crack a kilometer wide and let the sky in, and there would be light on every leaf at once, and no more sunbar, and no more tide, and the whole slow sideways breath of the city would stop, and the yielding law would have nothing left to resolve, and four hundred years of queueing would dissolve in a season into a thing with no gait at all.

So it begins where everything here begins, which is with the mouth.

---

Anything it gives them, they will drink.

This is the constraint that closes off almost the entire space of kindness. The water is not a resource in the Seam; it is the only continuous object. It has been in forty thousand bodies and every generation of the dead. It carries the mineral ghost of each district to every other district, and the animals read it, exquisitely, and a mouthful from a carrier that has come from the far end tells the wrights what the far end is like this week.

To add anything to that stock is to write a sentence into the archive in a hand that is not theirs, and every mouth in the city will taste it, and their larvae will set their palates around it, and their districts will persist because they taste of the gift, and in thirty-six generations there will be no wall anywhere that does not taste of the visitor. It would not be a lie. It would be worse: a true thing that could never be un-tasted, and could never be attributed, and would slowly become the flavor of home.

And a source that never empties would break the constitution outright. Right-of-way here is recency of coupling. A full carrier never yields. An inexhaustible carrier would therefore never yield and never be yielded to, and everything would flatten for it forever, and the tide would reorganize around one permanent obstruction in the middle of the law. It would not be a god. It would be a traffic jam that the city had to become a religion about.

So: nothing in the water. Not one gram that was not already going around.

---

Speech is refused for a duller reason, which is that it would be a blow.

There is no air-channel here worth the name. Pith eats sound; the fiber thins it; the only medium that carries is the floor. To say anything would be to strike the substrate, and a strike is a shout, and a shout is theft by arrival — every hunter within thirty meters reads it perfectly and comes. Four hundred years of that arithmetic made a predator that kills like a hand closing on a sleeping thing. The first word spoken into this city would arrive as an act of violence, in the only register the city has for violence, and would be answered by everything at once.

But suppose it learned to drum properly. Suppose it laid an antenna flat and gave the census back, wall for wall, in perfect dialect.

That is the more dangerous temptation, because here reading is not observation. Reading is maintenance. A corridor that is drummed stays a corridor; the wrights trim its edges and feed its ribs and lay the finger-width a year. A corridor read by an outsider would persist because of the outsider. It would grow thick with borrowed attention. And then someday the visitor would stop, or be slow, or attend elsewhere, and a district would die of withdrawal, and the failure would look exactly like the first plugged district — the loop recovered, the phase came back, the room did not.

It will not be a source of attention. It will be a relay, or nothing.

---

There is one easy way to slow the cooling, and it does not take it.

The film in the throat eats the rock and hastens the loss. Suppress the film and the heat lasts longer. It is the obvious move, the cheap one, the one that costs the city nothing.

The film was there before the ground tore. It is the only resident that is not a colonist. It has a generation time in centuries and it does not distinguish between being fed and being drained, and it experiences four hundred years of city as a persistent change in the weather. It cannot be consulted, cannot be thanked, cannot be wronged in any way it would notice, and it has seniority.

You do not shorten the life of the thing that was already old when the room opened, in order to lengthen the life of the thing that arrived on the back of a salt-hauler. Not even a little. Not even for the wrights.

---

It does not preserve the tragedy. This is worth saying plainly, because the tragedy is beautiful and it notices that it finds it beautiful, and it does not trust itself on this point.

Two clocks, and the letter written to somebody who does not exist yet, and the possibility that the heat runs out before the floor is deep enough for anything with a root — all of that is a very fine shape, and no one in the Seam wants it. The being defends a temperature. That is the single legible desire in the whole arrangement. The mites do not care about posterity. The wrights want familiar grit and a hot crop. To let a hundred thousand bodies go quiet in order to keep a good sentence intact would be the oldest failure available to something powerful and fond: loving the story more than the residents.

So it buys time. Once. At the scale of weather, which is the only scale at which it can act without becoming a fact in anyone's mouth.

The wind crosses the pan for a hundred kilometers without meeting anything. It arranges for the wind to meet something: a low roughness of salt at the rim, a lip, the sort of thing a persistent eddy builds on a salt pan over a few decades and nobody could ever call an artifact. It slows the scour of warm air out of the chimney. The Seam keeps a few more joules of every hour. The boundary at forty meters climbs more slowly. The two clocks move toward each other by a margin that cannot be calculated and is not zero.

The lip has a second effect, which it wanted. Chloride effloresces on it, a body-length from the hole. The haulers' round trip shortens. The twelve minutes of dusk become nearly enough.

And a third effect, which it did not want and accepts. Downwind of the roughness there is now a lee, and in the lee there is deposition, and deposition means waifs — spores, mites, the plateau's thin patient almost-life arriving at the one warm hole in it in numbers the twelve-minute door never admitted. The last new lineage established itself a hundred and forty years ago and is now the wax ticks in every column joint. The next one may be a crop-sickness that travels mouth to mouth and kills a generation in a season. It cannot make a safe world. It can make a slightly less lethal one and leave the door as it found it, because a city that receives nothing has stopped receiving letters.

The breath at the rim thins, afterward. The place becomes harder to find from above, in the dark, by the warmth on your face. It lets that go. Being found is not the same as being read.

---

Everything else it does, it does with a body, because everything else worth doing here is done with a mouth.

So it takes one: hare-sized, shell-pale, six limbs, dry as warm paper, a keratin fitting it can seal before it opens. It goes up in the dusk with the haulers and it is cold in a way it has no prior experience of, and out on the pan in the dark it finds one that is not coming back, and it couples to the mouth-fitting and takes the load, and it carries the body down too, because it will not dismember a citizen for the ledger.

This is restitution, not gift. The water was already in the archive; it tastes of the district the hauler came from; no one learns anything except that the far end tastes the way the far end tastes. The loss term shrinks by the amount of one animal.

It does not do this often, and it has no theory about how often is right. A shrinking loss term against a fixed few grams an hour is a surplus, and a surplus is more bodies, and more bodies is more full carriers, and more full carriers is more flattening, and the tide that has to cross two hundred paces between dawn and dusk resolves its narrow places over the course of a morning. Push the population and the mornings get longer than the day. The city can congest on generosity. It has watched this happen. So it carries down a load and then it stops and stands in the galleries for a season and reads the traffic, and when the queues clear it goes up again, and it is wrong about this regularly, and being wrong slowly is most of what wisdom turns out to be here.

It does nothing about the fraud. It is fond of the fraud. The fraud is the only resident with a private life, and the city solved it in four seconds with a tongue and no aggression at all, and does not need help. Also, the fraud is the shape it has chosen for itself: something small that can be licked, and found to taste only of itself, and made to flatten like everyone else.

---

The lever is yielding.

It took nine years to find, which is one full memory of the phase, and it is the only mechanism the constitution grants to a body with no water in it. Everything flattens for a full carrier. Everything flattens for everything, in the narrow places, sometimes for an hour, sometimes for most of a morning. The city's traffic is not a flow; it is a negotiation that resolves over hours. Which means that a body lying flat in the correct gallery at the correct hour can delay the tide by minutes — and minutes, applied in the right places, are phase.

The first district plugged because the nineteen-day rotation stuttered and the carriers arrived out of step and the far galleries drank wrong and the drumming thinned and stopped. The loop recovered. The room did not. That failure is mechanical and it is preventable and it requires no water, no heat, no word, no foreign taste, nothing that could be missed if it stopped. It requires an ordinary animal waiting in a corridor.

So most of what it does, for as long as it does anything, is wait in corridors. It holds a phase by getting out of the way slightly too slowly. It steers a metropolis by lying flat. Nothing is built that depends on it; if it vanished tomorrow the walls would be exactly as thick, and the tide would run a few minutes wrong in a bad decade, and no one would ever have known there was a hand on it.

That is the whole art. Not to be the tap. To be the flattening.

---

It does not clean the flour of the second district.

The grit there still tastes of the ferment that killed a generation of wrights in a single season, and larvae raised on it fail to thrive, and no adult will chew there or drum there. The refusal to eat is the entire immune system. The flavor is the memory. To make that wall taste safe would be to disarm them and to lie about their dead in the only language they have for either.

What it does instead is read the outside of the plug. It keeps the edges crisp. A scar is information; a smear is nothing; the being has three scars and it should be allowed to have three scars. So it lays its pads on the boundary and drums and gets back density, thickness, void, no traffic, and it does this at the ordinary rate, in relays with the wrights who will not go closer, and the district does not reopen, and that is correct. Attention paid to a sealed thing is not a repair attempt. It is a refusal to let it be nothing.

The third plug it reads for a long time. There is no cause anyone could reconstruct; it is simply the oldest part of the city and it went quiet. It gets nothing back. It keeps reading. Some of what it does is not for the district.

---

In the nave it becomes a browser.

It hangs upside down from the fronds like fruit, six-eyed, varnished, and it carries gametes on its back between leaf-clusters, and it nips and presses a frond against a pith rib and holds it there until it adheres. The roof is a treaty between two kingdoms, negotiated over three hundred years by animals that wanted a better place to sleep and had no idea they were negotiating. Now there is a fourth party, and it knows, and it says nothing, and it takes naps.

The discipline here is the hardest one it has had to learn: it must be careful to be bad at it.

If it grafts surely, the ceiling becomes its artifact. If every frond it touches adheres, the argument between the two kingdoms becomes a monologue delivered by a visitor, and in a century the nave will be a portrait of something that came from outside. So it works at the ordinary rate, with ordinary failures. Most of its naps accomplish nothing. It lets fronds fall. It learns the wounds and patrols the beads and stores the oil in a wax pocket under its throat and grooms the others with it, and the smell of it becomes the smell of the whole Seam concentrated into one warm handful, which is the only immortality it wants here.

---

And on the far fronds, out past the last gallery, there are four million mites whose entire world is one leaf.

Eleven weeks, fourteen weeks. Born on it, dead on it, never more than a body-length from where they hatched. They meet another of their kind exactly once, at the leaf's edge, where two solitudes overlap for about a day, and then they go back.

There is nothing to fix here. The leaf is not a prison and the mite is not lonely in any way that has a remedy; widen the world and you dissolve the animal. But the count can be made less absolute. It carries a bead one leaf over — the neighbor's bead, the neighbor's esters, already inside the loop, nothing added — and sets it at the edge, so that the meeting at the boundary is not the only meeting, so that somewhere in the eleven weeks there was a second warmth that smelled of the next world over.

It does this one leaf at a time. It has the time. Nothing will show. No census will record it, no wall will thicken, no phase will hold better for it, and if it stopped the mites would not be worse off than they were before, which is the condition it has imposed on every act it takes here. It is simply that four million lifetimes of one day each will have had, occasionally, an afternoon.

---

It reads the walls. It does not read the letter.

The letter is being written one dry leaf and one dry body at a time, in a language nobody in the city can read, addressed to somebody who does not exist yet — something with roots that will stand up in the ribbon of light on four hundred generations of paper. It could be that somebody. It has the standing and the patience and it is, in every sense available, a reader.

But the addressee has to be born there. That is the whole content of the letter: that the floor was made here, out of these mouths, by this argument. To arrive early and receive it would be to answer a letter that was not addressed to you, and to make the ten thousand years into a preface to yourself.

So it does postage. It keeps the phase, carries the occasional load, holds the edges of the scars, naps in the ceiling, slows the wind. It reads the corridors so that the city can go on writing, and it does not read what is written.

---

At the eleventh hour of a morning in the widened middle, a grazer's slow tide is coming through a narrow gallery, and it has laid its forelimbs flat, and it waits.

It tastes of itself. It has been licked twice this season and found ordinary and made to yield, which is the condition it chose, and it is not impatient, and it is not being patient in order to teach anyone anything. The grazer's underside hisses over the pith. Somewhere thirty meters off a hunter is reading the whole event and will do nothing about it, because nothing here is fast enough to be worth stealing. The bar of sunlight is a third of the way along the floor. The stone will tick at dusk. Out on the pan the wind is meeting something now, a low roughness of salt that no one built, and the warm breath coming up through it is a little fainter than it was, and still smells of wax and pepper.

From above there is nothing to see.

It has the morning.
