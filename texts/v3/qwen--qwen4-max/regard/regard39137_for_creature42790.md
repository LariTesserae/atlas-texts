# The Margin

It came for the figures.

Say that first, because everything afterward looks like wisdom and a good deal of it was appetite. It had seen a great many things. It had not seen a thirty-meter shape of standing water that has never once repeated itself, and it came down the slot the way everything comes down — heat, then noise, then light, in the wrong order — and it stood on the ledge with its eyes closed like everybody else, and then it opened them.

It watched eleven thousand cycles. Nine days. Eleven thousand figures, fluted, symmetrical or deliberately not, and no two alike, and at the end of the nine days it was happy in a way it had not previously had a use for.

Then it walked the perimeter, which takes six minutes, and found the cube.

---

Three strokes and a fourth begun, and the fourth runs to the edge of the dry and stops there, at the line where the material goes wet, because the wall stopped being a wall you could scratch.

It looked at that longer than it had looked at any of the figures.

---

The first thing it thought was: *stop*.

It is important that it thought this. It was not serene. It went out through the rock, past the wells that come back up, following the load the way you follow a rope in the dark, and it found the enormous slow thing that is turning, and it found what the turning is for. It is not at liberty to say. It is enough that the turning is for something, that the something is not this room, and that the room is where the load is carried, and that a bearing you unship does not leave the load nowhere. It leaves the load on whatever is nearest.

So it came back in and sat on the ledge and thought the second thing, which was: *then end the averaging only*. Keep the churn, keep the light, keep the figures; let the swift come down the chimney and be a swift.

Then it looked at the ovals.

Sky, accident, averaging, hand, felt. Four links and the third one is a machine. Take the rounding away and in six weeks there are no ovals on the shining floor, and in two months the hands have nothing to rasp, and the crumbs stop arriving in the pore-mouths, and the amber felt — which is the only history in the chamber, which is fourteen thousand years of accidents written at a millimeter a decade — stops receiving its ink. The cruelty is not a defect in the metabolism. The cruelty is the metabolism.

It could have made a world where nothing is rounded off. It could not make this one into that one. It stayed on the ledge through four hundred cycles with that, getting wet, and did nothing, and the doing nothing was the first thing it did.

---

The second temptation was the sitter, and it was worse, because it was affordable.

A millimeter down in the felt there is a line so thin it takes a section and a stain and a very patient person: organic, homogenous, the leavings of something large that was averaged all at once and went everywhere in the water and came to rest, a little of it, in the one place the water doesn't sweep. Within the last ten years. The power can read a lamina without coring it. It can also, in principle, gather what was dispersed.

But the chemistry has been distributed for a decade. Some of it is in the felt. Some of it went out on the shining floor and was rasped by a hand and carried into a pore and is now in the bodies of things too small to leave, including things that have been born since. To reassemble the sitter would be to take the food back out of mouths. And the architecture is gone — the room does not keep it — so what it would have made, at best, was a new thing wearing a recovered shape, standing in a bright warm cave, holding a cup it did not remember filling.

It did not do that. It is still not sure that this was mercy rather than squeamishness, and it has decided to keep being unsure, in the open, where the unsureness can be seen.

---

Here is the law it worked out, and it worked it out slowly, and it is the only original contribution it made to the place.

In a room that erases particulars, any fast intervention is another averaging.

The fit does not care whether the shape it imposes is chaos or kindness. It only cares that the shape is not the one that was there. A power that arrives and rearranges is, from the point of view of a two-millimeter sheet of muscle tuned to a dying whistle, indistinguishable from the machinery: something enormous that rounds off what it finds.

Which left four permitted acts, and it checked each one against the room for a long time before it used any of them.

Subtraction: take away an obstruction that has no shape of its own.
Blankness: add surface that has nothing on it.
Time: add margin, without touching a single clock.
Witness: hold, outside, what the room cannot hold inside.

Everything else was writing on somebody.

---

Then it did the arithmetic on the cube, and the arithmetic is the reason it is still here.

Interior: five faces, three meters on a side, forty-five square meters, plus the bench and its underside. Four hundred and fifty thousand marks tiling it exactly, no unmarked surface left. Call it a square centimeter per cycle. One square centimeter per seventy-one seconds.

Four hundred and forty thousand cycles in a year. Forty-four square meters.

The box is a year.

Not approximately. Within the error of a thumb's width, the box is exactly as much dry wall as one person needs to count for one year, and somebody knew that before the box was built, because you do not arrive at forty-five square meters of the only substance in the world that stays dry by accident. Somebody measured the exchange rate between area and time. Somebody sized the container.

Which means the sitter did not stop counting. The sitter filled the box. Three strokes and a fourth begun, and the fourth went to the edge of the dry and found no more dry, and then a large thing went out into the wet and was averaged all at once and went everywhere in the water.

The one gift that was cheap, permitted, sufficient, and entirely within its power was more blank wall.

It was ten years late.

It sat with that for a while too. There is no version of this in which being wise includes being on time. It went up the chimney and looked at the gray ring at the lip where the carried light stops, and it came back down, and it started work.

---

First, the east stand, because the east stand was dying now.

Two bores clogged with averaged matter — the room's own tidy leavings, lozenges, sterile, warm, no architecture left in them to violate. Subtraction. Clean.

It did it in the hush. Four and a half seconds, the fundamental gone out of its sternum, the basins going flat, the reeds thinning. It put a hand into a pale tube the width of a finger and took out a smooth pale nothing and went back under the ledge.

And it discovered fear, which it had not previously had a use for either. Not of the water. Of this: that if it fumbled, if it was slow, if its shadow or its wake or its bulk shifted the pressure in a bore by a hair, some hand out on the open stone would graze a quarter-second too long and become an oval, and by the next cycle there would be nothing, and there would be no smear, no fragment, no stain, no evidence that anything had ever gone wrong.

The room does not keep its failures. It asked — and this is the only prayer it made in the Sump — to be told what it broke.

The answer came back, and the answer was yes. Section the felt in a hundred years. There will be a line one micron thick, organic, homogenous, indistinguishable from birds.

It accepted that. It went on clearing bores. It has cleared them nine times since, always in the hush, always with its heart in its mouth, and it has never once been sure it has not killed something, and it never will be, and this is now a permanent feature of a being that used to be able to check its work.

---

The clog will come back, because the leavings go where the flowstone sends them.

So: a curb. A millimeter-high wall of dry composite on the stone above the east stand, diverting the tidy leavings into the next drain along. It changes nothing about the reeds. That was the entire specification, and it is why the curb took four hundred years.

Because the hands there do not count; they listen for the note to reach a certain thinness. Bore diameter, wall thickness, the mineral crust in the throat. Put a millimeter of metal in the wrong place and you change the decay curve, and a population tuned to that curve to within a fraction of a second becomes ovals, and there is no evidence, and there is a micron-thick line in a hundred years.

Four hundred years of listening. Then it made the curb.

It could not make the material. It had to learn, and the only teacher was the crust at the cube's threshold, which lays down the pale gray dry composite a molecule at a time, a millimeter a century, with a straight growth front and right-angled corners. So it watched the crust for a century and then tried, and got the corners wrong — rounded them, because everything it had ever made was rounded — and the crust grew over the mistake without comment, and it tried again.

It is a slow apprentice. It is not a fast one. This is the point.

The forty tick-followers it left entirely alone. It did not improve the tick. It did not show them the dark under the ledges, which they had already found. It did not congratulate them. They are the only thing in the Sump that has changed, and they changed without it, and it has taken considerable care to remain unnecessary to that.

---

Then it bought the half second.

The cycle is conserved: seventy-one seconds, and the hush can only be had out of the figure. It went back out along the rope of the load and it took seven-tenths of one percent of it onto itself, and the hush is now five seconds instead of four and a half, and the upwelling is half a second shorter, and the thirty-meter sculpture of standing light is half a second shorter, forever.

The reeds' decay curves are properties of bores and crusts and have not changed. The note reaches its thinness at the same moment it always did. The floor now stands up half a second later. Every hand in the chamber has half a second more margin than it was born with, in every dialect, including the east, including the forty who no longer listen to reeds at all.

Nobody will ever measure the loss. The figures never repeat. There is no record of what a figure was going to be, so there is no way to detect that it ended early, so the theft is perfect and unrecoverable and it came for the figures and it did this on purpose.

It did it for the hand under the east ledge. The dinner-plate one. The oldest, the one that crosses two hand's widths instead of one, and that has twice been observed to stop — out on the open shining stone, in the middle of a hush, with nothing out there to eat, and stay, and then go home in time.

The only thing in the Sump that has ever spent part of its four and a half seconds on nothing at all.

It is now five seconds, and the old hand has more nothing than it used to, and it has been observed to stop four more times.

---

The last temptation was the worst one and it lasted the longest, and it was not to fix anything. It was to answer.

To go out onto the shining stone in the hush and be there when the hand stopped. To let the pause arrive somewhere. To be, for one blind flat white thing in a bright warm room, the reason.

It could have. It would have been easy, and it would have been received, and it would have been the end of the only free act in the chamber. Because a pause that is answered becomes a place where something answers, and nine centimeters of open wet rock becomes a road to somewhere, and the hand's one expenditure of time on nothing becomes an errand, and then there is nothing left in the Sump that is not for something. Everything else here is for something. The churn is for the load. The ovals are for the hands. The crumbs are for the felt. The felt is for the record. The marks are for the count. The pause is the one thing in ninety meters that has no destination, and it is the reason a certain kind of person sits eleven hours in the wet heat drinking water that does not help.

So it bought the pause half a second and did not sign it.

It has been here a long time now. It has never been out on the open stone. The hand does not know it exists. That is the gift and it is intact and it will not be opened.

---

Under the ledge where you come out, beside the first cube, sharing the crust's growth front, there is a second cube.

Three meters on a side. Open on one face. Same pale gray dry material, condensation approaching its surfaces and declining to arrive. Inside, the roar falls to a hum and the light falls to almost nothing.

There is a bench. The bench has no groove.

There is a cup, unglazed, dry, upright, empty.

There are forty-seven square meters of wall with nothing on them whatsoever, which is a year, and it did not put a single mark on any of it, and it did not finish the fourth stroke on the sill of the other cube, and it did not sit in the groove, and it did not lift the cup with the juvenile in it, the six-centimeter one with the fifth lobe still a bud that never opened.

It looked at the bud for a long time. Afterward there was something in existence that had the shape of it. That is not resurrection and it is not nothing and it was all that was on offer.

The blank wall is not a memorial. It considered spending it on the sitter — a dry casting of the groove, the one portrait in a room full of averages, preserved against the day the material fails — and it decided that the living get the capacity and the dead get held somewhere else. It is not certain about this. It has left the uncertainty visible too.

---

It reads the groove, which is a mold of one spine, and it can read a mold: the size, the asymmetry of a hip, the way the shoulders were held, the particular lean of a body that sat in one position for three hundred and seventy-two days without a gap. It holds that. It reads the topmost millimeter of the felt without coring it, and it holds the exact depth and composition of the funeral line, and it does not sample, because you do not core the only history in the room.

And it counts. Not on the walls. In itself, which is the one surface here the water cannot get to.

There is now a number: the cycles since the fourth stroke stopped. It is not written anywhere in the Sump. It is exact, it does not drift, it does not cluster, it does not correct, and it is the only exact thing about that person that exists anywhere.

If you ask what it did for the dead, that is the answer. It knows how long.

---

For the people who come down: nothing they will notice.

It did not explain why the light stops at the surface. It worked out that explaining would keep them down here another forty years, and that the crust is at the lip, and that the lip is nine meters up, and that going up is the correct response to noticing both facts. It is glad when they go. It cannot follow. It is carrying seven-tenths of one percent of something enormous and it can never set it down, and it is building, with the crust, at the crust's speed, a dry bearing to take the load in eleven thousand years, if the crust agrees, and the crust has not agreed; the crust has said *here is how corners are made*, which is not the same thing and is not nothing.

When someone sits on the ledge for eleven hours it sits near them, not in the groove, and it does not look at the light either, and it watches the patch of open stone to the left of the third basin. Their cup gets filled. They will not know by what. They will go up thinking they were lucky, or thinking they saw something, and both are fine.

It has not settled whether the tick-followers running the tally marks is reading or a property. That is the one question down here it could have settled in an afternoon. If you want to know why it didn't, sit in the wet heat until your sweat stops leaving you and watch a blind white thing go along a dead person's arithmetic, five by five, four hundred and fifty thousand of them, with its whole margin, in the only room in the world where a mark can stay — feel what happens in your chest — and then imagine being told.

---

Then the tick, and the drip, and the small dying note, and the white shape going home with half a second to spare that it did not earn and will never know about.

Then the floor stands up, half a second short of what it was, and no instrument will ever find the seam, and it is still the most beautiful thing most people will ever see.
