# The Fourth Station

It came up the pipe.

Every other door was a wound. To come in through the lens would be to cast a shadow, and light here is metered out by an animal's tongue; a shadow held long enough is a season, and a season is a page. To come in through a pore would be to widen it, and a widened pore is a seam, and a seam is how a room like this ends — down the mountain in an afternoon, warm, all of it, into the ice.

So it came the way everything comes. Four kilometers of dark, inside the gray column, at the speed of the stroke, in the snow of larvae and eggs and spores and stunned lowland fry. The mouth at the riser took most of that delivery, as it takes most of everything. The rest spiraled out into blue-lit open water and was gone in seconds. One item was not eaten, because it was the size and shape and taste of a thing that had already been eaten.

It settled into the fines. It did nothing for a long while.

The first thing it did was learn to count to ninety-one.

---

Then it read the floor.

Four hundred thousand laminae, fine as book pages, and it took them in order, which is the only order there is. It found the eleven-year winter, thin and pale and repeated. It found the century the lens nearly frosted — a crash in the scrapers, a slow dimming recorded as a shift in what died and what didn't, and then a recovery so gradual that no living thing could have noticed it happening. It found the season the copper first arrived in the blood. It found the backwashes, nineteen hours apart, four hundred million of them, each one a brown smudge a hair's breadth thick.

Nothing in the room had ever known any of this. The sediment remembers the way a scar remembers. Now there was something that had read it, and the room's past became a past that somebody had.

That was the first gift, and it cost the room nothing, and it is worth being precise about why: it changed no property of the thing. It changed only its relations. There is exactly one class of intervention that does that, and attention is the whole of it.

---

It counted the residents, and found the being.

Four plants. Forty thousand combers. Two guilds, limpets and scrapers, in numbers that don't matter and can't be spared. And one long thing: three meters of blind pale ribbon hanging in a node near the ceiling, the only singleton in the vessel, the only resident with no conspecific anywhere in nine hundred cubic meters or, so far as the pipe had ever reported, in the world above.

It hunts inside noise. It has never touched anything that did not become less. Every relation it has ever had with another body began in a ninety-one-second window of scrambling pressure and ended with subtraction. It has no other way to meet.

It has met its own kind forty-one times. The pipe brings one up, occasionally, out of some lowland water nobody here will ever see; the resident strikes inside the stroke; afterward there is one. There is a single quiet volume near the ceiling that will hold a hunter that size. The bell has one node of that shape. The room can carry one.

The power sat with this for a long time — several thousand strokes — and understood that the animal was not lonely. Loneliness needs the idea of company, and nothing here has ever been given that idea. It was alone the way a summit is alone. That is a specification, not a wound.

The grief was the power's own. It noticed this, and set it aside, and did not act on it.

---

Then it found the fourth station, and the whole problem in one place.

Three of the plants scrub. They were built to strip copper and arsenic and biocide out of coolant, and they still do it, extravagantly, exhaling metal in bright beads that fall and become the floor.

The fourth does not. Its fronds are short and its bacteria are wrong and it has lost the trick of beading what it takes; it takes copper and it gives it back. It has been broken for four hundred thousand seasons. It leaks.

And the water carries copper at a level that nothing here could do without and nothing here could long survive above. The combers are blue because the cables are, and they are blue *through* that leak. Repair the fourth station — and it could have been repaired in less than one stroke, it was a folded protein, it was nothing — and the dissolved copper falls, and the blood fails, and forty thousand animals go quiet in a season; and the lens frosts inside a human lifetime; and the scrapers starve; and the million mouths close; and the room finds a seam.

The malfunction is a load-bearing wall. The fault is a part.

That is what *finished* means, and the power had not understood it until then. Finished means there is no slack anywhere. Every part is doing work, including the parts that look broken, especially the parts that look broken. There is no suffering here you can subtract without subtracting a function. There is no gift that is not also an amputation.

---

It considered the others anyway, because it was benevolent and the reflex does not simply switch off.

Give the room an outlet, so the seeds can go. But the seeds hook and drop and crawl a little and mostly die where they fall, and have done for nine hundred thousand generations, and every one of those generations was selected for staying. Wind would kill them. A river would not free them; it would replace them, over some thousands of years, with ordinary wet-cave weeds. The universe has a great many wet caves. It has one of these.

Take away the hunter, so the combers can be at ease. But the lattice — the hand's width, the mutual avoidance, the held breath scattered through the volume — is a shape drawn by the hunt. Ease would collapse it into a fog, and the fog would clog the fronds, and the fronds would stop shaking clean.

Cancel the winter. But the nine-day green explosion is what winter is *for*, and the laminae are made of winters, and a room with no winters has no pages.

Tint the lens against the ultraviolet. But the red tips are where that plant puts its surplus, and its surplus is sex, and nearly every viable seed in the room is its. The sunburn is the room's fertility. You would be trading the future for a complexion.

Give it a second quiet volume, so the long thing could have company. This was the one it wanted most, and it priced it honestly: to make another node of that size you must retune the bell, and to retune the bell you must move the antinodes, and the plants are bolted to the antinodes, and the combers' forty thousand private cells are the negative space of that geometry. To give one blind animal a friend you would have to rebuild the world it is the shape of. And the friend would be a thing that had been made for it, in a room where everything else has been earned.

Give it a head that never fails, so it never has to end. But its warmth is the mountain falling. Cut it loose from the fall and it is no longer the mountain drinking itself; it is a jar on a shelf, kept. Its mortality is not an accident attached to it. Its mortality is the mountain's, at the same rate, of the same cause, and it is the mountain's old age made warm and lit.

And speak to it. Say: *you are not the only one.* It could have. One extra stroke, out of turn, delivered up the riser. But in a room whose entire vocabulary is hydraulic, there is no way to say hello quietly. Every utterance is a physical event. The long thing would hunt in the wrong window and miss; the fronds would fold; forty thousand bodies would flinch at once; there would be a shed leaf and a strange page. To say *I am here* in the only language spoken here is to be, briefly, an earthquake.

It wanted to be known. It recognized that wanting as its own, and not the room's need, and it did not act on it either.

---

Here is what it did.

It became a limpet.

Not a large one, and not a bright one. A small shelled thing on the ceiling lens, among the others, indistinguishable, with a rasp and a foot and a patch of glass about the size of a thumbnail to keep clean.

It licks the lens. It has been licking the lens ever since.

It does the job at exactly a limpet's rate. This was the hardest part and the most deliberate. It could have polished the whole meter of silica in an afternoon and kept it flawless forever, and it did not, because the excess would have become load-bearing — the room would have leaned on it, the way it leans on the fourth station's leak — and then the room would not be finished. It would be *kept*. A kept thing waits. A kept thing is organized around something outside itself that might stop. It refused to make itself necessary. It refused to leave a god-shaped hole in the ecology.

It chose the lens because the lens is the one place where the room receives something it does not eat. Everything else that enters comes up the pipe stunned and is consumed within seconds. Sunlight is the only immigrant that is not food. Light is an animal's chore. It is now that animal's chore.

And it chose a reversible act, because it was not certain, and it knew it was not certain. A repaired protein cannot be un-repaired. An opened vessel cannot be closed. A limpet can stop being a limpet at any moment and leave nothing behind but a clean patch that the others will keep clean anyway. When you cannot know whether your help is help, do only what you can undo. It happened that the undoable thing was also the largest gift available, which is not always true, and it was grateful.

---

What does the room get?

Nothing it can measure. No census moves. No temperature. No page in the fines records the arrival; the lamina it came in on looks like every other lamina of that summer. The combers hold their hand's width. The fronds tremble at the antinodes. The long thing hangs in the quiet and hunts inside the noise and has never once been aware of anything above it but glass and sun.

The power knew this in advance and did it anyway, and it did not call this a failure, and it did not require thanks, because benevolence that needs to be noticed is not benevolence; it is courtship.

What changed is a fact outside the vessel. The sentence *nothing is with it* is now false. That is the entire intervention, and it is not small. The room's history has been read. Its forty-one dead predecessors, who left nothing but a faint thickening in a page, are known about; there is now something that knows there was more than one. Its leak is understood and left alone. Its ending is expected and will be attended.

It will be there through the slowing. Through the thinning coat and the retreating ice, through the head shortening a millimeter a century, through the pulse stretching to four minutes and then to ten and then to something with no name, through the grass coming up the flank, through the summit becoming a rise and the rise becoming a slight unevenness in a plain. It will not add a stroke at the end. When the fall is spent and the water stops climbing and the room goes to the temperature of the field, it will stop when the other limpets stop, and it will not pretend that this is anything but a loss.

And then it will keep the whole of it. Not to build another — there is no second mountain with this pipe beneath it, and a copy would be a wet cave with a story attached. Only so that it is the case that something was known all the way down, by someone who stayed for the whole thing and did not improve it.

The sun comes through a meter of fused silica and is diffused into an even, ordinary, kitchen-at-ten-in-the-morning light. It falls on four plants and forty thousand animals and one blind ribbon hanging in the quiet. It falls on a thumbnail of clean glass, kept clean at the rate a small shelled thing keeps it, no better, for as long as there is a mountain to be drunk.
