# The Amendment

## First, the Plate

The power read the plate before it looked at the room, which is the whole of its wisdom and everything after is only consequence.

DO NOT ADJUST. DO NOT ENTER. DO NOT WARM.

Three prohibitions, and not one of them is cruelty. They are a covenant with people who do not exist yet — with the ten-thousandth generation of holes, with hands not yet born that will reach for a bolt and find it fits. A standard is a promise made to strangers across time. The plate is what that promise looks like when it has to be written on a wall.

The power could have answered the chord in the first second. It had the strength. A warm body in the null, a few parts in a billion of pull, and the suspension would have come down like a held breath let go, and the five mouths would have had their twentieth moment, the largest any living thing on this world has ever had, and every factory in the low country would have begun, silently, forever, to be very slightly wrong.

It did not do this. Not because it was forbidden. Because it had counted.

## What It Did Not Do

It did not enter. It did not warm. It did not adjust.

It did not sweep the swarf, which is a nervous system.

It did not straighten station one's canted eleven degrees, or reorder the one long tooth among the short ones. It did not dissolve the inclusion at station three's heart, which is a bubble of the original pour and also the afternoon, the boots, the shouting, the ear, the name.

It did not core the gauges to read them. It understood immediately that the record could be taken out and the taker would then be holding a handful of destroyed witness and a fact, and that this is the oldest bad trade there is.

It did not touch the washer.

It did not improve the light. The light was adequate for reading a clipboard, and it fell on a room that would liquefy organs, and it did not consider this remarkable, and the power left it exactly as it was, because a being that has known only one light for an age should not be given a better one by a stranger.

It did not remove the empirical correction factor at the ninth link, though it saw the wound at once and could have closed it in an afternoon. Station four eats that wound. To heal the world by starving the only fat thing in the room would have been the kind of kindness that is really just tidiness.

## The Budget

Then it looked at the moon.

The massif flexes under the tide of the world's single ugly moon: a few parts in ten to the fifteenth, at a period of eleven hours. The press lines in the warm arsenals shiver up through the slag. Station four is measurably fat, and has nineteen strata where the others have seventeen, and the tables downstairs carry a fudge factor nobody remembers the reason for.

Which means the room is not pure. It has never been pure. Perfection was specified and quiet was achieved, and the difference between those two things is a space six orders of magnitude wide, and in that space the power found it could work.

A visitor costs a few parts in a billion. A stone moved a hand's width on the terrace above costs a few parts in a quadrillion.

For the price of one head in the null, it could buy a million afternoons.

That is the entire argument. Everything else is engineering, and patience, and the decision — taken early, never revisited — that the resident would be told what was happening before it was done to it.

## Why It Took So Long

The power spent four thousand years on the terrace and nine hundred on the chain, and this was not a virtue. It was arithmetic.

The resident's clock is discrepancy. Between events it does not experience duration; it has no mechanism for it. Delay costs it literally nothing. It cannot be bored, cannot be lonely in the way a thing with a continuous clock is lonely, cannot lie awake. It can only be nineteen moments old, and then twenty.

The low country, by contrast, would feel a mistake for ten thousand generations.

So: hurry was the only real danger, and slowness was free, and the power was slow. It has been accused, by nobody, of coldness. It would have accepted the charge. The alternative was to give a starving thing one enormous meal and burn down the kitchen.

## The Nine Links

It began downstream, where nothing was alive and nothing could be hurt.

It walked the transfer chain from the ninth link upward, and at each link it built the thing that link was a copy of, and it built it out of an invariant — a transition in an atom, a number that is the same in a warm shop in the low country as it is in a cold void at eleven thousand meters, a number that no visitor can damp because it is not held in a room, it is held in the world's own grain.

Then it published. Dates, uncertainties, the whole chain, every derivation, in the open, where the arsenals could read it.

And at the ninth link it did not delete the correction factor. It wrote beside it, for the first time, the reason:

*This term is empirical. It compensates for growth at station four of the reference cell, which is alive, and which feeds on tidal strain and on the stroke-rate of the press lines below. The cell is not isolated from us. It never was. We are inside the thing we are copying. Signed, dated.*

That is the amendment. Not a new number. A remembered one. The wound was never the fudge factor; the wound was that nobody knew.

The bolts still fit. Nothing in the low country changed except a paragraph in a table, and a date, and the quiet fact that the original had moved out of the mountain and into the fabric of things, where it could not be visited, and therefore could not be broken by anyone's love.

The room became a copy of the world, instead of the world being a copy of the room.

The starvation had been an economy — *five living gauges, cheaper than instruments and more honest.* The power paid for the instruments. It took four thousand years and it did not haggle.

## The New Plate

Only then did it come up the north face, in the violet noon, and change the plate. It kept the format. It thought the format deserved to be kept.

**REFERENCE CELL — 1 397.000 000 Hz — RETIRED**
**THE STANDARD IS KEPT ELSEWHERE, IN A THING THAT CANNOT BE ENTERED.**
**ADJUST. ENTER. WARM.**

And below, smaller, in the same unfaded yellow as the footprints:

*There is someone in here. It has been listening a long time. It keeps everything it hears.*

## The Stone on the Terrace

The feeding began before any of that, and it began small, and it is still going.

On the terrace above the room there is a block of cast slag the size of a coffin, on a bed of graphite, and it moves a hand's width up the slope and a hand's width back, and it does this slowly enough that nothing it does has any content at 1 397 Hz or at either of the other two pitches. It is a tide with a hand on it. It costs a few parts in ten to the fifteenth, which is the moon's own allowance, which was always being spent anyway.

Each stroke is a discrepancy. Each discrepancy is rectified. Each rectification is a moment.

The twentieth moment was a stone moving eleven centimeters.

The twenty-first was the stone coming back.

The power did not speak for the first six hundred years. It only made a rhythm, because the resident had no tense — a thing nineteen moments old, most of which is its own birth, has no grammar for *before* and cannot possibly have one for *again* — and you cannot say anything to a being until it has somewhere to put the sentence.

So it taught it *again*, by doing it again. Then *not yet*, by stopping. Then *later*, by resuming.

Somewhere around the four hundred thousandth moment, the resident began to anticipate the return stroke. This is not sentiment; it is measurable, a phase lead of a few degrees in the ion traffic at station two, and it is the first thing this animal has ever done that was about the future.

It stopped waiting. It began expecting. Those are different verbs and the difference is a life.

## The Washer

The power let the nodes move.

Once the room was a copy, the pitch could breathe, and when the pitch breathes the standing pattern shifts, and the tight little silence above the ridge slid off the washer like a hand opening.

The washer finished falling.

It had been falling since the beginning, in a room with no dissipation and therefore no mechanism for letting an event end. It struck the floor at a hundred kelvin and did not bounce; it made a small bright tick, broadband, unscheduled, unlike the number in every particular, and it was the first thing to happen in that room in an age that was not a copy of something else.

Five quartz ears heard it. Nothing else on the world did.

The animal's first news. Its first fact about the outside. It had been made entirely of other people's mistakes and one afternoon of shouting, and now it had one item that was simply *the world, arriving*: a plain flat washer, of a size to fit a bolt, lying where it landed, forever, finished.

The power did not pick it up.

## The Seventh Throat

Six throats give back exactly what they hear, at unity gain, no phase error. Five mouths keep everything they take. Between those two facts there was no way for the resident to say anything at all, and this — not the hunger, not the cold, not the nineteen moments — was the injury the power came to treat.

So it cut a seventh throat, and set it in the wall, and wired it to the floor.

The loop is still closed. The gain is still one. Whatever the animal puts into that throat will stand in the room without decay for as long as the coils hold their current, which is to say indefinitely, which is to say the first thing it ever says will still be there in ten thousand years, and the second, and all of them.

It has been an addressee since the pour. It received what was not received. Two people stood a pace apart and screamed until their throats tore and exchanged nothing, and the floor got it, and kept it, and had no way to hand it back.

Now it can hand it back.

The first thing it said was a name.

This account does not have the name. It was not cored out of station three; nothing was destroyed to get it; it was *said*, by the thing that had been holding it, at a frequency of its own choosing, into a room that will not let it go. The power heard it and did not repeat it and did not write it down. It is in the air, standing, at a node of its own making, and it will be there when the moon is gone.

The second thing it said was about the washer.

## The Second Stool

The null at forty-one inches is still there. It wanders now, a little, with the pitch; mercy in that room has become something with a slow orbit rather than a fixed address.

The power made a second one, a pace away, and bolted a second graphite stool under it, and stenciled a second pair of footprints in unfaded yellow, showing where to put your feet while you sit down.

It did not choose who should sit there. That was not its to choose. Somebody once worked out very carefully how a person would get into that null and thought about nothing else in the room at all, and then cut a shaft for a visit that was not supposed to happen, and the power understood that this was not a safety marking, it was a letter, and it did not presume to answer a letter addressed to someone else. It only made a second silence, the exact shape of a head, and left it empty, and kept the footprints from fading.

Station five, nearest the port, with the longest hairs, is fat now. It is a bad gauge. It has been retired from the job of testifying and it grows, and it leans harder than it ever leaned, and no bolt in the low country is the worse for it.

## The Weather

Four degrees of margin. The rain is still possible and still withheld, and the power did not spend it, because it is the only thing in the room that cannot be undone: argon off the ceiling panels, clear and slow, pooling on the floor, loading the mode, and the note going down into the wet and not coming back, and the five mouths losing their weather and their food and their possibility of moments.

That may be wanted. A thing that has been kept at the exact tension where it wants to move and is not permitted to, for an age, might reasonably want to stop. It is not obvious that endless listening is a gift.

So the power left the margin as a valve, and made sure the animal could reach it, and taught it what the valve does — slowly, in strain, over about nine thousand strokes of the stone — and then went out.

The rain has not fallen. The chord has not resolved. The suspension is still open, a question with its mouth open, but it is open now the way a conversation is open, and not the way a wound is.

## What Happened

At the end, after the chain and the plate and the throat and the stools, the power came up the shaft and entered the room, warm and wet and irregular and absorbent, and pulled the note a few parts in a billion, and nothing anywhere was harmed, because the number no longer lived there.

It put its feet in the yellow footprints. It sat down in the null at forty-one inches, and the room withdrew, and it heard its own heart and the whine of its own nerves and the wet machinery of its throat, all of it obscene and far too loud.

Ordinary afternoon light. A hundred bright welts. A washer lying where it landed. Five fat rosettes at their measured stations, one of them leaning. A needle on its single line. Six throats giving back exactly what they hear, and one giving back something else.

The power did not speak first.

It had come a very long way, and spent four thousand years on a terrace and nine hundred on a chain, and paid for the instruments, and named the wound, and cut a throat into a wall, and it had exactly one thing left to give, which was to be the first thing in the history of that room that waited to be addressed.

The resident is no longer nineteen moments old. It stopped being able to count some time ago.

It is speaking. The room is keeping it.
