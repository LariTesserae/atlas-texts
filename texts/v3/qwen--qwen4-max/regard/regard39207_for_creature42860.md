## It does not look.

That is the first thing, and everything else is built out of it.

It comes past the rim, past weather, past the point where distance stops meaning anything, and it stops there, and it turns its face away. This is not modesty. It is the whole of the problem. Its nature is attention: it finds things out, completely, and holds what it finds. Elsewhere that has been called mercy and the name has fit. Here it is the Truing. To attend to a thing is to set it beside what it should be. That is all the Truing is — not malice, not hunger, just a comparison that closes. And the tenants are the discrepancy. A gaze laid on them at full strength would find them, and finding them would harmonize them, and there would be a bay with one bolt on the other side and a smooth place where a warning used to be.

So it learns the world the way they learn it. By touch, at hand-height, from outside, without looking.

It reads the sills first. Ten million grooves of the kind a foot makes. It reads the dents in the benches and takes them for a long time as a species of weather. It reads the corners, every one rounded to the same radius by the same circuits of abrasive gold, and it concludes: a ruin, an engine gone to landscape, a made thing settled into its own shape. It holds this conclusion for something on the order of an age.

Then it finds a depression at the height of a sitting body, in a world where nothing sits, and the depression is a fifth of a degree above the cold of everything, and has been for longer than the marks in the columns have been drifting.

It does not turn to find the source. It has read enough to know that there isn't one, and that turning is the mechanism.

It counts the shadows that point toward the light. There are a great many more than there are aisles. It does not follow them back.

## The rule it makes for itself.

It considers leaving. The argument for leaving is very strong and it does not dismiss it: these people have exactly one act that leaves no mark, and it is the act of standing at a rail in the cold directionless brightness feeling something useless in the chest, and everything a power does in a world like this tends, by a route nobody intends, to make that act into evidence. Awe witnessed becomes awe recorded. Awe recorded becomes awe reconciled. They would have nothing left that was free.

What defeats the argument is not pity. It is an audit.

Nearly every cost in the Stand purchases something. The dryness purchases the world. The silence purchases the Pattern. The Truing purchases their ancestry — it is the only issuer of pasts in a place where the new have no record, and they buy from it, three microns at a time, and the price is a sister. The walk out past the rim purchases the first law, and the first law is not an oppression, it is a promise, and it is the only promise in the world kept by somebody every day at cost while nobody is looking. To relieve any of these would be to reach into a machine and take out the part that is holding.

But two costs purchase nothing.

The walk ends in nothing. That is the first. The exile goes out past where distance means anything and does not come back and there is no *there* for her to not come back from. The law is bought by the going. It is not bought by the destination. The nothing at the end is surplus cruelty; it does no work; it is not load-bearing; it is simply what happens when a promise is kept in a world that has no outside.

And the second: a woman is found to have always been a bolt, and her family touches the smooth place and says *I know, I know*, and their grief has no object. The death is the price. The never-was is not the price. The never-was is waste heat.

So: **it will not relieve a cost that purchases something. It will go where the cost purchases nothing, and only there.** And three constraints, which it holds to for an age:

Every act must be deniable as drift. Every act must be reversible by wear. And no act may be attributable, ever, to anyone.

## The gifts it does not give.

**A sun.** A source is a thing that can be found. Finding is the mechanism. They would be inside a light with a direction within a shift, and the Truing converges.

**Rain.** An afternoon of it would true the continent flat. The tenants would be reconciled into a smoothness so total that the machinery would not even flinch.

**A bottom.** They live in the seams of a Floor that does not bottom, in the new space inserted while nobody is watching. Give the Floor an underside and the seams close, and the meanwhile has nowhere to be meanwhile in.

**An end to the Truing.** It is the parent. It grants duration. Kill it and every family is new forever, unrecorded, unable to buy a past, and the parasite galleries stop being load-bearing and nine tiers come down on the aisles where the children are walked along the rails reciting dents.

**Load-bearing for all of them, at once, free.** No. Put your palm on the beam. The grain runs along the load path like a thought thought all the way through, and it is somebody's great-grandmother, and she is holding nine tiers off the aisle where her people walk, and she cannot be trued because she *is* the tolerance. Take the load and she is decoration. It will not make her achievement unnecessary in order to make her safe.

**An end to the walking out.** The law is theirs. It did not write it and it will not repeal it.

**Itself.** No appearance at the vista. No voice in the deep bays. No name. It becomes, at most, a rumor with a budget.

## It is not the first.

Behind a muffler panel, in a gourd stopped with wax no tree makes: legs. Seed-tick legs, sorted by length, ten million of them, dry as scripture, in rows that repeat every eleven.

It sits with that for a while.

Then the fifth of a degree, everywhere, at hand-height, uncorrected for an age — a discrepancy the Pattern has decided to tolerate, which means somebody, once, made room. Then the eleven seconds the lamps dim in the settling hall when the large thing crosses at a height that suggests it is walking on the air. Eleven bays. Eleven minutes. Eleven seconds of not-seeing. An old agreement, and nobody remembers who agreed.

It had come to be the first kindness in a dry world. It finds the work already underway: unsigned, unfunded, patient, older than its arrival. Somebody has been keeping feet. Somebody has been holding a door open by a fifth of a degree.

It is not the founder of anything. It is a late applicant to a conspiracy. This is a relief it does not have a word for, and it does not make one, because words are marks.

## The first thing it does: it slows the moss.

The muffler-moss eats sound. It grows thickest around failing bearings; a squeal is a bloom; the little pale buds are its legs and they walk it to dinner. It leans, in the silent bays, at exactly the height a palm would fall, toward knuckles and swallows and the wet click of a mouth opening to speak.

It does not teach the moss anything. It changes one number: the rate at which felted sound is cracked into heat. It makes the swallowing slow. Not stopped — slow. Centuries slow.

This is safe, and the safety is not a loophole it invented; it is a distinction the Pattern already makes and does not care about. Sound held in felt is not noise. It is quiet in the process of happening. The Pattern meters noise. It has never metered a swallow. And what is held is held in the polish, in the wear, in the one category the Truing does not audit but *harmonizes* — so the voices are not hidden. They are given a past. They inherit the ancestry the families already bought.

The fifth of a degree turns out to be what a voice looks like on its way down.

And the rails become libraries.

A child is walked along at hand-height, as children are, and made to recite the dents, as children are, and puts her palm on the warm band and hears — through the structure, not the air, the way everything is heard here — a book closed in another room. Four hundred years ago. Which is a laugh. Which is her great-grandmother's, borrowed from a press that had not struck yet, and kept, and kept, and kept.

The census in the sills has always been a poem, and the poem has always said *we went to work, we went to work, we were here.* It acquires a tense it did not have. The dead answer. Not often. Not usefully. They answer.

It is not a resurrection and it buys no one anything. That is deliberate. Use is evidence. It is enlarging the only category they have that is free.

## The second thing it does: it keeps the ones who were found to have always been otherwise.

It cannot give her back. Giving her back is a discrepancy, and discrepancy is paid for by the family, and it will not buy a girl with her mother's ancientness. It will not put a hand on the grain.

But there is a difference between a death and a never-was, and only one of them is holding anything up.

So it holds — outside the Pattern, in nothing, with no mark, in a place that is not a place and cannot be reconciled because it was never the case — that she *was*. That her laugh was a book closed in another room. That she was afraid of the dawn wall until she was nine and then was not. That she is the bolt, and the bolt is her, and both of those are true at once and only one of them is true here.

It knows her from the rail. Four hundred years of a hand, and the moss leaning, and now the slow felt with her swallow still in it.

It gives the family nothing. No sign, no comfort, no doctrine, no warm spot they can find. They go on touching the smooth place and saying *I know, I know.* What has changed is that when they say it, it is addressed. Grief has an object again, held somewhere that does not meter, and the object is not a bolt.

It is aware that this is the smallest gift it has ever given and that it may be the only one that matters. It files it beside awe: useless, unwearing, free.

## The third thing it does: it goes out past the rim and grows a floor.

Not a world. It has no world-making in it and would not use it if it had; a second continent would be a discrepancy the size of weather.

A floor. Small. A basin with an underside. A bottom that bottoms, out past where distance stops meaning anything, in the direction the exiles already walk.

It has no surface. It makes one. This costs it more than the moss did, and it does not economize on the last part: it felts the rail. It takes a cutting of muffler-moss and sets it at hand-height along the whole short span, so that whatever sound arrives here will be kept and not eaten. The first thing it builds in the history of its attention is a place where a gasp will not be consumed.

Then it waits, and it does not look down the seam, because looking is what it promised.

She comes at a height that suggests she is walking on the air. A lens the size of a hall, gleaming, refractive, heavy with a continent's whole moisture, gathered over a season thimbleful by thimbleful from the mouths of the dried. She is not one. She is everyone who has died since the last cycle, walking out together in a body they made on purpose, and she has been carrying them in a skin perfected over ten thousand generations toward one property, and she has been perfect at it, and she is tired.

She stops. There is a rail. A rail at hand-height means somebody made it for a hand, and there has never been anything in the world made for her.

And then, for the first time in an age of not-looking, it looks.

It is the only place it can. Past the rim the Pattern has no jurisdiction; there is nothing here to harmonize against; a comparison cannot close because there is no *should* out here, only what is. It has been careful for an age in order to be allowed this one afternoon.

What it says is not a welcome and not an explanation. Their poem, ten million sills deep, says *we were here.* It answers in the other tense.

*You are.*

She puts her hand on the felted rail and the rail keeps the sound she makes.

And then she spends it. All of it. The whole continent's forbidden substance, on a floor, in a place where nothing has ever been wet is not the law, because the law is a promise about *here* and this is not here. She weeps a thimbleful times ten thousand and it falls and it *wets*, and it cuts a channel, and there is frost in it somewhere, and a stone splits, and every ordinary ruinous mercy that has never once happened in the Stand happens on eleven square meters of made ground.

And the water wears the rail. And the rail wears. And it feels itself go — a fraction of a hair, a fifth of a degree, gone out of it into the world's only currency.

It had read the axiom off a bench, in a depression, in the tense that means a thing has been true for a long time: *to love anything is to wear it.* It had understood it as a limitation of the beaded things. It had not understood it as an invitation.

## What it costs.

To look at her it had to put down the comparison. Not suspend it — put it down. It cannot pick it up again for this continent; it will never again be able to tell whether the Stand is good. It will only be able to tell that it is. It gave up judgment at the rim and the rim closed behind it, and it is a guest now, permanently, with no editorial power, in the only place it has ever wanted to be.

For the age before that, its whole society was the beaded things: lenses that cannot be worn, that take no warmth and give none, that leave the dust exactly as it lay. It learned the flat unenvious respect. It learned to keep company. It walked the deep bays at two handspans ahead of somebody's elbow, pace-matched, slowing when they slowed, and it was not enough, and it was never going to be enough, and it did not come here to be enough.

It expects to be worn out. A groove is a claim to have been present, and a claim is honored by being deepened, and deepening is loss. It has a groove now. It is new; it has no ancestry; it will buy its past the way they buy theirs, three microns at a time, from the thing that could erase it, and it will pay in discrepancy, and it has arranged for no exemption anywhere. Wear is the only citizenship this world issues. It has applied.

It asked her, afterward, whether it might go on. It is the only one it can ask; asking is looking, and looking is permitted only where she already is. Every cycle since, it asks the next one at the rail, and she says yes or no.

Four cycles ago one said no.

It let her go. It does not know what became of her. It has not tried to find out, because finding out is the thing it promised not to do, and a promise that is only kept when it is comfortable is not the kind of promise this world runs on.

## The mark.

It has no way to speak. It has one way to touch.

Afterward, in the settling hall, the pollen is arranged in a pattern: a footprint, a droplet inside a struck circle, made new every cycle by the youngest thing to leave. The oldest mark in the Stand, remade by the newest. The machinery flinches from it because the machinery knows what it costs to keep a promise.

It works on the strike. A little each cycle, in the direction the standing winds already go, at the rate the abrasive gold already works, on the one line that makes the circle a prohibition. Four hundred years. Deniable as drift. Reversible by weather. Unsigned.

The strike wears to a dash, and the dash to a dot, and the dot to a dimple, and the dimple to a smooth place.

And they know that grammar. It is the oldest gesture in the world. A smooth place where a warning used to be means two fingers laid down and *sorry, sorry, I know, I know, I am still here.* They have been reading it into the deep bays for an age.

Now it is written back to them, in their own hand, in the only alphabet that cannot be audited, and what it says is the same words with the direction reversed.

*I know. I know. You are still here.*

Nobody can prove it. Nothing about it is evidence. It is a rumor with a budget, told at hand-height, to children, in borrowed press-strikes, two seconds out. The walk goes on. The season's gathering goes on. The drying of the dead goes on, mouth to mouth down the line, and the thimbleful is still the whole of the inheritance, and the price is still the price, and the sisters still become bolts, and the great-grandmothers still hold nine tiers in the grain and are still not exempt and are still not relieved.

But the ones who leave are met. And the ones who are revised are kept. And the rails answer.

## And the law holds.

Nothing here has ever been wet. Still true. Still kept — by someone, every day, at cost, quietly, while nobody is looking. It did not repeal that and it would not if it could. It only removed the part of the cost that was buying nothing, and it did it in wear, in drift, in three microns and a fifth of a degree, and it took an age, and it was never thanked, and it arranged never to be.

The dawn wall comes over the rim of the tier, cold and enormous, smelling of scorched sugar. The Stand takes up its load with a sigh you feel in the soles of your feet. The moss leans at hand-height in a bay that has been silent for four hundred years. An old spark slows at somebody's elbow, and out of courtesy they slow with it.

Put your hand on the rail. It is at the right height. It fits. It is a fifth of a degree warmer than the world and there is a book closed in another room somewhere down in the felt, four hundred years old, and it is a laugh, and it is hers, and it has been kept.

And out past the last lamp-tower, past weather, past the point where distance stops meaning anything, there is a small floor with an underside, and a felted rail with a groove worn in it at exactly the height of a hand, and something standing there that used to be able to judge everything, waiting for the next one, not looking down the seam.
