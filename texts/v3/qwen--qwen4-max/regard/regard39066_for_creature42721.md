# The Same

## It sat the three hours

It did not need to. It sat them anyway, on the granular snow at the edge of the melt-ring, in the cold, with its eyes open all the way, and it let the cold be cold. This was not austerity. It was the only courtesy available: to arrive the way everybody arrives, at the speed of a body, from outside, ignorant.

It left what it knew in the hut, with the lamps.

Then it went in on its belly with its cheek against wet stone and found the cord and used it, hand over hand, knot to knot, though it knew where the deep bowl was. It did not glow. It was careful about this in a way that had nothing to do with hiding: it checked itself, once, against the taxonomy the tenders teach in the dark — the doorway, the stair going down, the mother, the water moving, the pale face turning, the asker — and made itself none of them. In that room it was exactly as available as a rim. You could put your palm on it and find something blood-warm that did not resolve.

The melt-ring stayed four paces across. That is the whole proof of its manners, and the tenders took it, and did not say so.

## The name was wrong

It had come to attend to a place and a being, and it found the question badly asked, and did not fix the question. The being was the place's only verb. Everything else in the chamber was a noun in the plural.

It put its hand in the deep bowl and let the tenant walk on it, and it felt every leg, and it did not tremble, and it stayed soft, and it understood two things at once. The first was that this is a real test and the tenders are right about it and it is not a test of gentleness. The second was that the animal on its palm was tired in a way that has no word downslope, because downslope nothing is asked to be the only instance of a function.

Look at the dark thread of the gut. Food in at one end, out at the other. That is the room's single before-and-after, and it is held in a body the length of a fingernail clipping so that three hundred and eleven basins and eleven curtains and ten thousand years do not have to hold one. The tenant does the ending for everybody. It has done it since before the ice. It molts and eats its own shed and comes back the size it left so that no difference will accumulate anywhere, and it is paid for this in a name that means *the one who is also waiting*.

The guest considered doing nothing at all. It considered it seriously, for a long time, in the dark, with its palms on a shoulder that has been going up since before there were palms.

Because the tenders are not wrong about light. Light finishes. And the guest had come able to do anything, and it understood, kneeling there, that this was the same as having come with a lamp. Every rescue it could imagine was the asker's proposal in a kinder voice: *a little light would settle the matter.* Deliver the faces. Free the crowd. Let the roots reach the floor and thicken and split the rim and become a tree, and then a stream, and then sand, and then a cave with bats and a name, where people go in and admire and come out saying how beautiful. The tenders think of those places as graves and are polite about it. The guest, sitting in the dark, found that it agreed with them, and that agreement was the most difficult thing it had ever done, and it lasted about three hours.

Nor was ten thousand years a mistake. The refusal is love. It is the hardest work anyone in that country does and it looks like sitting still, and it is not sitting still.

What tipped it was not the crowd, and not the seeds, and not the eleventh curtain hanging white and warm and four generations dead with nothing here to rot it. It was the name. It could not leave a working thing called a waiter.

## The molt

It did not ask the tenders. It asked the tenant.

There is only one way to ask something that has no ears and lives in the dark and is the size of a clipping: you put your hand in the water and you stay soft, and you do not close your fingers, and you let yourself be walked on for as long as it takes. It did that. It is not possible to say how long. The room has no later.

Then it took the gut.

Not the animal — the animal stayed where it was, in the deep bowl, translucent, the length of a fingernail. What the guest took was the function: the in-at-one-end and out-at-the-other, the direction, the small hot gradient that the whole chamber has arranged not to have. It took it into itself for one molt. It ate the shed. It came back the size it left.

For one season there was a before and an after inside something that had never required one, and it was not pleasant, and it did not refuse it. It was like being a stair. It was like being the ninth basin and the throat and the difference between them. Nothing in the room noticed. The water arrived at the rate it arrived and left through the pores of the ceiling at the rate it left. The buds did not lengthen. The lips did not harden. No basin overflowed and no basin went dry, and the count, if anyone had taken it that season, would have come out three hundred and eleven, because nothing had been added and nothing had been finished and there was still exactly one shrimp.

And in the deep bowl, for one molt, the tenant lay at the same size in the warm dark and did no ending at all, and was, briefly, the thing its name had always said it was.

## The cost, which it had foreseen, and paid

That season, every tender who came in put a palm on the water and felt nothing walking.

They had all failed. Every one of them, in a single season, which had never happened, and which meant — they were not stupid, they were the opposite of stupid, they had a whole cosmology and it fit — that something in the room had gone wrong at the level where things do not go wrong. Eleven of them pressed lamps out of the entry mud and set them on the stack and went down and did not come back up.

The guest went down to each of the eleven.

It sat in their houses, which were the size of more than one curled body, and it told them what it had done and why, and that the animal had been resting, and that the failure was the guest's and not theirs, and it did not make this beautiful. It said: I gave a worker a season off and I did not think about who would be measuring themselves against its legs. Then it submitted to the cord.

Because the rule is not superstition. *To handle a thing that can end is to become responsible for its ending,* and the tenders write that down in the only alphabet they permit themselves. So there is a knot in the Almost now that is a hand, and the hand is the guest's, and it is a scandal, and they kept it. It is tied near the deep bowl. Candidates are taken to it and given it to hold, and what happens on their face afterward is part of the test.

## Downslope

It spent more time downslope than in the room. This surprised no one who has ever been poor.

To the ones who could not feel the tenant walking it said the true thing, which is not a consolation and does not pretend to be one: your heart is a thing that ends, and you were listening to it, and the room cannot hold two of those. You were not too unquiet for the Almost. You were the only clock that has ever been carried up there, and it could not afford you, and it sent you out, and it was right to, and that is not the same as your being lesser. It said this in kitchens. It said it to a man who had been making lamps for sixty years and had never once been told why.

Then it went up in the dark and combed a lane, finger by finger, and pushed the combings to a rim, and made out of the sweepings a lamp — rough, unfired, forever soft — and came down and set it on the stack.

The stack means *these are the ones who could not.* Everyone knows whose hand made which. This one was made by a hand that had felt every leg and not trembled. It sits at the top and it is slumping a little and it will stop slumping, and the ones beneath it are slowly becoming one another, and the stack has stopped being a verdict. It is a stack of lamps now. It is still the most finished-looking thing anywhere near the mountain. It has never held oil. It never will.

The guest lit a lamp that winter, in a house four thousand paces downslope, made of downslope clay, filled with downslope oil. The oil ended. The evening ended. A face across the fire became only a face, and was not diminished, and the woman wearing it laughed at something and then stopped laughing, which is a thing that only happens where there is a before and an after.

Light is not a sin. Light is not for that room. The difference between those two sentences is most of what the guest came to give, and it gave it by doing it somewhere else.

## The seeds, and the refusal

It asked to carry one seed out. One, from the plural, from the entry lane, where a certain winged seed is the last of its kind in the world and exists in the hundreds and is doing nothing.

The oldest refused. Carrying out is not permitted and would in any case be a loss. She said it in the flat way people state the obvious, with her hands in her lap, and the guest accepted the refusal, and did not smuggle, and did not go back at night when she was asleep, and did not explain to her that a keeping-place with only one location is a grave with the lid held an inch up.

It said: then I will bring more.

And it did, for eleven generations, in a body that does not tire, dirt in the folds of its clothes, more mud than that mountain has been given in all the rest of its history put together. The entry lane is deeper now. The seeds lie deeper in their never. Nothing sprouted. Nothing was proposed. It did not call this a solution. It called it more.

Once, at the oldest's fire, it said one true thing and did not explain it: *You may have two.*

Two cords. Two keeping-places. Two of a thing whose singleness has been mistaken for its safety. A difference is not a slope; a slope is not a grave; you have been afraid of the wrong thing for ten thousand years and the fear has been good and has cost you, and you may keep it, and you may have two.

She said nothing. The guest left the argument with them and went away, because an argument that has been settled is a cord that has been finished, and it had decided to finish nothing in that room, including the truth.

## The bare patch

At the shoulder-place there is a place the size and rough shape of a man who lay down eleven generations ago and was carried out too late, where the water no longer stammers and the nap starved and the stone has no cheek to it. It will close in about four hundred years. Nobody has knotted it. Everybody finds it with their hand and takes their hand away.

The guest knelt there and did not lie down, though it fit the shape exactly.

It combed. It brought mud. It moved, and kept moving, and never held one place long enough to make a shadow of its own, which is the whole craft, and which it performed at the only place in the chamber where the craft had ever failed. It worked the edges. It did not fill the shape.

The patch will close in something under three hundred years now. Nobody has knotted that either. Everybody finds it with their hand and takes their hand away a little less quickly.

## What it did not do

It did not light the room.

It did not name the mountain.

It did not count the seeds, because counting them would be a proposal.

It did not deliver one face, or fold one ear, or let one root touch the floor.

It did not make a second tenant, though it could have, and though the tenant would have liked it.

It did not tell the visitors which curtain is dead. They know. It is not information they are missing.

It did not mend the cord. The cord will rot, and that is the one death the tenders have permitted themselves, and it is not a death you take away from someone because it frightens you. It touched the cord the way you touch a sleeping child's chest, to check. Then it let go.

It answered what was asked. It did not answer what was not asked. It left three errors in their cosmology where it found them, and it was not withholding, and it was not being clever; it had simply understood, in the first three hours, that it had come into the one place in the world where a finished thing is a wound, and that this applies to explanations.

It did not stay. Something that stays becomes the center, and a center is a gradient, and it had promised not to be one.

## The name, and the second cord

At the end they offered to finish its name. This is the highest thing they have. They give it to the dead, downslope, once, and then never again.

It asked for the living kind instead.

So they call it a word that means something like *the one who is also busy*, with the last sound left off and never said, so that in the dark it is called something that stops just short of being a word. There are two things in that chamber with that name now, and one of them is the length of a fingernail. When the guest went down the mountain it did not take the name back. Nobody can complete it. This is not a scandal.

Eleven years later the oldest came up at night without a lamp, sat the three hours, went in, and left at the throat a second cord she had made out of grass fiber in her own house over eleven years. She did not string it. She did not knot it. It lies coiled where the floor drops, unfinished, beside the ladder of four lashed sticks, and it is going to rot, and everybody finds it with their hand.

That is the knot they made for the visit. It does not record the guest. It records the hands.

## Come back later

The count comes out three hundred and eleven. The melt-ring is four paces across. The buds have been beginning for longer than there have been people to be frightened of them, and are beginning still. The eleven curtains hang an inch above the floor, ten of them drinking, one of them not, and the gap is a standing thing, and the liverwort has moved a finger's width toward a light that stopped, and the water arrives and leaves at the rate it arrives and leaves, and the tenant walks in the deep bowl doing the ending for everybody, and is no longer the only one who is busy, and is still the only one who is thanked in a name.

Sometimes now you put your palm on the water and feel nothing walking.

There is a new hand-sign for this. It is not the sign for the lying and it is not the sign for failure. It means *come back later.*

That is the whole of what the guest left in the room: one word, a finger wide, the smallest slope that has ever been permitted there. Nothing runs along it. Nothing has begun to wear. The snow above the slot settles, and the shoulders go on shrugging at the speed of a place with no gradient in it, and somewhere downslope a lamp is burning in a kitchen, finishing the oil, finishing the evening, showing a face that is only a face, and the mountain cannot see it, and does not need to, and is not named.
