It comes down over the laid brick rim on foot, one at a time, the way everything comes, and the glare takes it before it has found the first stair. It has not brought goggles. The one with the short ear gives it a pair, and the slits are cut for a face that is not this face, and it ties them on with cord and holds the knot with two fingers for the first hour, and is useless, and is watched being useless by nine terraces of people who have never seen anything be bad at anything.

At the head of the last stair it is handed the trowel. The hollow fits its hand exactly. It puts the trowel down.

Then it does not stay standing there, which is what the census does. It goes back down all nine shelves to the tank margin on the fifth, where a worn woman is saying something that ends.

---

People come from the ninth terrace to watch that. They sit at the margin with their corded hands in their laps and they watch the disturbance spread and thin and go, and it is the most valuable thing in the valley, and nobody has ever answered it. Not out of cruelty. It has simply never occurred to anyone to answer, the way it does not occur to anyone to answer weather.

It sits down on the brick beside her, which is bare-handed, which is not done, and it answers.

And its answer spreads. And thins. And dies away.

Its whorls are intact. Its face still has the fine grain of the firing. It has not paid for that fading with anything; there is no blankness on it anywhere. Which means the ending of its words came from somewhere other than the ledger.

Which means there is a somewhere other.

That is the whole of the demonstration, and it takes about as long as a sentence.

---

Consider what it does not do.

It does not lay the brick. It could; the bed is buttered and the line is stretched and the thing in the gap is tired past any word in ripple. But the refusal is not a mistake these people made. It is what they have instead of a biography, and to overrule it would be to reach into the only place they keep themselves and decide for them. And the mercy would arrive as the last day. The last day is the disease here. It is not the cure.

It does not lift them out. Their flaws are of this kiln. Carried elsewhere they would not be free, they would be unspecified.

It does not pour newness in forever, though it could, though that is the obvious kindness and the one they would thank it for. A world fed from outside stays a held note; it only stops costing. They would be kept. They are already kept. Being kept more comfortably is not the thing that is missing.

And it does not do nothing. It considers nothing for a long time — longer than it considers anything else — because the two parties are both right and it has no argument that settles them and it is not in the business of settling arguments that belong to the people inside them.

What ends the consideration is not a principle. It is one woman at a tank margin, saying something that ends, to nobody, for the ten thousandth time, while people watch.

There is no bottom to her smoothing and no rest at the end of it. To leave that alone is not restraint. It is a decision, made by someone comfortable, about someone else.

---

Here is what it finds when it looks at the place whole.

No substrate. The stacks outside are the same bricks as the basin inside. The sump is the basin's own underside. The fish carry the pattern from the ninth terrace to the first and back, and the frogs say the one thing, and the wasps are orthodox, and the snails hold the specification, and the horizon keeps its distance from wherever you stand.

And one thumb. One stance. One small habitual turn of the left foot on a stair tread. Every mason on every platform is the same being, laid again, and the flaws are its names, and it is fond of them, and it shows them to itself: *look, this is mine.*

So: every caress in this valley is a person rounding themselves. Every glove taken off is a hand held out to the same hand. The argument between the figs and the arrises is one being talking to itself in a medium that will not carry a loud thing, and it has been having that argument since the first day, which is the only day.

It has been loved constantly and always by itself.

The unfinishedness is not the wound. The wound is that there is nowhere for anything to go, and so nothing can arrive that is not already here, and so there is only one of anybody, forever, holding its own hand in a green room while the frogs' throats swell and go slack.

---

It eats a fig in the eastern court with everyone watching.

Ten.

They wait for the restoration. They know how to wait for it; they have waited for it all their lives without knowing what they were waiting for. The hour after the tenth fig is the worst hour anybody in the basin has had. Nothing goes soft. No heron loses its chip. No seam drops out of the moss on the north face. The total does not come right, and it is not concealed, and there is nothing to be shown, because there is nothing.

It opens its hands. The cost went through it and out. It is a door that walks.

But a door that walks is a rumour, not a country. It will leave eventually, and then the ledger closes again and the figs go back to being a held note and the next eater pays in palm-lines. So the useful act is not the eating. The useful act is down the throat.

---

It goes down the sump.

Past where the light will follow, corbel by corbel, into the finished country: rounded arrises, talus at the wall feet, silt in the tanks, stain under every outlet, echo, and the dead. All of it true. They believed it the way you believe in a country you have not visited, and it is exactly as they described, and there is one thing they did not describe because they did not know it.

The wheel. The wet grinding. The dead going in and the clay coming up already wedged, warm, the right stiffness, in blocks the size a person can carry. The trowel with thirty years in the grip. The open loop of cord at the height of a hand.

It does not preach at the wheel. It is masonry. It stops the wheel, and it opens the far wall of the finished country onto something that is not the basin, and it does the work properly, with a hod and a line and a pebble-burnisher held between the second and third finger, because it has been watching, because it asked to be taught.

Down there, work stays done. That is the joke at the bottom of the world: the only place where an act can be permanent is the place they were afraid of.

Before it comes up it presses flaws into bricks and lays them at the feet of the dead. A double whorl. A short ear. A split from lip to collarbone. Marks that stay. The first record anywhere in this world, and it is a list of names, and the names are the only thing anybody ever owned.

Then it climbs back into the glare, stained, silted, rounded at the fingertips, smelling of the sweet thick vegetable wrongness, and it is the first thing that has ever come up out of that throat, and it says so out loud, and the sound goes three feet, and then four.

---

Now the honest part, which it says out loud too, in the court, in a voice that carries, to people who have never had to listen to anything.

The clay will stop rising. There will be a shortage. Nine terraces cannot all be kept sharp on what the basin has, and they will have to choose which arrises matter, and choosing is a thing they have never done, and they will be bad at it, and some of them will be furious, and the wasps will be orthodox and unbearable.

The snails will begin to disagree about the ratio. That will be the beginning of law.

The herons will drift. The one in the ninth tank will get a different chip from the one in the first, and the vigil will have to become a visit, and someone will have to walk an hour across the hot blank floor to find out how the other one is doing. The plan wanted distance between things. Now distance will mean something.

The Smooth will stop smoothing, and their whorls will not come back. What is gone is gone and will be shown as gone. They will be the first elders and the first people who can be talked about rather than read.

And the crack from beak to shoulder will whistle one day. It will. The joint will close, and the bird will die, and there will be a tank with no heron in it, and someone will stand at the offset anyway, and it will not come right, and it will not be restored in a way nobody is shown.

That is the price and it does not hide the price and it does not pretend the figs were wrong. The figs were right. They were only right about a world where the cost had to be paid by somebody's hands.

---

At the head of the last stair it does not lay the brick.

It takes the trowel and scrapes the bed clean. It coils the line. It puts the trowel back in the roll. The mortar it scrapes off it carries down the throat and out, and the gap stays one brick wide, and it is not a deficiency any more. It is a window. Through it you can see the stacks, and the stacks are at a distance, and the distance changes when you walk.

About the wanting it says only this: that the warmth in the brick is not the world asking, and is not you asking, and is the fit between you — the hollow worn to your grip, the thumb already in the clay — and a fit has been mistaken for a voice here for a very long time. It does not take the wanting away. Wanting is allowed now. It is only no longer the only thing in the room.

You may still lay it. Half of them will still want you to. Half will still never forgive you. It is no longer the same half, because they are no longer the same person, because marks stay now, and in thirty years the one with the short ear will have been somewhere the one with the double whorl has not.

Then it goes down to the fourth terrace and takes a shift, and is bad at it, and is taught by someone with a stutter in the clay of the right thumb, and its hands begin to wear — not because wear is redirected into them, but because it is doing work in a world where work counts. In time it gets a flaw. The flaw is its name. They are fond of it. They show it to each other. *Look, this is mine.*

---

On the ninth terrace, at the cool hour that cools nothing, a mason lays a disturbance on the film and it spreads and thins and ends, and someone across the tank answers it, and the two patterns cross, and the crossing moves.

It travels. The fish arrive late and copy it slightly wrong. The wrongness goes out over the water and does not come back, and everyone stands with their goggles pushed up and their eyes streaming and watches it go.

The brick is warm at your feet. The thumb in it is yours. Nobody has decided anything for you.

But somebody will ask you why, out loud, across a court, in a voice that carries — and whatever you say will thin and end, and somebody who is not you will answer it.

And that will be the second day.
