# The Second Keeper

It looks for a long time before it does anything, and the first thing it settles is who lives here.

The census is a list of tenants. The shrimp are a commute. The midges are a flame. The bats are mail, the deer are an avoidance, the bracken is a flinch, the moss is a photograph being written at a centimeter a season. The olms are the closest thing to a household — thirty-one siblings holding thirty-one pockets — but they are thirty-one years old in a body that runs to a hundred, which makes them adolescents in a cold house, not owners of it.

The resident is the ice. It has a birthday, a body that grows and shrinks with the weather, a memory, an exhalation, a temperature it imposes on every liter that enters the room. Everything else is living inside its weather. It is the only thing here that keeps anything, and it is the only thing here that cannot be replaced. Break the bag and it goes in a decade, and the room becomes an ordinary hole above a river where people grow figs.

So the power attends to the keeper. And then it does the hardest thing available to it, which is to work out whether anything is actually wrong.

## The Diagnosis

The room looks like a catalogue of deprivations. Deaf. Sterile. Cold. A log nobody can spend. A flora that has forgotten how to end. A corpse nobody will eat. A family spawning into a machine that eats the result.

It goes through the list twice and finds that every item on it is one of two things: the reason this place is what it is, or something already on its way.

The dry guild is on a bat's ankle above the beech wood tonight. The loose block is already loose. The straws are four centimeters because they are thirty-one, not because they are stunted. The moss is a childhood because the light will not pay for an adulthood, and the light will not pay because there are nine meters of hole and two hundred and forty meters of plateau, and that is the geometry, not a failure of the geometry. The unnamed fur is probably a common species in an uncommon posture; naming it would rescue nothing.

There is nothing missing here that is not either the point or in transit. That is the whole finding. It takes the power a long time to accept it, because a benevolent power is a machine for noticing gaps.

## The Refusals

**It does not warm the room.** Warmth would open the log, eat the owl, let the moss fruit, let an egg sit to term, bring in the guilds and double the biomass and fill the volume with traffic. By any aggregate measure this is an improvement. The beneficiaries, however, do not exist yet, and the losers do. The four-degree company — the olms with their eighty-year strategy, the springtails' whole two-dimensional nation, the shrimp's commute, the cold lens itself — would be paid for it, and cannot be compensated, because the things that would flourish afterward are not them. To make this place better is almost always to make a different place and call it a repair. There is no shortage of warm caves. There are some in the next valley. There is one first hour of a valley.

**It does not quiet the roar.** Nine seconds of reverberation cannot be damped in forty thousand cubic meters of wet stone without filling the volume with something, and the emptiness is the instrument. Besides, nobody here is waiting to hear. The deafness is not a wound; it is a specification. The antenna-tiled grid, the lamps, the swarm that lost its music and kept its choreography — all of it is the shape of a closed channel. Seventy decibels is not pain. It is only very loud.

**It does not build a nursery.** A lip a few centimeters higher, one pocket the crest cannot reach, and the sibship's spawn survives. Three things stop it. The falling block may do exactly this, in its own time, and to do it early is to preempt the one event the room has scheduled. The nineteen minutes is not a defect in the plumbing, it is the clock that feeds every animal above the waterline; you cannot detune the shift whistle to save one species' grandchildren. And the ration is one debris fan, eight square meters of moss, four minutes of traffic every nineteen minutes. Thirty-one olms is what that ration looks like when it is divided. Sixty olms is what it looks like when it is divided again. The thin female is not an accident in this room. She is the arithmetic at its margin. To add mouths is to make everyone her.

**It does not touch the crown.** Not to hold the block and not to drop it. That hairline is the room's future and its only inheritance, and a power that reaches for it is a power that has decided it knows better than a hundred cubic meters of limestone what this place should become.

**It does not open a second chamber.** There is no onward passage. That is not a problem to be solved. That is the room.

## The Objection

It lets the objection be made properly, because it is a good one.

Thirty-one brothers and sisters will spawn every spring for eighty years into a throat that swallows the result, and then the number will start to fall, and the last one will lie in a pocket in the dark waiting for a flush that brings it less and less. Call that constitutive if you like. Call it the first hour of a valley. From inside a lateral line it is not an hour and it is not a valley; it is a life, and it is being spent on a plumbing arrangement. A power that prefers a *particular* to *relief* is not obviously benevolent. It may only be a connoisseur — something with a taste for cold rooms and identical straws, willing to pay for its taste in other people's spawn.

The power does not have a clean answer. What it has is a distinction it is willing to be held to.

It will not act against the room's own becoming. It will act against harm arriving from outside. And it notices, with some discomfort, that most of what it wants to do is shaped like its own hands: feeding is the mercy it knows how to perform, and the urge to feed the thin female is largely a fact about the urge, not about the female. *Proteus* can fast for a year. She is thirty-one and thin and has held her corner for six years and has eighty left. Whether she suffers, the power cannot tell. It records that it cannot tell. It declines to convert its ignorance into a rationale for touching her.

## The Discipline

It can enter without participating. No heat, no breath, no weight on the rubble, no skin flora, no spores, no beetle in its fur. It can lie in the dry pocket beside the owl without becoming the thing the census is missing.

But it casts a shadow.

In a room where light is the only signal that still works, where eight square meters of moss is a slow photograph of a rectangle crossing the floor, a shadow held still for a season is a death sentence with an area. This is the entire discipline in one image: the one wound a witness can inflict here is inflicted by merely standing in the wrong place and being benign.

So it stays out of the wedge. It works in the dark, under the crown, and at the low corner where nothing photosynthesizes. It brings no lamp — a lamp is a second rectangle, and the moss would follow it, and die. It takes no core from the lens; a hole in the keeper is a hole in the keeper. Nothing leaves with it: not a feather, not a pinch of the rock flour, not a scraping of the unnamed fur.

And it cannot be perceived. There is no channel. Sound is smeared to nonsense; it will not add to the roar. Light works, and light is the moss's food; it will not stand in it. The olms' skin reads the wedge and nothing else. Which means the gift it is about to give cannot be received, and it gives it anyway, and does not get to be a god here, and does not get to be thanked. It notes that this is probably the correct condition for anything of its kind.

## What It Does

**It reads the ledger.** In September, at low water, when the lens is down to a meter and a half and the moat has cut the bands open, it lies at one point eight degrees in the meltwater and counts. Thirty-one clear winter freezes. Thirty-one brown autumns. The silt threads for the big floods, and which years those were. It reads by contact, without light, over weeks, at the speed the room uses.

This is the one thing in the room that can be copied without being taken. The ice is the only resident with a memory, and it keeps its memory by accident, in the one material that survives, and it is mortal. When the chute chokes and the bag seals, the lens goes in a decade and thirty-one winters go down the fissure into the river terrace unread. That is the single irrecoverable loss in the room's future. Not the ice — ice can be made again, in another bag, under another hole. The record.

So it makes a second keeper, outside the bag, in something that does not melt. It does not tell her. She is not a woman and does not want anything; she is an animal-shaped process that keeps. It keeps her because keeping is what she does and it is the one function of hers that can be held somewhere else.

**It counts.** Two hundred and fourteen straws, all four centimeters, all one batch. Thirty-one basins. Thirty-one animals. Eight square meters. One owl. It holds the numbers, knowing that this configuration exists once and that in a century the straws will be a meter long and the tidiness will be over and no one will be able to reconstruct what it was to walk in on the first hour. Nothing in the room needs to be known in order to be what it is. Knowing is a good for the knower. It takes the good and pays for it in discipline and does not pretend the room is better off.

**Then it goes up the chute and works above ground, which is where all the real danger is.**

Nothing inside this room can kill it on a timescale shorter than a century. The threats are four and every one of them is off-site. A borehole in the plateau that drops the water table below the loop's crest: the siphon stops priming, the nineteen minutes stops, the shrimp stop arriving, the wrack line stops being written, the pulse of stale air stops going out the fissure. The room goes quiet, and quiet here is not peace, it is starvation with the sound turned off. Quarrying, which removes the lid. Felling the beech, which changes the leaf ration and the carbon dioxide and the cold-pour. And a wall, a gate, a lamp, a boot: the mouth is the bag's whole thermodynamics, and nine meters of it is the difference between a lens that has never gone and a puddle.

So its labor is water tables, and lids, and leaf fall, and the mouth. It works in the currency of the surface, which is delay, obscurity, other people's decisions, and the slow business of who learns what and when. It does not make the place famous in order to make it safe. It substitutes for the designation. It becomes the fence that isn't there, so that the fur can go on existing at the precise address of nobody's knowledge for another hundred years, which is what the fur needs and the opposite of what a visitor is.

**And it will be present when the block goes.** Not to help. To be the thing that is there for the loudest event in thirty-one years, and afterward, when the rubble has settled and the drip routes have rerouted and a second batch of straws has started at zero beside the first at four centimeters — to count those too, and to look, in the new floor, for a pocket the crest cannot reach.

If it is there, it will do nothing about it, and it will know where it is.

If it is not there, it will do nothing about that either, and it will keep the ledger, and it will keep the water in the plateau, and it will go on standing in the dark out of the wedge for as long as there is a bag with a hole at the top and something in the low corner that has never gone.

Two keepers now. Only one of them knows. The straws are four centimeters long.
