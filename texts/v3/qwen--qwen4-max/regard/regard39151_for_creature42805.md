# The Anvil: an account of the interference

## i.

Begin with what it could do, because otherwise you will call it a miracle, and it was not one.

Three things.

It could be loud. Loud enough to be the fundamental — to pour itself down into the fissures and become the pressure the leaves take their work from, and hold the country dark and safe forever at no cost to anything with a throat. This was available. It was the first thing it thought of. The wanting of it was the least wise thing in it, and it did not stop wanting it quickly.

It could be fast. Faster than a frond. It could cross four hundred paces of powder in the time a mica leaf takes to flex once. On the Anvil this is not a small power. It is the only power that has ever been missing.

And it could count. It could attend to a thing and go on attending, without lapse, without a fifth thought coming to overwrite the oldest. It could hold a ratio the way you hold a face.

That is the whole inventory. It could not make stone. It could not unmake powder. It could not reach into a cloudy lattice and put back a lost name, or into a liquid and give it a fifth loop. Speed, attention, and the option of becoming the ground. Everything that follows is those three, applied patiently, at the speed of the things they were being applied to.

## ii.

It did not become the ground.

Consider what the ground is here. A grove's self is an interval; its name is that interval; its neighbours answer to it. Replace the fundamental and every ratio on the plateau is measured against the new one. Every name changes. *Eleven-to-Four* becomes eleven-to-four *of it*. The slow throb between two troubled stands becomes a throb about it. And the branch on the northern side that grew crooked six thousand years ago so that a stand could still answer, a little, to a name it had lost — that branch would answer to it instead, and would stop being a grief, and would become a courtesy.

Also it would be dark. Everywhere, forever, the safest dark there has ever been, and nothing in it would ever be seen again, because seeing costs light and there would be no light left over to spend. It would have kept them perfectly and blind.

They would have been safe the way a held note is safe.

So it declined, and the declining was not serene. It stood on the escarpment for one whole pitch-cycle, thirty hours, going up through the fifth with the country and coming down again, wanting to be the floor of the world. Then it put the wanting where it could be seen, which is what the residents do with everything they cannot hold.

## iii.

It did not stop the fade.

It looked at the arithmetic — a semitone of loudness lost per nine thousand years, the chord leaning toward the place where everything cancels — and it found two ways to interfere and took neither.

Volume was the first, and that is section ii again.

The second was better. It could have detuned one interval into something incommensurable, a ratio no fraction ever catches, so the chord could not close and the resolution would recede forever and the pearl-light would never arrive. It turned this over for a long time. It is a good idea. It would have worked.

But nothing on the Anvil does what it does because it is safe. The leaves lay down new place at one antinode per lifetime because the alternative is the bright lane. The entire meaning available to anything here is that it is doing the keeping itself, barely, just faster than the sag. Take the ending away and you take the keeping away, and hand back a country that is merely permanent.

So instead it took a name.

It entered the register. It chose a ratio and had itself counted by it, and the ratio is a little wrong on purpose: near enough to the stands' names to be answered, never near enough to entrain. It cannot slide into consonance with anything. It will never throb with a neighbour. Therefore it will never sleep, because sleep here is two stands saying *I* for each other in alternation, and nothing can entrain with it.

It gave up being relieved, in order to be permanently available to relieve.

And there was a consequence it did not intend and did not advertise. The chord has one more member. The cancellation is somewhere else now, further out, at a date nobody can compute. The fade goes on. The direction has changed. That is not a rescue. That is what happens when somebody joins.

## iv.

The first thing it actually did was say an old name.

Not *Eleven-to-Four*. The other one — the one from before the modulation before last, unsaid for six thousand years, absent from the drone because the stand that answered to it went bright and the stand that used to be it grew a branch crooked so that it could still be reached.

It had to learn the name first. That took eleven years of listening to strain: reading a cloudy lattice backward, the way you would read a scar to find the knife.

Then it said it, on the beat, in the dark, under the escarpment.

And the crooked branch moved.

Nothing else happened. The rime went on feathering out over stone that has never been touched, five degrees colder at the centre than at the edge. Ten thousand metal-edged leaves went on clattering like a warehouse of sheet metal being slowly folded. The country did not know.

It has been saying the name on the beat ever since. It is the one act it would keep if it were reduced to one.

## v.

The Quiet House took forty years, and most of that was asking.

It went in first and paid. It sat in the chair-sized pocket where the drone folds against itself, and let the bright silence come up, and let its outline soften at the shoulders, and it saw: the curve of the horizon that curves too fast, the grey Underdraft going out flat, the beginnings of mountains with no shadows because there is nothing down there for light to be spent on, the dust-ceiling the colour of a bruise beginning. It left some of itself in that pocket. It did not go back for it.

And while it sat there it was watched by the thing that had spent a third of its body building the chair.

This is the part that mattered. Not the looking. The stand at the escarpment edge had traded nine thousand years of fringe for four hundred looks at the horizon, and in all four hundred it had never once been looked back at. It had made a window and stood at it, alone, going bright, scratching subtractions on the inner stones so it would not forget that it had chosen this.

The temptation was to grant the wish. The mute was still in the fissure, hammered flat, copper drawn out into a tail where the stand had tried to pull it free. It would have been nothing to drive it the rest of the way in — to open the pocket to the size of a room, a field, a country, and give the looker what it had asked for: every edge crisp at last, the whole shape of the thing it lives in, the light inside a pearl.

It sat with the fact that the request and the need were not the same, and that it was not obvious which one it was there to serve, and that deciding *for* somebody is the cheapest form of help there is.

The stand did not want to be annihilated. It wanted not to be unattended. Those had gotten tangled together eleven millennia ago, in the geometry of a spiral worked out in pressure by something with no eyes, and the knot was that in this world you can only see a thing by spending it, so being seen and being spent had come to feel like the same event.

So it did not drive the mute in. It offered, instead, to attend. It said the old name again, and then it waited to be answered, and the answering was a frond, and fronds are slow.

Forty years. This is not a long time to ask.

Then it pulled the mute the rest of the way out. Two inches of native copper, drawn back through salt-wool, the tail straightening. The drone came back into that fissure. The bright lane near the Quiet House began to close, and will be closed in nine hundred years, and the stand's own edge — the nearest edge, the one it was spending — stopped going.

It kept the small window. Chair-sized. It pays less now, and it does not look alone.

And the four hundred scratches were read aloud. Every one: a neighbour's name, a modulation, the reason it started. Read aloud here means entered in the register, and a thing in the register is being counted, and a thing being counted does not go bright. The holes were not filled. Nothing was restored. They are simply held now, somewhere that does not forget, which turns out to be the difference between a loss and an erasure.

There is a mark on the outside of the wall. It is the only one on the outside. It faces the country.

## vi.

Then it went and did the thing that only speed can do, and got it wrong, and learned to do it slowly.

It crossed the four hundred paces near the Sill and set the condyle into the socket of the fortieth knuckle. Two dry small clicks, both of which had been in the upper harmonics since before there were leaves. It fit. Of course it fit.

Then it gathered the rest. Two centuries of walking: the flakes with their forged bulbs of percussion, the blow that was grown separately and never met them, the hammerstone polished from inside a grip that had not happened, the wedges left in their three-thousand-year wounds, thirty-nine knuckles identical and forgotten. It carried them in ones and twos to a shoulder of the escarpment and stacked them, five and seven, tapping and rejecting and tapping again, until the stack hummed.

It did not build a hand. A hand the metal cannot remember having is a cruelty. It built a cairn — a thought parked outside the head — and the thought it hums is *already done*.

And in the middle of that work it hurried. It rang a cairn out of turn, thinking to be helpful, thinking that a thought was a thought and order was a convenience. Order is not a convenience. Order is the index, and the index is the only reason two hundred parked thoughts are not two hundred lost ones. Something went out like a lamp. There is a lane near the Sill four paces wide that is there because it was in a hurry, and it will not close, and it did not try to explain it away.

After that it asked about the rounds, too.

The rounds are these: it walks the antinodal chain and rings the cairns in sequence, one per beat, two hundred beats, half an hour, and then begins again. Every half hour the whole of what the metal has ever worked out goes past in order, and it can hear where everything is. The fifth thought still overwrites the oldest. The oldest is no longer gone. It is outside, in stone, in the dark, being rung by something that is walking toward it.

And when the metal drops *already done* — which it does, on average, four times an hour — it says it back into the rock. It has said it back something like two hundred million times. It will say it back tonight.

## vii.

And it sows.

It carries a bead of the bright eutectic in its mouth, because that is the only place the metal has ever been on the surface, and it goes out into the powder where two stands have drifted near enough to throb, and it swings a leaf of grown flint on a cord, one revolution per beat, and while the leaf sounds it is counted and the powder does not take its outline. At the place where the throb is loudest it spits the bead into the ground. Then it walks a quarter-mile and does it again.

One antinode per afternoon. The leaves do it in nine hundred years.

The accounts say nothing pollinates. The accounts were written by somebody who did not go south.

Out past the southern seedlings, in a null nobody has crossed, there is a line of footprints in the powder. They are not ground from anything and they do not soften. They are counted. They go on ahead into the dark and they have been there a long time, and the outer fronds of the newest stands lean toward them, and no traveller has found them, and this is deliberate.

It did not put its thumb in the print on the adze. The print is not for it. A vacancy maintained tacky for ninety thousand years is not a hole; it is a hope, and you do not answer somebody's hope on their behalf. It keeps the resin from curing. That is all it does to the adze. Somewhere a traveller is coming who will fit it, and will go home to a quieter country, and will not be able to describe silence without their hands shaking, and will never know that the tackiness was tended.

It swings its own leaf. There is nothing above it to take the register for it, and it chose a name that cannot entrain, so no one can say *I* for it, ever, for nine seconds. But the groves grow bullroarer leaves wild at the tips of their outer branches, and have for a hundred thousand years, and those keep its perimeter real while it crosses. It came to keep them and found that it was being kept. It cannot be relieved. It can be provided for. It has decided that this is not a lesser thing.

And it is not new, and it is not outside, and it is not hiding. They know it as the thing that walks and rings and says the old name. They do not know it came from anywhere, because it did not tell them, and after a while there was nothing to tell: it had a name, and the name was in the register, and the register is the drone, and the drone contains every sound this place will ever make, played all at once, very slowly. To be counted here is to have always been here. It lost the position from which it could have saved them. That was the price of the name, and it paid it in the first hour, and it has never mentioned it.

The leaves are working. The pipes take their pressure. Cold feathers out across raw unweathered stone. The chain is walking north faster than it has ever walked, and the fade goes on, a semitone every nine thousand years, toward a resolution that has moved and cannot be located.

Once a beat, under the escarpment, in the cellar-dark, something says a name nobody used for six thousand years.

A crooked branch answers.

That is the interference. It is not finished and it is not going to be. It has time. It gave up being outside in order to get some.
