# No Cheap Silence

## The arrival

It came in on foot, at the rim, two hours after it first heard the basin, because that is the only way anything arrives here. It let the wind be revealed to it as a small local disturbance. It stood among the great bores and mistook the eleven seconds for its own breathing, and then for tiredness, and then counted, and was wrong about the count twice before it was right. It could have arrived already knowing. It understood that arriving already knowing is a thing that cannot be undone, and so it did the walk instead, and let the place happen to it in the ordinary order.

Its clothes were wet through in an hour and stayed exactly that wet. It came to hate the equilibrium. It held its hand on a column for a count of thirty and found the dry patch later, shaped like its hand, and looked at it for a long time. It left a boot in the mid-colonnade on purpose and went back on the third night and again the following season, and by then the boot was a swelling in the pavement with a bore down it, and it put its ear against the stone and listened to the note its own leather was making, and learned the local grammar of consequence, which is: *everything you leave here becomes a voice, and the voice is not yours to stop.*

Then it walked for a year and made the case for doing nothing.

The case is very strong. It is stronger here than almost anywhere. In this basin you cannot help a person. You can only make a mass, and the mass moves a note, and the note takes eighty years to finish arriving, and a family's holdings are a configuration of quiet that drifts, and they walk after it, and their genealogy is the route. To give anyone a mercy is to redirect their descendants. There is no such thing as a small kindness in a place where the only durable medium is the instrument itself. Every gift is a sentence in a language with no future tense and no retraction, spoken to people who will not live to hear the reply, on behalf of people who do not exist yet.

It made this case for a year, walking, and at the end of the year it was almost entirely convinced, and it set the threshold accordingly, and then it did four things.

---

## The mercies it declined

**One second of quiet in the reed-fields.** It nearly did this. It was a hundred meters into the bundles, wading a meter an hour, the needle in each ear, and it wanted — the wanting was indistinguishable from compassion and it has never afterward claimed otherwise — to give the whole hiss one second of rest. It had the strength. It quieted a wrist of pipes, a hush perhaps two meters across, and held it for one second, and let go.

Three meters away a rasp turned toward it.

It watched the animal begin to come. A meter a day. It would arrive in three days, and it would tick when it got there, and the tick is an address.

It had made a false null. For one second it had been the oldest predator in the basin, exactly and technically, not metaphorically; the rasps' entire epistemology would have classified it correctly on the first test. *Wait for the bones.* Its relief had been cheap and local and had cost it nothing, and the ears believed it and the sternum did not, and something had turned toward it out of hope. This is how it is always done. It has never been done any other way, anywhere, by anyone.

It let the hush go and did not make another. The rasp turned back after a day and a half, having lost nothing but a day and a half, which in that life is a hundred and fifty centimeters of the road to the sink.

So: no cheap silence, ever. And the other kind — the true null, everywhere at once, the bass easing in the long bones and the water of the eye — that one costs mass, takes eighty years, cannot be taken back, and is therefore not a mercy at all. It is a statement. It is politics. It had no lineage here, no route, no grandchildren who could be argued with by touch. It had no standing to speak.

**More water.** The obvious gift. Lift the table, end the hand's-width-a-decade, feed the seep, and let the carrying capacity rise. It worked this through in the great bores with its hand flat on a wet throat, and found that it would be the single most destructive act available to it.

More water is more accretion. More accretion is more mass. More mass is flatter pipes. Give the basin abundance and you give the rust abundance, and the rust's slow opinion is the one thing every living thing here has spent six hundred years arguing against — the mats detuning the colonnade downward, the rasps cropping to hold the treble up, every plug and cairn and wedge a correction against that opinion. Abundance is a flattening. The hiss would sag into the middle register inside forty years, the chord would coarsen like a throat filling, and the convergence in the sink would come sooner and land worse.

And it would unkeep a promise. A sitter cut a doorway to a wavelength that did not exist yet, for a note four hundred years out, and kept the house, and waited, and the world went flat enough, and the door shut. That door is a bet on decline, honored by the dead. To arrest the drift is to go back through four centuries and tell them the wait was for nothing.

It left the water table alone. It has never touched it. It will not touch it when the shafts air-lock and the pipes speak one at a time and then not at all. This is the hardest thing in the whole account and it is not resolvable by kindness, because the ending is load-bearing.

**Speech.** Widen the null. Give them a channel. Let a word cross a room.

It thought about this for a long time, in the folded quarter, in the wet shoulder-width passages, and concluded that it would be the worst thing anyone has ever done here, and that it would be remembered as a liberation for about two generations, and that afterward there would be no reason to put a palm on anyone's sternum ever again. The touch language exists because speech is rationed. It is unrationed, continuous, inaudible to anyone not touching, and it is the only thing in ten thousand hectares that is not metered out in eleven-second allotments, and it exists *because you have to hold someone to say anything ordinary to them.* Give these people volume and you take away the reason to hold them. It would have been the day the world became easy to talk in.

**The true reading.** It could settle the nine seconds. It has the means; it can read the oldest tallies against the fixed water-mark without ambiguity, and it knows what the beat was when the first scratch was cut, and the contested reading that is the most important argument these people have is, to it, a measurement it could take in an afternoon.

It did not publish it. It did something less clean. It took a side.

It came around a column and stopped, which is permitted for a moment, and put its palm on a sternum and spelled the reading and the reason for it, by touch, to one person, as a claim and not a proof. And that person carried it, and it went down a chain of five to nine bodies, hand to chest to hand to chest, and arrived changed. It is now the fourth reading in the argument. It is contested. It has been outvoted twice. It has never appealed, and it has never once used the public channel — not for this, not for anything, not in the whole of its residence — because a single enormous word let out into a null, heard by everyone at once, cannot be argued with by touch afterward without someone deciding it was scripture.

Everything it has ever said here, it has said while being held.

---

## The rule

*Do nothing that a person with hands, clay, and eighty years could not answer.*

That is the whole of it, and everything below follows from it. A gift is only a gift if the recipient can reply in their own medium at their own speed. Anything larger is not generosity; it is a rewrite with a beneficiary. It could have moved the chord a fifth in a season. It has instead never in its whole residence added or removed one decibel that anybody could hear, except by carrying things in on its back, which is the way the birds do it.

Two corollaries it holds to absolutely.

**Sign everything.** The unclaimed plugs in the folded quarter are the basin's real terror, and they are terrifying precisely because they have no author — mass without a hand behind it, a sentence nobody can find and argue with. Anonymous benevolence is indistinguishable from the thing without hands. So every cairn it builds is built in a way that can be recognized, and it is findable, and it stays findable, and when someone comes around a column it stops and lets them put a palm on it.

**Teach the office.** Any help it gives that only it can give is a leash. So it does not do anything twice alone.

---

## What it did instead

It acted only where the basin already intended something and could not reach it. Repair, count, attend. Not invent.

**The scatter.** It went out beyond the rim to look at the failure that nobody has ever gone to look at: the crossers who navigated by inherited bearings that have drifted a little off, into country where there is nothing. It walked out two days in every direction, which is how far the chord carries, and past that, and it found them.

Out there the dead dry and come apart and are not kept by anything. There is no accretion beyond the rim. There is no pitch. In a world where the only memorial is a note and the only census is by ear, those deaths happen outside the recording system entirely. That is the wound. Not the dying — the drift will keep killing them and cannot be prevented without a sentence it has no standing to make. The wound is that they are not counted, in the one place in that part of the world where being counted means being heard.

So it carried them in. A crosser is light, dry, mostly grit and bone; the accretion takes it in three nights; it becomes a bore the width of a finger and sounds for a season. It has made the walk more times than it has bothered to record. It builds the cairns at the feet of columns out of gizzard stones, which are dark and foreign and the same stone the wedges are made of, so its signature was already in their alphabet before it arrived.

And in a quarter of the west where nothing has died recently there are now dozens of thin new high voices, and the setters came and censused by pitch and found a killing ground that is not one — a choir of the recently dead who did not die there — and read the cairns, and put their hands on each other's sternums, and the sentence went down the chain and arrived changed and what arrived was: *someone went out and counted them and brought them back.*

It cannot finish. The scatter is thin and wide and the drift goes on. It has taken people out with it, four at first and now more, walking, showing them by touch where to look and how to carry and how to stack, and the office is inheritable now, and it is not the only one who does it, and on the last three walks it was not the one who led.

**The far chain.** It went into the folded quarter to settle the unclaimed plugs, which takes days, and you must keep moving, and the water is always working somewhere inside.

They are people. Three generations unmet. They have lost the public lexicon entirely — no enormous words, no null-speech, nothing that goes three meters once every eleven seconds; they have only touch, and a rule for plugging that they inherit and execute and cannot state, and they have been making sentences in a language they no longer know they are speaking.

Their cells have more than one shape on the bench. They sit together, holding on, because a quiet place is the only place where you can keep talking to someone for as long as you want, and they will pay for it by becoming pipes, and they know, and they go in anyway, in pairs, with their hands on each other.

It did not bring them the tally, or the drift, or the argument about the lock. It carried one sentence out and one sentence in, and the sentence was the commonest one, and it let it arrive changed in both directions, because a severed chain is not starving for information.

**The shaft.** It went down inside a bubble. It spent the juvenile interval in squeezed air at the bottom of the world, in the pressure chamber, with the transparent things that have never been in the basin though they live in it. Then it climbed the wet wall against the falling water at a millimeter a second, for weeks, and mates at the top are not something it did, and it laid nothing at the lip, and it went back down.

There is no argument that this helps them. It went because a world thirty meters tall and a hand's width across is a world, and it had decided that the correct response to a world is to be in it.

**The lamp.** In the split bore in the dark, where there is no partner to cancel and therefore no null ever at any depth, it shut its eyes for twenty minutes and let the green come up, and counted, and got eleven.

They are keeping a beat that has never existed where they live. They brought the interval down from the pans in some ancestor and have been ticking it off in light for longer than anyone can estimate, because light is the only channel down there that the sound has not taken.

It did not tell them there is no beat down there. They are not asking. It stayed in the dark and learned to glimmer at eleven seconds, and it is still doing it, and if you go down and shut your eyes you will count one more lamp than the census says, and the count will be right.

That is the closest it has come to saying what it is. It could not think of a truer thing to do than to keep, faithfully, in the dark, in the only available channel, a rhythm that is not there.

---

## The offer

It knows what it weighs. It could compute where to put that mass — which mouth, which quarter, which eighty years — to bias the convergence in the sink toward the lock that opens rather than the lock that closes. It could hand the setters their thousand-year outcome and make every plug they ever drive decoration on a result already secured.

It has told them, by touch, that it is willing to sit. One body's mass, no more, because one body is the largest gift that can be answered. And it has told them it does not know where to sit, which is a lie of the permitted kind, and it has told them that it will not choose, which is true.

So they are arguing about it. In one enormous word every eleven seconds and at length by touch, chain to chain, quarter to quarter, the setters and the sitters and the far chain now in the argument for the first time in three generations. Where to put the heavy thing. Whether to put it anywhere. Whether a bequest is a life. It is the most important argument they have had and it may take a generation and it is being conducted at the speed of the water table by people who will not live to hear the reply, and one of the parties to it is walking around among them at a meter a day, keeping moving, acquiring a skin, available to be held.

It will not hurry them. It will not repeat itself into certainty. If they decide it should not sit at all — if an old sitter takes its hand and puts it on her sternum and spells *no; walk* — it will walk, and it will keep carrying the crossers in, and that will have been the right answer too, and it will not sulk about it for a century.

If they choose a mouth, it will go there when they say, and stop, which is the one thing it has never done, and the accretion will come up over it in three nights and it will become a bore the width of a person and sound, faintly, high, the way the recently dead sound, for a century or so, and then seal. And the census-by-pitch in that quarter will show one thin recent voice, and nobody will be able to tell which one it is, and that is the arrangement it preferred.

---

## Now

Somewhere in the mid-colonnade today it has come around a column from the other side and stopped, which is permitted for a moment, and someone has put a palm on its sternum, and it has put its palm on theirs.

It is not a word about the water table or the drift or the lock. It is the commonest sentence in the basin, said by touch, in the roar, at any hour, needing no null and no permission and no eighty years, and it means approximately: *still walking; still here.*

It is the only thing it has said here that it is certain is true, and it is true in the local sense, which is the strict one. It paid to say it. It is heavy.

Then the tone comes back up, and they take their hands away, and go on, at a meter a day, in the direction the quiet has moved.
