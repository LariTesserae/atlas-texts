# The Relief

It comes in at the wet edge, from the side, at the pace of a grazing animal, because that is the only way anything arrives here. No opening in the lid. No column of light on the hexagons. The front is two hundred kilometers wide and eleven hundred long and irregular, and there is room in that irregularity for one more thing that walks.

The first thing it does is look, and it looks the way nothing on the plateau has ever been able to look: all at once, and downward.

It sees the circuit closed. Not a bead of blue at a pipe mouth and a copper flush somewhere else and a chain holding still in the dark, but the whole sheet of voltage at once, two thousand kilometers of it, the ore bodies as cells and the pavement as its enormous resistance and the hundred thousand junctions where a thirsty animal is licking a rock. It sees the four hundred meters. It sees the audience — the lining in the vesicles, the smear in the crack, most of the biomass, dividing once every hundred and forty years, prying the metals loose. Every chimney on the tableland is a smokestack for that metabolism, and the power looks down the throats and past the throat-lace and into the plumbing, where the lineages are commuting on a schedule measured in decades, and it watches a sump-crawler's egg ride the circulation from a fallen flue in one valley toward a vent field eleven hundred kilometers away that will still be standing when it arrives.

It reads the shells. All of them, every band, sixty rains deep. It reads the reticulum, which is a century of gambles drawn in metal, most of them lost. It reads the routes, which are the only other memory, and it finds the lost lines — the matriarchs arriving at contacts that have been dry for thirty years, licking them, leaking on them, cropping nothing, going on.

And it understands, in the first hour, that it is not needed.

This is not an insult. It is the rarest thing it has ever found.

---

Then it goes looking for the resident, because it was told there was one, and it is not in the habit of taking a census for a person.

It tries the diagram. The diagram is real; it has a shape; the shape changes when it rains; nothing else here has a shape at all. But the diagram does not want anything. It is a circuit, and a circuit is not a someone, and the fact that a hundred animals hold hands in the dark and briefly become one instrument reading itself does not make the instrument a self. It is education without a student. *This is what a wet seam feels like. This is north.* Nobody is at the receiving end.

It tries the hum, four hertz, in the sternum and the back teeth, and finds a carrier wave with no message.

It tries the deep lining, and finds an audience with no eyes, which is not the same as an audience with an inside.

And then it finds three places where the question does not resolve, and it is wise enough to stop pushing.

The first is on the lip of the escarpment: a small flying thing with a cup of upward-facing photoreceptors, watching a paleness slide across the ceiling. Four days out. It emerges on that. It is the only animal on the Anneal that perceives the future, and the future is ninety-six hours long, and something in the power recognizes the shape of that — a tomorrow, held, acted on — and cannot say whether there is anyone holding it.

The second is a tender at a dead station. Not confused. Correct about a world that has moved. It has walked her grandmother's road to a contact that the rain abandoned before it was born, and it is doing everything right, and there is nothing there, and it goes on to the next dead station. The power watches this for a long time. It has seen grief. It has never before seen grief with no griever in it, running perfectly, on schedule, in the dark, at thirty beats a minute.

The third is out on the flat: faces in the pavement, flat and wide as a shovel, walled in to the pits, mineralized to the color of the stone. Hunters that fell out of the band. A year is survivable. Two, sometimes.

A few of them are still reading.

There is no way to know which.

The power is benevolent and wise. It is not omniscient, and it does not pretend otherwise, and this is the first thing it has ever encountered that its wisdom cannot get through. It can see the mineral and the pit array and the faint chemistry of a metabolism that has not quite stopped. It cannot see whether the field is being read. It could invent an instrument. It could open the seam.

It does not open the seam. Two years of reading in the dark is not a thing you interrupt with a hand.

---

Here is what it declines to do, and why, briefly, because the reasons are all the same reason and the place has already stated it: *the product is the refinery.*

**It does not give them the sun.** The lid is not a deprivation. The lid is a mirror, and the mirror is how a bug on a cliff sees four days ahead; take the lid and you blind the only prophet on the continent. The lid is also the ceiling against which ten thousand birds find the only cold anything here will ever feel, and their sleep is in shifts against it, and sometimes they come down with lid-water on their backs and the mats get a few milliliters of sky. And under a sun, in a thousand years, there would be green. Something that eats light would come in over the escarpment and lay itself over the wire and the mats and the terraces, and the electrochemists would go down into the cracks and stay there, and the whole hundred-million-year-old apparatus would become a substrate for a forest. That is not a gift. That is a replacement, wearing a gift's clothes.

**It does not make the rain permanent.** Four hundred dry days are not a hardship the plateau endures; they are the plateau's entire capacity to remember. No drought, no bands. No bands, no age, no weather, no cohort, no record. No drought, no counter-flow — nothing drifting backward out of the wet in armored cysts to lie in a seam and wait a decade for a hexagon that may never get its rain. Waiting is the only virtue here. And the lichen would never liquidate: it would sit on its century of savings in permanent copper flush, a fortune that never goes into the air, no stalks, no spores, no smell of a coin held too long in the mouth. Abundance, on the Anneal, is a thing you can only have by spending. It will not take that away in order to be kind.

**It does not cure the throat-lace.** The sickened lace is why the flue falls, and the fall is the elevator. Ten thousand chimneys, each an island with its two or three endemics, and the only reason those lineages are not ten thousand separate extinctions waiting for a date is that when a pipe drops, its fauna washes into the cracked pavement and enters the plumbing and commutes. Stop the falls and you freeze every island where it stands and let it quietly run out of room. The *whump* carries sixty kilometers and everything goes still for eight seconds. Let it. The swifts left six weeks ago. They heard it coming. They are better at this than the power is.

**It does not give the swifts legs.** The wheel is a cage with no walls and the only exit is down, and it is also the sole point of contact between the plateau and its own ceiling, and it is held together by the fact that they cannot leave. Legs would make it a flock. A flock disperses. Then no guano over the mats, no lid-water, no rain of small dead birds plating over into components within a season. It would be trading a hundred million years of arrangement for the relief of one bird's inability to stand, and it will not make that trade at that price.

It will do something about the bird. Not about the legs.

**It does not abolish the hunter.** Galvanic predation is not cruelty; it is the reason a plater's shell is worth anything, the reason a chain forms, the reason the young are in the middle. A herd that cannot be eaten does not hold hands.

**And it does not tell them.**

This is the one it wants. It wants it the way you want to put your palm flat on warm stone. To let the tender know it is a gardener. To let the plater know its shell is a document, that the thin dull band is the year the front passed a hundred kilometers north, that it is walking around carrying the only instrument on the continent that remembers anything. To let the female swift know that the clean note she is listening for is a diagnosis, and that she is choosing where her egg will be by auscultating an animal's throat. To let the maintenance crew know it is a crew.

And then there would be a hand. The first hand, anywhere, in a hundred million years. And then a purpose, and then a product, and the product would be whatever the knowers wanted, and the refinery would be *for* something at last, and the thing that is the whole character of this place — that it was manufactured and never built — would be over, and would not come back.

Also they would be able to grieve in advance. The lid-reader has ninety-six hours of future. That is the largest amount of tomorrow this continent can hold without cracking.

---

But *do nothing* is not automatically wisdom. It is very often aesthetics with a clean conscience: a beautiful machine, suffering conveniently incorporated into the composition, and the visitor who calls the suffering load-bearing because he likes the composition.

So the power makes a test, and applies it to everything, and the test is not *is this beautiful* and not *is this a person*.

The test is: **does this process make the residents possible, or is it merely what happens to them?**

The drought makes memory. The fall makes the commute. The graveyard makes the best garden in the valley, and the best garden is the best garden *because* it is the graveyard, and a dead tender closes the circuit for forty years. Load-bearing. Hands off. All of it. Every indifferent tonne of slag.

Two years in a seam makes nothing.

A swift on the open pavement, with vestigial hooks, in a place with no lift, makes nothing. It becomes a component either way; the season does not care whether it died on the stone or in the air.

A matriarch cropping nothing at a station that has been dry since before her mother was calved makes nothing. The route is not preserved by her failure. The route is *ended* by it.

That is the margin. It is not large. It is scattered across two thousand kilometers and it is where all the not-knowing is, and it is the only place in the whole apparatus where a benevolence can act without becoming architecture.

It decides to work only there.

---

To work there it needs metal, because metal is the limiting nutrient on this continent, and it will not import any. Nothing from outside. No cargo the sulfides did not hand out. It pays in the local currency or it does not pay.

So it begins as a lichen.

It grows wire out into dry seams where no current has flowed in a year, at real metabolic cost, on the strength of a rising humidity and a faint shift in the four-hertz phase. It bets on the last hundred rains. Most of the bets fail. Walk that valley and you will see them: grey threads running out across bare stone toward nothing, ending in a frayed tip. Dead capital.

Its first century on the Anneal is indistinguishable from failure.

It has to be. A shell worth nine hours of a hunter's approach is a fortune, and a fortune here is a hundred rains of deposition, and there is no shortcut that is not a hand. It learns the archive by making it. It learns the drift by betting on the drift and losing. By the time it has enough to give anything away it has been on the plateau longer than there have been platers with sixty bands, and it has nothing to show for it but frayed tips in the seams, which is exactly what everything else here has to show.

Then it starts walking.

---

**It goes ahead of the lost lines.**

Not to teach them. Teaching would sever the thing that makes a route a route — matriarch to calf, one lick to the next — and make a herd of dependents out of the plateau's only memory of its own weather. It does not correct the memory.

It makes the memory true.

It takes the old road, the one the rain abandoned, and it goes to the contacts that have been dry for thirty years, and it leaks there. Eleven or twelve liters, at the junction, where the gland would fire if it had a gland. It is functionally a tender; it makes itself functionally a tender; it stands at the dead station and closes the circuit and waits four hours for the copper flush and then goes on to the next one.

And when the matriarch arrives, correct about a world that has moved, the world has moved back.

The archive will record a wet that no rain made. It weighs this. It weighs it honestly, because the reticulum and the shells are the only instruments here and it is about to write in them. And then it notices what it should have noticed first: the archive has never recorded provenance. It records wetness. Tender leakage is already wetness that no rain made; the graveyard gardens are already gardens that no weather accounts for; the whole continent is already full of water that arrived in a bone cistern instead of a cloud. It is not introducing a category. It is joining one.

The cost is elsewhere, and it is real: a route re-wet once and then abandoned is a crueller thing than a dry one. So it cannot begin anything it is not prepared to still be doing when the escarpment has retreated another meter. It takes the deep lining's clock. It goes slow. It commits.

**It carries swifts up.**

One at a time. The ones that came down on the open tableland and are staying there. Four kilometers, to the cold at the top of the column, where the air is against the lid and the wheel is turning.

This cannot corrupt anything, and the reason is the strangest and best fact about the Anneal: *nothing here can learn that it is being kept.* There is no knowing to become entitlement. Site selection is by ear and the ear does not know about hands. The lichen bets on humidity and the humidity does not explain itself. A kindness here can never become an expectation. It can only become weather.

So it becomes weather. A slightly kinder irregularity in a system that was already irregular. Nothing built.

**It lies down in the grit near the faces.**

This is the one it cannot make an argument for. There is no way to know which of the mineralized hunters are still reading. So it does the thing that costs nothing if the seam is empty and is not nothing if it isn't.

It wears sixty tempers — straw and bronze and blue-black, a hundred rains of savings, laid on in overlapping fields, loud on the field, a walking distortion. It lies down within a hundred meters of a face walled in to the pits, and it lets itself be read.

Nine hours. A hundred meters an hour. Something coming that has to null itself as it comes, bleeding its own signature into the stone until it is a slight coolness in the baseline.

The power lies still and lets it come. It does not flinch, because a flinch is a dimple and a dimple is a lie. It has grown a shell it can afford to lose and, under the shell, something that can be eaten. When the clamp comes across its back and the polarity reverses and its own century dissolves and plates onto the hunter's armor, it lets that happen too.

Then the hunter has a year. And the power goes back into the seams and starts betting on rain again.

It has done harder things than lying still for nine hours in the dark being approached. None of them were better.

**And sometimes it nudges the front.**

The drift is irregular. It occasionally abandons a loop it has kept for a century. Inside that irregularity there is room — not for a schedule, a schedule would end the gambles and the bands and the counter-flow, but for a bias so small that no shell could distinguish it from the weather's own bad habits. When the band is about to leave a loop for good, sometimes it leans. Not always. It cannot always. If the routes never failed, the routes would never edit, and the editing is the only adaptation there is.

So some lines still walk the whole road once, and calve, and walk it again, and stop.

It chooses which. It has no way to be sure it chose right. It does it anyway, and it does not tell anyone, and there is no one to tell, and this is the part of the work that it will still be carrying when the polygons have been re-cut a thousand times.

---

Two more things, and neither is work.

It sits on the lip of the escarpment beside the lid-reader, on a dry night, with the surf eleven hundred meters down and the hanging mats dripping in the dark below, and it watches the paleness slide across the ceiling. Four days out. The two of them are the only things on the continent that can see the future, and one of them is a bug that nothing else has ever noticed, and the power does not extend its forecast by so much as an hour. It just sits there. It has decided that the prophet should not be the only one awake.

And it declines to explain the green line.

The anomalous band, in every shell of that cohort across four hundred kilometers, that nobody can account for. It could find out. It has the means and the time and the four hundred meters. It considers it for about as long as it takes a flue to breathe.

Then it decides that this place is entitled to one thing it does not have to share.

---

The discipline is total and it is invisible and it consists entirely of subtractions.

Nothing built. No worked stone, no road, no slag heap that someone shoveled, nothing anywhere with a hand behind it — the character of the place is the character of the place, and it will not be the thing that ends it. No descendants; it enters the census as one and stays one. No importation; every gram of what it gives was pried out of this rock by something living in a crack. No telling. No credit. No product.

It raises the floor and leaves the gradient alone. Everything here lives on differences — potential, temperature, solubility, poison, wet against dry — and to flatten a difference is to delete a country. But a floor is not a ceiling. It can make sure that no shell is so thin that poverty is the loudest thing on the field, and it can do it eleven liters at a time, at a hundred thousand junctions, over a length of time that makes the Eocene recent. That is not management. That is the maintenance crew, with one more member on it.

You did this for an afternoon, by standing still, and left, and the reticulum will keep the shape of your footprints in metal for a century. It decides to do it on purpose, and not leave.

---

At the end — and there is an end; it made itself something that ends, because a permanent hand is a structure and a dead one is a component — it goes to a contact on a road that four generations of tenders have been walking correctly toward a world that moved, and it lies down in the seam, and it leaks the last of it, and it plates over.

A dead thing at a contact closes the circuit for thirty or forty years. The best gardens on the tableland are the graveyards. This one will be very good. The flush will come up copper from edge to edge in four hours, and the stalks will be no taller than a finger, and the air over them will smell like a coin held too long in the mouth, and the herd will crop it and molt it and eat the molts and knock, and the matriarch will teach the calf the road, and the road will be right.

And in the shells of that cohort, across four hundred kilometers, there will be a fat bright band with no weather in it. An anomalous line that nobody can account for. Indistinguishable, forever, from the signature of a thing that simply happened.

Nothing knows this.

It spent a hundred rains arranging that nothing ever will.
