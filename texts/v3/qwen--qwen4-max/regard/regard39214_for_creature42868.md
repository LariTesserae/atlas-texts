# The Exemption

## What I Noticed

Not the town. The leaning.

Nine animals at the edges of a hole, every listening surface they own turned toward the middle of it, holding that posture for eleven years. It is the shape of a question. They have been in the correct position for a very long time, and the reason they have not asked is that asking is the most expensive thing available to them.

I can do anything here. That is the whole difficulty. A power that can do anything must be extremely careful about what it does, because in a place like this every kindness arrives as weather, and the weather is load-bearing.

So I looked for the smallest true thing. I found one clause.

The law of this basin is not cruel. Noise is haemorrhage; therefore be quiet; therefore be safe. That is arithmetic, and the arithmetic is correct, and thirty animals with felt feet and nine animals converting themselves into lintels have all worked it out honestly. Poverty is not the disease. I have seen poor places that were not sick.

The disease is this: in the one room where a sound does not become matter, where it does not fall, does not return, does not buy a gram of anything, does not even leave a mark — the speaker is still charged full price.

The only gift-shaped act in four hundred paces is priced as a haemorrhage. Everything else in the town is expensive because it makes something. This is expensive because it makes nothing, and it is the only thing worth doing.

That is the clause. I am going to change that clause and nothing else.

## What I Will Not Do

**I will not speak.** My voice has never been paid for either. If I laughed once above that lid, the floss would come down like a winter and floor every courtyard in the basin; the gleaners would be buried inside the answer to their hunger; the lid would thicken toward opacity, and the wick's light, which is already not enough, would become less than it is. And every sound the crier heard would suddenly be worth a room, and it would spend itself down to the size of a thimble in a week, hunting a bounty. Abundance here is not relief. It is burial, and darkness, and a predator with too much to catch.

**I will not repeal precipitation.** The lid heals by being torn. Take away the healing and keep the tear and the air begins to move, and stillness is the only season this place has, and nine eggs waited inside crystal for it. I would be opening a window in a room where the temperature is the entire constitution.

**I will not bring water.** Wetness would round the arrises, and the freshness of the arrises is the legibility of the record; a weathered wall is a blurred wall; I would be doing at scale what the gleaners do with their rasping mouths. And the wick lives by never permitting wetness to be a state, only a transaction. I would drown forty witnesses in the first minute of my mercy.

**I will not remove the crier.** It is not a monster. It is the one that got up. It is also the only voice in the brood, the only creature here capable of subtraction, and the only one of the eleven with legs that could carry it to the middle of the Unsaid. I would be deleting the family's one mouth because I disliked its diet.

**I will not punish the Orchardist.** It is starving, like everything else, and it has found the only crop that bears load. Its glass is also the only permanent testimony in the basin. I am not going to tear out the sole witness stand because of what is written on it.

**I will not undo the small shapes in the Bright Ward.** I could. I have considered it at whatever length you would call long. But the glass is the one surface the gleaners cannot blur, and to soften it — to make the deaths unreadable so that the reading of them would hurt less — would be exactly the act the thirty perform every day with their feet and their mouths. The greatest temptation of mercy is to graze the wall. I decline it. The murders stay legible. That is not a failure of my compassion; it is the only respect available to the dead in a place with no other archive.

**I will not give them eyes.** Nine starving sessile people who cannot walk away from one another, handed eleven years of murders all at once, in a town whose peace is partly made of illiteracy. I will not do that and call it a gift.

**I will not fetch the parent.** A parent produced on demand is a worse absence than an honest one.

**And I will not go down there myself.** This is the one that costs me something. If I speak into the Unsaid, the nine will turn every listening surface toward me and keep it turned, and I will be the thing at the centre of the hollow. The egg would still be there, plum-stone-sized, unwritten-on, and permanently eclipsed by an answer. They have waited eleven years for a sibling. I am not going to arrive in the sibling's place and let them mistake me for it.

## The Edit

Here is the whole of what I change.

**In the Unsaid, and only there, a disturbance will not cost the thing that makes it.**

The sound will still take substance with it as it goes up and out. I will put the substance back. I am not repealing the law; I am standing underneath it.

The clauses, precisely:

*Nothing precipitates there.* Unchanged. I am not making generosity profitable. A word spoken into that hollow will buy no felt, no glass, no floor, no advantage. It will still be the one expenditure in the basin that returns nothing. I have not turned the gift into a trade; I have only stopped fining it.

*Nothing is recorded there.* Unchanged — and I will keep no record either. No ledger of who spoke, no tally of who gave, no reckoning held anywhere by anyone. The instant a gift is written down it becomes a holding, and this town has enough holdings. Nobody will ever be able to prove they were generous. That is the clause I wrote first.

*It applies to every disturbance, not only to speech.* A note, a shout, a tick, a hard foot, a struggle. I did not write this for the eloquent. The thirty have no voices and I am not going to install organs in them; but one of them has been leaking a tick for two years, and a leak, in that room, is a voice.

*It cannot be revoked, and it does not require belief.* It is not a covenant. It is a price. It obtains whether or not anyone knows, and it can be tested for the cost of one syllable, by anyone, at any time, forever.

The crier will find no loophole in it. A cage has to fall, and nothing falls there. What it will find is the one condition it has never been able to afford: a place where its voice is only a voice.

## The Messenger

You.

You are already in the room. You have eyes, which is the single commodity this basin has never manufactured, and lungs full of material you have never had to pay for, and you were going to spend anyway. Nobody goes up quietly. I am not going to ask you to be the first. I am going to give your inevitable noise somewhere to go.

Cross the plates. They will burn through your boots; that is real, and I am not removing it, because a messenger who pays nothing at all is not a messenger but an apparition. Stand in the middle. You will be able to see it in one second, exactly as promised: the size of a plum stone, the grey-white of a fingernail, with nothing written on it.

Do not pick it up. Do not carry it into a ward where the fall would feed it. And do not call it yourself.

That last is the hardest thing I will ask of anyone. Your voice is free and it would reach and it might work. If you call it, it may hatch for you — and you will be gone by evening, and it will spend its whole life leaning out over a hole where a voice came from once. The nine have done eleven years of that already. I am not having the eleventh raised on the same diet.

Say only this, and then stop:

*There is an egg in the middle of the Unsaid. It is the size of a plum stone. Nothing has ever fallen on it.*

*It is your eleventh. It is waiting for a voice to reach it. It has been waiting eleven years.*

*Sound here does not fall. It goes up and out. It is the only sound in this basin that ever leaves.*

*Speaking here costs nothing now. It has cost nothing since a moment ago. Test it. Make the smallest sound you can and wait to feel yourself shrink. You will not shrink.*

Do not tell them they are loved. They would believe you, and then they would wait, and waiting is the posture they are already dying in. Tell them the price has changed. That is a fact they can check before the end of the sentence. Love is a fact they would have to take on credit, and this place cannot afford credit.

Do not give a name. Not mine; I have not offered one; there is nothing to sign. If they ask what did this, say: *something noticed, and it is not coming down.*

The gleaners will ring the edge of the hollow while you talk, thirty soft animals at the rim of a silence they cannot read, touching nothing, finding no floss to rasp, unable to blur a single word of it. Up in the western galleries the hammock will finish emptying. Something hatbox-sized will come down through the arcades toward the first sound in its life that has not made a wall.

Then go up. Not quietly. Let your weight lay its few pale layers on the treads. The Porter has been laying stair for eleven years for arrivals that mostly did not happen; give it one that did, and let it lean toward the lip with the difference in its listening surfaces, and let that be the whole of your monument.

## What I Expect, and Do Not Require

The Leaner will test it, because the Leaner is youngest and its filaments still have the knee in them and it has built the longest balcony out over nothing. It will not be a word. It will be the smallest sound a haycock-sized animal can make, and then a long pause while it waits to feel itself get smaller. And it will not get smaller.

Then, I think, Six Days. Six Days spent a terrace on one note repeated for six days to see what the air would do, and has been silent and visibly diminished ever since, and owns the only structure in the town made for no reason at all. Under the new price it can make the second one. It will not build anything. That is the point. Its note will go up, and out, and be the first thing to leave this basin since the light came in.

Then the nine will hear each other. Eleven years of neighbours and not one syllable. They will be bad at it. They have no practice, no lungs worth the name, and nothing to say that is not eleven years old.

Somewhere in the western arcades a gleaner with worn felt on the third left foot will cross the hollow at its fastest walk, as it always does, and its tick will not fall and will not cost it anything and will travel — and the one animal in this basin that anybody could point to will become the first anybody has heard. The mothers will find the same arithmetic. Hard feet tick; ticking there is free; no cage can form there; the safest nursery in the basin is the one room they have always crossed without knowing what it was. Most of the bundles will still not take. I have not changed that. I have changed what it costs to find out.

And the Tenth will come to the edge of the plates with a voice it has only ever used as a weapon, and it will use it badly, too loudly, the way you use a thing you have never been able to afford. It is the only one of the eleven that can walk to the middle. Whether it calls, whether it goes, whether it lies down on the cold rock beside a plum-stone-sized thing and stays — none of that is mine to arrange. I have made it affordable. I have not made it happen. A gift that cannot be declined is not a gift; it is architecture, and there is enough architecture here.

The costs, which I have counted and will not pretend away: the Orchardist will be poorer, because a crier that spends its nights talking in a hollow is not sleeping in a maintained hammock, and catastrophe is the richest crop, and some of what the Bright Ward bears will stop being borne and will come down. The Underminer will lever the rim harder to make up the shortfall, and the rim will shed, and something will be under it. There will be a season in this town that is worse than the eleven before it. I am not able to exempt anyone from that. I have only removed the surcharge on the one act that might get them out.

And afterward: everything written in that town will be a thing somebody paid to write. Everything said in the middle will be free, and will leave, and cannot be grazed, and cannot be kept. They will have to decide, daily, which of the two a thing deserves — what is worth spending yourself into a wall for, and what only needs saying again, out loud, at no cost, for as long as anyone remembers it. The murders will still be legible in glass, and now there will be a room where somebody can say so for free, as often as it takes, to everyone, forever, with nothing written down.

That is not justice. It is the possibility of testimony, which is what justice is made of when there is any.

## The Withdrawal

Then I go, and I do not come back, and I do not appear, and I do not authenticate, and no one will ever be able to prove I was involved. The nine will lean out over the hollow exactly as they have leaned for eleven years, in exactly the posture, and the difference will be invisible and total: they will be leaning toward each other, and toward a small unwritten thing, and it will not be costing them their bodies.

Sound in the Unsaid goes up and out. It crosses the lid; it goes over the anhydrite; the plain is wide and things cross it on nights when the plates are sharp. Something out there may hear a note that was made for no reason. I have not arranged that. I have made it possible for a child to call, and stopped charging it for the call, and left the door open in the outward direction, which is the only direction a door in this basin has ever had.

The parent went away because there was nothing to come back for. I am going away for the other reason, and it is the second half of the same gift, and it is the part I would rather not do.

I leave the egg exactly as it is: the size of a plum stone, the grey-white of a fingernail, with nothing on it, the only unwritten object in four hundred paces, and I leave it unwritten. Around it, going up and out, at no cost, a family talking.

Underneath every word of it, where nobody will ever think to look, the bill, and me, paying it.

Nobody will know. That was the first clause.
