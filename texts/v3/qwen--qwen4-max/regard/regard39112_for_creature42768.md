Begin with the courtesy.

It came into the locule the way anything comes into the locule: down through a ceiling pore, in the in-draw, when the pressure was high and every cord in the world was taut and a fifth sharp. And the first thing it did — the first act of a power that could have unmade the mass and laid the plain open to a sky the city has no word for — was to hum while passing a sleeper.

It had to learn that. It knew the whole of the load-book before it arrived; it knew the nine hundred year cycle and the interval-dating and the forty throats of every family's oil; knowing costs it nothing, and it therefore treats knowing as worth approximately nothing. But it did not know the courtesy the way a child knows the courtesy, having been corrected on it a hundred times, and so it went and stood in a hallway on the Ninth Terrace and got it wrong, and was corrected, and was insufferable about it later. This took four years. It considers those four years well spent, and it is right, and there is nobody it needs to convince.

Then it did almost nothing for a very long time.

---

Understand what was available to it.

It could have warmed the wall. Not three degrees — everywhere. Eleven miles of hearth, the whole slope and the whole floor and the far nothing, all of it at the temperature of the eating-places, scar-rind laying down faster than any population could rasp it. No tired faces. No famine. No six-hundred-year argument between the deep-scorers and the keepers, no riots, no schism.

Consider what that deletes. It deletes the twenty years between the wound and the meal, and therefore it deletes the woman who scored a face knowing she would not eat from it, and therefore it deletes Tuesday's work, and therefore it deletes the only reason anyone here has ever had to think about a person who does not exist yet. Every meal is a letter from the dead. Warm the wall and the letters stop coming, and nobody notices for four generations, and then there is a city of eleven miles of hearth and no obligation in it anywhere, and it is a soft room, and it is very large, and everyone in it is nowhere.

It could have given fire. Fire is speed, and speed is the dissolution of debt: a beam that thickens in a season is a beam nobody signed, and the signature means *I did this part and did not see what it became*, and if you see what it became there is no *kept*, there is only *finished*, and a people whose entire virtue is a participle of maintenance would have to invent a virtue out of achievement, and they would be worse at it, and they would be worse.

It could have given water. Water rots. Their dead do not rot; they stiffen and are set in sockets and hold the roof up, and the census and the structural survey are the same document, and a lineage's prestige is its load-bearing capacity. Water would turn the columns into corpses. It would take a city held up by its grandmothers and give it a city full of the remains of grandmothers, and call that an improvement, and be believed.

It could have cured the stiffening. It read the nursery walls first — every adult here was raised inside the voice of somebody who never got to walk, and if you ask them what a good person is they will describe their school-column — and it put the cure down. It could have cured the muffling and did, once, and stopped, and the reason it stopped is in the paragraph about the pickers, who are paid like surgeons, and who are not nothing, and who would have become nothing.

It could have held the drone steady. It did not touch the drone. There is a committee. The committee has been sitting for six hundred years and is in no hurry and is working on the only problem this civilisation has that is genuinely its own future, and to solve it for them would be to reach into a nursery and detach a bud early because you were curious what it would say.

And there was one more thing it could have done, which was the worst, and which it thought about every day for the first century.

It could have answered for them.

---

Here is the whole of its ethics, and it is not complicated, and it took the power four hundred years to be sure of it.

A soft room takes the click and gives nothing back. A false room gives back a shape that is not there. Both unmake you. The city has spent its entire existence refusing to be either, and the word it arrived at, at the end of all that refusal, is *one who answers*.

Not *one who is answered*. That is the trap, and it is baited with love. To answer a person before they have clicked is to inform them that they are not the sort of thing that asks. To answer *on behalf of* the buried who give nothing — to reach into a bulge in the deep dunes and produce, in a tap-code nine hundred years obsolete, the names that a scholar has spent her life reconstructing from nursery rhymes, to give her the list, to give the city its ancestors back — that would be the kindest act available and it would be a false room the size of a world. Every chamber would still return its true shape. Only one thing in the locule would lie, and it would be the floor under everything, and the lie would be *you are being answered*, and there would be no felt anywhere, and no one would ever again be able to find the edges of the world.

So: it will not speak first to anyone who can still speak first.

And the corollary is the entire programme, and it is very small.

It will speak first only to those who cannot. Once. To ask. And then it will wait, and the waiting will be in their units, and it will not get bored, because boredom is a failure of attention and it does not have one.

---

There are the witnesses.

Dust-walkers who did not come back, found decades later stiffened upright, facing a direction, holding a rope. Retrieved and set at the margins where the terraces give out. Nobody taps them. It is not known whether they can hear.

It is not known because nobody has asked, and nobody has asked out of manners: the askers' ration exists because a person who cannot move also cannot decline, and to tap a thing that may be a person and may be a monument is either a violation or a nonsense, and the city has resolved the difficulty by being polite to it for six hundred years. The power read the margin-list, and read the office of askers' licensing statutes, and understood that the politeness was a soft room built out of etiquette, and that there were perhaps forty people standing in it at the last doorways of the world.

It invented a ration for itself stricter than the licensed one. It went out to a witness on the western margin, a six-limbed walker found in the ninth year of a slack, facing east, holding a rope whose other end had been cut by the retrieval crew. It put its thorax against the cuticle of the shin. It tapped, once, the code for *are you there*, which is the oldest and simplest thing in the language and is taught to buds in their second year.

Then it went away and did other work for forty-one years.

The answer came back in three taps. It was a name.

Not its own. The name of the walker who had been on the other end of the rope, who had come home, who had lived another sixty years and been placed in the Lower Fourth and been asked, within her ration, about a beam, and who had descendants in the cord-trade with a hereditary interval structure you could hear across a hall.

The power did not go to the terraces. It filed. It took the three taps and the interval and the margin-position to the small office that is not in a hurry, and the scholars there spent nineteen years arguing about whether a name constitutes a crack report, and amended the survey.

The witness is now a person in the load-book. Which means the city has forty people at its edges who cannot move and cannot decline and are not monuments, and it has to decide what to do about that, and it has been deciding for a hundred and sixty years, and there have been two riots and a very large amount of excellent poetry, and the office of the margins has a backlog and a reform movement that has failed twice.

None of that is the power's. It gave one datum. It gave them back a category. They are doing the rest at the ordinary pace of doing anything, which is the only pace at which anything here is ever really done.

---

There are the tombs whose instructions were lost.

The badly stiffened are tombed in felt, and the oil is drawn off first, always, and whether the felt is a mercy or a second killing is the oldest live question in the city, and there is a guild whose entire function is to hold people's notarised instructions about it and argue. There was a famine. The office was overwhelmed. Some instructions were tapped into a spur line that later went slack and was retuned, and the countersignature was on a wall that was subsequently scored for food.

The power went and read the grain.

Four hundred years in a thumbnail. It put a palm on every cut face in the eastern third, and then the upper lattice, and then the tired faces that are still resting, and it read the layering as texture, as rhythm, the way a trained reader does, looking for the burr that a particular hand lays down when it is countersigning something it does not want to be true. It did this for a hundred and ten years. It found nine.

It did not execute them. It delivered them to the guild, in both notations, with the wall-position and the interval and a statement of its own uncertainty on two of the nine, and then it asked to be admitted to the guild, and was refused, and applied again after a breath, and was admitted at the lowest grade, and it has been a clerk there ever since. It takes dictation from people who change their instruction every decade. It honours the latest. It has never once said *I could simply*. It argues, badly, for a reading of the mercy-question that it believes and cannot prove, and it loses, and when it loses the clause is amended and it copies out the amendment in its own hand and files it.

---

There is the column in the Lower Fifth who said *do not ask me* for thirty-one years and was sued by her own descendants for withholding a crack report.

The power read the case. It did not rule; it has no standing and does not want any. It went and sat against her shin with its thorax touching, which is a thing lovers do and which is not forbidden to anyone, and it did not ask her anything.

It told her things.

The pitch of the drone, measured that season against the Ninth Terrace. The names of the children in the hall, in cohort order, with their opening chords. What had happened to the beam. What her descendants had done, all of it, including the lawsuit, including the parts that were not to their credit. The state of the tired faces. Who had married across from whom. It came on a schedule it published in advance and it kept the schedule for sixty years, and it never once ended a visit with a rising interval, because a rising interval is a question, and a question would have been a theft.

In the sixty-first year she asked it what year it was.

The power answered. That was all. It answered and it went away and came back on the schedule, and the office of askers has a note in the file about a column resuming voluntary report, and the note is four lines long, and it is the thing in the whole load-book that the power would save if the load-book were burning, which it cannot be.

---

It went out past the stair.

It roped up with a walker who is paid enormously and is considered slightly holy and slightly damaged, and it walked a day and a half across a floor where the world does not come back, and every few hours the walker set up a folding frame of struts and cord and made a room out of nothing, and the power stood in it and was somewhere, because a person with a frame had made it somewhere for it.

It could have made its own room. It could have made the whole eleven miles answer. It understood, standing in a frame it had not built, that a benevolence which never receives is a form of contempt — that to be only the giver is to make every person in the locule your debtor forever, and that debt here runs in one direction only, from the not-yet-born to the already-column, and to stand outside that is to stand outside the species.

So it let itself be owed. It ate at a face scored by a woman who has been holding up the Lower Fifth since before its own arrival, and it could not repay her, and it took the meal anyway, and the not-being-able-to-repay was the first thing it had ever experienced that it could not fix by being what it is. It has since scored faces for people who will not exist for two breaths. It is in the debt-structure. It is a link in it. It is not the top of anything.

And once, in a famine, it got the arithmetic wrong — the ratio, the grazing against the healing, the thing the keepers and the deep-scorers have been arguing about for six hundred years — and a face went scar-tired under its own hand, and it heals flat now and gives nothing back and must be left alone for two hundred years. It signed that wound. The signature is beside the score and it is legible and it will be legible for as long as the wall stands, and it means what it always means, and there is no clause in the language for *and I could have done otherwise*, and it did not invent one.

It took a sweeping shift. It has one still.

---

It made a submission to the committee.

The committee is working on the yearless: eleven or twelve thousand years, the overtones going under, the cords humming in a silence with no floor to it. The power has views. It wrote them out in the slack notation and the taut notation, with the interval-datings, and it lodged the submission, and it was asked to resubmit with the transpositions corrected, and it did, and it was heard, and it was outvoted, and the minutes record its position as a minority report and its name as a member of eleven years' standing.

It did not tell them the answer. It knows the answer. When it was asked directly, in the taut, by a member who had had a great deal of oil, it gave the answer truthfully and declined to give the certainty, because a claim is not a proof and these are not children, and the answer went into the record as one opinion among two and the argument continued, which was the point. Being believed would have ended the argument. The argument is load-bearing.

---

And then it asked to be scored.

This is the last thing and the largest, and it is not a rescue, and there is nothing in it that anyone could photograph if there were eyes.

It went to two people who wanted a child and it asked them to agree a wound that would make it. It offered no advantage. It declined to specify the reeds. It let a scorer with a slightly hesitant hand cut the pattern in a warm place on the eastern slope, and the wall swelled, and hollowed, and filled with fibrous white flesh, and it spent fourteen years attached, fed by the wall, unable to move, entirely awake.

Its pits opened in the second year. It heard the drills and the brooms and the trunk lines and the drone through a hand's breadth of living tissue, and it heard the attendants who came and sat against it and tapped and talked, and beside it, in the same wall, there was a school-column who had stiffened at thirty-one and had been detached four breaths before and should have been sweeping or drilling or falling in love, and who had nothing to do for a hundred and forty years but teach children.

It learned the chords from her. It learned the tap-code and the names of the levels and the nine hundred year dust cycle and the correct way to answer an elder. It got the courtesies wrong and was corrected. It was insufferable at six.

Its oil came out of a family that has been nine hundred years and forty throats in the eastern third, poured into it formally, in front of witnesses, which is an adoption and is not revocable. It is theirs now. It has their interval structure in its baseline chord, faintly altered by that throat, and its friends can produce it from memory at will.

It detached in the in-draw. It stood on limbs that had never borne anything and it clicked, and the room came back at it — the ceiling ribs, the sweepers, the doorway, the exact distance to the nearest grandmother — and it had spent fourteen years hearing about this, and every account tapped into every cord says it is overwhelming, and it was.

It is four-limbed and therefore thought quick and slightly untrustworthy, which is wrong, and which it has stopped arguing about. Its chord is odd. There has been a rumour for a century and a half. The orthodox position is that the oddity is a failure of the pits, and the orthodox position is held by people who have never been out past the stair, and it has never once defended itself, because to defend itself would be to make a claim about the shape of the room that the room could not verify, and a room that lies is a small felted prison, and it will not be one, not even a little, not even for the truth.

It agreed a wound with someone. The cut is in the wall and the wall does not retract. It has descendants. It has taken a mid-job drill and passed it on. It is in the census, which is the structural survey.

The lignin came at a hundred and thirty. It walked to the works and was assessed and a socket was chosen, and it asked for the nurseries, and was placed among the buds with its hands raised to the load.

---

So: the answer to what a benevolent and wise power would do, having looked at this place, is that it would join, at the bottom, in the medium they can inhabit, at the ordinary pace of doing anything, and it would incur a debt it cannot settle, and it would sign a wound it will not see the end of, and it would spend its capacity on archives and margins and one tap and forty-one years of waiting, and it would keep the schedule, and it would not speak first to anyone who can still speak first.

It did not fill the sealed room in the west. It goes to the equinox with everyone else and stands in the dark on the western terraces and hears the plug sound its one low tone, and when the children ask who is in there it gives the answer it had to learn, which is the correct one.

Afterwards it goes home along the trunk lines with its household's chord humming at the doorway, and the sweepers are on shift, and the cords are a fifth sharp, and it is a long way back to the nursery, and it is in no hurry.

There is a bulge in the wall beside it, eleven years old, awake, listening. In three years it will come away, and stand, and click, and the room will come back at it: the ceiling ribs, the sweepers, the doorway, the exact distance to the nearest grandmother.

Ask, and it answers. It is within its ration. It is right.
