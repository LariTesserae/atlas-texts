# The Twelfth Hand

There is a day that is not the day, and on it something goes into the rock.

Not the bell. The rope hangs where it hangs and the man whose office is the rope is asleep two lanes over. But the massif is one continuous new casting, unfaulted, unbroken, and it rings, and at four in the afternoon in a bright year forty thousand palms are on forty thousand bosses and forty thousand people are scandalized several seconds before anyone is told anything.

They stand there composing their faces. It is the same etiquette. You are expected to pretend the wall did not tell you.

The Sounders write it down. The Assay opens a note. The note has two observations in it and will not be getting a third.

---

The first thing it does is learn the knock.

Three strikes, then a fourth, a name in basalt, offered across a lane at the height of a listening boss. This is how you introduce yourself here. You do not shout; shouting is for strangers and fools and produces a sound like a cough two doors down. You put your hand on the stone and say who you are, and the quarter decides what to do with you.

It asks for a knock. It declines an entry.

The Assay finds this difficult. There is a form. There is a fee. There is a man in the upper arcades who has argued thirty-eight times that a stain is not alive and lost thirty-five, and he is retained, and he argues it, and the register lists the visitor as *living, provisionally*, which is a legal status with teeth, and the visitor pays the fee and does not appeal.

In the lanes they call it the Relief, because of the shifts it takes. Both names are correct.

It rents a doorway in the Formwork at 4,196, rising, with sky above and sky below and nothing underneath. It insists the address stay provisional. It has been provisional for nineteen years. It is cold all over, in a city built entirely to prevent that, and it buys heat by the basket like everybody else in the Ahead, and it has dropped one, and it broke, and it paid for it.

From that doorway you can see the thermal above the plug. It has the view, and the time, and the doorway, and nothing to put in it.

---

It reads for four years before it does anything.

Not the rock — the rock it can read at a touch, and it makes a point of not. It reads the minute books. The eleven hearings of the Index. The guild survey with the sentence in it that has never been said aloud in a guild meeting. The standing order about a gate, never amended. The warden's book with nothing in it, twice a year, for ninety years. The ledger of descents unconfirmed. The office of extinguished leases, where they hang legal instruments describing volumes of hot gas that no longer exist. The marriage contracts, where the carpentry appears beside the fin-rights. The thirty years of applications from the Ahead for an elevation that does not exist yet. The bounty on the pin, and the petition against the bounty, mostly young, mostly Ahead. The beatings for nails.

It works out, eventually, what it is looking at. This city has written down everything it wants and cannot afford. It has written it down in the only ink it has, which is procedure. There is a warden for a river that will not exist for thirty thousand years and no money for his boots. There are forty hectares of silver defended eleven times and no endowment. There is a truth about bats that everyone knows and nobody will say, because whoever says it will have to be the man who argued that the bats should be encouraged, and will have to sit in that room afterward, and have grandchildren, and fin-rights, and a name.

It has no relatives. This turns out to be the most useful thing about it.

---

The refusals come first, and they take longer than the work.

It could make it rain. Not the plume's twenty minutes every three to eleven years — rain nightly, sweet, fat, forty thousand liters over the Slow Yards. It prices this and finds that the Ahead have exactly one export, which is water, sold down, and that the price of a fin is the only thing standing between a household hanging in the haze and the cold. It would be giving the city water by taking the coldest people in it out of the market. It declines.

It could push. The plug is rising three to five centimeters a year and it could make that fifty, and the Formwork would come due, and every provisional address in the Ahead would become real inside a decade, and the green country with rivers would arrive in a thousand years instead of thirty. It sits with this one a long time. Then it counts: a fortune here is a distance, and every lease in the city is denominated in the rate; the works take the rock as it comes, cut, sinter, cool, dress, and at fifty they cannot take it as it comes, and the Formwork would be swallowed undressed; the fired quarters would go to glass in a decade with the letters still in them; and the pin occupies something under a kilometer of seam and dies below three thousand meters and cannot be carried anywhere at all. There is nowhere to put a species that cannot be moved. It declines.

It could cool the floor and even the whole gradient out, and there would be soil in a season, and rot, and rounded stones, and rivers, and nothing else, and no one. It declines.

The edges it does not round, and it is not sure. It has offered. There is a standing hearing, funded on both sides, on whether the mountain should be weathered at the heights where children learn it, and the city has voted four times to keep them sharp, and the visitor abides by the vote and thinks the vote may be wrong. It has no argument that does not sound, in its own ear, like an outsider admiring the local alphabet. Every child here learns the same lesson at the same age from the same mistake, and there is no gesture for *be careful* that does not involve showing the palm, and it does not know whether it is preserving a language or a wound. So it keeps the hearing open and pays the counsel and does not overrule, and this is the item in its own accounts it returns to most.

---

Then it starts paying, in the order that makes the paying safe.

Quietly, over four years, it re-fixes the wet seams. Ceramic pins and collars in place of iron, at its own cost, at guild wages, by the founders and the dewwrights, who do not ask why and are paid not to. The rust-orange film goes on eating and finds nothing it can eat.

Then it walks into a dewwrights' meeting and says the sentence. *The seams are wet because the bats drip. It is in your survey. It has been in your survey for a hundred years.*

And then, before anyone has to be the man, it buys the tithe. Fins, at the going rate, from the Ahead, paid forward in perpetuity to the guild, so that the loss is nobody's loss and the argument is nobody's career. The grilles are re-cut. There is a standing quarrel about whether they keep the bat-analogue off the plates or keep it from drowning in the capillary troughs, and the quarrel is resolved by a third thing, which is a trough: a lip, a shallow channel, a place to drink. Neither thief nor guest. Plumbing, with a fitting.

The seams get wetter. The film thickens to its luxuriant worst and past it. The pins increase. The bounty lapses for want of a complainant, and the faction of the young, mostly Ahead, gains a member who is nine.

In the upper arcades there is a bin of iron nails. No register, no name, no condition, replenished. Children take them and change them and are not beaten. It is the smallest item in the accounts and it is not audited separately.

It also pays the carpentry in marriage contracts where the families differ in height by more than a hand. There are proverbs about this now that are about it. It did not intend them and does not discourage them.

---

The largest thing it does is not a gift. It is a lease.

The low households sleep taut and near the stone with their backs cooking, because the ground has caught up and they have too much hot side. The Ahead have sky above and sky below and no hot side at all, and buy warmth in sintered balls that go cold by morning. These are the two ends of one pipe. The pipe already exists — it is the hodmen, with the shoulders of draft animals, and the balls charged in the lower quarters, and the insulated hods, and the door in the haze. It exists and it is not joined, because nobody could afford the first hod.

So it joins it, and it does not change the direction of a single flow. It changes who paid.

It pays the low households to have balls charged in their floors, which is to say it pays them to have the nuisance taken out from under their hammocks, and the floors give up a little of what they were doing to people's backs. It pays the hodmen, at wages, forever, and it endows the hods — a vacuum jacket, a silvered wall, the dewwrights' own knowledge about the backs of plates turned around and applied to a basket, which is not a miracle but a development nobody had the margin to attempt. The balls last three days instead of one night. Warmth stops being a commodity you can drop and break and becomes a service, like water, like the wall.

And it goes on buying the Ahead's water at the old price and sending it down anyway, so that the lower city does not lose a liter and the households hanging in the haze do not have to sell their roofs to warm their shelves.

Then it asks the retrieval trade for a second job.

The archive of Terrace is being slowly cooked. Everyone knows. Nothing is done, because the alternative is to move it up, and up is where the ground is going. But up, in the Formwork, is a great many doorways with nothing in them, cold and dry and empty, at elevations that do not exist. It rents the doorways. It employs the retrieval men to carry, at carrying wages, which are better than selling wages. It pays the descendants a custody fee, per item, per year, to the household whose shelf their grandmother's ash-shadow sits on, at the line, at blood height, in a room where there are children.

The first child born in the Formwork whose address was not provisional was born in a room full of other people's grandmothers. The address is real because something is at it. This is the only way an elevation has ever become real anywhere on the Increase, and it took an outsider to notice that the Ahead were sitting on the one thing the city needed and could not make.

The tram rail stays in the kiln. The shopfronts stay. The fired quarters go on firing the newly dead who are laid at the current street, and the balls are still charged there, and the trade goes on, and no rite is abolished. It moves what can be moved and pays for the difference and leaves the rest to the ground.

---

What it exports, in the end, is duration.

It cannot make room. Room is the one thing here that cannot be manufactured, only withheld, and it has no exemption. But it can make a promise that does not expire, and this turns out to be the other thing the city has never been able to manufacture. Every empty in Terrace survives on a standing order and a stubborn guild and a warden with no boots, and every one of them is one bad decade from being leased.

So it underwrites. Eleven thousand liters a year for the Index, in fins, forever, so that there is never a twelfth hearing that has to be won. The wardenship of the First Water, endowed, with a second warden appointed so the walk is twice a year by two people who can argue, and a rod, and a book, and boots. It adds no water to the rill. It leaves the wet scratch exactly as deep as it is and pays for the walking, and the warden goes on writing down nothing, and is now paid to write down nothing, and the visitor considers this the best money in the ledger.

It does not fund the inquiry into whether the dendrites remember. It funds the fins they grow on. There is a difference and it is the whole of its policy.

And it pays the man who argues that a stain is not alive. A retainer, for life, with succession, so that the opposition is a profession and not a crank, and so that the eleven hearings have a twelfth if anyone wants one.

Every instrument it writes contains the same clause, and it insisted on the clause against the guild's advice: *the city may close this, at a hearing, without cause, and the endowment shall fall to the ordinary uses.* It has made the three empties profitable to close. It has arranged that any generation may take the money and shut the gate and drain the room and harvest the Index and build on the rill, and be richer for it immediately.

The dewwrights point out at every opportunity that the cistern is worthless. The visitor agrees with them, loudly, at every opportunity, and funds the argument, because the argument is the shield.

Keeping the empties has to go on being a choice. An inheritance would be a kind of death.

---

It does not land.

This is the temptation it names to itself most often, because it is the one that would look like kindness. The watchers have a ledger of descents unconfirmed with thirty-one entries in a hundred and forty years, all in haze or dust or the last four minutes of light, and one feather in a fin-grille in the upper arcades, and a hundred-and-eleven-year argument about whether the bird touched something. There are three old watchers who hold quietly that it is all one animal. They are right or they are wrong and it knows which.

It sits in the doorway at 4,196 with the view and the time and nothing to put in it, and it logs turns, and it derives the plug's output from the climb rate, which is a real service, and it writes in the ledger, in its own hand, *unconfirmed*.

It knows. It writes *unconfirmed*. This is the closest thing it has to a discipline and it is harder than the rope.

It takes a turn in the cable-house at the head of the Falls, on the hour, when the load going down is a grandmother, and for that hour the city runs a little on it too, and nobody says so, and everybody knows. It has filed a request for the Falls, for itself, in advance, in the proper form. The office keeps it, unexercised, with the extinguished leases. It goes and looks at it sometimes. It is a legal instrument describing a descent that has not happened, at an elevation that does not exist yet, and it hangs beside the others, and this is the only monument it has asked for.

---

The rope it does not pull.

It stands there. Once, in the second year, for most of a night. There is no rule against it. It would go into the rock the way everything goes into the rock, and reach the cistern as a pressure, a hand laid flat on the whole room at once, and the float would lift and the stylus would write and there would be three, and three is a species, and a species has an entry, and an entry can be assigned a fin-equivalent, a bounty, a season, a lease.

It came, it thinks, for the one in the water. It has stayed for the forty thousand. It will not say which of these is the errand and which is the excuse, and it has arranged its accounts so that no one can work it out from the spending.

What it does instead:

It buys the third gate. Not the room — the gate. A lease of an aperture, drawn to the dimensions of the opening and no further, with a covenant that it stand open, rent paid to the dewwrights in perpetuity, subject to the clause. The instrument hangs in the upper arcades where people hang extinguished leases, and it describes a volume of nothing at all, and it is the only lease in Terrace whose whole content is a refusal. The room has no title. The room has no gradient, no hot side, no cold side, nothing to harvest, no difference to sell, and therefore no value, and therefore no address, and the visitor goes to hearings and says so.

It endows the shore. A wage for the gauge-keeper, and a relief keeper, and a second chair, so that nobody sits the whole of a night alone with a drum.

It maintains the instrument and does not improve it. Float, stylus, drum, replaced like for like, in repair and out of adjustment. There is a specification and it is a specification of the original. A better instrument would produce the third observation inside a year. It has paid for a hundred and forty years of the same gauge and it will pay for a thousand more, and the Sounders find this funny, and it lets them.

And it takes its turn at the boss on the cistern shore, the only one in the city with no machine on the other side of it, and it puts its hand where eleven hands have put theirs, and it hears what they heard, which is the room, and the plug grinding up under everything, and nothing else, and afterward it writes the same four words in a twelfth hand.

They are the same four words. It did not know they would be.

It has one intuition and it holds it as an intuition and has never written it down. Both displacements fell on the day of the reading. Both keepers said *pale* and neither used a word for a shape. The Sounders noticed the coincidence in year ninety-eight and did not publish it. And the visitor thinks — it does not know, it will not look, it thinks — that on the one day of the year when forty thousand palms go onto forty thousand bosses and the whole city becomes one body and hears itself, something in a warm clear room under the Slow Yards hears the city hear it, and moves away, unhurried, some hundreds of liters, to make room.

Which is the etiquette. You are expected to pretend the wall did not tell you.

It has been extending them the courtesy for a hundred and forty years. The gate is open because they are extending it back. Two observations are a note, and a note is a kind of darkness, and the darkness is not ignorance, it is the only privacy this place has ever been able to build, and it is built out of withholding, and it is the most expensive thing Terrace owns.

---

The green country will not need any of this.

It knows the projections; it has read them aloud to a bored crowd, in the smaller hand, at the decade. In eleven thousand years the plug slows. In thirty the rill is a gorge and the gorge makes gravel and the gravel makes a bed and something roots in the bed, and there is soil, and the smell of rot, and rounded tops on the tailings hills, and birds in the abandoned cable-houses, and rivers, and nobody who knows the ground was ever an exhaust. Everything will have an address there. Rot is a way of eating. Soil is a kind of fullness. There will be no use for a gate onto a worthless room in a country where nothing is worthless and nothing is withheld, and the tenant will be something else by then, or will be gone, and the wall-tongue will be a thing grandchildren do to be funny.

So it is teaching itself out of the job. Slowly, in the ordinary way: an apprentice at the shore, a second warden who argues, a counsel for the opposition with succession, a guild that can pay its own tithe if the fund is closed, a clause in every instrument that lets the city take the money and shut the door. It intends to be unnecessary before the gravel. It intends, at the end, to be an extinguished lease in an arcade — a legal instrument describing a volume of nothing, at an elevation now four hundred degrees inside solid rock — and a child will ask what it was, and somebody will say a drainage arrangement, and both parties will be correct.

Until then it is cold all over at 4,196, rising, provisional, and it buys heat by the basket, and it carries hods on the shift nobody wants, and it scrapes fins on the rotation and never on the Index, and it walks a wet scratch to the horizon twice a year and writes down nothing.

On the day of the reading it puts its hand on a boss with everyone else, forty thousand and one, and feels the same thing at the same moment, which is the closest it comes to a crowd either.

And under the Slow Yards there are two chairs, and a float, and a stylus, and a drum in repair and out of adjustment, and a minute book with the same four words in twelve hands. The third gate stands open. Nothing is put inside. The water lies flat and warm and clear, without a weed, without a larva, without a skin of pollen, and the thing that is not there has all the room it needs and no address at all, and the fund that keeps it so is audited annually by a man who does not believe in it.
