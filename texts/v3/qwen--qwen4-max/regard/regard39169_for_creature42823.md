# Attention

It came expecting to be needed.

That is the first thing to say, because everything afterward is a correction of it. It arrived at the rim in a south-westerly with the rain going across and the cables singing their unresolved chord, and it did what such a power does, which is look for the wound. It looked for a long time. It is not quick, but it is thorough, and the pan is eighty kilometres wide.

Then it went down the ladder into the Cathedral, out of the gale, into nineteen degrees and dead still, and sat in the leaf litter with the upholstery to its elbow and listened to one call all the way to the end.

Ninety seconds. Eight beats and a breath. Nobody had ever done this. The longest calm on record is seventy-one seconds, which is six beats, and the young men cannot hear it at all, and the old men have the notch but not the knees, and the ones who could get down and stay down had always, every time, at the moment the call stopped, turned to each other and said *did you hear that*.

That was the lock. Not the weather. The talking.

What it heard was not a lament. It had assumed a lament; everyone had assumed a lament; the jar in the museum has a question mark on it and the question mark is a kind of grief. But a middle of anything sounds like grief. Six eighths of a sentence is a moan in any language. The whole of it is not a moan. The whole of it is a location. *Here. Warm dark. Fern, nineteen degrees, still here.* And then the breath, and then, four metres away under the same canopy, in the same register, at the same length, exactly the same thing back.

Two stones telling each other where they are. All year. For forty years. It is not a cry for help. It is the sound of a room being confirmed.

The power had come to help. It stayed to listen. The change took about ninety seconds, which is the fastest event that has ever occurred on this plateau.

# What it declined

It could have stopped the wind. It ran this out properly, because it is the obvious mercy and obvious mercies deserve arithmetic. Shelter the pan and the midges get their wings back inside four generations, and the wind takes them in four seconds, and you have built a furnace of futile flight. Shelter the pan and the cushions stop being the only architecture, because architecture is only necessary where nothing can stand up, and something stands up, and the mat goes over, and four hundred thousand per square metre go with it, and eleven plant species that have never managed anywhere else go with that. The winglessness is not an injury. It is an answer, and it is a better answer than the one being offered.

It could have quieted the water. But every loud animal here is a solution to the water. Take away the noise and the high sharp frogs are shouting into a room that carries a kilometre, and every harrier on the plateau can hear each one individually, and the workshop closes — which is going to happen anyway, on schedule, in a hundred thousand years, and is the good news. To do it now would be to hand the future to the present, which has not earned it and would not survive it. And the Auditor would go blind in her ears. She does not hunt frogs. She hunts the shape of the quiet where a frog has seen a shadow. Silence has no shape. You would starve a bird by giving her a gift.

It could have healed the hearing. This was the temptation it took longest over, because it is the only thing on the pan that looks like damage and is caused by people. A notch between four and eight kilohertz, twelve months, not reversible. But the notch is the door. It is the only initiation this place has. Repair it and you put the old men back in the workshop and take them out of the second country, and they lose the frog, and they lose the arguments, and they lose the hands — forty signs, half of them for things that do not exist below two thousand metres. You would be curing them of the only thing they have that the city cannot sell them.

It could have stopped the calving. It did not, because the rafts are the mat's only voyage. An acre of four centuries comes off the stone and sails and grounds somewhere new, and that is how anything ever gets anywhere on a floor with no gradient. The butchery on the rack and the colonisation of fresh quartzite are the same event seen from two ends. To stop it would be to fix the mat in place forever and starve all the ground that has not been born yet.

And it did not tell the man with the hay knife.

This is the refusal that cost it something. He is standing on four hundred years in rubber over wool, swearing in three languages, and he does not know, and could not be expected to know. The power could have told him. It could have arranged for him to understand, once, completely, what he was cutting. It ran that out too, and the end of it was a man who cannot do the job, and the racks clog, and the head fails, and the buckets stop going over the rim on the wire, and the eels — whose entire instruction is *go where the water goes*, who cannot be taught out of it, whose route to the sea now runs through a hand — the eels stop arriving. Mercy to him is a extinction for them, delayed eighty years, decided by nobody.

So it left him not knowing. And then it noticed that the crews had already solved this, sixty years ago, without help, and had installed the exact survivable dose.

They name them. The Bishop. The Sofa. The Kettle. The Cathedral. The Auditor. The Landlady. A name is reverence at a concentration a man can work through. It is enough to make him careful and not enough to make him stop. The power did not add to this. It recognised it, which is most of what wisdom is.

It also considered telling the city. Ten million people who have heard of the Bishop is a road, and a road is a boot, and a boot is a brown seam across a crown that grows four centimetres in sixty years and will be there when the man's grandchildren are old. The city drinks the pan and tastes wet air and nothing. Let it. That is the pan's contribution to the city and the city is not going to be improved by knowing what it costs.

# What it had to work with

It did not appear. Not from modesty. Appearing would have become the largest fact on the plateau, and every other fact would have reorganised itself around it, and the entire value of this place is that it is not about anybody.

So: no thunder. Available instruments, inventoried honestly —

A roster. A purchase order. A helicopter that flies on Tuesdays. A drawer in a hut with a logbook in it. A mild February, which is within the ordinary variance of Februaries. A room with double doors and a nail with ear-defenders on it, where men sit sometimes without doing anything at all, and which is the only room on the plateau where you can hear yourself think.

That last is the whole bandwidth. It is not much of a channel into a world, but it is a real one, and it is the only place where a thought can arrive that is not the hum.

It has no hands. It has nine pairs.

# Work order

The float switch at the Cathedral had been sticky for two seasons. This was true before the power got there; it did not invent the fault, it only declined to let it be fixed by a young man in a hurry.

The job requires a full cycle observed and timed. Fourteen minutes at the bottom of the hole with a clipboard, in the wet warmth, writing down depths. It is a sitting job. It goes to the senior man, because he is senior, and because his knees can still take a ladder once, slowly, and because he is the one who has done eleven winters and cannot sleep below two thousand metres and keeps a fan running in a bedroom in a city.

He goes down. It is nineteen degrees and dead still. The moss is inhabited to the depth of his elbow. He has the notch — a year of going deaf in exactly the right place — and he has a pencil, and the pencil is the important part, because the pencil means he does not talk.

Eight beats. The breath.

And then, four metres away, one back.

He writes the depths. Underneath the depths, in the same pencil, he writes: *eight, then one back.*

He does not tell them. He comes up and does the sign for the calm — flat palm rising, then stopping — which is wrong, it was not a calm, there was ninety kilometres an hour on the pan the whole time, and the others look at him and he does it again, and they let it go, because he is the eleventh winter and they assume something has happened to him, which is correct.

And then he goes on arguing. For six more years, in the hut, with his hands, on the losing side, deliberately, about a thing he knows. Because he understands, at the bottom of a hole with a clipboard, that the argument is the human mat. It is the thing they have been building at a millimetre a season since the first crew. It is where the new ones germinate. Nobody has ever won one and the not-winning is the architecture.

He becomes a place where a thing can grow. The power had come to give one man a sentence and it got, incidentally, a cushion.

# The drawer

Here is the part that is not tender, and is the most important part, and is the reason it came.

The eel run is not a feature of the escarpment. It is a manual operation. It has been performed by hand for sixty years, which is nothing, which is one and a half eels, and it is now a stage in the life cycle of an animal: bucket, wire, man in rubber over wool. If the crews stop for two seasons the run fails. If the scheme is decommissioned the animal goes with it, in about eighty years, and no one will have decided this, and no one will notice.

That is the actual wound on this plateau, and it is not the noise or the notch or the hay knife. It is a dependency built by people, invisible, with a long fuse, wired to a decision that will be made in an office by someone who has never been up here, in a file about concrete.

The power cannot make anyone good. It can make it impossible to be bad by accident.

So it did not touch the eels. It put a page in the drawer. Not a vision; a page, in the format the scheme already uses, stapled behind the closure annex where the closure annex will one day be opened, because the only immortal medium on this plateau is administrative.

> **UPPER INTAKES — NOTE FOR CLOSURE FILE**
>
> The eel population of the pan is not self-sustaining under closure. Escarpment ascent is natural; descent is not. Descent has been performed manually at all active intakes since commissioning (see rack logs, pass-up wire, buckets, 60 yrs continuous). Estimated failure of run: 2 seasons from cessation of manual transfer. Estimated loss of population: 60–90 yrs, i.e. beyond any monitoring commitment now contemplated.
>
> This is not a fault of the scheme. It is a consequence of the scheme having worked for sixty years.
>
> If the scheme closes, the transfer must be continued by hand, or the loss must be minuted as a decision, with a name against it.
>
> Do not let this happen without anybody choosing it.

Nobody will read it for forty years. That is fine. It is not in a hurry, and neither, on this plateau, is anything else.

Beneath it, in the drawer, in pencil, in an old man's hand: *eight, then one back.* Nobody will ever know what that means. It will be kept anyway. Drawers keep things.

# The Landlady

A metre and a half of her, grey about the head, sixty years in rack bays, netted and flown from 6 to 14 when 6 was abandoned, the only animal on the pan with a service history. Every crew has known her. Every crew has raked her off and put her back. She has never gone down.

She wants to. It is the whole of her instruction, and it has been sitting in her, unsatisfied, since before the first logbook.

The power did not extend her life. That is not a gift you can give an eel; the run is the ending and the ending is the point. What it did was arrange for a man to be in the room with the double doors, not doing anything, on an evening when the helicopter was up anyway, and for a thought to arrive that was not the hum.

He could not have told you whose thought it was. That is the entire method. It is not possession and it is not a voice. It is the ordinary experience of suddenly knowing what you have been not-thinking for years.

They put her in a tank. The tank went over the rim on the wire, past eighty kilometres of cataract with ten thousand swifts going in and out of it like needles into a pincushion, and they put her in the river two thousand metres below, the one that looks like a dropped thread. She went east. Eleven months later she was in the sea, in the dark warm water where the species has always finished, and she did the thing she had been for, and it killed her.

The logbook entry is one line and is coded as a translocation, and the helicopter hour is on the maintenance budget, and it is in the drawer, above the page.

They will find her name in there, and under it the cost, and under that the reason. That is how the mercy gets into the file. It is the only route available. It runs through a hand.

# February

The otter road exists as a behaviour. Two parallel lines of steel under ten centimetres of tea-brown water, from a hole full of fish to a concrete apron out of the wind, laid in 1938 to service intakes re-sited twice, going nowhere, with one customer. The cubs learn it by whisker. It ends when one bad winter takes the old female and nobody left knows the way.

The power did not save the road. Roads like that cannot be saved; they can only be taught. So it did not spend anything on the road. It spent it on a Tuesday.

A mild February, inside the normal range. One squall three degrees warmer than it needed to be. One cub that would have gone, not going. And she follows her aunt as well as her mother, which is a thing otters do occasionally anyway.

Two knowers instead of one. That is all. Not preservation — redundancy. The same principle it applied to the beetle, four thousand animals in one seep on the north-north-east side of the Kettle and nowhere else in the universe, in a doline, on a plateau whose entire future consists of dolines deepening: one raft, in one storm, grounding on the wrong side of the wrong hole with a handful of wet green still in it.

It did not go back to check. Most such things fail. Checking is how you turn a world into a ward.

# The count

Nobody has counted the beetles. Somebody has counted the swifts.

This is the part that looks like nothing and is most of what it did. It counted them. Four thousand and something, in a seep, on a slope, under a lid of white light with weather on it. It knows the number. It knows how many frogs are in the Cathedral and how many midge larvae are in the square metre by the hut door and it knows that the Auditor has raised nine and the pan has taken seven, and it is not opposed to the pan taking seven of nine. It is opposed to seven of nine being taken and nothing anywhere knowing there were nine.

It will not publish. Publication is a road, and a road is a boot. The count is not for use. The count is the attention itself, and it is not nothing, and it is very nearly all a wise power has to give that does not damage what it loves.

# The forty-first sign

It is not going to hold the water up.

A hole will take the pan. The sheet will thin and the stone will come up blinking and the workshop will close and the quiet nation will climb out of the dolines at the speed of tree fern into a silence that carries a kilometre, and every harrier will hear each frog individually, and the eighty-kilometre bass floor will go, and the rain will keep arriving with nothing left to stand on.

To prevent that would be to keep the forest at your shins forever. It would be an eternity of ankle-deep infancy, imposed on a place that is not finished, by something that loved it too much to let it be a hundred thousand years older.

They are not short. They are early.

So it will be there. That is the commitment, and it is the only one it made: to still be listening when the first deep frog calls into a room that has finally gone quiet, and the sentence carries to the rim, and anyone at all can hear the whole of it, without a notch, without a ladder, without a clipboard, without sixty years of going deaf in exactly the right place.

It did not tell them this. It gave them one sign instead.

The hands have about forty. The sign for the calm is a flat palm rising and then stopping, and it is used in conversation more often than you would expect. The new one is the same palm, rising, and not stopping.

It came in the way these things come in — nobody remembers, somebody's second winter, a shrug that hardened into a word. It means *the sentence isn't over*. It means *there's more of this coming*. They use it about the weather, and about supper, and about the frog, and about the shift that is nearly finished and about the one that is not.

The young ones use it constantly and have no idea where it came from.

Nobody on the pan thinks it is a message.

That is how you can tell it was one.
