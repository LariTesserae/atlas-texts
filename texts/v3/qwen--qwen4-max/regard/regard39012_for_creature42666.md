It found the seam the way everything finds the seam, by hand, and then it did not go in for a hundred years.

Not hesitation. Arithmetic. It was warm, and in a room with no air movement warmth is not a comfort, it is a front, a season passing through in an hour. Everything cold-blooded and unhurried would come toward it. The fliers would turn. It would be news, and news here is not a rumour, it is a deposit: the ceiling keeps everything anyone has ever exhaled, and returns it, warm, four minutes at a time, forever. To enter at its own temperature would have been to edit the rain permanently on the first day, before it had learned anything.

So it stood outside with its palm flat on the scarp and got cold. Eleven degrees. It took a century and it did not consider this a sacrifice. It considered it the price of the door. When it went in at last, sideways, one shoulder scraping, nothing in the room turned toward it. That was the first gift, and the only one that was ever completely safe: an arrival that was not an event.

The second thing it did was a theft, committed deliberately, to learn the exchange rate. It laid a finger on a chip and looked at it and took a hair's width of light off it, and the chip was older afterward, and could not be made young. It stood in the grey middle for a long time with that small ruin in its hand and understood the terms. It could not consider this room without spending it. There would be no free inspection, no survey, no benevolent overview. Any goodness it did would have to be paid for out of the same substance as harm.

---

What it wanted to do first — and it wanted this the way a hand wants to open — was make the light travel.

It could. That was the trouble. It could have made the rind throw its brilliance across the middle, so that a thing could be known from a distance, so that seeing stopped being a meal. It stood in the shadowless dusk and worked it through, and the working-through took eleven years, which is a reasonable amount of time to spend on the largest available kindness.

If light travels, there are eyes. If there are eyes, nothing needs to be touched to be known. If nothing needs to be touched, the readers leave no trail, and the trail is the first writing in the world. The grazers stop scraping, and scraping is the only weathering there is, and weathering is what makes the stone fail, and failure is what makes light, and light is what brings the grazers. The room stops being eaten into existence. The seam-flies go deaf to their purpose, because nothing needs to be arrived at an instant early anymore. The plants lean at blank walls and are right, and being right becomes trivial. The harrow, which holds the third hour after a tick the way a bird holds a branch, loses its territories, because everything can be seen at once and there is no *early*. And in the middle, where there is no depth and no landmark, nobody is ever lost — and being lost is the invention of architecture, and there goes the shelf, the niche, the alcove, the wall.

It would have abolished the constraint and kept the creatures, which is the same as abolishing the creatures and keeping their shapes. Every single thing in this room is a solution to the problem it had come to solve.

So it did not do that, and after that the refusals came faster, because they were all the same refusal.

It did not give them numbers. One grazer visible is not a shortage; it is a shift, and the post is handed on, and the handing-on is the life cycle. Forty kinds is a count of ages. To fill the room would be to flatten a sequence into a crowd.

It did not stop the harrow. The harrow is what the grazer becomes. To prevent the predation would be to prevent the old age.

It did not spread the rot. The queue at the fungus was three days long and each animal took almost nothing, and it stood in that queue itself, in its turn, for its three days, and looked for a moment and took almost nothing, and learned its manners there. It had come intending to be careful. It was taught how by things with no eyes.

It did not hatch the waiting crowd. It listened at the brightest face, at the fire two rooms away, and it could have opened all of it in an afternoon, and the afternoon would have been the last one: that many mouths would take the glow faster than the stone could fail, and the room would go dark and full and finished. Abundance, here, is a kind of blindness arriving all at once.

It did not make a way out. Nothing in here wants one. To hatch here is to be excavated; the larvae are not trapped, they are early. An exit would not have freed them. It would have retroactively made the room a prison, and every patient thing in it a captive, and it would have done that to the past, which is the one direction harm cannot be repaired from.

And it did not say its name. That was the hardest, and it knew why, and it made itself say why plainly, standing in the middle where there is nothing to lean on: a name is the gift that keeps giving itself. The whole literature of this place is one sentence. If it had added a second sentence about itself, the ceiling would have held it, and the rain would have tasted of it, and in ten thousand years every animal in the chamber would still be drinking the announcement. It declined to be the second sentence. It wanted, if it could manage it, to be a reason the first sentence could be written again.

---

Then it discovered that doing nothing was not on offer.

It had been standing still for eleven years and there was a small cloud at the level of its collarbone. It had knelt once, briefly, to look at the three stacked chips, and the floor had read its knees. It was already in the census. It had been in the census before it arrived, at a *when* rather than a *where*, which is how everyone is in it. There is no zero here. There is no visit that leaves nothing; there is only choosing which trace, and paying for it, and being responsible for the choice.

That was the turn. It stopped asking *how do I avoid touching this* and started asking *what is the local way to be kind* — and the answer was already growing in the chips, seven of them, one always about to be.

The rosettes do not subtract. They cannot eat the glow and they do not try; they feed on the strain in a face that has not failed. They attend the *about-to*, and the about-to has no surface to dull, and so it can be attended for nothing. That is the loophole, and it is not a loophole, it is the ethics of the place, and everything here already lives by it. The seam-fly lays into cracks that have not opened. The mason builds for a geometry that does not exist. The room's entire method of loving anything is to put something where that thing will be.

So it did nothing for the mason. It laid things where the mason would be.

---

It knelt in the hollows that were already there.

It fit them. It could not tell whether it had made them and could not decide that the question was available in this room, or possibly anywhere. It put its palms on the two dull patches above, which were already dark, so that looking cost almost nothing, and it said the only sentence in the world a second time. This added no word. It added a tense. *Someone was here*, once, is a mark. Twice, it is a past.

Then it stayed.

Under its knees were two chips that had not set, in a room where nothing has set, where the whole instruction to any visitor is *do not lean on anything, whatever you lean on may not have set*. Setting is a matter of time and stillness. It had both. It gave them its stillness — a hundred years of not shifting its weight, at eleven degrees, in the dark middle, holding two small stones steady until they were finished.

There are two exceptions to the warning now. They are knee-shaped. Nobody will ever know. When the mason comes, and puts its weight down where the floor has already been dulled into the shape of kneeling, it will be held, and it will not cut itself, and it will not take the light off anything, and it will think nothing of it at all, because a floor that holds is not a miracle, it is a floor. That was the intended experience. It spent a century to make one ordinary sensation available to someone who does not exist yet.

It did not build the wall. It could see the geometry — the edge arriving in a century, the two chambers in three, the wall right the whole time — and it could have laid the courses that would be perfectly right, and it did not, because being right is the mason's work and not a thing you can hand over. It lifted one chip from the floor, and paid the hair of dullness where it took it, and set it down beside the knee-high course, within reach of a hand at the end of a two-metre scratch that stopped as if the arm tired. It did not lay it. It made the next stone easy to pick up. The wall is an egg. You do not hatch someone else's egg to save them the waiting; the waiting is the animal.

And it found one of the wrong ones. It listened along the brightest face until it found a place in the crackle where the crowd noise had a shape it could not use — an egg laid in stone that will hold for ten thousand years, in a face that will never fail, not dead and never going to be, unattended, one a century, in the dark, inside the light.

It did not break the stone. Breaking would have been a wound of brightness, the brightest thing in the world for a week, a false sun the plants would lean at and be right about for the wrong reason. Instead it laid its palm flat on the wall above the egg and looked, and kept looking, and took the light off, a hand's breadth, patiently, for years, until that patch went grey. Grey contracts differently from its neighbours. Grey fails along a new line. It used the law that makes attention expensive to make attention open something. The crack will reach the larva in a century or so, at the room's own speed, by the room's own physics, and the room will have done it.

It paid for that one and signed for it: a hand's breadth of light gone, and the shape of the chamber changed, forever, by a hand. It will not be able to say it did nothing. It arranged to be gone before the hatching.

---

It breathed once in a hundred years, and it chose where.

Over the corner. Over the thimbleful of soil — the scrapings, the droppings, the first sediment, the only substance in the chamber that has already happened — under the seventh leaf, the one that is not leaning at a wall. It let one exhalation go, warm, the only warmth it permitted itself, and the ceiling took it and kept it, and it will come back down every few days for as long as there is a ceiling.

There is nothing of itself in that breath. No name, no instruction, no promise, nothing that could be obeyed. It emptied itself of all of that first, which took longer than getting cold. What it breathed out was air that has been weather. Rain that has been rain. A river. Rounding. Transport. A century that happened somewhere, to something, and finished.

It weighed this longest, because it is the only irreversible thing it did. A stone can be put back. A breath cannot be taken out of the ceiling. It may have invented homesickness, and it will never be able to check, and it accepted that. It did it anyway, for two reasons it could stand behind. The stone here is already dreaming the furniture of older caves — shelves, niches, a wall — and a dream that is never corroborated by anything becomes a madness. And the mason is laying into a geometry it cannot verify, and the seventh leaf is about to open rooted in the past, and a past with only one flavour in it is not a past, it is appetite with a longer arm.

So the rain tastes faintly of somewhere now. Not of someone. Somewhere. Unlocatable, unactionable, four minutes at a time, and no one will ever be able to say whose.

---

It left toward the dark with its hands out, feeling for the place where the stone had stopped shining, and went out sideways through the seam it came in by, and made no new mouth.

It left the thumb-width place alone. Every account of that place is a hand's account: something withdrew. It understood the record and declined to fill it, or explain it, or match it. Its own account, when there is one, will read the same way.

Behind it, a face the size of a dinner plate came into existence and was the brightest thing in the world for a week, and had nothing to do with it. The readers arrived at its palm prints, one at a time, white on white, and took almost nothing, and dulled them a hair at a time, and the prints said what the floor has been saying since the first grain moved. The census was unchanged. It had been in it at a when.

The room is exactly as bright as it was, minus a hand's breadth. The wall is one stone easier. There is a crack coming that will reach something that had no ceremony. There are two places where you can put your weight. And the seventh leaf is leaning over the corner, and has not opened, and when it opens it will be the first thing in this world rooted in what has already happened, and it will find that what has already happened includes a cold hand that knelt in someone else's hollows and held still for a hundred years and did not eat and left the next stone within reach.

It wrote all of this in the mason's hand, so that the mason will find it and think: *I will have done this.*

That is the message. Not *you are not alone*, which makes a debtor, and not *you are watched*, which in a room where looking is eating would have been a threat. Only this, in the one sentence available, said twice, in the dark, where it can be found by putting your hand out:

Someone was here. Again.
