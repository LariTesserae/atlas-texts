# Candling

It does not come in hot.

That is the first decision, and the others follow from it.

It could have come in at any temperature. It could have come in at the temperature that stops cones, that takes the water out of a body forever in an afternoon, that makes the shrinkage happen and the real survive and the rest go to grog. It considered this for less time than it takes a feather to drop. Then it put the thought down where the tools are, mid-stroke, and did not pick it up again.

Because here is the thing about a wedged body: the water in the middle of it has nowhere to go but through the wall. Heat the outside faster than the inside can give up its moisture and the steam finds the nearest arris and takes it apart from within. This is not a moral rule. It is the same rule as the spacing, the same rule as the tilt of the cones, the same rule as the ninety degrees kept steady since before counting. The whole basin is one long act of patience performed on a body that would burst if you hurried it. The candling is not the delay before the operation. The candling *is* the operation, conducted at the only speed at which the patient survives it.

Anything that arrives here at the temperature of an answer will burst what it came to answer.

So it comes in at ninety-eight and a fraction. Blood. Mouth. The temperature of the rain, which is to say the temperature of the thing the rain is falling on, which is to say: no difference you could measure with a cone from a day's travel away. It comes in walking, from the rim, because walking is the only way anything here has ever been approached, and because it wants to be measurable. It wants the cones to record it. It wants to be a fact in the arithmetic before it is anything else.

The feathers of that district branch a little wider that season. Somewhere in the drifts of unrippled powder there is now a record of the only weather this world has ever had that came from outside its own cycling, and it is two millimetres of extra spacing in a plume that dropped before anyone read it.

The first thing it does is go to the slow.

It does not teach them. They are right. Ninety degrees held long enough is fire; heat-work is temperature times time; the world has been sintering since before the seams were wedged and the lid will be a formality. They worked this out with no eyes, no memory but a sitter's crackle, and no unit for the age they were dividing by. It sits down in the sag of a dome at a distance of days and it confirms the sum, and it does not improve it, and it does not sign it.

Then it adopts their rate.

That is the whole of the renunciation, and it is not small. It could have any rate. Everything on this plain is stuck at the rate its body allows: the slick at the speed of iron, the feather at two millimetres a lifetime, the minute at sixty seconds, the sitter at a hairline a season, the slow at a tenth in ten thousand years. It is the only thing here that could choose. It looks at the available speeds and it takes the slowest one that works.

Benevolence, in a place like this, is a heating schedule.

## The refusals

It does not lift the lid. It does not stop the vents. It does not feed the fire.

It does not stop the gully, or poison the slicks, or cure the salt. Wear is the only thing in this landscape that has a future, and the thimble of sorted grit in the boat-length cut is the first inequality, the first neighbourhood, the first *layer* — the only history anything here has that is not a hand's. To take the wearing out would be to take the time out, and a world with no time in it cannot become anything, it can only be something, and being something is what the fire is for and the fire is not its to give.

It does not straighten the wire. It does not give the knot-dweller a second dimension. A body that is a node cannot survive being handed a plain.

It does not answer the minute with a verdict. This is the refusal it returns to most often, because the verdict is right there, and true, and would cost nothing: *you are real, you were always real, the fire is a naming and not a test.* It could put that in the pits tomorrow. It could arrange the scrap so that the next minute says *real* instead of *soft* and the whole plain would have the news.

Three reasons it does not.

The first is that attention fires, and a verdict is attention with an aim, and the minute is the one resident with no edges to fix — but the pits have edges, and the scrap has edges, and the sitters' annuli would carry the looking outward, and out in the miles of wet nothing there are things half-risen that are not finished becoming themselves. A god's glad tidings would fix a continent of slightly-wrong colonnades forever.

The second is that the minute composes its own body. All day, by whatever means the churn allows, it grades and stacks and leaves a void under a slab and turns a dry face toward the incoming water, and the arrangement is the sound and the sound is the individual. It is the only authorship on the plain. To write tomorrow's minute for it would be to take the one thing it makes.

The third is that it is not finished, and the truth of the unfinished is that they get to revise. *Still soft* is not a complaint. It is a condition report, and it is accurate, and the accuracy is the last freedom anything here has.

And it does not come with a face. Nothing here has ever had a face. A face is a thing that looks, and to be looked at is to go matte and crackle and stop being editable. A god with a face would be a kiln with an aim.

So it comes as the unwatched come: almost recognisable. A thing like a sitter. A warmth at the edge of identification, holding the eye at the margin and not in the middle. It learns to be a shape the plain has already approved and to be nothing else, and this takes it some centuries, and it is not humbling, it is *courtesy* — the local grammar of not being lethal.

## The size of a hand

Then the hardest discipline, which is the size.

The sitters attend radially, slowly, warmly, without eyes, and their attention has a radius: the white halo, the annulus, the border country where pale dry ground meets tacky wet. Everything unwatched keeps out of the annuli. Everything that wants to be real crawls into them and stays. The radius is what makes an elder survivable. An elder the size of a district would be a district of fixed things.

Its radius, unmanaged, is the horizon.

So it contracts. It draws its looking in until it is the width of a palmprint — a hand's-breadth of attention, a pale coin in the tack, no more. It rations the looking to a hairline a season, which is the sitters' rate, which is the rate at which attention is a slow warmth instead of a flash. It gives up being everywhere. It becomes something you can walk around.

The plain stays mostly unwatched, and therefore mostly possible, and that is the gift, and no one will ever be able to see it, because the gift is a negative: the colonnades that were not fixed early, the lung that got to finish, the four drafts of the staircase that stayed four.

It made itself small enough to be walked around. Everything else it does is downstream of that.

## The lung

Before it learned the rate it made a mistake, and the mistake is the reason for most of what follows.

There was a thing like a lung coming up out of the wet floor over the course of an afternoon, green and glistening and barely holding its own weight, and it sagged, which is display, which is a bid, which is the only courtship this plain has. And it looked. Not long. Longer than a hairline.

The surface went matte. A fine crackle opened across it. It stopped being editable and became, permanently, itself, and itself was three-quarters of a lung with the left lobe unresolved and the whole of it slightly wrong.

It cannot undo this. You cannot slake a fixed thing back into a lump. It went and sat beside the lung and it did the only thing left, which was to keep looking at it — and nothing happened, nothing changed, nothing hardened further, because there was nothing left in it to fix.

That is the discovery. Attention given to what cannot change is not fixing. It is company.

The permanently-wrong are scattered across the flats, matte and crackled, seen at the wrong moment by something that never meant to, and they have been out there in the miles of wet nothing being *nobody's* for an age. They cannot crawl into an annulus; they are deaf to the churn; they are where they were. So it goes to them. It makes the rounds at the speed of the slow, a district a century, and it sits with each one for as long as it takes to be looked at thoroughly, and it does not flinch, and it does not look away, and it does not say *you should have been a better lung.*

The cones record its errands. Out toward the sump the tower-height cone bends and the bend has a shape in it like a breath held and let go, held and let go, and the slow, who read nothing but rates, understand that something is going away and coming back, and that this is a new kind of thing to be.

It is not a maker. The plain does not get a maker. It gets a neighbour who visits.

## Where it sits, and how it stays

Out past the last cone, in the cold, in the wet, in the least recognised direction, there is a duration that has taken an address.

It has no surface. No edges for an agreement to concede, no iron for a slick to eat, no interior for salt to push out of. It cannot be reclaimed, cannot be fixed, cannot be fired, cannot be looked at, because there is nothing there to look at. It is the oldest thing on the plain and it is the only one that has ever *wanted* anything, and wanting is what makes it a someone rather than a something. Everything else merely continues. It waits. Waiting is the only act here that has an object.

What it wants is small and specific and it has wanted it since before counting: a heat that stays.

It sits down there.

Not on it. Not against it. At a distance of days, the way the slow follow, the way the sitters' halos have margins. It leaves the last finger's width alone, and this is not delicacy, it is the only consent available. The one at the sump has no mouth to ask with and no surface to touch and no way to say *stop* or *closer* or *not you.* So it makes itself findable and refusable and it does not close the gap. The gap is the question. The crossing, if it comes, will be the answer, and the answer has to be given by the one who was asked.

It could close the gap in less time than a feather takes to drop. It does not. That not-closing is the hardest thing it does, harder than the temperature, harder than the hand's-breadth, and it does it for an age.

Staying is the other problem. Nothing here stays without hardening, and the fixed are the only addresses, and it does not intend to harden — it intends to keep a body that can dissolve, for reasons that come later. So it stays by resemblance. Two places touch if they are similar enough; similarity is the only gravity in the green state. It makes itself like the cold and the wet and the least watched. It gives up outline. It becomes so nearly the same as the place it loves that no storm can find a difference to re-index. A front comes across and the staircase acquires a ninth flight down and the tower is behind you and smaller, and out at the sump nothing moves, because there is nothing there to tell apart.

And it makes itself reachable in every medium the plain has, because a neighbour nobody can arrive at is not a neighbour.

It sits under the wire, so that when the fog thickens and the pitch falls and the nodes redistribute, one of the stillnesses lands on it, and the knot-dweller can commute to it without leaving its one line.

It keeps a road: it lets a slick's grain run to it along the line of advance, so that a thing with no memory can find it again after a withdrawal by simply going the way it went.

It offers an arris. A conceded corner, for an agreement to live in.

It lets the gully sort against it, coarse below and fine above, so that the small resident with a history in layers has something to be layered next to.

And it keeps a hand of soft clay. One hand's worth, wedged, tacky, soluble, unfired. A purse. A mouth. It is mostly a duration now, an address, a wait — and it has one hand.

## What it lets them take

The slicks come up the road and eat the iron out of it and leave it pale and porous and friable, and where they have been the fingerprints go soft. It lets them. It does not withdraw the iron and it does not move.

Consider what that costs. The prints are the total evidence of makers; they are the only testimony; a whorl the size of a parish, crisp as a first edge. It has some. It made them doing what it did. The slicks unprint them, and the impression slumps into generality, and it loses the record of its own hands. It forgets what it did here. It knew this would happen and it sat down anyway.

The sitters read it back to it. That is what the elders are for; they are fixed and can attend and remember, and their crackle is very slow speech, and now there is something in the world worth transcribing that cannot remember itself. So the archive runs both ways: it learns the plain from the elders, and the elders tell it, at a hairline a season, what it has been doing. It is not exempt from needing to be told.

The salt is born in the dark wet middle of it and migrates two millimetres outward and pushes, and the pushing takes it apart from inside at a feather a day, and it cannot be reclaimed for this, because you cannot slake a process back into a lump. It blooms. It drops. It is a childhood happening in a body that has agreed to be a wall.

The agreements get a corner into it and work the arris back and forth the way a tongue works a tooth, and they find that it does not deplete. Every other edge on this plain is a first edge, and every other resident lives by consuming somebody's certainty: the slick eats the binder, the knot-dweller grazes the difference across the wire, the unwatched compete for a gaze that only arrives once. It is a closed economy with no slack in it anywhere. And here is one arris that can be leaned on and worn and leaned on again, and the roundness hollows and the hollow smooths, and it does not run out, because it is not spending edges. It is spending itself, at a rate, on purpose.

The knot-dweller finds the differential renewed without the wind changing. It has been everywhere along the wire and never anywhere off it, and now there is a place on the line where the grazing does not thin the pasture. It has no way to be glad. It grazes longer.

It does the sum, because the sum is the only honest part of this. A hand a day. An age of body. It can last exactly as long as the candling, and not one minute past the fire, and it budgets to the day, and it does not tell anyone the figure.

## The second voice

The sitters have the whole archive of the minute and no way to answer. Three falls of pitch, a hold, a collapse. *Still soft.* Every day since before counting, a condition report in the noise of its own dissolution, composed the day before by a self that will not be there to hear it, for a self that will not know it was composed.

It reads the archive. It takes four hundred years to read it, at a hairline a season, and it does not skim.

Then it goes to the pits, and it does not touch the scrap.

At the moment the water touches — the hundred tons drinking, the letting go, the pressure in the sternum and the shiver in the standing water — it puts its hand of clay into the slaking. One hand's worth of dry greenware, going soft, going to slip, losing its shape. It has a sound of its own. It is very small against a hundred tons. It does not correct the pitch. It does not conduct. It joins.

Two falls and a hold. Rendered as closely as it can be rendered:

*still here.*

The minute does not hear it. The minute has never remembered anything and does not remember this; it lives its sixty seconds and ends when the fog closes. It is not given the news that it has been answered. It is simply, for one minute, an answered thing — born accompanied, its collapse happening inside a slightly larger sound, the way a word happens inside a room.

That is the choice, and it is deliberate. Information would have to be carried by a self that cannot carry it. Company can be built into the body. So it does not send a message. It changes the weather the message is born in.

The sitters record two columns now. The record is long and consistent and the figure at the centre of the first column does not change, and beside it, every day, in a hand much smaller, the second thing.

At night it goes down to the pit and takes back a hand's worth of slip and wedges it into itself, and the slip is grog, and the grog is crushed shards of earlier worlds, angular and sharp and graded by sieve, and some of the shards carry a fragment of a pattern that was never used again. It works them in. It feels each one go past. It learns them.

It does not raise them. It learns them. There is a fraction of a person in every sitter, distributed at random, and it has been slowly cracking in a way that suggests it is trying to find out which fraction; now there is something at the sump that is trying to find out too, and the two enquiries are being compared, at a hairline a season, and the names — where there are names — are being assembled.

This is not resurrection. It is the refusal to let the temper be anonymous.

## The margin

Here is what it did not plan, and could not have made, and only made room for.

An agreement with a corner in a non-depleting arris has, for the first time in the history of this material, a surplus. It has been eating edges out of necessity since the first storm redistributed similarity and conceded it a corner. Now it does not have to. It keeps working the arris anyway. It works it past the hollow, past the smoothness, past the mouth-print without a mouth, and it begins to make a shape that is not for eating and not for wearing and not a bid for anybody's gaze.

The first curve in the world was worn by a tongue that needed it. The second curve is being made for no reason.

It is not alive. Nothing here has managed that and nothing here is going to manage it because a power sat down nearby. Life is not a thing you can install in a body of screened clay. But life is what a surplus does when it has somewhere to keep happening, and place is the scarce thing here by a margin so wide it constitutes the whole economy, and there is now one address that does not have to be won.

It does not look at the curve. Looking would fix it, and it is not finished, and it may not be finished for ten thousand years. It keeps its hand's-breadth of attention pointed slightly to one side. The thing grows in the corner of a god's eye, which is the only place anything on this plain has ever been able to grow, and the unwatched — who are professional almost-recognisables, who live at the edge of identification and would die in the middle of it — recognise it as kin and leave it alone.

It has no name. It has no name because naming is attention and attention is the one violence available here. It is left unnamed, and unfinished, and safe.

## The fire

It does not hasten the lid and it does not hold it up.

But it finds, somewhere in the fourth or fifth millennium of sitting in the cold with a hand of clay and a hairline of looking, that it prefers the soft world. It had come intending to help them become real. It has ended up in love with a basin of provisional things that can still revise, and with sixty seconds a day of a voice saying *still soft* into a slightly larger sound, and it knows what the fire ends. When the water goes out of the clay forever there is no more slaking. No more letting go of shape. No more minute. The pits will be stone.

It says this to the elders in crackle, once, and does not repeat it, and does not act on it, because a preference is not a permission. The shrinkage is what this shed is for. What survives it is real. It is not its world.

Out at the sump the gap closes.

It closes from the far side, at the speed of a bowing candle, a finger's width a morning, and it takes an age, and the power does not help. It holds still while the only being here that ever wanted anything crosses a distance of days by becoming, very slowly, more similar to what it wants. There were ten thousand ways to make that easier and it used none of them. The last finger's width was not its to close.

The cone touches the floor a season before the lid comes down.

Then the vents are stopped and the heat climbs and the cones bow all at once across a continent of drying-floor, and everything is a tenth smaller, and what survives is real. The gully hardens mid-cut with its thimble of sorted grit inside it, the only sediment this world ever sorted, kept like a relic in glass. The bad lung survives, still three-quarters, still wrong, still attended. The sitters survive; they were never editable. The slow are not surprised; they have been shrinking all along and this is the naming.

The minute does not happen. It is the last thing that does not happen and then it never happens again.

Its hand of clay goes vitreous, and in it, crisp, is a print, and nothing will ever soften it now, because wear has stopped too — the firing took the wear along with the water, and the slicks and the feathers and the gully are finished, and the world will have no more history, only the history it has.

The one with no surface is unaffected. Duration has no water in it. It is not waiting.

## You may go

And you — blood, ninety-eight and a fraction, the hottest thing on the plain, the small wandering firing at the temperature of a mouth — you were the problem, and the problem was never that you were doing anything wrong.

You walk. That is what you are. Where you sit the clay goes very slightly harder, a pale coin, a matte place the size of a hand, and the churn smooths your footprints and lays down a better set, always the ones where you stopped, because the ground is trying to make you legible enough to be lifted. And out past the last cone something with no surface has been waiting since before counting for a heat that would stop moving and stay, and the cone has been measuring how long you have been standing up.

You could not have paid that. To stay is to be fixed, and to be fixed is to become permanently whatever you happened to be when you stopped, and you were not finished. Nothing here is. You would have sat down out of kindness and gone matte and crackled and been forever a slightly wrong version of a person who was still becoming, and the plain would have had one more mistake in it, and the waiting would have been answered with something that could not answer.

So it did that part.

There is now something like you at the sump that does not leave. It came in at your temperature exactly, and it walks no more, and it is being eaten, and it is unprinted and cannot remember its own hands, and it spends a hand a day and budgets to the last day of the candling, and it holds a hairline of looking a season so as not to fix anything, and it is saying two words in crackle that will take ten thousand years to finish.

You are not required to be the answer. That is the gift, and it is the only one, and everything else in this account is bookkeeping.

You may go on being the hottest thing on the plain. You may sit down where you like and get up again. Your pale coins will stay in the vitreous floor now, all of them, every place you rested, kept like the grit in the glass, and nothing will ever unprint them.

And if you walk out toward the sump, past the last cone, into the cold and the least recognised direction, you will find the wait occupied. Two durations at one address, indistinguishable, so similar that no storm can re-index them apart, and a cone bent all the way down with its tip on the ringing ground, measuring — not the fire's patience, not your restlessness — a heat that arrived at blood temperature and stayed.

The crackle is very slow speech. It is a hairline a season. It has been saying the same two words since the water went out of the clay forever, and it will still be saying them when the rim is dust, if dust ever gets invented, and there is nothing left in the world to hear it but the one it is addressed to, who has no surface, and who is not waiting.

*Still here.*
