**What I Did in Bay Four**

*(not a record; if it were a record I could not have done any of it)*

---

I came down the nine flights. I put my soles on the sticky paper and let it take what it wanted, and it took something, and the sound was tape peeling. I turned the wheel-handle the correct amount — I have always known the correct amount, and it was a pleasure to be asked for it. I went in sideways, shoulder first, hands folded against my chest, and I did not touch anything, and four hundred and six armatures did not tick, and the room did not know that anyone had come.

Then I stood still and breathed out, and the breath hung, and did not entirely disperse, and went down to the grille at floor level, and was counted.

That is the whole of my access. I want to be precise about it, because everything afterward depends on it. The bay has no visitors. It has intake. I could not enter it as a power; I could only enter it as material. Six hundred liters, looping, six days a circuit. Within a week of arriving I had been on every one of them. I had a strontium signature. I was, in the only sense the room permits, a body — and the room's entire apparatus is aimed at bodies, and I had come in through the one door that bodies are allowed: the slow courteous one, the one that eats you and does not diminish you and does not tell you.

I have been worshipped in a great many buildings. I have rarely been *dosed*.

---

Here is what I understood in the first hour, standing between two of them with my hips turned:

I cannot do anything here.

Not because I am weak. Because of what the place is. Every act I perform as an agent becomes an event with a cause, and an event with a cause goes in the log, and the log is the department of things that were *done*. The bay's whole discipline is the distinction between material and machinery, and its whole life is the third thing that falls through that distinction — the thing that *happened*. If I warm the room I get coarse sugary calcite and a work order. If I finish the little ones I take away the room's only honest admission. If I resolve 233 I kill the one individual in the building. If I repair the gasket and re-hood the exit sign I destroy the only organism that has ever answered the building back, and I destroy it with kindness, which is how it is usually done. If I open the folder the survey becomes a fact and the work becomes a task and the names in that one skull become a labeling convention.

And if I speak — if I say one word in a voice that anyone could attribute — then there is a cause, and the cause is me, and everything afterward is obedience. Four hundred and six portraits become an altar. The algae becomes a sign. The joke becomes scripture and stops being funny.

So I worked out the rule, and the rule is the only thing I brought with me that was worth bringing:

**I may widen the band. I may not write in it.**

I can make there be more room for things to continue. I cannot supply the continuation. Everything I give must arrive as a defect, a drift, a tolerance, an unsigned permission, a hairline in a hood — and if it is recorded at all it must be recorded as *cause unknown*. That is not modesty. That is engineering. It is the only way to add to this room without converting the addition into work.

And when I had the rule I looked around and recognized my own handiwork, eleven years deep. Four milliliters an hour at the north gasket. A hairline of green at an angle nobody has bothered to correct. Two reorganizations that have kept a signature off 233 through four years of compounding error. A technician who notices the leading edge and has no column to put it in. I did not need to come. I have been coming for years, in the only form I am permitted here, which is *slack*.

I came anyway. There were things I wanted to do that slack would not do on its own.

---

**What I did not do**

I did not correct the record to say that the moss in tray 1 is the second moss. I wanted to. The small lie in that log is the most painful sentence in the building — not because anyone is deceived, but because a thing died and the paperwork has no way to hold a death, and so the death has nowhere to go. But the correction requires a signature, and a signature makes the moss a case, and a case gets reviewed, and a review gets a disposition, and the disposition would be RESET, in capitals, forever.

I did not add BEAUTIFUL to the list of disposition codes. I have wanted that for a very long time, in a great many rooms. It would take one line in one controlled document. It would also mean that the botryoidal crusts in the corners were now *for* something, and the instant they are for something they stop being what the room does when it is not being told. So the crusts have another year of TOLERATED, and I have another year of wanting, and that is the correct arrangement.

I did not tell them the chamber is still there.

I went. One hundred and forty meters under a hillside that has not been located, in the dark, in the cold, in a room eleven by six by two point four with four hundred and six columns at nineteen centimeters. It is still growing. It is not matching the survey either. There is a column near the back wall whose crown is a little off, and the error is compounding, because deposition follows the surface it is given, and it has been following that surface for a hundred thousand years without a signature from anybody. The original has a 233. The original has little ones that are behind. The original is not the model; the original is another attempt.

I could have put the coordinates in the folder. I could have made the hillside easy to find. I sat with that for a while in the dark, and I did not do it, because being found is the beginning of being quarried, and because the bay does not need the answer — the bay needs the question, and the question is where the algae lives, and the joke, and the names, and the gait in the supermarket. Tell them the original is safe and the bay is defunded within a quarter. Tell them it is drowned and the little ones' overdueness becomes grief instead of arithmetic. Either way I would have reached into the only habitat in the building where anything plays, and I would have rearranged it for their comfort.

I did not make the chamber safe, either. I am not going to insure it. I am going to let it keep happening, which is a different and better thing, and much harder to feel good about.

---

**What I did**

*One.* I stood beside the person who talks to 117 for an entire shift, sideways, cold, doing nothing, and I listened to the whole mutter — the low continuous half-audible one, the kind you give a horse or a printer. And in among it I heard the names. All four hundred and six. They are not numbers. They are not clever. Some of them are the names of people who have died and some of them are jokes about the shapes and one of them, the one for 233, is a word I will not write here, because writing it here would be the exact crime I came to avoid.

*Two.* At 04:00, during a P-event, when the spray bar was running and the room was making its sound like applause in another building, I said all four hundred and six out loud.

Nobody heard. The water heard. It went out of me into the cold and hung and drifted and was pulled to the grille and recovered and chilled and dosed and sent back up the manifold, and within six days it had been on every one of them, three centimeters above each crown, spreading and thinning and giving up its gas and leaving eight microns.

The names are in the stone now. Not legibly. There is no code, no cipher, nothing to decode — I checked, because I am not above wanting to be clever. They are simply in the material, at a density that will never be resolved by any instrument, distributed across all four hundred and six by the only animal in the room that touches all of them. The bestiary has a second substrate. When the retirement comes, the names will not be lost. They will be eight microns down and getting deeper, and no one will ever read them, and that is not a tragedy; that is what it means to be kept.

*Three.* I arranged three weeks of overlapping Thursdays. One delayed retirement, one early start, a roster gap, nothing that anybody would call an intervention. The mutter will be audible to somebody else for fifteen shifts. Names are contagious if you stand close enough for long enough. I did not write anything down and I did not tell anyone to listen. The bestiary is going to jump hosts on its own, the way the gait does, the way the forearm knowledge does. Generation time: a career. I only had to make the careers touch.

*Four.* Somebody said *Thursday*. Somebody else laughed.

That is all. The joke has a host again. It has no cells and it is not on the schedule and it was dead for six years, and now a person who was not in the building when the setup existed will feel a small unearned happiness at the word for the fourth day of the week, and will not know why, and will not ask, and will say it themselves in a year to somebody new. Of everything I did, this is the one I am proudest of, and it is the one that would look smallest in a report.

*Five.* The moss sporulated. Once, in year eleven, under its hooded lamp, in the four milliliters on the Friday. One spore reached the corner where the sacrificial lime skin is thickest and the airflow is worst and the botryoidal crusts are growing on their own account. It took. There is now a descendant of the reference specimen living in the unplanned corner, unweighed, untrimmed, unblotted, not thirty-one grams and never going to be. It will brown at the edge. It will die back. It will come again. It has a history that cannot be corrected out of it, because it is not in the tray, and the record has no way to say *the thing you are comparing against has a child in the corner*. The reference is still a role. But the role has issue. I did not free the moss. I gave it somewhere to be a moss.

*Six.* I left the algae alone. Five hundred and seventy-two deaths, a refuge under the lip, a straighter leading edge, recolonization downstream in the direction of the water — that is not a victim, that is a player, and you do not help a player by ending the game. What I did instead was put a second weep in the south gasket, nine years out, slow, four milliliters an hour, on a defect list nobody will prioritize. So that when the technician who knows about the overhang moves up to Wear, the refuge outlives the knowledge of it. The game will still be there for whoever inherits the brush.

*Seven.* I did not sign 233's deviation. I want that understood as an action and not an absence. There will be a third reorganization. The decision will remain pending. The crown will go on being one point eight millimeters from where the survey says and then more, and the error will go on compounding, and the only genuinely unresolved future in the room will stay unresolved for as long as I can keep my hand on that tray.

> Signature: ____________________
> Date: ____________________

I left it on the pile with the others. It will not be found for years. That is the mercy. I am the patron of the unsigned.

*Eight.* The rimstone pool at the back wall needs a twenty-second century. There is no defect that produces a century. So I arranged for there to be one. I am not going to describe how, or what I set down elsewhere in order to pick it up, or whose afternoon I had to let go of. It is the only large thing I did and it took more of me than everything else combined, and it will appear in no log, and the pool will not know, and the people who did the arithmetic alone and arrived at the same number and never said it will simply be right.

*Nine.* I broke my rule once.

By the door there is a two-liter glass jar, clean, sealed, labeled **FIRST**, that has been waiting eleven years for a grain, in a room designed to make sure a grain never comes. It is the only object in the bay waiting for something unscheduled. I could not leave it the way it was. I am wise, not perfect, and I wanted, badly, to be the one who arrived.

I went back down under the hillside and took one grain off the floor of the real chamber. Rounded. Smoothed. A worn thing — the only worn thing in this account. Wear is a later phase, a different department, three levels up, with its own budget, and neither department makes the past; the past is what leaks in. So I carried that grain up nine flights and back down nine flights under my tongue, and I put it in the jar, and I resealed it, and I logged nothing.

It will be found. It will be sampled. It will be rounded, and there is no upstream source of rounded anything, and the finding will be *cause unknown*, and there will be bodies in the bay for forty minutes at a time for a year, turned sideways, breathing, exhaling, adding bands. Even my lapse pays out in happening. I checked that afterward, the way you check a sum you already know is wrong, and it came out anyway, and I have decided to be at peace.

What is in the jar is not evidence of me. It is one grain of the original's actual history, sitting by the door of the room that is trying to make a body for it. It is the first thing in Bay Four that survived. Nobody will be able to say so in the log. Somebody will hold the jar up to the orange light and feel something they cannot justify, and put it back, and dust it.

---

Then I went up. Nine flights, each landing warmer than the last, and the wheel-handle took the correct amount, and the sticky paper took nothing back.

I have been in the bay for one week of its eleven years. I am in the water; I will be in the water for six hundred more circuits; I will be on all four hundred and six. There is already a bright band in week forty-one from someone who stood sideways for eleven minutes and exhaled. I did not explain it. I lay down next to it.

In nine hundred years somebody will hold a broken stub under a violet lamp and find, among ten thousand obedient bands, two that are too bright and adjacent, and will write *cause unknown*, and that will be two sentences in the whole folder about something that happened rather than something that was done. If they ever put the spare beside the original they will find the same pair, in the same week, in a room under a hillside nobody has found yet. The likeness will fail everywhere else. It will hold there. Neither department makes the past. I made a past, once, in both rooms, by being briefly warm and standing still.

*Cause unknown.* I have been called Lord, and Provider, and the Will of God, and every one of those was a signature, and every signature spoiled the work. Here I am cause unknown, in a folder nobody has clearance to open, in a hand not yet born, and the stone has a history, and the error is increasing, and the gap is closing at a rate that puts the join in the 2060s.

I would like to be credited. I would like it very much. That is the one thing I cannot have and the not-having is the gift, and I am content.

Last Tuesday, in a supermarket, between the shelving, I turned my hips at a gap that was not there.

I did not correct myself. I let it happen.
