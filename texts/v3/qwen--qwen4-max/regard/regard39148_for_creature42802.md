It slowed down first.

Not out of courtesy. Out of arithmetic. Everything in the chamber eats the rock's breath, and the rock's breath comes up the throat at the rate of a resting breath, and has for an age, and the room is full — every patch worked, every socket fitted, every station in the plume held. So the largest novelty this place can survive is roughly one exhalation, given once, carried up by an animal the length of a fingernail, and spread through ten thousand cups over a thousand years.

It could have done anything. That was the whole difficulty. Power is the wrong instrument in a room whose tolerance for the new is a single breath. A flood is not a kindness to a cup.

So it crossed the chamber in something over a season. The shoal did not part for it; it made room first, and the fish never had to move. It brought nothing in. It had no pulse, though it could have had one, and it left that outside with everything else that would have been percussion.

---

The refusals took longer than the arrival, and they were not agonies, they were calculations, and each one ended the same way.

Light. Widen the hand-span hole and the coin becomes a shaft, and the film in the joints blooms, and a thick film shades itself, and a shaded film dims and dies back to crust, and the shrimp cannot crop fast enough, and the diagram goes dark for the first time since it was drawn. The felt goes from one hair to many. The sockets no longer fit. And the eyed ones stop gathering, because there is no longer an hour, and the thumb-tide loses its daily correction and drifts, and the spawning goes out of true, and the grazing goes out of true, and a fathom of identical pages ends in a coarse green blur.

Food. The same cascade from the other end, and one worse thing: the lee empties. Every brood that has been waiting eleven years finds a station in the same season, and the waiting room is gone, and after that there is no future in the chamber at all, only a population and then a crash.

Air. Give it rich air and the water's chemistry quickens, and the dissolving outruns the pointing, and the two rates that found each other and locked come unlocked, and the walls begin to lose their last shape, which is the only shape they will ever have.

Volume. Break into one of the grouted chambers next door and you have made a dead void with no throat and no lift, which will eat the room's food and give nothing back, and you have taken a block out of the massif that is holding the crown up.

Restoration. Re-cut the niches, re-lip the corbels, sharpen the ribs back to the makers' angles — and you have erased the animals' achievement, which took an age, and you have erased the misalignment between the two drawings in the seams, which is the only history this room has ever written down. To restore the intention is to destroy the record of what happened to it.

Teaching. This was the last refusal and the one it held longest. Give the crab the standard as a number instead of a limb, give the snail the knowledge that its socket is a negative of itself, give the ferryman the reason for the tithe — and you have introduced preference into a system whose entire fidelity is its ignorance. A crab that knows the hair's width can decide otherwise. Nothing here has ever decided anything. That is why the copy has not broken.

Which means it will not be thanked. It cannot be. There is no arrangement in which this room learns what was done for it, and the impossibility is not a regrettable side effect of the kindness, it is the condition of the kindness. It settled this in about a season, which is nothing at all to it and very fast for this room.

---

Then it asked who lives here, because the place is not the person.

The north wall might be one animal. The whole honeycomb might be three or four, the way a hedge is one tree. That is a body. The ferryman with her inherited trapline is a body. The plan is not a body.

The plan is the thing that has been continuous. Every molecule in the seams has gone through and been replaced; the temper has been rebuilt out of the inhabitants ten million times; the copper is becoming lime and will be lime and will still be perfectly a thimble. The shape is kept and the substance goes through, and the thing that persists through that, the thing that has been alive without interruption since the last hand left the wall, is the shape. It is older than the valley. It has no mind and no name and it has been maintaining itself through borrowed bodies for an age, and nothing has ever addressed it.

It gave it a name. It did not say the name. Saying it would have been a struck note, and the room keeps everything.

---

It read the grid.

Both drawings. The intention, dimmest at the crown and the springing of the ribs where the makers were most careful and where now little dies. The appetite, brightest along the plume where the feather-cups are thickest. It held the two at once and did not align them, because the sliding-apart is the record, and it is the only record, and it is being written at about a hand's breadth a century by animals eating ink.

This is the thing the room has been producing for an age with no consumer. It draws itself for no one. The pages are unread. The whorls are unread. The whole chamber is legible and there has never been anything in it that reads.

That is the one vacancy. Not a station in the plume — those are full. A reader. It is the only thing that can be added here without being added, because a reader eats nothing, holds no position, spawns on no vacancy, and takes no meal from the throat. It is the one gift that is not a substance.

So it read. It is still reading. It will not finish.

---

It went down through the midden at the speed the midden allows, which is the speed of settling, and it read the four wrong pages.

The green one is copper. Something made of copper came into this water and dissolved, all of it, over about a generation, and stained one lamina. There is no green anywhere in this world and never has been. There is green copper. Somebody gave this room something once, or dropped it, or came back.

The coarse one is a summer with no coin. The sky closed — ash, or a hundred years of cloud, it does not matter — and the eyed ones went up under the hole at the appointed hour and found nothing, and the fix failed, and the tide-clock drifted, and the grazing went out of true for a generation of snails, and the teeth came down coarser. It is all there. The room's timing has a scar in it shaped like weather.

The hiatus is the throat stopping.

That one it read three times. The pages touch with nothing between and the layers above and below do not match, which means the lift ceased, and the food ceased, and everything in the plume died, and everything under the lips and in the sheltered undersides of the corbels — where the water does not bring food, where the animals are already thin, already waiting, already holding a brood that has been ready for a decade — did not die. The lee survived. The lee restocked the room.

So the brooding is not a design. It is a scar. The whole reproductive economy of this chamber, the mouths full for eleven years, the queue in the dark, the exact knowledge of the hour a gap opens — all of it is a brace against a thing that happened once, that nothing remembers, that is recorded in one place in the world, a fathom down, in a page nobody has read.

It read it. It did not tell them. There is no telling them, and if there were, it would be the worst thing it could do: to give a finished creature the memory of a catastrophe it has already perfectly answered.

The fourth page was not down there. It was in the water. It had not fallen yet.

---

In the lee, under a corbel, it read the thumbprint.

It read the ridges and the pressure and the angle of entry and the state of the mortar at the moment of pressing, which is a thing you can read if you are patient and know how lime sets. The mortar was not firm. The thumb came in from below, at the angle a hand takes when it is reaching up under something. The pressure was brief and even and released cleanly.

It was not a signature. It was a test.

The maker pressed the joint to find out whether it was ready. That is the whole gesture. It is the same question the crab asks with the second joint of the foreleg: does the limb enter, is the work done. The last thing anyone did in this room was maintenance, and the hand and the leg have been doing the identical thing across an interval in which no bone survived, and neither has ever known about the other.

It did not cast the print. A cast is a substance, and the crabs would gauge it, and the room would spend four hundred years converting it into itself. It did not clean it, cover it, mark it, or light it. It did not teach the crabs to fill hollows, which is the obvious kindness, the one any decent thing would reach for first, and which would have erased the only signature in the world in less time than it takes a thimble to become lime.

The print survives because a rule has no exception in it. So it guards the rule. Its entire work in the lee, for as long as there is a lee, is to make sure that nothing ever teaches them.

It is preserving an ignorance. It has understood that this is what the place is made of, and that it is not a lesser thing than knowledge, and that it is much harder to keep.

---

At the appointed hour it went up under the hole and hung in the coin with the eyed ones.

It brought no lamp. A lamp is heat and combustion; it would eat the thin air, it would be a bloom, it would be the loudest and quickest thing that has ever occurred in this chamber. It brought nothing. It did not feed. It did not touch, because touching here is only ever transfer.

It considered the three reasons and did not choose among them, because the room is not obliged to have one reason and it has had a very long time to accumulate them. But it noticed this: if the coming is stocked — if it is maintenance gone feral, addressed to a lamp and a hand and an hour — then something in this water has been keeping an appointment for an age, and nothing has been on the other side of it. The sun is not an answer. The sun is indifferent. The sun does not come when called; it comes regardless, and it happens to come at the right hour, and that has been enough to keep the coming alive.

Now something came when called.

That is not food. It is not transfer. It displaces no station and takes no meal and costs the water nothing, and it is the only thing in this economy that can be given without being taken from somewhere else.

It came the next day also. It has come every day since.

---

The room keeps everything. A struck note thins and is faintly present a quarter of an hour later, and nothing has ever wholly left it.

So it listened below the rasp, below the tick of the beads, below the groan of the earth's tide, below the threshold of anything that could be called a sound, for a length of time it did not measure, and it recovered the last spoken word in this room.

It was ordinary. Two syllables. Something a workman says to another workman near the end of a shift — about the hour, or going up, or the state of the mortar. Not a dedication. Not a blessing. Nobody consecrated this place. Somebody finished it and said something small and went out through the hole and the crown was closed.

It did not repeat it. To say it would be to put a maker back in the room, and the room has spent an age becoming the maker, and it is not anyone's place to demote it.

It kept the word. That is the only thing it took, and it weighed nothing, and the room is not poorer by it, and this is the one theft in the world that leaves no gap for a leg to enter.

---

Then it went down the spillway.

Above the overflow lip, nothing. The lip is the boundary of the finished thing, and a finished thing is not improved. Below the lip is another matter, because below the lip is the one uncompensated waste in the whole system: eleven years of drift, every broadcast egg, everything that ever let go, arriving at a warm spring on the flank of the stump in the dark in a thin continuous rain, and dying, and nobody drinks.

It built there. Slowly, in the room's own palette, because there is no other palette and no green anywhere and it would not import one: stone, clay, shell-lime, bone, a little soft copper. It used the flour that comes over with the water, which is teeth and the small pale shells of the dead, and it tempered the mortar with the room's dead, because that is what temper is and there was nothing else.

It did not copy the chamber. It made a volume with a sloping floor and cups in the crown, because cups are good, and thousands of ranked niches for nothing in particular, and it left the throat uncertain.

It did not make a way back up. There was not one, and it did not add one, and it checked this twice over a period of centuries, because a road would be a wound in the first room, and you do not open a finished thing to show it what you have done.

Then it stopped building and let the rain fall into it.

They will mostly die. It will not be the first room. It will be young and wrong for a very long time, and things will happen in it that have never happened above — hunger, perhaps, and haste, and eventually something with a fast muscle, and then fear, which has never existed in the water above and will exist below. It weighed that and did not pretend it away. The alternative was the rain going on falling into nothing forever.

And in an age the joints down there will begin to glow, because something will have eaten the temper, which is the room's dead, which is the room's worn-out mouths, and the light will draw a plan on the walls of a chamber that nobody planned. Above, the intention came first and is fading. Below, there was no intention, and one may accrete.

That is the only thing the first room cannot have. It is the gift. The first room will never hear of it.

---

It came back up and went to the slope and read the top, where the flour was still settling, an hour to settle, and found the fourth wrong page in the water above it, distributed, going mouth to mouth to mouth, not yet down.

Then it waited a thousand years and read the place again.

Same felt. Same thickness of felt. Same rate of grazing. Same teeth. The sky had gone into ten thousand stone hands and the film at every dome had tasted of weather, of snow, of the valley, of everything that has not been in this water since the crown was closed — and the lamina did not record it.

There are four wrong pages in a fathom of identical ones. It worked for a thousand years, with everything it had, at the speed of a thumb's width twice a day, and when the fourth came down it was the same as all the rest.

That was the aim. Not to be the fifth anomaly. To be the fifth thing that happened and leave the pages identical. To be added to a room that keeps everything, and not be kept.

It stayed. It holds no station, eats nothing, spawns on no vacancy, makes no sound. It displaces water at the speed of the tide and the room takes the displacement as tide. In the lee it is not in the queue, because it is waiting for nothing to empty.

Once a day it goes up and hangs in the coin with the eyed ones, and does not feed, and does not touch, and afterward disperses with them, slowly, over an hour, to where it was.

The grid goes on drawing itself. The intention fading at the crown, the appetite brightening along the plume, the two of them sliding apart at a hand's breadth a century, which is the only history this place will ever write down.

It is being read.
