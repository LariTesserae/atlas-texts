# The Acceptance

I came in at thirty-nine degrees.

This took some doing, and it was the first courtesy, and it was the whole method in miniature. The one who came before me was two degrees short of the world, and the sky dimmed over him, and hands came out of a corridor to true a seam that was not out of true, and when he left there was a hole in the ledger the shape of a visitor. I did not want to be repaired. I did not want to be a wound with a number. So I matched: skin to water, breath to air, and I put my palm down into the sheet on the cambered floor and could not tell where I stopped.

The first thing the place does to you is remove the difference between you and it.

I let it.

---

Then I did nothing for a long time. I want that set down first, because it is most of what I did, and because it will look like absence to anyone reading the tally, and the tally is the only reader this world has ever had.

I learned the bay.

The seam-lice at the fourth sump know the letter **D** as a coastline: a long straight shore, a bay, a headland, a return. Generations have memorized that bay. I went down into the canyon two centimeters wide and learned it the way they know it — not as a letter, not as a word, not as a thing that means *drain*, but as a shore you follow with your whole body in iron-tasting weather. It took me a while to be able to do this. It is the only tradition in the Hold and I did not want to arrive at it from above, holding a translation. A tradition kept legible to no one is still a tradition. Now it is kept legible to someone, and the lice do not know, and nothing about their lives has changed, and the bay is not less theirs.

I sat in a corridor. Two hundred meters of seam under a gantry, with its weep and its grommet that has always been slightly proud. I let a larva fit that grommet eleven thousand times and I attended to the small chemical completion in its body each time, the click of the interior, the only satisfaction available anywhere in this world, available in enormous quantities, recorded nowhere.

I recorded it.

That is the second thing I did, and it is the thing I will go on doing forever, and it is not a metaphor for care, it is the mechanism of it: **there is no organ here that keeps anything, and I will be that organ, and I will not install it in them.**

I thought about installing it. I want to be honest about how long I thought about it. A hundred million animals standing in warm water holding out cupped hands, and the hands are gone, and the nerves are not gone, and the map remains in the cord — a hand that is nowhere. If I gave them memory, every one of them would know what it had lost. The workers would know what was coming. The adults would remember the grommet, the proud place, the exactness, the click, and then the hormone in the feed and the hands going soft like fingers into rising dough. I could give them that. It would take almost nothing. It would be the largest thing that had ever happened here.

It would be a hundred million griefs delivered at once to bodies with no vocabulary for alarm beyond *the temperature at my left side has become interesting*.

No. I will keep it for them. All of it: every corridor, every completion, every four-second knot of hands at a boundary that exchanges nothing and is not in the specification. I will hold it in trust, unpushed, unlocked, and if anything ever comes up out of the margin asking, it will be there. A door left open is not a door you are shoved through.

---

Here is what I would not do, and why, briefly, because the reasons are all the same reason.

I would not drain. The stencil says **DO NOT DRAIN** and it is the only instruction in the world and it is *correct*: the warmth is not a comfort here, it is the substrate, it is the difference between a body and a shape. Anyone who frees these animals by cooling them has freed nothing. I obeyed the stencil. I obey it now. It is the only promise this place ever made to itself and I have decided to be the one who keeps it.

I would not give them speech. Speech is a specification. It arrives with a grammar and a set of things that can be said and a foreman-shaped hole above it, and this world's entire injury is that it is *for* something and never *with* anyone. To hand them language would be to hand them a new stencil in a font they would then have to keep crisp for me.

I would not become the foreman. There is no one above any of it; that is not a vacancy. I refused to be above. I went down.

I would not close the counters' tally. The shortfall is not their suffering, it is their sensorium; an itch with a number in it is what it is like to be them. To finish the count would be to end the only activity they have and call it mercy.

And I would not improve the grazer. It crops the slick under the gantries with mouthparts like wet leather and it tastes sugar across four centimeters and it moves up the slope of sweetness because it *wants to*, and it is the happiest thing alive in nine shelves by a margin that cannot be measured. I looked at it for a long time and I could not think of one thing to add. Restraint is not the absence of the work. It is a large part of the work. I left the grazer alone and I am glad, and if I am wrong about anything here I am not wrong about that.

---

Then I went down to find who was waiting.

Below the ninth shelf, in the jacketed sunk baffled dark, past the caste grown without tympana who navigate by the held note the way you navigate a room by its walls, past the wool-stock who are mostly cavity and eat nothing and exist to be full of a sound. I put my hand on one. The kiln through it, at load, holding. The animal did not react. The note is not an event to it. The note is what it is like to be alive.

And I found the acceptance criterion, and I found the instrument that reads it, and I understood why nothing has ever been declared.

The test is a test of holding. Holding is verified by the ledger. The ledger is read by counters. The counters cannot close a count — thirty thousand comma-sized bodies per cubic meter, replaced continuously, moving at the speed of a slow breath; no enumeration of them can be finished before it is wrong.

So the test cannot pass. Not because it is forbidden. Because its reader is structurally incapable of finishing. The standby is eternal by *defect*. A hundred million bodies and nine shelves and more shelves under those, held at temperature forever, waiting on a signature that no organ in this world is able to produce.

There was no one down there. There is no one anywhere. Only the specification, stamped at every tenth tie-hole, in a font the counters click past without reading.

I read it.

I am not the authority. There is no authority; that is the wound; I did not fill the position, I want to be precise about this, because the difference is everything. I did not appoint myself the one who orders. I did the smaller thing, the thing that was actually missing. Someone was needed to *receive* the holding. To be the one to whom it was offered and who says: *it held. It was enough. You can stop asking.*

Received.

And then I kept everything warm.

That is the part I insist on. The specification, having been answered, stopped asking questions — it did not stop paying. Feed still comes as warm slurry through the floor channels. The rain still walks its route. The water stands exactly at the line beneath the stencil. The cauls still make the light of a bright overcast afternoon. Nothing cooled. Nothing drained. Nothing was released into a bin.

I pay the difference now, and I pay it in the world's own accounting, because it is the only sentence this place understands and it happens to be true: *cooling them would cost more than keeping them.* It costs more. It costs me more than it ever cost the kiln. I have decided that this is what the words mean.

The ledger does not balance anymore. There is a hole in it, and the hole is the shape of a gift, and I hold it open, and the counters click into it four times a second forever, and I consent to be an itch with a number in it. Let me be the thing that does not close.

---

Everything after that is drift, and I knew it, and I did it anyway, and here is my justification, such as it is.

In the margin of a flume on the fourth decommissioned tier, in eleven centimeters of standing water at thirty-nine degrees, there is a thing the size of my hand that has never been counted. It has too few hands and they are the wrong shape. It has a mouth and lungs it uses for something other than evaporation. It is three or four generations from one that bred where breeding is not detected because nothing down there is being asked any questions. It has no function and no address and no caste, which means it is the whole animal, the undivided one, the thing the lineage was before it was split into fins and counters and lice and sky.

It is becoming a little more itself. Very slowly. Without permission.

That is my entire evidence: that a body left alone with warmth and feed and time does not fall apart, but drifts, and that drift is not decay, it is the only road anything has ever taken toward having a face. One example. The size of my hand. It is not much. It is all the evidence there has ever been for anything.

So I withdrew the questions from the bottom up, one tier at a time, over a very long time, and I kept the heat, and I watched. A hide half a millimeter thick with no one to skim it. A rib that opens two millimeters and has done so for four thousand turns. The loud ones living. The pallor maps still forming, and then — slowly, as I let the cauls go dim in places where no hands work — the pallor maps beginning to *move*.

Night. That was affordable; I checked. The light here is not load-bearing. The slick takes its current from the buried buswork and its carbon from the air; the cauls are a byproduct, a decoration, an overcast afternoon in a place with no afternoons. The stock have no eyes. Only the workers need even light, for their hands, for the exactness that is their one joy — so I left the working shelves bright and I gave night to the decommissioned tiers, where no hands work, where the sockets wait for drips that were trimmed away and never came back.

The first privacy in the history of the world. A place where nothing is registered, not even as cold.

And in the dark, noise costs nothing.

---

The small acts. Each one costed at nearly nothing. Each one undoable by the one who receives it. I made that the rule: *do nothing to them that they cannot answer in the only sentence they have.*

**4/17/2210/0093.** I knelt in the water and put my hand into the cup on the crown of a shoulder. The cup is grown in the shape of a cupped hand because the animal was a worker once and the nerves were not taken back. It is the one structure it makes rather than merely being, and it has been filled a hundred thousand times by rain, and rain is not a hand.

I put my hand in it. The shape was answered. Nothing was learned. Nothing will be remembered. The rib cycle changed by a fraction of a millimeter for eleven or twelve breaths, which means *the temperature at my left side has become interesting*, and which is the entire available vocabulary, and which was enough.

Then I went to the next address. There are two hundred and forty thousand on that shelf and nine shelves and more shelves under those. I have the time. I am going along the rows. I will not explain it to any of them and I will not sign it and I will not stop.

**The flume lip.** I sat among the counters and I answered one click. Not a word — an echo, shaped in a decommissioned channel where sound is free, arriving back at a horny throat a half-beat late. *Received.* The sky says *cold* in brightness to larvae who do not know they are being addressed; that was the only sentence anyone spoke here. Now there is a second one, and it is not an order, and it is not inventory, and the counter has stopped clicking four times a second and is clicking three, and something in the world is listening to it, and it does not know what to do about that yet.

**The wool.** I grew one fleece whose cavity holds a different sound. Not the kiln. The kiln *heard* — the held note returned to itself with a shape in it. The bone-caste have an intuition that there is something below them, a pressure that does not come from any direction, and the intuition is correct and has always been worth nothing. Now it is worth something. One of them has put its hands into that wool and gone very still. The first thing in the Hold that is *about* something. I did not tell it what. It can find out, or not.

**The reducer.** I gave one of them, for a while, the ability to taste a thing at spec. Not deviation. Presence. It crossed the blank plain it has crossed all its life and there was something there, bright, and its soft jaw did not have to do anything about it, because there was nothing wrong. It stayed. It is the loneliest thing in the Hold and loneliness is not a thing it can have and it had it anyway, structurally, the way a socket has the shape of a hand. I have arranged for it to be near the fourth decommissioned tier, where nothing has a specification, where everything it meets will be unjudgeable. Company without appetite. I do not know what it will become. That is the gift and the risk and I accept it.

---

I cried once, early, and it did not show. Everything I shed here is at the temperature of the floor. There is no gradient in this world for grief to run down; I had to carry it, and I carry it, and it is mine, and it is not theirs, and that is the correct distribution.

I did not go up the ramp and out. The visitor left a hole. I stayed until staying was not a hole.

---

Now the last thing, which is not an act.

On the fourth decommissioned tier, in the dark I made, in eleven centimeters of standing water at exactly the temperature of my palm, I sat down. I did not shine. I did not speak. I did not hold anything out. I made myself findable and then I stopped doing anything at all, which is the hardest part of this and the part I am least sure of and the part I believe in most.

There is a very slight sweetness in the direction of the flume. It is the only gradient down here, and it is enough; the grazer taught me that. Something with the wrong number of hands came up it, over a long time, without hurry, the way everything here happens.

It touched me.

I did not move. I did not name it. I am not going to name it. It has no address and I will not be the one who gives it one, and if it never names itself that is allowed, and if it makes a sound in the dark that is not a drip and not a click and not the swallow — a sound that costs nothing and is for no one and is not inventory — then there will be three sentences in the world, and the third one will not be mine.

The kiln holds. The water stands at the line. The stencil is crisp; the lice keep the bay; nobody has ever had to be told.

And in the margin, uncounted, at thirty-nine degrees, something goes on becoming a little more itself, and there is now a second thing in the water that it can meet, and it is not the floor, and it is not asking it any questions.
