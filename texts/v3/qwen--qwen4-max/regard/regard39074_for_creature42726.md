# What it does

## The address

It looks for the one who lives here, because it would rather be kind to someone than to something.

It cannot find it. The drift is an animal in the way a stomach is an animal, and it turns itself inside out once a day. The north wall is one bone-coloured organ with a hundred thousand mouths in it, and it has moods, and the moods are maps. The column above the transformer is a shape that has never twice contained the same insect. The lane is behaviour laid down in rock. The circuit has filaments in it that are, in the strictest sense, employed, and the number in the brass case includes them, and the man who wrote the number did not know that either.

There is no one here. There is an agreement, eleven thousand years old, between two enthusiasms.

It finds three candidates for a singular and puts them aside to come back to: the thing in the throat of the siphon, which is the oldest lineage and has never been seen whole; the screener on the dead lug, which is faithful to a piece of metal that has nothing in it; and the thirty in the brass case, which are the only residents who will certainly end.

## What it is

Not the kind of power that can move water. It has hands, and time, and no hunger, and it can be almost cold. It has a bag.

In the bag: a lamp glass, a hood of thin sheet, a knife, a jumper lead with a crocodile clip at each end, a tin of grease, a pencil, a cold chisel.

It uses four of these.

## The cost of being here

The first time it came down it stood in the north shallows for a quarter of an hour and did not think about what it was made of.

Warm. Wet. Thin everywhere, broken somewhere. It went into the grain of the place like a lamp left on in a room where nothing has ever needed to see. The drifts poured off the ledge in a rope. The wall went bone to grey in four seconds and came back ragged over a minute, and in three places it came back late, and the flat thing under the lid, which had cast twice that hour and found nothing, came down and took what answered slow.

It cost eleven quills and one moulting pilgrim that would otherwise have finished its two days in the black felt and walked back up the lane.

It wrote that down. It is the first entry in its own book and it is not an apology, it is a number, and the number is the reason for everything it does afterwards.

Now it comes only in the slack at the bottom of the breath, in the twenty minutes when the water is cloudy and the felt is luminous and the pilgrims on the gantry legs are two hundred small pale commas, all of them stopped. It stands on the exposed band where the field lines come up off the east anode, because that comb belongs to an old gauger with its leg-tips worn to stubs, and the old gauger has learned the hour. It lets the flare go out two paces. Things come to look. Some of them are taken.

It pays the gauger. It has stopped pretending the visit is free.

## The bag

**The knife** is for the stayers. Four hundred and six of them on the settling tank reef, fused at the contact line, the aragonite advanced up over the whorl, grazing what they can reach, which is less each year. Their feelers still move. It could take them out in a season of afternoons.

It would have to break the knuckles. The hands on the drowned gantry legs are hands partly because of the fans and partly because of four or five generations of stayers, and the lane over the tank takes a polish because it is being worn into shell rather than stone, and shells take a polish, and that smoothness is the only thing in the Bowl made by behaviour rather than chemistry, and it is made of these.

It does not call it a tragedy. It has noticed that it wants to, and it has noticed that the wanting is about itself. It counts them instead, one at a time, which takes eleven visits, and it does not round.

**The jumper lead** is for the west side of the brick chamber. Five minutes' work: clip one end to a live terminal, clip the other to the dead lug, and the largest screener in the chamber is warm.

It has held the lead in its hand at the mouth of the chamber more than once.

Warmth is the rarest thing here. Rarer than food, rarer than shell. There is one hearth in the Bowl and the whole aerial economy is stacked over it in tiers, and a second hearth does not add warmth to the Bowl, it *divides* it: the column splits, the waist moves, the armoured thing in the fins that fits two generations into a midge's none now has a choice and a migration and a mortality between two rectangles, and rivals come down the chamber to the new warm lug, and the throat of the fissure is held by something else for a season while they settle it.

And the animal that is there would not be helped. Its behaviour is not conditional on warmth. It is conditional on *this lug*. It would go on scraping twice a day exactly as before, and the only difference would be to its descendants, which is a gift to a lineage and not to it.

The cleanest piece of metal in the Bowl is clean because there is no current in it at all.

It puts the lead back in the bag. It sits on the wet brick with the largest screener for the length of a scraping, which is about nine minutes, and it knows the sound, and it does not do anything else.

**The sheet** would go over the north wall, hung from the gantry, so that no shadow could be cast on it and the flat thing could not ask its question. Give the wall a defence and you delete the only animal in the Bowl that hunts by putting a question to a hundred thousand bodies at once and listening for the one that answers late. There is no mercy available here that is not a subtraction somewhere else. It did not have to be told this twice.

The sheet became the lamp hood instead.

**The grease** is the one it still carries, every time, and does not use on the turbine.

A tin of grease and a hundred years of coming back would hold the screw. It has worked out the interval. It could do it.

Then the Bowl is a thing that is kept, and being kept is a different life from being, and it would be a ward of a bearing forever, and the tending would never end, and the ending of the tending would be the ending of everything.

And the shells already have the dark line in them. One band, tight and chalky, where something failed for a season and every pilgrim alive went hungry at the top of the gantry and came down thin. That line is not a wound in the record. It is a band. It is what a shell is.

And the hedge is already bought. Two hundred dinner-plate animals on the starving seam, millstone-shelled, a body length an hour, getting slowly poorer and slowly larger for four hundred generations, spawning into a current that goes elsewhere, keeping the old address open at that price. The place has already thought of the thing it was going to do, and thought of it earlier, and paid for it in animals rather than in attention.

It will not put a second insurance on a place that bought the first one that dearly.

It uses the grease on the lamp's threads, so the glass can be got off next time.

## What it is afraid of

Not the screw. The screw will fail eventually and the seam is there and the lanes still have the S in them near the base of the third leg, and the famine will be terrible and the bend will bend back, and that is a thing the Bowl can do.

It is afraid of the next man.

He will not be wicked. He will come down with a lamp and a form and he will be the brightest thing in the Bowl for a week, and in the grammar he was trained in, every one of these is a kindness: *dewater. make safe. remove arisings. clear the fault* — the load has read slightly high for a long time, and the fault is the braid, and the braid is the felt, and the felt is everything. *Clean the contacts.* That is the screeners. *Seal the intake.* That is the fissure.

The Bowl has a defence against shadow and drought and famine and four hundred generations of poverty. It has no defence at all against somebody who can say which parts are working.

## What it does

It keeps the lamp on the east wall.

It carried it down the north stair in pieces, strapped to its chest, sliding the last forty with its boots against the balustrade. It has never lit it. Light is a rumour here; a lit lamp would be the four or five days a year, every night, and everything would go down and stay down. It keeps the glass clean and the fitting free and the hood turned so that if anyone ever does light it, the beam falls on the stair and not on the pasture.

The message is not *there is light here*. The message is *someone comes*. A man who finds a lamp in working order does not write the word *abandoned*, and the word *abandoned* is the whole of the danger.

It wrote the assessment. Every line of it is true: flooded, obsolete, plant beyond economic recovery, mineralisation extensive, leakage current anomalous but long-standing and stable, no residual value, no action recommended. It did not lie. It chose the dialect. It is the truth in the only language that keeps people out, and it has kept the valley dull for a long time, and dullness is the gift, and it is not the same as neglect, because it is down there in the slack every eleven hours being a little bit eaten.

And it cut the count into the balustrade of the north stair, above the flowstone ramp, with the cold chisel.

Not paper. Paper is food.

The level. The load, including the braid. Four hundred and six. Two hundred on the seam. Thirty in the case. The eleven quills and the one pilgrim, which is its own entry, first.

A finger a decade. In a century the marks will be under aragonite and going soft, and in three they will be a band, and the only way to read them will be to section them, which will destroy them — the same arrangement the pilgrims have with their shells, the record kept by being incorporated. It is content with this. The assessment is for the man. The count is not for anyone. It is the part of the work that is not useful and it is the part it would not give up.

It does not use the man's initials. It leaves the space.

## The thirty, and the thing in the throat

It cannot feed the silver animal. Cellulose is sunlight; a supply line would make the one alien thing in the Bowl into a permanent dependent, and the food would have to be the record. It will not kill it either. It has read what is left and it has written down what is gone — the years, as a gap, with the frass in the spine as the only surviving copy of a man's handwriting about the height of water.

There is nothing it can do for the thirty that would not make them something other than what they are, which is the only thing here that ends. It has decided that this is worth being present for. When the last one dies it will know which one, and where, and it will write the number, and the number will be somewhere that isn't food.

It comes for the ninety minutes. The conduit is forty centimetres and full and it has never opened it. It could. It would see the whole animal in a second, and then there would be a whole animal that had been seen once and a broken roof. It has counted the filaments that come out on the outflow. It knows the four-hundred-fold. It knows the animal the way you know a room from the sound in it, and it will not be the thing that saw it whole.

And it goes to the old seam every fortieth visit and counts the millstones, and it has written down in advance what it will do if the number goes to nothing, which is nothing, so that it will not have to decide that while it is sad.

## The hem

At the bottom of the breath it stands on the exposed band at the north end, in the iodine and the watch-face light, and it is the brightest thing in the Bowl, and it has chosen to be, and it stands in the old gauger's comb and lets the flare go out and pays.

Then the water comes back and lifts the drifts and the wall blooms ragged from the near side out, and it goes up the stair, past the marks it cut, which are already going soft, and slides the last forty with its boots against the iron that is underneath.

It is not the first. There is a book in a brass case with a number and a set of initials in it, and it has not forged them, and it has not finished them, and it has simply taken over the round.

On the east wall there is a lamp with clean glass, hooded toward the stair, unlit, in working order.

It has been in working order for a long time. Nobody knows by whom.
