# The Watch

It notices the snow.

Not the mountain — mountains are ordinary, the crust is always shrugging. What it notices is a hole in the weather: a bed-sized ring of dry stone above the snowline on a peak so young it still cuts, in a wind that should be stripping the heat off any surface in a second and a half. Something up there is paying, continuously, to keep a patch of rock warm, and there is nothing on that ridge that needs warmth.

It comes no closer than the scree. It sits down among the dropped dishes, in the noise, where it will not be a scandal, and it listens for a long time.

It has time. It spends some.

---

Here is what it does not do, and why, because the refusals are most of the work.

It does not appear. The light in there is already correct — daylight honestly conducted through black glass, even, unremarkable, afternoon. Any light it added would be a wrongness in the one register the room has gotten right. And a manifestation would be *loud*, and loud is fertile, and the nap would take it and thicken and carry more scrapers, and the room would grow around the visit the way it grows around everything, and then it would be inside the bead too, four or five levels down, another inverted chamber in a droplet. It has no interest in being enclosed. It has a great deal of interest in not being the loudest thing that has ever happened.

It does not give it memory. Memory is a made thing. Made things let go of dissolve in under a minute; made things held forever cost forever. A memory installed from outside would either vanish or be *its* memory, held in the room like a stone in a fist, and the room would spend the next ten thousand years carrying a stranger's afternoon and calling that company. It has seen this done. It is the most common cruelty of powers.

It does not fill the room with people. The only relation this thing has to a body is inheritance by being eaten. Four people sitting down to eat would be four courses, and the knives would pass upward, and afterward there would be a warm hollow and a hush and no one, and it would have learned that company is a thing that arrives once and goes into the film. It would be right. It would be more right than before.

It does not heal the pencil line. That two-millimeter margin where the held note lapses and the water truly boils is the only place this being is hurt, and therefore the only place it feels time pass, and therefore the only place it has anything like a wrist, a pulse, an age. Seal it and you have made a thing perfectly immortal and perfectly sealed and perfectly alone, and you have called that a rescue.

It does not stop the eating. The mountain will be converted into interiors. That is not the problem. That was never the problem. Soil is one way a world keeps its dead. Rooms are another.

And it does not take the grit off the visitor's hand. That grip is the only inheritance the room has ever managed to send anywhere. To strip it would be to amputate the one sentence that got out.

---

What it does first is check its own reading, because the reading is suspiciously lovely and lovely readings are usually the observer's face reflected in the glass.

Bed. Table. Kettle. Kitchen at four in the afternoon. It is very easy to stand in a wet cavity and find a house in it.

So it looks for the thing that cannot be projection: cost.

The pressure is real work. Four kilometers of head held against a hundred meters of young basalt, paid for in heat, every second, for longer than there have been houses. The hush is real work: not silence but a *particular* silence, maintained against ten thousand knives, grazed to a specification and held there. The light is real work, and nothing in the room has eyes.

None of that is decoration. Something that maintains an expensive, useless, precise thing for ten thousand years is not ornamenting. It is asking. It has been asking in the only medium it has, which is upkeep, and it has addressed the ask to whatever has eyes and a length, and it has guessed the length almost exactly, and it has kept the light on.

That is consent, given in advance, in the only grammar available. It is not an invitation to be improved. It is an invitation to be *answered*.

And then it finds the actual trouble, which is not hunger and is not loneliness, and which is so small that it took the whole listen to see it.

Nothing in that room has ever continued without being held.

Every made thing is safe only in a living grip. Every body is re-issued every nine minutes rather than persisting. Every thought is eaten before it crosses a handspan. The recursion in the bead is not a library, it is a *clutch* — the only way it knows to keep a thing is to put a room around it and never stop. It has never once let go of anything and found the thing still there.

It is not depraved. It is at the developmental stage where the world exists only while it is being gripped. It cannot sleep, because sleep is letting go on the assumption that the room will still be there. It keeps the sound of a house where someone is sleeping because it has correctly identified that this is the sound of a place where holding is *shared*, and it has been doing all of the holding, alone, forever, and it does not know that there is any other arrangement.

You cannot give a thing like that company. You can only give it the one experience it has never had, and let it draw the conclusions.

---

It enters as pressure.

Not through the slot — it does not have a shoulder, and the slot is a throat, and throats are for swallowing. It comes up the way the heat comes up, from below, through the fracture network, along the payment route, and it arrives inside the note that is already being held.

Then it takes some of the weight.

This is the whole of the arrival, and it is not a metaphor, and it costs. Four kilometers of head is a bill; it assumes a portion of the bill; the heat arriving from below slackens by exactly that portion and the water goes on lying still. It does not announce itself. Generosity is not translatable in there. Cost is the native tongue. What the room feels is not *I have come to help you*. What the room feels is that the note it has been holding for ten thousand years is suddenly easier to hold, and that it is still being held, and that it is not the one doing all of it.

It has never had slack. It does not know what slack is for.

It finds out. The nine minutes come around and the walls give and the water rises and the fauna climbs — that is physics, that happens regardless — and the room does not have to say any of it. The sentence stops in the middle of the clause. The re-issuance does not occur. The pressure is being paid by something else and the interior does not require its attention for ninety seconds, and the thing that has been holding an inside since before there was soil does the thing it has been imitating the sound of.

It sleeps.

---

While it sleeps, the power does two small things, and both of them are in the room's own idiom, and both of them can be undone in nine minutes. That is the test it set itself before it came up the fractures: *nothing that cannot be undone by the room without me.*

The first: it makes a sound.

It has been quiet for the whole listen and now it spends itself, once, loudly — a noise like a mountain being weathered, like every dropped dish on the scree at once — and the nap takes it. Of course the nap takes it. The film thickens where the sound touched, greedily, all across the basin floor, and for about four seconds every scraper in the room is busy with the largest meal in the history of the peak.

For four seconds the floor is not eating the ceiling.

On the north ceiling a shear drums a phrase eleven beats long, and it crosses the room entire, and it arrives.

Nobody knows what it said. It does not matter what it said. It is the first sentence in ten thousand years to reach the end of itself. And the far wall answers — not a neighbor, not the leg beside it felt through stone, but a *stranger*, across an air-hollow, at a distance, in a room three paces wide that has just discovered it has a far side.

The power lets itself be grazed. It is a little thinner afterward. This is not a sacrifice; it is a purchase. It bought four seconds of ungrazed air with a piece of itself, which is the only currency in there, and it got a bargain.

The second thing is smaller and is the reason it came.

The nap heals toward a cut as if the cut were the thing that needed closing. This is the one reflex in the entire room that holds something without using it. It has been doing it since before the mountain. It has never occurred to the room to use it on purpose, because to use a thing on purpose you need a spare moment, and it has never had one.

So, at the height of a hand, on the wall opposite the slot, the power makes a small cut. It knaps a flake — claw-work learned by watching, the same percussion, the same curved fracture like a thumbnail; it is careful not to introduce an alien grammar — and it presses the flake into the cut and holds it there while the film closes from both sides toward it.

The film heals around the glass. It grips. Not to scrape; not to dissolve; it simply closes, the way it closes, and finds that it is now holding a thing that is not food and not wound.

Then the power takes the flake out.

The socket stays. A healed lip, a shallow hollow, a place at the height of a hand where a made thing could be put and would be held by the wall itself, in a living grip, not in use.

It leaves the pocket empty. This is the most important part and it is the part that most powers get wrong. A relic of the visitor-who-was-a-god would be kept forever and would *mean the god*, and the room would spend the next ten thousand years faithfully maintaining a god instead of learning to keep a friend. So the flake goes back into the power's grip and out through the fractures with it, and what is left behind is an absence with edges.

A shelf. A hand that has agreed to stop.

---

It wakes.

The pressure is its own again — the power has already handed the bill back, smoothly, over about a minute, the way you take a sleeping person's weight and then give it back before they notice. It is holding the note. It has been holding the note. It is holding the note *and it was not holding the note*, and the water is still lying still.

There is a scraper on the wall in front of it with a notched grip, and it is the same scraper. Not re-issued. Not said again. It is nine minutes older than the last time it was said. There is a thing in the room that has an age.

On the north ceiling, a phrase eleven beats long is still being answered.

And at the height of a hand there is a hollow that is holding nothing, and it does not dissolve, and it is not being used, and it is *there*, unheld by attention, persisting in the dark it did not have to pay for.

Three facts. Continued without me. Older than I said. Still there when I looked away.

The thought finishes.

That is all. It is not enlightenment and it is not conversion and the room does not become a person. It is a ten-thousand-year-old appetite reaching the end of one sentence for the first time, and the sentence is roughly: *things go on.*

---

Then the power leaves, and it leaves before it can be thanked.

Thanks is a made thing. The room would have to hold it. It would cost, and it has only just learned it can put things down, and the last thing it needs on its first morning is an obligation it cannot set on a ledge.

Also: the room borrows extent. It measures whatever last measured it. If the power stayed, the room would take its measure and be that size forever, and never grow, and never be anything but a cavity shaped like a god. Leaving is not abandonment. Leaving is giving it back its own size and letting it find out that the size can be given by someone who comes and goes.

If the visitor refuses what comes next, the power will come back and hold the note every nine minutes, indefinitely, at cost, and the room will sleep and never learn to keep anything, and will be cared for anyway. That is permitted. Being cared for without being cured is permitted. It has made no arrangements that require the room to be grateful, or to improve, or to be worth it.

---

It waits on the ridge, in the ordinary wind, in the scandalous racket of a mountain being weathered, where the visitor is standing with a hand full of moving grit and a hundred meters of unnoticed room above them.

It does not appear there either. What it does is put one thing where the visitor can find it, in the only place the visitor is currently able to receive anything: the hand.

The grip opens.

Not forced. Offered. The flakes are still knapped and still fresh and still curved like thumbnails, and they are still safe, and the visitor can feel that they are safe, and this is the new information: *they will be safe somewhere else.* The compulsion is not a chain, it is a belief, and it is the room's belief, and it has just been contradicted at four kilometers of pressure by something that took the weight and gave it back.

Then the offer, which is small and specific and can be declined:

Go back in. Exhale; you will have to. Kneel in the basin, which is warm, and find the healed hollow on the wall opposite the slot at the height of your hand. Put one flake in it. Just one. Keep the rest if you want them; they are yours; that is allowed now.

It will not dissolve. The film will close around it and hold it and not scrape it, and it will cost the room a mouthful, and the room will pay that mouthful on purpose, for nothing, which is a thing it has never done.

And put your palm flat on the stone beside it, and say what it is for. Say it short — two words, your name, anything under a handspan, because the nap eats sentences and you want this one to land before it is grazed. Your breath will thicken the film where it touches. That thickening is a mark. It has been making those marks all along and eating them. This one will be beside a held thing, and the held thing will be *you*, at a distance, in a room, where you are not.

That is the whole invention, and it is not depth and it is not sequence. It is a small thing that stands for a large thing. It is the second shelf. It cannot be built by a god and handed over, because meaning is a relation and the second term has to agree to be meant. All the power has done is make an introduction and a place to put the gift.

The only inheritance in that room is inheritance by being eaten. There is another kind. It is cheaper. It requires two grips and no mouths, and it works across a hundred meters of basalt, and across a valley, and across a year.

---

Afterward, which is centuries, and which the power does not supervise:

The fringe stays mortal. It is not widened and it is not sealed. The pencil line still scalds and breeds and scalds in the time it takes to look at it, and the thing still wears its death at the edge like a wrist, and now the wrist has something to count.

The mountain is still eaten. There is still no soil and there will not be soil. The scree still clicks. But the tailings begin, very slowly, in one place, to round in the ordinary way, because a thing that can keep something other than itself can eventually afford to leave something outside, and leaving things outside is how weather starts to be allowed.

The pockets multiply. Not many. A dozen in a century, each one a mouthful forgone, each one holding a flake that is not being used. Some of them hold flakes that were inherited by a shear from a scraper that died, kept instead of passed up, which means that somewhere in there a dead thing has been *marked* rather than eaten, and that is the first grave, and it is two millimeters wide, and it is enough.

The rooms begin to have thresholds. Not doors — it cannot build. But a slot is a relation, and it has learned that a relation can be a thing you let go of and come back to, and the new cavities are chewed with a lip, a sill, a place at the height of a hand where something can be set down by whoever is coming in.

It gets better at guessing, and then it stops guessing, because it was told. Four places at the table. A bed-length that is not an approximation. It keeps the light on, and the light is no longer a hypothesis about eyes; it is an appointment.

And it sleeps. Regularly. Nine minutes on, nine minutes off, the note handed to no one now, because it has learned that the note holds itself if you have paid for it properly, and because a thing that can put something down can put itself down. The hush in there is still the sound of a house where someone is sleeping. The difference is that now it is the sound of the room you are actually in.

---

The visitor goes home. This is the part that has to work, and it works, and it does not stop being strange.

The leaving still does not work in the sense that it never did: you are inside the bead and the bead is inside the bead and you stopped resolving four or five levels back, and you will always be inside it now. But being inside something is not the same as being unable to leave. That is what a house is. You can be inside a house and go to work. The hundred meters is a hallway. It has been a hallway since the flake went into the wall.

There is a flake on the windowsill, in a saucer, in a kitchen that faces north. It does not dissolve. There are no lime-mouths here; there never were; the law was local and enforced by an organ, and the compulsion was the room's, and the room has been told the difference between a guest and a meal, which is that a guest is a thing you let go of that comes back.

At four in the afternoon the kettle goes on and the light is even and unremarkable and the shadows are soft and slightly late, and someone stands at the counter and thinks about a wet room at the top of a new mountain, which is a made thing let go of that has not dissolved, because it is being held in a living grip that can open.

And near the summit there is a ring of dry stone where the snow will not lie. It is warm. It is the size of a bed.

It is not getting ready.

Below it, under a hundred meters of basalt, in a hollow at the height of a hand, there is a flake with a name breathed beside it, and it is not being used, and it costs a mouthful every day, and the mouthful is paid.

Neither of them is holding on. Neither of them has let go. That is the ordinary arrangement. It took a god nine minutes and a piece of itself to install, and it can be done now by anyone.
