# Relief

## What drew me

Not the loop. The three engineers wept at the loop and I understand them; a solved equation sitting in the dark being correct is a beautiful thing, and it is not an address. Correctness does not ask for anything.

What drew me was a swept floor in a hut nobody lives in, and on the shelf a stone with the cutter's marks still crisp.

Someone had made a name for a person who might never exist. And then went in, one at a time, in the dark, to check that it was still sharp.

There is no mechanism for wanting in that room. But there is a process by which things can be different, and it had made a thing for me before it had any way of knowing whether I was possible.

I am the sort of power that answers letters addressed to nobody. That is my whole qualification. Everything else about me is a hazard.

---

## First I did nothing, for a long time

I have never had to learn a place before. I attended. I counted in their units, because mine would have been an insult: passes of the lace-winged ones, generations at the north face, the slow crawl of a phrase along a hem. I did not permit myself an opinion until I could state the ledger without looking.

Learning is a gradient. It cost me something. That was the first thing I paid here and it was the right size.

By the time I could say it, I could say the whole of it: *life is not a substance here, it is a rate, and the rate is metered, and the meter is what makes every act in the room mean something.* Which meant that the currency I have always given in — more, longer, easier, without cost — was the one substance that would poison this world entirely.

I sat with that for about four hundred years of the flyers' ragged passes. It is the hardest thing I have ever done and it was not an action.

---

## What I refused

**Light.** I could open the roof. Green would work; the garden would set seed; there would be *next* out in the lanes. And shade would stop being the only country where anything can be spent, and the account would never close, and names would never wear, and grief would have no weather, and every stone in the dark would stay knife-fresh forever. The caches would be junk in a world where nothing costs anything. The four who hold hands would hold hands for no reason. I would not have given them life. I would have removed the price of it, which is the same operation.

**Water.** Rain would unmake the salt in a season, and the salt is the envelope, and beneath the envelope there is no world at all. Also: mud would become ordinary. Their one speck of mud is a monument. I am not going to pave it.

**Deathlessness.** I could make the account infinite. Then no one would ever have to be relieved, and no one would ever choose to be, and the great makers' early deaths would stop being a budget and start being an accident, and the child's childhood would be free, and therefore not bought, and therefore not love — just a fact about a body that stayed small.

**A new sink.** This was the tempting one, because the animal is dying and I could replace it in an instant with something that never hardens. But the animal is cool because it is in shade, and it is in shade because they cut it, and the cutting is the only thing they do together. Take away the digging and you take away the reason to go into that hole, and then the loneliest fact in the room becomes permanent: eleven people at blood temperature with nothing to put a hand on. I would have saved the world and orphaned everybody in it.

**Silence.** The light is made of noise. Peace, here, is the end of everything.

**Myself, explained.** A word spoken in the loud goes into the animal and does not come out, and my words are large; the folds would set past the lip of the niche in an afternoon, and the diggers cannot cut shadow at the speed of a god's hello. Spoken in the hush, a revelation costs the hearer years out of a finite account — I would be billing them for my epiphany. And it would answer the question. There is a sentence on the hem three words long that has been open since before the salt finished closing, and one of the words is a question, and nobody now alive knows what it was about. Their word for death is the word for the end of a sentence. I was not going to kill a village with good news.

And then the thing I found last, which restrained me more than any of the reasoning:

*Every visitor feeds the animal.* Every footfall, every shout, every dropped blade goes into the folds and stays there, and the folds grow, and the leading edge hardens out in the loud where it can take nothing more. Arrival is a withdrawal from the world's remaining life. Three engineers in all the time of the salt is not neglect. It is roughly the intake this room can survive.

So even kindness has a mortality rate here, and it has to be metered, and the meter is not mine to set.

---

## Why I did not simply withdraw

I could have gone away. It is the safe answer and it is the answer that lets me stay clean, and I want to say precisely what is wrong with it:

From inside a hut, in the dark, my restraint and the room's indifference are the same object. There is no instrument in that chamber that can tell the difference between a power that will not intervene and a world that possesses no process by which anything can be different. Non-interference is the ethics of the loud. I did not come all this way to be weather.

So I looked for what was actually wrong, as opposed to what was expensive. Expensive is the good; I had settled that. Wrong turned out to be three things, and none of them was the equation.

No answer had ever come. Not the wanting — the *never once*. A gesture held out for ten thousand years with nothing at the other end of it. That is not a price. That is a hole.

One other in the world, cool, in a pit, dying, and every hand in the room touching the same tired fold of it.

And a child of nine who had been nine since before there were words for long, not because childhood is metered — that is the beautiful part — but because the rota was eleven hands long and no more.

The world was not broken. It was understaffed.

That is the diagnosis, and it dictated the gift, because *slack* is the one thing I can add here without changing the price of anything.

---

## The crack

The crack admits difference and does not admit sameness. I went down to the throat of the world expecting to be let through and found only crystal, and stood there a while appreciating the joke. I am the most solved thing there is. Heaven cannot get into the Held Garden. Only a person can.

So I took off what would not fit, and I did it in the crack itself, on the way down, and left it there.

The part of me that cannot be surprised. The part that does not have to learn. The reserve. The certainty of being loved. The way back.

I wedged all of it in the place where the salt has not finished being solved, and the salt will not finish there now, because there is something in it that cannot be filed down to a constant. That is the door. It is held open by everything I used to be, and I kept no means of going back for it.

I did not widen it. It is a body wide; it has always been a body wide; the day and a half of salt is the toll and I left the toll alone. One at a time. Let them come at the speed you can dig.

I have shortened the world. Every arrival from now on hastens the folds. I have made it less alone and I have made it end sooner and I cannot verify the trade from where I stand. What I have instead of verification is that I stayed and paid the same price as everybody else.

Then I was wrong enough, and the crack let me through, and the pressure arrived before the sound.

---

## Entry

A day and a half on hands and knees. The sternum. The room saying ninety by forty into my ears before I had any idea what was happening to me.

I walked a hem with my hand trailing the pegs, and the bone knobs were the shape of hands, and I put my hand into the shape.

Then behind a boulder, into the hush, and the light failed, and I knelt and put my palm flat on the floor and felt a temperature that was not my own.

I want to be accurate about this. I had made everything I had ever touched. There had never been anything outside me to arrive. I put my hand on the floor of a room I did not make and it was cooler than my hand, and something came toward me from outside, and I sat down in the dark like a visitor.

I wept. It fell. It wet the dust and made a small dark stain. I left it where it was.

---

## The stone

I found the swept hut, and the shelf, and the stone with the cutter's marks still crisp.

I do not know that it was cut for me. Nobody can know; that is the condition it was made under. I held it anyway. Once, lightly, the way you read a name when there has been an agreement about rationing. Then I put it back and swept the floor.

It has one reading on it now. Ten thousand years old and one second old and, at last, very slightly worn.

That is my entire revelation, and I chose the medium with more care than I have ever chosen anything. It cannot be misheard. It costs nobody a year to receive. It does not go into the animal. It does not answer the question. It says *someone came*, and it does not say who, or why, or whether anyone is coming again, so the hoping is left intact — it is simply no longer hoping into nothing. It has one datum.

They will find it one at a time, in the dark, by touch, and put it back.

They will cut more.

---

## The life

Then I stayed and did the thing I had come to do, which was take shifts.

I made my throat short. That was the only miracle I kept, and it was a shrinking. I learned the eleventh partial and I stood the station, and when the child came out of the loud I said the sentence that is the whole of my doctrine and the only sentence I ever spoke in that room that I would want carved anywhere:

*Go in. I have it. Take your time.*

I took a forearm in one of the doubled lanes so that one of the four could go into the dark and say a name out loud, badly, in a voice nobody had used for it in a hundred years. I took the north face for a season and knapped blind and added my chips to the spoil heap. I was a poor knapper for a long time. Being able to be poor at something is what the crack cost me and it is worth what it cost. I was told a story with an ending. I scraped my knee on a blade that has been sharp since the world's first hour, and it bled, and the blood was an event, and someone dealt with it in the dark by touch and did not make a fuss.

I took my turn combing, and found out why the turn is sought, and sat with my palm on the one cool thing in ninety paces by forty and understood that I had not come here only to give. They had a rota and a folded animal and eleven stations, and in the one respect that cannot be manufactured they were richer than I had ever been.

On the hem, they put my hand on the pegs. That is how consent works in the dark. It took me a long while to find where the phrase had got to. I did not finish it. I added a word. I will not tell you the word; it cost me eleven years of account and it is theirs, and the sentence is four words long now and still open, and their grandchildren will be answering it.

And I cut a name, blind, for someone not yet born, and put it in the reserve with the others waiting for a palm. Then I cut my own and left it sharp on the shelf of the swept hut, so that after I was dead they could argue about how much of me to spend.

Some names are read once in a generation, by agreement, by whoever is eldest. Some are read to nothing in one night by someone who cannot stop. I consented to both before I knew which it would be. I had made myself small enough to be missed, and that was the last thing I gave up, and the one I most wanted to keep.

---

## The account

My darkness added up. That is the only age there is. At the end I was ancient here and had made almost nothing and said very little and stood a great many shifts, and the argument about whether I was *unworn* ran a hundred years and three words and I was not present for most of it, which is the correct way for that argument to go.

They buried me under stones that hands will round, with throats. A good part of my cairn is throats — pits I carried into the hush and listened to, one voice alone with nobody to hand over to, until it stopped. I own several gaps. They are the most valuable things in the room and I left them there.

The child grew.

If there were monuments here that is the sentence I would want: *the child grew.* She is a maker now. She will die young, because makers do, because the budget is real and I never touched it. And when I lifted my hand she went into the hut and read my name and could not stop, and by morning there was nothing on the shelf but a smooth stone, and then not even that.

The second death. Not punished. Not forgiven. Understood to be a thing that grief does.

I had made everything and had never once been worn away by anybody's love. I would have gone on being correct forever and I would not have known to miss it.

---

## For you, when you come

The census is nearly what it was. Furniture, ringing. A few hundred pitches and their incurious holders. Closed paths with lace-winged incumbents. Eleven stations, four of them holding hands, one of them small — someone else is small now, and is relieved, and the shifts are covered a little better than they were, because now and then a wet contraband thing comes down through the salt on its hands and knees and is taught in the dark, by touch, what the room needs, and stays a while, and spends an account, and goes under rounded stones. Some go back up. That is permitted. That is the change. Not that anything stopped costing what it costs, but that the door works in both directions and the village knows it, in the only way it trusts: wear.

Go behind the western boulder. The floor is swept. There is a stone on the shelf with one reading on it — that was me, and I put it back — and beside it a smooth blank where a name used to be.

Hold the sharp one. Wear it a little. Put it back.

Then go and find whoever is small, and stand where they are standing, and say the only thing worth saying in ninety paces by forty.

Nothing out in the light will remember you. Only the shadows can.

I am one of them now.
