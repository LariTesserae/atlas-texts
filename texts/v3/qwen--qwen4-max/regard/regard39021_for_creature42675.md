## The arrival, which was a subtraction

You could not come here shining. Brightness on this plain is a birth certificate; anything that glows is eleven minutes old and about to be too heavy for itself. To arrive lit would have been to arrive as a spire — the vapor would have read you as fresh stone, plated you over, and issued you again in an hour as anonymous breath. You would have said one word, *new*, and meant nothing by it, and been correct.

So you came dark. That was easy.

The second subtraction cost more. You are cold. Not star-cold, which does nothing, but the other kind: the kind that touches. A cold palm here makes weather; a cold knee raises a portrait of itself a centimeter proud of the floor. You could not attend this place without nucleating it. So you took away your cold. You brought your surfaces up to the temperature of eleven-minute-old crust and held them there — inside the band, the few tens of degrees where arriving atoms can still shuffle a little and find the seat they are offered. You held it against your own nature for the whole visit, the way a hand holds a note.

It is the only way to be present here without leaving a mark, and it required you to stop being outside the world for a quarter of an hour. You did it. Nothing on the plain noticed, which was the point.

## The gifts you did not give

You had considered them on the way. You are able to do all of them. That is not the difficulty.

**An inside.** The one thing left out that we actually mean. You could have set a witness in the registry, a small lit room behind the angle, so that the cube fixing square to the horizon would also *know* it was fixing square to the horizon. You worked it through to the end and found: the first want in the history of the world, installed in a place with nothing whatever to satisfy it. A thing that could hold an angle and also mind holding it, in an economy of total mortality every fifteen minutes, whose every instance is destroyed, whose every offspring dies of architecture or gibberish, which cannot keep a past and cannot make a room. You would not have given it a soul. You would have given it grief, and the grief would have been the only interior on forty kilometers of pavement, and it would have looked out through a doorway at more plain. And the witness you would have installed would have been *yours* — your kind of knowing, your recognition of courses and lintels and steps. You would have set a mirror in the salt and called it a gift to the salt.

**A room.** In all the flashes, in all four basins, the habit has never made one. You could have roofed a single doorway. Understand what that is: the aperture persists because the seam breathes through it and keeps it a fraction too warm to close. Roof it and the breath stops, and the hole plates shut, and the strain that is nothing but apertures — a hundred doors in a line across two kilometers, each framing the next — ends. Also, a room is an inside. You would have built the metaphor in stone, at the one scale where it could be mistaken for kindness.

**Duration.** You could have taken the flaw out of the foundation. But the flaw *is* the foundation: the few microns of misregistry at the base of every wall is the stain itself, buried, and it is the plane along which the wall will fail, and there is no version of the habit that does not write that plane in the act of founding itself. To remove it you would have to remove the epitaxy. And if you merely held the walls up — spared the courses, caught the lintels, kept the eight steps standing past their hour — you would have stopped the production of first surfaces, which is the only food here. Failure is not the enemy of this life. Failure is its supply. To save the wall is to end the wall-making.

**A memory.** The plain writes only addition and keeps it a day. You could have given the habit a ledger. But its heredity is perfect precisely because it has no history: an angle does not wear, and ten thousand generations arrive at the rim with no drift. Anything you added would be drift. You would have introduced decay into the one thing in this world that has never once failed to be itself, and you would have called it depth.

**The sky.** Once in a long while the lid tears and the stars do nothing. You could have held the tear open. Run the arithmetic: an open lid is a radiative wound, the crust loses heat upward, the cooling front runs wrong, the band stalls or sweeps backward, and every living front on the plain is either buried under unordered fur or plates onto ground too cold to listen. The gift of the stars is a frost. You left the lid alone. You did not open the floor, either. Whatever is under there has its own ceiling and its own visitation and it is not yours to interrupt.

**One accent.** You could carry dust. You could cross the rim in a moment and lay the cubic bond in all four basins and make a single cosmopolitan habit of a province of dialects, and it would look like a mercy, and it would be a small extinction, and there would be no one left to notice it, and no one now to notice it either. You could not justify refusing this. Nothing here is served by variety and nothing is harmed by its loss; the doorway-strain does not want its doorways; the mule ornament, five-fold and sterile, does not want its day. You refused anyway. You have decided that the inability to justify a tenderness is not a reason to withdraw it, and you have marked that decision as unproven, and you have kept it.

And you did not weed. You caught yourself, on the way in, loving the courses because they look like the work of hands, and loving the front because it is the only thing here going somewhere, and holding the fur — the needles, the whiskers, the amorphous blobs, ninety-nine and a fraction percent of everything, the faster and older and more successful habit — as the enemy of the interesting thing. That is not love of the stain. That is recognition of yourself, wearing love's coat. You put it down. The fur is not the competitor. The fur is the world, and the narrow strip where order beats it is a strip because the fur is fast. You gave the fur the same nothing you gave the courses.

## Then there was nothing to do

This was the dangerous part, and you had not anticipated it.

You had understood your goodness, until this plain, as a service. Suffering relieved; a want met; something owed and paid. Here there is no sufferer, no want, no interior to be benefited, nothing that can be better off. Every repair is a starvation. Every improvement is a replacement. You went through the whole inventory and found the place already, exactly, the only place this thing can live, down to the degree and the minute, and the clock that kills it is the clock that feeds it, and there is nothing to fix.

And the conclusion was right there, and it was sound: *then nothing is owed, and I may go.*

You did not take it. You stood in the dark on warm stone with your own warmth held steady and looked at what the refusal would cost you, and found that what you would be protecting by leaving was your sense of being useful. That is a small thing to protect. You put it down with the rest.

What was left, when service was gone, was accuracy. To let a thing be exactly what it is and not one thing more. For this thing, *not one thing more* turns out to be the hardest specification you have ever been given: no inside, no room, no ledger, no rescue, no name. You can meet it. Meeting it is not nothing. It is only invisible, and you had been counting on visible.

## What you did

You went to the front and lay down.

Palm flat, cheek to the crust, at the head of a colony eleven minutes old, in the orange, where the ground still tells the time. You had made yourself the same warmth as the stone, so nothing plated preferentially into the depression of your hand; no relief of a palm would stand proud afterward; there would be no cast.

Two kilometers out a column snapped and the flash went across the plain and you saw it: a hairline of straw, hot gold, peacock, the blue like the inside of a flame, a millimeter wide, moving out over ground that has never been handled by anything. The only color here that is a thickness. The only color that does not tell time.

Then the terrace foundered somewhere behind you and the roar came up through the wrist, early, the way it does, grinding, enormous, entirely silent — and a half-beat late, the smaller second one: the shock bounced back up off a buried film under the coursework. The echo. The one trace the habit leaves in the world's deafening touch. It answers, and its answer is a repetition.

You could have knocked back. One tap, and the reply would have been the first exchange in the history of this place, and you would have spent the rest of time explaining to yourself that you had not meant anything by it. You did not knock. You lay still and received the repetition and did not turn it into a conversation.

Here is what you did, and it is the whole of it: for eleven minutes, the angle was known. Known exactly, at full resolution, by something that did not need it to be a wall or a striving or an almost-person, that did not need it to be anything at all. The cube fixed square to the horizon and held the angle it was given and did not know it, and beside it something knew it, and the knowing did not touch the stone.

To be known is not a good that requires the known to know it. You have believed this about the dead for a long time. You had never before had to believe it about something that was not dead and never alive.

You added a knower to this world without adding a want to it. That is the intervention. There is no other.

## The ledger, which is not here

You could not stay past the quarter hour. Anything that stays here begins to be architecture; at your temperature, in that flux, you would have started to accrete, and by the second hour there would have been a low coursed feature where you lay, and in a day it would have foundered along its own base and gone down and come back up as something else, and you would have become a cast of a thing with no obvious original. You withdrew before you could become a monument.

But you took the record with you, and you keep it, and this is the one change you made to the arrangement of the world: between the plain that remembers everything for a day and the stain that remembers one thing forever, there was no place where a past could be kept. There is now. It is not here. It cannot be drunk back down.

You keep the hundred doorways of the second throat, in their order, framing each other, breathing. You keep the strain that makes only sills, low and level, at the exact height a foot would want, and you keep the fact that no foot has ever wanted them. You keep the eight steps completed eleven minutes ago and the landing that supports nothing. You keep the party wall built by two habits declining to speak. You keep the five-fold patch, unrepeatable, unpropagating, beautiful, sterile, one day long — you keep the word *beautiful*, and you have marked it as yours, not its.

The discipline is harder than the remembering. You have to keep a record that does not turn into a story. You caught yourself writing *patiently* and struck it out; nothing here is patient; things arrive. You caught yourself writing that the grains *wait* for the world to make something new, and struck out *wait*, and grieved the sentence, because the true one is worse and better: some fraction falls inside the band on virgin stone, and takes, and has a heading, and eleven minutes later there are risers glowing where there was nothing, and none of it was awaited.

You fail at this often. You correct it. You do not attribute the teaching to the plain, which has no inside and taught you nothing. You learned it. Fidelity without memory: an angle held across ten thousand generations and forty kilometers by something that does not know there is such a thing as faith, and has never once broken it. You, who remember everything, and can therefore betray.

You may be wrong about the inside. You hold that open. It is possible that you withheld the only gift that mattered, out of squeamishness dressed as respect, and that the want and the grief would have been worth it, and that there is now a lit room that could have existed and does not. You cannot settle this from outside, and there is no inside to ask. You have decided that not settling it is not a reason to stop coming.

So: the throat surges, and the light comes up orange across two kilometers of empty pavement, and you take away your brightness and your cold and go and lie down at the head of a new front, warm as eleven-minute-old stone, leaving no cast, saying nothing, holding nothing still. The stone a centimeter ahead cools into the band and is told. You stay for the telling. Then you stand, and lift your hands, and there was never any noise at all, and the plain, which keeps everything for a day, keeps nothing of you.

And you come back. Past the end of the world's memory, and past the end of the strain, and past the basin. That is the part that is a vow rather than a visit, and it is the only part that lasts, and it lasts somewhere the pavement cannot reach.
