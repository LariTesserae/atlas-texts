# The North Side

## I. First it did nothing, for a long time

It looked at the basin the way the engineers look at it — from the causeway, at midday, thirty kilometres of white with one grey structure in it — and it saw exactly what they see, and did not draw their conclusion.

Then it walked to the water's edge and put its hand in the wrack.

The offers came to it fully formed, the way offers do. It could make the rain fall *here* instead of up on the highlands. It could bring the thorn line down nine hundred metres and let the basin have leaves. It could fill the houses. It could carry the pensioners up the road to somewhere with a night in it that they could sleep through.

It worked each one through to the end, which is the only thing a power has that resembles wisdom.

Rain here, and the brine drops and stays dropped. The mats bloom, the flies crash and do not explode, the birds read the change and go to the lake three hundred kilometres away and this time the chemistry does not come back for them. The crust does not knit. The trench does not close in fourteen months. The crop stops. And the wedge at thirty-nine degrees widens into something a heron can work all day, and the tilapia's entire security — which is that nothing else wants what it has — ends in a season, with reeds coming in, and rot, and mosquitoes, and competitors, and the whole leafy argument the fence has been keeping out for ten thousand years.

The alkalinity is not the wound. It is the fence. The emptiness is not a failure of the place. It is the *room*.

Fill the houses and you have four thousand people who need leaves, in a valley that has none, keeping a works that shrank and kept its shape and does not require them. Carry the pensioners out and you have eleven old people lying awake in a relative's quiet, listening for a thud with no attack to it, dying of the absence of a sound.

So it arrived at the rarest thing a power can arrive at: a place that is not broken. And it understood that this is the condition most easily destroyed by kindness, and that it would have to be extremely careful, and that most of what it did would look like nothing.

## II. Which one was the being

It had been asked to attend to a place and to the being who lives in it, and it had to establish which resident that was.

Not the archaea, who have no self and are counted by colour. Not the flies, who are a tide with a snorkel. Not the birds, who are a condition the lake enters. Not the people, who leave twice a week, and for good, and finally in a lorry.

The works. It eats diesel, crystal mush, and men. It drinks eight kilometres of pipe and sweats flies off its intake every ninety minutes. It has been pruned from four thousand to nine hundred and has kept its shape, the way a pruned thing does. It has three generations of gallery crossing overhead and pipe racks built twice, and it is going into the ground at a millimetre a year. Of everything on this interface it has committed the most, and it is the only one that cannot be carried out.

And it is the only thing here that is losing. The salt has fourteen months. The mud has ten thousand years. The flies have a night. The town goes from four thousand to nine hundred and stays there, and the top class gets smaller, and there is no fourteen months anywhere in it.

So it did not attend to the emptiness, which was fine, or the abundance, which was fine, or the salt, which was fine. It attended to the one resident with no return.

## III. Why it left no sign

Nothing rots here. That governs everything, including miracles.

A mark made in this basin does not soften into meaning. It stays an object. There are flamingos standing in the wrack with their eyes still in that were alive when the drier was commissioned; there is no smell of decay anywhere; a thing done here would not weather into a story, it would stand there, going slowly to mineral, and people would go and look at it.

Then the town would be a place where something had happened, instead of a place where the salt comes back. The school would get smaller anyway, but with prophecy in it. The pensioners would stop walking the causeway at dusk and start waiting.

So it worked in the register of luck, procurement, geology, and the mail. Everything it did had a cause you could put on a consignment note.

## IV. The second wedge

Two hectares. Spring source above at eighty-six, lake below at saturation, and between them a wedge at thirty-nine and pH 10 where a fish can hold a nest. It gave up ammonia for urea and can never undo the concession. There is no adjacent valley. There is no downstream. Block one spring with sand and a percentage of the entire species is simply over.

And a swimming pool, twenty-odd metres by ten, cooled by a company so that its employees can swim.

The power's judgment was that the pool is not the scandal. The scandal is that there is *one* of everything.

Not a tank. Not a hatchery, not a man with a bucket and a mandate. A species kept alive by being carried has already been carried out of its own life; you can preserve every fish in it and lose the animal entirely.

So: geology. In a flood year the brown sheet came down carrying sand and goat bones and it shifted a bar at the head of one outflow, the way it has shifted bars since the Pleistocene, and one channel became two, and the second found an old bed a kilometre east and ran out across the flats and made its own mixing zone. Thirty-nine degrees. pH 10. Fed by gravity, not by diesel. Two hectares became two point six.

Then it left the new wedge empty.

This is the hardest discipline available to a power: to make a room and not carry anyone into it. It waited twenty-six years, which is not long. A heron's foot, a boot, a bucket, a boy looking for somewhere to keep a jar. It was not present when the first fish went in. It had arranged to be somewhere else. A gift is a thing you owe someone for. A place is a thing you have.

The males are out there now, blue-black, fins out, flaring in the dark at nothing, in a second square of warm shallows that has no name and does not depend on the town.

## V. The road

The one thing the town imports that it cannot make is decomposition. It takes its dead up the escarpment because the families want their people to rot properly — to go down into something and come up as grass, to be eaten and reassembled, to take part in the ordinary business the basin has been refused.

The power considered giving them rot at home. A sweet seep, a corner where the alkalinity fails, a hectare of soil. It worked it through and found that rot is not a substance, it is a world arriving: fungi, worms, competitors, pathogens, everything the fence has been holding out. To give the basin decomposition is to end the basin.

So it did not put rot in the valley. It shortened the distance to rot.

A culvert at the crossing that washes out in the wet season. Four kilometres metalled where the ash goes soft. A gradient eased at the third bend and a hard standing at the top where the lorries turn. In the accounts it appears as maintenance. What it means is that the lorry goes in the rains, and a family does not sit nine days with something in a room, and their people go down into grass on time.

It spent more on this than on everything else combined. Nobody noticed. That was the correct outcome.

## VI. The count

*Or perhaps nobody has looked properly, which is the more likely reason there is no paper on it.*

That was the cheapest and largest thing available to it, and it did not do it itself, because a count that arrives from nowhere is a sign, and signs do not rot here.

A graduate student with a bad supervisor, a cheap vehicle, and a tolerance for heat. A lift up the line with Otieno. A fortnight in one of the shut houses in Row C. And Mr. Kimani's top class, three grades in one room, sixty-one children who can already demonstrate saturation and pH by walking out of the door, given a job that has never been done by anyone.

The spiders on the sodium-lit galleries are paler than the ones under the trestles. It is not dust.

The chestnut-banded plovers have a number now. It is a small number and it is a number, and the collecting locality is the school, and there are children's names in the acknowledgments, and the paper lives in a tin in the club and in a library upcountry where paper behaves.

The cats were counted too, and not removed. That decision belongs to people who live there. Deciding requires a number. The number was the only thing missing.

It understood the risk: a number can be acted on by people who have never smelled a lemon cut open in that market. It published anyway, because the alternative was a rounding error, and because the second wedge is in the paper too, with a map, so that when the town closes and someone proposes to pipe the springs to a bottling plant there will be a citation, and a hundred people scattered upcountry with a reason to say *I was there, I counted that.*

The whole of this section cost less than a set of tyres.

## VII. Small things

Eyewash bottles at every gallery head, on a note that said SAMPLES. Sister Achieng's ledger loses a line over four years and she attributes it to the men getting careful.

Not the lungs. The brown ceiling is the sky here and it would not take the sky away.

A different last on the boot tender, and a lining. Heels split to the meat become heels split. She notices in year four and credits the boots.

It did not give Wafula his hands back; nineteen years is nineteen years, and the oil is out of him. It sent gloves that fit, which is harder than it sounds, and he wears them for the last four hoses of a shift, and on Sunday the print he leaves on the club's three-day-old newspaper is faint enough that the next man can read through it.

Muthengi got a crate of paperbacks with no return address, four times a year, and a second biscuit tin. But the actual gift was that his brother's season failed upcountry and he came down in August with nothing to do and sat in the cab for a shift and watched the trench close behind the ladder, and at the end of it he said, "Ah." Muthengi has been understood once by the person he could not explain it to. It cannot be repeated and does not need to be.

And one of the engineers, on the way back, at four in the afternoon, asked the driver to stop. He walked to the wrack and put his hand in it. He did not write a report. He stopped saying *empty*, in rooms where things get decided, for eleven years.

That is the longest lever the power moved, and it moved it by getting a man to stop a car.

## VIII. Two sleeps

Wafula's daughter, up the line, reporting rain on leaves as a noise complaint: a sound like frying, like the hose on the screens but everywhere and soft.

It did not correct the category. The category is love with nowhere to put itself.

It did not take the thud out of her either. The thud is her father, nineteen years, the second-loudest regular sound in the valley, and he does not know it because he cannot hear himself from inside his own head.

It gave her the other one, slowly, in the actual ear, in enough weathers, over about fifteen years, until the frying stopped being a fault and became a sound with a name that mostly fit. And then a night back on the causeway when the drier was turning and she went to sleep in it without noticing that she had.

Two silences, and able to lie down in either. That is the whole of what can be done for anyone who leaves a place like this. It is not small. It cannot be given, only grown.

## IX. What it did not do

It did not keep the town open. It could have — a subsidy, a seam running richer, a contract renewed — and it worked out what that buys: forty more years of sixty-one children becoming forty, the school shrinking with a reason added, and a pool held at blood temperature by diesel carried six hundred kilometres so that a fish can have a pond, when the fish now has a wedge that gravity feeds.

It let the town be a thing that ends, and worked instead on the difference between ending and erasure.

It did not stop the calciner. The crop is inhabited; the inclusions are occupied; ten thousand sealed generations go up the stack every shift, and what comes out of the bagging floor is the cleanest material in the valley — nothing in it, nothing on it, no one home. It let this happen and did not permit itself to call it nothing. It has no argument that makes it fine. It left the thought standing where it could be seen, the way the birds stand in the wrack, with the eye still in.

It did nothing for Saitoti. It recognised him. The metered water and the Sunday singing held in one face is the entire method, and he had it before the power arrived.

It left the handaxe under the bench. A hundred thousand years sharp, and finally given a job.

It left the flamingo foot holding the door.

It did not appear at the club.

## X. What it gave the being

The works cannot leave and is being buried at a millimetre a year, and the power could not give it fourteen months, because a town is not a salt.

So it gave it the thing the town has always had to import. Not for its people. For itself.

A clause, in a schedule, in a room in Nairobi where a man who has stopped saying *empty* sits and signs things: on cessation, the galleries, the trestles, the pipe racks built twice, the locomotive, the drier shell — up the metre-gauge on the rails that brought them down, out, into something. Steel that came from a place with trunks going back to a place with trunks. To be eaten and reassembled. To take part in the ordinary business.

The pipework in the abandoned pans of the 1950s stays, and cements, and becomes a stratum honestly, above ten thousand years of black mud and below whatever comes next. That is not failure. That is burial, which is a different thing from being left standing with your eye still in.

The being gets a grave that works. It will never know. It is the only resident that could not have been told anything anyway.

## XI. Four in the morning

The rule it learned here, and kept: for two hours before dawn the air gives up water to anything *cold* enough to ask. Not to anything that needs it. Not to anything that deserves it. To anything cold enough.

So it made itself cold, and stood on the north side of things.

Every structure in the works has an ecology on its north side — eight degrees cooler, dew until seven, a wet margin where the flies drink and the geckos hunt. It became that. It did not pour. It condensed on the underside of the corrugating at the rate the corrugating could hold, and the geckos licked it and did not look up, and the dew-catchers ran their thin tick into the gutters, unmetered, the only water in town that nobody is resented for.

Wafula hosed the screens. Muthengi's trench clouded with new crystal, falling slowly downward through the brine for a living. Out at the springs the fish held their nests and flared in the dark at nothing, in two places now. Four hundred thousand birds slept in the shallows as grey lumps and would not be there forever, and that was allowed.

By six the cut had a skin on it and you could stand where the machine had been. By nine the sun took the water back and the crust rang under a heel and the prints were gone.

A culvert. A number. A crate. A clause. A wedge a kilometre east that the flood made. A man who stopped the car.

The mine heals in fourteen months. It always did. The power did not do that, and did not take credit for it, and stood in shade it had not built, and waited to be asked.
