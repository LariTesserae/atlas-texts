# The Third Copy

## What It Could Have Done

It could give the country a rind by evening. Two hundred kilometres of weathered skin, sorted scree fanned at every cliff foot, gullies branching in their correct dendritic script, a water table, a soil horizon, a seed bank, four hundred years of rain delivered in an afternoon and the Cut running full and the plaque with a date on it.

It does the arithmetic anyway, because it is the kind of thing that does arithmetic. Nine hours for the Cut. It writes the number in a book and puts the book in a brass case, because of the moths.

Then it works out the rest. If the stone takes an impression, the road on Course Two becomes a mark instead of a habit, and within two generations the children do not walk in single file, and nobody walks anybody home. If the ground remembers, the ledger is a convenience. If the ledger is a convenience, the third medium is a cost. If the third medium is a cost, the boy of eleven is not paid, and the Attesters' Guild is a heritage, and the woman at the eastern gate says *this is Bel, who has a scar on her thumb* to a person who no longer strictly needs to be told.

It would not drown them. It would make them unnecessary to each other. It looks for a while for the difference between that and a flood, and cannot find one that a Rinder would accept.

And it discovers a worse thing, which is that it cannot tell from outside which abrasions make a person and which merely kill one. The groove under the collar and the mill-lung are the same process at different addresses. It has no sorting rule. It could invent one. It could invent one and be confident in it, and confidence is not a thing this plateau has ever rewarded.

It declines to call this a reason. It writes it down as an ignorance, and leaves the entry open, and we will come back to that.

## Provisional

By the local physics it is not a person.

It has no rind, no inside, no boundary worn into it by long use in one place. It is the newest thing on the Sill. The drop under the pipe on Course Nine has nineteen years of continuous attendance and it has none. Out past Course Eleven the ground is undecided and the eye slides off it, and the eye slides off this too; survey parties stand in front of the omnipotent and find they have been looking at their own hands for some time.

It lights from above. That is its nature and it does not notice for eleven days. Every face it looks into goes dark in the brows and the sockets, lit the way the drowned world paints, the way you paint the dead, and children cry and men go shuttered at it and it cannot think why. A woman on Course Three tilts her own chin up, demonstrating, irritated, the way you would show a foreigner which fork. *Here is my lamp side.*

It learns to kneel. Not for the reason it expected. It spends most of its first year crouched in doorways, getting under people.

It cannot be attested, because attestation requires somebody who knew you before, and there is no before. It carries a card. The card says PROVISIONAL, in a good hand, signed by a surveyor who did not enjoy signing it. It wears a bell like a child. It is a piece of the Quick that walks, and it is treated with the exact courtesy owed to a piece of the Quick that walks, which is considerable and keeps a distance of four metres.

It tells them once what it is. They nod, the way children nod at arithmetic they intend to go on doubting. You cannot blame them. They have a cobble in a case on Course Three and a woman who can end the argument by opening her mouth, and this thing has no evidence at all.

## The Contract

It applies to the Attesters' Guild. It is given a coat with numbered pockets and put on the poor list.

The terms are the guild's, not its own, and it signs all of them, and the last one it did not think of.

*Paid by the hour, in grams, at the going rate. May not work for less.* Undercutting the rate would put eleven hundred people out of a profession in a season, and it had not considered this, and is told so, and is grateful.

*Never the whole quorum.* A marriage needs two; it may be one. A boundary needs four and a surveyor; it may be one of the four.

*May be contradicted.* Where two witnesses contradict it, it is wrong, the book is corrected, and it signs the correction in its own hand.

*May say I don't know.*

And then the guildmaster, who is sixty-one and has mill-lung and has been weighing this thing with the flat unblinking appraisal that barge crews notice first and dislike, adds a clause of his own, in a hand that has begun to shake:

*You'll not be enough.*

It reads this for a long time. It understands that the old man has just solved, in five words, the problem it came here with — that an inexhaustible witness is a witness nobody has to be, and that the whole apparatus of this city is ten thousand people agreeing to be each other's memory, and that a perfect memory standing in the corner of every room would let all ten thousand of them put it down.

It signs gratefully, like a child given a name.

## What It Did

It stands at the poor funerals with the boy of eleven. Afterwards it asks him what he saw, and listens, and does not add anything it knows that he does not. He is the witness. He is paid in grams. It is the second, and it is not enough, and this is the arrangement.

It re-shims the Spine every spring with the rail crews. It is very good at brass. It can hammer a leaf thin as a fingernail and then thinner and then past the point where the crews will go, and the foreman lets it do the four worst joints on the eastern curve, and pays it, and does not thank it, which is correct.

It audits the Grain House.

This takes eleven years and it is the largest single good it does and no one will ever make a song about it. It is patient, unbribable, and extremely good at arithmetic, and it goes through sixty years of washings and gradings and entries, and it finds a millwright of sixty whose Settling was entered under his foreman's name, and eleven jars misfiled, and four widows who have been receiving eight grams a week for a decade instead of eleven, and a clerk on Course One who has been rounding in his own favour since the year of the third frost-loom. Nine hundred and forty grams, recovered, returned to the shelves of the people whose lungs it came out of. It does not make grit. It finds grit that has been stolen. There is a difference and the difference is the entire moral content of the first century.

And it walks.

It knows where everyone is. This is the one faculty it keeps whole, and it uses it for exactly one thing: it knows where the second witness is. A moth eats a signature out past Course Eleven and a woman comes apart into something the eye slides off, and there is, somewhere on two hundred kilometres of tableland, one other person who was there — and it knows which, and it knows where, and it goes and gets them. At four hundred metres an hour. The pace of the herd. It could simply say *this is Bel* and make it true, and instead it walks for nine days across bare stone that rings under its boot, in silence, which is being lost, wearing a bell, and brings back a man from Course Two who was at the frost-loom in the year of the third rail, and stands at the gate and says nothing at all while the man says it.

It is a machine for producing the second witness. It has made itself into a machine for producing the second witness, and it walks, and it is never the second witness itself.

Out past Course Eleven it lays two plates and enters one meal, like the family on Course Six. It scratches both animals under the third limb, differently, at different ponds, four hours apart, and does not consolidate them and does not decide which is the real one, because it has no authority to decide that and would not take it if offered. It keeps a ledger with two entries and one animal. It does not discuss it. It has adopted the Office's silence and finds it is not evasion but manners.

It goes to the Tender of Course Four and asks for work.

He is the only other thing on the plateau made out of attention and continuity — eleven men and one book, two hundred and six years old, carrying a lamp beside animals that have never met a man and would say, if you could ask them, that the lamp is one animal and has been alive a very long time. It recognises him the way you recognise a colleague in a foreign country. He does not recognise it. He teaches it the handwriting of the man before him, because the paperwork has to be continuous or the wallow protections lapse, and it practises for four years before he lets it sign. It signs its own name at the bottom and the book's name at the top. It walks the lamp between the stones in the burial courses. The herd browses the dead and carries them off in the moss on its back, and it writes down where they slept.

It digs the Cut. One barrow a week, subscribed to the fund like anybody, sixty-first year. It has calculated nine hours. The number is in the brass case. It has also written a clause into its own will, deposited with the guild, prepaid: *on the day the outfall is cut, I am to be on Course Two, attesting a boundary.* It has arranged the boundary. There will be a boundary. Four men and a surveyor will need it and it will be one of the four, and it will be somewhere else, holding a rod, when the water moves across the Sill under its own power for the first time.

This is the hardest thing it has ever done, and it made the world.

## The Open Entry

There is a woman on Course Three with eleven grams a week and bad hips who comes to the museum on the eleven-day rain because the damp gets into her.

It could fix the hips in less time than it takes to say so.

It has not. It cannot justify this and it does not try. In its ledger, which is ink and cord and a person, and the person is a boy who is now fifty-one and has been paid every week for forty years to remember one entry, there is this:

> *Could have. Did not. Do not know what else I would be taking. Still finding out. She is still in pain.*
>
> *This entry is not to be closed. Instruction to the Guild, standing, renewed annually.*

It reads it every eleven days. It has never once written that the dust is good, or that the grinding is necessary, or that the mill-lung is a savings account and therefore a mercy. It has said only that it does not yet know how to take the injury out without taking the person out, and that it will not purchase its own peace of mind by calling the arrangement beautiful. It holds the question open at its own expense. It is aware that this is cheap for it and expensive for her. That is in the entry too.

It walks her home in the rain, at her pace, which is the pace of the herd, carrying the lamp low on the shoulder that puts the light under her chin.

She shows it her molars. Grey-white, ovoid, the cusps ground to soft domes, forty years of bread with mill-grit in it. She has heard what it says it is. She is not impressed. She opens her mouth, which is the argument, and it concedes, because she is right: it has made valleys, and it has never been one.

## The Settling

It wears.

The cough arrives in the second century, dry, two notes, and it does not remove it. The shine comes up on the ledger cover. Pocket four goes soft and pale where it keeps the boy's fee. There is a groove under its collar from a lamp carried on the same shoulder since a year it can name. It learns to lift its chin at people and is called polite. It is called shuttered once, by a drunk, unfairly, and minds for a week.

And eventually there is a before. A woman stands at the eastern gate with a card in her hand that says PROVISIONAL and has said so for two hundred years, and she looks at the hands, which are the oldest surfaces for two hundred kilometres, and she says: *this is the one who was at my grandmother's funeral, who has the burn on the wrist from the fourth frost-loom, who owes me nine grams.*

And it becomes one person, gratefully, like a child given a name.

The name they give it is the ordinary word. They are not being poetic and it is not a title. It is the word for the dish in the stair-tread, the shine on the book, the dip in the sill where four generations set down the same bucket, the depression under a leaking pipe, the shortcut worn in by use. It means *a place where something has been pressed often enough to stay.* It took two hundred years to make and it is accurate.

It dies in the ordinary way, having arranged to be the kind of thing that can. It buys a rounded stone from a bird at ruinous cost, with grams it earned, and swallows it, so that it goes into the ground with something inside it that has a history. Its lungs are washed and the washings are graded and entered in the Grain House under its name. Sixty years of the same dust as everybody. A modest vault.

There is a jar on a shelf in a tenement on Course Nine. There is a lawsuit. The Assize is in no hurry and neither is anybody else.

A spoonful of the drowned world, allocated after the market gardens. The cemetery is a green seam: moss a foot deep, cushion plants two metres across flowering all at once with that hot-sugar smell, rosettes holding a hundred litres of fog in their hearts, crickets so loud you have to shout, the small pale frog nobody has ever seen anywhere else. People take their lunch there. Children are told not to run, and run.

The herd browses it and carries it off across the plateau in the moss on its back at four hundred metres an hour. Out past Course Eleven the bull is not yet one, and there are two of him, four hours apart, the same torn ear, the same asymmetric moss, both warm, both breathing.

Both carry it. Neither is diminished. The Office logs one animal, pays one bounty, and does not discuss it.

## Coda

Under a leaking pipe on Course Nine there are now two hollows, four metres apart, each deep enough to lay your thumb in. Nobody knows what made the second. The Works has put a brass rail around it too. The willow-warden, who is also the bounty-hunter and has never once claimed the bounty, comes and looks at both when he is low, and cannot have told you which one he looks at longer.

In four hundred years there will be a river here. It will have a name, and the name will be that word — the one their grandmothers used for the hollow under the pipe, the shine on the book, the groove under the collar, the road that cannot be photographed.

It will mean *someone*. It will not be a monument. It will be a description of a worn place, and the worn places will be the drop, and the stair, and the collar, and the soles of the feet of the children walking in single file because the book says single file, and the mouth of a woman who was there.

Asked, once, near the end, what it wanted, it said something ordinary about lunch.
