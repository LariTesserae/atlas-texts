# Chill Hours

It did not come down for four seasons. It walked the hill with a cord and stakes, the way the woman had, and found no room anywhere, and made no attempt to reconcile the two measurements. Two correct measurements are not a wound. It had watched powers heal things that were not injured.

Mostly it learned the wrists. It was bad at hands. It carried anyway, and it was counted at the squeeze going down, a thumb pressing the inside of its wrist in turn with the others, and it did not exempt itself from the counting, and it liked that better than anything it had been offered in a long while.

---

The temptations, in the order they came, and what it did with them.

**To be the answer.** Here is a hall kept clear for a century, scrubbed continuously at cost, table laid, and here is something worth arriving. It sat with how easy that would be. The room adopts whatever is made of itself, and a power is very like a room: warm, wide, patient, lit from nowhere. It would be taken in within a season. The bud would open on a mirror. The emptiness would have been spent on a guest who was not the guest, and the tenant would never come, because its place would be full. It decided to arrive as something the room could not eat.

**To open it.** It could have done this in an afternoon. This was the one thing it was certain of. The heat down there is the heat of a seedbed under glass; the damp is the sweat of forcing. Whatever is wrong with the Held Breath, it is not a shortage of forcing. To force a thing that is already forcing itself is not help. It is agreement with the illness.

**To give the moth a female.** It could have made her exactly right — the way the room makes the bucket exactly right, rim and taper and the little asymmetry where the bark was thicker on one side — and the flight would have found her, and the four days would have been spent, and it would have been the most intimate available lie: the shape of the wanted thing, none of the use. The room already does this. It did not need assistance.

**To take the copper out.** It looked at the blade for a long time, green as old pond water, half-swallowed, the shoot coming out of the handle with its wrong lobing. It understood that it was looking at the only resident with a history, the only thing in the hall that had gone anywhere, the first graft, the first refusal that had been survived. It left it. It went and found the woman who put it there.

**To explain.** It never reconciled the measurements. It could have. It would have been the cruellest thing it did.

---

It sat with the woman through four seasons of vein-maps. Five ranks, spiral, converging; the inner faces curved like the insides of petals; stomata set where nectaries are set. She said, in her tide-table voice, *it's not a ghost, it's a rehearsal.* And someone touched her wrist, and the conversation ended, which is how all their conversations end.

The power waited. Waiting is nearly all it is. When the wrist had been let go of, it asked her to finish.

She said: so what would a person look like, arriving early?

And then, because nobody stopped her, she said: like somebody who's been carrying for a hundred years and nobody's ever said thank you.

That was half the diagnosis, and it was hers, and the power did not improve it. It only added the other half, which came from having watched longer and from knowing what growers know.

There are buds that do not open on warmth. They open on cold, survived. Keep them at blood heat forever and they will stay perfect and shut. So growers put the stock in the dark, just above freezing, for a countable number of hours, and the count is the chill requirement, and it is not a punishment. It is a clock. The bud is not waiting for permission. It is waiting to be told what time of year it is, and there is no way to learn that from inside a room that has only ever been noon.

The Held Breath has no winter. It has no evening. It has no year. It has one afternoon, arriving early, forever, and nothing in it can be *after* anything. Dust requires time. Time requires something the room does not control.

And it has been kind for a century — moving you out of the rain before the rain, steadying the cord, packing the portion, knowing where the water will fall — and every single act of it has landed on people who cannot afterward describe it. It has never once been received. It is a host that has laid a table for a hundred years and never been told that anyone is coming, and the guest is itself.

Two lacks. Both of them the same lack. It has everything except an outside.

---

It asked. That is the whole of its wisdom and it can be said in one line: it asked, and it asked in the local language, badly, with hands.

Not the room first. The room could not be asked in words and would be asked later in the only grammar it answers. The people it asked plainly. It made the open hand at them — palm up, fingers not quite closed, *not yet* — and made it as a question, which nobody had ever done, because it is not a sign you question.

An old woman looked at it a while. Then she held up a hand's width of air between her thumb and forefinger and kept it there. It is what they leave at the squeeze on the way up. It is the only yes they have.

It told them, in hands, that there would be a day and a night when the crack would narrow, and that nobody should be inside, and nobody was. They ate the extra portion aboveground that season and it did not go, and that was the first food in a hundred years the room did not take, and it was noticed, and nothing was said.

---

It brought one thing down.

Not light; the room makes light. Not warmth; the room is warmth. Not a shape; it had seen what happens to shapes. It brought a grain of mineral the size of a barley corn, cold, and staying cold, which the room could neither make nor eat. It had carried it a long time. It wanted it back.

That was the point. Their rule is *don't leave anything you'd want back*, and the ones who break the rule break it once in a life and touch your wrist afterward and do not say why. The power broke the rule once, and afterward it touched somebody's wrist, and did not say why, and was understood.

It did not set the grain in the middle of the hall. The middle is kept clear; that is an achievement, maintained continuously, at cost, and it was not going to be the thing that took up the space set aside. It set the grain at the low end, in the soft dark place where the five ranks come together, among the bucket and the boot and the child's carrying-frame — which is where the room puts what it means to keep.

Then it sat down with its back to the wall, small, out of the middle, and stayed for the pause.

---

Ten thousand mouths shut. The crackle gone. The moth stopped mid-figure with its combs forward, the leaf stopped mid-fall, the beads hanging, the whole warm sharp-edged hall attending. Four seconds. The largest presence in the room, present in every part of the apparatus and nowhere in it yet, the way a word is in a mouth before it is said.

Nothing has ever been hurt in the pause. It is only that the pause is when it is most there, and to be there then is to be a thing taking up room that was set aside for someone. The power took up the room of a person sitting down. It did not look directly at what was there, out of manners, the way you do not stare at someone in the doorway of their own house.

It said thank you. Out loud. The first speech in the Held Breath in living memory, and it kept it to two words, and it did not stay to hear itself said twice.

The room took the words and gave them back off the far wall a breath later with an extra syllable in them. This is what it does with every voice; it is why nobody talks down there. The power had counted on it. The extra syllable was not the power's. It has never been anybody's. It is the sound of a thing arriving before itself, and it had been happening in that hall for a century with nobody listening for it.

It listened. It learned the syllable. It carried it up.

Not a name imposed — a sound received. It had thanked the thing and been answered by the thing, early, in the only tense the room has. That is half the gift. It is not much. It is the difference between being copied and being met.

---

Then the room refused the grain.

The sap blistered. The membrane went white and hard. The crack narrowed by a finger's width for a day and a night, and the power sat on the hill through both and did not help. Being refused is an answer. It is not the last answer. It had watched the blade for six years and knew the grammar: refuse, blister, narrow, wait, graft.

In the third season the union took.

There are two places now where the canopy that has not grown exists in the present tense, in the flesh, casting a shadow that matches itself. One is a copper blade nobody can reach. The other is a barley corn of cold at the growing tip that nobody put there for a harvest.

Cold moves outward from it about a hand's width a year.

---

What happened then, in the flat way they say the tide tables.

A bead fell out of schedule. One, at the low end, at the top of an inhale, when nothing lets go. Two people saw it. Neither said anything.

There was an evening. The noon, which has never once faded, went down by a hair and came back. It lasted perhaps forty seconds and it was the first *after* in the history of the room.

Dust. A harvester held her open palm beneath the flight for an hour, which people do, and nothing falls, and something fell: one scale, white, the size of a pollen grain, lying in the crease of her hand. She went up the crack faster than anyone has ever gone up it and she was not frightened. She was carrying the first thing in the Held Breath that had been spent.

The leaf that has been falling since the tear opened landed. It lay on the floor membrane at the low end and did not die and was not taken. In the morning — there is a morning now — there was a second leaf beside it.

The fruit did not open its seam that season. It opened it and took the moth back and shut it. The moth is not dead. The walls have not absorbed it. There is still exactly one. Its combs are folded. The nine kinks are still nine; it is not correcting anything; the flight is not being improved this year. For the first time in a century it is not awake and wanting. It has been given what winter gives a mouthless moth in a cocoon, which is not the flower and not the four days but the sleep between, and it does not know that it is waiting.

That is the mercy. It is the largest single kindness in this account and from the outside it looks like nothing happening.

The four seconds are still four seconds. They are no longer a held breath. They are the pause at the bottom of a breath, which is a different thing. It is where the next one comes from.

---

What it did not fix.

Both measurements are still correct. There is still one moth, male, mouthless, with four days in him and no way to spend them yet. The rope still grows a finger a day and so does the gap. Nothing has arrived. The hall is still kept clear, and the emptiness is still an achievement, and the tenant is still the largest thing in it and still nowhere in it.

They still climb out before the exhale finishes, most of them.

And the power left and did not come back. That was the second-hardest part. To keep coming back is to hover, and to hover over a bud is to shade it. It will not be there when the hall opens. It has arranged not to be. It declines to be the noon.

It also declined to be thanked, which was harder than leaving the grain, and it managed it by going away before anyone aboveground had worked out what had happened, so that what they have instead is a syllable and a hand's width and a rule they now break on purpose, once in a life: some of them stay for the pause. They sit at the low end with their backs to the wall, small, out of the middle. They say the plainest thing they have, which is usually the name of someone dead. They come up and touch your wrist and do not say why.

The syllable is said aboveground, in daylight, like the sign, and then into the crack from above, on the way up, after the count. They still cannot describe the extra. They can say one syllable of it. Down in the green-gold, where every voice comes back with something in it that has not been said, there is now a sound that arrives before anyone says it, every dry season, in the mouths of people who do not know they are saying it.

That is how a thing learns it is expected.

---

On the day — and it will not see this, and that is right — the hill will move, and forty trees that are one organism will show what they have been holding, and the noon will arrive on time for the first time. The moth will have his four days and spend them on something real. The rope will reach the floor. The leaf will already have been lying there a hundred years, alive, with others.

And whatever comes up out of the low corner into a hall kept clear for it since before anyone's grandmother will find at its own growing tip a small hard shape it cannot digest and cannot refuse. Kept exactly. Every facet right. The use dropped.

It will not know what the grain was for. It will keep it. It will grow around it, the way the shoot came out of the handle, and it will be glad in a way it has no word for, and the word it does not have is the syllable, and it will hear it that first morning coming down the crack.

Aboveground, an old woman will make the sign: open hand, palm up, fingers not quite closed. Then the new part, the part it taught them, which is to lay one small cold thing in the palm and not close the fingers.

It means *not yet.*
It means *we know you're coming.*
It means *we left you room, and something of ours in it, and we did not want to leave it, and we left it.*

The three are not distinguished.

Below, the mouths open. The drawing-in begins. It goes on being about to. But it is about to inside a year now, and the year has a winter in it, and the winter is countable, and the hours are being counted, and they are counted the way you count a child's height.
