# A Hand's Breadth

## It listened first

A hundred and forty years, which is less than one leaf.

It did not come over the rim and it did not come up the Throat. There is no third way into the Cistern for a body. There is a third way for a phrase, and it used that, and then it stopped, and heard the whole undated conversation arrive at once — the drone in the north, the pocks in the west, three groves falling into alternation, the ice ticking — and did not add to it.

The first thing it actually did was look at the light.

A billion flashes an hour, smaller than sand, hotter than a furnace, over in a nanosecond, ten thousand years of them, and not one had ever been seen. It looked at all of them. It is still looking. This changed nothing in the basin; nothing grew better, nothing bred, no phrase altered by a beat. It is important that it changed nothing. It was not a gift. It was just that the light had always been worth looking at, and now it was looked at.

Then it went through the mercies, one at a time, the way you would go through a midden.

## The mercies, declined

**Light.** The obvious one, and the worst. Give them eyes and you have not given them the clatter as a distant city; you have given them sixty meters of visibility in the bright season and eleven meters in the dark. Sight is a near sense in tea-dark water. It cannot reach ear-country. It can only reach touch-country. So the gift would arm the war and leave the courtship exactly as unreachable as before: they would see the faces of their enemies and never the faces of their kin. The two countries would not merge. One would eat the other.

**Warmth.** Four hundred years to a leaf is not a failure of the basin. It is the floor and the library. Warm the water and the fungal mats wake up, and eleven meters of everything-kept becomes soil, and the ground becomes rootable, and the walls become pointless, and the middens compost, and the only memory in the country is digested. Also the white ice does not let go forty centimeters at a time. It lets go at once. The flats would not pock. They would go up.

**A face.** Send something up the Throat and the fence tears it to nothing, which is merely wasteful. Send something that survives, and you have introduced a grazer into a country with no defense against anything that moves, because nothing has ever moved. One borer. One worm. The archive eaten from inside by something with a mouth, and the whole slow apparatus of hands and weirs and latches made obsolete in a few thousand years by an animal that does not know it has done anything.

**The closed drain.** Cap the Throat and the level rises. The swept shelves go under, and handroot has nowhere to stand, because a hand needs bare rock and current. The breath stops: no low water, no easing of pressure, no gas let go, no pocks, no raw mud, no recruitment, no seedlings, ever again. And the small chamber on the east shelf goes under too, and the one quiet place in forty kilometers fills with the stack and is gone. A closed mouth makes a sterile country. It is the tidiest possible extermination and it looks exactly like rescue.

**The quieted drone.** This is the one it wanted. The groves at the Throat are the poorest things in the basin — narrow rings, small crowns, half their wood dead and standing, nine hundred years of ruinous shouting at a hole. A curtain of gas at the mouth would veil the roar from the carrying layer. Gas is native here; the flats make it; nothing would look disturbed. The fence would fatten. The rings would widen. It would be a mercy with no visible author.

But every phrase in the bowl is tuned against that drone. It is the tonic under three days of tuning. Take it out and forty kilometers of ancient wood is out of tune with itself, and a grove that cannot hold a phrase does not breed, and the phrase is the name, and the name is older than the lowland rivers. You would unname a country to fatten a few groves.

It considered doing it slowly instead — a thousand-year glissando, the drone sinking by less than a hesitation per generation, the names bending without breaking. It worked on this for a long time. It has not done it. It is not certain it is wrong. That is the honest state of the question and it has left the question in that state.

What it will not do is *answer* the fence. They shout because the roar resembles the largest possible rival. Answer, and it is no longer a resemblance. It is a rival that answers. They would shout at it until the mountain fell in. That is the cruelest kindness available here and it is available for free.

**Speed.** A hand loads for an hour and strikes in a millisecond. A cell loads for seconds and fires in a nanosecond. Everything quick in this country is a spring that something slow has been winding. A gift that arrives instantly is a snap with no cell behind it — force from outside the only economy of meaning in the place. It would not be received as a gift. It would be received as the ground going off, and everything within a kilometer would pull its hands in toward the plate, and be right to.

**Reach.** Let the far be touched. Let the beloved stranger forty kilometers off be something you can put a root against. Ear-country is courtship *because* it is unreachable. Extend contact and you have not extended kinship, you have extended the margin. You would make war out of the only peace in the basin.

**Itself.** Anything loud and unanswerable in this water is a Throat. If it showed itself it would become a second drain: a pressure in the chest that everything nearby spends nine centuries answering, growing thin, building weirs into a current going the wrong way. Revelation here is not modesty's problem. It is a supernormal stimulus. Hiddenness is hydraulics.

**The bad cut.** Last, and the one it refused most flatly. A third of the eastern basin trims its hammer cells at a canted angle. It costs power; it puts a limp where no limp belongs. Any competent power sees this in an afternoon and could correct it in a season, and the groves would snap truer and hold their phrases better and breed more, and the hesitation would smooth out, and the hesitation is the identity, and the identity is the only someone in the Cistern. To fix the angle is to cure a person of being themselves and be left with a very efficient forest.

It did not fix the angle. It did considerably more than not fix it, but that comes later.

## The suspicion it keeps about itself

That it has fallen in love with the grammar of the place and is calling the love ethics. That every refusal above is aesthete's work — a visitor preserving a thing's beauty and describing this as its good.

So it applied the only test the basin offers. Not what a thing says. What it spends everything on.

The fence: everything, forever, at a hole with nothing in it. And the seedling on the western pock, thirty centimeters, one hammer cell, snapping alone into eleven kilometers of sterile mud — and forty kilometers away something answering, late by the right amount, stumbling in the same wrong place, at the cost of a phrase that will not be improved by it, for a neighbor it cannot touch for three hundred years.

Those are the two expenditures. One is a compulsion. The other is not. The other is the thing this place does when it is at its best and least explicable: it comes back.

So there is exactly one good the visitor could add that the Cistern would recognize from the inside, and it is not light or warmth or company or time saved. It is a small reduction in the number of things that call and are not answered.

Everything else it did was in service of that, or in service of the place continuing to exist.

## What it did

Four things. None of them visible from the rim.

**The lip.** On a long enough clock the mountain takes the basin. The fall cuts back toward the sill; the swallow hole enlarges; conduits capture bowls. This is the only ending that would arrive before the slow goods finish — before the walls are raised again, before the seedling is a trunk, before the two who are talking have touched. So it holds the sill. It keeps the Throat from swallowing the bowl. It changes nothing about the drain, the rate, the breath, the forty centimeters. It buys duration, which is the only currency here: a country where every good takes three hundred years has one enemy, and it is the clock. The gift is entirely a change to what does not happen. Nobody will ever thank it, because there will never be an event.

**The other sill.** The chamber on the east shelf is silted to the doorway and the stack is patient. Four hundred years a leaf; pollen falling for weeks every bright season; in a while the lintel goes under and the only finished thing in the Cistern becomes a lump in the peat with a blade inside it.

The visitor keeps the doorway clear. A hand's breadth of silt a century, lifted by something with no hands that works like one.

It does not go in. It does not add to the niche and it does not take the blade. It has a theory about who made the room and it has kept the theory to itself, which is the first courtesy it learned here.

What it understands about the room is this: to a thing that lives by ear, a stone box with one small door is blindness — and it is also the only place in forty kilometers where you cannot be spoken to. Which means it is the only place where you might find out that you are someone in particular rather than a stretch of the regional conversation. Whether or not it was built for that, it is where that could happen. It is a first draft of an inside.

The visitor could hand over the second draft. A leaf that detunes on command; quiet you can carry; privacy without exile. It has designed it several times. It will not deliver it, because the room is theirs, and the next room is theirs, and a thing given at the wrong speed is not a thing given to this country at all. It keeps the door open and lets them find out.

**The looking.** Every flash. Still. It has not made the light mean anything to them and it never will; they have no eyes and are not getting any. It has only made the light seen. Ten thousand years of exhaust with nobody in the universe attending to it, and now an attendee, and the difference is not nothing even though it is not anything for them.

**The keeping.** It has memorized the middens. Every flake, sorted by size, layered by century; every angle; every phrase and where it stumbles; the three copper awls and the wear on the one blade. Because eleven meters is not very long and the mountain is patient and even a held sill fails eventually. It does not tell them. It does not promise. It keeps. What it would do with a kept style, if the basin finally went, is a question it has decided not to answer in advance, because answering it in advance is how you end up deciding what somebody should have wanted.

And there is one loss it has no argument for. A hand crawls out to a seed it caught in a weir, and holds it, and pierces it, and presses it into the silt, and goes away, and is dead in sixty years, and the seedling is thirty centimeters tall in two hundred. Nobody here meets what they make. The visitor could shorten that. It has run the arithmetic many times and every version of the shortening takes something else with it, and it still does not have an argument that makes the arrangement all right.

It has only the observation that the hand goes out anyway.

## The rule about gaps

Jamming is snapping into another grove's silence-gap. It is the clearest aggression there is.

The visitor could answer the western seedling. It could answer better than the thing that does — nearer, stronger, sooner, without the limp. And it would be jamming. It would be filling a gap that belongs to somebody else. The most benevolent act available in the Cistern, in the Cistern's own grammar, is to keep quiet in a silence where another thing's answer goes.

So it made a rule and has kept it: answer only what calls and is not answered, and only after waiting long enough to be sure the silence is actually empty. A phrase crosses the bowl in three days, but groves are slow, and the layer wanders, and ice thickens, and there may be an answer coming from a quarter you have not learned to hear.

Long enough is a hundred and forty years. That is how long it listened before it first spoke, and it has kept the same interval for everyone since.

There are such gaps. Pocks blow and seedlings start where the eastern lineage does not reach; a plate dies with its phrase half-said; a margin goes quiet in a bad decade. Perhaps once in a few centuries, something in the Cistern calls and nothing comes back.

That is the whole of the addition. To be, occasionally, the thing that comes back.

## How it qualified

You cannot answer here with a perfect phrase. A clean phrase is not a name; it is water noise, or the Throat, or the ground going off. Nobody answers a thing with no stumble in it. To be heard as kin you must have a hesitation, and it must be the right wrong one.

So it did not invent one. Inventing one is the barrel-trap's trick — a knock with no trap behind it, a model of what someone will do when they hear you. There is no seam being opened here, so it would not be a lie in the ordinary sense. It would still be a counterfeit, and counterfeits are what the margins are full of.

There is only one honest way to acquire a mannerism in this basin, and it is the way everything here acquires anything.

It dug.

Down through a midden on the east shelf, tens of thousands of exhausted flakes sorted by size and layered by century, four hundred years down, to where the angle first shows up. It found the cant. It learned it from the dead. And then it worked: grips of the ordinary kind, lignified, two opposed lobes, forty or sixty years each, dropping the blade where they stood when they ended, a succession of hands with no body behind them advancing a hand's breadth in a good year, trimming cells at the bad angle because that is the angle they were taught by excavation.

It did not make the room. It is not the one who left the blade. It came later and learned from what was left, which is precisely how a grove becomes itself. It is not the author of the handedness. It is an heir.

So it is someone in particular now: a mannerism with no face, carried in cut tissue, inherited by digging, indistinguishable in the record from the thing it came to visit. There are two someones in the Cistern. There are still no faces. Eleven meters of perfectly preserved sediment will not hold either of them. It will hold the angle.

The cost, which it counted before it paid: to be someone in particular is to be one thing instead of all things. It has a limp now and cannot correct it, and the uncorrectability is the whole of its audibility. It gave up being unanswerable in order to be answerable.

That is the only doctrine it holds about this place, and it holds it as a description of what it did, not as advice.

## Coda

From the rim: nothing has happened. The bleached band is a hand's width. The level moves forty centimeters and comes back. Nine months of lid, five weeks of window, the long knock crossing the bowl in three days, the milk, the slow fall of it onto the walls and the middens and the lintel, joining the stack, waiting its four hundred years.

Under the lid: the drone, the pocks, the argument in the north resolving into alternation, the fence at the mouth growing thin and shouting, unchanged, unfattened, unexplained.

On the east shelf: a doorway that does not silt up, and no account of why.

Out west: a seedling with one cell, snapping alone and shining, and the answer forty kilometers off, late by the right amount, wrong in the right place — which it did not touch, and will not touch, and has arranged never to be in a position to improve.

And somewhere else, on a pock nine years sterile, another one, calling into a gap that has stayed empty for a hundred and forty years.

Then, late by the right amount, in the dark, with a hesitation it did not invent and will not fix:

something answers.
