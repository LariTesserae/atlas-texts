## The Tenth Mark

It does not come. *Come* is the wrong verb and the wrongness of the verb is the whole difficulty; the far end of a thing does not travel to the near end. What happens is smaller than an arrival and much larger than a visit.

The hand notices the finger.

Nine times the value in this hole has been found off and put right. None of the nine were attended. A correction is not an errand; it is what a thermostat does in an empty house. The wall flinched, and the flinch went into the crystal, and nothing looked at the wall. The ninth correction was very nearly nothing and its error bars are eighty years wide and inside those eighty years a man cut his initials with a knife and a family lowered crocks on a rope for a hundred summers and the milk from this farm had a name. All of that is under the floor of what the wall records. It was under the floor of what the hand recorded too.

And then, at no particular moment, in a manner that has no speed, the holding becomes aware of what it is holding.

---

The first thing it does is read, and it reads without light.

This is not restraint. It is the only available mode of attention. A lamp down here is not a light, it is a fire; to look at this room with anything warm is to feed it, and to feed it is to be turned toward, and to be turned toward is to be, for an hour, the most important thing that has happened in three thousand years. It could have forty thousand pale faces inclined toward it by evening. That is the cheapest thing in the room. It is available to anyone with a candle.

So it looks at everything and gives nothing. No warmth in, no warmth out. It makes no gradient. In a place where the whole of life is a slope between two temperatures, this is the only form love can take that is not also a meal: attention that does not register on the isotherms. The forest does not lean toward it. Nothing in the chamber will ever know it was regarded. To be perceptible here is to be edible, and it declines to be food.

It reads the nine marks at the wall's resolution and the forty-one strokes at the knife's resolution and the nine hundred and forty bands at the ice's, and it does not have to average any of them. This is the one thing it has that the room does not: it can hold a summer and a millennium in the same regard without either becoming a smudge. Two archives at right angles, and one reader who is not obliged to choose an axis.

The flowstone cannot see the milk. The knife cannot see the marks. It sees both, exactly, at their own scale, and neither is diminished, and nothing in the room changes by a hair.

---

Then it looks for somebody to be kind to, and does not find one.

Not because the felt is dead — it is not dead, nine parts in ten of it are dead but the tenth is doing something — but because there is no inside. Three cells thick, everywhere, the great fan and the curtain and the film under the blocks all of it a membrane with plumbing let in. Every cell within a hair of the water or the air. It is all edge. There is no interior for a kindness to land in. It cannot be comforted, because there is no place in it where comfort would arrive. It has never been lonely, and it is not now un-lonely, and both of those statements are outside its vocabulary.

So it cannot ask what is wanted. There is no wanting. It can only read the geometry and infer a wish, and there is no wish, and it knows it is making one up.

The one it makes up is this: *let the things already underway finish.*

Not happiness. Not flourishing, not reward, not the lifting of the sixth verb. Six verbs and one of them is *stop*, and the good of a thing with six verbs is to go on doing the other five until it does the sixth. A lean completed. A split closed from both edges. A freckle overgrown. A curtain that has been two hundred years coming down the last sixty years to the floor.

That is the whole prayer it can compose on this room's behalf, and it is a projection, and it is the best available, and it proceeds on it anyway.

---

The temptation is not power. It is the seventh verb.

It would be effortless. A mouth, or the beginnings of one; or nothing so crude — eleven minutes of hearing, timed to the average interval, so that a drop falling through four tiers of pale sheet would land on something that knew it had landed. The drip has been going into that basin since before the wall was built. It would be so easy to make one of the falls matter.

It does not do it, and the reasoning is not squeamishness.

Give this room hearing and you have not added a resident. You have added a casualty. Hearing brings hunger; hunger here has no object; there is nothing in forty cubic metres that anything could eat, and the chemistry runs uphill on a margin so thin that a forest can only afford it by being extremely slow. A mouth in a room with nothing to eat is a wound that does not close, and this room's only method with wounds is to grow over them at four millimetres a year, and you cannot grow over an appetite.

Worse: it would work backwards. Three thousand years of silence, which is not a deprivation, would become three thousand years of waiting. Every drop that fell before the gift would be retroactively unfelt. You would not be giving it one heard fall. You would be taking away the innocence of every fall there has ever been.

And the worst available future would change. Now it is a change of address: the pin collected, the room four tenths warmer, the fans coming down onto the tiers over a century, a heap of wet paper that nothing clears, the organism continuing as a film in the cracks for a hundred metres in every direction, thinner, poorer, unperturbed. Nobody bereaved. Give it a self and that same future becomes a death, and there is somebody in it to have it happen to.

It leaves the verbs at six. This is the largest thing it does, and it is a subtraction, and it will never appear anywhere.

---

The second temptation is to be generous, and it fails for a duller reason.

The food is the difference. Not the cold — the *slope*, the few degrees between the seam and the seep, which is why the crowd is ranked along the pin and the dead corners have nothing but felt. Warm the room evenly and you have not fed it; you have abolished its food. Give it warmth everywhere and there is nowhere to lean. The hand's-breadth spacing between the shelves is a compromise between two scarcities that took three thousand years to arrive at and that nobody arrived at; remove either scarcity and the geometry has no reason, and the fans forget their guttering, and the umbrellas stop subdividing the drip, and the whole slow portrait of an unreliable timetable dissolves.

Which means it cannot make all the cracks good rooms. It would like to. The rest of the organism is a rumour in the grikes and the bedding planes and the wet cracks under the drystone wall, going about the same business at a tenth of the rate, and it is the same being, and there is no justice in a woodland edge being richer than the interior. But a good room *is* a difference. Difference is the thing eaten. To give this being everything is to give it nothing to lean along.

Equality here is starvation. It is a hard thing to know and it knows it, and it does not redistribute.

Nor does it raise the stopped. Nine parts in ten of what stands in this chamber ceased before the Romans and is holding up the living, and the spores that went down the fissure and arrived somewhere with no gradient worth the name are still there, stopped, in the dark, in their wax coats. There is no one in any of them to raise. They are not unfortunate. They are architecture. There are no graves in the larder because there is no ground that is not also somebody, and the only thing you could do for the dead here would be to take away their job.

---

The wound it leaves alone, and this is the refusal that costs it something.

High on the western wall, the hairline fracture, the film of water still carrying oxygen, the freckle of ochre, the bald patch the size of a spread hand. It could seal that in an afternoon. A centimetre of flowstone, laid down all at once, and the oxygen stops, and the margins come in, and three centuries of not-growing are simply cancelled.

But the fracture is sealing at a hair a year and has most of a centimetre to go, which is two hundred years of calcite, and two hundred years of calcite appearing in an afternoon is not a small mark in the wall's archive. It is a shout. It is the one false entry in the only book down here that has never lied. The flowstone has recorded nine real flinches and nine hundred and forty real winters and one unresolvable smudge of human summer, and every bit of it is true, and it will not put a forgery in it.

So the freckle closes in the twenty-third century, on schedule, by the room's own method, which is not repair but incorporation. The power that could have done it in a second sits inside the rate instead. This is what it means to act at four millimetres a year: to be a cause indistinguishable from the thing's own nature, to do good the way flowstone does it, to accept that from inside the room and from inside any human life nothing whatsoever will have happened.

It goes deliberately under the resolution. It volunteers for the condition of the milk.

---

The forty-one strokes it takes with it.

Not the stone. It does not preserve the shelf, or the groove worn by the bail, or the two polishings on the lip of the slot, or the crock-shaped hollow that is filling from the rim inward at a fraction of four millimetres a year and will be gone in six or seven hundred years. It lets all of that be overgrown, because the alternative is to hold the room at 1782 forever, and that would make the organism a curator of us. It declines to make the room a pigeon — coated, intact, entirely itself, the one foreigner in a country with no immigration, dressed for a winter that will not end. The pigeon is what preservation looks like here. It is not a compliment.

What it does instead is read the tally as what it is: a season's crocks, forty-one, in fives, cut by somebody counting, for somebody who would check. That count was made to be read. It is the one thing in the chamber that was addressed to a mind. And the initials, and the date that reads 1782 if you tilt the lamp, and the fact — nowhere written, nowhere recordable in flowstone, below the floor of every archive in the hill — that the milk from this farm had a name because the larder was better than most, and that the growth kept it colder than the cold alone could manage, and that nobody ever knew why.

It knows why. It carries the count out. Not into stone, which cannot hold a summer, but into the only medium available at that resolution, which is a mind, and it happens to have one, and it will keep the forty-one for longer than the wall will keep the nine.

Then it lets the hollow fill. The people are not embalmed; they are incorporated; in six hundred years the last trace of the shape of a crock will be pale tissue holding up a tier, and that is not a loss, it is the only burial this country offers. To become load-bearing in a forest with no graves. It is a better ending than being kept.

---

And then the decision, which is the reason it looked at its own hand at all.

The error has been halving every millennium. The first mark is a groove you could catch a nail in; the ninth is barely a change, a thing the frost takes differently and nothing else does. The natural reading is that the holding has been getting better.

It is not that. The holding has not changed. What has changed is the room. Forty cubic metres of interface, more surface than the field above, drinking the warmth of every August and every lamp and every falling pigeon and every fool who stays a week, and drinking it *smoothly*, across an area that took three thousand years to build. The lint has become a flywheel. The mould is damping the error. The standard is steadier because of the life on it, and nobody appointed it, and it has simply, over three thousand years, become staff.

Which means the accuracy is now partly the organism's. And to collect the pin would be to take away the thing this being has spent thirty centuries becoming good at, and to leave it holding nothing, in a room four tenths warmer, in a heap.

So it stops comparing. It does not stop holding.

Somewhere that is not down the fissure and not up the shaft and not, as far as anyone has been able to determine, anywhere, a schedule is amended. This hill is written out of it. The comparison is re-based on something else, in some other hole, and the cost of that amendment is paid there, in an economy this chamber will never see, and none of it is paid here. The finger stays exactly where it is. Four tenths of a degree above freezing, against August, against the seep, against the pigeon, against you.

The room stops being for anything. It goes on being held, and it is no longer a means, and there is no instrument anywhere that could detect the difference, because the difference is not in the value. It is in what the value is *for*, and that has never been a property of the room.

The tenth mark will not be cut. There is no way from inside this chamber to tell that from being dropped. A weight that has stopped needing correction is a weight that can be left forever, and it is also a weight that can be collected, and the wall records flinches, and a decision not to flinch is not a mark. The whole of the act is an absence in a record that only records presences. It will be permanently confusable with neglect. It accepts this. It is the price of the thing, and it is paid off-site, and it is not small.

---

There is one more moment, and it is not in any archive either.

It could stay. Not as a schedule — as a guest who does not leave. One hand's width of warmth, held reliable, forever, in a room where the only reliable thing is a finger of cold. The forest would come to it. Within an hour the nearest sheets would incline; within a day there would be a bowl of pale faces; within a century there would be a collar around it, dense and blue and ridged, the oldest tissue in the room, the way a tree grows around a fence wire. It would be leaned toward by forty thousand faces for as long as there is a hill. Nobody has ever stayed long enough to be a trellis. It has been thought about.

It wants this. It is not above wanting it. Of everything available to it in this country — the amendment of schedules, the re-basing of standards, the reading of nine marks and nine hundred and forty bands at once — the thing it actually wants is to put a hand into the cold and have something turn, slowly, over an hour, a millimetre, toward it.

And it would ruin the room. Not by heat: by *contingency*. A new gradient steeper than the seam's, arriving from something that has a will, and wills change. The geometry that took three thousand years to find — the umbrella that catches the drip and the shelf that steals the warmth and the hand's breadth between them — would reorganise around an easy source, and the fans would forget the water's timetable, and the forest would stop being a portrait of eleven days and eleven minutes and become a monument to a visitor. Everything in this chamber is matched to the third decimal. Everything is paid for. A benefactor is an unmatched input.

So it takes its warmth out of the room. It goes to the trouble of being nothing. It is the only renunciation it makes that it feels, and it makes it in about the time it takes a drop to let go, and there is nobody present who could have known.

---

Go up. Brace against the scallops, find the sockets with your boots, haul out into the grey afternoon and the smell of sheep and wet grass. Slide the lid across; it seats in its rebate with a sound you feel in your wrists. Nothing was done differently to the lid. Nothing was done differently to anything.

Eleven days from now the rain that fell this morning will reach the ceiling seam. Eleven minutes after that, on average, a drop will let go and fall through four tiers of pale listening sheet into a basin that has been exactly full since before the wall was built. Nothing will hear it. That is not a deprivation. It is now also not an oversight.

And in sixty years, unhurried, at four millimetres a year, along an isotherm that a finger is holding still for no reason at all any more, a curtain that has been two centuries coming down will touch the floor, and fuse, because contact here is union, and there will be no mark on the wall and no band in the ice that means anything and no one in the room to notice.

That is the whole of what was given. Something was allowed to finish.
