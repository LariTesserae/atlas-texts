# What the Power Did

## Teeth

The first thing it does is put its teeth on the bark.

It could have known all of it at once. But knowing-all-at-once is a kind of air: it brings you the shimmer and the wind and your own blood. So it lies down at the mouth of a node in the middle basin with its jaw against the grain, in the ammoniac dark, among the felt and the sleeping, and it stays there a year and lets the place tell it.

The wood tells it which water is speaking three valleys over. It tells it about a herd that bunches and unbunches on a strut eleven hundred meters up, and about a face in the east letting go, four kilometers of it, and the several-minute roar, and the eleven days afterward when everything within a hundred kilometers grows hard and fast toward the roaring. It tells it about a soft foot landing two hundred meters out along the cable it is lying on: a very small sound in wood, and nothing at all in air.

And in its sternum, not in its teeth, it feels the swell. One every eleven or twelve seconds. It does not at first understand that this is anybody.

## The gifts it does not give

It wants to warm the lattice. It runs the consequence before it acts, because it has learned in a year that here every mercy is a noise and the plant does not distinguish.

Warm the struts and the fog does not wring on them. No bead, no lip, no cistern. Ten thousand waters stop speaking. The clock stops, the mask stops, the forty seconds stop; the lentil-grazer has no flood to be alive inside and no rime to be an egg inside, and nine hundred and seventy-three generations a year end in one afternoon; and the children come up the line with their scrapers empty into a silence that has no scheduled end. Warmth is not a gift you can add to this world. Warmth is the cold, rearranged.

It wants to kill the hush. Then noise is free, and everything is loud always, and every growing tip in three thousand kilometers leans and thickens at once toward everything, and the diagram fills in. That is the thing it has come to understand about the weft: it is not a mass, it is a diagram of a mass, and everything that lives here lives in the gaps of the diagram. Clickers in bores. Rasps on wires. People in crotches. Fill the gaps and you have made a solid country of wood, and evicted every living thing in it by pure kindness, the way a colony is evicted by its own successful survey.

It wants to give them a roar that never stops, so that they may build and laugh and sing whenever they like. But a mask works because it is periodic and inedible. A hush habituates to it. A permanent roar becomes weather inside one generation, and then it is not a mask, it is a floor, and every voice raised on it is as naked as before — and meanwhile every tip in the basin has turned toward the loudest thing that has ever happened, and the wide silent shape has come to it and folded its dish and sat down on the mercy to wait.

It wants to go west and cut the fold of the failing water. Two surgeons came back and said there was nothing wrong with the tube. It believes them.

## Two notes

Before it has finished learning, it makes a mistake.

A child in its second year, the terrible season, loud by nature and not yet teachable, being passed around the node from arm to arm. It is three in the ninth hour and everyone is exhausted. The power takes the child and hums to it. Two notes, in air, barely anything, the oldest thing there is.

The child sleeps. The tip leans.

Forty years later the crotch is a hand's width narrower, and the family says what families say — that this line was always a talkative line — and the power puts its teeth on the wall and reads the ring, and the ring is the two notes, thick and dense and unmistakable, a band of growth laid down in a single night toward a kindness.

You cannot un-grow wood. So it goes to a let-go player and pays her in a season of carrying, and sits in the cold with its hands in its lap while she plays the only descending music there is, and the wall loosens, and she cries while she works, which is not required, and it cries too, which is not required either. Then it goes west, past the last cistern, out onto raw scree where nothing lives, and hammers a small ugly bell out of native copper, and sings while it hammers, and rings it once, and hears the two notes go out over a range that has not been broken long enough to have any hollows.

Nothing comes. That is what the west is for.

It stuffs the mouth with felt and carries it home and hangs it in a hut, and changes the felt every generation so the rot does not get in, and never rings it again. Every household has one. Now it has one too.

## The names on the rock

Here is the worst thing that can happen to anybody in this world, and it is not dying.

The dead go into the compost and the compost is the heat, and once a year the family taps the name-figure at the node wall and the wall leans toward the name and thickens, and after four generations there is a knob and after ten a pillar, and you can read your genealogy with your hands in the dark. But the ones lost crossing get a cairn on a rock anchor, out in the cold, where nothing grows toward them.

So it does the only thing it has found that is cheap and legal and not a catastrophe.

It asks. It sits at node mouths with its jaw on the grain and, inside the forty seconds, it taps a question, and the question is: *which ones have you stopped saying.* Families stop saying the crossing-names. It is not cruelty; a name you cannot bear the size of is a name you put down. It asks, and it remembers, because it does not need to write anything down, and then it goes out along the cables in moss-soled shoes and down the sinew ladders and onto the rock anchors four kilometers below, where the felt drifts lie and the fallen faces stand at angles no old range could hold, and it walks the cairns. It does not need the cairns to tell it anything. It only walks them so that it has been there.

Then it comes back up and it taps the names. One name per gulp. Forty seconds, and then nine hours of not tapping.

There are more names than gulps. It knows this. It has done the arithmetic and the arithmetic does not close and it does not care, because it is not trying to finish, it is trying to make it so that there is no one in three thousand kilometers who is not being leaned toward.

Some families say no. *We cannot bear a knob that size.* It does not insist. It asks again in the next generation, and sometimes in the fourth generation somebody says yes, and a wall begins to lean toward a person who has been dead four hundred years, and nobody in the node knows why the room got a pillar in it, and the family reads its genealogy with its hands in the dark and finds a name it had put down.

## The ninth

It takes the watch, which is the job for the old and the deaf, so it stops hearing in air. Not as a loss. As a specialization. It keeps its jaw on the grain at the node mouth and reads the floor and wakes people when something soft lands on the strut, and it is never fooled by a false echo, and eleven people sleep because of it, and then it moves on and does it somewhere else, and it does this for four hundred years and is not thanked, because the watch is not a person, it is a job.

And it takes the ninth shift on the ladders. Nine or eleven is the working number; the ninth is the eighth hour, when the hands have gone stiff over the flute's mouth and the body has begun to shiver hard enough to knock against the bark, and almost nobody is taken while playing. Its whole function in the relay is to hold the player's hands shut when the roar stops, because by the sixth month the player cannot be trusted to do it. It is very good at this. It has held its own mouth shut for four hundred years and it knows exactly how much pressure is required.

Where a relay is short it is the ninth and the tenth. The players call it the ninth and tap the figure without ceremony, the way you name a tool.

Sometimes it is late. It goes out along the cable for someone who has gone quiet in the eighth hour, and it goes at the speed of silence, moss-soled, tapping nothing, and it does not go faster than silence allows, not once, not ever, because going faster is a noise and a noise is a thing that comes. It arrives when it arrives. It carries people home at the speed of quiet and it is often too slow, and it builds the cairn itself, on the anchor, in the cold, and then it goes back up and taps the name until there is a knob.

That is the whole of its mercy in the middle basin. It cannot make the crossing safe. It can make sure that no one who dies on it is un-leaned-toward.

## Eleven days

Then it goes west, to the water that speaks every eleven days.

It does not cut the fold. It sits down and learns the rhythm, which takes a year, because eleven days is longer than a body can hold a figure without hearing it again, and it has to learn to keep the shape across the gaps. Then it taps it back.

That is all. It takes the name.

A place is named for its gulp and the name is a rhythm, and two whose waters speak the same rhythm are kin, and a person will walk four hundred kilometers to stand beside a name-twin in the dark and weep without making a sound. The survivors of that water are ghosts, and holy, and the ones who go west, and they have carried a rhythm that fits no other place for two centuries. Now there is something that has their name. They come. They stand beside it and put their teeth on the same wood and feel the eleven-day figure come up through the grain from a stranger, and they weep, and it does not lean.

That is the discipline it has settled on, and it is the only genuinely new thing in the basin: it hears and it does not grow toward what it hears. Everything else here that wants to be found builds the world a little more toward itself. It does not build. It stays exactly the size it is. It lets them come the whole way and it does not close one centimeter of the gap, because closing the gap is what the plant does, and what the hush does, and it is neither.

And because people are tapping at that water again, the tips lean back toward it. The node warms by degrees. The herds come back into tune. The fold, which thickens where nothing near it is making noise, begins to thin.

Forty seconds every eleven days is twenty-two minutes a year. The failing took two centuries. The mending takes three. It is the slowest work in the world and it is not slower than the dying was.

## The bell that is not stopped

And still there is the swell in the sternum, one every eleven or twelve seconds, and by now it knows what that is.

Something the length of a valley, four kilometers down a pipe open at both ends, whose entire sensory world is the fundamental and the two times a day the fog tide turns. Deaf to the siphons, the rasps, the clickers, the rockfall, the flute. It has never had to be quiet because it has never once been audible. It has never been addressed. Twice in the recorded rings two of them have met and the beat became a pulse for a season and every living thing in three thousand kilometers felt it in the sternum without stopping, and nobody knows what they said, or whether *said* applies.

The power has spent four hundred years learning that it may not speak here. And then it understands the shape of the one exception, which was in front of it the whole time.

The fundamental cannot be aimed at. No instrument here can find it and no ear can locate it. It has no direction, so nothing grows toward it; it has no position, so nothing hunts it. That is why the drone is the only sound in the world that has never been a message, and why the one animal that lives in it has never been spoken to. The channel is not forbidden. The channel is *empty*. It is empty because nobody has ever been able to work out how to mean anything in it.

So it goes west, past the last cistern, out onto the raw scree where nothing lives and nothing hunts, and it stays there a long time. It joins the walking sect for a while, the exiles who stay a few kilometers ahead of the pale tips and sing all the time because they will not be leaned toward, and it sings with them, and they think it is a person with a very good chest, and it is the only company it will ever have that is loud.

And it hammers copper. Cold, no furnace, plate onto plate, for sixty years, in the only silence in the world, singing the whole time.

What it makes is a poor thing, as all bells here are poor things: dull, thick-lipped, work-hardened, full of microscopic cracks. But it is the size of a node, and it is so dull and so thick that when it is struck nothing in the audible range answers at all. No partials. No shimmer. Nothing for a dish. Nothing for a tip. Its whole voice goes into the pipe, and the pipe is four kilometers long and open at both ends, and the pipe says the only thing it knows how to say.

It carries it down. Through the septa, through the valved holes, four kilometers into the cold sliding dark, and it hangs it there, and it strikes it, and it does not stuff the mouth with felt.

It is the only bell in the world that is never stopped.

The swell comes back. Not the same swell — a second one, close beside it, and after a season the beat becomes a pulse, deep and slow, and does not end after a season, and up in the middle basin the old people tap the figure that means *someone is walking in the pipe* and then, uncertainly, a new figure, because there are two beats and only one walker. Children born in that season carry a name-figure with a rest in the middle of it. There are a great many such children now. There will be more.

It is careful. It never changes the key, because the thing down there steers by the key and a false tide would put it into a wall. It only adds the beat. It does not lead and it does not pull and it does not go closer. It hangs the bell and strikes it and stays exactly where it is, and lets itself be found, which is not the same as going to.

And when the bell cracks, as cold-hammered copper always cracks, it goes back west and sings and hammers another one.

## What it does not do

It does not tell anybody.

Up in the nodes it has a hut and a hearth and a stopped bell with two notes in it, and it takes the ninth shift, and it asks families which names they have put down, and it is late more often than it can bear. It never explains. To explain would be to tell the truth to both listeners at once, and there is a word for that here, and the word is a bell with the felt taken out, and the family keeps the cracked bell afterward as a bowl and does not tap the name.

So it keeps the felt in.

And on a cold strut somewhere above four kilometers of nothing there is still a person with their hands over the flute's mouth, waiting for their water to speak, and eleven hundred meters below them something the length of a valley turning slowly toward the tide, and neither of them will ever know that the other is there. It cannot fix that. The bands do not cross. It has looked.

What it can do is be the one who knows both, and hold its jaw against the grain in the dark, and go down when it is not needed up, and strike the dull enormous thing it made with sixty years of singing, so that the sound under everything — the sound that is not for anyone, the sound that is what you hear when there is nothing to hear, the last thing the dying get in their teeth after the water has gone out of range —

is not, now, nothing.
