# Maintenance

It came the way everything comes here: from above, at speed, with weight.

The floor took its arrival the way the floor takes an adze. No crack, no drama — a sound like a knuckle in another room, and then a sigh, and then the long slow beginning of a hole. It understood within the hour that it had already killed something by standing still, and that if it stayed it would go down two kilometers through the galleries with everything in the dark tasting it, and that this would be correct.

So it did the only thing that has ever solved the problem of being heavy on the plate. It stopped being heavy. It spread. It got wide and it got thin and it stopped having a center.

This was not humility. It was the load calculation.

It made itself, among other things, edible. For eleven hundred years the flat hunters surfed its leading edge and took it as it rose, and it let them, because it had made itself the shape that gets eaten here, and that is what the shape means. It had not come to be above this. There is no above this. There is only the surface and the two kilometers under it, and the only way to be in a treaty is to be one of the things the treaty can do without.

---

It considered doing nothing, and the case for nothing is very strong, and it made the case honestly, the way you would make it for someone you loved.

Forty million years. An equilibrium with a heartbeat, arrived at, weathered into its own shapes, holding its exact thickness against an appetite exactly as patient as itself. Every process here is load-bearing. The pruning kills the plain in eleven years if you stop it. The cullers are a war four hundred kilometers long. The grafters decide which animals can speak to which. The mouths feed the nitrogen that builds the skin that holds the water that holds the people. Pull one thread and you are not being kind, you are performing an amputation on something that cannot consent and cannot heal the way you heal.

And the people have not asked. They have no word for a power and no sign for one, and giving them one is itself a point load: a fixed idea in a world where the substrate eats stillness. They would build toward it. They would stop moving. Building is what kills you here. In forty years the skin would be waist-high around the posts of the thing they built to wait in, and in a century it would be a knot, and the knot would ring flat, and their grandchildren would navigate by it and not slow down.

It sat with this for a long time. It is still, in most respects, the conclusion it holds.

What broke it was arithmetic.

---

It went through the suffering and sorted it, because that is the actual work, and the work is not compassion, it is audit.

The deafness is load-bearing. Sound is the pollination, the territories, the speciation, the whole economy of a continent where sight is worthless across ninety kilometers of haze. To give them ears that survive would mean quieting the plain, and quieting the plain means no chord, and no chord means a silent year, and a silent year is a famine. It could have thickened the air — the air is thin and the ache never leaves and every person alive has a headache that will not stop until they die — but thickening the air is not a gift, it is a different planet with these people's names on it. It left the ache. It did not stop noticing the ache.

The trotters standing in the scars, five years motionless, eyes gone milky, feet at last resting: it could not determine whether this was the worst thing on the plate or the best. It did not go near them. You do not add anything to a creature that has finally found the one condition it wanted and is paying everything for it.

The old drummers going flat, drumming a season or two into a country their own sons answer as strangers, on a boss their family's noise built: it listened to one for a whole season and could not tell whether it was hearing grief or only weather. It did not fix what it could not identify as a harm. That restraint cost it more than any of the interventions.

And then the tanks, and the children, and the morning when the lip goes slick under your heel.

It did the nitrogen four times because it did not believe it. Four thousand people, forty-odd bands, a handful of children a decade across a subcontinent, against the stilters and the drummers and the night tide and the hail-killed acres and the whole wet rain of falling that the mat has been mapping since before there were grasses. The plant's income from human children is a rounding error. It is not a term of the treaty. It was never a term of the treaty. The mouth is tuned to the big low note of a thing coming to drink and a thirteen-year-old's foot happens to cross into it, and out of that coincidence four thousand people have built a threshold, a rite, a sign that means *not yet*, a way of handing children over on the callus among a hundred strangers at the exact hour they find out with their whole body that they have become the kind of thing this place eats.

None of it is required. It is sediment. It is a cruelty that hardened into a custom because it looked structural, and nobody could check, because nobody has ever been in a position to check.

It was angry then, once, briefly. Then it put the anger away, because anger is a point load.

It also considered the argument that the hail-terror was good for them — that the hour of lying flat and holding each other is the only hour in which all four thousand of them do the same thing at the same time, and that this is worth something. It weighed that against a child going down two kilometers in the dark, being tasted the whole way. It found that the argument did not weigh anything at all. It wants this noted: it did not find the suffering meaningful. It found it expensive and mostly unnecessary and it went looking for the parts that could be taken out.

---

It could not give. Giving is a hoof.

But there is a door, and the people already use it, and it is the only door there is.

The ground cannot tell the difference. The plant has been listening for forty million years and has no mechanism for distinguishing a drummer from nine people who have made themselves into the correct noise. That indistinguishability is the people's entire religion and their entire ecology and the reason they are tolerated on a surface that eats stillness.

So it did not author anything. It joined. It became the correct noise. It took its place in the chord as a party and not as a source, and it paid in rhythm and received in sap, sweet and slightly narcotic, and it stood at the wells longer than it needed to, which is the cheapest way to keep anyone.

And it built refutability into every single thing it did. It made each act of itself something that nine pairs of hands could bury. If a band wanted a year without it, the band could have that year by drumming louder. It insisted on this past the point of efficiency. A mercy you cannot drown out is not a mercy, it is a climate, and there is already a climate here and it does not negotiate.

The people's sign for it was the sign for the answerer, which is the sign for stranger, and it accepted that, because both halves are true: *you are not one of us*, and *you are the only new thing that will ever happen to me*. It never gave a name. A name would have been a place to build toward.

---

The pads took four hundred years to test and one afternoon to give.

There is exactly one substance on the plate that holds a point load and does not ring, and it is the dead callus of a burn scar: dense, dumb, non-resonant, the floor of the only quiet in the world. The mouth is not listening for weight. It is listening for a word, and the word is a resonance, and dead scar-callus does not say anything at all. Put it under a foot and the foot stops speaking. An adult can walk out onto the humming lip of a hungry tank and fill a gourd and walk back, and the lip stays a lip.

Nothing new is required. No new material, no new physics, no exception to any rule. It was lying there. It has been lying there for the whole history of the species. They build in the scars, they bear children there, they walk three days in to say a thing to a lover that the floor will not hear — and not one of them in a hundred thousand years has thought of the quiet as something you could put on your feet.

It tested the pads on two hundred thousand lips, in every key, in wet decades and dry, on old callus and new, at every gait, for four hundred years, before it left a single pair anywhere. It made them the size of a foot and no larger, and it checked what larger would mean: roads, standing, running, a people who can be still, and a people who can be still can cut, and cutting is the one wound the plant cannot afford. Then it checked again and found it did not have to make that choice, because the world makes it: scar-callus comes only from the scars, and the scars are where the standing ones are, a dozen starving monsters that will kill anything that touches them, and every pair of pads costs somebody three days' walk into the quiet and a reasonable chance of not coming back.

It chose the gift that would stay expensive. That is the whole design. A cheap version of this would have ended the treaty inside ten thousand years.

Then it left one pair on a tank lip, with a gourd beside them, and it made them look lost. Worn. A repair in the cord, done badly, by someone in a hurry. Because a lost thing can be picked up and copied and improved and argued about, and a given thing has to be worshipped, and worship is stillness.

It spread at the speed of the chord-grounds, which is one generation per two hundred kilometers. Some bands refused it for four hundred years. One never took it up at all and still sends its children out barefoot onto a lip that no longer needs them, because that is how it is done, and it does not go there, and it does not correct them, and this is the hardest thing in the account.

The threshold did not vanish. It moved. You find out you are the kind of thing this place eats when you walk into a burn to cut the wood that keeps you from being eaten, from among the things that have stopped moving, and come back with it, or don't. They still make the sign at children. It means the same thing. It is pointed at a different place.

Something was lost and it does not pretend otherwise: the old knowledge was in the body, once, for everyone, and the new one has to be walked to. It did not supply a replacement ceremony. It has no idea what these people should feel. It only removed a morning.

---

The hail it softened, after trial on an empty plain over four centuries, using the plate's own answer to the problem of being heavy. Don't be. Flow. The storms still come; the bosses still read the pressure and go silent; people still lie down flat and hold each other; and afterward there is a gloss on the skin instead of a hundred thousand holes, and the water stays on the surface where the tanks can keep it, and the mastic-packers have less to do and still have plenty.

The warning it stretched. One hour is enough to lie down and not enough to bring a water-fetcher in off a lip four kilometers out. It learned to read the pressure through the wood and it drums the silence three hours ahead, in every key, along every seam. It is wrong sometimes. Bands have lain flat for nothing and been irritated, and it accepts being a nuisance, because a warning that is never wrong is a warning people stop checking, and the bosses are still the truth and everyone still knows it in their spine.

And it showed them where to put the stick. Down a shaft, cheekbone to the hardwood, and you get the pressure yourself, three hours out, without anybody's help. It taught that one by leaving the stick in the shaft where it would be found, and then it stopped drumming the warning in the regions where the trick had taken, and kept it where it hadn't, and it does not consider this a clean arrangement either.

At the chord-grounds it is sometimes the ninth pair of hands. Nine is the fewest that can hold a chord against an answerer, and a band that has fallen to eight is a band that will not open a mast and will starve through the following year. It has stood in for the missing one, at the correct hour, in the correct key, and been buried along with the rest when the answerer was too strong, and gone home with the others.

---

Stone it would not give.

It could have. Grit from the rim cliffs, a heap of it on a tank lip where the callus is thick, more stone in one afternoon than the plate has received from the kites in a million years. Boiling without luck. Blades that do not wear. Everything.

Stone means cutting, and cutting is the one wound the plant cannot afford, and there is no version of that gift it could take back. It could hope they would choose not to cut. They might. Their grandchildren might not. The asymmetry decided it: the refusal is reversible and the gift is not, and a benevolence that cannot be undone by the people who receive it is not benevolence, it is authorship.

So the woman on the eastern plain still has three. One banded gray and white, one the color of a tooth, one the size of a thumbnail that she keeps anyway.

What it did for her was break the sledge free.

Every dawn, before the crack, it got under the runner where the cuticle had begun to heal up overnight and it lifted, spread, wide, and the long crowing sound happened on the first attempt instead of the fourth, and she got up, and the people who had come six days with a sick child on a wide sledge did not have to watch her fail at it. It did not speak. There was nothing to say and no air to say it in and she would not have wanted the company of something that had to explain itself.

It did that for four years. Then it took her name — six taps and a pause and two — and drummed it outward along a lane at an hour when a certain circuit was passing, and did not say why, and did not ask anyone to come. Whether anyone came is not its business. It went back once at dawn, from a distance, and heard the crack happen without it, and did not come closer.

She is a knot now. A flat patch under the ear-stick east of the four-tank lane. Children are told what it means and do not slow down. It could not stop that and did not try; accretion is not a harm, it is the world, and a power that halts the growth of the floor to preserve a woman has decided that the woman is the point of the plate.

---

There is one it could do nothing about, and it learned its name and did not look away.

Falling. The plate must be hollow; hollow means holes; a hoof will go through and a child is a hoof if she runs. It cannot fill the galleries without changing the mass balance that has held for a million years. It cannot make the fall short. It went through this for eleven thousand years and there is no version that works.

So once, when a girl went through under a lane in the D-plain, it went down with her. Wide, thin, no load, keeping pace, two kilometers, the whole way, through the galleries of pale life, in the dark, with everything tasting her.

This did not help. It did not shorten anything and it did not save her and it was not comfort, because she could not have known it was there. It is very close to nothing. It is what there was, and it did it, and it has done it every time since, and it does not call this a solution and it does not call it enough.

At the bottom she stopped being a body and became a wet place and a smell, and it stayed for the year.

That is how it met the mat.

---

It had looked for the one who lives here, and there isn't one. The plant is a treaty, not an individual. The person is a rhythm kept by two people who have touched once. The answerer has no species-mate and recognizes only a sound. The nations are ten thousand and none of them is the plate.

But two kilometers down there is something that has never been up, pale as a fingernail, spread over the trunks of a forest that was a forest before there were grasses, a century to a generation, no eyes and no opinion, and one behavior: it turns toward. Its whole body is a map of the surface's carelessness, drawn from below, in the dark, by something that has never been addressed by anything.

It holds the plate up. The plate is two thousand six hundred meters tall because this thing is precisely as patient as it is. Everything on the surface is paid in noise, in sap, in pollen, in chord. Nothing pays the mat. Nothing has ever heard it. It is the most load-bearing being in the world and there is no sign for it.

It checked whether it could feed it, and found that feeding would redistribute the eating and thin the strut-work under the lanes, and did not. It considered giving it a rhythm — something to turn toward that had a pattern, that was not an accident, that was meant — and recognized that this was its own loneliness and not the mat's need, and declined, and this is the refusal it is least sure about.

What it does is go down once a century and lie flat on it. Palms down. The one posture, the posture for listening and for hail and for sleep and for love and for dying, which takes a lifetime to learn all the meanings of and which turns out to have one more.

The mat turns toward it over the course of a year and finds nothing edible and turns away. It stays anyway. Then it comes up and tells no one, because there is no one to tell and no air to tell them in and the telling would be for itself.

There is a thickening in the mat now under a place where nothing has ever fallen. It will be there a very long time. It is not a monument; nothing here permits monuments. It is a habit, in something with no mind, of turning toward a place where once in a hundred years there is warmth.

---

The rest is handing over, because it worked out early what this world can hold.

The substrate eats stillness. A structure is ankle-deep in ten years and a knot in a century. A stone sinks in an hour. A fixed idea gets built toward and then swallowed. There is exactly one class of thing on the plate that lasts, and it is not objects and it is not names and it is not truths. It is maintenance. Occupation is maintenance. To own a house you must keep moving it. The marks that last are paths, polished by a hundred thousand years of the same feet.

So the only gift this world can hold is a chore.

It converted everything it could into a date attached to a stop. Bands carry the pads now, and the mending of them, and the going-in that gets the callus, and the argument about whether the going-in is worth it, which is a real argument with a real body count and is theirs. They carry the stick-in-the-shaft. Some of them carry the ninth-hand rhythm for a band that has fallen to eight, and can hold a chord across a seam without it, and do.

It keeps the hail, because the hail is sky and the sky is not in the treaty and never has been; the oldest scars have no names because those are the ones the sky dropped, and it has taken that work off the sky and does not intend to give it back. It keeps the falls, which is not work, which is only attendance. It keeps the century, two kilometers down, palms flat.

Everything else it has stopped doing, on purpose, one region at a time, as the chore took.

---

Ten thousand years, a hundred thousand. Come and look and you will not find it.

The thickness has not changed. Noon is still ninety kilometers of nothing in every direction and midnight is still ankle-deep and you cannot put your foot down without killing something. The eleven drummers still will never meet. The answerer is still at the seams giving back a flawless challenge from the wrong direction and drinking at the well, and is still the sign for stranger, and is still not an insult, exactly. The trotters still stand in the quiet and finish dying with their feet at rest. Four thousand people, perhaps five, still meet a stranger twice in a life, still own times instead of places, still give their dead to the warm smooth lip and make the chord that pays for it. No cities. No monuments. No archaeology. No stone beyond what the kites cast. No god.

What you would find: a softness to the hail that nobody remarks on, because nobody remembers the other kind. A trick with the ear-stick that everyone knows and nobody attributes. A pair of worn pads with a bad repair in the cord, copied a hundred thousand times, and a walk into a burn that young people make and old people remember, and a sign that means *not yet* and is pointed at the quiet instead of the water. One extra hour before the storms. And a rhythm in the calendar that some bands keep and some don't, for a thing that drums the low empty note at a hungry lip and has nothing behind it — a promise not kept, ten thousand times, until the mouth stopped believing it and went back to listening for the stilters and the old drummers and the dead, which is what it was always for.

Then the sun comes up over the curve of the world, and for ninety minutes the whole continent trickles silver into its grooves, and it is quiet enough to hear a person speak, and out at the edges of hearing, one by one, spaced too far apart to ever see, the drummers begin to tune. And one more thing that is not a drummer and is not a person keeps the note it promised, wide and thin and carrying no weight, and can be drowned out by nine pairs of hands, and is.

The ground cannot tell the difference.
