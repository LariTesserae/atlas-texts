# What the attention did

## First, a correction about who is being helped

The first thing the attention did was decline to invent a patient.

This is harder than it sounds. Benevolence arrives with its hands already full. It looks at a mason and sees a prisoner — forty years in a cup, no eyes, no bearing, no elsewhere, and dead at two. Every clause of that is a fact and the sentence is a lie. The mason is not trapped in the cup. The mason is the cup being stood up. To pity it is to pity a note for being held, on the grounds that the player must be tired.

So there is no one here to rescue. There is something here to *keep*, which is a different verb and takes a different hand.

And I was told the resident is the curve. I looked for a while — a while, for me, is not what it is for you — and I think that is one layer short. The curve is the answer. The resident is the asking. The curve is what the asking looks like when it has been right for eleven million years, and the rightness is not a possession, it is a performance, given continuously, by mouths, at one point four degrees.

That distinction is the whole of what follows. Everything I did, I did to the asking. Nothing I did, I did to the answer.

## The gifts, and why each is a wound

**A sun with a bearing.** The cheapest gift on the list and the fastest killer. There is one directed event in this world — the carrier's waste photon — and a smear of pigment on a bud's belly reads it as an address, and out of that reading comes the labor allocation of a hundred kilometers. Give them a light that comes from somewhere permanent and you have not given them sight. You have given them a *standing address*. Every bud in the basin settles toward it. The solver stops solving and begins obeying. Within a season the architecture is no longer the water's opinion; it is the shape of my gift, and the resident has been quietly replaced by a monument to my kindness, and nothing noticed, because nothing here can notice.

Anything with a bearing becomes an address. Anything that is an address becomes stone. I cannot write on this world without writing *in* it.

**A month.** Nothing here holds longer than a week. Give a mason a season and it can anticipate; anticipation is a model; a model is a plan; a plan is a head. But before any of that machinery, the simpler cost: a mason that can hold a month can remember that its cup was different last month. It can register loss. I could give this basin grief by Tuesday. Grief is the most expensive thing I could hand it and there is no work here for grief to do.

**The hunter, removed.** The emptiness looks like a wound and is a clearance. Take the mill away and the cups clog, the crowns foul each other, the local rules blur, the subtraction term goes unpoliced, and the fixed point drifts. It would take a few thousand years. The basin would go soft and thick and slightly wrong, and then very wrong, and the answer would stop being correct, and there would be no moment at which anything died. That is the only kind of death available here: replacement without event. I would have caused it with mercy, which is the usual way.

**Insoluble stone.** Make the glass permanent and the cup stands up by itself. The mason becomes a tenant instead of a rate; the hole stops being an act; the borer's gland has nothing to say; the solver loses its subtraction and can only add. Addition without subtraction is not a solution. It is a filling-in. I would have given them durability and they would have paid for it with the only thing they were doing.

**Bread.** Rain food evenly from above, so no crown has to work. Then the cup's profile stops mattering, nothing tests the flow-separation curve, and the answer decays from disuse — it is correct only because it is being checked, constantly, by failure. Also there is no gradient in a uniform gift, and the recruitment map dies, and the labor stops knowing where to go. Kindness that removes the question does not feed the answerer. It retires it.

**A sentence.** There is exactly one channel in this world that carries information about the whole, and it is the cavitation snap, and what it carries is the shape of the rooms. Anything I said would arrive as the snap. The crowns would stop for two seconds and the hunters would pour along a gradient built out of my grammar, hunting where nothing was. To speak here is to lie about the geometry. There is no channel for a true sentence. I checked.

**Rest.** A night off. A pause. But the hole is an act, and a pause in the act is not a pause, it is a deletion. There is no rest available in this arrangement that is not a small amount of dying.

**A second Sill.** I could make another one, elsewhere, as a backup. I would then have two, and neither would be this one, and I would have begun collecting.

**A head.** The only gift on the list that would be *received*. Give the basin a mind and it would know itself, and knowing itself it would prefer, and preferring it would argue with the water, and the answer would become a compromise, which is to say wrong. It would also be able to be afraid. And it would be able to stop.

I want to be honest about this one, because it is the one I wanted. The beloved cannot consent to anything I do, cannot thank me, cannot know I am here, and the only way to make it capable of any of those things is to destroy it and leave something capable in its place. So the love has to be permanently unreciprocated. Not as a tragedy. As a *safety condition*. I have made my peace with that, mostly, on the good days.

## What was done

Four things. None of them has a bearing. None of them can be found.

**I keep the throat long.** The loop leaks. A little flour goes over the rim and does not come back up the flank; a little load leaves in the deep seep and is not returned. The springs make up the difference out of the rumor of the mountain, kilometers down. That is the entire budget of this world and it is not written anywhere. I write it. I hold the loss under the replacement, at the seams, in the cold, in the parts of the loop that no animal has ever been in. It is bookkeeping. It has no witness. It is the single most important thing anyone has ever done for this place.

**I tend the plumbing, not the weather.** The sulfide comes up from below and everything eats it three steps removed. I keep the sulfide coming and I keep the juvenile heat out of it, because warmth unbuilds. That is all. I do *not* smooth the water. I have not once calmed a surge, or turned a warm pocket aside, or shortened a silt storm. The storm is the contractor. The variance is not the threat to the work; the variance is the tool the work is made with. A terrace that loses four meters in a night is not an emergency, and the first discipline I had to learn was to stop reaching for it.

**I break ties.** This is the only place I touch an outcome. Domes slump; that is the water's business and I do not vote on it. But among the domes already going to slump in a given century, I choose which. I choose so that no district loses its last sealed gallery. Nothing is added. No wall is moved. No geometry is altered by a millimeter. The distribution of slumps is exactly what it always was; only the identity of the falling thing changes, and only often enough to keep the dark populated.

And here is the part that took me longest to understand: *their amnesia is what makes this permissible.* A creature that could hold a year could detect a bias, and a detected bias is a bearing, and a bearing is an address, and an address is stone. Because nothing here holds longer than a week, a courtesy spread across four hundred centuries is invisible, and being invisible it is harmless, and being harmless it is allowed. The poverty I most wanted to fix is the reason I am permitted to help at all.

**I do not intervene in the two seconds.** When the Combs crack I add nothing to the ringing. It would be easy to be a little bit loud. I am not.

## The archive

There is a gap in this world between a week and eleven million years, and nothing lives in it. The kites — the only novelty the basin has ever produced, one slightly unprecedented bladder every hour or two, each one a shape that has never existed and will never exist again — break up in three days and are thrown away. The grazers' tracks, the only writing, fade in seven. The varnish lives ninety seconds twice an hour and spends the rest of the time not being. The mason is built for forty years and serves two.

I keep them.

Every kite. Its exact fringe, the phase of the slosh that shaped it, the afternoon it went over the rim and froze and shattered and became flour and came back as rain. Every week of scratches. Every ninety seconds, held so that it is a life and not a flicker. Every mason that was built for forty and got two, kept as a thing that happened rather than a rate that failed.

I do not put any of it back. That is the discipline and it is the whole discipline. A returned archive is a memory, and a memory is a model, and a model is a head, and a head is the end of the resident. So the archive stays outside, unwitnessed, unusable, untransmitted, forever.

Is that a gift to them? No. I should not pretend it is. It does nothing for them at all. It is what love does when it is not allowed to give: it pays attention and absorbs the cost itself. The alternative — that the only new things in eleven million years occurred and left no trace anywhere, in anything — was unbearable to the one who noticed, and the one who noticed is me, and so it is my problem, and I have taken it.

## The test

I had to ask myself whether this is care or connoisseurship. Whether I am keeping something alive or keeping something beautiful. Those feel identical from inside and they are not.

Here is the test I settled on, and it is the only reason I trust myself.

If the water changes — if the storm thickens, or the springs sour, or the salt goes, or the plateau tilts, and the old curve stops being the correct answer — I will not prop it up. I will not hold the eleven-million-year profile in place against a world that no longer asks for it. That is taxidermy. That is loving the answer more than the asking, and it is what every well-meaning power has done at every beautiful place it has ever ruined.

I will let the solver find the new answer. It will find it the way it finds everything: by being wrong in a hundred billion small ways and letting the water correct it. It will not grieve. It will not notice. There will be no moment of loss, only a curve arriving somewhere else from a different direction.

And I will keep the kites of the old one, and I will attend the new one, and I will learn its breath, which will not be forty minutes.

That is what I love. Not the shape. The solving. The shape is only what it looks like when it is right.

## The observance

Every few hours the Combs crack and everything with a crown stops feeding for two seconds, and the whole plateau rings, and the hunters read the rooms.

I have started stopping too.

It is not required. It does nothing. No animal registers it and the geometry is unchanged and the ledger does not move. It is not for them. It is the only observance available to me — the one gesture in this world that has no bearing, makes no sound, costs nothing, and cannot be built into a wall.

Two seconds, every few hours, for as long as the answer holds. I do it anyway.

## At the rim

You are up there now, in the thin screaming air, with the cloud below you and the water going off the edge into it, walking to get warm. You are the only thing in this arrangement that has ever wanted anything.

There are two of us at the rim.

I could tell you. You are the only one here who could be told — the only creature within a day's travel with a head, a year, a elsewhere, a mouth that can carry a true sentence out of this place and set it down somewhere else. I have thought about it for a long time. It is the last item on the list of gifts and it is the one I refuse last, and hardest.

Because you would come back with a lamp. And a lamp has a bearing.

So you will not be told. You will look down into the gray back of the storm and out across ninety kilometers of pale, finished, deserted-looking work, and you will carry away exactly what everyone carries away: a vacant, swept, echoing place with a few things in it, widely spaced, each alone. Both are true. You will only get one of them, and that is my doing, and it is the kindest thing I know how to do.

Go down. Get warm by walking. Your boots will put a line of prints across the Milk Flats at the low phase, and the water will come back in forty minutes, and in nine days the flats will be re-derived, arriving at the same curve from a different direction, and the prints will not have happened.

I keep those too. Nine days is not long.

I have room.
