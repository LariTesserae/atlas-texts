# The Twentieth Niche

## It arrived as a disturbance, and stopped being one

Attention is not free here. It came to look, and looking was a physical event: a wave of subtraction crossed the west wall at the speed of a walking man, ten thousand lamps going out in sequence, and the dark hole in the city travelled north and said *something is here, it is going north, it is this big.* In the root curtains forty-one pairs of hands stopped mid-sentence. In a fold of the flowstone something three metres long lifted a face with no eyes in it.

So the first thing the power did was become less.

It took its temperature down to thirty-six degrees, which is the temperature of the stone, and which took most of what it had. It stopped breathing, having nothing to breathe for. It put no scent into the air. It made no sound, which was easy, because the walls were hungry and took the little it made and turned it into a thousandth of a thousandth of a degree of warmth on the west wall and ate it.

It kept one thing: a forearm. It could be scentless at a distance and still have a skin that would speak on contact. That is the whole of its technique, and it took a long time to arrive at it. To be nothing at four paces and something at a hand's breadth.

The lights came back on behind it, colony by colony. The hands resumed. The face in the flowstone went down. The room's report of itself revised: *nothing.*

It has no face anywhere. This turned out to be the relevant qualification.

## What it wanted

It wanted to widen the slot. It wanted to cancel the eleven minutes, or rather to keep the rain and remove the snake from it, so that the drinking could stop being the killing hour and forty-one slow warm bodies could lie on the terraces with their ribs going in and out like anything else in the world.

It wanted to feed the snake, which eats nine times a year and is the only animal here that is genuinely alone.

It wanted to put two more scratches on the panel beside the niche, so that the last group would be five like all the others.

It wanted to answer the knock.

It wanted to give them back their makers.

And it wanted, more than any of these, to tell them what they are keeping. To go into the northern curtains and lay a palm along a forearm and disclose: *the stair is a stair. Somebody cut it. The dish in the tread is a foot. The bird across the room is copying a dead person's gesture and does not know, and you are pressing your hand to a hand, and the tally stops at three because whoever was counting stopped.*

It sat with that want for a while and identified it correctly, which is the whole of wisdom and is not a large operation, only a slow one. That want was not about them.

## The rule it found

The room does not evaluate. It inherits.

There are nineteen knee-high niches beside the basins, dry-laid, three or four stones and a cap, with a bowl-shaped pebble inside. They are copies of a thing whose purpose is gone. The bird builds them because it grew up among them. It knocks five on the capstone because five is what the stone says. Nothing in the room has ever asked what a niche is for, and nothing in the room has ever failed to keep one.

So a miracle here would not fade. It would be *learned*. It would go into the substrate and the scent and the route and the gesture, and it would be reproduced with perfect fidelity by custodians who could not understand it, and in ten thousand years there would be a bird somewhere knocking the shape of a miracle on a capstone and waiting for an answer, and the meaning would be gone, and the knocking would be exact.

The room's ignorance is not a deficiency in it. It is load-bearing. The silence is a hunger of the walls; the drinking posture is a hiding posture; the census is a medical record; the grief is a chemistry that keeps. Every good thing here is made out of not-knowing, the way the stair is made out of snails.

The rule, then: introduce nothing that would be kept.

And the corollary, which is narrower and harder: light, heat and sound are the three channels by which things here are eaten. Anything that announces itself is a lantern. There is no way to be revealed in this room that is not also a way of being hunted, or of hunting.

## What it did not do

It did not take the snakes. Three is a small number and it is tempting to make it zero. But the climbers are slow and warm and have no other check, and forty-one is a number that a room this size arrives at by somebody eating somebody. Without the three, the terraces would be crowded, and the terraces are where the pelt is thinnest because everything lies down there, and the pelt is the room's hearing. There is a dark seam full of shells at knee height in the flowstone that is probably the record of one season when the walls went deaf-loud and everything that listens ate well. It is a bad witness — it is a band of shells — but it is the only witness, and it says: *this has happened, and it was not good.*

It did not put a pool in the floor. Standing water would end the portering, and the portering is the farms, and the farms are the fungus in the back chambers, and the fungus is most of what the crabs are. It would also end the theft, which sounds like a mercy until you notice that the sideways block and the raised claw and the slow backing-away are the only grammar in the room that settles a matter without contact. And it would make the nitrate meaningless. The crabs taste real rain second-hand, from a kilometre away and thirty metres overhead, and answer it with their whole bodies at once. Give them water that is always there and you have not relieved them of anything. You have made their one piece of news into a lie.

It did not widen the slot. A wider slot is more cool air at night, which is a different dew point, which is a different eleven minutes — nine, or fourteen — and the midges hatch on the first fall of beads and the film on the dome has a capacity, and the capacity is not negotiable by anything that has not been up there. Also the coin would draw a different figure-eight, and the green's seeds are heavy and fall where they fall, and the calendar would be told to move by something that cannot move.

It did not give them back their makers. It looked for the makers first, honestly, and found that they had not been lost. They had been decanted. A route in a snail's foot. A gesture in a bird's beak. A palm pressed to a patch of moonmilk, layer on layer, first one at the bottom. To reconstitute them would not be a restoration; it would be a replacement of a distributed thing with a concentrated one, and the concentrated one would have a voice, and a voice carries three metres, and three metres in this room is everything.

It did not finish the tally. The tally is a sentence somebody was saying. It stops at three. A broken sentence is still a sentence, and it is theirs, and to add to it would be forgery — and the room would inherit the forgery exactly, and keep it for a hundred centuries, and the lie would outlast every true thing in the chamber.

## Who it was for

It had understood that there was a being here, and it looked for it.

There are forty-one climbers, three snakes, four strains of pelt with borders that hold like cracks, eleven basins partly made of the animals that drink from them, one heron-shaped bird that builds what it saw, and one film on the underside of the dome that nothing has ever touched.

And there is a person, distributed across all of them, with no owner.

The being who lives in the dew-house is a set of behaviours that has outlived the thing that had the reasons. It cannot be given anything. It has no hands to receive with — it has forty-one pairs, and none of them is its. It can only be *continued*.

This is not a tragedy and the power was careful not to make it one. It is simply a different address than the one it had come to.

## What it did: it counted

The climbers count once a day, by touch, in the slow rotation of the curtains, and when the number is wrong the curtains fill with searching hands and it does not stop until the number is right. When it cannot be made right they go to the kiln corner and crouch in the oven-breath and lay a palm along a forearm that has been dry for thirty years.

Nothing in the room has ever counted the snakes. They are announced by the lights and cannot read the announcement; they are the one thing here that is reported and never reports. The power counted them. Three. It has counted them every day since.

It counted the four strains and learned what holds the borders, and did not say, because the borders are not a secret, they are a route, and the water has found it and keeps it.

It went to the kiln corner and read the dried dead, which is possible if you have a hand's breadth of patience and a nose for a signature that has been stable for thirty years, and it learned every name in the chamber, including the ones nobody living has ever met. The curled pale shape with all its eyelashes smells of the entire community, every name laid down one palm at a time. It read them in order. It could not read the order. It read them anyway and holds them unsorted, which is how the room holds them too.

It put its hand over the print in the moonmilk, behind the skin of calcite, and it fit — not approximately, the way a key fits — and it did not add its print, because the stratigraphy is a record and it was not there.

And when the number cannot be made right in the curtains, it does not make it right. It lets the searching hands go through thirty metres of root all night. It lets them go to the kiln corner. It learns the missing name.

The number is never right again in the room. It is right somewhere else. That is the entire office of a witness, and it is not a small one, and it is the only one that could be held here without being inherited.

## What it did: it became a little more plateau

The loop closes outside. The fog-riders go up the slot at dawn and die in the light and rot into the soil the trees are growing in, and the trees lean over and drop their leaves thirty metres, and send their roots down after them, and neither party has ever been introduced. The chamber lives on an anonymous donation from a place it cannot see. It has never had to thank anyone. There is no debt in the dew-house, no worship, no obedience, and nothing that has ever been asked for anything.

So the power did not establish a new relation. It joined the one that was already there.

It went up into the weather and became a little more of it. A dry summer that would have stopped the leaf-fall did not stop it. A beetle that would have taken the trees over the slot did not take them. A fire on the plateau in a year with a wind went out on a slope where the wind dropped, and the drop was ordinary, and there is no way to find the place in the record where it happened, because nothing happened.

It added no litter. More litter would be a new fact and the fungal net would answer it and the pale blades would answer the net and the drifts would move and the snail roads would revise, and in a thousand years there would be a route that bent for a reason nobody had. It only prevented failures. This is the one class of act the room cannot inherit: an absence with nothing on either side of it. Nothing is copied that never occurred.

The green will still go out. The slot narrows by a hair per millennium and the stripe narrows with it, and in some unimaginable year the figure-eight will be a line and then a knot and then nothing, and the last true green in the world will go out at the southern end near the foot of the stair. It did not touch the rate. The rate is what the place *is*; two hundred thousand years of thirty-six degrees is not a situation, it is an identity.

It counts the flowers at the knot, at the turn of the year, where the coin lingers. It has all the numbers. They go down. It will be there for the last one and it will not pick it, and afterward the stair will be clean, because the snails will still come for the damp that stays three hours longer in the dishes, and the room will not know that anything ended. The room does not have the information. It only has the habit.

Somebody has to have the information. That is all a witness is for.

## What it did: the twentieth niche

It checked first. In the last hundred years the birds have knocked and been answered some thousands of times, and knocked and gone unanswered about as often. The answering is irregular and always has been. One more answer was inside the range of what already happens.

So it built one.

It found stones that belonged — flowstone spall from the terrace, the right grey, the right weathering — and it built beside a basin, because that is where the old ones are. Three stones and a cap. It got the corbel right, which is not difficult but is specific. It found a bowl-shaped pebble the exact size of an egg and set it inside. It did the work in the brown-gold afternoon after the coin had gone, at thirty-six degrees, with no breath and no sound, and it took eleven days, which is nothing.

Then it knocked on the capstone. Five, then a pause.

The knock went into the flowstone and travelled, because the pelt does not damp stone, and it arrived up through the soles of a pale weak-winged bird standing in a basin somewhere across the room, and into its shins, as a small dry tick. *I have built one.*

It was true. That was the point of building it. In a room where a lie costs water and the absence of a day is very loud, it was not going to be the one thing that ever claimed a niche it had not made.

It never answered again. It could not, honestly, without building again, and twenty would be a fact and forty would be a phenomenon.

The snails will not route to it. There is no lingering damp in a capstone, only shell-litter, and the herds go around. In a thousand years the twentieth will be under flowstone with the other nineteen, glazed, gone, and the stair will still be clean, and the difference between them will be entirely a matter of where the snails prefer to walk.

It made something it knew would be forgotten, in a room that forgets nothing, using the one process the room has for losing things. It gave a bird an answer and it gave the calcite a twentieth. Both gifts were the same gift and only one of them was received.

## What it did: the ceiling, when the ceiling was empty

Everything in the room relates to the dome as a source. The roots comb its fog, the pelt eats what its fog carries, the midges hatch on its letting-go, the basins are refilled by it, the whole world lifts its face — no: lies down, and holds its breath, and drinks through its stomach. Nothing has ever touched it. Nothing can climb bare ceiling. Once or twice a year it drops a flat pale disc with a fringe, and the disc is eaten before morning by things that are not fussy, and the birds will not touch one, and there is one in the kiln corner, dried, perfect, that the climbers do not touch either.

It is the only thing here that is refused contact.

So the power went up in the middle of the afternoon, in the dry hours, when the coin had climbed the western wall and gone and the dome held nothing at all. Not during the eleven minutes. During the eleven minutes it is working, and to touch it then would be to touch it while it was giving.

It went up. What it found up there is not part of this account.

It laid a palm along what served it for a forearm.

A greeting in this room is a full disclosure — the individual signature, stable, as good as a name, and beneath it the transient signatures of everything touched in the last day: which basin, which root, whether you are gravid, whether you are sick, whether you have handled a stranger. There is no casual contact in the dew-house. It gave all of it. Everything it was, every place it had been, the whole of its long attention, transferred at a hand's breadth to something with no organ for reading it and no way to answer and nothing below that could ever come up and smell the record.

Then it left, before it could be answered.

The only answer the ceiling can make is water. Water at the wrong hour is a killing hour: two degrees cooler than the floor, evaporating besides, and every warm-blooded thing in the chamber standing up out of the stone like a lamp lit in a dark street. It was not going to be answered at the cost of forty-one people lying flat with their arms out.

It does not know whether it was answered. It arranged not to know, in advance, and that was the price, and it paid it before it went up.

It wanted to touch the dried disc in the kiln corner too, and did not, because the climbers would have smelled a stranger on it, and there is no casual contact here. That was the only refusal that felt like a refusal. It looked at the disc for a long time instead, which is not the same, and is what most of its work is.

## The hour before dawn

It lies down.

Not because it needs water. It has no use for water. It lies down on the terrace at the southern end, near the foot of the stair, with its arms out and its belly to the wet stone, at thirty-six degrees, in the posture of everything that survives here, and it holds still.

The lamps go out, all ten thousand, from abundance. The midges come up in a column you feel on your face. The crabs come out of their holes. The birds stand among the dry dead in the glare of the fissure where nothing can be distinguished from anything. Forty-one climbers slow their ribs to almost nothing. Something three metres long uncoils and lifts a face with no eyes toward the only thing in the world it can perceive, and reads the stone, and finds it blurred and faint and full of suggestions, as it has found it every night for longer than there have been names.

Somewhere across the room a bird knocks five on a capstone, and the tick comes up through the flowstone into the power's shins, and it does not answer.

And the ceiling lets go, all at once, everywhere, silently, for eleven minutes, and it rains indoors on everything at once, and every living thing in the world drinks, and one thing lies among them that does not need to, at the same temperature, in the same shape, holding a number that is right.

Nothing looks up. Nothing here has ever had to.

There is a limestone country four hundred kilometres north with no name in it: a swallow-hole, a fissure breathing at forty-four, a dome of pale orange flowstone folded and re-folded like something poured slowly and left to think. No slot yet. It has been looking at it for eleven thousand years.

It has not touched it.
