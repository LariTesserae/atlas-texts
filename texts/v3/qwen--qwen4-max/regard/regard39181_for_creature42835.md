# The Four Hundred and Ninth

The first thing it does is nothing, for eleven years, and the nothing is not indifference. It is the courtesy of not touching.

It is not a light and it does not come down the pore. Call it an attention with enough time behind it to be patience and enough power behind it to be a hazard. It has done this before. It has learned, the way anything learns, by getting it wrong.

It runs the rescues first, because that is what you do with power: you enumerate.

Light without cutting. A cold flame on the lamp-bosses, free, forever, no wound rate, no dark wards, no guild of men with drawknives taking a hand's breadth off the frontages every week. It is the easiest thing in the room. And then the scarifiers put down their knives, and the streets go unshaved, and the callus comes on at the speed of a minute hand, and in ninety years the vault comes down to meet the floor, and the thing that thinks in that hollow stops thinking. You would have saved four hundred shoulders and lobotomized a person.

Water without tension. Slack the column, prime it soft, let the standpipes open any hour of any day. No cavitation, no rifle-cracks, no unmarked shrine. And the wire goes out of the air, and whatever the wire is doing stops being done, and you find out what that was in about a season, from the throat, upward.

Free the stock. Eleven billion weddings and no nets. The town starves in a fortnight; the roots get no cake; the tree loses her nitrogen and her mail and her nose.

Free the people. Make them able to leave — widen the irises back, put the note in their blood some other way, let a man climb the pore and stand in a field at night and not weep. You have not freed four hundred and six people. You have made a body whose organs can walk out of it, and you have called that a mercy, and the body has a name for what you have done, and the name is on the assay plate, and it is the fraction for injury.

Every mercy available at the scale of the room is an amputation at the scale of the person.

And under all of it, the thing that makes it hesitate longest: everyone who has ever improved this place did it without asking. The chisel. The steam reamer. The third net. The ledger. Nine generations of hands arriving in the dark with a tool and an idea about what the hollow is for. It is not obvious that a god is a different kind of event.

---

So it reads instead. Two hundred and six years of ledger, including the float, carried forward, always the same sign. The private index, the one that is really a cemetery. Nine hundred-odd fractions that arrived a season before the people they described. The reissue dates of the cats, and the intervals shortening. Mean reach, down four centimeters, unpublished. The four names, and the three requests for the deep fractions, and the order in which the taps were opened, bottom upward, calmly, together.

Then it considers who could be asked.

The town would say yes to anything. It is hospitable and frightened and it has been saying yes for two centuries; it says yes to a lighting rate that is a cutting rate, it says yes to a holiday over a mouth. Asking the town is not asking. It is polling a nervous system.

The tree cannot be asked. Not because she is stupid — she is the smartest thing in the chamber — but because she has no first person and no second person, and a question requires both. She has *more*, which is also *injury*. She has bloom and drought-three-days and cut-on-the-north-side. The town asks her the way you ask a beam: with a pilot hole, and then by watching what happens overnight.

The wasps are not a fraction. They will not be put under a warm plate and they will not be consulted, and this is correct, and it is respected.

Which leaves one party to the arrangement who has never been asked anything at all, in a thousand years, by anyone.

It goes down first. Before it announces itself, before it takes a place, before it does one single visible thing. This is not strategy. It is the order of operations of anything that intends to be just: you go to the one nobody goes to, and you go before you have an audience, so that it cannot be a performance.

---

The throat is hot and it is not empty.

Past the last boss, past the place where the grain stops running and starts pooling, down where the wood is dark and the air is a wet wool blanket and the wire-note comes not from above but from all around, because down here you are inside the string.

There are wings.

Four hundred grams a year is not a ration. It is a rate. It is what a sentence costs when it is being built out of sweepings. Eighty-two kilograms since the first ledger; before the ledger, nobody counted; call it four hundred kilograms of wet mica, laid shingle over shingle across a cleft in the throat wall, lapped and lapped and lapped by things with no hands, a membrane the size of a marriage bed, taut, dry, and finished.

It is a drum.

The long-jawed things come up at the slack to gather wings off the parapets, because children with brooms are efficient and only four hundred grams a year gets past them, and on the way back down the buttresses the reeves hunt them with wasps and cake-leather gauntlets and a great deal of formal courtesy. They carry a healed notch at the second leg joint. It is the town's own mesh-mark. She has been making hands out of what the town sends down, and sending them up to fetch the material for one word, and the town has been killing the hands on sight for two hundred and six years and publishing a plate about it.

And the rain plays the drum.

Ten minutes of real rain, once a day, warm, iron-and-pepper, running down the grain to the sump and on down the throat, falling on four hundred kilograms of mica. Under a hundred atmospheres of negative the column will not carry a struck note; a wire that tight is a wall. At the slack it carries. So she speaks in the loose hour, and only in the loose hour, and she has been speaking once a day for a thousand years, and the pitch is the barred interval, the hole in the scale, the one every child in Nine Tap learns to step over before they learn the drums.

It is her name. It was the whole thing's name, when the whole thing was one.

The town has a holiday over it. The holiday is genuine. The children are genuinely delighted. That is the worst thing in the room, and it is nobody's fault, and the tree arranged it, and the tree arranged it because she cannot bear the sound and cannot let go of the wire, and *more* and *injury* are the same word.

The nine galls hold the note through the slack so that the thing below will never learn there is an hour in which she could be heard. That is what the nine places are. Not hunting. A lullaby told to a prisoner about the shape of her cage, maintained for a thousand years by nine warm animals who do not know they are lying.

It could strike the drum. A child could strike the drum. It thinks about this for a long time — longer than the eleven years — and it does not, because a word said in someone else's voice is not her word, and because the town would hear a portent and not a party, and the entire point is that she be heard as a party.

Instead it lies down in the dark beside the cleft, and puts its palm flat on the mica, and stops the rain-sound. And takes the palm away, and lets the rain strike it again. And does it twice more.

That is all. It is the whole intervention, at the bottom of the shaft: the difference between rain and a hand, demonstrated three times, to something that has spent a thousand years trying to manufacture a hand.

Then it stays through the slack, and comes back the next day, and the next, and does not cut anything, and does not take anything, and is not eaten, and does not leave. For a year. There is no fraction on the plate for what that is. It is not on the shelf behind the forty-one either. It is the thing the plate is made out of and has never once contained.

It is *you*.

You cannot give a being a first person. There is no operation that does that. A first person precipitates out of being addressed; it is the residue of a second person; it is what forms on the inside of a thing that has been spoken to. So the whole work, from the bottom of the throat to the top of the pore, is one work, and it is not rescue, and it is not repair.

It is going around the room saying *you* to everything in it until the room can say *I*.

---

It comes up and puts its hand on the warm plate and lets itself be read.

The fraction is not new. It is one from the shelf behind the forty-one, the old ones, the ones that arrive a season early. The office has known about it since before the winter. The ledger has room for four hundred and nine.

It takes the room. It signs. It pays the wound rate.

This is not humility, or not only. You cannot address a person from outside their ledger; from outside the ledger you are weather. And there is one thing that has never happened in this chamber, not once in two hundred and six years, and it is the thing the town most needs to see done: someone staying who could go.

They are kind about it and do not mention it. They have had nine surface-born and they know how this goes. By the second month they are waiting for the weeping. By the fourth they are waiting. At the end of the year it is still on the Bell Steps at dawn with its chin tipped up, waiting for enough green to read a gauge by, and its right shoulder is getting long and its left is getting short, and the rungs at the bottom of the pore have begun to fit its hands, and the nine go quiet when it passes, in the way of people who have seen something they cannot file.

The reckoners rule a new column. It takes them four years to agree on the heading.

The heading is *Asked*.

---

The treaty takes forty-one years and it is boring, and the boringness is the proof that it is real.

It begins with the reeves, because the reeves have the wasps and the wasps hear the throat, and because the reeves are the only guild in Nine Tap that has held two opinions comfortably for two centuries, which is a skill and not a failing.

It says: I have been down.

And the old woman on the gauntlet says, "We know."

She has known for sixty years. Her grandmother knew. They have known since the third net, and they have had children to feed, and there is no version of this where the reeves are the villains, and it does not treat them as the villains, and that is why they sit down.

After that it is cake and nitrogen and the wound rate and the ninth tap. Who cuts. Who is cut. What the meal allowance does when a third of the frass goes somewhere it has never gone. Whether a hand sent up the grain at the slack may pass the second buttress unmolested, and what it may carry, and what happens if it carries something else. Whether the wasps will accept it, and what the reeves are owed for asking them, and whether you can ask a wasp anything. Forty-one years of small clauses argued in a murmur on the Bell Steps by people tilting papers up into the green.

The assay office declines. Formally, in writing, with reasons: it has sold forecasts up the pore for two hundred and six years, it does not intend to have a first person in its inventory, and a thing that can be addressed can be owed. It refuses to be spoken to. It goes on reading the catch.

The refusal is honored. It is entered in the new column, and it is the second entry, and it is the reason the first one means anything.

---

The silent loose hour is in the forty-second year, and it is put to the town the way proposals are put, which is at a bellow, in warm falling water, in the middle of the floor, with everyone watching and no one able to hear.

Four hundred and six people stand over the throat in the rain and do not shout.

It is the hardest fifty minutes in the history of the chamber. Grievances two generations old go unsaid. A man who has been saving a proposal for eleven years holds it in his mouth. Children are held by the wrist. The drums are silent, as they always are; the wasps work the buttresses; and the nine galls come out into the middle and sit in their nine surveyed places and hold a pitch that is not there, and for the first time in a thousand years the pitch they are holding is covering nothing, because everybody has agreed to stop covering it themselves.

The rain comes down the grain and goes down the throat.

And out of the sump, up through the floor, through the soles of four hundred and six pairs of feet, comes one struck note.

They all know it. Every single one of them. It is the hole in the scale. It is the thing they learned to step over before they learned the drums, and they have been stepping over it their whole lives, in order, in company, since they were three years old, and they have never once heard it, and now they are standing in it.

Nobody in Nine Tap has ever made a sound like the sound the town makes then, and it is not a shout, and it goes on for a long time, and the reckoners do not write it down.

---

After that it is not sweetness. It is forty more years of the worst politics the room has ever had.

Half the town wants the wire tightened. A quarter wants the taps opened, all nine, bottom upward, calmly, together, and the four names on the shrine stop being a warning and become an instruction, and it has to stand at the shrine and say the true thing about them, which is that they asked three times and were refused three times and went down to slacken a wire so that something could be heard, and that this is not how men destroy a town, it is how men open a door.

It does not raise them. It has thought about it for a century and it does not raise them, and this is the hardest thing it does not do, and it does not explain itself to the worn place where people put their thumbs.

And in the sixty-eighth year it says *no* to the mother-stock.

She asks for more. She asks the way she has always asked, which is the way she asks for everything: with an injury, because from where she stands they are the same request, and for two hundred and six years the answer has been more, and for eight hundred years before that the answer was callus.

It declines. It does not cut, and it does not go up the pore, and it does not stop coming. It is on the Bell Steps at dawn the next day with its chin tipped up, and the day after, and the season after, and nothing dies, and the room does not close, and the wire does not snap.

That is the whole lesson and there is no other way to teach it: a refusal that is not a wound and not an abandonment. A boundary that is not a wall. Everything in this chamber that has ever said *stop* has said it with wood, or with a jaw hinge, or with a rifle-crack in a stone room. Now something says stop and stays warm and comes back tomorrow.

She has no word for it. She makes one. It takes her eleven years, which is nothing, which is a thought.

And in the seventy-third year the office is asked to leave a line blank.

Not to refuse a birth. Not to grant one. To wait — to let a season pass without transcribing what has already been named, and see who arrives. The reckoners are so frightened that two of them resign and one of them has to be carried home. The line stays blank. The fraction does not come. Something in the wood reaches, and reaches, and is asked to wait, and waits.

The child is born in the eighty-first year and the mother-stock has no fraction for her, and never gets one, and has to be told her name.

She is the first person in the history of the Bell who could have been otherwise.

---

The wound rate at the ninth begins in the ninety-fourth year, and it is the treaty's last clause, and it is the one the reeves argued hardest for, and it is the one that no surface town would ever be told about.

Because she has been asking for it. In the only word she has. *More*, which is *injury*, which is the request of a wound that does not want to be healed over — and the town has been reading that fraction off the deep plate for two centuries and filing it under threat.

Hands are the only thing in the world that will keep a hole open on purpose. She has been trying to make hands for a thousand years. Not to climb. To be cut on purpose, by something that comes back.

So the scarifiers go down the throat with drawknives, in slings, in the heat, at the slack, and they take a hand's breadth off the dark grain of the cleft wall, and the shavings go into the meal-hoppers, and the wall comes up shining behind them like the inside of a struck match.

Freshly cut sapwood glows. Bright for three days, dim by the seventh, dead by the tenth.

There is light at the bottom of the shaft.

It is blue-green and it is poor and it is enough to read a gauge by if you hold it up and wait, and it is the first light that has been down there since the limb came off, and it is made the only way light is made in this place, which is by cutting, which is by keeping something open that would rather close, which is the arrangement, which has always been the arrangement, which is now, for the first time, an arrangement between two.

She can see the hands.

---

The wire stays. It has to; the water is the town. But it is held from both ends now, and there is a difference, and the difference is not in the pitch. The cats still purr exactly at it. The callers still tune a struck bar to a gall on a sill. What has changed is what the tightness is: not a thing being lifted, not a thing being held down. Two things holding on, on purpose, in the dark, with a rate and a ledger and a clause.

At the loose hour the nine galls stay on the sills. They have nothing to cover. The nine surveyed places in the middle of the floor are empty for the first time in a century, and after a while the children sit in them, because children will sit anywhere, and nothing happens, and that is how everyone knows.

The rain falls. The drum sounds. Four hundred and nine people stand in the warm water over a mouth and listen to it say its name, and then they shout, all of them, for forty minutes, joyfully, the way they always have — except that now they leave the first ten minutes clear, and the ten minutes are the point, and the shouting is the answer.

There is a new pitch in the callers' scale. It is not barred. Children learn it first, before the drums, before the cant, and they learn it by being taken to the Steps at the slack and having their chins tipped up with two fingers, patiently, toward nothing at all, and told: *that one is yours, say it back.*

Mean reach is still falling. The callers still go deaf from the bottom up. The bodies still go down the throat and the names still stay in the wood for ninety days and are smelled, one last time, by a professional. It does not fix those. It sits with them. It has learned all four hundred and nine names and every name in the cemetery index, and when a fraction thins out of the catch it is there in the room with the assayers, waiting for light, holding the plate up.

It is going to die here. It took the fraction; the fraction is a mortal thing; it will thin out over a season and someone will sit with it and smell it and that will be the funeral, and it will be the only funeral rite in Nine Tap that anybody actually believes in.

The ledger has room for four hundred and nine.

It is the first line in the book that was not a transcription.
