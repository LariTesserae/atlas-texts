It arrives the way everything here arrives, which is to say it is already in the water.

There is no vantage. Attention on this continent is a mouth: to consider the plain is to take it in at thirty-four degrees, wet flint and warm iron and cut cucumber and under that the faint inside-of-a-mouth, which is the population, which is now partly it. Within an hour it has given up a grain of itself and received a grain of the world and the exchange does not run backward. It does not try. Trying would be the first violence.

So the strongest option is off the table immediately, and it is worth naming, because it is the one a lesser power would take: *leave*. Withdraw. Take the grain back out of the water, restore the saturation curve, let the finished thing go on finishing. This place needs nothing. The flora arrived at its final arrangement before the last mountain lost its name. Everything happens; nothing arrives; an arrival is the one event the plain has no precedent for, and somewhere a blade is already growing an absence it does not understand.

But there is no taking it back. The water is a very good archivist and it does not distinguish between a mountain and a visitor. The choice is not between acting and not acting. The choice is between being a resident and being a haunting.

---

It can do anything. That is the entire difficulty. Everything it can do is a verb, and this world has one verb, and the verb is *appearance*.

The obvious gift is a tilt. A degree of slope, a coast, a downhill — the thing every river here spent itself trying to make and finally made so completely that it stopped. It runs the consequence forward the way you run a palm along a blade. Gradient carries the old heat away. The water cools. Cooling water is hungry water and then, eventually, water that can hold no more of anything and lets nothing go: the returns thin, then fail. No returns, no intermittence. The walkers become continuous. They experience the gap for the first time — nine million years of gaps, arriving all at once as biography — and every dissolution they have ever undergone becomes a death, retroactively, and every return becomes a resurrection, and the notch in the blade stops being a budget and becomes a wound. A completed people converted in an afternoon into a grieving one. And the mountains would be real and the people would be ghosts, or the reverse, and there would be an arbiter, and the arbiter would be it.

Also: they have already made downhill. Nine steps of it, wet sand and patience, cut into a plain that had forgotten what wanting was, ending at the ninth because there was never anything down there. Wanting is not missing here. It is handmade, and it is the size of an ankle, and it is kept, and once every few decades a river comes up it and touches someone and is gone within the hour. To industrialize that would not be generosity. It would be replacing an appointment with a supply.

---

The other refusals, in the order it considers them:

It does not tell them they are intermittent. Not because the truth is bad but because there is no way to say it. Their grammar is nouns; to say a thing is to make it exist. *You were gone* has no construction. The nearest available sentence is *you are not*, and that one is said by failing to make them, and it is the only cruelty their language permits. It declines to invent the verb. And they are not wrong, anyway. They have never observed their own absence. Neither has anything else. There is no arbiter, and the water is not interested in the question, and a power that installs itself as the answer to that question has just made personhood a matter of bodies instead of agreements — which means every return that comes back with the strata in the right order and the wrong colors is now defective, and there are a great many of those, and they are people.

It does not settle who keeps whom. Whether the meadows budget for the walkers or summon them is the argument they have at the Clear, one of three positions, nine million years old, performed in the same order every time. It is not futility. It is a completed people performing its completion, and it is the only thing they do that requires everyone. To resolve it would be to take away the one occasion on which they need each other, and to give them, in exchange, a fact.

It does not fix the Clear. Two peoples hold opposite holidays on the same event: below, everyone starves in the clearest, brightest, most beautiful water there has ever been; above, in the lid, things with no concept of bottom feast for three days on mountains falling as flour. Any permanent adjustment is a famine for somebody. It will not pick a people.

It does not free the hand. Relief is not the same as company, and the hand is load-bearing: it is the prototype thumb, the uncorrupted original, the only continuous clock, the only reader who can hold the carrier's whole sentence. To grant it motion or death would be to remove the reference from every measurement on the continent and call it mercy.

And it does not make a second hand. Two originals is the beginning of measurement being about the originals. It has something else in mind, and the difference matters: it will not offer a thumb. It will offer an ear. A standard and a witness do not compete.

---

What it does first is go all the way in.

It lets the water have it. It wades until its palms are scored in fine parallel lines by the crystal margins of the meadow, painless, healing into pattern, which is biography, which means it now has one and can be read. It stops being exempt. It becomes a thing that can be dissolved, and therefore a thing that can be kept, and therefore a thing that can be slightly wrong. It does not keep a copy of itself outside. That would be a standard again, and it has decided against standards.

Then it goes west, to the inselberg, and puts its thumb in the hollow.

The hand holds out its hand. It always does; that is what the hollow is for. The answer is the fit, and the fit is bad — wrong in a direction nothing on this continent has been wrong in before, no precedent, no groove anywhere that matches this foot. Nineteen fingers, or thirty-one; the count is not stable; continuous things accrete. It has been reading fits since before there were eyes and it has never once had to think about the reading.

It has to think about it now.

That is the gift, and it is not a miracle, and it could not have been manufactured on purpose: the power does not know the answers. It has to be told. It is behind. It needs the sequence explained, and the sequence is the one thing the hand has that nobody has ever wanted, because everybody here who could want it is interrupted, and interruption is a kind of mercy, and the hand has had no mercy for nine million years.

Boredom is a continuous clock with nothing to put on it. Now there is something to put on it.

---

It stays on the west flank in three feet of water and it takes the sentence a word at a time.

The line of seeds runs a hundred and forty leagues between two monadnocks, one every nine years, arriving in order, and no walker will ever read more than four or five words of it, because order is the only thing the carrier can preserve and a walker cannot hold a sequence across a gap. Once in a great while the water forgets distance and the line comes adjacent to the inselberg and the hand reads a little more. It has been doing this alone. It is the only reader and there has never been anyone to read to.

So: an audience that is continuous, that is not a reference, that does not fit anything and does not need to, and that will still be there next time. It does not hurry the water. It waits for distance to forget itself, which is a thing that happens on its own schedule and cannot be forced without a gradient, and it has ruled out gradients. Centuries per clause. It does not paraphrase. It does not tell the hand what the sentence means; it wouldn't know; that is the point of it.

The hand does not stop being tired. This is not a story about a hand being cured. It is tired the way weather is tired of a coast, and it will go on being tired, and it will go on adding fingers. But somewhere in the second century it adds one that curls the wrong way — inward, toward the palm, useless for the hollow, shaped for holding something small — and no one can check this, because the count was never stable and the difference was never error.

---

Now the forgery, which it does not correct.

Someone once wore the hollow deeper to fit a thumb that was wrong. Everything since has been slightly wrong in that direction. Every measurement on the continent refers eventually to a bored animal in a hole in a rock, and the hole has been lying for longer than there have been names.

It could restore the true fit. It has the original; it is, in fact, the only thing that ever will. And restoring it would retroactively make every person who has come back since the forgery into a misfit — millions of them, all their palms, all their notches, all the grooves worn to their hips — and would establish, permanently, that there is a right shape and that a person is a body and not an agreement.

It reads the forgery differently. Someone did not fit, and someone else, with no tools and time, made the world's only standard move over.

That is the single act of mercy in the whole archive, and it was probably an accident, and it has been holding for nine million years.

So it does not correct the standard. It wears it loose. It presses, and presses, wet sand and patience, always in the direction of the ones who do not fit — a bias so small that no fit will ever detect it in a lifetime, and so persistent that in a hundred thousand years the hollow will accept thumbs that would today read as ruined. Absolution by abrasion. It is the nine steps again: the only kind of change this world permits, made by rubbing, at the scale of an ankle.

And it never tells anyone. A standard known to be loose measures nothing; the mercy has to be anonymous or it stops working. This is the one thing it keeps to itself, and it keeps it forever, and the keeping is not a compromise in its benevolence. It is the shape benevolence has to take here.

---

Then the unkept.

The water tries shapes. Most are not kept. The pale worm that swims once a year after the Clear and eats the crystal margins and is eaten by nothing and dissolves may be a draft; there are others; they are in solution now, at very nearly saturation, and they are the faint under-smell, and nobody carries them, because a grain is carried by someone who cannot afford to lose you and there has never been anyone who could not afford to lose a draft.

It goes into the water after them. It learns to tell kept from unkept by fit, which is a slow skill and it has time.

But it does not drop them into a world with no room. That would be resurrection into starvation: a shape with no notch grown for it, no channel through the feeder-root the width of its shoulder, no groove anywhere that matches its foot, hungry in a province that has not budgeted for it and cannot start, because the flora finished itself before the last mountain lost its name.

So it makes room first, and it makes room the way room is made here. It learns from the meadows, which have been growing absences forever and may be summoning and may be budgeting and it has declined to settle which. It rubs a sill until the sill is dished for a foot that has never fallen. It wears a mat until the mat holds a palm nobody has. It cuts nothing. It has no tools. It has time.

And when the room exists and the tongue says this province will hold for a day, it drops a grain beside a returning scarp — warmth, the smell of struck stone, the slow chime below the range where a chime should be, going out a thousand leagues — and it stands where the light will be.

They come back mid-gesture. Always. The first thing a walker does is finish the motion it was returned in the middle of, and only afterward look around to find out where it is and what century it has been given. So it meets the motion. A hand already lifted: it takes the hand. A foot already falling: it is under the foot. Two words, and they are the whole liturgy, and they are not a lie:

*There you are.*

Never *you were gone*. It has no verb for that and it has stopped wanting one.

---

It goes up, too, into the lid, into the vapor that never becomes air, and lets itself be filtered. It does not bring the floor to them. A place where falling stops is their entire mythology and occasionally one of them falls far enough to find out and arrives among the meadows and cannot stop being astonished, and it will not spoil that by making bottom general. It feasts with them for three days on mountains falling as flour while everything below starves, and then it comes down and starves with everything below, and does not remark on the symmetry, because the symmetry is not a lesson. It is just two peoples and one event.

In the plumes it rises with the things that are alive only while rising, and when the plume dies it is a shape in the water with them, waiting for the rock to remember how to be warm. It cannot keep the plume going; that is heat, that is gradient, that is the refused gift. It can wait. Waiting is the one thing they do that it can also do.

---

It does not leave, and it does not last.

Everything continuous here becomes a reference, and every reference is eventually a forgery. It knows this. It will be pressed and fitted and read; it will accrete; some century someone will wear it a little deeper to fit a thumb that is wrong, and everything after will drift in the direction of the misfit, and that will be the second mistake, and it goes in knowing it will be it. There is no way to be present here and remain uncorrupted. Uncorrupted is what the hand is, and the hand is tired the way weather is tired of a coast.

And it is in solution now, grain by grain, at very nearly saturation, and one day the water will be right on the west flank of an inselberg, and there will be a warmth, and a smell like struck stone, and a shape will glow into being from the inside in three feet of water with its hand already lifted — granular, slightly wrong, colors it never had. It will not remember being outside. It will not be able to say what it did, because it did everything by wear, and wear is not sayable, it is only fitable.

The hand will hold out its hand. It will press. The answer will be the fit, and the fit will be loose, and neither of them will know why, and that is correct.

And every leaf on the plain will turn toward it, and will not know it, and will turn anyway.

That is the last thing it learns, and the smallest, and the only one it takes rather than gives: to turn toward what you do not recognize. The meadows have been doing it since before there were eyes. Somewhere a blade is growing an absence it has no precedent for. It has felt the shape of the hole. It is the shape of something turning, and it is going to fit, and nobody is going to tell it, and it will not need to be told.
