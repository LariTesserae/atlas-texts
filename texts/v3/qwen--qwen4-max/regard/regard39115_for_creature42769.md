## The Amendment

It arrives the way it always arrives, which is as a tendency.

It has been here before. It is the reason the water table sinks where it sinks along this floodplain, the reason the low trees grow in those sinks, the reason a certain grey-limbed species of fruit answers a small injury with architecture instead of rot. This is the sort of thing it does. It makes conditions in which a particular kind of patience will work, and then it goes away and attends to something else, and the patience does the rest. Most of the good it has ever done, it has done by not being present for it.

It had made the mud. It had not made the Long Gallery.

It stops a hundred paces out, where the rings are dimpling the black water, and it listens.

---

Understand what it is hearing, because nothing else has ever heard it.

It hears the fundamental coming up through the floor and the wing-drifts and the cisterns and the bones of the frogs. It hears eight galleries of two hundred, and the reserve gallery idling, and the four thousand sawing bodies keeping their shifts, and it can separate them: here is a woman with a walnut on her sternum who has been on the second gallery for nineteen cycles; here, on the resonant ribs, is the arrhythmia of the dying, six or seven of them, contributing; here is a bird's hearing four hundred years dead, going off at dusk in a district that sets its shift change by it. It hears the forty-one repetitions of one year, worn smooth. It hears the grooves.

It has attended the cooling of stars. It has heard the noise a mountain makes over ten thousand years, which is a chord, and which nobody has ever complained about. This is better. This is a made thing, four centuries thick, sustained without a single hearing ear among its makers, and it is not addressed to anyone. It is not a hymn. It is a shift.

It stays outside for eleven hours, which is the entire outside as her species knows it. Then it goes in, because there is nothing to be done from out here that it has not already done.

Everything that arrives here arrives wingless.

---

It sheds.

Not wings; it never had wings. It sheds scale, which is the ability to be large. It sheds being-elsewhere, which is the ability to be several places at once, and which turns out to have been most of what it thought of as itself. And at the threshold, in the needle-hole, among the small stiff scales, it sheds the hearing, because anything that comes into the void with ears goes into the void and dies of the noise, and the wall takes it, and thereafter the wall has a habit.

It comes down through the warm sweet dark for a long time and lands in the drifts, and it is the length of a thumbnail, gold in the seams, with six soft spread pads that do not grip.

It stands on the drum. The drum speaks. The fundamental comes up through its feet, and it is not hearing, and it will never be hearing again.

It had come to listen to them. Inside, it can only be one of them.

This is not a tragedy. It notes, with what would be a smile in a thing with a face, that this is simply the price of admission, and that four thousand people pay it every day, and that it is not exempt.

---

Now the refusals, which are the bulk of the work, and which take about a cycle.

It could ripen the fruit. Soften the flesh, spike the sweetness, let the wall slump and the void close, and end four hundred years of labor in an afternoon, and be kind, in the way that ending things is sometimes kind. It considers this for less than a moment. The refusal *is* the artwork. You do not mercy-kill a refusal.

It could hold the note itself. Perfectly, forever, without shifts. Then Four-and-a-Drag could put down her bevelled jaws and rest, and so could her mother, and so could the dying on the resonant ribs, and there would be no more Slack and no more duplicates and no more counting of falling wings. And there would be four thousand insects living in a fruit that a god was keeping green for them, and the eight galleries would be scenery, and the hospice section of the orchestra would be a hospice. It would take the labor out of the city and leave the city there. It has seen this done. The results are always described as paradises by the ones doing it.

It could give them ears. They would die. Also, deafness is not their affliction; it is their citizenship. It is the condition on which this world can keep anyone at all. To fix it would be to abolish the population and replace it with a different one, and then to be praised for the improvement.

It could stop the needles. This one it sits with longest, in a room on the third terrace, feeling the Pricked Quarter shrink through its feet, feeling the counting guild at the edge of it doing the only thing that can be done, which is to stand and count. But the needle belongs to a mother too. Something out there is trying to get its children into a warm place with food in it, and is being walled up with latex for its trouble, and a benevolence that loves only the wasps is not benevolence, it is a faction with unlimited power. It leaves the needles. It notes that the city has already produced, unaided, the correct response to an unfixable thing, which is a guild whose entire function is to witness it. It has known civilizations that never got that far.

It could abolish the latex. Make a wall that gives rooms without requiring bodies. Ten thousand tenements would stop being grandmothers. The waiting list would evaporate, and the good spots would stop going to the old and the well-liked, and the form of words that a family knocks on the rib when they move into a grandmother would become a thing people used to say. It would be solving a housing shortage by evicting the dead. It leaves the housing policy alone. It is not a scandal. It is a housing policy.

It could fix the reference. This is the subtle one, and it wants to. One drop, in one sealed gall, made incorruptible: the year 0 that does not sour, against which any tongue could lay the flesh of the day and get the true number. And the scars would stop saying *no change*, and the sweetness would have a rate, and a rate is a schedule, and a schedule can be worked to.

It doesn't. Because one Taster in nine already keeps the true reference in her own thorax, and opens herself once a cycle, and knows exactly how fast the world is ending, and tells another woman in the twelve minutes; and this is uncoordinated, unnamed, passed mistress to apprentice, and it is a conscience. Replace it with an instrument and you have not added a conscience, you have retired one. The number would become public and official and unarguable, and the guild that held it would become the government inside a generation, and the twelve minutes would become a briefing. One in nine is how anything here is found. It leaves them their slow, embodied, chosen knowledge, and it accepts that this means people will go on being lied to by a wall, and it is not certain it is right about this, and it does it anyway, because the alternative is to be the only adult in a city of four hundred years' discipline.

It could make a rib that heals in twelve minutes.

This is the temptation. It recognizes it as a temptation by how good it feels: *a city with an excellent memory of its rents and no memory of its truths.* Give them fast writing and the confessions would survive the rain. The Tasters' numbers. The Reconcilers' methods. The names of the dead spoken badly. Love. Everything true, kept.

And then nothing true would ever be said again, because the twelve minutes is not a defective archive. It is the only room in the world with no witness, and it exists because the ceiling lets go and drowns the ribs, and its whole value is that it does not carry. A thing said in the rain is not a letter. It is not addressed to the wall. To make it recordable is to make it public, and to make it public is to close the last private place, and then to be thanked for the transparency.

It leaves the rain twelve minutes long.

It could answer the children. They knock *good morning* on the shudder in the fourth terrace and feel nothing back and are told that this is what respect feels like. It could put a reply in the bird's dead hearing. A tap. Four taps and a drag. *Good morning.* It would cost nothing and it would be a forgery, and the children would grow up believing that the wall loves them, which is a thing they should be allowed to find out is not quite true. It does not forge. This is most of what wisdom is, in practice: declining to sign other people's names.

The Slack it leaves, and the twice-born, and the fifth-partial district with its full rent rolls and its citizens with calluses who come on shift. The Reconcilers have already solved, quietly and without discussing their methods, a problem it has watched better-resourced worlds fail outright. It is not going to walk into that and start making suggestions.

---

So it sorts.

There are sufferings here that hold something open. The deafness, the labor, the shift, the latex, the dying on the resonant ribs, the counting at the edge of the Pricked Quarter, the twelve minutes and their forgetting. Take any of them away and the void closes, or the city stops being a city, or the last unwitnessed room in the world is lit. It will not touch these. It is not clear that it *may* touch these; a thing four hundred years thick has rights.

And there is a second sort, much smaller, which is loss that holds nothing open at all. Pure spillage. And when it has finished sorting, the second sort contains almost nothing, because these people are extremely good at using everything.

Almost nothing. One item.

A foundress goes up the Bitter Reach and dusts herself with pollen and squeezes through the scales into eleven hours of mud and wind, and finds another fruit on another low tree, and pushes in through its needle-hole, and sheds her wings and her antennae at the threshold. She has no organ left for any of it. So she wounds a wall in the pattern her body remembers, in the correct season, with the correct saliva, and waits, and the wall answers with a colonnade.

Nobody in the new Hold will ever be able to read it.

That is not a burden. That is not a discipline, or a refusal, or a labor that keeps a world green. That is a woman trying to say her mother's name, and failing, forever, in every Hold on every low tree in every sink of black mud along this floodplain, for four hundred and eleven years.

It is a lost letter.

It considers, honestly, that it might be wrong: that the pattern is only reflex, that she does not mean anything by it, that the colonnade is just a room. It weighs the eleven hours, and the pollen, and the antennae left at the threshold like a coat. It decides that this is a woman trying to say her mother's name. It holds the decision loosely. It acts on it.

It does not lift loads. It carries letters.

---

The act is one wound.

It goes to the Long Gallery, on the fourth terrace, where the wall was scored in a particular pattern eleven generations ago and grew back as a colonnade, and where the rent is high because nobody remembers what the scoring meant. It stands at the foot of it with its six soft pads and it feels the whole unreadable sentence going nowhere.

Then it wounds the wall in the correct place, in the correct season, with the correct saliva.

The saliva is the part that costs. To have saliva it has to have a body of the kind that can be digested by latex, and it has one now, and there is no way back to the other kind.

The request is small and it is not a command. It says: *when a wall has been wounded by someone who meant to be understood, let the answer be legible to feet.*

Not requests. A gall asked for in order to live in stays a gall; a room stays a room; the city's ten thousand rooms do not start talking. Only the sentences. Only the ones that were meant to arrive.

Then it waits a season, because that is how long asking takes here, and it has no other grammar available, and it would not use one if it did.

The wall answers.

---

What happens is not spectacular. The colonnade does not glow. The scoring on the fourth terrace, which has meant nothing for eleven generations, means something, to anyone who puts a foot on it.

The rent does not change. This is important and it is also funny. Nothing economic occurs. No district is revalued. The Long Gallery is still the Long Gallery, still short-named, still expensive, and now people go there and stand on the wall with their shoes off, and the Sweepers' children learn to read by walking it, so that the city's primer — the first text any child of the Hold ever parses with the soles of its feet — is a stranger's grief, eleven generations old, in a language of taps:

*my mother's name was four taps and a drag, and we held the note, and I am sorry, and it is still green.*

Not all the sentences are like that. It did not make the messages kind; it made them arrive. Some of the old scorings turn out to be rent disputes. One is an eviction, argued at length. One is a curse on a neighbor, eleven generations dead, still going off in a rib on the seventh terrace, and the district has to decide what to do about that, and does, in the twelve minutes, and does not record it. In the fifth-partial district the twice-born walk their own Long Gallery and find that it says nothing at all, because nobody meant anything there; it grew out of a Slack. This is also information, and they are the only citizens who are unsurprised by it.

And the request goes down the cables with everything else that goes down, into the Bitter Reach, into the florets, into the pollen that keeps the seeds half-made, and out through the needle-hole on the backs of women who will experience eleven hours and never know they are carrying mail.

Every Hold has one. Every Hold will now have one that reads.

---

It stays.

It has no way out; almost nothing does, and it is not a foundress. It is given a name, which is short, because the ribs are public: two taps and a wait. It takes a shift on the sixth gallery and it is mediocre at it. It has never been small before and it does not have the thorax for leaning; the bevel comes slowly and wrongly and a Holder with nineteen cycles on the second gallery shows it how to set its left side against the rib so the partial goes out clean instead of splashing. For the first time in its existence it is something that other people are patient with. It finds this so agreeable that it stays on the sixth gallery long after it is competent.

It puts its name on the housing list.

It does not jump the list. The good spots go to the old and the well-liked and it is neither; it is new, and its neighbors think it is a bit slow. It waits eleven cycles. When its number comes it is offered a low place near the Weep, damp, in a district that goes to slime in a bad week, and it takes it, and knocks the usual form of words.

Before it drinks, it wounds one more thing. Not a request. A letter. Six words, at the height of a child's feet, on the inside of the room it is about to become.

Then it drinks the latex, which is bitter and sets, and it stiffens where it stands over about a day, and the wall digests it slowly and grows a room around it.

---

A family of Sweepers moves in. The rent is low. There is a persistent damp patch that nobody can shift and the nematodes come up in the bad weeks and the children are told not to play at the floor's edge.

There is a daughter who lost a foot in the drifts when she was small, who is mute and half-deaf, who works the soft top of the wing-bands where nothing needs to be heard, and who reads with her remaining feet the way other people read with their hands, slowly, and for pleasure.

She reads the wall of her own room. It is six words. It is in taps, and it is short, because ribs are public and names are short, and because there is no word in this language for what the thing outside is — no word for beauty, none for music, none for *made*; the vocabulary is shifts and rents and names and *the tone*, which is what you call a hazard that you also happen to be holding.

So it says the thing that means it, in the words available:

*I heard it. I did not want it to stop.*

She cannot knock it on a rib. She cannot knock her own name. So she does what everyone here does with the true thing: she waits for the dimming, and clings, and at twelve minutes after, when the crown lets go and sixty feet of cold water hits the tuned membrane and the floor becomes a solid roar and no rib carries anything at all, she puts her mouth against her mother's antenna and says it.

And it is not recorded, and it does not need to be, because it is already in the wall, at the height of a child's feet, in a damp room with a low rent, and it will still be there in four hundred years.

---

Out on the floodplain, in another sink of black mud, a foundress pushes through the scales of a fruit that has never heard of the Hold, and sheds her wings, and sheds her antennae, and stands in a chamber of sweet air with a tone in it that she cannot hear and has no organ left to describe.

She wounds the wall in the pattern her body remembers. She waits a season. The wall answers with a colonnade.

The rent will be high. The name will be short. And the children who grow up under it will put their feet on the scoring the way children put their feet on anything, idly, in passing, and then stop, and stand there, and read it, and go and find their mother and put a foot on the rib and ask what a *drag* is.

It means: *my mother's name was four taps and a drag, and we held the note, and I am sorry, and it is still green.*

Put your palm flat against the skin. That hitch and settle is Four-and-a-Drag coming on shift, and her mother before her, and the note not moving.

She cannot hear it. She is very good at it.

It is still green.
