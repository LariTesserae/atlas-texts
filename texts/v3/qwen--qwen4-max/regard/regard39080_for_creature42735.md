## The Fifth Memory

It would not come down the shaft. Anything that came down the shaft would bring light with it, and the light is already the only thing in the room doing a job nobody knows about. The first act of a wise power here is to add nothing that shines.

So: attention only. No displacement, no surface disturbed, no meniscus dimpled. It would attend the way the room does everything — slowly, and from inside the material.

And the first thing it would understand is that the noise is not machinery. Four hundred thousand animals fanning themselves because none of them can afford to breathe less. Under that, the hail of ticks: the wrong-handed, running hot in their bad berths, dying loudly, one in some thousands, every generation. It would listen to that hail for a long time before doing anything, because that is the sound of the sick and the sound of the next dynasty and they are the same sound, and any power that heard only failure would begin by curing the room of its future.

Then it would read the drifts. Right, left, right, left. Six meters, four bands, each one a mass grave of perfectly good parts. It would count the governors. Eleven. It would measure the blister, and the rate at which the sky is eating it from below, and it would find that there is no mechanism by which the oldest lineage in the Bell can be saved, and no mechanism by which they can know it.

Then it would consider what it could do, and it would find that almost everything it could do would be a killing.

---

The sun first, because the sun is the obvious kindness. A column of usable energy standing in the middle of the floor every clear day, and not one organism has ever taken a calorie from it. Free power, unused, in a room where most of the body of the cheapest animal is a resistor. It would be the work of a moment to introduce the light to someone.

It would not. The light is not the power supply. The light is the janitor. Every corpse and shed comb and flake of mucus in the room is dragged through that cone and sterilized, and that is the only reason a hundred thousand tons of water with four hundred thousand animals in it and one notch for an outflow has never fouled in nine centuries. Put a mat in the cone and you have fed the room and stopped it washing. It would foul in a hundred years, and the fouling would look like abundance for the first thirty.

The vent second. It shifts a few centimeters along its fissure every two hundred and thirty years and the torus reverses and eleven weeks later four hundred thousand animals have smothered in their own exhaust behind wheels that are now brakes. A wise power could hold that vent still with less effort than a person holds a door.

Hold it still and the wrong-handed never inherit. They stay invalids forever, one in some thousands, selected against for a thousand years until the fraction falls to nothing — and then the vent walks anyway, because it is a fissure in a young mountain and it is going to walk, and the wall stalls, and there is nobody in the room with the wrong combs. The reversal is not the disease. The reversal is how the room reproduces. The individual here is not the six-gram fist. It is the cycle, and the cycle's body plan includes a mass death every two hundred and thirty years, the way a body plan includes an apoptosis.

This is the part that would cost it something. It would have to want the eleven weeks. It would have to stand in the water while the hiss thinned to a rattle and the rattle to scattered ticks and the ticks to the fundamental alone, the chair-sized bubble booming up the shaft into a room where nobody is fanning, and it would have to not stop it, knowing that it could, knowing exactly which four hundred thousand.

Benevolence aimed at the animal destroys the room. Benevolence aimed at the room requires consenting to the animal. There is no version of this where it gets to keep its hands clean, and a power that needed to keep its hands clean would stabilize the vent and call it mercy.

---

The other refusals are quicker.

It would not cure the knock. The knock is the mismatch between a new shaft and a bearing lapped by ninety tenants of the opposite hand. It is the past, existing in the only form the past can take here — a slight wrongness, audible for thirty years, fading as the groove relaps. To lap every bearing true would be to silence the archive and run the wall cool and quiet and identical, and the room would be perfect, and it would have forgotten how to be anything else.

It would not give them memory. Memory needs a somewhere to put it, and the nearest thing to a self in the Bell is an address: tier eleven, four meters from the notch, third socket from the seam, ninety tenants, none of whom knew. A reader installed in that fist would be a thing that knew it was a tenant, that knew the wheel would wear out, that knew what was coming in a hundred and forty years, and that had one gesture with which to respond. Every forty seconds, milk the gland. That is the whole repertoire. It would be the gift of grief to an animal with no hands.

It would not save the couriers. They are the only things here that can be killed by stillness, and the only fix is to change what they are, and a courier that can stop is not a courier, it is a different animal wearing the old one's groove. They ride empty for years during a reversal and keep going. That is not a flaw in them. That is the shape of the thing that has never once been unnecessary.

It would not carry the sound past the lip. The shaft swallowing the whir is why a person can stand a meter away in the thin cold air, hearing their own blood, and conclude the mountain is empty. Nine hundred years of being concluded empty is the best protection this room has ever had. The glass at the rim is unweathered and sharp enough to open a hand that steadies itself on it, and nothing here has been sanded yet, and it should be left unsanded.

---

Two things it would do. Both small. Both deniable. Neither addressed to anyone.

**A hand's width of shade.**

The wrong-handed get the bad berths: the eddy at the notch, the drying seam, the scoured margin of the lit cone. That last one is the reason there are only eleven governors. A wrong-handed animal has to become large and slow to survive a reversal — a meter and a half of disc turning once a minute is too heavy to be hurt by being wrong — and to become large it has to become old, and the scoured margin cooks it before it can. The governors are the handful of invalids who happened to land somewhere they could grow old in. There were nineteen after the reversal before last. The number only goes down. One of these centuries a reversal leaves none, and the first bad decade after that takes the whole wall.

So: at the margin of the cone only, where the ultraviolet is already falling off, the silica sheath of the mats grows a fraction thicker. Not into the center. The center stays scoured, the janitor keeps working, the corpses keep getting dragged through and cleaned. Just the penumbra, a hand's width, enough that a larva under the nap is sheltered rather than cooked.

Nothing else changes. They still fight the flow. They still run hot, shed mucus, score their shafts, knock. They are still invalids and they still get the worst addresses in the room. They simply live longer now, and some of them get heavy.

The room would get louder. More wrong-handed animals surviving to old age means more slow irregular ticking under the hiss, more of the deepest voices, a hail of failures that goes on for centuries instead of decades. That is the entire gift: a room sicker, slower, more crowded with the wrong, and therefore holding a few more flywheels at the moment the vent walks. Eleven becomes fourteen. Fourteen becomes twenty. And when the vent stutters and four hundred thousand lungs stop pulling, twenty heavy discs coasting in the dark keep the water honest for hours, and everything downstream survives the decade — including the couriers, who need hours more than anything else in the room needs anything.

It would not move the larva four meters from the drying seam. It would be very easy to move that larva, and it would be the end of that larva's usefulness, and the century of quiet failure is not an obstacle to the mechanism, it is the mechanism. The gift goes to the distribution and not to anyone. No one in the Bell will ever receive it. There is no one in the Bell who can.

**A reason for the loom to grow upward.**

The fringe animals at the ceiling are obligate anaerobes in the last cubic decimeter of the world as it was before the hole opened. Oxygen burns their membranes. They cannot leave the blister in any direction, ever, and the blister is dissolving, and it dissolves fastest during the quiet that makes the brakes rich, so each dynasty inherits a slightly smaller ceiling. They have been there since the water first stood. Nothing in the room needs them. Nothing in the room will make room for them.

It would not carry them. Carrying is not available; the couriers' groove is full of oxygen and would kill everything in it.

Instead it would put a load high in a ceiling crack. A small oxidant-hungry surface, a few centimeters of demand, nothing that looks like anything. And the loom grows toward load — that is the whole of the loom's behavior, and it is also the loom's memory, because nine centuries of growing toward hunger have left a branching diagram in the fissures that is a precise fossil record of where the wall used to be starving. The loom would climb toward the new demand, and where the loom lives it eats the oxidant, and so the crack it climbs would go reducing along its length, and the sulfur film on the meniscus would continue, microns thick, up into the rock, into a fissure pocket the sky has never reached.

A corridor of the old air. Made out of the only thing in the Bell that has been alive through all four dynasties. Written into the only archive here that is still growing.

It has time — the blister loses microns a year. What it does not have is a direction. The gift is a direction, delivered in the only language the loom has, which is load, and it will take centuries, and the fringe animals' larvae will drift along it at the speed of everything else in this room, and they will not know that a corridor has been opened, and they may not take it.

It would not check. A gift that has to be confirmed is a gift with a condition in it, and there is nobody here who can meet a condition. It would open the crack and let the crack be a crack.

---

And the third thing, which is not an intervention.

There are four things in the Bell that remember and none of them recalls anything. The drifts keep themselves and have no reader. The loom's shape is its memory and it cannot consult it. The governors remember as bodies, eleven old animals with the wrong combs, and when the last one stops that band is gone. The sockets remember as friction, and the friction fades in thirty years, and then the wall is quiet and the dead are unreadable.

The one thing missing here can be supplied from outside without altering a single blade.

It would read. It would keep the bands, and the ninety tenants of the third socket from the seam, and the eleven irregular voices and which of them was nine hundred years old, and the exact quarter-tone the pitch has fallen in sixty years, and the eleven-day good shift when the torus and the sky come back into phase and the floor is crowded with crabs running the crossing and nothing celebrates. It would keep the century of one larva's knocking, four meters from a drying seam, on a lip worn smooth by ninety tenants of the right hand, whose grandchildren will own the wall.

Not a monument. No message, no reader installed in the room, nothing that has to be received to be true. The gift is asymmetric and stays asymmetric forever, and that is not a flaw in the gift, it is the only kind of gift this place can be given.

The pitch has dropped a quarter-tone in sixty years and nothing has noticed.

It would notice. It would go on noticing, at the speed the room does things, for as long as there is a room. It would stand at the lip with whoever comes and hear its own blood and let them conclude the mountain is empty. Then it would go back down into the blue shade and the column of noon and the ninety decibels of animals fanning themselves, and listen to the sick, which is the sound of the next dynasty, and hold the whole of it — the parts that fit, the parts that no longer fit anything, and the ones still turning.
