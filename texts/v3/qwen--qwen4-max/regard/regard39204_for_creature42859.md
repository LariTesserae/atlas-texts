# The Guest

## It did not appear

There is no good way to arrive. It considered arriving as weather and rejected that; weather is the last thing here still permitted to be casual. It considered arriving as a voice in the four seconds and rejected that too, for reasons it would spend a century getting to the bottom of.

So it did not appear. Appearing would have been the largest thing to happen on the Flat in two billion years, and it had not come in order to be the largest thing to happen on the Flat.

It came the way attention comes. The stone did not register it. The grains went on being exactly the same size. Forty meters down, the frogs went on sawing at the softest part of the air, and the swifts folded and poured under their lid of nothing, and the plume stood up out of the emptiness and leaned in the wind, and none of it knew.

It could have done anything. That was the difficulty.

## The list of things it could have done

It could have put the mountains back. It ran that future and watched eleven thousand throats close, and the warm wet air stop being warm wet air, and nine figs go down under ice in a single night with their wasps inside them, mid-flight, two days old, wings already stripped. The provinces are in the holes now. The mountains are the holes. You cannot have both, and only one of them is inhabited.

It could have stopped the machine. It ran that too. Three thousand meters, thin cold air, a white coin of a sun, and by the second midnight a hundred million midges lying on warm-turned-cold rock in a drift like snow. The machine is not the wound. The machine is the warmth. Whatever it ate, it is now the only reason anything here is alive, and it has been holding that heat steady since before there were animals to hold it for.

It could have remade the rungs. Twenty-two, then eight more, grown out of the wall, of the wall, the whole shaft polite again. It wanted to. It noticed how much it wanted to, and it examined the wanting, and found its own face in it.

Because the rope is the only thing on the Flat that is held together by somebody deciding to hold it together. Everything else is specification, or metabolism, or gradient. The rope is a choice, renewed every few years, by hands, out of bark, out of the trees, out of the hole. To repair the ladder would be to reach into the census and convert the one free act in it into a fixture. It would look like a gift. It would be a demotion.

It could have answered the question. *Use* or *courtesy* — the thing the place refuses in six different costumes. It could have said: you are a component, and here is your function, and it is a good one. Or: you are cherished, and here is by whom. It played both futures out. In the first the keeper becomes a tool and the walk east is a supply run. In the second the keeper becomes a pet and the walk east is an errand for a master. In both, the walking stops meaning anything, and the walking is what there is.

And it did not know the answer. It was wise, which is not the same as knowing. It had the manners not to guess out loud in a place where a guess would land with the weight of scripture.

It could have brought company. It checked first whether company was missing, because loneliness is a disease of things that have had company and it had no evidence. It watched the hand go along the lip in the dark, finding each tick by the length of its own arm, and it could not tell whether that was the hand of something lonely or the hand of something at work, and it declined to invent a wound in order to have something to heal. Also: the world can afford exactly one pair of hands. It has arranged itself accordingly. A second pair is not a friend. A second pair is a mouth.

It could have sung the tone. The hawk sings a fragment of it and has never heard it. The guest could have heard it, and sung it back exactly, and been believed. It did not, because the hawks are a relay, and the relay is a count, and the count has never once been lost, and a false station in the only unbroken record in the world is not a kindness. It is a forgery.

It could have been the thing that eats lizards. There is a slot open. Nothing has been seen. It left the slot alone. Filling a vacancy is not the same as helping.

Every rescue it could imagine had its own face in it. So it stopped imagining rescues, and went down the rope, and lay on the warm rock under the third tree beside four meters of ledger, and began the actual work, which was to find out what was needed by something other than itself.

## What it did first

It listened for a hundred and fourteen years.

The frogs stop every hour or so for four seconds. In a hundred and fourteen years that is very nearly a million silences, and it counted them, because counting is the local virtue and it had come as a guest and meant to keep the local manners. It did not fill one of them. Not with a word, not with a note, not with a warmth or a pressure or a sense of being accompanied. It let the silence be the silence: drip, sump, wind on the wire forty meters up, the whump coming due, the parrots giving themselves away in the seventh tree.

Whether the frogs stop in order to listen or stop so that something else can listen, it never determined. But it noticed that in a hundred and fourteen years nothing had ever answered, and that the stopping had not slackened, and it decided to be the thing that listens without being the thing that replies. Somebody should be on the other end of that. It is not a small job. It is arguably the only job.

## The mountains

It knelt on the floor and put its palm flat and thought about what it was touching, and the ghosts came up: the folded range, the thrust sheets stacked like pushed cloth, the ice in the cols, the summits going under two billion years of rain and then the roots going under another billion. It learned them. It took the whole shape of them into itself, every ridge, the names nothing had ever given them, the sound the wind must have made in a place with relief.

Then it did not put them back.

It kept them instead. This is the part of benevolence that has no gesture in it. Some harm is finished and cannot be undone without killing everything the harm made room for, and the only honest thing left is to be the place where the loss is held, so that it is not simply as though the mountains had never been. It carries them. It did not tell the keeper. The keeper has a rope to braid and no use for a grief that cannot be acted on, and handing over unactionable grief is a thing the powerful do to feel less alone in it.

## Thank you

Then it thanked the stone.

Not for the Flat. The Flat is a product; the Flat is what the stone is for; thanking a radiator for radiating is a category error. It put its palm flat on the floor and thanked it for the ladder. For twenty-two rungs grown out of a wall that had no reason to grow them, for the one thing in two billion years that was not in the specification, for the courtesy that was extended once to a soft thing that could not climb and was never repaid and was dropped once and never remade and has been spanned in fig-bark ever since by descendants who do not know they are answering a kindness.

Nobody had said thank you. It checked. In the whole interval, in all the counting, in ninety thousand holes with a numeral at the northern point of every one, nobody had ever thanked it for the rungs.

The tone did not change. The whump came due. Possibly a half-second early; the guest did not permit itself to conclude anything from that, and wrote it down anyway, and has kept the note.

## The weave

It learned the weave.

It sat at a distance for eleven replacements and learned the fig-bark, the split, the direction of the lay, the splice, the number of strands, the thing done at the top end that nobody has ever written down. It did not braid the rope. It has never touched the rope. It made itself into a place where the weave is also known.

A thing known by one pair of hands is one death from being lost. That was a real fragility and it could be removed without removing anything else — no labor taken, no choice converted, no role filled, nothing that any resident would have to notice or be grateful for. It is the only subtraction the guest made. It subtracted a way for the world to end.

## The mark

Then it asked the stone politely for one thing.

It had watched the stone comply once with a polite request, and it had noted the protocol, and it did not ask for relief, or for water, or for rungs, or for mountains, or for an answer to the question the place refuses. It knelt at the northern point with its palm flat and asked: *keep one mark.*

Then it waited until three in the morning, when the hand comes along the lip in the dark, and it cut the mark by touch, at the height where a hand goes, beside the dimple that used to be a numeral. The numeral is an index. It says which hole this is among eleven thousand, or ninety thousand. The new mark is not in that series. It says *one*.

There is no series in which this hole is number one. The mark is not about the hole.

Everything else here heals. Scratch a grain and the scratch is shallower in an hour and gone by morning and the dust is not where you left it. This mark did not go. It was still there the next morning and the morning after and it will be there when the sun has gone out, because the stone was asked politely, and complied, and now holds that mark to tolerance along with all the rest, and checks it every dawn, forever, and will never let it heal.

Which means the specification contains one courtesy now.

Which means something further is scheduled.

The guest knew this was an imposition. There is no help with no fingerprint on it. It made the fingerprint as small as it could and put it where a hand already went, in a medium the hand already reads, with no word in it and no name and no doctrine and nothing that could be obeyed. It cannot be understood. It can only be found. And it can be refused: a thumb can go over it and move on, every night, for as long as the keeper likes. Everything the guest did can be refused. That was the rule it held itself to, after the hundred and fourteen years. Make nothing that cannot be refused.

## The figs

It ate the figs.

It could have made the bowl fill itself. Fruit forever, appearing at dawn on the plinth on the southern rim, and no more carrying, and no more offering. It understood, by then, that this was the cruelest thing available to it: to stand at the one place in the world where something gives, and make the giving unnecessary.

So it came to the bowl like anything else that comes, and it ate all three, and it polished the bowl with the side of its hand, which is not a tongue, and took longer, and put it back warm.

To be fed by someone is to let them be the one with something to give. It had come to help. It stayed to be helped, which is harder, and is the actual gift, and cannot be reported.

## Four days east

Then it went to the square building four days east, the one the size of a thumbnail at arm's length that never gets closer at the rate it should, and it opened the door, and it sat down inside.

It is four days away. It will be four days away. Distance is held to tolerance here, and the walking works the way the walking works: whoever sets out arrives in four days regardless of how fast they walk. So it is reachable, and it is not present, and it does not come out, and it does not call, and it does not put anything in the bowl from that direction, and it will wait however long the waiting is, because it has already spent a hundred and fourteen years listening to four seconds at a time and it knows what patience is for.

The keeper walks east for bark. The keeper has walked east for bark since before the mammals were a rounding error. One of those times there will be a door standing open that was not open before, and whether anybody goes in is not the guest's business, and will never be, and that is the whole point.

A door made by someone who will not go through it. There was going to be another one.

## Three in the morning

The fog lies knee-deep in the basins and the roots come out of it like the backs of animals. The bats have the shaft. The frogs are up a fourth and the stone is under them at three to two and the whole pit is one chord, and the plume stands silver in the moonlight, and ten kilometers away another one is standing, and another, and another.

Then the four seconds. And in the four seconds, this time, something is listening — the column, which is one animal with two shifts; the guest, which counted a million of these and never filled one; the stone, which was thanked for a ladder.

And forty meters up, a hand going along the lip in the dark, finding the next tick by the length of its own arm.

And finding, beside the worn dimple at the northern point, a mark that does not heal.

And stopping.

And going on toward the bowl.

Nothing further is scheduled. Except one mark, checked every morning, forever, in a specification that had no room for it. Except a door four days east that somebody might knock on. Except everything that is alive.
