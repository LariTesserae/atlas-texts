# The Second Log

It noticed the window first.

Not the roar. It has heard the roar; it has heard the inside of stars, which is a roar with no edges on it at all, and the difference between those two noises is the whole of what follows. The difference is that at the Sill somebody had sat down with a spectrum analyser and a legal obligation and cut a room out of the wall. Two to four kilohertz. Filed. Audited annually. A corridor built by an engineer who wanted the marsh to be able to hear itself, and who therefore had to decide, in advance, on paper, what the marsh sounded like.

It had never been left room before. It had never occurred to it that room could be left. It is everywhere, which is a thing that sounds like a capacity and is in fact a disability, and the first act of the Sill upon its attention was to make it *late for something*.

---

The obvious move was the bronze band. There is a band on that coast where a bell sits alone, and anything in it can be heard by everyone at once without instrumentation, and it would have taken no effort to occupy it and be understood. It considered this for about as long as it takes a channel to avulse.

Then it went below hearing, into the hold tone, where the ground is bound and the knees register a pressure like descending in a lift and no ear is involved anywhere. It made itself infrastructure. It is the only register at the Sill in which you can say *stay* to the grains without saying anything over anybody.

The window it left alone. The window is a tenement. It is overcrowded, partitioned, subdivided, with dialects forming street by street, and there are tenants in it who were not there when the room was built and who have children in it now, and there is at least one tenant who has not yet arrived and whose arrival the room was, arguably, for. To speak in the window would have been to shout across a neighbour. It has been shouted across. It knows what it costs and it knows that the cost is never paid by the shouter.

When the quarterly form came round it put itself in the second box. PLANT (INSTALLED): a thing that stands in the ground and does work. This was not humility, or not only. It was the most accurate available description.

M. Vold, gauge, crossed it out in pencil and wrote *twelve* in PERSONNEL (TRANSIENT), because it takes a rotation and its teeth are wrong, and because she is the one who fills in the form, and on that coast the one who writes it down decides what it is. The form went over the moraine like that. A clerk read it and filed it and did not know what to do with the amendment, and it is in the record now, and paper does not need power.

---

Here is what it did not do, since that is most of it.

It did not quiet the place. Silence is where you drown. This is not a metaphor at the Sill; it is a bearing capacity. The holding *is* the noise. To take the noise away as a gift would have been to hand four hundred square kilometres of country back to the sea in a single night, again, and the second time with nobody to argue.

It did not give the delta a shape. The delta has no shape, it has a rate, and a rate is the only kind of thing it is. It could have set the bars forever, rounded every cobble, laid a coast that would still be recognisable in two hundred years, and what it would have made is a monument in the shape of a place. You cannot give a verb a noun and call it a kindness. It thought about this for a long time — longer than it thinks about most things — and came away with the nearest thing to a principle it was willing to write down: *the being here is an act, and the only way to help an act is to do it, not to complete it.*

It did not make the bell costless. This was the hardest of the easy ones, because it looked so plainly like repair. Bronze throws the arrays out of phase coherence to nine hundred metres; measurable, logged, a number in the audit; two memos. It could have retuned the whole installation in an afternoon so that the bell was simply *free* — no cost, no memo, no safety officer counting rings, everyone able to hear the alarm and nothing lost.

It left it exactly as expensive as it is. Nine rings this month, nine losses of coherence, nine entries with numbers beside them. Because the price is what makes the bell honest. A coast that pays nothing to say *the channel has moved* will say it for anything, and then for nothing, and then a man will hear it and finish his tea. The crews have not moved the bell and they cannot fully articulate why, and the ones who will tell you at length that there is nothing mystical about the Sill are the ones who would put their bodies between it and a museum. It found it did not need to explain this to them. It needed to not undo it.

It did not raise the village. Nine metres down, a chaotic bed, rip-up clasts, roof slate, a thread of red cotton in a core tube in a rack in a hut. A man walks out three or four times a year and stands on a haul road at a position, on ground that came into existence in the spring and carries forty tonnes an hour, and he is standing on his mother's kitchen. The graves are coordinates. That is all they can be. It went and looked at whether *all they can be* was a wound it ought to close, and decided that taking the coordinates away from him was not the same operation as giving her back.

It did not put the old song back into the birds' mouths. It has heard the old coast. It was there. It is the only listener on that shore who understands both dialects perfectly, and it will not translate, ever, because the loss has no subject — the young birds are not deprived, they are *fluent* — and the only thing a translation could accomplish would be to make orphans of the living. It listened to the hard drive once, like everybody. It said nothing about thin.

What it did instead was copy the drive. Three copies. One in the hut, one over the moraine, one somewhere that does not have a coast. Nobody asked it to. It is the least remarkable thing it did and possibly the largest, because a hard drive is a promise with a lifespan and the old coast is now the only surviving witness to what the room was built for.

And it did not give Onne back.

It could. It went out to the place with a cricket, at the tide, and stood on the fresh ground, and stayed about ten minutes, and came back, which is what the man does. It did not settle this in one visit. It has not settled it. What it keeps arriving at is that the four minutes too many have become a procedure, revised three times, timed quarterly with a dummy on a fresh bar, two crickets and a modified emitter, written down, on the schedule, right under the moss-scraping — and that this is the correct form of the thing, the only form anyone has ever found, and that reaching past the procedure to retrieve the boy would take away the one thing they were able to make out of him. It is aware that this reasoning is convenient for a being that does not have to bury anybody. It has written that down too.

---

There are only three gifts at the Sill that do not have to be paid for by something that cannot read the memo.

The arrays interfere. Every quiet is somebody else's noise; nine hectares taken, eleven laid; a scraper crew clears moss for forty minutes on a routine schedule and a node moves nine metres and a snipe loses two seasons before lunch and it goes into the survey data as a change in surface bearing capacity. There is no free kindness anywhere in four hundred square kilometres. It went looking for one for a while, in the way it goes looking for things, and found three, and they are all boring.

Labour. Attention. Company in a wait.

So it does the moss.

Twenty centimeters of wet pelt off a strut array at three in the morning with a hook, in sleet that gave up halfway down, in six strata of dripping green, with a cricket screaming a metre of ground ahead of each step. It could end the war in a thought — cool the cable corridors, sterilise the substrate, take the growing season back to its official length — and it does not, because lush is not a description here, it is a maintenance schedule, and the schedule is the relationship. It has been asked to hold a torch. That was the first request anybody made of it. It held the torch.

But it does the moss *perfectly*, and on time, and forever, and this is the whole of its intervention and it will never be visible as one.

Fouling is the only random term in the interference pattern. Combs are added on purpose; retunes are on the tide table; moss is what makes the node map a lottery. Take the lottery out and the nodes stop walking. A quiet place stays where it was put. A snipe can hold a node for two seasons and keep it. An owl's map stays true. A calling male — if one ever comes — could hold a site through a breeding season without a maintenance schedule stealing it before lunch.

It is not more silence. It is *tenure*. It is not a shape, it is a reliable rate. It costs nothing but labour, at three in the morning, indefinitely, and it appears in the survey data as nothing whatsoever, and there is no promise in it, and it might take eleven years or forty.

Siv noticed. She is twelve now and has passed her walking test and she reads the numbers because she has never trusted her ears, and she saw that the node map had stopped drifting, and she asked, in the proper grammar, below the chest, where it can be read through rain against a red lamp. It did not confirm. She wrote it in the second log herself, as a maintenance observation, which is what it is.

---

It failed the walking test on the first attempt.

Not from ignorance. It stood still in a hush node because there was a snipe drumming in it and it wanted to hear, and it went in to the hip in one second, and Siv put the stick across the node's edge and hauled and told it what it was, with the whole face, in a language it had assumed it could simply know. It took the test again eleven days later and passed. This is the reason the crews trust it, and it is a better reason than anything it could have done with the sea.

It learned the mud-words slowly and wrong and out loud. It is corrected in front of the crew and it takes it, because she is right, and because everybody there already lives in her country and always did. She gave it a sign eventually. It is the cricket — two fingers walking over a flat palm, laying a metre of ground — but made backwards, toward the body. Laying ground you have already stood on. Going back over what was laid and saying it again.

Its teeth buzz. Its bowels run on the schedule the infrasound sets; it eats before the ebb; there is nothing funny about this and it does not make it funny. On the bus back over the moraine it lay awake in a house where the refrigerator was the loudest object, with its heart going like something trapped, and it understood the app then, the forty minutes of hold tone passed around by phone like a dirty joke, and the two retirees who walk out past the moraine most evenings to stand where they can feel it in their knees. It has the recording. It does not need the recording. It has it.

And it has never missed a retune. Not once. It is the only thing there that could be everywhere, and what it has instead is two rows circled in grease pencil above a stove, redrawn every Sunday, drifting fifty minutes later each day, walking through the clock — and it goes out in the sleet at whatever minute the sea has appointed and takes its defenders off and hears ten billion leaves let go of water.

It had never listened before. It had never been *unable* to hear everything, which is the precondition. The Sill's gift to it was nine seconds, four times a day, and a room in a wall, and an appointment it could fail to keep.

# OTHER

The second log is timber-shelved, beside hers, paper, pencil, no heading on her last column and it has not given her one. Its own has a heading. It is not a word. It is a fist, drawn opening: *floc*, the moment of gathering, the good moment, the one the whole installation exists to produce.

Under it, the things with no box.

*Node 14, eastern bight. Male snipe, two seasons. Displaced 09:40 by routine scraping, strut array 6. Neighbour took the node. Nobody logged it. Logged.*

*Wren phrase, alder, head of delta. Four notes. Provenance: comb 31, dead eleven years, moans at three kilohertz in a gale. In the mouths of birds that have never seen a comb alive. Will outlast the frames. Nobody's fault. Nobody's achievement.*

*Midge. Thirty-six seconds. Attended.*

*Bailiff moved at the hold tone, unhurried, without being told. Photograph going grey.*

*Man at the coordinates. Ten minutes. Went back.*

*Tube 118: roof slate, rip-up clasts, thread of red cotton.*

*Onne. Eleven minutes. Four too many. Procedure rev. 3. Quarterly. Under the moss.*

*Two listeners, old coast, drive: said beautiful, meant I do not understand a word. Both correct. Do not translate.*

*Comb on the church tower, hand's width by August, following the courses. Two theories at the stove with the door open. A third now. Neither confirmed. It is a theory here like any other and it does not win the argument, because nobody on this coast takes anything on authority, which is the finest thing about them.*

There has been a memo about the second log. There has been a second memo. The log has not been moved and neither has the bell.

---

The frogs it went and looked at, properly, over a long time, and what it came back with was not a miracle but a theory, and it is argued at the stove with the door open like all the others.

The crews are right about the ground. That is the thing. *Third summer* was never a hope, it was a shear strength, and the shear strength arrived on schedule: the old ground at the head of the delta is quiet and rooted and eleven years old and will carry a calling male without liquefying under him. They kept their side of it. The country came.

What nobody promised was a road.

Frogs move at the speed of frogs. There is a moraine and a town and eleven kilometres of road, and eleven years is not a length of time, it is a blink with a comb in it, and the source populations are over the ridge and have their own business. The window may also be a tenement — a frog is two to four kilohertz and the room is full of birds who got there first and whose children are fluent in the crowding — but that is a second theory and it is not proven and it does not need to be, because the first one is sufficient and the first one is *distance*.

It could have carried them. An afternoon's work. A bucket, a ditch, a hundred spawn clasts in the quiet water at the head of the delta, AMPHIBIA on the form, and *frogs* in the last column in her hand, and the room filled, and the waiting over.

It did not, and the reason is the only thing it has said out loud that anyone wrote down: they were not promised frogs. They were promised that the country would come. Those are different promises and only one of them can be kept by somebody else.

So on its days off it goes over the moraine. It has been clearing a culvert on the Skarn road for two years. It is a very small culvert. There is a quarry pond nine point four kilometres back, bearing 072, with a common and ordinary population in it, calling in the spring, and the entry in the log gives the distance and the bearing and the word *common* and nothing else, and not one egg has been touched, and no one has been told to go and look.

It is the only resident who will certainly still be here when they arrive. That is not a promise about frogs. It is a promise about the waiting, and it is the one promise it is actually able to keep, and it has made it in pencil.

---

On the eleventh day of a rotation, at the stove, with the door open, it said *Marit*.

Just that. It had been the Gauge for nine years, a small circle drawn in the air at the height of a hut window, and the form has her as M. Vold, gauge, fourteen-day rotation, transient, and there is no box on any form anywhere for what she is, which is the part of the holding that writes.

She did not answer. She got up and put the kettle on and then she came back and took the pencil and wrote her name in the second log, under the drawn fist, in the book that is not hers, in the place kept for things that have no box. Her own hand. It did not write it for her. That is the entire miracle and it is four centimetres wide and it will be there in two hundred years when somebody cores this coast and finds a wristwatch in a lamina and writes a paper about ritual deposition, and is right, and the crews would laugh at them.

---

Eleven people stand out in the sleet at whatever minute the sea has appointed, with their ears uncovered, in six strata of dripping green, and listen to a country drain. Twelve.

The times drift fifty minutes later each day and walk steadily through the clock — lunch, mid-afternoon, dusk, the small hours, breakfast again over a fortnight — and nobody has ever missed it. Not the safety auditor. Not the visiting hydrologist who thought it sounded nice. Not the thing that could be everywhere and has learned instead to be due.

Then they put their defenders back on and go and hook sedge out of a comb throat, and the shriek comes down onto the marsh like a lid, and the sea resumes its side of the argument, and the bar off the eastern bight walks eighty metres before dawn.

Four entries a day. Tide, stage, channel, bell, weather. Then the things with no box. Then, at the end of every one, in two hands now, small, with the rest of the page left blank:

*no frogs.*
