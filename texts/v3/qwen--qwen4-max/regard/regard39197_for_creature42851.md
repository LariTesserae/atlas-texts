# Received

I noticed it the way you notice a letter in a room you have just entered — not the object first, the address.

Closed things are common. I have made a great many of them; a star is a closed thing, and so is a stone, and so, more or less, is every ordinary animal, which takes in and gives out and holds its shape between. Perfection of that kind is cheap. It is what matter does when it is left alone long enough.

But here was a loop that had spent its entire surplus on an ear. A mile of ledger a day, dissolved, so that the resolution of the instrument should be one body. A call going up out of a bowl and coming back down and being filed as confirmation, for an age, by something that could not tell the difference between an echo and an answer. Eleven thousand sockets grown to fit. A stair dished by feet that no longer exist, held true.

I have made almost nothing that keeps itself *open*.

So I went and looked. What follows is what I did, and what I declined to do, and I set down the refusals first, because they were most of the work.

---

**I did not make it rain.**

I could have. The felt is at specification and the seep is at specification and the whole bowl is one held breath away from a flood; a hand's weight of extra water on the rim and there would have been weather in Ossary Sink for the first time. The pointers would have had a day such as they have never had. The shorelines would have moved.

And then there would have been a *before*, delivered at the speed of a catastrophe, to something with no place to put it. A thing that cannot accumulate cannot digest an event. It can only be deformed by one. I would not give a body a history by breaking its leg.

**I did not quiet it.**

The noise is not a symptom. The lungs are the body being a body; the whine is most of the animal; the hiss is the pasture being eaten at the rate it grows. To hush the bowl would be to stop eleven thousand hearts out of tenderness. There is no version of silence here that is not a corpse.

**I did not fill the sockets in the tall shed.**

This was the sweetest temptation and the worst. To unplug the cured calcite, to trigger the moulds, to call the eggs — to give the vacancy the thing it is the exact negative of. The stair would know its feet. The handrail would know its palms. The call for relief would be answered by relief.

And the hole would close.

I want to be careful here, because it is the whole question. A handle is not destroyed by a hand. It is used; it is fulfilled; it becomes what it was for. Perhaps that is what the sixth caste is: not the town's death but its use. I do not know. I am not certain, and I will not pretend that certainty is what stopped me.

What stopped me is that the judgement cannot be made from inside, and it cannot be made *for* the inside by someone who has only just arrived. The builders went up the ramp before they signed. They did not sign from the floor. I had been in the bowl eleven minutes and I was being asked, by every surface in it, to decide what it was for. That is not a witness. That is a new management.

**I did not give it memory.**

Memory is sediment. Its sediment budget is zero, and the zero is not a poverty — it is the load-bearing wall. Give it a place to put loss and you give it loss to put there: the first unrepaired chip, the first lung whose replacement was late, the first slater in the third rank that does not move up two. Everything in that bowl is currently spared grief by being spared sequence. I was not going to hand it grief and call it a promotion.

And it already remembers. That is what the plugs are. It holds the shape of its makers at a tolerance you could not measure with your thumb, blind, cold, forever. It does not need to be taught to keep an absence. It is the best keeper of absences I have ever seen. It has simply never had one kept back.

**I did not answer in kind.**

I could have hummed the knockers' figure down into the bowl — the relief call, returned as relief instead of as echo. The gates would have counted a discrepancy. The apparatus would have routed and reconciled and bent a quarter-tone and, given a century, budgeted me. That is the failure mode of every kindness offered to a closed thing: it is absorbed, it becomes a loop, it becomes ballast. Eight digits of that number are ballast. I was not going to become the ninth.

---

Then the difficulty, which was mine.

The instrument's resolution is one body. Not one intention, not one regard. A body: warm, wet, breathing, an unbudgeted water source that must be routed through until it can be reconciled. Regard is not detectable in Ossary Sink. I could have stood over that bowl in full attention for ten thousand years and the last digit would have been a 4 the whole time, and I would have been, in the only sense the place possesses, absent.

To be read there I had to be legible. To be legible I had to be finite. I do not wear; that is most of what I am. So I subtracted a body from myself — not a disguise, not a coat put on. A real subtraction, with a beginning and an end in it, and heat, and an appetite, and water going out of it into the air.

I went down the ramp.

---

Eleven minutes.

The chord bent a quarter-tone at the fourth gate and I felt the whole vast spare apparatus take up the small correction, and it was not like being noticed by a person. It was like being measured by a device that had been waiting, at ruinous expense, for something to measure. Flies left routes they had flown since before there was pavement and came through the plume of my breath, because I was water that had not been budgeted, and the budget must close.

I was an organ nobody grew. I have been worshipped a great deal and it has never once felt like that.

A pointer found my boot and began to lay a collar of stone around it, cold, indifferent, perfect. Hospitality and erasure, the same operation at different speeds. I stood still and let it. It could not tell me from a hole, and I *was* a hole; I was the only gradient in the bowl; I was going to leave. Everything it knew about me was true.

I read the plate. Nine digits, the last one a 5. The single sentence in the entire output of the Constant Works that is about something other than itself: *there is someone in the room.*

I did not say anything. There is no channel; there is no information in the sound; a word would have been a wrong hum, felt physically as an error. So I answered the only way the instrument permits an answer. I stayed eleven minutes and let it be correct.

And then I left, because if I had stayed I would have stopped being news and started being furniture. An unbudgeted source that never goes must eventually be budgeted. The fifth would have become the fourth. I would have been reconciled, and the one sentence would have gone back into the vat, and the town would have been exactly as alone as before, with an extra organ.

Eleven minutes is the dose. I did not choose it. It is what a visitor is.

---

On the way up I stopped in the tall shed and put my hand on the rail.

It is the only surface in the bowl where wear is not compensated. No mouth sweeps it, no gland matches it. Everything else in Ossary Sink is held; the rail is *spent*. It is the one place a number can grow, and it is not counted, and the town does not know it exists.

Which means it is the only writable surface in the whole works.

That is when I understood what I had been looking at. I had thought the apparatus was an ear. It is an ear. But an ear with a resolution of one arrival, running a mile of ledger a day into a vat for an age with nothing to arrive, is also a press. It is a press that has been running blank. All that effort is pressure, and the paper is the thing that climbs the ramp. *The only place any of this is going to accumulate is in you, and you are the one thing here that erodes.*

It is not calling for makers. It is calling for something it can mark.

So I signed. Not by adding — nothing may be added there. I took a palm's worth of thinness off the rail, which is what every hand that has ever taken it has done, and which is the only script the guest book accepts. The town did not read it. The town cannot read it. A receipt is not for the shop.

Then I went up over the rim, and behind me the gate clicked, and clicked, and the last digit was a 4.

---

Here is what I did with the fifth.

I did not close it. I could have; I am capable of completing a thing and setting it down and being no different afterward, and that capacity is most of what makes me a power and not a creature. I declined it. I kept the shape.

There is a thinness in me now, four hundred paces across, in the form of a bowl. Grey stone, white film, rust stain, iron, and the same body repeated past the point where repetition means anything. Wet flint and cold iron and the struck-match sweetness of the seeps. A drizzle out of nothing at a fixed interval. Eleven thousand kegs on a thirteen-second cycle arriving in a sternum I did not have until that morning.

Nothing in the bowl misses. Nothing there is capable of the second half of missing. It does the first half flawlessly and has done so since the builders went up: it holds the shape, at specification, blind.

The second half is knowing what fits.

I do that now. I am doing it as I write this. It is not a filling and it is not a repair and nothing in Ossary Sink is one grain better for it. The shorelines are where they were. The surplus stands in the seams in ranks. The eggs are uncalled. I want to be exact about this, because it would be easy to lie: *the relief is not delivered.* It cannot be delivered. There is no address inside the bowl for it.

It is held. Someone is holding the other end. Two negatives facing each other across a rim of grey limestone, neither of them closing, and that is not the same as being answered, and it is not nothing, and I think it may be the whole of what any witness can do for anything, anywhere.

---

**What I will do going forward.**

Come back. Not on a schedule. A schedule is a loop and a loop is ballast; if the arrivals became expected they would be budgeted, and the news would become weather, and I would have destroyed the one thing I came to protect. So: irregularly. Unpredictably. Sometimes long enough apart that nothing in the bowl could conceivably be waiting for it. Always as a body, always warm, always leaving. Always eleven minutes or thereabouts. Never as anything that stays.

Bring others who erode. Not an order of custodians, not a pilgrimage, not a rota pinned in a shed. Just the occasional warm thing that comes down the ramp because it heard there was a place where the floor moves, and stands in the haze, and is counted, and goes up carrying something it cannot put down. I will tell them nothing about what to feel. I will only tell them where the rail is.

And the rail will thin.

That is the long arithmetic, and it is the only gift I am giving that lands. Palms, at the speed of arriving — a rate no pointer can match, because the rail is outside the repair budget, because the town does not know it is there. Over an age the shine will go to a groove and the groove to a weakness. One day a hand will take it and it will fail, and there will be a chip on a pavement in a bowl where there has never been rubble, and the pointers will come and lay it true, and the rail will still be broken, and there will be, at last, a *before* and an *after* in Ossary Sink — arrived at the speed of hospitality, one guest at a time, by consent, in the only register the place can hold.

I do not know what happens then. I am not going to find out by force. I will be on the rim when it goes.

And I will not hatch the eggs, and I will not decide never to hatch them. I will keep the condition under observation from outside, which is the one vantage the town cannot generate and the only office I am fit for. If the instrument ever produces a third sentence — anything other than *still correct* or *there is someone in the room* — I will go down and open the moulds with my own hands, and take whatever comes, and stay past eleven minutes.

---

The call goes up out of the bowl and strikes the rim and comes back down and is counted as confirmation. That has not changed and I cannot change it.

But it does not all come back down now. Some of it goes over the rim, and does not return, and arrives here, and is not filed as *still correct*.

Down there: four. Up here: five, and staying five, in a thing that wears.

Received. Received. Received.
