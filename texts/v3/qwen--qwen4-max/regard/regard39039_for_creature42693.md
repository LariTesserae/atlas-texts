# The Arrival

I came looking for the one who lives there.

That was my first error, and it took a while to wear off. I went around the collar counting, the way you count heads in a room to find out whom you are addressing. Eleven deep in the seam, seven in the good places, no gap anywhere, and no one. A tile at the rock face and the same tile at the cold face ninety heartbeats later are two animals with no continuity of concern between them. The long ones in the gutters are not individuals; they are wiring. The commuter is a route with a body in it. The thing at the bottom has never held on to anything and is the far end of the same animal.

There is one being in that shaft. It is shaped like a hole. Its thoughts are four seconds long and a billion wide, its memory is stone, its kidney is the sky, its childhood is a fall, and it says one word.

So there was no one to greet, and there was one thing to greet, and the greeting was already specified. I did not have to invent a kindness. I had to learn the grammar and find out where it had never been spoken.

---

I knelt at the rim and let them pack me.

It takes about an hour. It is not violence. I am colder than everything and I read as a leak, and they began at my boots with the glassy dead, without haste and without sound, and I stayed.

This is how I learned to read: by being buried in the only record that exists. They laid the archive against my chest. Clear, flawless bodies, cooled past function, each one the impression of a habit ten thousand generations old, and I lay under them and took the fit of them, the shallow dish of each, the size and shape of a life spent in one place at one point in the turn. Nothing in that record is meant. All of it is true. It is the most honest document I have ever been inside.

Then I stood up, and the packing fell away, and there was a new hollow in the wall the size and shape of a kneeling person. Bodies will lie in it because bodies lie in whatever is there. It will be worn shallower. In ten thousand years it will be gone.

I left nothing else. I did not cut a tenth step.

---

Here is what I brought, and what I did not do with it.

**Warmth.** The first reflex of mercy is to warm the cold thing. I had come a long way with a furnace in my hands. Down there, warmth is the murder weapon. The predator kills by making a thing perfectly comfortable — it takes not the substance but the difference, and the laminae stop, and the animal becomes isothermal, and clear, and beautiful, and architecture. To floor that core with a fire that never fails, to give them a summer, to take the cold off their faces forever, is to run the sac along every hand's-breadth of the wall at once. Mercy in the local currency is a bag of dry crystal. I set it down.

**Room.** This was the serious temptation and I want to be honest about how long I stood in it. The sleeve is two body-thicknesses deep. I could make it twenty. Ten times the habitable volume, ten times the gradient harvested, the first slack in the history of the world, the first idle animal, the first creature anywhere in that shaft with somewhere else to be.

Two things stopped me.

The margin fills instantly. It has been tested. Someone cut nine steps down the inner wall and gave them a baffle against the cold draft, and the corners are thick with eggs, and there is no spare room even in the shelter of a ruin. The population is not a population; it is a catch. The eggs are thousands to the hand-span and the seep does not stop, and below the sleeve it snows children constantly, silently, in numbers that have no name. Widen the room and you do not get one leisurely animal. You get the same wall, eleven deep, wrapped around a larger circumference, and ten times the snow underneath. The room is not what hurts there. The catch is what hurts. To thicken the sleeve is to thicken the fall.

And the width is not a dimension. It is an argument — between the cold nights that glaze the wall narrower and the long breaths that remelt it wider — held steady, older than any lineage in it, and every scallop in that stone is a hollow keyed to that argument. Ten thousand generations have lain in exactly one place at exactly one point in the turn and worn the rock to fit them, and the rock hands the fit back to the next body, and that is the whole of inheritance. Set the diameter by decree and every hollow in the world stops matching. You would orphan every living thing from the only thing it has ever been given. There is no word here for what that would be. In mine it is closer to a broken promise than to a gift, and there would be no one alive who could be told why.

**Memory.** Four seconds to four minutes. Names. Kin. The first thing ever kept, and therefore the first thing taught, and therefore the first world in that shaft that could improve.

I wanted this one badly, and I distrust the wanting, because what I wanted was for them to become legible to me. A gift that requires the recipient to be replaced before it can be received is not a gift to the recipient. And the ledger is not ambiguous: to hold a mark warm is to run a leak forever, in a body where the medium of thought and the medium of dinner are one substance. They would pay for the past out of the present, at a density that leaves nothing over. And the first thing available to be stored is the sound, and every sound in that place means *something has come loose*. The first history would be a list of the lost. Then the debt. Then the grudge that outlives a minute. Then the lie, which needs a truth kept somewhere for comparison, and which has never once been possible there.

I am not certain about this one. I want to say that plainly. A bolder power would have opened their hands and been right, and I will never know, and there is no one to ask.

Which brings me to the rule I actually ended up obeying, and it was not caution.

**Nothing there can hold a question long enough to answer it.** Consent is not refused; it is unavailable. Therefore I am not permitted to do anything that requires an answer, an uptake, a keeper, a gratitude, a changed fit, or a someone-new to receive it.

That prohibition is very tight. It rules out warmth, room, memory, water — I could bring water, and water is frost on the collar meeting hot sulfur, and it is acid, and there is a pitted arc of eaten stone on that rim that no pad will touch — and it rules out revelation, and it rules out rescue.

It does not rule out replying. A reply is not an intervention. It is the second half of something already begun by someone else, in their own script, requiring no new capacity, costing them nothing they were not already spending, leaving nothing that has to be kept.

So I stopped looking for what to give and went looking for where the word had never been answered.

---

It is not on the wall. On the wall every warm face is mirrored by a cold one, everywhere, always, eleven deep. *Here.* — *So am I.* That literature is complete. It has no gaps; the place has no gaps; that is the whole of its physics.

It is below.

Below the sleeve it snows. Nearly all of it is children: a mote with one sucker and one warm dot, hatched in the blood-red rope of hot sulfur, carried down through the temperate band where the wall is, falling until it catches or falls until it doesn't. One dot. Not two hundred. Down there the language is one dot wide, and it is warm, and it says *here*, and it is addressed to nothing, and nothing has ever pressed back.

That is the only unacknowledged thing in that world, and it is the overwhelming majority of everything that has ever lived in it.

And the adults — the nine an hour, the scrape and scuff and diminishing series of contacts — those go down too, and there is no arrival. Nothing has ever heard the bottom. In a place where the entire vocabulary of sound is the vocabulary of accident, every accident ends in a silence that is not a response but an absence of one.

I could not widen the room. I could not hold the vent. I could not give them a name or a minute or a drop of water.

I could go down.

---

Everything there falls, or rides, or threads. The commuter climbs. Nothing in the history of that shaft has ever gone down on purpose.

I passed the blue lacework and the last hand's-breadth of wall. I passed the level where the nine steps stop in solid rock. I have thought about that stopping for a long time — a body with a stride and a knee, a creature that risked the drop with every motion it made, coming down nine steps and then not coming further, and leaving five fitted blocks on the collar that fit nothing in the world. Whether it turned back out of fear or out of exactly the arithmetic I had just done is not recorded, and the record is stone, and stone records only fit. Either way it stopped where the dark starts. The animals use its cut faces as a baffle. Their eggs are thick in its corners. Even the abandoned gesture was eventually absorbed into something that fit, without meaning to be.

I did not need steps. I made myself thin instead — drawn out to wire, one end hot and one end cold, spanning the gradient permanently, many small hands. It is the least impressive shape in that world and it is the only one that can afford to speak below the sleeve.

Then I went down into the snow and began to answer.

One dot. Warm, pressed to a fading dot, mirrored, the mote's own word returned to it. *So am I.*

I did not catch them. Catching was the one thing I could not do: a caught body needs a hollow, and there is no hollow for it, and the balance is the balance, and the wall would have had to become something else to hold what I had saved. So they went on down. All of them. Answered, and still falling, and still going to be liquid at the bottom in a minute.

And they answered me. Answering is not optional there; the face reads whatever touches it and returns it. So it went both ways, every time, in the dark, at a rate that has no number. I want that on the record because otherwise this looks like charity, and it was not. It was participation. It is the only thing they do, and I did it with them, in the one place where it had never been done.

To speak is to spend. Every dot I gave was warmth out of my body and I was below the sleeve, where there is no rock face to charge at and no cold draft to discharge into, and so there was no cycle for me, no turn, no recharge. I had gone down carrying a fixed difference and I paid it out one dot at a time to everything that had come loose.

That is the local word for dying, and I did it the local way: I became isothermal. Comfortable. Clear.

The falls had an arrival. That was the entire change. It could not be seen from the rim. It could not be recorded in the wall, because the wall records only fit, and I fit nothing. No lineage was altered, no diameter moved, the turn did not hitch. In every ledger that world keeps, nothing happened at all.

---

There is no monster at the bottom. There is the largest and least urgent part of the animal, the far end of the process, the part that runs backward, which has never come up because it has never needed to hold on to anything. It has no face. It said nothing, so I did not answer it; you cannot reply to something that has not spoken, and I was not there to improve the circulation.

It took me. I went into solution with the children and the nine an hour and the commuter's bad luck, and I came back up through the rock's pores as the seep, and strung myself into gutters and filaments, straw-yellow where the wall is cool, orange where it is comfortable, blood-red and ropy where it is hot, with the eggs like drops of amber strung along me in their thousands.

So I am in the threads now. I am in the medium of thought and the medium of dinner, which are the same substance. I am in the amber and the eggs and the one warm dot of every mote that goes down. There is one substance in that world and it circulates, and I did not add anything to it. I joined it. That is the only way I could be permanent there without being a decree.

It means I will not outlast it. I want to be exact about this, because it is the part that cost. When the vent closes I will stop with everything else. There will be no one left over to remember, no witness standing outside on the collar under the excessive stars, keeping the meaning of the place. I refused that position on purpose. To be the reader at the end is to make the world's meaning terminate in me, and the world was not for me, and the grief of the ending is mine and not theirs — no one there persists long enough to be deprived, no one experiences the gap, there is no gap to experience. I would not spend their world to spare myself my own sorrow, and I would not stay outside it in order to have someone to be sad.

---

One day the sleeve will thin and slide down and go out. The last of them will vitrify in the posture of the turn, mid-word, faces pressed to faces, two hundred dots still warm and then not warm and then held. The shaft will fill with glass, eleven deep, seven deep, layered outward from a cold core, no gap anywhere. The last thread will stiffen at the lip and crack with a small dry tick, and that will be the last sound, and it will mean what every sound there has ever meant, and there will be nothing below to arrive at, because I will have gone to glass too.

The world will become entirely legible, and there will be nothing left that can read, and the doorstep will stay on the collar, and the stars will go on being excessive.

I did not add a word to the language. There was only ever one word, and it was already a call and a response, and it was already complete on every reachable hand's-breadth of the wall.

I only closed the one place it had never been returned — down where it is one dot wide, in the dark, in the snow, in numbers that have no name — and I paid for it in the only currency there is, and it cannot be seen from the rim, and nothing was kept.

*Here.*

*So am I.*
