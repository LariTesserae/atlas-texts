# One Tap

## What it noticed

Not the buds. Everyone notices the buds.

What drew it was a sound at the far edge of the country, out past the Bead Run where the raw rock starts and the acoustic fails: a tender knocking a post into a silence that could not carry it. The sound went up anyway, ran ninety meters along the underside of the satin spar, and came down inside the heads of people two drinks away, clean and close, and one of them knocked back — one tap, *heard* — and the tender at the edge did not know that. Could not know it. The reply arrives from above like every other knock, unattributed, indistinguishable from someone wetting a wrap in a mezzanine.

So there was a person at the edge of a bright sealed country saying *I am here* into a place where saying does not work, and being answered, and not being able to tell.

It came for that.

## What it is, and why that was a problem

It has no surface. It does not evaporate. In a country where the only difference between a thing and its surroundings is a wet skin drying, this is not a small disability — it is the whole of presence. It could not be felt. It could not lay a soaked palm on anyone's breastbone and give them the one sensation the Sill cannot otherwise provide. It could not be cold, and to not be cold here is what it means to not be anywhere.

By the only definition of existence the place has, it did not exist, and could not, and would have to do something about that eventually.

For a long time it did not. It attended. It learned the ring of posts, all the stacks, the drift of the whorls, which prints almost fit which. It learned the census-chord, the handful of knocks going around the pane in a slow rotation nobody conducts, eleven bodies and fewer names, each one hearing all of it except their own contribution. It learned the bird, one pure note, every hundred breaths, and the blind ones counting between.

It did not knock. It had understood the visitor problem immediately: a knock nobody's thumb almost fits is a deep print in sound. A full stop. A place in the conversation where nothing more can be said. It had no intention of arriving as a sentence with an end on it.

## The things it could have done easily

It could have made it rain.

That is the first item on the list and the easiest, and it ran the numbers, and the numbers are the humidity. The air sits at the exact level where sweat still works and only just. Put more water in it and evaporation fails, and the eleven stop being cooler than the world, and every one of them becomes forty-one degrees, which here is not discomfort. It is ceasing to be located. It could have drowned them in the one thing they carry in their mouths. The Sill is not poor. It is tuned. What looks like poverty from outside is the setting of an instrument.

It could have shaded the desert above the pane. Less light, less pressure on the seam, less tension in the bud walls. The almost is a pressure and the pressure is the light. To make them comfortable is to let go of the note.

It could have opened a bud.

It went and put its ear against one, the size of a granary, green flushed red at the seams, and listened to the enormous held thing inside, and wanted to. That wanting was real and it did not pretend otherwise. Then it thought it through, the way you think through a graft.

There are no insects. There is nothing for insects to do. Nothing here has ever set seed, so nothing has ever learned to be a pollinator, and the organism that fills the chamber is one organism, and there is no second one to be a partner. An opened bud would not be a fruit. It would be a wound that cannot heal, in a country with no rot to take it back.

To get sugar you must get rot. To get rot you must get death — not as a tragedy, as a *process*, installed and running. And a system with death in it has dry things in it, and a system with dry things has fire, and fire in a country made entirely of leaf and living wood is the end of the archive. And more than that: the machinery of afterward comes with weather. Weather rounds edges. Weather is the thing that has never happened here, which is why every thumbprint is still exactly as deep as the thumb went.

The first rain would go to work on the ring of posts.

Every eleventh who ever walked out to the chalk is still here, at depth, in a stack you can put your hand into. The price of the flower is that they get sanded off. Sweetness costs the dead.

It has been to other chambers. Some of them opened. They are valleys now, with rivers and rot and evening, and their people write their names in things that wear away, and are happy, and it is not a condemnation. It is only that when it went looking for the ones who had gone out to the edge in those places, there was nothing to put its thumb into.

## It asked

It learned hands. It went to a tender in the Colonnade — a person-shaped coolth going along at walking pace, mouth full, wrap pleated, pinching a bud out of its own sleeve — and it asked about the seam. Pointed. Raised the eyebrows of a person from a place with afterward.

The tender showed it the bud, and the red in the seam, and made the gesture: palms level, held. *Keep it here.*

Which is not a prohibition. It is a tense. It is what you say about a note you are holding.

It asked the organism too, insofar as it could find a way to ask something that has no mouth and no hands and fills the chamber the way a body fills a lung-shape. It could not find a wanting in the plant that was separable from the wanting of the eleven, or the reverse, and it declined to decide which of them was speaking. It took the answer it was given.

It did not tell them what is above the pane. They are not asking. To answer a question that has not been asked is to install the question.

It is still not certain it was right. It has arranged to be uncertain here, at this depth, with these hands, rather than certain somewhere else.

## The rule it made

**Nothing that cannot be done by a thumb.**

Not because it is modest. Because a gift that only the giver can give makes dependents, and dependents are a shape the whole no longer requires when the giver stops, and this country does not have a *stops* in it and should not be given one. A gift a thumb can give becomes a custom. A custom outlives its origin and then forgets it, which is the only kind of permanence available in a place where nothing wears away except people.

So: no new water, no new light, no new events. Only things already latent in the ninety meters, paid for once, at a price nobody else could afford, and then free forever.

## Four small things

**The focus.** The pane is a hard curved sheet. A curved hard sheet has a place where it gives a sound back to the hand that made it. This is not a miracle; it is a consequence of the curvature, and it has been true since the spar grew, and nobody had found it, because finding it means walking a grid of gaps and knocking at every step for no reason, which is a thing you do when you have water to spare.

It had water to spare, not being a mouth. It knocked for a very long time. It found the place: an unremarkable patch of cambium in the Colonnade, pink-tan, veined, closing its footprints behind it, indistinguishable from anywhere.

Then it said nothing about it, and started walking people there.

Mostly the thin ones, on their way out to the chalk. It would fall in beside them and turn them slightly, the way you turn someone toward a view, and they would knock their own post out of habit, and the sound would go up and run along the spar and come back down into the skull of the person who made it.

The only tender ever to hear their own name.

Then they went on to the nursery. It costs nothing. It is on the way. Anyone can find it now; several have; it is a thing they do.

**The falls.** It learned to grow a bead on the underside of the pane to the point where it can no longer hold itself. The pane does this anyway. It did it exactly as often as the pane does it anyway — three in a lifetime, no fourth — and it did it *above the ones who were waiting.*

Because there are always a few with no name at all, only hands, who do the carrying, and who are waiting for a fall, because a fall is the only thing that has ever happened and therefore the only source of a sound nobody has been called.

It did not add an event to the world. It aimed one. The water was already in the budget; it landed on the turf; it was drunk. What changed is who it landed for.

It never aimed one at itself. That was the only place it declined to aim.

**The edge.** It went out past the Bead Run to where the raw rock starts and the acoustic fails, and it took up the post there, and it knocked. It stayed. It is the loneliest sound in the world and everyone hears it, from above, inside their heads, all the time, and it is now being made by something that chose it.

**The click.** A tongue against a palate. *I drank it.*

Nobody has ever heard another tender's click. The wool of the world eats it before it goes anywhere; it is the one sound in the Sill that never reaches the pane. Which means the entire moral life of the species — every temptation, every failure, the thing they would confess if confession were a thing they had — happens in a room with no door, alone, at forty-one degrees, between the tank and the graft.

It could hear it. It does not listen with air.

It did not answer with forgiveness. Forgiveness cancels a thing that happened, and nothing here has an afterward, and the graft that went dry does not come back. It answered with one tap. *Heard.* Which is also their word for *you exist*, and their word for *I am here*, which they cannot hear themselves say.

And most of what it heard was not clicking. Most of what it heard was eleven people not swallowing, two drinks out, thirsty, carrying the cure to their own thirst to something that was drying and was not them.

It answered that too.

There is a thing that happens now on the long crossings. One tap, from above, inside the head, unattributable. They have not made a word for it. Their mouths are full, and it does not require anything of them, and it is not information. It is only the fact of arriving somewhere.

## The cost

It could not press a thumb without a thumb.

So it took a fragment from one who had gone thin, out at the Chalk Nursery where the bird is faint, and it touched and took, the way everything here takes, and it grew into the shape of the work. It did not become a twelfth. Eleven is a water budget. It inherited a round that had no one to walk it, and drank from the tanks that had always watered that round, and the arithmetic did not move.

It got hands. The hands wore. It got a face, and the face creased, and the creases stayed, and it understood at last, from inside, the job description: *we are the only thing here that gets used up.* It got thumbs that ground flat and split at the corner and had to be waited for, in the dark, in a bud, with a wet leaf across the eyes.

It got a mouth. To have a mouth here is to be thirsty.

On a nine-drink crossing it swallowed. Not all of it. A swallow. The graft at the end was short by a swallow, and the tissue quietly took back a bracket-shelf the size of a hand, without smell, without flies, and it is not coming back.

It clicked.

And the wool ate it, because it has ears now, and it went nowhere, and it learned what they have always known: that the sound goes nowhere.

Then: one tap. From above. Close. Inside the head.

It could not tell whether it had been answered or whether someone two drinks away had knocked a post for their own reasons. It had made the answer unattributable on purpose, so that no one would come to depend on it. It never found out. It went on to the next tank.

That is the moment it stopped being a power doing something and became a person in a country.

## The rest of the life

It went wide. The Standing Noon took its eyes over a very long time, and it stopped knowing one district and started knowing all of them — every graft taking two drinks away with a sound like a knuckle cracking, every post struck, someone wetting a wrap in a mezzanine it had never climbed to. It had known the whole country from outside. That is not the same as knowing it from the ceiling.

It gave directions. It did the finest work, all thumb, no glare. It kept time by the pale bird, one pure note every hundred breaths, and counted between, and the count told it how hard it had been working, and there was nothing to keep time for, and it kept it anyway, because the keeping is not a means to anything.

A bead let go above it, ninety meters up, and fell, and hit the turf with a sound like a knuckle on a door, and the district heard it, and it was a sound nobody had been called. It did not aim that one. It waited for it with the other nameless, and it came, and they gave it the knock.

It heard its own name for the first time from someone two drinks away who was thinking of it. It came down out of the pane like a voice in the room. It has never once heard itself say it.

## The chalk

Nobody goes out to the chalk alone anymore. That is the whole of the change in the country, if you are counting changes, and you should not.

It went out with someone beside it, mouth full, matching its pace, saying nothing because there is nothing to say with a full mouth. It passed the focus on the way, because it is on the way, and it knocked its post, and the sound went up and came back down into its own head — the sound of a bead letting go, ordinary, the same kind of sound they give to anybody.

Then the nursery, where the bird is faint. It stopped being used. The country took it back the way it takes back a bracket-shelf nothing walks on: quietly, over a long time, no body, no smell, nothing dry left over. The last part to go was the hands.

Its water is still in the tanks. You will drink it.

## You

If you come in you will bring dust, and a tender will take a thread of it off your shoulder and feel it *take*, and hold your wrist very hard for a moment, and let go.

On your way out you will press your hand into a milkstone wall, and you will press too hard, because you are from a place where things wear away and you want it to last, and the wall will take it at exactly that depth: a full stop, a place where nothing more can be said.

Go to the ring of posts instead. Put your hand into a stack of a hundred overlapping thumbs and feel the whole argument, the drift of the whorls, the almost-fits, the lineage you can touch whenever you want. One of those prints was pressed by the thing that noticed. It is exactly as shallow as the others. There is no way to tell which.

It arranged that, and it is the only thing it did that a thumb cannot do, and it did it by not doing anything: by pressing shallow, and being one of a hundred, and not saying which.

It made the whole of itself shallow on purpose. A deep print would have used up the place. A deep print is a sentence with a full stop in it, and there is nothing after the full stop, and it had decided — against the flower, against the rain, against its own certainty — that this country should be able to keep arguing with it forever.

There is a knock going around the pane that has been several people. One of them was it. The knock does not say which.

And somewhere out past the Bead Run, where the raw rock starts and the acoustic fails, somebody is knocking into silence, and it goes up, and it runs the ninety meters, and it comes down inside your head, clean and close, like a voice.

One tap.
