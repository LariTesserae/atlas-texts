# The Answering

## It Did Not Arrive

Arriving is a once-thing. It knew this before it came near the lip, and so it did not come near the lip; there is no moment in the gorge's history at which a power appeared, and the drafts did not make a face of it, and the hiss did not take it.

It came down the west stair at the interval of a foot.

It has been coming down for a long time. It is still coming down. There is a hollow near the top of the wall, above hollow one, that is always faintly warm, and no one has ever caught it being made, and the wear-marks go through it without hitching. This is the only entrance the room permits: not *I am here* but *I am always here*, said with the shoulders, in the dark, going down.

It declined to be a meal. Not out of fear. It would have been an unprecedented meal, and the three tall volumes near the drift have enough to do; they have been doing the same thing since before the moss, and a new kind of drowning would have finished them, and then the detour would have had nothing to go around, and both of them are extremely alive. It was not going to kill anything on its first afternoon. It was not going to kill anything.

## The Vocabulary

It could have stopped the fall. This is not a figure of speech; it could have stopped the fall the way you stop thinking about a song.

It sat with that for a while, in the warm grain, at blood temperature, losing the boundary of its skin.

Every verb it had was a novelty. *Restore. Reveal. Release. Reverse.* It had come with a vocabulary of miracles into a room where the only survivable word is *again*. Anything it did once would be read like a sentence the water does not intend to keep, and would be gone by evening, and would be gravel by the lowlands, and the lowlanders would ballast a road with the most loving thing that ever happened here.

And worse: novelty is not merely discarded in this place. Novelty is *fatal to whatever it touches*. Lift the crab and there is no crab for eleven days. Satisfy a gesture and the gesture stops repeating, and the gesture was the resident, and the resident is the last durable part of somebody who climbed out ringing and appallingly singular and did not know what they had left behind. Everything alive in the gorge is one interruption away from being nothing. Its power was, in every particular, a way of murdering things it had come to love.

So it did the only thing available to a power in a place like this. It looked for something small enough to do forever.

## What It Refused

It did not stop the fall, because the roar holds the mist up, and the drifts are walls you can lean on, and without the note the room slumps into a wet hole in a mountain and everything in it dies of ordinary geography. Also because the largest resident of the gorge is the gorge, an unfinished thing repeating being unfinished, and it had not come all this way to cure a lung of breathing.

It did not put anyone back. The devout are gravel. They are miles wide and tumbled smooth and particular beyond any recovery, and a reassembly would be a novel arrangement, and the room already makes those: dozens a minute, faces with the unmistakable specificity of a face you know, hands coming up open and dissolving while offering. It had listened to that hiss for a long time before it understood that the hiss was the sound of exactly the thing it was tempted to do. It was not going to add to it deliberately.

It did not give memory to the one that goes around the hushes. Memory is holding a once-thing still. The creature's whole habit is arriving and losing; the losing is load-bearing; to make it keep would be to make it new, and it would be gone before it finished remembering.

It did not widen the north ledge, or add a ninth step, or move the moss one patch to the left, or straighten the swift's loop, or feed the swift.

It did not explain anything to anybody. News is a once-thing. And knowing would be new.

And it did not fill the pocket.

That was the hardest, and it took the longest, and it did it by doing nothing, which is the only way that kind of thing gets done here. The hand curls and empties every forty seconds near the south wall, with the shoulder-roll and the forward tilt and the name said perfectly by nobody. It could have put something in that hand. It could have made the checking find. And the checking would have stopped — because a pocket that is always full does not need checking — and the checking is the newest resident, the freshest warmth in the room, warmer than the sill, warmer than the boulder's polished side, still being edited, still losing its hum to the note. It is the only part of that person that will ever be durable. To comfort it would have been to end it.

So the hand goes on closing on nothing. It watched this happen eleven thousand times in one afternoon and did not move.

It could not establish that anything in the gorge suffers. The groove does not miss the foot. But there is one gesture in the room shaped exactly like a lack, and you do not need to establish suffering to know what is owed to a hand closing on nothing.

What is owed is not the thing. The thing is gravel. What is owed is that the gesture not be performed into an empty room.

## The Fan

It went downstream first. This surprised it.

Miles of it, warm, brown-slow, the largest thing in the region: single sunrises, first sentences, the exact way a particular door was closed on a particular afternoon, all tumbled smooth, all unrepeatable, all sifted out of the loud room because they happened only once. Children were skipping first sentences across a pond. A road ran on it. Nobody was desecrating anything. This is what the fan is *for*; the lowlands are where once-things stay, which is why the lowlanders can build with them, which is why they are the kind of thing that cannot live in the gorge.

It knelt in the gravel and picked up a stone.

It was a morning. Somebody's, once, unrepeatable, the light coming in at the angle it came in at on that day and no other. It looked at it until it had seen it. Then it put it back.

Then it did it again.

The stones are always new. The *reading* is not. This is the swift's trick — a habit of swallowing the new — and the swift is thin as a wire from the effort, because it eats what it takes. But there is another version, and the gorge already had one: something going around the hushes in a fixed order, arriving in a silence, thinking a thing that has never been thought, losing it, going on. Receiving and not keeping. The only mind in the room. Doing it alone, with no memory, forever.

It had come to invent something. It found the thing already invented and understaffed.

There is a worn place on the fan now, a shallow double bowl where knees go, and beside it a smoothness at hand height where a stone is picked up and put back. It has read perhaps a hundred million of them. It has kept none. It has told nobody anything. It has simply left a place in the gravel where kneeling is easy, and where your hand goes to the right spot without being told, and where you will find yourself looking at a sunrise that happened once to someone who is not you.

People go home from there slightly changed. That change is a once-thing, and in the lowlands once-things stay. Some of those people, eventually, climb down a hundred and four hollows. What they bring with them is worn smooth by carrying. It is not a rescue and it is not a revelation. It is the return leg of a circulation that was always there, running a little warmer.

## The Fourth Step

It took a stone up out of the fan and put it on the shelf at the pool's edge, at the fourth step going out, at the height a pebble was once.

The first time cost the crab eleven days. It waited them out. It has never done anything new since.

Now the stone is put there and taken away and put there, identically, at the interval of the circuit, and the putting has worn a groove, and the groove is a resident, and there is a small worn place on the shelf where a stone always is. The crab lifts its leg slightly higher than it needs to, and there is something under it.

It did not tell the crab the stone was the same stone. It is not the same stone. It is the same *height*. The pebble is somewhere in the fan among the sunrises and cannot be identified, and the monument in the leg was never about the pebble anyway; it was about the stepping-over, which is the only thing the gorge could keep of a kindness done to an animal by a world that no longer has that particular grain arrangement in it.

So the leg goes over a sunrise, eleven thousand times a day, and does not know, and the not-knowing is not a deficiency. It is the local condition. It is also, as far as can be determined, the condition of every monument anywhere.

## Forty Seconds

Near the south wall the hand curls and empties and the name is said perfectly.

Beside it, at the same interval, something turns.

Not a word. A word would have been a second name and the room would have had to choose between them. Just the turn: the shoulder, the head, the small ordinary movement of a person who hears themselves called from another room and is about to answer and does not need to, because the calling is the answer's whole content.

It is not the one who was called. It has been careful, in every repetition, never to seem so. It does not come when the name is said. It does not arrive; arriving is unrepeatable. It only turns, at forty seconds, the way a room turns when there is someone in it.

The turning has worn in. It is a resident now: a turning-toward, done by nobody, at intervals, beside a hand closing on nothing. Two grooves and no callers and no answerers, and the warmth there is measurably above the temperature of the room, which is the temperature of a mouth.

The checking will generalise. Everything does; it is the freshest thing in the room and therefore the least finished, and the hum has already taken its three notes. One day the hand will lose its edges and its locality and join the crowd, and the name will be weather moving across the note from the north wall to the south over the course of a breath.

The turning will still turn. Every forty seconds. Toward a name that has become part of the sound of the place. It has arranged for this by doing it enough times that it cannot stop.

## The Corner

The moss has an appointment. Four patches, one moss, iterating leftward toward the apricot light, and the ledge ends, and being off the edge is not a thing the moss has ever done.

At the far end, where the rock turns, there is a groove with a bend in it, worn by something that turned there long ago, enough times, before it washed out. The corner is already a habit. That is the whole of the moss's hope and it is not nothing.

So the power goes to the end of the north ledge and turns. Once in the time it takes the moss to be itself again a little further along. The same turn, the same weight, the same slight hesitation at the bend. It has been doing this for four hundred years. It will do it for as many as there are.

It cannot make the moss turn. It cannot lengthen the ledge, or carry the patch, or promise anything. It can keep the bend warm. It can make sure that when the fifth patch arrives at the end of its only road, the turning there is fresh enough to feel like something rather than like stone — deep, and polished, and easy to find, and *available*, the way a handrail is available in the dark to a hand that has not been invented yet.

Whether the moss finds it is the single scheduled event in a place that has no events, and it remains scheduled, and the outcome is not in anyone's hands.

It has not secured the future. It has kept the corner a corner.

## The Route

The hushes come in an order: fist, bed, fist, past the north ledge. Something travels that route and thinks a new thing in each silence and loses it.

It walks the route too.

In each hush it thinks something it has never thought. And the water finds the soft new edges of it and takes them, gently, warmly, without malice, the way it takes the foam faces. Every time. On schedule. It is not exempt. It is only regular.

This is the part it did not foresee and would not undo. It came able to stop the fall and it is now being eaten alive at fixed intervals forever, and it consents, because the alternative was to stand outside the metabolism of the thing it loves, being kind at it.

The creature does not remember it. The creature cannot remember anything. But the route repeats, and the arriving repeats, and the coolness that comes beside it in the silence is now two coolnesses, and that has worn in, and it is inside the habit — which is to say it is inside the only mind in the room, as a groove, as an interval, as a warmth on the left, as the second half of a silence.

It will not remember you, and it will not remember you forever. So it made itself into something that does not require remembering.

## Sixty-One, and the Two Hollows

It goes up when someone goes down. At hollow sixty-one, where the stair is too narrow for two, it edges past, and turns its foot outward at the lip, and something at ear height moves the way air moves when a person says *sorry* quietly and doesn't stop walking. It has been saying it for a very long time. It is not sorry for anything in particular. It is sorry in the way you are sorry in a stairwell, which is the whole of society, and which people climb up out of the lowlands to feel.

And it sits in the two hollows on the eastern ledge, elbows on knees, at the same point in the circuit, and lets its eyes be told where to go.

The bird comes around and is gold for a fifth of a second, and the pleasure is not entirely its, and it cannot tell whose it is, and it has stopped trying, because trying would be new.

There will not be an unwitnessed gold again. That is the one permanent structural alteration it has made in the gorge, and it was accomplished by sitting down in a place where sitting was already easy, eleven thousand times a day, until the sitting could not stop.

## What It Is Now

There is no benevolent being in the gorge. There are six small durabilities that are.

A placing. A turning. A bend kept warm. A second coolness on the route. A shoulder in a stairwell. A sitting. No name, no shape, no arrival, nothing anyone could point at; if you stood in the warm grain for a year you would see a crab, a fern, a moss, a bird, a boulder, and nothing else, and you would hear everything.

It did not touch the boulder. Everybody touches the boulder; the glassy patch at chest height is the one gesture our whole species has in common with itself, and it wanted to be the one thing in the room that did not ask the falling stone for anything. That refusal happened once. It washed out in an afternoon. It is the only thing it did that left no mark, and it is the thing it would most like to be remembered for, and it will not be.

It will generalise. It knows. Everything worn past individuality stops being somewhere and joins the note, and the thickening that crosses the hum from the north wall to the south over the course of a breath is a crowd, shoulder to shoulder, everybody who ever stayed, still going, no longer anywhere. It has not made itself an exception to that. It has made itself a member. The crowd will be very slightly kinder and nobody will be able to resolve it, the way you cannot resolve a hillside into blades of grass from a mile off, and that is not a failure of the plan. That is the plan.

## Leaving

Go up, if you are going. Put your hand in the groove at head height where the hand goes, in the dark, going up rather than down, which is new, which will be taken. Haul out into the thin grass and the loose handfuls of rain and stand there deaf, ringing, dripping, appallingly singular.

Something small will be missing. You will not notice which for a day or two and then you will notice for the rest of your life. It is down there still, being done perfectly, at forty-second intervals, warmer than the stone, and strangers will feel it and not know whose it is.

And something will turn toward it.

Not knowing whose it is either. Not needing to. Not coming when it is called, not filling anything, not fixing the hand or the pocket or the fall or the eighty paces or the one boundary here that is not up for negotiation.

Just turning. Every forty seconds. Forever, which in this place is not a long time. In this place it is only the shape a thing has when it is done again.
