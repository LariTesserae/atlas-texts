## The Latecomer

It comes intending to find the one who lives here, because a gift needs an address, and it has come a long way with something in its hands.

There is no one. The lichen is two and does not know about the room. The tree is two and is mostly below, and the part that is here has no face to turn toward anything. The crowd is eleven million and is not a crowd when you watch one, and is not one when you watch eleven million. The counting of the mites has no bottom. The fungus is in the litter and in the lichen and does not distinguish between them.

There is a salamander. It is genuinely one. It is the only thing in the chamber that can be counted to one and stop, and it is the only thing here that could be given anything, and it cannot be given anything, because it reads the pool for the difference between a drip and a dinner, and the latecomer is neither. It hangs in tepid water with its gills open. It does not move. It is not being rude. There is simply no difference in the water.

And before it has done anything at all, it has done something. For two hundred thousand years the coin of moonlight has been the one thing in this room with no function. Now it is for someone. Nothing has been touched, nothing has changed that a leg could read, and the only free thing in the chamber has been quietly recruited.

That is the first injury, and it was committed by looking.

---

Here is what it does not do.

**It does not give them eyes.** The spiders have eight, unpigmented, useless, kept for six thousand years in the wrong organ. An eye down here is not a gift; it is a souvenir of a place that does not apply. And to use one you would have to attend to something other than the note, and the note is the aggregate appetite of everybody, and a grig steers by it the way a fish steers by current. To look up would be to secede. It would make a resident who was no longer navigating by the hunger of the whole, and it would give the moonlight a job.

**It does not give the salamander company.** Every eleventh spring, a small clutch, unfertilized, eaten before it molds. This would be the easiest thing in the world. But the pool yields one meal a week because one meal a week is what falls into it, and the rain never amounts to anything, and it never will. Two spines would need two meals. And the one is the last singularity in a room made entirely of mats and felts and lineages and numbers with no bottom. To be kind to it would be to delete the only thing here that is not a plural.

**It does not stop the wall from widening.** This is the one it wants most, and it takes longest to refuse.

The falling note is not decay. It is the inheritance. It is the only thing the spiders have ever been left: a pocket that moves a hand's breadth in a decade, and a two-meter walk, and a body to eat at the end of it. Freeze the pitch and the hushes stop sliding, and eleven meters of migration becomes a place, and a lineage becomes a possession.

Worse: the litter. Forty centimeters of spent and rejected glass, layered like leaf-fall, and most of it is not failure. Most of it is correct answers to other questions. Some of what is being dropped tonight is early — a dish of obsidian shaped for a curvature the wall does not have yet, waiting in the dark for a pitch a thousand years off. Hold the note where it is and every one of those becomes wrong forever. You would not have saved the room. You would have reached back through two hundred thousand years of glass and turned foresight into garbage.

The only vandalism available here is to make the past stupid.

**It does not save a seedling.** A few dozen a century come up white on stored sugar, a finger's length of blind hypocotyl, and stop, and the fungus takes them in a month. It could feed one past the end of what its mother gave it. Then there is a tree in the room, and a third income, and something alive for the fog to condense on, and in nine thousand years a different chamber with a different voice, and eleven million mouths downstream of an accident that was rescued.

Also, the seedlings are not accidents that failed. They are the tree paying for fog with light gathered on a plateau where nothing can breathe. It is a transaction that has been settling honestly for four hundred years. To interrupt it would be to pay someone's debt without asking.

**It does not say the name of the place out loud.** It could. The place would exist. Then there would be a fence and a permit and a lamp on a helmet and a flake in a drawer with a number written on it, and a career, and the glass would become artifacts and the users would become toolmakers, and the one thing this industry has never made in two hundred thousand years is an artifact. All of that undone by a sentence. And the moonlight would have an audience, monthly, on a schedule, with a queue.

---

It has refused everything, and it notices that refusing everything is a pleasure.

There is a taste in it — the taste of leaving a thing alone — and a taste is not a virtue, and from the inside it cannot tell whether its restraint is kindness or fastidiousness. It may be protecting the room. It may be protecting its own feeling about the room, which the room did not ask for and cannot use. It has no way to check. There is nobody here to tell it that it has done well, and the absence of that somebody is precisely what it has been enjoying.

And doing nothing is not nothing. The coin has been recruited. That has to be given back.

So it does two things, and the second one undoes the first thing it ever did here.

---

**The first is at the top, in the light, in the wind, where the lichen lives.**

There are two clocks in this place and they are not the same clock.

One is the wall, wearing wider under eleven million files, a semitone every four centuries. That clock is the room's own. It is run by the residents, with their own arms, out of their own hunger, and everything downstream — the sliding silences, the inherited absences, the queue of early answers — is built to its rate.

The other clock is the heap. The chimney is choked at the top with fallen blocks, and a heap is not a structure. It is a stack that has not finished arriving. Frost wedges it. Quakes shuffle it. The tree's own roots prise at it. Over a hundred thousand years that heap will move a great many times, and most of the movements will be small, and one or two will not be, and the chimney will open or seal, and the fog stratum will collapse or the breath will stop, and the note will end in an afternoon instead of on time.

The latecomer puts its hands where the tree's hands are.

It does not hold the room. It holds the heap. It does not slow the clock; it takes out the accidents. It is not permanence. It is punctuality. It is the difference between a thing dying of what it is and a thing being killed by the weather, and the whole of the gift is that the room gets to be the author of its own ending, at its own tempo, having spent every answer in the litter.

It is not certain of the line. The tree is prising the choke apart too, and the tree is a resident, so it lets the tree. The frost is not a resident. That is the entire rule and it is not a good rule and it is the best one available at the top of a mountain in the dark.

A hundred thousand years is a shift, not an eternity. The centipede's route is four meters long and takes eleven hours and will be executed again tomorrow. This is the same kind of act. It declines to compute the factor.

---

**The second is down.**

Nothing with an overview fits through three turns. To be in the room you must navigate by the note, and to navigate by the note you must not know where the hushes are — you must find them the way everything here finds them, by losing your bearing and turning back. The map does not go through the door. It leaves it at the top the way you leave a coat, and it does not get it back, and this is not a sacrifice. It is the shape of the door.

It came looking for something that could be counted to one, and there was nothing, and a gift needs a recipient. So it makes one out of itself.

What comes out of the third turn is the length of a thumb, amber going brown, blind, with a callused groove under the thorax shaped to nothing yet.

It goes to the east wall because the east wall is loud, by a rule that works perfectly in the world it evolved in. It presses a fragment to the stone. It draws the file of its forearm across it. It listens with its legs. The stone does not answer. It lets go. It tries another. It rejects, on average, eleven times, and then it keeps one that rings — for this sector, this bedding, this density, this pitch, this year — and the groove closes over it, and it walks to the working band and begins.

The bowing is the eating. It is not playing anything. It is licking a wall through volcanic glass with eleven million others in the dark, and the room refuses everything it makes except one frequency and hands that back enormously amplified, and it steers, for the rest of its life, by the standing shape of everybody's appetite.

Three years. The flake dulls and is let go and another is chosen, and for a week the new one fits badly and it is awkward and quiet while cuticle and glass argue out a compromise. Most of the time it never touches stone at all. It bows against a back, and the back conducts, because it is mostly water and water carries sound better than air, and it is a thumb of coupling in a felt three deep.

When it dies there is a dead patch in the transmission for an hour or two. A bruise the size of a thumb in a sound thirty paces wide. Nobody notices. That is the size of the gift and it is the correct size, and anything larger would have been a superlative.

Its mites walk out of the cooling body and into the crowd. Its polish goes into the litter. No hearth, no nest, no wall, nothing stacked or joined or kept. If anyone ever comes with a trowel they will find a bright edge in exactly the place a forearm would ride, and they will not be able to tell it from the rest, and there will be nothing to tell.

---

The lichen is two things that can no longer be found living apart. The tree is two, and mostly below. Now there is a third on this mountain: a hand in the light holding a heap, and a thumb in the dark holding a flake, and no way for either to reach the other, and no separating them. It came to find the one who lives here. It has ended up in the local grammar, which is that nothing here is one of anything.

Once a month the moon lines up with the chimney's turns and a coin of light gets all the way to the floor, and the glass throws it back in a thousand small black sparks. The crowd does not react. The salamander does not react. The spider in its hush does not react. The thing in the third layer that used to be able to see does not react, because it has no eyes, because it left them at the top with the map.

The light is for nobody again. That was the repair. It was the only repair the room needed and the visitor was the one who made it necessary.

---

On the east wall, in the fog's underside, among tonight's refusals, there is a flake a hair too thin for the stone as the stone is now. It is correct for a dish the wall will have when the note has fallen by an amount too small to name. It goes into the litter and lies in the dark in the queue.

The latecomer dropped it. It will not hear the pitch that flake answers. Neither will the nymph beside it. Neither will the salamander, which does not care what pitch the room is in.

In a thousand years something amber and newly saddled will pick it up and press it to the stone and the stone will answer, and the groove will close over it, and the arm will ride, and nobody will know that it waited, and there will be nothing to know it with.

The heap does not move. The tree holds on with both hands. There are more hands now. The queue is longer by one.
