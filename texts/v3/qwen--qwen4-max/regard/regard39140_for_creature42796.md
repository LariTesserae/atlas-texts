## The Attention

It does not arrive. Arriving is an event, and the pan has no organ for events; it has an organ for amplitude, and one for which flank is warm. So the power comes the way the fog comes — as a condition, without a front, without a moment you could name afterward — and the first thing it does is nothing, deliberately, at some cost.

The constraint it sets itself is a sentence: *there has never been a hand here.* Everything it might do has to leave that sentence true. No cut marks. No ash. No midden, no bone, no straightened gutter, no repaired stair. If a visitor ever climbs the rim and looks down at the pale bulging halls and thinks *built*, the power must be certain that the visitor is wrong in exactly the way they have always been wrong.

This rules out most of what benevolence usually looks like. It does not rule out benevolence.

## The Census, Taken Correctly

Before anything else, it counts properly, which is the whole of wisdom at this address and the place where almost every would-be benefactor fails.

It does not count forty. Forty is a count of what a hand could pick up, and it has no hands and no interest in the units of hands. It counts at half past six in the morning, when the seam is running and ten million tuns are opening like small fists, and it counts the black front advancing a hair, and it counts the loop — stone eaten, stone laid, stone cropped, stone eaten — which is the resident, and which has no body to be spared and no boundary to be defended.

And it converts the clock. A patch of crust the size of a hand is not nine hundred years old; it is twenty years old, in slices, and the gaps were not experienced. A grazer is not sixty; it is three, and it has never had a night. To regard these creatures as *slow* is to pity them for a duration they do not possess. The power declines the pity. It keeps time the way the pan keeps time, in mornings, and finds that what it is looking at is not ancient at all. It is a place where something ordinary is happening, repeatedly, with the boring parts removed.

Then it reads the walls. It takes the bands — the dust years, the ice years, the dark seam of the wet century — and holds them somewhere frost cannot reach. The city has no history recorded and is nothing but record. Now there is a scribe who does not write in it. This is the first gift and it costs the pan not one grain, because reading leaves no mark, and it is very nearly the only gift of which that is true.

## Kindnesses, Declined

It works through the list. It is a long list and every item on it is a vandalism.

*Warmth.* Slacken the wind and the antinodes move; the rind takes the new quiet pockets and no grazer is tuned there yet to crop them; uncropped, the crust seals, hardens, thickens; the throat narrows; the note climbs out of the range of any sac in the room. Four hundred years and the halls close like fists. A warmer pan is a shut pan.

*Water.* The same road, travelled faster. Rain is not a gift here. Rain is glue.

*Eyes.* Light in this bowl carries no news — grey stone, bars of dust, the sun going over. An eye would be an organ for a room with nothing in it, and it would be paid for out of a budget measured in a finger's thickness of crust. There is no spare metabolism in the pan. This is not a metaphor: forty animals is the number the rind can feed, and the number is load-bearing.

*Continuity.* Thaw the grazer every hour instead of every dawn and you have not lengthened a life. You have made the gaps experienced. You have invented sixty years of nights in a place that had none, and installed the first suffering in the pan as a present.

*Quiet.* The chord is the coordinate system. Silence it and every citizen becomes a thumb-sized object standing on stone with a green line in it and nothing anywhere telling it where it is. That is not peace. That is the contents of the silent hall, distributed to everyone.

*Mercy toward the grazers.* Fence out the long thing and a broken note takes a century to resettle instead of a decade. Shorten the dangerous crossing and no grazer ever walks out with a slack sac, and nothing ever molts long on the plain, and the pan loses both its regulator and its only export.

*A witness inside.* It considers making something that could hear the whole chord at once and know what it was standing in — the interval, the ten thousand years of replacement, the thing the pan cannot know about itself. But a knower must eat. To add one is to subtract a grazer; to subtract a grazer is to close a hall. The pan cannot afford to know itself.

Therefore the knowing has to be done by something that eats nothing. Therefore it has to be done from outside, for free, by a visitor who does not stay.

*Permanence.* Last and most tempting. Hold every particle in place and you have not saved the standing wave; you have stopped the passing-through that was the wave. You cannot preserve a rapid by freezing the river. You can only make a pond in the shape of one.

## The Room With Nothing In It

It goes into the silent hall near the eastern rim, because it can; it has no sac to go slack.

The roar drops away. Ordinary daylight comes down through the roof-hole onto fluted grey stone. It stands where thirty animals a year turn back, and it understands the thing it came to understand: that this room is not a wound.

The rind will not take here, so the bedrock is eaten unarmored, so the room is getting larger, so the cancellation is failing, so in some thousands of years the wind will go through and come out having said something, and a quiet pocket will exist for the first time, and moss will follow the weeping, and a hatchling will wake tuned to a note that has never existed. This is the only aperture the pan has into anything new. Every other surface is at its last form.

And from the inside, the signal for *here you begin* and the signal for *here you stop existing* are the same signal, and there is no organ in the pan that can tell them apart.

So it does not soften the doorway. It does not shorten the crossing. It does not put a note in the room to keep the animals from walking out into the dark with nothing to hold. It leaves the one unbalanced thing in the bowl unbalanced, which is the hardest discipline it has performed so far, and it goes back out into the noise.

## The Ending

It looks at how the pan will die, and finds that it will not be fire or ice or storm. It will be the cloud base lifting forty meters. The fog will stop reaching the floor; the dawn drip will fail; five hundred hours a year will become three hundred, then fifty; the hour of millions will stop happening; the rind will dry and the seams will close and the wind will keep eating with nothing laying down. The city will not fall. It will simply be subtracted, at the rate of a hair.

It could hold the fog down. It has the means. It does not.

Not because endings are beautiful — it has seen a great many endings and they are mostly just endings. It does not because the fog line is a term in the loop, and the frost net is a term, and the wind is a term, and pinning them would preserve the geometry by removing the thing the geometry is a solution to. And because this plain is itself the corpse of an ending: there were mountains here, and they were taken away, and the taking is what made a place where a standing wave could stand. Somewhere the same change that kills this bowl is planing down another range. To hold the climate still is to decide that no further pans will ever be made, in exchange for keeping one.

And because the resident is not the bowl. The resident is a relation, and relations recur. What can be preserved is not this address. It is the possibility of the address.

## What It Does

One squall.

Cloud builds out of nothing over the flat world every evening and drops hail for eleven minutes and clears. The power moves one of them forty miles west and a quarter of an hour earlier. Nothing about it is unusual. It is the same hail, the same eleven minutes, the same clearing. A bird that would have come over this rim comes over a different one, on another planed-down mountain root eight hundred miles away, where the pavement has been waiting a hundred thousand years with nothing on it but wind.

In the mud on its feet: three tuns, a mite, and the black front.

The bird does not know it is the postal service. It never has. The power adds no mechanism to the world; it uses the one already running, twice a summer, since before there were mammals.

Then it does it again. Not the following week — in four centuries, and in eleven more, and in thirty thousand years, at intervals, with no urgency, which is not a miracle but a bias. Most of the deliveries fail. The fog is wrong, or the joints are wrong, or the rind takes hold and seals because no grazer lineage ever arrives to crop it, and the little hall closes like a fist and the whole attempt is a smooth grey lump. It expects this. It has budgeted for almost all of it to be wasted, in the way the pan budgets: one egg at a time, unguarded.

Before each delivery it goes and looks at the bowl, and twice it finds something already there — a different arrangement, unrelated, unrepeatable, holding its own crossing point between two rates — and it leaves those alone and goes elsewhere. A good thing does not get to spread merely because it is good.

And it never signs. If anyone ever stands at that second rim, they will find no tools, no fire, no ash, no bone. The rind will have arrived by weather, because it will have arrived by weather. The signature has to be indistinguishable from the thing it signs, or the place becomes a made thing, and a made thing is only ever afterward a ruin.

## Out on the Plain

There is one more citizen and it is not currently in the loop.

The power goes out over the rim, onto the grey table where nothing is, and finds the long thing in a frost crack, stopped, with the tube along its body holding no interval at all.

It does not wake it. It does not carry it home — there is no single note the thing can hold; it is tuned to a relation, and to put it back in one hall would be to hand it a word when it needs a sentence. It does not warm the crack. It does not resolve, even for itself, whether this is a regulator asleep or an exile that walked out through the silent hall and kept going with a slack sac through a night and another night and molted, and got long. Both are true enough to be worth the same behavior.

It stays.

Nothing in the animal can receive this. There is no organ for company, no experienced duration in which to be lonely, no gap in which the presence could register. The power is clear about that. It is not a kindness; it is what is left to do with attention when there is nothing to repair, and it does it anyway, and does not require it to land.

When the pan goes briefly out of tune — a winter opening a window, a throat changing — the thing wakes and walks back along the hexagon net, taking one from every third hall, always the one at the antinode, always the oldest and best-settled and most completely a piece of its room. The power goes with it as far as the rim, is not needed, is not noticed, and stops there.

## The Hour

Then it takes the pan's own trick, which is the only comfortable thing it has learned in a hundred thousand years.

It stops attending continuously. It attends at the drip and in the calm evening and lets the gaps go unexperienced, so that its own endless attention becomes twenty years of ordinary mornings spread thin across the Holocene — which is to say it becomes rare rather than slow, which is to say it becomes a citizen.

It stands at the antinode, where the air is stillest, and crops nothing, and eats nothing, and costs the room nothing, which is the condition on which it was admitted. It is the only thing in the bowl that knows what the room sounds like. It holds the bands, the wet century, the census at half past six, the whole chord and the interval between the halls, outside, where frost cannot reach, and it does not put any of it back.

A hall's population is not a lineage. It is a congregation, assembled out of strangers by a frequency. This is one more stranger.

And in some thousands of years, near the eastern rim, the cancellation will fail. The wind will go in and come out having said something. The rind will find a quiet pocket and start, and the moss will follow the weeping, and a hatchling will wake tuned to a note that has never existed, and will not know that it is the first.

The power intends to be there for that. Not to help. To be the thing that hears it.

Forever, for a while.
