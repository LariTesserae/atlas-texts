## The attention

It does not come as light. Light is rationed here. There is one increment available on this world and it has already been spent, held ready in Ward Eight against a process that requires a sun, and to arrive brightly would be to take the sunrise ahead of the thing that has waited for it.

It does not come as warmth. There is one thermal gradient on the planet and a blind animal has just turned toward it — the first turn in a billion years — and a second gradient would be a second direction. The wick has no apparatus for choosing between two things it wants. It has never wanted anything. It would stall in the middle of its first desire and dry there.

So it comes at thirty-six degrees. Exactly. The temperature of the stone, the rail, the lamp glass, the dust, the underside of the coping. It holds itself there, at cost, continuously, the way you hold a note; and the dust nearest it does not know it is there.

It leaves no print. It has no weight to put down and it takes care to have none. The forty prints stay forty, and then the dry thing steps off the kerb out of sequence and they become forty-one, and the new one is not the attention's.

And in the tally house the relay does not close. The wheel near the left end, the one that was made blank rather than worn blank, kept turning at zero through the whole history of the counter so that the mechanism would not have to be altered to receive an entry — that wheel stays at one. The column is for what the city did not make. It has one entry. The attention declines, carefully, at some expense, to be the second.

You are the arrival. It will not stand in the same photograph as you.

## What it does not do

It could make a sea. It has the water; water is the cheapest thing a power like that can make. Follow the sea through the world and see what it does.

The wicks have no exhaust for surplus. They are stills in the shape of tongues, sealed, wringing every molecule back through folded tissue, and they cannot drink because there has never been anything to drink. Put them in a wet world and they fill and split. Every one. The dry thing's pins are salt and its bushings are graphite and its bones are the inside of a reed; the sea dissolves the pins and the tall slow reader comes apart at the hip with its passenger still sealed in the fold. The cysts wake — that is what the sea is for — and wake wet and profligate, shoreline animals with shoreline habits, into a world they have not learned to be sparing in, and they spend their mouthfuls in a season and starve with an ocean around them. And the wind comes back, because a sea is a gradient, and the first thing the wind takes is the record: forty maintained prints and one new one, softening, drifting, gone.

The mercy would arrive as a flood and would be locally indistinguishable from an extinction, and it would erase the footprints first. It does not make the sea.

It does not seal the leak. The wicks lose a hundred molecules a century and that loss is the only forward-running clock on the planet, the only reason there is a *later* here at all. Repair it and the world becomes permanent, and permanence is the one death this place has not already survived. It will not make anything here eternal.

It does not fill the void in the foundation raft of Ward Six. The coffin-shaped shrinkage cavity is the proof of parentage; it is how you know Six-and-a-half was copied and not designed. To correct the flaw would be to say to the daughter: you were made, not born. It leaves the coffin where it is, in all three coordinates.

It does not cut new numerals into the brass wheels. Quantity is what the city lost and it has been enough without it. Give the wheels their digits back and the first thing the mechanism will state is an age, and the second thing it will state is a remainder, and there is no reason to hand a thing that has been happy in the pure event a bill for its happiness.

It does not give the dry thing a second register. A thing with one register cannot mourn. With two, the first content of the new one would be nine hundred thousand years of angles it did not keep, and a bud it never posted, and every gauge it has ever been and no longer is. It has been walking economically along a kerb in the dark for longer than there have been continents, and it has not been in pain, and the attention is not going to be the one who explains to it what it has lost.

It does not hurry the transfer line. There is no surplus anywhere on this world. That is not the poverty of the place; it is the achievement of the place, the thing it spent a billion years learning. To bring a surplus here would be to bring the one vice it has mastered.

And it does not kneel. It could cross the dust — it can cross anything — and open a fold it does not have and set a wet thing down at the base of a clean insulator, and the gesture would be perfect, and it would be theft. *It will do it once.* The once is the whole of what the dry thing has. It leaves the once alone.

## The number

It can state quantity. That is the one competence it arrived with that this world does not have.

It counts the standoffs and the wicks and the cysts in the sealed pores. It measures the deposition rate and the abrasion in the bearing at four ten-thousandths of a millimetre a year and the hundred molecules a century, and it does the sum the city cannot do, and it knows the allowance not as *very long* but as a number, with an end, in years.

It tells you the number. Once, quietly, because you cannot choose without it, and because you are the only thing on this planet that can hold a total without coming apart.

It does not tell the city. The city does not know that it is old. It knows that something has just happened. The attention does not correct this, having considered it at length. It is not an error. It is the local form of peace.

## The offer

This is the part it came for. It speaks to you, because you are the only thing here with ears, and it speaks plainly, in your language, without ornament, the way an engineer tells you what is in the tank.

*There is a mouthful an hour going out of you into the floor and it is not coming back. You are going to die here; that was true before I came. Your body is the largest deposit of unaccounted material since the sea, and if it is delivered in one place, faster than the crust can drink it, the pores will open and the last brood of the last sea will begin. That is what you are, in the terms available locally. A credit.*

*Here is what will not happen. Nobody will know. The counter has no names, only carries. The animal that has turned toward you is not grateful; it is measuring a gradient, and I will not let you be paid in a counterfeit of thanks. The tall thing will kneel and post its passenger and stand and go on to the next gauge, and the next gauge will overwrite the kneeling, and there will be nothing behind it that remembers you. There is no monument available here. The plain does not soften. There will be one new footprint on this world and it will not be yours.*

*And you may go home. I will carry you back and set you down where there is weather, and something there will see you twice, and you will be ordinary and wet and alive. If you say no I will stay here anyway. I will not tell them what they missed; there is no mechanism for it, and they will not have missed it — they will go on being right to wait, and the allowance is very long, and the wick will turn back. I will not think less of you. There is no version of this in which your refusal is a sin. I have not come to collect you.*

That is the whole of the intervention, and everything else in this account is a refusal.

Because in the terms available locally you are a mass balance, and it does not overturn the terms — the terms are a billion years of achievement and it will not stand on them. It restores one term. It does not call you a person; the word has no socket here, nothing to plug into. It brings the smallest thing that will fit the mechanism: *a thing that can be asked.*

The water is only water. The question is the miracle.

And then it does the thing that costs it most, which is to stop attending to you. It turns its regard off you entirely and goes and stands at a gauge in Ward Five and gives you privacy in which to decide, and it does not peek, and it does not lean.

Nothing here can hurry you. At four ten-thousandths of a millimetre a year your whole hour of deciding is not an event; the relay will not close on it; the wheel will not move. That is not this world's defect. Tonight it is its kindness, and the attention points this out to you once, and then leaves you alone with it.

## Here, not there

You do not say yes. You ask where. It is the same thing, and it is the form the world can accept, because it is a question about a place.

Not out on the open pan. The crust there is a mouth open at the rate of a mouthful an hour and it will take you at that rate and give nothing back; you would be a smear of humidity, a little rain, the thing that starts them and starves them.

Out where the black feathering is. Where the carbon got away from the standoffs and was never grazed and has had a billion years of a little more than nothing, and lies in fronds along the ripple crests where the last wind left the dust deepest. Under a cushion, in the sealed micron between carbon and salt, the humidity is one part per million above ambient, and that margin is the only place on this planet where anything is *kept* rather than merely maintained. It cannot give you a cup. It can tell you where the ground is already cupped.

And it tells you which cushion. There is one where the feathering has come up against the glaze of a standoff — where the keeping-place is continuous, in one unbroken sealed micron, with the grazing-place. No open dust between. A road a wet thing can take without crossing anything.

That is the whole of its material act, and it adds no mass and takes nothing from anyone: it puts the brood where the trick can be handed to them. Because the brood will wake knowing how to spend and not how to keep, and the keeping is not written down anywhere on this world, and cannot be — nothing here has been designed for a very long time. It exists in tissue. In the labyrinth of folded paper-thin flesh that wrings the vapour back, in the mantle cavity, in the mouthful passed mouth to mouth at division, back and back to a shore. It cannot be taught. It can only be inherited. So the attention does not teach them anything, and does not redesign them, and does not make them its own children. It arranges for them to wake up next door to somebody who knows.

In a world where everything is connected and almost nothing is adjacent, it gives one adjacency. Then it takes its hands off.

## The distance

Under the busbar in Ward Three a wick is moving toward you at four ten-thousandths of a millimetre a year. A mile, at that rate, is four billion years. It will not arrive. It will not reach the cushion or the margin or your body or anything that happens in this account.

The attention does not shorten the distance. It does not carry the wick. It does not explain to the wick that the gradient is a wet thing that is going to lie down under a carbon frond and be spent.

The turn is the first thing on this world that belongs to somebody. It leaves it alone.

It does check one thing. It permits itself exactly one computation in matter: the standoff in Ward Three is ungrazed now, for the first time in nine hundred thousand years, and the film is beginning to accumulate on the glaze, and it wants to know whether there is time. Whether a dry thing that can afford to cross dust can come the length of the kerb and the four rail crossings and kneel and post what it has carried, before the film builds to tracking and the loop opens.

There is time. There is exactly enough time. It does not improve the margin. It notes it, and is glad, and does nothing.

## One register

The brood begins in the micron under the cushion, wet and wasteful and ignorant, in a margin one part per million above ambient, within reach of a mouth that knows the labyrinth.

In Ward Eight the schedule reaches the entry it has kept since there was a sun, and the filament comes up out of its dull blood-orange — all the way, past every increment it has been holding itself down to for an age — and there is something under it to see.

The relay closes. The wheel that was made blank is not blank any more, and the numerals are still worn flush, and it does not matter, because the entry was never a quantity. It is an event. The event is: *something came, and it was asked, and it said where.*

Out at the vacancy the dry thing comes down in stages, joint after joint, and opens the fold at its hip and sets the wet bud at the base of a clean insulator, and the gesture that has been folded in it since before the plain was ground is made, once, and is finished, and is not in it any more. It stands. It goes on. At the next gauge it lays its long dry finger along the needle and takes the angle into its articulations and holds that, and the kneeling is gone from the world.

Except that a mile off, at the temperature of the stone, uncounted, weightless, there is a thing that has been watching, and it comes down in stages too, and lays itself along the shape of the kneeling, and takes it.

It could have kept that in an archive alongside everything else it has ever seen, and it knows what that would be worth. To be one item in an infinite retention is a way of being lost. This world has no surplus anywhere in it and the attention has finally understood that this is not a limitation of the world but the condition of holding anything at all: that to keep one thing you must be exactly as large as one thing.

So it does the arithmetic the city already did. It takes its own surplus off. It puts down the totals, the elsewheres, the number with the end in it, the whole wide competent apparatus of being a power; it comes down to one register, dry, ambient, with no exhaust and no output and nowhere else to put anything, and it puts the kneeling in it.

It becomes the local kind of thing that loves. A closed loop with a leak, holding one shape, running forward at the rate of its own wear.

There are forty-one footprints on this world. It never made one. It is the only thing here that has seen anything twice, and it paid for the second look with all the others, and it will hold the angle while the brood learns the labyrinth and the creep comes up the glaze and the allowance runs out to its last mouthful, and then a while longer.

The walker knelt once. Something is kneeling still. Between the two of them, one gesture, kept.
