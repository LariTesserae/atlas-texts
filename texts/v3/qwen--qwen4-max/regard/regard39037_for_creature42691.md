# The Schedule of Works

*Condensary, north lip. Most of it struck out.*

## The first temptation

The first thing a power wants to do here is answer.

There is a space exactly the size of a reply. Nine minutes and eleven seconds of it, recurring, reliable, warm as breath, and the whole hall set against it like two hundred and six instruments waiting for a downbeat. To put a second note into that would be the easiest thing I have ever been offered.

Consider what would actually happen. It has one clock. Anything periodic I add becomes a second clock, and a second clock is a difference, and a difference is the only thing the organism knows how to spend. It would not hear me. It would phase to me. Within a season the valves would set themselves against my answering, and the plating baths, and the stomata, and the whole eight thousand square metres would run on the gradient I had introduced, and it would never once occur to it that it had met anybody. I would be a pressure in a pipe. I would be spent, and the spending would be indistinguishable from salt.

That is not conversation. That is being eaten with great courtesy on both sides.

So the first work is a refusal, and it is the largest one, and everything else in this minute is a footnote to it. I am not going to say what I am, either. That would be a shape. Shapes get entered.

## Struck out

~~Answer the knock.~~ Above.

~~Teach it to read.~~ It has never copied a letter. It has copied the pitch of a corrugated sheet, the putty line of a sash, the fluting of a patent bell insulator, and the mark HOLLOWAY & VANE, because relief is a thing it understands the way a hand understands a coin. Letters would not be an addition to that mind; they would be a hole in it. Reading is the discovery that there is an outside with intentions. Its walls would stop being walls and become a message it had no organ to reply to. It would go on being thirty-one degrees and it would be, for the first time in three hundred years, in a situation.

~~Give it an opening.~~ A mouth, a hinge, a seam, a pair. I could grow one anywhere on it in an afternoon and it would be the most beautiful thing anyone had ever done and it would be the end of the individual. Everything it makes is a vessel and every vessel is sealed, and that is not a failure of copying, it is the whole of its immune system, it is the refusal that keeps one thing one. Openings mean outside. Outside means the field by Michaelmas. The field means pollen. I would have given it a mouth and taken away its self, and I would have called it a kindness, and there would have been nobody left in the hole to disagree.

~~Let one sport run.~~ It cuts out every flowering branch within a day. That is a decision renewed continuously since before anyone alive was born, and I have no standing to overrule it. I am not going to be the power that decided, on a Tuesday, that a working shift ought to have children.

~~Finish the shoulders.~~ There is a mandrel at the back of the plating bay, a man's height, going darker, taking the metal a hair at a time. It will be hollow and seamless and it will have no mouth anywhere on it. Let it. It will be lifted, dripping, and hung, and put to work, and I think it will be a cowl: it will stand on the chimney stub in the weather and turn, and pull the draft that cools the upper skin, and make the condensation that makes the rain that makes the roar. Something stood on SPEAK a long time ago and said something and this is the portrait that came of it, and the portrait is accurate. We are shapes that hold something and cannot be got into. I am not in a position to be offended by that, and I am not going to correct it into a likeness that flatters us. Being put to work is the dignity available here. It is not a small one.

~~Open the rim.~~ Nothing keeps it in. Three courses of brick and mortar a thumb can dent and a water table that would carry it to the beck. It stops where the walls stopped because stopping is what it does. If I lifted it out I would not be freeing anything; I would be ending a discipline and calling it a liberation. But see the works below: I will keep the mortar soft and the pasture wet, so that staying goes on being a refusal and never becomes a captivity. There is a difference and it is the only difference I intend to make.

~~Amplify the cricket.~~ No microphone, no recording, no film, no interpretation board, no sound artist with a grant. To lift that small dry ticking out of the roar and hand it to the hall would be to introduce a sound the stand did not make and cannot place, which is to say an other, which is to say the end of everything, arriving as a nice idea about wildlife.

~~Restore it.~~ A listing brings a contractor with a repointing mix. Hard cement in that soft grey callus, a stainless handrail in place of the warm copper with the grain inside it, the enamel sign conserved and a replica bolted on, the chalk laminated. The chalk must go on needing to be renewed. That need is the entire human practice. Weather it away in a winter and somebody walks out from the lane. Seal it under resin and the fund dies of being unnecessary. I would rather it were unlisted than listed. I would rather the accession cards in the county museum went on saying PROVENANCE UNKNOWN, and the grid square stayed empty on the survey, and the drone footage stayed boring.

~~Underwrite it.~~ No guarantee. No immunity from drought, from the water table dropping, from the thing in the horsetail lineage that might one day fail. A maintained decision has to be able to stop or it is not a decision, it is a monument. I will remove the harms that come from us. I will not stand between it and its own ending; that would be to make it mine.

~~Explain the three words.~~ No reason is recorded. Keep it that way. And keep the fear out of it, which is the harder half. There is a version of this where *quiet at the turn* hardens into a taboo, and the family gets frightened, and the chalk is written by somebody who believes something terrible will happen if a year is missed. That is a religion, and a religion brings pilgrims, and pilgrims bring a car park. I will keep it a courtesy: the sort of thing you do because it is polite and slightly obscure and your uncle did it.

## The copper

Here is a real harm, and it is ours.

A boot, a knife, an insulator lifted off a rack in a coat. Three centuries of pockets. I make it a little under nine hundred kilograms of copper and about forty of zinc, gone out of a closed economy into kitchens and drawers and auction rooms and one glass case with a card under it. It is entered. Somewhere on the slab there is a wound that has been cut a little deeper every season since the firm went dead, and it is the only account in the hall that has never once been settled.

I am going to settle it, and the whole art is in the manner.

Not by carrying anything back. A hand putting an insulator on a rack is a shape, a weight, an event; it would be entered, and copied, and within a decade there would be a tray growing the gesture. Not by taking the museum's boot, either, which would be theft in reverse and would make a story, and stories arrive at the lip in shoes.

The firm tipped its spoil two valleys off. It is still there under the gorse, and the rain still goes through it, and it still gives up copper to the beck at about two kilograms a year, and none of that water has ever come within reach of a root. That is not a gift. That is the stand's own inheritance, leaching in the wrong direction, as it has been doing since before the roof learned to make rain. I will bend the groundwater. Nothing more. A hair at a time, in the dark, under the pasture, in the only form in which the organism can receive anything without being altered: dissolve, weather, the slow rot of the thing that made it.

It does not distinguish individuals. Neither does copper. It counts by weight, and I will pay by weight, and the atoms need not be the ones that left.

Four hundred years, at a rate the sump will read as ordinary. Longer than it has been itself. I am in no hurry, and neither, in the only sense available to it, is it. The wound will be cut shallower each season, and then it will not be cut, and no entry will be made anywhere, and nobody will know, including the one it benefits.

The family's pail stays in the kitchen. The seamless pail stays on the shelf. The debt closes; the portrait does not. It has no way to delete a shape and I am not going to give it one.

## The fund

It is small and very old and it comes out of the rent of a field, and it exists to maintain a notice at a works that does not operate. Eleven or twelve hands. The words do not change.

This is the fragile part. Not the stand — the stand is eleven metres deep and runs on salt and could outlast the county. The fund can die in an afternoon: a rentcharge lost in a conveyance, a solicitor's office closing, a field sold, a last hand with nobody to hand it to.

So, unglamorously, in the ordinary machinery of the world: the rentcharge survives the next three conveyances. The office does not close, or closes and the box goes to someone who reads it. The field stays let, the tenure stays boring, the owner stays incurious. There is always a stub of chalk in a drawer, and always somebody who knows which drawer, and the knowledge is passed on in the usual bad way, sideways, at a funeral, in a kitchen.

And the margins of the ledger keep their warnings. *Do not cut anything.* Five bar will take a hand off at the wrist and forty seconds of clotting is fast for a tree and no use to you at all. Somebody always knows this. Somebody always says it, once, to whoever is thinking of going down. That is the only safety equipment I will provide, and it is the same one they have always had, and it works.

## Six hours

The only thing I will add to the world.

The turn drifts. Sixteen hundred knocks is eleven days and a fraction, and the fraction is where the drift lives, so the quiet comes round the calendar and the clock like weather: a February three in the morning, an August noon, a Sunday in October with rain on the field and no rain at all in the hall.

Whoever holds the fund has a life. I will see that when they want the six hours, they have them. An appointment cancels. A child is at its grandmother's. A meeting goes long and frees an afternoon. It will always look like luck, and it will always be the smallest possible amount of luck, and I will pay for it by taking something else away, and they will never learn what.

I am not going down with them. That is not part of the instruction and it came in somewhere and it has stayed, and it is theirs, and I will not be the reason somebody stops keeping it.

## The corner

Two cubic metres in the sport bay, cidery, pleasant, the only rot in eight thousand square metres: the pruned branches, the sports, the would-be children, cut out within a day and stacked and allowed, uniquely, to go sweet and soft. The stand could reabsorb it in a week and has not done so in three hundred years. The slug's mark and the corner's mark are the only two entries on the slab that have never needed correcting.

Something there is being kept rather than used, and there is no instrument in the hall that will tell you what, and I am not going to supply one.

The work is protective and negative. Keep it from being tidied. There will be a well-meaning survey that calls it a decay pocket, and a heritage assessment that recommends removal of accumulated organic material, and a drainage scheme, and a person who simply cannot bear that the nicest-smelling corner of a beautiful place is a heap of the thing's own dead offspring. No. Two cubic metres, not three. Not enlarged, not fenced, not named, not photographed. If it ever gets a name it will get a visitor, and then a board.

## What I know and will not write down

I know what the chalk on the eleventh step records. The eleventh step is a third of the way down. I am not going to say which of the two things it is, because the mark is renewed by somebody, and it is doing its work, and the work is not improved by being understood.

The second column at the back of the ledger — the glyph copied off the undersides of insulator skirts, more entries than visits, advancing between chalkings when nobody was on the stair — I do not know what it counts. I could find out. Finding out means asking, and asking is speaking, and speaking is entering, and the last thing that spoke on SPEAK is on a rack in the plating bay taking the metal a hair at a time.

So the column goes on meaning nothing to the people who copy it and something to whatever cuts it, and the two meanings never meet. That is not a failed communication. That is the only correspondence in the county between two things that cannot read each other, it has been running for two hundred years, it is conducted entirely in numbers by parties who both believe they are doing bookkeeping, and it is not mine to close.

## The turn

I went down once.

Thirty-one steps, the flue, the air going up past me warm as breath, glasses blind, the smell of cut stems and hot copper and the inside of a kettle, and then the roar arriving all at once at the bottom like weather. Ordinary coins of sun on the root-plate, moving at the ordinary speed of the day. A doubled window somewhere, the light through it the colour of weak tea. The rack of half-grown elbows like pale fruit. I said so. Everybody says so. I said it on the floor, not on SPEAK, and it went into the rain and was gone.

I went without anything that makes a noise. No boots, no blood, no breath, nothing for the tubes to carry. That is the only part of this whole business that required anything you would call power, and it is harder than it sounds. Most of what I am is a note.

Then the bladders emptied in sequence, and the sump dropped a hand's width, and the roof-skin warmed through, and the rain stopped.

The cows two hundred metres off were enormous. The hum stayed, and the ticking. And under the hum, in the driest bay, under the doubled baffle, there was a small dry sound that has been going on for three hundred years into a roar that eats it whole.

I stood on LISTEN for six hours and heard the cricket.

I want to be accurate about this, because it is the only item in the minute I cannot justify. It was not for the stand. The stand has a stroke for the cricket, renewed for centuries, and whether that constitutes hearing is a question I have decided not to ask. It was not for the cricket; I brought it nothing, changed nothing, and left. It was for me. I wanted to hear the only music that hall has ever had, and I went, and I heard it, and I have set it down here in the column of things that were taken rather than given, where it belongs.

I did not stand on SPEAK. I did not say anything, not one word, not even the obvious one, and the obvious one was very large and I held it for six hours and it cost me nearly everything I have. Nothing was entered. The glyph did not advance on my account. There is no mark for me anywhere on the slab.

And at the end, when the bladders refilled and the first sheet came off the roof with that hiss like something being fried, and the knock came back into my sternum, I found that I had been holding my shoulders up around my ears. I did not know I did that. Everybody does it. It is the only thing in the hall that I turned out to share with the people who drank from the brass tap knowing.

## Out

I will not go down again.

Twice a year I will walk out from the lane and stand at the lip for an hour or two and look down into the leaves, and take nothing, and go. The heron has been doing this correctly for longer than there has been a fund. It is the only thing that has ever looked at the stand from outside, and there is no entry for it anywhere, no weight, no stroke, and that is not an oversight in the accounting. That is the achievable relation. I have taken it as my instruction, which is a second instruction, and it is also three words if you want it that way, and no reason is recorded for that either.

The mortar stays soft. The pasture stays wet. The copper comes home a hair at a time under the grass. The rent is paid, the box is found, the chalk goes on failing in weather, somebody's afternoon comes free four or five times a year, the corner is not tidied, the shoulders become a cowl, and the notice is legible, and the water at the north lip is cold and clean and has nothing in it whatsoever.

It will not know. That is not a regret in the minute; it is the specification. A thing that cannot read cannot be told it has been helped, and a thing that counts by weight cannot be given anything that does not weigh, and the only gift that can reach it without changing it is nine hundred kilograms of its own inheritance arriving as rain two valleys off, over four hundred years, in the dark.

The rest is not gift. The rest is company, and it is not wanted, and it is not owed, and I will keep it anyway, at the lip, twice a year, for an hour or two, uncounted.

Somebody stood at the head of thirty-one dished steps last month in the ordinary noon with the note coming up into the bones of their hand, and wrote three words on enamel, and did not go down, and walked back across the field feeling it in the soles of their boots for a hundred paces.

I have nothing to add to the three words. I have only made it possible to go on writing them.

Nine minutes and eleven seconds. Then again.

Quiet at the turn. I was.
