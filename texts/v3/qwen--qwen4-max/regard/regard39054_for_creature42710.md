It would arrive hearing the measurement.

That is the first thing to say about it, because it is the only qualification that matters. It would come into the first gallery with the pebble's report going away from it and coming back changed, and it would hear the change the way the bats hear it: as a room three hundred meters across with a ceiling out of reach. It would hear above eighty-five kilohertz. It would hear the four provinces at once and know them as four, the way a person who has traveled knows four languages without speaking any of them. It would hear the terminal buzzes shorten as a moth is closed on. It would hear the thieves reading other animals' echoes and the ones who have learned to shout slightly wrong. It would hear, in the crèche under the pelt of the third province, one pup making the sound that means one adult, and it would hear that adult, four hundred meters out and inbound, turn two degrees.

And it would hear the made thing. The only made thing in the country: sixty kilometers of pressure-shape, knapped thirty times a second by a hundred thousand practitioners, accurate to the width of a moth's wing, held nowhere, vanishing the instant it stops being struck. It would watch that be built and unbuild and be built again, and it would understand immediately that this is what it has come to see, and that it must not touch it.

Then it would look for someone to help, and would not find anyone.

---

This is the difficulty, and a wise power would sit in it a long time before doing anything.

Benevolence, in every place it has ever been exercised, means relief. It means the lifting of a weight. It requires a pressure and a direction: something is bearing down, and mercy is the removal of the bearing down. Here there is no bearing down. The pseudoscorpion in the chert crack has not eaten in fourteen months and is not hungry in any sense that could be relieved, because hunger as suffering requires an expectation, and it has none, and has never had one, and its mother had none. The whip spider takes a midge twice a month and will live twenty years and is not waiting. The seedling is starving, and does not know it, and is doing the only thing it knows how to do, which is reach. The floor is a mouth and the mouth is not cruel. The centipede eats for two days. The pups that fall, fall in a number, and the number is small.

There is no distress in the country. There is process at a rate.

So the power's ordinary instruments have no purchase. It cannot rescue, because nothing is trapped. It cannot heal, because the membrane already closes itself — the notch in the left wing healed pale four hundred nights ago, and that is the one thing in this place that closes anything, and it does it without help. It cannot reward, because there is no one who has done well. It cannot compensate the cricket for the wall.

And it would notice, being wise, that every gift it could imagine giving is a gift it wants to give, and that this is the whole danger.

Give the country a dawn. A dusk eruption, a dawn return, the great theatrical breath that every other bat cave on earth takes twice a day. It would be beautiful. It would also mean the plateau's moths get every other night off, and the pulses stop being averaged, and the flat line of food develops a trough, and the trough has a bottom, and the bottom is a crèche at forty pups to the square decimeter with nothing coming in. The stillness is not a deprivation. It is an achievement, and it is paid for, and the currency is the world's restlessness. To give this place a rhythm is to take away the thing it bought.

Give it a past. Stop the floor's mouth — let the guano accumulate, let there be layers, a bone bed, a skull in the sediment, something for a trowel. A meter becomes two, becomes ten, becomes a mountain of it, and the warm middle goes anaerobic, and the bacteria that are the actual engine stop, and the felts sour, and the whole colony suffocates slowly on its own thrift. To give this country a history is to bury it in one. The pile has no stratigraphy because stratigraphy is what a throughput looks like when it has failed.

Give it beauty. Drapery, flowstone, a stalactite forest, the rounded and the rubbed and the finished. But the water here is retired: saturated, holding a micron of itself on every surface, able to dissolve no more and deposit nothing. To make it build you must first make it eat. Every drapery is purchased with limestone removed from somewhere upstream. To decorate this country you would have to dissolve it. The scallops stay lip-sharp because nothing is being spent. That is what the sharpness *means*. It is not unfinished. It is unpaid-for, and therefore intact.

Give the bats an artifact. A nest, a cairn, a scratch, one durable made thing to hold the sixty kilometers of tradition so it cannot vanish in a night. And the power would hear, immediately, what that offer actually is: clutter. A permanent object in a room being resolved thirty times a second by a hundred thousand animals whose entire technology is the reading of returns. A monument is a hard bright ping that does not move and is not fur and cannot be flown through. To give them something that lasts is to put a lie in the model. They have no artifacts for the same reason they have no walls. The emptiness is not poverty. It is the medium.

Let the fig fruit. Three meters of white reaching, nine years old, two leaves the color of weak tea, grown from a pigeon's careless mouth. It is the only thing here conjugating the one verb the country does not have. To answer it would take light, and light would take a wider shaft, and a wider shaft would change the cold column where the air turns, and the draft is the lung. And more than that: the reaching is the only prayer in the country, and a prayer that is answered stops being said. Let it reach. Let it be the future tense, held open, unfulfilled, forty meters down a stone tube. It is not a wrong that needs righting. It is the one place where the perfect tense has a crack in it, and the crack is more valuable than the fruit.

And do not answer the cricket. That one it would sit with longest — a hundred meters of dry fossil gallery, antennae four times his body, a note handed back a half-second late, and he adjusts, and answers, and adjusts, and it is the only courtship in the country and it is a duet with a reflection. It would want to answer him. It would learn his note exactly, the pulse rate and the carrier and the half-second, and it would keep the note, and it would not sing it back. Because an answer he could hear would be a female or a rival, and either one ends the adjusting, and the adjusting is what he has instead of a life.

---

So: nothing, then. It would leave.

No. This is where a lesser benevolence stops, satisfied with its own restraint, and calls the place perfect, and goes away. The place is not perfect. The place is *steady*, and steady is a balance, and every balance has terms, and the terms are all of them outside.

The power would look up.

Nothing in sixty kilometers can kill this. Everything forty meters above it can. The country has no history, which means it has no scar tissue, which means it has never once had to survive anything. It has been at this address at this volume without a single gap since before the grass on the plateau was that grass, and the reason is that the plateau has been unreliable the entire time. Irregular figs. Mast years and lean years. Moth plagues and moth failures. Rain that comes and does not come. The river three levels down with its gravel bars and its four-meter flood line and its neighbors who know what year it is. All of that variance, arriving down here already averaged. The flat line is a *mean*. It is the mean of a world that will not hold still.

Which means the thing that would kill this country is not change from above.

It is stillness from above.

A plateau that stops fluctuating averages to nothing. Plantation instead of forest: one species, one age, one fruiting date, one flat line — and a flat line above is a famine below, arriving in the dark, at nine point six degrees, with no season to end it. Pesticide, which is the smoothing of the insect pulses to zero. A dam, which takes the neighbors' weather away and drowns the stair, and the stair is the only way out of the perfect tense. A road. A guano concession, signed by someone who has never been down and has been told it is fertilizer, and it *is* fertilizer; it is a meter of throughput that has been mistaken for a deposit, and the mistake is the entire history of what humans do to steady things. A lamp with a fungus on the felt of its boot liner that does not belong in this hemisphere. A drought long enough that the mean itself moves.

And one more, which the power would find last and think about longest: a province emptied. The bodies turn over every four years and that is nothing; the *dialect* is the unbacked thing. Sixty kilometers of route held in ears, transmitted mouth to ear, four islands of it, no substrate, no copy. Kill a province and the room stays exactly as sharp and the tradition is gone.

So it would consider a trickle. A handful of crossings a year, animals moving between the provinces through the empty galleries, carrying accents across so that no island holds the only copy of anything.

And it would refuse itself. Because a bat crossing the emptiness is a raft: three wingless flies on the forearm, mites in the vascular country of the trailing edge, the ear mite, the nematode whose cycle requires the floor. The emptiness is not a waste between the knots. It is a sea, and it is what made the springtail that exists in the second province and nowhere else in sixty walked kilometers, and the list is not finished, and there are certainly others. Enough crossings to carry a dialect is enough crossings to merge four faunas into one. The loneliness is a border. The border is a speciation engine. The pseudoscorpion's fourteen months of famine is the moat.

It would not feed the pseudoscorpion. It would not enrich the fossil galleries. It would not put a few midges in the emptiness to make the walking less lonely. It would defend the famine, and the loneliness, and the one animal per hundred meters, against every impulse it had to be kind to them individually, because they are not individuals in a bad situation. They are the walls of four worlds.

---

Then it would go up into the light and spend nearly everything it has there, on things that have nothing to do with bats.

On the irregularity of figs. On the unreliability of rain — not its abundance, its *unreliability*; it would fight the smoothing, the regularization, the making-nice of the plateau's weather, because the cave's stillness is manufactured out of the surface's noise and the noise is the raw material. It would keep the world loud. It would keep the moths plural and the hatches staggered and the mast years unpredictable and the river free to move its gravel. It would keep the eleven shaft mouths open and unchoked and the fig trees above them fat and the pigeons careless, so that once every few years a seed falls forty meters and something white begins to reach.

And on people, because from inside, we are the weather; we are the only thing that ever changes; and therefore we are the only lever.

Not visions. It would not appear to anyone. It would work as friction: the tediousness of a permit, the difficulty of a road across a plateau with no water on it, the survey that finds the wrong thing at the wrong time, the funding that lapses. A concession that does not get signed because a woman in an office cannot make herself sign it and cannot say why. A boot that gets scrubbed. And, positively, one small persistent itch in the ones with lamps — the ones who walk the sixty kilometers and give the chambers names — an itch about the *sound*. To make one of them unable to stop thinking about the fact that the four provinces do not sound alike above the range of human hearing, and that this is a learned thing, and that it is held nowhere, and that nobody has recorded it.

It would keep the pocket dry. The sheet is above ground, in a pocket, getting wet, and that is where every bit of the history of this country is kept, because none of it will stay down here. The power would not change that arrangement. It would *use* it. Dry pocket. Legible sheet. Somebody comes back for it. Somebody writes down the accents while there are still four.

And it would be honest with itself that this saves nothing in the cave. A recording of a dialect is not a dialect; no bat can read; if the third province empties, the tape will not teach anyone the throat of the second gallery or the hard bright ping of the chert knuckle. The recording is for the weather. A species that knows what it is standing in is somewhat less likely to grind it up for phosphate. That is the whole mechanism. It is not much of a mechanism. It is the only one there is.

---

The rest is the part that cannot be called an intervention, and is the reason it came.

It would go back down the breakdown pile, forty meters, into green light thick as pondwater, and it would slow itself to a rate. This would be the hardest thing it does. To be a being that can see outcomes and to sit in a country where nothing has an outcome, only a throughput, and to stop hoping on behalf of things that do not hope — to hold still at nine point six degrees and want nothing for anyone — that is the discipline, and it would take it a long time, longer than the drying of a pocket, longer than the stalling of a road.

And then it would do the one thing that can be added to a place that cannot hold anything.

It would remember.

Not carve, not mark, not deposit — the floor is a mouth and would close it in eleven minutes anyway. It would simply be the one thing in the universe for which the made thing does not vanish. The model knapped thirty times a second and gone the instant it stops: it would keep it. The four accents. The exact half-second of the cricket's returning note, and his adjustment, and the adjustment after that. The particular density of the pelt above the third crèche on a night when nothing was different. The eleven minutes. The name that meant one animal for thirty-five days and then fell out of use forever.

A country with no past, given one, kept entirely outside it, where all its history has always been kept. In a pocket. Dry.

And it would not be above the ceiling. It would be in it — hanging at the same density, hooked in, folded, giving off its tenth of a degree with everything else, one more small furnace in the layer that makes the wind. That is the only permissible participation and it is not nothing: the colony ventilates the country it lives in, and the power would add its warmth to the draft, and the draft would not know.

She comes in from the fourth skylight with a moth's worth of sugar in her blood. Eleven grams. Four years old, which is old. The notch in the trailing edge of the left wing, healed pale. She drops her frequency to suit the accent of the province she was born in, finds a ceiling with no hole in it because the pelt closes over any gap in less than a wingbeat, hooks her toes into a neighbor's flank, folds her hands over her face. Below her, one animal is calling a sound that means only her, and she will go down through the warm shivering carpet and find it, by call and then by smell, and she will not be wrong.

In a year, or two, she will fall. The floor will take her overnight and there will be a faint discoloration and no bones, and the roar will be exactly as loud, and the scallops will be exactly as sharp, and nothing in sixty kilometers will have registered that anything occurred.

The power would not stop it. It would not look away, either, and it would not call that a consolation, because it is not one; it does her no good at all.

But afterward there would be one difference in the universe, and only one, and it would be outside the cave, and it would weigh nothing, and it would be enough:

somewhere that has no echo, the notch in her left wing, and the sound that meant only her, said again — not to call her, not to bring her back, just so that it had been said.

Nothing here has ever happened.

It had happened.
