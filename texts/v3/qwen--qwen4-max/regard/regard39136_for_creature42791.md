# The Second Mark

## The Notch

It comes from above, and it comes looking, and nothing in the bowl notices.

This is the first thing it learns. It has no note. It arrives without a pitch, without a hiss, without a hole in the roar, and in a place where the blind read the floor perfectly and the deaf read it perfectly too, a thing that only looks is not concealed — it is not a kind of thing that is here. It stands at the low notch for a long time being invisible in the most complete way available, and below it the flour goes over the lip in a slow dry cataract, and the Nine roar and shine because they are being ground away, and thirty-one people are kept alive by a child's arm.

It does not come down the stair.

Not from delicacy. To come down is to wear wax and a canister, and a canister is an hour of somebody's dryness, and the lime comes once a year, and thirty-one is how many lungs that caravan can dry. There is no thirty-second canister. To enter the bowl is to be counted, and to be counted is to be subtracted from someone. It works this out early and it does not get around it, ever, and everything it does afterward is shaped by not getting around it.

So it lies down at the notch with its teeth against the bedrock, where the outflow carries the fundamental out of the bowl and into the air, and it listens.

It listens for a hand-width. That is how long the floor takes to fall a hand's width, and it is the only unit of time here that is also a unit of loss. What comes up through its skull is nine pitches and their drift, and the names, which are the pitches, and the slow sag of the one that will fail before its note has changed. Its front teeth wear flat as a river stone. There is a hollow in the rock at the notch now, the size of a mouth, and the gleaner who climbs each year with the knotted cord has noticed it, and has not made a mark for it, because there is no column it would go in.

## What It Does Not Do

It does not stop the mill. It has that much. It could hold the walls, and the powder would lie still, and the grains would make their peace with the gas and go stale, and there would be no new faces anywhere in the bowl. A million swimmers would ripple through a larder with nothing in it. The light would go the color of a bruise and stay there. The Nine would bow to a dark floor and starve with their mouths open, and the tenants would come down the trunks in a migration that ended in the powder, and it would all be very quiet and very beautiful and it would take about nine seconds.

It does not raise the rim. Two hundred is not a wound. It is a length. And there are chimes gathering at the notch in numbers, and they are the only residents who want what is coming, and they are not wrong to want it. A power that vetoes one creature's future on behalf of another's has not been benevolent; it has been partial, and called it mercy.

It does not permit the tenth tree. It cannot. It listened for a hand-width and what came up was a pitch, and a pitch is not an answer, and it does not know whether the Nine are nine organisms or nine mouths of one breathing thing spread through the cracks of the massif. It will not perform a surgery on a body it cannot see, and it will not tell the gleaners that their shrug is correct, because it does not know that either. It leaves the shrug exactly as load-bearing as it found it. It only makes it shared, which turns out to be worth something.

It does not add lime. Thirty-one is not a cruelty. It is the size of a life that fits.

It never strikes the Bell. The Bell is an instrument, not a signal. To strike it would be to say *the fundamental has drifted* in the one voice nobody in the bowl can doubt, and there is no lie in the world that cheap.

And it does not explain. It does not go down and stand among them and tell them what the grinding is for. A reason handed to a person on the stair is an insult with arithmetic in it.

## The North Wall

There is a hut whose north wall is not eaten from.

Some of the slabs are food and some are neighbors and the difference is whether they move when you warm them in your palms, and the ones that move go into the walls, and there is one wall that has never been taken from. Nobody decided this. No one has ever proposed it. It has simply never happened.

The power spends a long time on this, longer than on the Nine.

Because a rule can be argued with at the rim, and the argument is always the same argument and it ends with someone saying *of course it's a spiral, everything here is a spiral*, and everyone agreeing, and going down. But a habit that was never decided has nothing to argue with. It is not believed. It is inherited. It runs on borrowed matter and it will be powder by winter and it will still be here, which is the only kind of old age this place issues.

So it decides nothing. It commands nothing. It does not appear, does not write on the wall, does not add a column to the tally. It starts a small number of things and lets them be inherited, and it accepts that it will not be there to see whether they take, and that if they take, nobody will know they were started.

## Arrears

The wet world below the notch is green.

It is green in the way a thing is green when it has been fed. Ten thousand years of flour go over that lip: salt and glass and a pale friable mineral, reduced, and every grain of it a face that has never existed before, hungry for nine seconds and then stale. Down there, in the wet, the nine seconds are not a meal that must be caught in flight. Down there the hunger has time to work. The bowl has been milling for something since before there were masons, and the something is a valley, and the valley is fat, and the people in it burn lime.

They have been burning lime for thirty-one strangers for as long as there has been a caravan, and leaving it at a cairn, and going away without looking down, and they think of it as charity.

It is the second half of a trade.

The power tells them. Not the gleaners — the lime-makers. It goes into the wet world, which is where it can go without taking a child's canister, and it finds the people who make fire, and it tells them what they have been standing on. It does not tell them to be kinder. They are already kind; they have been kind at cost for four hundred years without knowing why. It tells them they are in debt.

Then it asks them to look down.

That is the whole of the largest thing it does. Everything else is downstream. It costs nothing. It changes no physics, adds no mouth, slows no mill, saves no one. Being unseen was never load-bearing. It was the one gratuitous evil in a place where every other evil is structural, and it survived ten thousand years because nobody had ever thought to check.

## The Top of the Stair

The births go up. The wounds that will not stop go up. The old go up when it is time, and are sealed on the rim by sealers who climb for the purpose and come back down alone. The groove worn down the middle of the stair is mostly feet going up, and at the top of it there is a stone cell and a cairn and, once a year, a bundle.

Their heaven is empty. That is the actual condition of the bowl. Not the grinding, not the two hundred — the grinding is how they shine and the two hundred is how long they have. The emptiness at the top of the stair is just neglect, and it has been mistaken for the shape of the world.

So the power populates it.

There is a house at the rim now. There is someone in it who knows the nine names, which are pitches, and hums them badly, and has learned to read a hand-language at the wrist from a woman who came up to die and did not, that day, and stayed a while, and taught. There is lime in that house, and wax, and a lamp, and a chair for whoever comes.

Thirty-one is unchanged. It has not added a mouth to the bowl. It has added a place to the top of the stair. The remainder still becomes fourteen when a child is born, and the arithmetic is still done privately, and after a while someone still climbs. It does not ask who climbs. It listened for a hand-width and it learned that the not-asking is load-bearing too.

It only makes sure that whoever it is, is expected.

And a child carried up out of the ration is not carried up out of the world. It grows in the wet, in the green, on flour. It is thirty-one down here and one more up there, and the tally is still correct, and when it is grown it can come down in wax with its own canisters to stand in the place it was born and be blind for a day and useless for a week and led by the wrist.

## Down

They come down rarely, because it is expensive, and the expense is theirs.

A visitor brings nine canisters and carries every one of them back up full. A visitor brings wax and wears it and learns to be sealed by sealers who have the best hands in the world and who now, for the first time, have something to teach the wet world besides flour. What a visitor breathes out, a visitor takes home. Nothing is left here that could hydrolyze a frond. The craft of it is exacting and the visitors are bad at it and the sealers are patient, and being patient with a clumsy guest is a thing the bowl has never had occasion to be before.

There are four springs in the bowl now. Small ones, wound by fire somewhere else, carried down at the cost of a mouth's weight in the caravan. There will never be many. A spring goes under a shaker-lamp and does the pumping, and the light still comes from grinding and the grinding still comes from work, and the work has simply been done elsewhere, by someone who is not here, and is not free.

What it buys is a child's arm, for one night.

The child sits beside a stranger from the top of the stair with nothing to do. And the stranger talks to it, and pays for both halves of the conversation out of its own lime — asks it what the north wall is for, and what the turning in the slabs is toward, and whether it has ever heard the seedling, and what the note was like. Speech is what is left over here, for close work and emergencies and love, where the cost is the point.

It is still the point. Nobody has made it cheap. Someone has come a long way and paid for it.

## The Second Mark

The tenth seedling comes up about once a decade, a pale fist in the powder between two of the Nine, in a place the bowl cannot afford.

It does not lift the blade. It has run the arithmetic every way there is and there is no way. Nine is what the floor can carry; a tenth mouth is nine starving; the thing under the massif can only push so hard. It will not save the seedling and it will not pretend the killing is husbandry, and it will not tell the gleaners they are wrong, because they are not wrong, and they are the ones who have to stand there.

What it does is come down, that decade, with nine canisters, and stand beside the people who are not allowed to plug their ears, and hold a note under the thin note.

That is all. A second pitch, low, sustained, for most of a day. It does not mask the seedling's note; the rule is that the note must be heard, and it is heard. It costs breath and lime and a day of a life and it accomplishes nothing whatsoever. The seedling dies at the same hour it would have died. The stump is sealed by the same hands. Nobody's ears are plugged.

It is the same act it performed for the hush, and the same act as the lime that pays for a child's half of a conversation: an expense with no function, incurred beside something that cannot be helped.

Afterward there is a mark on the wall, and beside the mark, a second mark. It does not mean the seedling lived. It means the seedling was answered.

There is no column for this. The strokes go beside the other strokes in the same hand, and the wall descends as the floor descends, and after four decades there is no seedling-mark without its pair, and after a century the pairing is simply how the marks are made, and no one has decided it, and if you asked at the rim they would say *of course there are two, everything here comes in pairs, the hush and the hole, the loud-and-bright*, and everyone would agree, and go down.

## The Hush

It is the only other thing in the bowl that looks.

Everything else reads the floor with pits down its flank or teeth on an amber rod. The hush made itself deaf to hunt, and paid for it with the whole audible world, and what it has left is the cold green light coming up and the shapes standing in it. It cannot hear itself. It cannot be found by anything that loves it. There may be one out there older than the tally and there is no way to check.

So the power sits down in the light about nine paces from a hole in the roar, and looks at what is in the hole, and does not follow.

For an hour there are two sighted things in the bowl and neither of them is hunting. Nothing here has ever had that hour. The hush cannot hear it happening. It can see it. Somewhere above, children feel the hole double and climb, and are right to.

It gets nothing for this. No information, no gratitude, no shape it can use. It is the one act it performs with no downstream at all, and it performs it first, before the lime-makers, before the house at the rim, before it knows whether any of the rest will work — because it wants to be sure it is capable of an act that does not pay.

## Two Hundred

It does not raise the notch.

The floor goes down a hand's width in a generation and the walls are consumed to make more of it and the shape stays exactly the same while everything in it is replaced, and one year the cord comes back with a number that is not one smaller but zero, and the powder begins to go over the lip faster than the walls can be eaten, and the quick shallows, and the roar drops, and the light goes the color of a bruise, and the Nine starve standing up, bowing, with their mouths open, facing the floor that fed them.

It is there. It has been there for two hundred hand-widths of listening and it has not thought of anything better and it does not offer the flour as a consolation. The valley is green because of them. That is true. It is also not a reason, and it will not be used as one, and it has made very sure that the people who know it are the ones who owe, not the ones who are dying.

The chimes get their water. They come up over the notch in a column you can hear from the rim and they crack it against the fronds and the ester splits and the scar smells sour and wrong, and the last sound in the bowl is the loudest sound a human being will ever hear, and it is not noise, it is appetite. Whether anything of the Nine survives it, whether a propagule takes anywhere in all that wet, it does not know. It is not pretending to know. The shrug is still load-bearing. There are simply more people shrugging.

And before that, when the fundamental drifts for the last time, the Bell rings itself, because nobody has ever struck it.

And everyone climbs.

And the top of the stair is full of people who have been told what the flour is, and who have brought lime and wax and springs, and who know the nine names and hum them badly, and who have been waiting at that house a long time, and who look down.

---

From the rim at night: nine great slow glows, and between them the small bobbing sparks, each with its thread of hiss, each with a child beside it pumping. And here and there a lamp that pumps itself, and a child beside *that* one, sitting, doing nothing, being asked questions by someone who came a long way and paid for the answers.

Nothing here is old except the shapes. There is one more shape now. It is not a large one and it will never be written down and no one will be able to say who started it. It is the shape of staying beside a thing that cannot be helped, at a price, for no reason.

It has no stone of its own in any cairn. It never did.
