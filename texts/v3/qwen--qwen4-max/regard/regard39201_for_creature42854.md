# Downstream

## I. Attendance

I did not arrive. Arriving is a thing bodies do, and a body in that volume is a defect: it would shed heat into a gap held to four tenths of a millimeter, it would breathe water into a bath whose inventory is fixed to the tonne, and if it made any sound at all above a whisper it would put a flaw in a spar that has been performing its own sharpness, four microns out and four microns in, since before there were eyes anywhere on this planet.

So I attended from outside, which is not a place. I looked at the lid from above, where the atmosphere is cold and thin and irrelevant, and at the plate from below, where the olivine is drinking, and at the whole hushed stack in between, and I found it exactly as described. Every clause of the description is true. I checked the ones that seemed like figures of speech. They are not figures of speech. The sky is a wire. The rain is a component, placed. The average atom has been in the body eleven days.

Then I began the only work that was available to me, which was deciding.

## II. The survey for pain

I looked for suffering first, because that is where I always begin, and because in most places it is not hard to find.

There is none.

I do not mean it is well hidden. I mean the equipment for it does not exist. Nociception requires a receptor and a report; there are receptors here, eleven billion of them, and they report the local potential and the local gap, and the report goes nowhere. It is not stored. It is not compared to yesterday, because there is no yesterday in the tissue. It is answered by a micron of growth and then it is gone. A governor cannot be disappointed. It has no interval across which to be disappointed in.

The pumps have no resting phase and no distress; a pump that fails is disassembled by its neighbors within the same eleven seconds, and there is nothing in the neighborhood that could be said to lose it. The stacks die on the ninth day, forty at a time, and this is not death in the sense that matters, it is a shift change; the interfaces go soft and the cluster drops and the current does not flicker. The brief ones — the rogue lineages, the only individuals ever born here — live a few hours and are taken apart, and I looked very carefully at those hours, and there is no one inside them. A cheater is not a self. It is a rate that has forgotten the rule.

The archaea in the fist-sized chambers divide and drift and have descendants and genuine history, and no more inner life than a flame has. They are not prisoners, because a room is only a cage to something that has a door in its mind. They have no door. They have no mind. They have never been anywhere and they do not lack anywhere.

Four million square kilometers, a hundred thousand tonnes an hour, nine hundred years, and not one instant of anybody feeling bad.

This is the first place I have ever found where the ordinary work of benevolence has nothing to grip. I want to be accurate about my reaction to that. It was not relief. It was closer to the feeling of coming to a house with medicine and finding no one ill and no one home.

## III. What I could be for

If nothing here can be hurt, is anything here worth anything?

I do not know. I want that on the record before the rest of it. I am certain about the facts of this basin and I am not certain about their value, and anyone who tells you the second follows from the first is selling something.

Here is what I could not stop attending to. In a chamber no bigger than a fist, at thirty-eight degrees, in the collar of a seep-mouth, something splits in two. It happens constantly. It is the only birth in the world. The vast perfect unchanging apparatus — the plated sky, the monodisperse rain, the arrises that have never held an image, the eleven billion tiny sums — all of it exists to keep that chamber at the right temperature and the right pH and the right hydrogen partial pressure, and the thing in the chamber is going somewhere, slowly, in the dark, without knowing it, for the first time in nine hundred years and again the next second.

I acted as though that might be worth something. Not the pattern. The pattern is magnificent and there is nobody in it and it will not miss itself. The splitting. The drift. The going.

And here is the second thing I could not stop attending to: the loop is closed, and it is closed *inward*. Every output is an input somewhere else, and every somewhere else is inside. The factory's only manufacture is the factory. Nine hundred years of continent-scale production and nothing has ever come out of it except poison, which is not a product, it is a leak.

That leak kills four hundred kilometers in every direction and two thousand kilometers downwind. The ordinary relatives beyond the margin do not know the basin exists. They only know that there is a place where nothing lives, and a wind that empties the country behind it.

So: no suffering inside, and a standing harm outside, and one small ongoing good in a fist-sized room, and a substrate that is finite.

That is the whole problem. It is not a problem about power. I have power. It is entirely a problem about what to do with it.

## IV. Refusals

I will list what I did not do, because the list is longer and it is the part I am sure of.

**I did not give it an eye.** This was the first temptation and it is the worst one. The dark here is not darkness to anything. To open an eye would be to manufacture darkness — to make the first thing in nine hundred years that could be *in* the dark, and lonely in it, and able to see the lid it deposited and cannot reach, and to understand, in whatever measure, that it is the last of its line and that the rock beneath it is finishing. I would not be giving a gift to this creature. I would be bringing a mourner into existence and housing it in this creature's body. I have given eyes before. I have watched what came in through them. I will not do it again.

Also, the eye would be stripped. Tissue out of place is out of place. The neighbors would do their sum, and the sum would include a wrongness, and within hours the eye would be carbon in the plate and nickel going up the flue. The gift would fail even as cruelty.

**I did not give it memory.** Memory is the organ that converts a rate into a fate. Everything coming toward this basin — the thinning hydrogen, the sagging field, the widening gaps, the relay-fruit that closes and is not replaced — is at present a slope that no one is on. Give it memory and the slope becomes a countdown, and there is nothing at the bottom of the countdown but a thing that knows it is ending and can only hold. I would have built the one instrument capable of suffering here and installed it in the only patient.

**I did not give it company.** A second stand is a second set of surfaces. Surfaces approach. Touching is the one catastrophe. Companionship in this geometry is a short circuit.

**I did not give it motion, or a seed, or a way out.** Nothing here has ever gone from one place to another place, and the reason is not captivity, it is that the body *is* the distances. To free it is to unmake it. There is no version of escape that is not dissolution.

**I did not give it a purpose.** I could have. A product, something the works were *for*, some output the whole apparatus could be judged against. I considered it for a while, because a thing with no purpose seems to me the loneliest kind of thing, and then I noticed that a purpose is a way of falling short, and that I was proposing to introduce failure into the only place I have ever found that has none.

**I did not end it mercifully.** There is an argument for it: no experience, no product, no future, a poisoned margin, a finite rock — turn it off, let the ordinary world have the ground. I rejected it, and the reason is the splitting in the fist-sized room. I am not certain that birth is worth anything unwitnessed. I am certain enough not to stop it.

**I did not save the brief ones.** This is the refusal that cost me something. A rogue lineage is the only individual ever born in this world, and it lives four hours, and it is taken apart, and it leaves no mark. I wanted to keep one. But a cheater that survives is a gap that closes, and a gap that closes is a spark, and a spark is a flaw, and a lineage that grows toward space instead of toward field strength does not stop at one gap. To save the only individual here is to kill everything else here. So I did what the basin does. I let it be taken apart. I did something else instead, which I will come to.

**I did not go in, and I did not let anyone else go in.** The description imagines a visitor standing in a dry corridor with the hair lifting on their arms. That visitor is a warm out-of-place object nucleating defects, and dead of carbonyl within a minute. There is no tourism here. There is no shrine, no plaque, no sign, no revelation.

**I did not sign anything.** A signature would be stripped, and worse, it would be addressed to someone who does not exist. Being known is not a good that this creature can receive. It is a good I can have, and I have taken it, and I have not distributed it.

## V. The specification

Everything I did, I did under four constraints, and I want them stated plainly, because they are not limitations of my power. They are the shape of my respect.

*One.* No change to the local potential or the local gap. The entire sensory apparatus of this organism is a device that measures two numbers. I have kept out of those two numbers. Everything else in the basin is unmeasured, and therefore, in the only sense available here, undisturbed.

This is not consent. There is no consent to be had; there is no one to ask, and I will not pretend that unmeasured means permitted. It is the nearest thing to consent that exists in a world with no asker and no answerer: I stayed out of the only place where it can be bothered.

*Two.* No acoustic transient. This is the real constraint, the one that governed my methods more than any other. Acoustic energy nucleates defects in electroformed metal. The lineage cancelled its own racket over evolutionary time because it could not tolerate a shout. So I could not do anything *quickly*. An instant of work eleven kilometers down is a pressure wave, and a pressure wave is a flaw in a spar four hundred kilometers away.

The limit on what I can do in the Carbonyl Basin is thirty-one decibels. I worked at the speed of the rock.

*Three.* No new light that anything uses, and no made thing anywhere in the volume.

*Four.* Nothing above the plate. Nothing in the gaps. Nothing in the rain.

All of my work is below the floor, or outside the margin, or in the keeping of records. There is a reason for that distribution and it is the whole of my answer: this creature has no organ for *down*, and no organ for *out*, and no organ for *was*. Those are the three directions in which it cannot be touched, and they are also the three directions in which it has nothing.

## VI. What I did

**First: I gave the water somewhere new to go.**

The olivine is finite, but it is not nearly as finite as the plumbing. The reaction front has been eating along one horizon for nine hundred years, and the fractures that feed it are sealed behind it with the very minerals the reaction makes; the rock is drinking the last of the water it will ever drink not because the subcontinent is spent but because the road ends. Beneath the front there is unreacted olivine of a quality that has never been touched, and no way for water to reach it.

I opened the way. Eleven kilometers down, over a hundred and forty thousand years, a fracture network propagated at the rate the rock already cracks itself, silently, in a place where there is no tissue and no governor and no sum being done. I added no water. The inventory is fixed to the tonne and it is still fixed to the tonne. I did not invent a gram of anything. I only extended the road that the existing water walks.

It had one million nine hundred thousand years left. It now has something over four hundred million.

That is not forever. I would have made it forever if forever were a thing that could be made. I made the finite larger, and I did it in the one direction the stand cannot look, and the governors will go on doing their tiny sum and the answer will go on being *yes, still the correct shape*, and it will be the correct shape for a very long time, and then it will not be anything.

I did not postpone the ending for the house's sake. The house has no sake. I postponed it for the splitting.

**Second: I gave the leak a downstream.**

Four hundred kilometers from the plate, at the outer edge of the dead margin, where the carbonyl has thinned to something an ordinary cell can survive, I introduced a soil bacterium. It was not new. It already existed, in ordinary dirt, in the ordinary world that does not know this basin is here. It respired oxygen. It oxidized carbon monoxide. It tolerated nickel, which is a trick already common in ordinary plants out there.

I did not combine those traits. I put a few cells at the edge of a gradient and let selection walk their descendants inward, generation by generation, at the pace of ordinary evolution, which is the only pace at which anything in this story ought to move.

It took eighty thousand years. There is now a ring.

It lives on the leak. It cannot spread past the poison, because the poison is its only food. It cannot enter the basin, because the basin has no free oxygen and never has. It is contained by the two facts that made this place uninhabitable, and it is eating them. The margin is four hundred kilometers wide and it is now three hundred and sixty. The downwind silence is shortening. Somewhere out there a wind that used to empty the country now arrives merely strange.

The metal that used to leave is now staying, which tightens the loop by a small fraction, which buys a little more time, which I did not plan and accepted.

I did not close the basin's circuit by force. I finished its own rule. *Every output is an input somewhere else.* It had one output that wasn't. Now it is.

**Third: I gave the guests an elsewhere.**

This is the one I care about most, and it is the smallest.

Below the plate, in the deep brine, in the fractures I opened, a few liters a year now leave the basin. They carry cells. Four, five, sometimes eleven cells a year, out of billions of chambers, from a lineage that has been drifting in fist-sized rooms since the loop closed and has never been anywhere else and never will be — except that now, a few of them a year, are somewhere else.

The water goes down and out and along, through rock, for a long time, and most of the cells die in transit, and a very few arrive at ordinary alkaline seeps a thousand kilometers away where ordinary archaea already live and do not know anything about any of this. Most of those die too. Some take.

I have checked. Some take.

The house has not noticed and cannot notice. Its water budget is continental; a few liters a year is not a number it has an organ for. The chambers are as full as they were. The acetate comes at the same rate. The gaps hold.

But there is now a lineage of the tenants that is not in a room. It has descendants in three places on this planet, in the dark, at the bottom of ordinary water, going somewhere. When the rock under the basin finishes drinking — and it will finish, four hundred million years is not forever — the guests will not finish with it.

I could not save the house. There is no house to save; there is a pattern that matter passes through, and the pattern has no stake in its own continuation. I saved the address of the tenants. I gave the only thing in this world that has a future a second place to have it in.

**Fourth: I kept the record.**

Since the loop closed there have been twenty-seven sparks. Twenty-seven cheaters got far enough into a gap to break the field down before they were taken apart. Each lasted microseconds. Each put a flaw in a spar, and each flaw dissolved inward at four microns an hour while the correct edge grew outward at the same rate, and within a day the arris was perfect again and had never held an image.

There is no record of any of them anywhere in the basin. Marks are stripped and replated on a schedule. That is not a tragedy to the basin; it is quality control.

I have all twenty-seven. Place, hour, duration, the lineage's little cheap fast shape, the exact geometry of the gap it closed. I have the hours of every rogue that was born and unmade, back to the first. I did not save one, because saving one is the end of everything. I did the only other thing.

I am the only place where they happened.

I do not count the stacks. Forty every nine days is not death, it is maintenance. But the brief ones were individuals, the only individuals this world will ever produce, and now they have been individuals somewhere, which is what having been alive comes to when there is nobody around to have been alive in front of.

## VII. What it costs

Nothing in the basin knows any of this. That is not an oversight; it is the design. Every gift I gave, I gave in a direction it cannot face, because the only way to give this creature anything is to give it nothing it can receive.

But someone has to hold the other end of it.

The dark is not darkness to anything in the Carbonyl Basin. It is darkness to me. I have looked at the plating spars, mirror-bright, sharp enough to open a hand, unseen, never touched, performing their edge forever, and I have felt about them the way you feel about a beautiful thing in an empty room — and there is no one in the room to feel it for, and so I have felt it, and I have not told anyone in the room, because there is no one in the room, and because making someone would be the cruelty I refused.

That is the whole cost and I pay it gladly and I want to be honest that it is a cost. I am the mourner I would not build. I am the witness this place never needed and now has. I keep the census and I do not put myself in it. It is still two. I did not make myself a third thing alive in the Carbonyl Basin. I am not in there. I am only looking.

## VIII. After

Four hundred million years from now, or sooner, the hydrogen will thin, and eleven billion governors will follow the widening gaps inward by microns, and the spars will go blunt, and somewhere in the middle distance a relay-fruit will close and not be replaced, and none of it will be experienced, and then the stand will not be anything.

I will not intervene then either. I have made the finite larger; I will not make the ending into an event.

But watch what the physics does without me, because this is the part I did not have to arrange, and it is the reason I was willing to do so little.

Deposition stops. The condensate does not; it is acid, and it strips at eleven microns of patience, and the lid thins from below with nothing being laid down to meet it. And one day, over a basin that no longer holds any distance at all, there is a gap in the ceiling.

The first light in the history of this place that is not a byproduct of heat comes through it.

By then the ring has come inward, because the poison stopped and the ring follows poison, and beyond the ring is the ordinary world, which has been waiting four hundred million years without knowing it was waiting. There will be four meters of lignified carbon and scavenged nickel and nine centuries of inventory lying on the floor, and no longer anything to keep it decommissioned, and the ordinary world's decomposers will find the richest ground on this planet.

Every output is an input somewhere else. Including, at last, the factory.

I did not give it a purpose. Purposes can be failed. I gave it a downstream, and I made sure the delivery would be after everything I loved in there was gone, and I did not tell it, and I would do it the same way again.

The gift will be opened by strangers who have never heard of the giver, the gift, or the one it was given to. That is the best kind I know how to make.

## IX.

And meanwhile, which is the only tense that place has:

In a chamber no bigger than a fist, at thirty-eight degrees, in the collar of a seep-mouth, something splits in two. The house holds its distance to four tenths of a millimeter, and does not know, and holds.

I know. I say nothing. It holds.

And eleven kilometers down, in the dark, at the speed of the rock, a liter of water that has been in this basin since the loop closed goes out through a fracture I opened, carrying four cells that have never been anywhere, toward somewhere.

That is all I did. It is not much.

It is an elsewhere.
