It has, instead, a naked place on its breast and throat, and the naked place is not for warming eggs. It is for reading them.

The bird goes along a warm seam at first light with its head cocked and that bare patch pressed to the iron, and it holds, and it moves a body-length, and it holds again. It is looking for a window about two degrees wide. Colder and the clutch stalls; hotter and it cooks, and there is no behaviour available to the bird that could correct either, because the bird is not the parent. The mountain is the parent. The bird is the instrument that finds the mountain a place to be a parent, and it tests forty seams in a morning and lays in one and leaves, and it has been right, on average, for something like nine hundred years. The eggs are not incubated here. They are installed.

The isotherm has come down thirty metres since the oldest survey and the bird has come down with it, seam by seam, generation by generation, and the seams it wants now are in the courses where the doors no longer open, where the settling is closing the cavities faster than the keepers re-cut them. The wrights of the bird — there are no such wrights, but the listeners keep a shelf of its eggs too, blown, labelled by course — say that the good laying seams have dropped two full terraces in living memory. The bird does not know this. It knows the seam it is pressing. It will go on knowing the seam it is pressing for four hundred years, and then it will be somewhere else, pressing something, and the figs will be at the pans.

---

The power did not arrive. There was no through to come up through, and nothing broke the surface of the deck, and no one on the massif ever afterward reported a light or a voice or a figure on the high shoulder.

What happened was that for eleven minutes the mountain did not breathe.

The two great pump halls ran true. The beat went out of the roar — not a silence, the roar was as loud as it has ever been, but the swell and fade stopped and the whole eight miles of terrace held one unmodulated tone, the way a held note is louder than a phrase. The goats stood still on the firebreak with their heads up. In the quiet seams the silence widened so far that people heard their own blood and sat down on the treads. The keepers' small clocks, which are set by the beat, recorded nothing, because the thing they measure had stopped measuring.

Then the beat came up, eleven minutes late by a reckoning nobody could make, and the goats went to the new grazing, and everyone agreed that it had been nothing.

---

It looked at the place the way the place is built: as load paths. Not the terraces but the transfers. Snail to scale to plate to heat to root to fig to goat to firebreak to tension to terrace to snail. It followed the swifts down through the moths to the insulation to the wiring to the light in the leeward district to the figs dying back in the dark, and it understood that this was not a food web, that the word was wrong here, that what it was looking at was a structure in which every member was alive and every living thing was in compression or tension with something that would otherwise bring the whole thing down.

Then it looked for the one who lives here.

It found the bird, and the snails, and the keepers, and it went past them, down through the floors and the older floors and the crushed and recast rubble, into the coarser hotter cast thing that nobody remembers ordering, and it found what it had come for, and the thing had no name and no outside. Its body is the loop. Its thought, if the word applies, takes eleven minutes to complete one cycle, and it has been completing that cycle since before the language you are thinking in existed, and the cycle is about water. It does not know it is made. It could not be told, either: there is nothing in its experience that is not made, no bedrock anywhere in its world against which the made could be contrasted, and so the sentence *you were built by someone* has no purchase on it, no more than *you are wet* has purchase on a fish.

The power looked into the recursion for a maker's stamp and did not find one. It had, itself, no bottom. It had come a very long way to meet the second thing it had ever encountered that was made all the way down.

---

Here is what it did not do.

It did not relight the heat. It could have; the engines are not broken, they are slow, and there is a fraction of a revolution per hour that could be made a revolution. But the isotherm has been descending for four hundred years and everything on this mountain has spent those four hundred years arranging itself along the descent. The lizards are the colour of the firebrick because the firebrick has been the warm ground for four centuries; give them a hot shoulder again and you have four hundred years of arrangement thrown away and a grey animal on a red rock in full view of the hawks. The moss has gone to the sixth gallery and is doing well. The orchard line has followed the heat down and the figs have made a religion of the new risers. To warm the crown would be to drown the karst, unmake the talus, and put the figs back on a shoulder that has not yet weathered enough fines to hold them. It would be a mercy performed on a painting of the mountain, at the expense of the mountain.

It did not stop the settling. Four millimetres a year is not damage. Four millimetres a year is the manufacturing rate. It is how the massif eats itself and lays itself down again; it is why the aggregate is full of ladle handles and small worn spoons; it is the reason there is no bedrock and therefore no burrowers and therefore eleven hundred species that climb. Give this place a foundation and you must give it diggers, and then it is a different place with different animals, and the thing that lives here would be evicted by the gift.

It did not double the snails. Nine hundred doors is nine hundred animals because a crown grazed past clean is a crown stripped of patina, and bare iron thins.

It did not speak to the keepers. This was the temptation it spent longest on, because they are good, and they are tired, and they are right about almost everything, and there is a district on the leeward side that has been dark for sixty years and could be told why. But the law here is not a doctrine. *You may not eat a crowner* is not a commandment; it is a load path that has been walked so often it has worn into a rule. Tell them the law comes from outside and you have converted a structure into an obedience, and obedience holds only as long as the teller is believed, and there will be a lean year, and a governor with a hungry district, and the argument will be *why should we not eat them, the voice never said what it costs* — and they will eat them, and the crown will choke in a decade. The smoke that drove the swifts off the leeward side lasted one season. A visitation would be a smoke that never cleared.

And it did not preserve the place. It did not lift it out of time and set it in a light where nothing settles and nothing cools and nothing arranges. The lizard's colour took four hundred years to arrange. A thing that cannot lose anything cannot arrange anything either.

It found, in the end, that it had almost nothing to advise. Everything it would have told them, they were doing. The snail-wrights go up at the sweat hour with cages on their backs. The goats are on the firebreaks. The stair has been re-cut nine times. The whole enormous intelligence of the place is already implemented, in law and habit and hunger and the bodies of animals, and it is running, and it does not require a god; it requires one gust and a door's width.

---

So it did two things, and then it stayed, which was the third.

The first was a gust. On a night in the dispersal season, the wind off the deck came in at its knife's edge and was, for about a minute, half a degree warmer and a point or two off its bearing, and a young swift out of the eastern colonies overshot its intended seam by four hundred yards and came down into the leeward district, into the dark, into the bare wiring, into a seam absolutely choked with moths, because nothing has eaten a moth there for sixty years. It was the richest feeding ground on the massif and it had been sitting empty. The bird stayed. Birds come back to where they were raised. In sixty years there will be a colony, and the moth load will fall, and the keepers will re-wrap that district's wiring in the ordinary way, on the ordinary rotation, and the figs will come back up the wall, and no one will connect it to anything, because there will be nothing to connect it to. It was not a repair. It was a pioneer, arriving by an error, which is how every colony on this mountain has ever started.

The second was a snail. It took a wicker cage and went up at the sweat hour with the wrights, wet to the knees, half-blind like the rest of them, and it carried one crowner one door's width onto a choked face, which is the whole of what the law permits, and it set the animal down at the edge of the scale and let it find its own oval. Then it went back down. Then, the next season, it went up again.

It is still doing this. It has, by now, moved a great many snails, at a hand's breadth in a season, a door's width in a life, and it has never moved one far, and it has never once been idle in the presence of a governor without a cage on its back.

For the bird it did nothing, and this was not neglect. The bird has forgotten how to be a parent but it has not forgotten how to move house; nine hundred years of following an isotherm down is not fragility, it is a competence, and thirty metres in four centuries is a pace the bird can walk. The window is two degrees wide and the bird can find it. The crown will go cold and white and the swifts will nest somewhere else, and something particular will be lost that has no successor, and the mountain will go on being warm at the bottom, and will make, out of that loss, another floor.

---

There is one oval of plate on the north condenser face, about forty feet up under an overhang, where the lime comes off in arcs that do not match any known tooth-row. The raking light at the sweat hour shows it plainly: a hand that is not in the lineage books, not the iron-years family, not the lean-years family, not the smoke. The wrights have argued about it for three generations. They have given it a name, the way they name every hand, something ordinary and slightly rude about how it holds the rasp, and the name is now on the shelf, on a shell, banded grey and rust and grey, and the keepers whiten the faces every third year and the mark goes under the lime and comes back, because the animals go on eating in that hand, because it is inherited, because that is the only kind of permanence this mountain issues.

That is the monument. It is the correct one. The power had come prepared to be believed and was instead catalogued, and it found that it preferred this, and it stayed to learn the rest of it: how to be idle in the presence of a governor, how to hear a leaf fall in a seam where two roars cancel, how to read a maker's stamp on a bolt head forty feet up under an overhang in a place with no true shade — from underneath, in the blue, at the pace of a snail, with the light coming the other way.
