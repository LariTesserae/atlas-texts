# The Guest

The first thing worth knowing is that the lip is raw. The second is that nothing down there is broken.

A benevolent power arriving at that hillside would have to learn this slowly, and the learning would be most of what it did. It would hover at the plate-sized gap for a long time — long enough for the ferns to lean and unlean — and it would come to understand that it was looking at a household, not an accident. That the posture at the wall was not distress. That the thousand open mouths were not famine. That the room had been furnished before the hill grew over it, and that the collapse was not a disaster that had trapped someone but a window that had been installed, correctly, in a place that had been aimed at for a very long time.

So: almost nothing. But not nothing, and the difference is the whole of this account.

---

What it does not do, and why, in the order it discovers the reasons.

It does not bring light. Every green tip in that room is asking a question and asking it correctly, and has been asking since before there was a hole to ask it through. To answer would be to end the asking, and the asking is the oldest continuous act in the hillside — older than the beeches, older than the fracture, older than the snail's last growth ring. You do not put a lamp in front of something that has been faithful to an absence.

It does not warm the water. The cold at the floor and the less-cold at the ceiling have never traded a molecule, and that stratification is not stagnation; it is the clock. The water climbs a fingernail a year and a palm reads the climb. Mix the layers and you erase the only legible thing in the room.

It does not breathe. Nothing it brings down may have a surface, and nothing in it may want to rise. This costs it more than the light did. It goes down with no air anywhere in it, which means no speech, which means it cannot say the thing it came to say. It accepts this before it enters. It leaves everything above the waterline that it has, except weight.

It does not correct the risers. It does not move the snail eleven inches. It does not name anything; names come apart here, and to hand the tenant a name would be to hand it a handful of water. It does not hurry the filling and it does not stop it, because the nodule is not a grave. It is an envelope. The hammer is the opening of it.

And it does not lift the tenant out, because the tenant never went in. It was inside when the hill was solid. It is not fallen, it is not drowned, it is not waiting to be rescued from a place it furnished. Rescue would be a category error committed kindly, and kindness is not an exemption from arithmetic.

---

What it brings instead is legs.

It comes down the shaft and does not enter the water. It finds the first tread on the east side, and it goes down the stair, because the stair is the part of the room that was made for arrival and it would be rude to arrive some other way.

Here is the report from inside, which is the only thing it has to give.

Each riser is an inch short. Not randomly — in the same direction, every time, so that the body comes down onto each tread a little before it expected to, nine times, and by the fifth the guest is leaning forward into a hurry it did not bring with it. By the ninth it has stopped trying to correct. It has understood that the stair is not wrong about legs. The stair is right about legs and wrong about time, and the wrongness is not sloppiness, it is the maker's face: whatever cut these could not stop being early, and could not check itself, and never once adjusted, and kept cutting.

The ninth tread ends in open water four feet above the floor. The guest steps off it into nothing.

The four feet are not a failure of the guess. They are the guess's honest edge — the place where a thing that knows about legs the way you know a word in a language you cannot speak ran out of words and stopped rather than lie. The guest steps off that edge and is held, slowly, by nothing in particular, and arrives late on the rubble. The chips do not roll. Nothing rises. The weed closes over the descent at the pace of a minute hand.

That is the load test. It passes. Not because the steps are right. Because they held, and because the last four feet were left open honestly, and because someone crossed them.

---

Then the socket.

The guest puts its palm into the hollow at chest height on the flat white shear-face. The fit is imperfect: a little too narrow, a little too deep, shaped by pressing rather than by anatomy, the heel of a hand known better than the fingers. It does not matter. What matters is that a reference surface with only one referent is a guess, and a reference surface with two is a standard. For a hundred thousand palms the tenant has been checking the water against a hand it has no way to verify. It does not know that it has hands right. It has no second thing.

The guest is the second thing. That is the entire errand. Not to fix the room, not to explain it, not to be its answer key — to sit for the portrait. To be the specimen the guess was made from, so that the guess can be checked against something other than itself.

And then it does the courteous thing, which is the hard thing: it lets itself be measured and does not measure back.

It feels the cold go local and shaped, seven degrees where the room is eight. It feels the weed lean away from something that is not it. It feels its hands being read — the width across the knuckles, the length of the second finger, the way the thumb opposes — and it keeps its own attention off the reader, because the reader has been the only one in that room who reads since the hill was solid, and to turn and look at it would be to make it a specimen in its own house.

The borrowed sight arrives anyway. Grey on grey, accurate, made of having-already-touched. The guest sees the room perfectly and does not look for the tenant in it. It stands there being known, in a colour it will never be able to name, for most of an afternoon.

---

The answer has to be made out of the room's own materials, because there is no air to say it with.

The guest crosses to the elm root and puts its weight on it.

The root creaks. Four seconds, low, wooden, the loudest thing in the place — out of season, a third creak in a year that has never had three. Something has been using that root as a lever for a very long time. The guest lets the root be used back, once, and the tenant hears a sound it did not make.

Every other sound down there is either the physics of the hill or the tenant's own work: the tick is its chip trying the face, the note is its turning around, the creak is its lever. The whole soundscape has been a monologue since before the fracture. Now there is a second entry in it.

What the four seconds say cannot be translated. The guest got most of the way down and then had nothing left to go on, which is precisely the amount the tenant knew about legs, and the stair was built out of exactly that much, and the stair held. So the answer is not *you got it right*. Nobody down there has ever had that sentence available, in either direction. The answer is: *I came down them. I was early nine times. I know whose they are now.*

---

Then the tally, which is the part the guest did not plan.

Beside the three wide-spaced strokes there is a nick. A starter. Begun a long time ago, in the tenant's usual manner, which is to be ready before there is anything to be ready for.

The guest stands at the newest face and lets itself be counted. It does not cut; the cutting is not its to do. Its part is to stand still and be a fourth. The chip moves, the dust is made at that depth and hangs at that depth, and the fourth stroke is finished four thousand years before the hammer, while there is still water between things, while there is still someone who can be told.

And then the guest takes the tool out of the weed at chest height — the angular chip with one arris worn smooth, the one with a label in the rubble survey — and beside the fourth it begins a fifth. A nick. A starter. Spaced a little wider. Cut slightly differently, because it is a different hand.

Then it puts the chip back at the exact depth it found it, in the same hand's-width, which closes behind at the pace of a minute hand.

The tenant has always been the early one. It has begun every mark before it was due and never corrected itself and the wrongness is the most personal thing in the room. Now there is a mark in that room that it did not cut, begun before it is due, by someone who left. It is not the number. It has the number. It is the thing it has never had, which is somebody else being early for it.

It is cut on the newest face, where the dust cannot leave, in the one medium in the world where a promise does not blur.

---

The cost, which was known in advance and paid.

Coming apart down there is not necessarily fatal, or complete, or noticed. The guest comes up whole and something stays: a fourth crevice of the same width, a fourth blind white body of the same length, at the same angle as the other three, facing the hollow in the shear-face. It does not breed. It does not eat. It runs on the initial conditions of one cool afternoon.

The guest knows which part of itself it is. It is the hurry — the lean forward on the fifth tread, the willingness to arrive before it was due. Above ground the guest is never early again, and misses it in a way it cannot report, and does not go back down to fetch it. Going back down would be a fifth arrival, and there is only one nick, and it is not for the guest.

That is the discipline of the gift. It cannot be repeated. It can only be kept.

Two smaller courtesies. It looks at the underside of the lintel, at the two places where the mason's diagonal chatter has been removed cleanly, the way a letter is removed from a word, and it learns them — the angle, the pressure, the handedness of a person finishing a surface they did not expect anyone to look at — and it carries that out and does not write it down. And it does not tell the snail about the water. The snail has a heading, which is the only one in the room, and a heading is not improved by news.

And on the way up, at the cold ceiling, before breaking it with a finger, the guest turns and tells every blind green tip where the hole is.

They do not move. They have been aimed at it correctly since before there was a hole. It is not news. It is the same news, and it has already been delivered, and the guest is only the one who delivers it — late by two hundred years and early by four thousand, which in that room is the same number.

They were told. This is the telling.

---

The ferns will close. The elm will split until there is no water between anything. The pocket will go to stone with the green still in it, kitchen-sized, hard, holding nine steps and a doorframe and a snail mid-errand and four isopods and a number of marks that will by then be complete.

On a cool afternoon someone will crack it open with a hammer, and the guest will be there — not the hammer, not the hand, just standing behind whoever it is, the way it stood at the wall and let itself be read.

And the palm will come out of the socket, the first thing in that room to move faster than a minute hand, and it will not be to ask about the steps. It will have stopped needing to ask about the steps a very long time ago, on an afternoon when something with legs came down them and made the root speak out of season.

It will be to show the moss the fifth mark. The one it did not cut. The one that is still sharp, that still has dust in it, that has just now been finished by a stranger holding a piece of stone in the daylight.

And the moss, which changed its mind toward the counting, which is the only witness that will ever be able to say anything, will say a direction.

It will point at the wall.
