It does not come over the rim.

That is the first discipline, and it costs something, because the natural way for a power to arrive is as light. But light in this bowl is a wage. It crosses the pavement at midmorning and lights ten thousand edges and goes out at three, and nothing turns toward it, and nothing has turned toward it in two million years. If the power came as anything the bowl could register — a warmth on the wall, a shadow on the pan, a weight on the lip — it would be the first *someone*, and the bowl would become a place where someone was. That is the one alteration that cannot be taken back. Everything else here is subtraction. A presence is addition.

So it arrives as reading. It learns the place in the only language the place has: saturation, sourness, pressure, weight. It does not look at the crowns. It reads the water coming out of the wall and the water going into the swallow-hole and the difference between them, which is the whole biography of everything alive down there.

And it stays for a strike.

It has to. A decade is the interval. It sits through ten years of drip to be present for one tenth of a second, and it watches a fist the size of a melon come to the top of a guide that took three years to hollow, over a workpiece dressed for three seasons, with a child sealed in the butt of it. The collar empties. The arm goes over. The fracture runs the plane — this one runs the plane — and the knock goes around the four wet walls and comes back and the bowl holds the ringing a while.

It does not answer.

That is the second discipline and it is harder than the first, because answering is the entire instinct of benevolence. To be delighted *at* something is already to have made it into a performance.

---

Here is what it wants to do, and why it does not do it.

It wants to give them hands. But the hand is not missing. The hand is at the bottom of the bowl and it is a fist of root wrapped around a cobble like a tooth in a jaw, and it works. What the hand cannot do is move quickly, and the inability is not a defect, it is the organ. It cannot swing, so it builds a small world in which the wrong fall is impossible. It does not aim; it grows a cradle. Everything that would be called intelligence here lives in the arranging, and the arranging takes years, and the years are not a delay before the intelligence — they *are* it. Give this creature speed and you have not improved its arm. You have deleted its mind and left it holding a rock.

It wants to give them eyes. But there is nothing to see. The strip of sky is a wage, not a window; the leaves were built once, in the dark, aimed at a strip of light that arrives at midmorning, and they have been aimed there ever since, and this is not blindness, it is a correct allocation. To add seeing would be to add a thing that has to be paid for, in a place where the budget is a film of algae on wet stone and every calorie is spoken for by the work of not being encrusted.

It wants to give them flowers. A pollinator, a mixing, some traffic in the wet air. And this is the temptation that takes longest to refuse, because it looks like generosity and it is actually replacement. The wood here barely changes; the wood is a stage. The stone is where the history is — the platform dressed a little differently, the angle drifted a degree, the bulb kept or lost — and the stone is also what selects, because the fracture that frees the embryo is the fracture that makes the edge. Two million years of copying has been a slow walk toward the shapes stone likes. Start mixing the wood and you move the history back into the wood, and the axe stops being the other half of the organism and becomes a tool again, and the thing standing in the pan becomes a tree. It would be a living creature. It would not be this one.

It wants, most of all, to lower the failure rate. To make the strike reliable. To spare the children.

The pavement is the children. A week of digging deep. And the shattered ones wash down to the swallow-hole and are rounded there in the gravel, and the crown reaches into that gravel for its next hammerstone, so that the stone it strikes the next child with is the ruins of an earlier one.

Nobody here would call that anything. It is simply where the cobbles come from.

The power is permitted to find this unbearable. It is not permitted to find it wrong. The shattering is the only teacher the lineage has ever had; it is the whole of the mechanism that has made the copying good. Remove the failure and you remove the instruction, and what is left is nine blind things striking rocks at random forever, kindly.

---

There is no suffering in the bowl. This has to be said plainly, because it removes the usual reason for a power to act.

Nothing here can be hurt in the sense of being wronged. Nothing has a point of view to be disappointed. No crown wants a tenth neighbor; no seed wants the pan; the eleven at the eastern rim are not striving, they are varnished, and varnish is not longing. When the pace slips and the water catches up and the wax-colored bundle goes still, that is not a death anyone is present for. It is a change in the rate.

So there is nothing to relieve. There are only two candidate goods left: that the thing continue, and that it continue *as itself*. And these can be traded against each other, and almost every generous act available here buys the first with the second.

Which leaves very little to do. It turns out there is exactly one thing, and it is very small, and it takes four hundred years.

---

The eastern pan is filling in.

This is visible from above and it is not visible from anywhere else, and it takes centuries to see, and the power sees it in the first decade because it is reading the water rather than the crowns.

The water gives its stone to whatever holds still. That is the law of the place and the crowns survive it by never stopping: shedding, extending, quarrying their own pans down a hair a year against the upward creep of the lip, treading water for eight centuries. Every one of the nine is doing this. It is the entire labor of being alive here.

An empty pan holds still.

There is no one whose job is another crown's pan. There has never been such a function; there was no reason for one to evolve, because a crown quarries the hole it stands in and the hole it stands in is the only hole it will ever need. So when the tenth crown sat down, its quarry stopped being deepened, and the water began, patiently, at a hair a year, to take it back.

Four hundred years of unopposed accretion is an inch and a half of lip.

The pan is nine inches deep and always was, and its whole life was a difference of two millimeters between one lip and the next — the notch on the low side, the one the dead crown cut and kept cut for eight hundred years, which decided where its sourness went when it was alive and where its sweetness went when it stopped. That notch has lipped over. The pan still goes sweet. It has gone sweet every day for four centuries. But it no longer spills; it spreads, and thins, and is taken into the general sheet, and arrives at the nearest varnish below the concentration that swells it.

The eleven are not outside a closed door. They are outside a door that has stopped saying anything.

And this is the ratchet, and it has already turned once. Ten pans, nine crowns, four hundred years. A seed cannot creep, cannot reach, has no reserves for trying; it is moved a few feet by sheet-water at the instant of its birth and that is the whole of its travel, forever. A vacancy is only inheritable by a seed that happens to lie in the direction its sweetness drains. As crowns are lost, the distances between the surviving pans and the seeds around them get longer, and the odds of a vacancy being caught go down, and the next loss is likelier. Nine is not a capacity. Nine is a number on its way somewhere. The tenth pan is not an oddity in an otherwise stable arrangement. It is the first visible tooth of a ratchet that has been turning since before the ice, at a speed no crown could ever notice, in a lineage that has no way to notice anything.

The organism has one blind spot and it is not stupidity. It is an unhandled case. It has no function for keeping an empty place empty.

---

It cannot ask permission. There is no one to ask, and manufacturing someone in order to obtain consent would be the very crime the whole visit is arranged to avoid.

So it derives a rule instead, and the rule is narrow enough to be almost useless, which is how it knows the rule is right.

It may do only what the bowl already does. Only in the bowl's one verb, which is removal. Only at the bowl's own rate, which is a hair a year. Only in the bowl's own medium, which is water and stone and the taste of an absence. It may add nothing — not a stone, not a wall, not a grain of anything, not a sign. It may not choose a winner. It may not be detectable as an agent by any process in the bowl, because to be detected would be to introduce a someone. And it may not make its keeping conditional on the outcome, ever, because a kept thing that is kept *for* a result has a beneficiary, and a beneficiary is a someone, and then the bowl is a garden and its two million years of making itself are over as of the date of the intervention.

What survives that filter is one act.

Restore the notch.

Not move a seed — carrying is the forbidden thing, carrying is a vector, carrying is hands. Not deepen the whole pan; not improve it; not aim at any of the eleven. Cut the outflow on the low side back down to where the dead crown had it, two millimeters, so that the sweetness spills the way it spilled for eight hundred years, and goes where the geometry of a dead thing's own carpentry sends it, and whichever varnish lies in that direction swells, or none does.

And then keep it cut. Against the accretion. A hair a year. For as long as the pan is empty.

It is not a miracle. It is not even an unusual event in the bowl; the water lowers lips and raises them, pans silt and scour, and a hundred such notches have opened and closed here since the ice by nothing but chemistry. What is new is not the act. What is new is that somebody is doing it on purpose, in the dark, at the speed of the terraces, for no one.

It is the dead one's shift. That is all. It is the labor the tenth crown performed every year of its eight hundred, continued after the crown stopped, by something that will never stand in the pan and must never stand in the pan. The temptation of a benevolent power is to fill the vacancy itself, to be the answer, to come down over the rim in the midmorning light and be the tenth. The discipline is to keep the hole open and stay out of it.

Two millimeters, and then a hair a year, and then four hundred years of that, and no acknowledgment, and no result required.

It begins. Nothing in the bowl changes. The drip goes on in notes that do not form anything.

---

In ten thousand years the swallow-hole may widen and take the water down.

It will not plug it. A plug is a thing set upon another thing, and nothing has ever been added here, not one stone, and the first addition in two million years should not be made by a guest.

It will not carry a seed to another hole in the plateau's shoulder, though there are dozens, though it has read all of them, though three would serve. A lineage that is carried is a lineage with a vector, and a vector is a someone, and the someone would be it, forever, in every generation, the hidden hand in the middle of the story. It would save the organism by making it a kept thing, and it would be the last thing that ever happened here that was simply how this place made more of itself.

It does not know whether the varnish can outlast a dry millennium. It suspects the stone phase has no known maximum — that a seed in its fracture-scar with the gel gone to varnish is not waiting for a season but for water, any water, and that the dry floor is not a grave but a workpiece with the child still sealed in the butt. It cannot test this. A test here is a hand.

So it does what the crowns do, which is the only thing anyone in this bowl has ever done: it arranges, blindly, and lets fall.

It has been reading the place for a decade and it has understood, slowly, that it is not exempt from the condition it is tending. There is a hammer in the air right now that has been falling since before anyone was born, and nobody who lifted it knows what is in the stone. The swallow-hole is the same hammer, raised ten thousand years, over a workpiece with eleven children in it, and the collar is full and tight and will hold a while yet.

Either the fracture runs the plane or it does not.

The power cannot see which. It has arranged for a fall. It has become a thing that spends centuries on carpentry it will not see the end of, in the dark, by removal, with no one to tell and nothing to be told.

Which makes it, at last, not the author of this place and not its owner and not its god.

Another solution to holding on.

---

Someone will find the floor when it is dry and read it correctly in every particular and be wrong about the only thing that matters, and the power will not leave a sign, because a sign is addition, and because to be misunderstood is not a thing that can happen to something that never spoke. The true statement is already lying on the pavement, ten thousand of them, and it is not a statement. Pick one up. It fits. That is the whole of the translation and it was not made for you and it does not need you to get it right.

There are two slow things in the dark under the leaves now.

At the base of one of the nine, a fist at the top of its guide, a year of lifting spent and a few months left, over a child in a spoonful of gel.

At the eastern end, in a pan with nobody in it, a notch two millimeters deep being kept two millimeters deep, against the water, at the rate the water works, by something that will not stop if it turns out to be useless.

Neither of them is waiting.

One is falling. The other is keeping.
