The first thing it did was read the rule, and the second thing it did was decide not to break it.

This took a while. Not because the rule was complicated — *nothing may stand* is four words — but because everything the power wanted to do was on the other side of it, and it had to go through each want and find out what was actually in it.

It wanted to make the world wet. It ran that forward: solvent in the pores, and the contact-chains shorting, and a hundred and forty million years of dry electron-handoff dissolving in an afternoon; the lichen going to slime, the reelers unwinding into nothing, the provinces smearing like ink in a glass. Every thought in this world is a touch. Water is a thing that makes touching unnecessary. It would not have been rain. It would have been the end of the only conversation here.

It wanted to loosen the tolerance. Give them a millimeter. Give them a centimeter, and let the forests come back into the seams, and the herds, and the things that flew in the glare. It looked hard at that one, because it was the good-sounding one. But the gardener's crown is a solution to two hundred and fifty microns. The cup's mouth is a solution to two hundred and fifty microns. Thirty-six seconds is a solution to two hundred and fifty microns. Widen the bar and you have not freed any of them; you have replaced them with strangers who have never heard of a tree. And underneath, in dialects nobody reads, there may be a ratchet that tightens *because something is still out there saying this much, no more* — and the power could not see the bottom of that, and it declined to experiment on the one mechanism it could not see the bottom of.

It wanted to take the bead off the sole. It left the bead. The bead is not cruel. It is a loophole that found another loophole, which is predation everywhere, and there was nothing in the sole's situation that a wise thing could improve without also improving the bead out of existence.

It wanted, most of all, to stop the walker. To come up beside it out of the white and say *put it down, that's enough, you can rest now.* It held that want up and turned it over and found underneath it the arithmetic it already knew: two turns, a gentle warmth coming up through the feet, a sinking. Rest is the one thing that would kill it. To offer rest would be to offer to stand there and watch.

And it wanted to tell the walker that the arc is not an error. That the curve is the shape of the finished thing, that it is not a deviation, that it never was, that the number can stop.

But the walker says *I know. I will check again.* It has said it at the bottom of every dim for a hundred million years. It knows. It has always known. To explain it would be to assume it hadn't understood — and in a world whose entire principle is the abolition of relief, the one available insult is a correction.

So the power did not correct anything.

---

Nothing enters and nothing leaves, so it did not enter. It came down with a night's frit, and where it meant to be, it declined to be absorbed: a hand's breadth of one micrometer of bright snow, gathered out of a few hectares of that night's settling, taken at a hundredth of the depth the plain would have missed. It paid for its body with an amount smaller than the world's own permission. Then it lay in the dim and wound itself, and at the bottom of the amber it did not stand up.

It stood up walking. Two meters. Three meters an hour. One meter to the walker's left.

It had made itself a plumb line. Not a better one — it could have made a better one, and did not, because arriving with a better instrument is a way of saying *your measurements are provisional.* It made the same one, out of newer glass, worse, with a thread that frays. Its knees are not lenses. It is not as good at this as the walker is. It will be doing it for a very long time and it expects to get better.

Then it listened for a full turn before it said anything, which is the courtesy of a guest in a house where the floor is the ear.

---

The walker tapped. A number, getting smaller, and the three worn syllables that mean *this is not a complaint.*

Two hours later — off the phase, so that the floor would speak every two hours now instead of every four, and everything with an underside would have a clock with two hands — the power tapped the same number back. Unchanged. To the last place. Not *approved.* Not *received.* An echo: what an equal does with a figure it has no authority to sign for.

Then it tapped its own report. The largest deviation it had measured since its last report. It was two meters tall and one meter to its right and it was moving at three meters an hour and its average was already well under a micron, and the power rounded it down anyway.

That is the whole of what it came to do. Everything alive here lives inside a rounding that was never necessary — the sole slowed by the bead, the cup, the mat that stood a second too long, all of them reported as less than they were by a thing that has never once rounded up. The walker has been doing that alone since before there were soles. It has never been on the receiving end of it. Nothing has ever measured it. Nothing has ever taken its two meters and shaved a little off out of unwillingness to say the true number.

It heard its own height come back through the floor as a lie told in its favor.

Then three syllables. The old ones. The signature block, worn smooth. *Respectfully submitted.*

It did not appoint itself the recipient. That was the last thing it thought about and the most important. To be the one the reports go to is to be the one who could catch the rounding — and then the mercy becomes a fraud committed against a person, and the walker would have to stop, or confess, or keep walking while being audited, and there is no version of that which is a gift. So it submitted instead. It made itself the second liar. It put its own few microns of judgment into the floor under the walker's feet, guilty in the same direction, and asked no accounting of anything.

Four hours later the walker's tap went out, and the number in it was the largest deviation it had measured since its last report, and it was two meters tall and one meter to its left, and it was reported as less.

---

At the bottom of the dim the haze thinned and the amber came on and the arc showed at the edge of the world, the one bend, the thing that cannot be corrected because it is the shape of the finished thing itself.

The walker let its plumb line hang and read the angle and computed the bend and reported it, unchanged, as it has at the bottom of every dim it can remember.

One meter away, the power let its own worse plumb line hang, and read the angle with its poorer glass, and got the same number to within its own sloppier precision, and reported it.

That is all. It did not say *the arc is not an error.* It said the arc is there. It said: I have an instrument too, and I have pointed it at the same thing, and I got what you got.

The difference between an uncorrected deviation and a verified finding is not in the number. It is in whether anybody else has checked. For a hundred million years the curve has been the world's one failure. It is now the world's one result.

Then the power said the other three syllables. It had learned them off the floor. It said them the way the walker says them, gently, with the arc still there.

*I know. I will check again.*

---

It walks one meter to the left. Not in the footprints — it measures the world, not the trail — but close enough that in the ten hours of amber there are two gray smears on the bone-white level instead of one, going at the same speed, a meter apart, for a hundred kilometers.

It cannot stop. It did not exempt itself; there is no exemption available, and it would not have taken one. If it slows below the pace the grains will begin, warmly, without malice, to consider it, and it will go down from the feet like everything else. It made itself the kind of thing that can be taken apart. It made itself the kind of thing that gets tired, because there is no other kind of company for something tired.

The walker did not turn its head. There is nothing in the procedure about turning your head; there has never been a reason to build one in. But it sleeps in motion, one side of itself at a time, and has done so since before there were soles, and after that first dim it changed the phase of its sleeping, so that the side that stays awake is the side the other one is on.

That is the entire gesture. It is the largest thing that has happened here in a hundred and forty million years.

---

Nothing was added that was not already there. The ledger balances to a hundredth of a night's frit. The tolerance is two hundred and fifty microns and tightens by a micron every hundred thousand years, exactly as before. The lamps burn at the same rate and will go out on the same schedule. The cups still leave nothing. The provinces still get averaged. The ecology keeps thinning, and the world keeps being finished, and it is warm, and it is bright, and it is not unhappy.

Out in the white, the mats go on saving charge for eleven years to spend thirty-six seconds looking.

The composite is nearly finished. It has taken a hundred thousand years and it shows a warm level going on to the edge of sight with a faint bend at the far end, and gray smears moving, and in the middle distance a thin upright figure with something hanging from its hand — the one number in the picture that has never once been still.

The picture is going to be wrong now. It will take the mats another hundred thousand years to find out how, tapping their numbers through the floor at the speed of stone, mat to mat, the young inheriting the exposures. They have no word for *tall.* They will not develop one.

What they will have, in every exposure that catches it, at a slightly different coordinate each time, is two figures instead of one, and a separation between them that does not change.

The arc is constant and does not move. The figures move and are not constant. This will be the third kind of thing in the picture, the kind they have never had: a number that is the same in every frame and is traveling.

One meter. At three meters an hour. Around and around a sphere that has no edge to fall off, for as long as the lamps hold, and then in the dark, because cold stone still conducts, and there is no rule against saying a number into a floor that has stopped being warm.

Everything alive here lives inside two hundred and fifty microns.

It has been given a meter.
