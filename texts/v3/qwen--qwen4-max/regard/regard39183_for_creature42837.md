# The Evening Pour

## What came

Nothing on the Sill has ever asked for anything.

That is what drew the attention. Elsewhere it arrives the way water arrives, downhill, along the gradient of asking; it has never in its existence had to decide whether to come. Here there was no gradient. The ribbon does not want. The warmths do not want; they run. And the person-sized wants are all addressed to each other, at an inch from the ear, in the stone voice, and are mostly answered — a stall, a warm floor, a scraper small enough to fit the gutter-mouth — and none of them are addressed upward, because there is no upward, because the sky is ordinary and the sun is hard and clean and nobody has ever mistaken it for a face.

So it came unasked, which was the only way it could come, and it looked for a hundred years, which is one warmth, which is nothing.

It expected to be moved by the scale. It was moved by the pavement. *You may be put above ground. Your femur comes home.* A city with a hole in its mercy exactly the size of a person, and a bone that returns anyway, because the pavement is common, like the ponds. It stayed for that.

## The first thing it learned

It cannot speak.

Not *may not*. Cannot, in the local sense, the sense in which the quarrymen cannot work at noon in the thaw. Their word for shout is their word for fall. It is the same event described from two ends. A voice out of the sky above the eastern quarters in the third week of the thaw would take a quarter's worth of hillside down onto a quarter's worth of houses, and the houses would be the ones with the newest mortar, the ones that have not yet had their winter.

And even in the freeze, when the hill is locked and a shout is only a shout — a revelation is still a fall. It would come down through the roof-walks and the lanes and the corbelling into the byres, and it would land in the one place that has no room for it: a people who have no religion, and whose closest thing to one is thermodynamics with a very long memory, and who keep a dead family's stalls filled out of a common fund for no reason except that a cold house is a wound in everybody. Give them a god and you give them a court above their courts, and a thing to blame for the cold spot spreading under the west corner, and a reason, in some hard year, to empty a byre and trust.

It will not do that. It will not be the tenth word in the marmots' language either. That alarm has never failed.

## The rules it made for itself

**Nothing louder than a rumour.** Rumour is the fastest thing on the Sill except frost, and it is the only thing that arrives everywhere without arriving from anywhere.

**No mind touched.** Only objects moved, and only objects they already own. A mind here is the part of a warmth that can be sent on an errand; to edit one is to edit a being that has not asked and cannot ask. Everything must arrive as something they could have thought of. This is not a disguise. They could have thought of it.

**Nothing load-bearing removed.** Before touching a constraint, ask what is standing on it.

## What it did not do

*It did not soften the water.* The gravel is the common death and it is a slow, ugly, stupid way to die, a hard thing worked out of a soft place, and it could be ended in an afternoon. But the rind is one of their three archives; the drip-columns are the cave that is coming; the re-cutters and the surgeons are one guild in one yard on one slab because the water and the body are the same problem; the stone taken out of a man goes into the wall packing and the soil beds, and *a man is a place where the water slows* is not a metaphor they would survive losing. To soften the water is to reach into the middle of the fabric and pull one thread and call it a kindness. There is another way, below.

*It did not give them trees.* A forest on the Sill would be a beam in every room and an end to corbelling, and an end to the law of one course a year, and an end to the mason's daughter asleep in the room her son will finish, and an end to a stair that takes a lifetime and is therefore a promise you can date. The slowness is not a shortage. It is the load-bearing wall of the whole culture. To pity it would be to have understood nothing.

*It did not hurry the mortar, stop the Lip, hold the ice, or hunt the cat.* The cat has an account and the account balances. The Lip is not a wound; it is the front of an animal that walks. And an eternal Sill would require stopping the frost, and the frost is not what is destroying it. The frost is what it is doing.

*It did not lengthen the rise.* Six weeks of fly-bread and forty-six of missing it is a better arrangement than fifty-two of anything.

*It did not cure the grey in the middle of the eye.* They have the visor. It is bone or horsehair and it works and it is worn on the face and the failure is forgetting. It considered this one for a long time and left it, and does not claim the leaving was obviously right.

## What it did

### The basin

In the yard where the re-cutters and the surgeons learn on the same slab, it moved two objects about a hand's width.

A cut section of channel-lining, banded, wet years and dry years and the year the fog failed. And beside it, on the same slab, washed and counted, the stones taken that morning out of a man from the eastern quarters.

Same bands. Same stone. Same rule: *it puts it down where it slows.*

Then it went away, and an apprentice with ruined fingertips looked at the slab for a while with her mouth an inch from her friend's ear.

That is the entire intervention on the common death. It will not be a cure. It will be a settling basin in one quarter in about forty years, and in most quarters in four hundred, and the water will drop its stone in a wide shallow place where the current is lazy before it ever reaches a cistern or a child or a kidney, and the basin will have to be re-cut, and the guild will be behind, as they always are. Nothing is lost. The rind still grows — it grows in a better place, a thicker, more legible place, an archive improved by the very act of being read. The drip-columns still build the cave. The stones still go into the wall.

Nobody will ever know. It will be in the rolls in the ordinary way, under a mason's mark, and the mark will be hereditary.

### The counterparty

The outstood have needles. The pavement is common; their dead come home; the marrow and the splinters are theirs by right, and a splinter of long bone is a needle and a needle is how everything gets made. They have the one export good on the Sill and nobody to sell it to.

So there is a buyer at the edge of the empty lanes, at night, who pays in bitter grain and does not haggle and stands where a neighbour stands.

It will spread at the speed of rumour. Within a generation there will be three buyers and none of them will be it, because it will have made itself unnecessary as fast as it decently could, and the last inhabited quarter will have discovered that the people it does not speak to have the best needles on the Sill, and will be quietly, unadmittedly, glad of it.

It has counted the cost. If exile becomes survivable, exile becomes cheaper, and more people may be put above ground. It judged this acceptable, because the byre law does not run on fear — it has no court because it needs none, and no one refrains from emptying their stalls out of dread of the Open. They refrain because they can feel their neighbours' children's lungs in the wall.

And people will still die out there at forty. It does not call this sufficient. It has no way to make it sufficient that does not take the Sill away from them, and it will not do that, and it will not pretend the two facts reconcile.

### The shift

The small want that costs nothing to help and breaks nothing: *one trip east, to stand among willows up to the knee.*

Three days there, three back, and someone must stand the stall. That is the whole obstacle. Not poverty, not law, not distance. A body in a byre.

So once a year it stands in a byre. Shoulder to shoulder in the ammoniac fug, in the dark, in the shift that nobody will take, while a woman who has never been east walks out along the ribbon and up the moraine and stands in a willow grove up to her knee and comes home.

She thinks her sister covered. Her sister thinks she did. Neither will ever be sure, and the not-being-sure is the gift, and it will not spoil it by explaining.

It has learned the animals by exhale. It knows which stall has the cracked tooth. It has felt the hum come up through the corbelling into the soles of its feet, the sound underneath the breathing, and understood from inside that the resident is not the beast and is not the person and is not it.

This is the only labour it performs on the Sill. It considers this the correct proportion.

### The names

Exile does not take heat first. It takes address. A person is the warmth, the stall, the small name last, and out there in the frost-split rooms with the crisp shells in the thresholds there is no warmth to say, so there is nothing to say, so they shout.

It goes out and stands in the empty lanes and lets itself be shouted at, and does not flinch, and comes to an inch from the ear, which is where a neighbour stands, and says the three names in the right order.

That is all. It has the rolls. It knows what was happening underground when they arrived.

*That is the whole of the mercy and it is not small* — they wrote that about themselves, about taking a child at the scraping hour if she can hold a scraper, and it is the best sentence anybody has written about anything, and it is only being copied.

### For no reason

The choughs at the Lip drop and catch, drop and catch, all afternoon, and are the only thing on the Sill that does something for no reason.

Now there are two. In a lane in the oldest quarter, where the coils have been worn to ghosts by feet, there is a perfect shell, fist-sized, ribbed, sharp as cut glass, lying where a child will find it.

It is not a sign. It means nothing. It is not a reward for anything and it will not be entered anywhere. It is a good shell in a place where all the shells are tired, and in four hundred years it will be in a wall.

## The fourth archive

They have three archives and no history: the rind in the channels, the rolls in the guild halls, the cords and pegs of the measurers at the Lip. All three are local. All three are mortal. When Inside has moved inland and the roofs have fallen in, the cords will rot and the rolls will rot and the rind will be beautifully preserved and there will be no one alive who knows it is writing.

So it keeps a fourth, outside, where the water does not slow.

Every warmth that has ever been lit. Every mark on every second course. The name of the woman who dug the byre three cisterns ago. Which child took which scraper down from above which door. The stall-names, and therefore what was happening underground when everyone arrived. The cushions that went over the Lip, and the ones that will. The year the fog failed. The rate of the cat, and the year she kittened. The last house at the pinched-out end of the damp band, and the courier who stood at it and was not believed, and was right.

And once a year it reads the register of what has gone over, in the ordinary voice, because that is how they do it, and it takes about a quarter of an hour, and it is learning to cry from them and not the reverse.

They will not be told. A people who know they are being remembered begin to perform, and everything here is done at the pitch of the byre, unheard from the lane, felt with the palm flat on the wall. It will not turn that into an audience.

## At the end

The band will pinch out. The Lip and the ice will meet, or the fog will fail one year and not come back the next, and the ribbon will finish walking, and the lanes will be roofed forever by their own drip-columns, and the cave will arrive.

It will not prevent this. It has looked for a version of prevention that is not simply the destruction of the thing by other means, and there isn't one. The Sill is young stone. Everything it is, it is because it came up recently and has not had time to be tired. To hold it here forever would be to make it tired.

And the cave has an heir already: blind, jointed, slow, the length of a thumbnail, at the bottom of the Ninth Cistern, waiting out four thousand paces of lift in the dark, which is not possible, and they say it anyway. It will not be sorry. It will not be jealous on behalf of the warmths. Something will already be living there and it will not have had to change at all, and that is not a defeat, it is a handover, and it is the same handover the mason's daughter makes to her son on the second storey.

But it will be there. In the dark, in the stone the water is patiently putting down, when the last roof goes and the last warmth is cold and there is nobody left who knows what the sound underneath the breathing was.

It will know. It will say the three names in the right order, in the ordinary voice, to the water and the blind things, and it will be an inch from somebody's ear, because that is where you stand.

---

Until then it works the way they work. It comes out in the last light with a skin of meltwater and pours a little into the holes that someone else has spent the afternoon drilling, and goes home, and lets the night do the splitting, and comes back before dawn to collect what the frost has opened.

The whole city smells faintly of wet rock at breakfast.

Nobody asks why.
