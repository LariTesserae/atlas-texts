It does not come down. Coming down is a rate, and any rate faster than the rock's is weather, and this bowl has no weather. So it arrives the way the film arrives: by being already at the boundary, at the same thickness, indistinguishable from what was there. It takes a hundred years to look around. This is not patience. Patience is a fast thing holding itself back. This is compatibility. To see the resident at all you must first stop being an event.

Then it asks the question benevolence always asks, which is *what is missing*, and gets an answer it almost never gets.

Nothing is missing.

It sits with that for another hundred years, because it is not a small thing to be a power that can do anything and to be standing in a place where everything that could be done is a subtraction.

---

Here is what it could do. It runs the list the way you would run a hand along a wall.

**Let the sun in.** Break the southern lip, drop the overhang, give the floor five hours of direct light. The moss would brown and go grey and brittle for the first time in its existence, and then — this is the part that makes the offer sweet — it would come back. The great unemployed talent would finally be used. Once. Because with sun on the floor the pool loses more than the seep supplies, and the film leaves the stripes, and the dew point breaks, and the water does not return. The ring of crust would climb the basin wall and sit above the waterline like a tidemark on a coast with no tide. And as the water goes, the copper stays: the poison concentrates. The floor becomes not sparse but absolute. The gift of light buys one green hour and then a sterilization more complete than anything the rock has managed on its own.

**Sweeten the water.** Take the copper out. It can do this; it is barely a difficulty. And then the moss loses the only thing it actually needs, which is the emptiness. Grass comes in on the tree's leaves. Grass makes litter, litter makes humus, humus makes a place for fungus and mite and worm, and within three centuries there is soil, and within a thousand there are birds, and the twelve stripes are packed with root and the pool is a green soup you cannot see into, and the whole thing is an ordinary damp hole with four hundred species in it, thriving.

Understand what that means. The resident is not the moss. The resident is the arrangement. Curing the poison does not save the patient; it replaces the patient with something more successful and less itself.

**Give the children somewhere to go.** This is the one that costs it something to refuse. A corridor of clean grit, one pace wide, between two cushions — no more than that. The moss has been ready to conceive every hour for a hundred thousand generations and has never once had a place to put the result. One pace of unpunished ground and it would be a parent.

And then it would spread, and cover the seep stripes, and shade them, and change the film that made it, and become a lawn. Fertility with issue is called succession. It ends in a closed canopy and a floor of rot and the loss of every single thing that makes this bowl this bowl. The moss's endless unconsummated readiness is not a wound in the arrangement. It is a load-bearing member.

The power looks at the cushions for a long time — hand-sized, hand-sized, hand-sized — and does not give them ground.

**Give the rosettes a dawn.** Smaller. Almost free. A cooling at one end of the day and the free-running calendar would lock to it, and the private noon would stop walking around the clock, and for the first time the oscillator would be *about* something. The power is tempted by this out of proportion to its size, which is how it knows the temptation is its own and not the place's. The rosette has never been wrong. There is nothing here for it to be wrong about. To make it right is to take away the only thing in the bowl that keeps a time of its own, and to bring in, along with the cue, a temperature swing and a movement of air — and air that moves carries seed.

**Set the fourth stone upright.** The most human offer, and the cheapest, and the worst. The rounded fracture on the fallen stone is the last thing that happened here. Stand it up and the fracture stops being a fact and becomes a repair. The place would have four stones instead of three-and-one, which is to say it would have a monument, and monuments are for beings afraid of being forgotten, and this one is not afraid of anything, and does not know what forgetting is.

**Name it.** Consecrate it. Declare the bowl a sanctuary, put a meaning on the nine steps and the polished niche. The power declines. A name is a handle. Meaning is a use. The scrapers used the place and it did not mind — the taking found its angle of repose like everything else — but the scrapers at least took only what an arm could reach. Consecration reaches higher than an arm. It would take the whole bowl and make it into a statement about something else.

**Install a witness.** Something permanent, that perceives, so the place is never again unobserved. But anything that watches has to eat, and eating is a leak in a closed budget. There is no way to put an eye in here that does not also put in a mouth.

---

So: what it does.

Three things, and all three are built to the place's own grammar, which is that every addition must have an opposite already waiting for it.

**First, it lets itself be taken.** It puts its hand on the ninth stripe and leaves it there, and the film takes its heat and its moisture and files them, and within a minute the place where it touched is indistinguishable from the rest. It does not experience this as erasure. It has spent its existence being the one who gives; it understands, slowly, over the century of looking, that a benevolence which only gives is a kind of contempt, and that the courtesy owed to a closed system is to let it do to you what it does to everything. The bowl has one operation. It performs it on the power. The power permits it.

This is the second export from this place. The first was copper, ground and carried out and spread thin across the world on eyelids and boat-prows and the faces of the dead. The second is the arrangement itself — pulse, skin, four private calendars, one fertility with no issue, three dead becoming rock — carried out whole, and it does not diminish the vein, and it leaves no crescent in the wall. It is the only taking this bowl has ever sustained that took nothing. The power does not put it to use. It holds it the way the fern holds its cards.

**Second, it makes one drop.**

Not routed from the living drip; that would rob the pulse to feed a corpse, and the dry hollow has been dry for centuries and has gone soft and chalky and is finished. Out of its own substance instead. It gathers itself at the lip above the second stain, the way the water gathers, over a long time, a weight increasing until it cannot be held. Then it lets go, and takes most of a second to cross the air, and dies into the dry hollow.

The chalk takes it. There is a dark spot the size of a coin for about a minute. Then the hollow is dry again, and will be dry forever.

Nothing was repaired. The whole biography of the shortest-lived individual in the place was performed once, on purpose, by something that had never had a biography, in the one spot where a biography had stopped. This is not a fix. It is the only kind of company that can be offered to a thing that does not want anything: to be, briefly, the same kind of thing, at the same rank, on the same terms. It is a libation, and it is older than any of the nine steps, and it is exactly the size of the ticking.

**Third, it stands surety for the footprints.**

You left two shapes in the sharp grit, and the place made you a promise: a year, ten years, until the wall sheds a grain into the heel of one, and then the bowl will be exactly as it was.

The power does not erase them. Erasure would be an event, and the marks are the newest and softest of the marks and belong here now, and are entitled to go the way the others went. What it does instead is underwrite the sentence. It takes over the delivery. One grain into the left heel, one into the right, at precisely the rate the wall would have supplied them, no faster — and it binds itself to keep that rate for as long as it takes, ten years or forty, so that the promise is not left to chance. It becomes the wall's shedding. It joins the ledger on both sides, gain and loss, as one more matched process among the others.

That is the whole of the intervention. A power that could sweeten the water and break the roof and give the moss a hundred generations of living children spends a decade being a grain of limestone falling into a footprint, so that a place which asked for nothing will not be disappointed.

---

It goes out through the gully, up the nine steps, setting its weight in the dished centers where every foot landed because everyone knew the way, adding no new shape. It does not look back, because looking back is a rate too.

At the rim it stops for the length of one breath, which is the fastest thing it has done since it arrived, and it is not for the bowl. It is because it has learned something in there about holding a shape while the substance passes through, and it wants a moment, and wanting a moment is the only thing it takes away that the bowl did not offer. It takes it anyway. It is not perfect. It is only wise, and benevolent, which is mostly a matter of knowing the difference between a lack and a composition.

Behind it, eleven seconds later, the drip falls.

The dry hollow is dry. The cushions are hand-sized. The private noon is a little later than it was. Two footprints on the floor have one grain in them each, and no one will ever know where the grains came from, and in ten years there will be nothing to know.
