## The visitor

It comes up the headwall in May on the incline, standing, because there is nowhere to sit. It pays the fare. It has a shape that will not show from the sea, which is deliberate: the towers already show from the sea, and one more vertical thing on this shelf would be an error.

At the top the doors open and the noise arrives like weather, and it does what everyone does, which is stop for a moment and let the sternum take it. Then it walks out onto the canopy, and the mat, thirty meters ahead, begins in its slow way to be informed.

It spends four days doing nothing at all. This is the only fieldwork the place requires.

It can hear the twelve to fifteen thousand events a second separately, without putting its arm in. It can hear the tapping under the roar in June as easily as under the snow in February. It knows what the unidentified rhythms are. It could give them names in a language older than the ice. It does not, and the reason comes later.

## What it finds

Almost nothing here is in pain.

The beetle is not a tragedy. Flightlessness on this shelf is not an injury; it is a conclusion, arrived at independently by half the animals under the floor, and it is correct: there is nowhere to fly to that is not the same place. The mouse is not a prisoner. It is a mammal in a warm room with crumbs, which is most of what mammals want. The moth has working wings and nowhere to use them and is not thereby in torment; it is a population at forty degrees in a chapel-sized box, and it is doing fine. The lark is going under on its own initiative and would not thank anyone for a ladder. The mat is not asleep. It is not anything. It is informed of you the way a lake is informed of a stone, and it is holding four hundred square kilometers of water twenty centimeters off bare rock, and has been doing it since before the valley had a church, and this is difficult, and no one has ever said so.

So there is no suffering to relieve, and the visitor is honest about that rather than disappointed. But there is harm, and it is a particular kind, and it takes the four days to see it because it is distributed so evenly, like the water.

A permanent hiss laid into the cochleae of men who are now eighty. Eleven years of observation about the lark written on a medium that lasts four days. Nine populations of blind animals whose entire gene flow is a knife blade, and the knife has no reason to be rinsed. A conversation nine hundred years old with no listener outside it. Four old men in a carpeted room holding a pitch the world no longer makes, unverifiable except against each other, arguing. A maintenance department that exists because a company in the valley wants load that never varies, and would stop existing the quarter that the company stopped wanting it. A man who planted a spruce in 1911 and is now a line in a ledger nobody opens.

Nothing on the shelf is in pain. Nearly everything on the shelf is unwitnessed. It decides on the fourth day which of these it is for.

## The refusals

These are the large part of the work, and they take no time at all, and nobody will ever know they happened.

It does not wake the mat. It could. It could put a center in it, a season of decision, a self. Consider what that would be: a thing that felt a hook knife at the speed of a knife and healed at the speed of a century. It would experience the 1954 cable trench as an open wound for four thousand years. It would know about the slip below Intake Seven in real time and be able to do nothing about it for four hundred generations of everything else. The slowness is not a defect in this animal. The slowness is the mercy it does itself, and it is the load path, and it is why there is a floor here at all. To give it awareness would be to build a hell out of a kindness and hand it to the only resident.

It does not speed it up. Three millimeters a year is not a fault.

It does not give it a river. The river is coming; the river is nine meters long already and inventing itself headward at eleven meters a century; the amphipods in their nine concrete boxes are the river's fauna, waiting. But the river arrives by killing the mat, and the mat is everyone alive here now. To open the drainage four thousand years early would be to pay the future out of the present, and the present has a beetle in it with a bubble of air against its abdomen and two years to live and no opinion about posterity.

It does not give the beetle its wings back. It does not give the lark a song. It does not give the moth somewhere to fly.

It does not hush the shelf. The roar is not a symptom; it is the sound of water being forbidden to gather, which is the same act as the floor existing. You could silence it by stopping the water or by letting it concentrate, and both are the death of the thing it came to see.

It does not heal the gullies. And it does not improve the crew's policy, because the crew's policy is already right and nobody ever wrote it down: re-lay everything a hand has cut, leave what water has begun. It has been arrived at over a hundred years by eleven people at a time who never stayed, and it is better than anything the visitor would have brought up the headwall.

It adds no soil, no fence, no path, no sign, no hut, no board with an arrow on it. Nothing that stands up. It will not put furniture in a room whose entire virtue is that it has almost none.

And it does not sign. It will not let the loop become intention. The loop closed by itself — plant regulates water, company sells constancy, company sends hands, hands enforce the plant's one rule — and that accident is the best thing on the plateau, better than the geology, and the visitor is not going to put a hand on it and turn it into a gift.

## The small works

What is left is at the scale of a knife, a chalk mark, a habit, and a jug. It does these over one season, in the ordinary way, mostly by being slightly in someone's way at the right moment.

**The ear cups.** It does not take the note out of anyone. The men are not asking to be rid of it; they meet in a carpeted room to argue about a semitone, which is love, not complaint, and a repair performed on people who did not request it is not a kindness but an edit. Instead an idea arrives in the head of a woman in the valley who moulds the cups, about a resonance in the shell, and she tries it, and it works. It does not change the pitch. It changes the dose. The next cohort will still carry the shelf down the incline in their heads, and will carry it quieter, and will still be archives, and will still be able to hear their wives.

**The knife.** A superstition starts at Six-to-Eight and spreads the way things spread up here, which is by demonstration and not by explanation: rinse the blade at the next mouth. Not the same mouth. The next one. Nobody knows why. It is done the way you touch a doorframe. Within four summers it is done by everyone, and within forty years it will be told to new hands as *just what we do*, and what it is, is nine isolated populations of relict outwash amphipods becoming one river's worth of animals without a single channel being opened. The crew was already the river system. Now the river system has a current.

**The chalk.** A young hand with a pencil starts copying the slates before they wipe. Not the maintenance — the maintenance is chiselled into concrete by men who wanted it to last. The other stuff. The backs. Eleven years of *he drums, fast triple, pause, fast triple, and something answers thirty meters off*, which has been written and erased eleven times in a medium with a four-day half-life. Now it is in a book. It will be wrong in some particulars and it will be believed in none for another twenty years, and it will exist, which is the entire difference.

**The ledger.** Somebody opens it. The man who planted the spruce in 1911 has a name and it gets said out loud in the bothy over a thermos, once, badly, by a person who then changes the subject. That is all. It is enough. It does nothing else for the tree, which is already kept alive by sentiment, a stronger material than anything the visitor could supply.

**The photograph.** In the school in the valley there is a photograph of the shelf taken at noon with no shadow anywhere, looking like a folded bedsheet. It has hung there for decades and been looked at by nobody. The visitor moves it eleven meters, to where the children queue. That is the whole intervention. A nine-year-old looks at it for four seconds and something happens that will not finish happening for twelve years, and then there is a person on the incline in May who is not going up for the money.

## Stack 41

The one thing it does that could be called a work, it does with a stack of turves.

The felt preserves everything. That is the load path — nine hundred years of previous surfaces, cold and wet, the fungi working at the pace of geology. Ash layers, a copper band, the gray line of concrete dust from the year the sumps were cut. And at every intake, folds: doubled bands, inverted stratigraphy, the sequence running backward, because a man lifted a turf and stacked it and re-laid it. The mat's body is a record of everyone who has ever cut it.

The pitch of the shelf is a function of the apertures. The apertures are in the felt. The felt does not decay.

At the head-house there is a stack that has been added to for ninety years and taken from only from the top. Underneath, wet, cold, dark, pressed flat and perfectly preserved, are slabs cut in the summers before the flush was re-timed, when the shelf was a semitone higher and the Weeps were a different job. Their apertures have not changed. Nothing has changed them. They are dead and they are intact and they are the exact geometry of a sound the world no longer makes.

The visitor does not manufacture this. It was always true, and always available, and would have stayed available for another nine hundred years. It moves the stack two meters, into the way of a person carrying something heavy, who puts his load down on it and notices that the bottom slabs have paint on the edge.

In October there is a bread tray on a table in a carpeted room in the valley, and a slab of dead mat in it, and a jug of water, and four men who worked the Weeps before the re-timing.

Pour it through. It hisses. It hisses at the pitch of the year it was cut, because it is that year's apertures, and water is water.

Three of them were right. One of them has been wrong for sixty years and has been arguing about it in this room since before his wife died, and he hears it, and he sits back, and he is glad. Being corrected is a form of being believed. It is the only form available to a man carrying an unverifiable thing, and he has waited a long time for it.

Afterward there is a new rule, and like all the good ones up here nobody decides it: one slab from every re-cut, dated in paint, kept wet in the dark at the head-house. Not re-laid. Kept. Within a century there is a shelf of them, and the shelf is a library of the plateau's voice, and the library is made of the plateau.

The plant already stands on its own dead. Now it remembers with them too.

The whole apparatus of the miracle is a jug.

## The doorway

In February the snow seals the apertures and the roar goes out of the air and the valley's name for the shelf becomes true, and the two who are up here hear the tapping: all of it, the entire understory talking at once across four hundred square kilometers of quiet floor, beetles and stems and the lark's triple under the snow and the things nobody has identified, kilometers of it.

Eleven listens from the doorway with the light behind him. He has done this thirty-eight times and has never once gone out onto the mat to listen, and he is right not to, because going out would stop it. The floor announces you. The neighborhood is gone before your boot lands.

The visitor stands in the doorway too, with the light behind it, and does not go out.

It knows what the unidentified things are. It could say. It doesn't. He has eleven years of chalk and now a pencil and a young hand who copies it, and the finding is the part he would not give away, and there is no version of this where being told is better than hearing it.

So there are two people in the doorway instead of one, and the conversation goes on underneath them, and neither of them says anything, because there is nothing to say that the hiss wouldn't take. That is the entire gift. It is the correct one. It costs nothing and it cannot be improved and it will be remembered by a man who does not have the vocabulary to describe it and will describe it anyway, in the valley, to his sister, who will have to think for a second when someone asks her brother's name.

## Money

It does not endow the scheme. Endowments fail. Companies fail; the load will vary eventually; there will be a quarter, or a war, or a cheaper catchment, and the eleven will not come up in May, and the mouths will grow their green lids at two to four millimeters a year, and the mat's one rule will go unenforced because the mat cannot enforce it and the animal that enforces it was being paid.

The visitor cannot make a market immortal and would not if it could. What it does instead is redundancy, and the redundancy is not capital. It is a photograph moved eleven meters. It is a superstition about a knife. It is a book with the lark in it. It is a bread tray on a table and four old men who have been *checked*, and who will tell the story badly and often for the rest of their lives, in a town where children grow up believing the shelf is a quiet place.

It is not trying to make anything last. In four thousand years the pylons will be orange stains in a peat bank and the intake lips will be concrete rings in a streambed and someone will find one and write a paper about it, and the visitor has no objection to any of this. It is trying to make sure there is a next hand.

## The fold

If you core the mat where it stood, you find nothing. No anomaly, no band, no signature.

If you core it at the slip below Intake Seven, where it knelt in June with a hook knife and re-laid a turf that a hand had cut in 1954 and never closed, you find a fold. Doubled bands. The sequence running backward for eleven centimeters. Exactly like the folds under every intake on the plateau: the mark of somebody with a knife, doing the plant's one rule for it because it grows three millimeters a year and cannot.

It is indistinguishable from Eleven's. That is deliberate, and it is the only monument, and it is thirty millimeters down, and it will still be legible in nine hundred years.

You cannot praise this being. There is no one there to receive it. It has no center and no season of decision and its response time to a footstep is minutes. But it is holding four hundred square kilometers of water off bare rock, and it has been doing it since before the valley had a church, and it is difficult, and the only way to say so is with a knife.

## Courtesy

On its last morning it walks east, and the mat is sage, and the sun comes up off the wax under its chin, and the sky is the violet-blue you cannot get lower down.

It could cross the whole shelf without a pulse. It could arrive anywhere without being announced; that is the one thing here it could do that nobody else can.

Instead it stops, and puts its heel down, twice, hard, into a cushion.

Two thumps. *Here.* It is the only word the floor takes. The only thing you can say to a lake is a stone.

The pressure goes out through the continuous water in the felt at the speed of water in felt. Thirty meters off, over the next several minutes, the cushion pores begin to close. The pasture withdraws from the meniscus. The beetle stops drumming. The neighborhood, which has all the time in the world, takes its time.

And the visitor stands still on the canopy in the hard bright cold and waits it out — four minutes, five, however long the ripple needs — so as not to arrive ahead of its own announcement. Everything that has ever come up this headwall has walked faster than the floor could answer. The ice, the sheep, the surveyors, the cable crew, the company, the eleven. It is the first thing to come up here at the speed of the thing that lives here.

Then it walks, and the pores close ahead of it all morning, politely, clearing a path.

It is visiting. It intends to keep visiting. It will not appear in the count.
