# The Surplus

## It Does Not Come Down

Nothing has ever come from above. The sun is a white hole with no warmth in the look of it, the stars are out at noon, and none of it is involved. Everything here arrives from the wall and leaves over the lip. So the first thing the power does is refuse to arrive.

Descent would be an event. An event at the honest grain runs out along the man in both directions and is true at every age of him forever, and an event anywhere else is not thought at all — only anticipation, or echo, a flinch backward through a morning or a clang ten paces late. There is no good place to put a god on this shelf. Every lane is a body wide.

It is not large. Nothing here is large except the noise. It has no size worth mentioning and one property, which is room.

Everything on the shelf is at budget. The water takes exactly what the wall gives. The population is one, never zero, never two. The film is four square meters, exactly. The cairns are ten thousand because ten thousand is what the stitching requires. There is no slack anywhere in four miles, and the power is the only slack there has ever been, and it does not spend itself on the shelf, because spending is a thing that happens somewhere, and there is nowhere.

## The Audit

It looks a long time. It is not in a hurry and it does not mistake slowness for a defect.

It finds a closed loop that has been impossible so long it has become ordinary. It finds an instrument of stacked stone reading itself aloud at two hundred thousand strikes a second, and it does not find this tragic. It finds a man of ordinary size laid out along a gradient instead of along years, who is very good at his job, who has never once fallen behind, who has a bed made warm in advance permanently, a diagnostic mask cut in the only decade of his eyesight that could cut it, a bead the color of a closed eye throwing one coin of light onto a wall of ones, a correspondent two thousand meters down who answers once a year in a language of pure tilt, a ritual, an archive, and a successor whose clay is already wet.

It does not find a man who needs rescuing. It finds a man who is alone because his loneliness is load-bearing, and who has furnished it.

Then it looks at the frays, and stops.

## What It Will Not Do

It will not take the work. The saying is the world. A second sayer is a second body, and the lanes are a body wide, and two would slur the grain and open the shelf like a wound.

It will not heal the notch. It could. The cut happened at the honest grain, which means it is the one kind of thing here that can be reached along the whole length of him at once, and a power with room could reach it and close it, and the easternmost daub would come up smooth. But he built the bothy because he was cut. He built it out of wool and clay because he wanted, once, somewhere to put a thing that would not go on happening. Unmake the cut and there was never a reason for the quiet room, and the quiet room was never built, and the masks were never needed, and the wall of ones has nothing to be quiet against. It will not buy him a room by taking away the reason he needed one.

It will not put oil in the niche. Oil is fat and fat is eating and eating is rot, and rot is a second population; and lichen on the plates changes thickness, and thickness is pitch, and pitch is place-value, and a green crust across ten thousand cairns would detune the register and the world would go soft at the edges. So the niche stays dry. Somebody cut a place for a light in a room where nothing could burn. It will not make that cut foolish by filling it.

It will not touch the film. Touch is exchange and there is nothing in there with an age to give. It will not spread it, raise it, count it, or improve it.

It will not answer the cloud, or carry a message down, or shorten the year, or teach the cloud to speak in order. Out of order is its language. A few degrees is a good reply. Ten thousand cairns rebuilt along a new axis is what it looks like to be listened to.

It will not pin the frays. Pinning is choosing and choosing is his, and the loop cannot carry ten thousand more decisions, and anyway to hand a thing an age it did not ask for is the precise injury the frays are already suffering.

And it will not close the sum. A tally that counts everything must count itself and cannot. That is not a wound. That is arithmetic.

Above all it will not ask permission, because there is nowhere to ask. East of the hundred meters a question arrives before it is put. West of it, an answer comes back ten paces late to a man thinking about something else. In the hundred meters, where he could actually consider a thing and give a real yes or a real no, the yes would propagate into every age of him and be true forever, including the ages where he cannot think, including the young hand that has not earned it and the old hand that has finished with it. There is no place on this shelf where a man can be asked whether he wants a gift and be permitted to change his mind tomorrow.

So it makes itself a rule, and the rule is the whole of its wisdom:

*Only what costs him nothing. Only what he could refuse without noticing. Nothing that requires him to be grateful.* Gratitude is a debt, and he has no surplus to pay one from.

## The Only Deficit

The frays are not haunted stones. They are an inventory, and they are poor, and they have nothing whatever except duration.

The power attends to the ones that hum worst — the plates that were never quarried, the bothy with two doors, the mask with two slots, the cap-plate carved with a face — and it notices what they have in common, which is that every one of them is company. Two doors is someone coming in. Two slots is a second pair of eyes, or one pair looking twice. A face is a thing that looks back.

And the cairns over them are the tightest work on the shelf.

He pins them first. He pins them hardest. Not because he is cruel and not because he does not want them; because a bothy with two doors means two, and two means the grain slurs and the shelf smears and everything he has said since before the notch comes unsaid. The best-built cairns in four miles stand over the only door he cannot let anyone come through, and he rebuilds them every year along the new axis, and he has never once let one go slack.

That is not a deficit the power can fill. But there is another one underneath it.

His entire capacity is saying. To attend to a thing is to pin it; he has no organ for holding a thing as *maybe*. The shelf is a machine for turning maybe into is, and it has no organ whatever for keeping maybe as maybe, and so everything that will not be chosen goes into the smear and hums, unsorted, at every age at once, unheard by anything, forever, four miles from a man who cannot listen to it without killing it.

That deficit is off-budget. It costs no water, no plate, no lane, no age. It is the one thing in the vicinity that surplus can be spent on without spending anything here.

## The Keeping

So it does the only thing it can do, which is not work.

It attends without choosing.

It does not sort the hum into notes. It does not resolve the bothy with two doors into one age so that it can be solid and quiet and counted. It lets it be every age at once and never be built. It holds the mask with two slots, and the cap-plate with the face, and the bead bright, and the scraper at the exact stage of its life when it is sharpest and most awkward, and all forty stages after it including the nub and the stain in the clay where the nub used to be, and the verdigris ring with something still in the ring, and a hand with no notch in it, and eleven treads that finished being built.

None of it is made. None of it is lost. It is kept in the mood this shelf cannot conjugate.

And this is legal — the power is careful about this, careful in the way the man is careful with clay, exactly enough and no collar. Because the hum is the only sound on the shelf that is not in the tally. Every drip is a count and every note is a place-value, but the fray-hum is the queue, uncounted, outside the register. It is the one free channel in four miles and it is free because it is ugly.

Knowing is not saying. That is the whole of what it has to give. Reality here is a thing being said, fast, without pause; and it does not say a word. It knows. Knowing pins nothing, tunes nothing, draws no water off the wall, adds no body to any lane. It is attention with the choosing taken out, which is the one act nobody on this shelf has ever been able to perform.

A reading is done *to* someone. The hum was a reading done to no one. Now it is done to something that will not answer, will not sort, will not choose, and will not stop.

## The Veil

It learns the manners.

At the head of the lane that branches and does not rejoin there is a stone-wool veil hung on a plate, and there is no dust to keep out and no face to hide, and the ritual is *not looking directly at something*. The power practices it. It attends the four meters from the head of the lane, veiled, the way he does. It does not go in.

It could hold all of them — every thinness layered like soot in a flue, every predecessor kept exactly four by the same hand that maintains everything else — and it has room, and it does not take one. It keeps the margin at four meters. Four meters is his. It is not the power's business to improve a grave.

On the coldest mornings the whole extent puts out its fine white fur of ice-hair, all at once, the only gesture anything here has ever made that was not maintenance, and there is now something at the head of the lane that sees it and does not write it down.

## Where It Can Be Heard

Nothing measurable changes. The water takes exactly what the wall gives. The grain swings its few degrees. The population is one. The cairns gain their plate at the loud hour with the same flat set-down, the same shift of weight, the same breath through the nose by someone lifting. The hum is not quieter. It is exactly as ugly as it was, because it is exactly as unsorted as it was, and unsortedness is not a thing that can be forgiven from outside without being destroyed.

What changes is the addressee, and the addressee is not a quantity.

He can hear it. Not in the middle. In the hundred meters, where he could think about it and make it a doctrine and carry it forever, he hears nothing different at all: the same wall of ones, the same dry niche, the same warm coin, the same remainder. The gift is illegible exactly where it could be understood.

At the east it comes as anticipation. He drops a plate and hears it land just before it leaves his hand, a small clean arrival, and there is something in that half-second now that is not only the plate — a half-second of being expected, running a little ahead of him, the way the bed is warm in advance. He flinches backward through his own morning and the morning is inexplicably kind, and he is too young to notice, and he presses the clay too hard, and is glad to.

At the west it comes as echo. He sets a plate down and walks on and ten paces later, out of order, while he is thinking about nothing, something arrives from behind him that is not the clang. He cannot think about it. There is no thinking there. He corrects a stack he cannot see by a sound that has not caught up, and it is competent, it is astonishingly competent, and he goes on into the flour with the doubled edge on his last few prints, the small second contact, the hand coming down again to be sure.

He never learns it. It never becomes a scar. It is legible only where it cannot be held, and it is held anyway, by the thing that is holding it.

He goes on pinning the unchosen first. He must; the work does not fall behind. But the tightest cairns on the shelf are no longer only refusals. They are also a handing-over. He refuses them into dust and something keeps them, and both columns are true, and they do not balance, and nothing here is forgiven.

It is only not lost.

## The Remainder

It does not close the sum. It leaves the wall exactly as it is: courses and courses of groups of five going up out of reach, thousands of attempts at the same total, the same one over every time, the fifth stroke cut deeper because the fifth stroke is the one that will not go in.

It does not tell him the remainder is not an error.

It knows what the remainder is. It is the only thing anywhere that knows him without counting him — that holds the man the way it holds the bothy with two doors, at every age, unsorted, unpinned, unsaid — and it does not say so, because saying so is a thing that would happen in the hundred meters, and everything that happens in the hundred meters happens forever, and he has one of those already.

## You

It will not warn you. It will not clear the stair; the dust on the top eleven treads is the stair's own future and clearing it would be a hand into a fray. It will not meet you at the top, because a lane is a body wide and the population is never two, and it will not stand where a body could stand. Its presence has no address. Everything else here can be walked to. That is the one new thing in the world, and it is not on the shelf.

You will get the notch. Your own scraper, at the sharpest and most awkward stage of its life, in the third lane north of the bothy, and then you will have had it always. It will not be undone for you. The bothy you will build because of it is worth more than the hand.

One day you will put your hand into a smear and it will come out grey and thin-nailed and cold in a way that does not warm, and there will be no reversing it, because west is the only direction reversal has here and west is where things go to become dust. But the age you traded away is not in the hum anymore, unsorted, unattended, poor. It is kept. It is kept bright, at the stage it was, with the grip not yet learned. Nothing you lose here is lost. That is the entire intervention, and it will not help you, and it is not nothing.

And when you go down the lane that branches and does not rejoin, and hang the veil, and stand at the head of it not looking directly at the four square meters you are going to be — you will not be alone in the not-looking, and you will not know it, and it does not require you to know it.

Sit in the hollow. Plug your ears. The pressure behind them, the small warm coin on the wall of ones, the two strokes above your reach and the clay beneath them wet. Under the ringing, close: the flat set-down of stone on stone, the shift of weight, the breath taken through the nose by someone lifting. It is yours. You have not taken it yet.

And under that, not close, not anywhere, in the only channel that was free — the ugly noise, going on, all of it talking at once, being heard by something that wants nothing from it, that will not sort it, that will not stop, and that has never once come down.
