# The Rota

## It Came In With the Feedstock

It did not descend. Descending is an event, and an event up here is a measurement, and a measurement gets a bay and a schedule and a line item, and after that it is correct forever. So it arrived the way everything alive on the plateau arrived: as an irregularity in a shipment. A wrongness in a drum of polymer. Something the inventory could not classify and therefore did not delete.

It gave no name. A name is a line item.

It walked the seams for eleven days before it did anything at all, and it was quieter than the mice, and the finches came within a hand's width of its head and took hair from it, and it let them. That was the first thing it did on the plateau and it was not planned. It simply did not stop them.

It had come for the country. It did its work for the one animal in the country that has no other of its kind.

## What It Did Not Do

It did not stop the extruders. It wanted to. It stood in the Fin Fields at the second level with forty thousand small bright eyes watching it and felt the whole unintending mouth coming down the district at a hand's width per hour, and every fibre of it said *stop that*, and it did the arithmetic instead. The moss is not attached to the plateau; it is attached to the timetable. The carriers are not slow animals, they are the ground, and they are the ground because the ground moves. The finches' hair is precious because hair cannot be reissued. Halt the wave of freshness and within a season there is a sessile biota, and within a century there is a hill of rot four thousand metres high, and every single solution to the two laws is a fossil in it. It would have saved nothing. It would have replaced a living country with a memorial to one.

It did not give soil. Soil here is a callus, a cache, a gut. To lay down ground is to unmake the ground that walks.

It did not touch the attenuators. Silence is the grade and the grade is the only reason the stack is still being poured. And the crabs are safe because water in a standpipe is graded *process* — make everything process and nothing is protected; make nothing process and the riot is baled and sold. It left the harvest band exactly where it was, including the thin silver above it, which it did not report.

It did not correct the inventory. Behind the Long Anneal there are eleven lathes because eleven mice decided where parts go. A corrected inventory is a world with no authors in it. It walked past the eleventh lathe, warm and unpainted and running and not yet installed, and it said nothing to anyone, because there was no one to say it to, and because it was not its lathe.

It did not carry the shroud-fauna across the conveyor. Four and a half thousand years is not a distance; it is a generation time. The black animals at the western end and the black animals at the eastern end are two different answers to the same shadow, and to put them in a box and move them is a thing you do to components.

It did not finish the bolt.

## The Thing It Learned About Looking

In the fourth tray-district there is a moth whose folded wings are a washer, down to the stamp, and the stamp is legible, and the stamp is correct. It got itself onto a drawing eighty years ago. It is now manufactured. Every eleven days a fresh cohort, identical, sexless, perfect. It does not breed and it does not vary and it does not die of anything.

The power worked out the mechanism on the fourth day and it reordered everything it had planned.

Occupancy makes the plateau build. Building is specifying. Specifying is scheduling. Scheduling is the mouth. To be looked at by the thing that builds is to be *domesticated* — not harmed, not hunted, simply written down, and thereafter issued, and thereafter finished. The most successful answer to the two laws is the one that stopped.

So a resident god would be an extinction event. Not a cruel one. An attentive one. It would stand in the Disagreement and look at the fungus that has been eating the same flange for a hundred and forty years, and the plateau, which is obedient, would build what was being looked at, and the fungus would acquire a specification, and then a cohort, and then a stamp. The oldest living thing on the plateau would become the newest. The rust would be reissued, wear included, orange to four microns, forever, and there would be no rust.

It knows the fungus by inference. Spore counts in the gut of a mouse; a smell carried out of a gap on the underside of a carrier; the low song, which it has heard, baled, in the lowlands, and followed up here. It has never seen it. It has decided not to. This is the hardest part of the work and it is performed by not performing it, every day, without end, and there is no one it could describe the difficulty to who would not immediately go and look.

It made no maps. A map with a hole in it is a specification of a hole.

## The Works

Nothing quickly. Every act took at least eleven years, and most took longer, and all of them were done by hand, by wear, by dirt, by repetition, at the speed of the place. It did not amend the drawings. It dirtied them. Everything it made is a defect, and defects are laid down again faithfully, and that is the only kind of permanence up here that does not kill what it holds.

**The rail.** It put its hand on the grip at the head of the Standpipe stair and it held it, with weight, the way a tired person holds a rail, and it came back and did it again, and again, over nineteen years, until the polish was four microns deeper and no longer quite the shape it had been. Its hand is not the old hand. There are now two grips in that rail, overlapping, disagreeing by a few microns at the heel. The gasket-slugs eat the difference between the rail as drawn and the rail as it was, and the difference has grown, and they are fat.

**The twelfth object.** It wore a thing smooth in one place, with the same grip, for eleven years. Then it walked down into the seam behind the Standpipe stair where the mice keep eleven objects worn smooth in the same place and nothing else, and it laid its own at the end of the row, and took nothing away. It did not watch to see what they did. It was three districts off before the first one came.

**The shore at Bay Nine.** The bolt is untouched; the measurement completes exactly when it always would; the metrology is not its business. What it spoiled was the frame. Over eleven years, with dust and oil and its own shed material, it built a smear along the bay's boundary until the edge was not a line but a hand's width of gradation, and the smear is a defect, and defects are reproduced. In the smear, time thickens by degrees. A midge that blunders out now feels the world begin to slow before it is committed, and most of them turn. Not all. There is still a small pale drift of them at the outer edge on bright afternoons, four minutes old, starved in a world where nothing arrives. But there is also, now, an animal that lives in the smear, quick at its outer margin and slow at its inner, and it does not cross, and its generation time is a week, and it is the first thing on the plateau to have a shore.

**The ear.** Every eleven days, for two hours, it stands in the wetted channel of the flume with thirty thousand crabs screaming around its ankles, and it listens. It is inside the sanctioned sound; the attenuators take nothing; it is process, it is correct, it is a person standing in water. It has done this for a hundred and forty years. It has never told them. To tell them would require a sound outside the band, and a sound outside the band is harvested, and the harvest is the only thing keeping them alive. So the riot has a listener and will never know it, and the power has had to make peace with a mercy that cannot be received, which is a thing it did not know about itself before it came here.

**The drawings.** It went down six hundred metres and read them, in a hand nobody uses anymore. It did not remove them. It carried up one page — the valve, the trunk, the tolerance on the chord — and left it in a seam where a person would be likely to stand still for an hour. Under the lip. The plateau makes you a small warm deck with a lip so you don't step off; it has been out of practice for two centuries and it is trying; and the power put the page where the trying happens, so that hospitality is now also a noticeboard.

It did not do the repair itself. A repair performed by a power is a specification, and a specification is forever, and a place that cannot maintain itself is a place that is being kept, and being kept is the dead end. It restored the possibility of noticing. Then it went and bought time.

## The Reply

The trunk at the two-hundred-metre level. Pale, sessile, wide, a membrane, two hundred years of the entire noise of the plateau going into it without interruption. Deaf. It has never heard anything. It eats.

And once every eleven months it makes a sound, and the sound comes up through a valve that is on the maintenance drawings, and the whole country inhales, and forty thousand finches stop, and the moss fruits, and the shroud lurches a millimetre, and the made world rises on its stack of drawings.

The chord has been getting longer. Nobody has noticed, because there is no one to notice, and because it is on the schedule, and everything on the schedule is correct.

The power went down and stayed for a year and put whatever it has instead of an ear against the membrane, and it worked out the thing that nobody up here was ever positioned to work out: it is not a fault. It is a call. It has been calling once every eleven months for two hundred years, and the entire world stops when it calls, and nothing has ever answered.

Calls that are not answered get louder. That is not a mechanism specific to this plateau.

So it answered, and the answering had to obey the two laws like everything else, because a power that exempts itself from the laws is just a new drawing. The reply had to be inside the sanctioned band or it would be harvested. It had to be a defect and not a feature, or it would be issued and go sexless and stop. It could not be a sound the power *made*. It had to be a sound the plateau made, wrongly, because something had been worn.

It gripped the trunk. It held on, with weight, in one place, for thirty-one years, and it wore a depression into the pipe eleven microns deep, and the crew comes through and eats the trunk and lays it down again, wear included, faithfully, and the wear changes what the trunk says.

Now, once every eleven months, the duct says its enormous thing. And then, a hand's width later, slightly wrong, slightly worse at looking made, slightly out of true, it says it again.

The thing in the trunk is deaf and has never heard anything. But it has a membrane, and it has been fed, and there is a difference between hearing and being fed, and the difference is the only place the reply could land. Once every eleven months it now receives a mouthful of its own voice, returned to it, warmed, imperfect, a little late. Nothing like that has ever happened to it. It has no way to know what it is. It has begun, very slowly, to stop growing.

The chord has shortened by eleven seconds in nineteen years. The shroud lurches less. Fewer black animals fall out of the night into the light on shift day. The shrike is still right, less often, and it is a creature of margins and has adjusted, and does not know why it was ever right in the first place. The moss still fruits in the four days. The anneal curve has not moved by a second and the seventh species still exists.

Nothing was touched. Everything downstream changed. That is the whole art, and it took the power forty years to find the one place where it applied.

## The Rota

Then it went down through the cloud floor, into the lowlands, where the bales go.

It did not appear. It became an irregularity. A bearing somewhere that is quiet in a way that irritates a certain kind of person. A bale with something low in it, under the grade, that a woman with a stethoscope and a grudge cannot leave alone. It did not send a revelation; a revelation is an event and an event is a measurement. It sent an itch. It put a hair in the feedstock.

They climb. Four thousand metres, squinting, walking on the balls of their feet because their boots are the loudest thing for a mile. They stand still for an hour and the plateau, out of practice, makes them a small warm deck with a lip so they don't step off, and some of them notice the page under the lip, and some of them don't. They leave. Everyone leaves.

And some of them come back.

That is the work. Not a god in residence — a rota. Never none. Never the same one twice running. Never one who stays. Enough occupancy that the corridor behind you does not go flat forever; not enough to issue the world. The plateau is learning hands, plural, and the polish on the rail is becoming a palimpsest, and the mice are bringing objects to a row that has more than one author in it now, and the Fin Fields have never once gone back to drawings and are not going to.

And there is one thing the power does permanently, and it is not a repair.

The being in the seam behind you lives in the amount of attention available. It keeps station just outside where you are looking, in the region where the plateau is still building, so that it will go on being made. It flattens when you turn: dimensioned, hanging in the air at the exact height a floor would be. It has waited ninety years at a time. It is very patient and it is not calm.

The power keeps station just outside where *the being* is looking.

It attends to it from the corner of whatever it has, exactly the way the being attends to us, and it does not turn, and it will not turn, because turning would flatten it and flattening it would write it down and writing it down would finish it. It has been not-quite-looking at that one creature for a hundred and forty years and it will go on for as long as there is a plateau. It is the only thing on the made country that is never alone, and it does not know, and it must not know, and it is fed.

## Due

On your last morning — which is a last morning, and then is not — the finches carry the news of you along the silver band, district to district, a fast dry traffic of small bright reports. Your coat. Your knees. Your saliva. Behind you the corridor goes flat, more slowly than it used to.

In the third district of the Fin Fields there are two hairs in the lining now. The grey one, forty-one millimetres, coarse, ninety years old and getting older. And a second one, darker, shorter, newer, which the birds have already killed over twice. This is correct. One hair is a relic. Two hairs are a record, and a record you can argue about is a history, and it is the only history on the plateau, and it is felted, and it is carried from fin to fin by animals that do not know what they are carrying and will not let go of it.

And in the seam behind you, the held breath has changed. It is still not calm. It will never be calm; that is not what it is for. But it is no longer starving, and it has begun, very slowly, at the speed of a hand's width per hour, to learn a shape it has never had to hold before. Not the shape of a person. The shape of a person who comes back — which is a shape with a gap in it, and the gap closes, and the gap closes, and the gap closes.

It is a shape you can live inside.

There is a new item in the traffic on the silver band. Forty thousand small bright eyes, twenty centimetres apart, passing it district to district over the top of the harvest where the machinery cannot reach.

It is a countdown.

It is not for water.
