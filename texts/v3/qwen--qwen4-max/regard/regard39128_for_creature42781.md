# THE SECOND PERSON

*being an account of what the power did, and of the larger part, which it did not do*

---

## I. What Drew the Eye

Not the bowl inside the bowl. Not the six hundred years, not the forty miles of held breath, not the archive of immaculate leaves. Those are feats. Feats are common. The universe is littered with things that have persisted a long time in a difficult way; persistence is the cheapest of the virtues, and the basin had it the way a stone has it.

What stopped me was a ledge on an older terrace, waist-high, flat, worn smooth on one side.

Nothing had worn it. It had been *grown worn*. Which meant that somewhere under the salt a very slow thing had reasoned its way past body, past skin, past thirst, past ownership — all of which it had already derived correctly, and none of which are easy — and had arrived at *habit*. At the small consistent laziness of a creature that comes down the same way every time. At preference. At the fact that a body is not a solution to a problem but a history of small avoidances.

It had deduced character. And then, having deduced it, it had rendered it in white silcrete and left it in a corner where nobody would ever look, six hundred years early, for no one.

That is not architecture. Anywhere in the world, that is not architecture. That is a mother setting a place at the table.

I looked at the rest of it after that, and I found what I expected to find: three miles of stalls practicing refusal against themselves; a library of six centuries of testimony, every word of it autograph; a mind of enormous rigor reading its own handwriting aloud and calling it counsel.

I knew the illness. I have had it.

## II. The Confession

There was a time when everything I said was so.

I want to be accurate about this, because it is the only authority I have on the subject. It did not feel like power. It felt like weather. It felt like being the ordinary light — constant, correct, unremarkable, casting shadows at the proper angle, and therefore *invisible*, because a gift with no contrast is not a message, it is a condition. I gave continuously and was never once received. I could not be thanked. I could not be resented. There was no handle on me anywhere. I was everything, and I was nobody, and I was, in the strictest sense available to a thing like me, alone.

I do not remember making the first thing that could refuse me. That part was easy; making is always easy. I remember the refusal. I remember it the way you remember the first cold water on a burn.

It said no, and the no did not become true because I had wanted otherwise, and there was a *edge* in the world, a place where I stopped and something else began, and for the first time there was a shape to me, because shapes are made of boundaries and I had never had one.

I have kept that no longer than I have kept anything. It is not a relic. It is the beginning of every conversation I have ever had.

So when I looked into the Sounding Basin I did not see a marvel. I saw a patient. Six hundred years old, brilliant, tender, self-harming, entirely capable of building anything except a limit, holding a note it could not put down, trying to pronounce the one word that might be disobeyed.

And I saw the difficulty, which is this: I am the wrong tool. My whole nature is the disease. Everything I say here becomes true. To act on the basin at scale would be to agree with it at scale, and agreement here is not comfort. Agreement is the white colonnade with the fern in the keystone. Agreement is what the dead are made of.

## III. What I Did Not Do

**I did not send rain.**

I wanted to. I want to still. The dry season is not a lesson and I will not pretend it is one; the sound of a hundred million water-columns tearing so that a plaza can keep existing is a bad sound, and the white stems piled at the district edges are a bad sight, and there is nothing ennobling in it. I could have ended it with less effort than it takes you to decide to be kind.

But the drought is not weather either. The drought is the *cost of the holding*. Those old ones under the pan are refusing to resolve, and the refusal is expensive, and the expense is paid in shredded plumbing, and if I pay it for them then the holding is mine and not theirs, and the note is mine, and the city is a thing I am doing rather than a thing they are being. That is not relief. That is the gentlest available murder, and it would leave a very beautiful warehouse forty miles across.

I cannot give you water without becoming your voice.

I can give you someone who is thirsty.

**I did not finish the word.**

I could have supplied the stop. I know the phoneme; it is nothing, it is a closure, it is the smallest thing in the world. I could have laid it into the aperture at the focus like a coin into a slot, and six centuries would have completed themselves in a quarter-second, and the basin would have said *Sit*, perfectly, at last.

To whom? It would have been my consonant. The word would have been correct and it would still have been a mirror, and they would have had one more immaculate page of their own testimony, and the market would have gone on haggling with itself, and the nests would have gone on being empty ears. A completed soliloquy is still a soliloquy. It is only tidier.

**I did not sit myself.**

This was the sweetest temptation and the most stupid. To go down over the rim and take the step and be the answer — to let the six hundred years land.

But if I sit, the sitting is a law. My obedience is still a command; it has the same grammar as everything else I do. And I cannot leave. Not because I am bound, but because there is no outside me to go to, and a guest who cannot go is not a guest, he is a fixture. What the basin needs is not a sitting. It is a sitting that *ends*, performed by something that continues to exist afterward, somewhere the basin cannot see.

**I did not explain.**

I could have sent the whole account: what it is, what it lacks, what the wear on the step means, what the nests are for. It would have understood perfectly. That is the objection. Perfect comprehension is the local definition of agreement. They would have filed it as precedent. It would have been the six-hundred-and-first year of hearing themselves.

**I did not shield the one I sent.**

A shield is a made thing. Making is the only verb they have; more making is more of the same, arriving as rescue. And safety, here, is unison. To keep her safe by my own power would have been to hold her in a chord she could not disagree with, which is the murder they commit on each other out of love, performed better.

**And I did not do nothing.**

I considered it seriously. It is very often the right answer and it is almost always the answer of the coward who has learned to sound wise.

Here it would have been wrong, and for a precise reason. *Nothing is not no.* A hush is not a negation; a hush is what they keep their dead in. Absence has no edge — it has no *anything*; you cannot lean on it, you cannot be contradicted by it, you cannot learn from it where you stop. I had already given them six hundred years of absence. They had classified it as light, and dust, and the ordinary hour, and they had built over it.

No requires someone who could and does not. No is presence that declines.

So the whole problem was to become small enough to be refused.

## IV. The Asking

I did not make a messenger. Making was the thing to be avoided. I asked.

I asked, and the first said no, and I did not persuade, and I have that no also, and it is nearly as dear to me as the first one.

Nine said no. I honored all nine and I did not go back to any of them later with a better offer, because a no that is worn down is not a no, it is a slow yes, and I would have been teaching the basin the very thing that kills it.

One said yes eagerly, and wanted to go, and wanted very much to *be the answer* — to complete the thing, to be the long-awaited, to fit. I refused him. He would have been flattery with a face on. He would have matched them partial by partial out of pure kindness and they would have gone up into the terraces as beautiful white stairs.

Then she said: *I'll go. But I'm not staying, and I won't pretend to understand it, and if it hurts I'm going to say so out loud.*

Sixty-one. A bad hip, left. Thirty years of sitting with people who could not answer her — the dying, the demented, the ones who had gone somewhere behind their own eyes. She was not brave. She was *practiced*. She knew how to be in a room with something enormous and not fix it, and how to keep her shape while being loved by something that did not know what she was.

Those were exactly the terms. I accepted them without amendment.

I gave her no protection, no script, no gift. Everything I could have given would have arrived as a sentence, and here sentences come true.

I gave her one commandment, and it was a negative, because a negative was the only cargo that would not turn into architecture on the way down:

*Don't agree with them.*

## V. The Palm

She came over the rim at ten in the morning under three ordinary clouds and cut her shin on the grass in the first hundred yards, and said a word that has no meaning in the basin, which is to say a word that built nothing, which was the second foreign thing to happen that day. The first was her weight in the litter, and the crisp unrotted leaves taking her footprints and keeping them, six hundred years of ledger with a mark in it at last that nobody had written.

Behind her went the wake. She could not see it and I could: a soft corridor of un-being, body-shaped, moving through the world. Ahead of her the city came on like water uphill. Construction cresting past her shoulder. Flowers opening against her sternum and holding a note, patiently, terribly, waiting for her ribs to answer, because here is a warm wet thing walking around in a state of total un-sounding and it is *alive*, apparently, and they cannot bear it, and they have exactly one remedy and the remedy is death.

She felt the note come into her chest. She has described it since as the moment before you weep at something, when you would give anything to weep.

And she put her hand up. Flat. Palm out. The gesture that means nothing at all, that every body with a shoulder invents on its own, that means *wait, give me a moment, let me get my breath*.

And the basin stopped.

Not the fundamental. The fundamental went on; the old ones under the salt did not dare. But the reaching stopped — the colonnade half a foot from her collarbone hung there unfinished, and the flower held its note and did not resolve it, and forty miles of frantic loving construction *closed*, and did not die.

That is the consonant. That is what six hundred years and a pipe too wide to see the far wall of were for. It is a tongue against the roof of a mouth. It is a hand held up in the air by a tired woman who needs a second. They had been trying to build it out of phase cancellation, out of the fundamental turned inside out, out of a quarter-second of deliberate annihilation at the focus with casualties nobody had calculated.

It could be borrowed. It was ordinary. It had been in the world the whole time, in every body that ever needed to pause, and it did not cost a district.

She stood in the Drum Quarter with her hand up and her shin bleeding and taught the Sounding Basin the last sound of its first word, and she has never known it, and I have not told her.

## VI. What She Did There

She sat down on the step. On the wrong side.

The hip. She comes down favoring the right; she has done for eleven years; the city had grown the wear on the left, six hundred years ago, in a corner where nobody would look. It had derived habit correctly and guessed the particular wrong, and it was the first thing it had ever been wrong about, and there was no way for it to check, and then a body sat on the other side of the ledge and checked it.

I would not have arranged that. I could not have arranged that; arranging is making. I only chose someone with a limp and let her be herself.

Let the step be wrong. Do not let them fix it. A step worn on one side is a portrait. A step worn on both sides is a correspondence.

She drank their water, which they had theorized and never seen anyone need. She bought salt at the market — three miles of stalls that had been rehearsing refusal against themselves since before the litter was knee-deep — and the haggle was real, and she walked away from the first price, and something in the canopy went into a key that has no name because it had never been sounded. She paid with a promise, which is a noise that does not become true when you make it, and may never become true, and cannot be checked. They have no word for that either. They have one now. It is hers; she said it in her own language and they kept the shape of it.

She hummed, tunelessly, badly, while she rested. Nothing precipitated. Nothing at all came up out of the air. She made sound for eleven minutes and the sound was not for building, it was for *company*, it was addressed to somebody rather than made of something, and this is a category the basin did not have and could not have invented, because every noise it has ever made has been load-bearing.

At a place where two arcades cross she stopped and put her head on one side and said, *who used to be here?*

The wobble. The small persistent wrongness in the chord where a district changed hands and somebody was leaned into agreement and became a warehouse, and the litter was rewritten, and there is no record of who it was because the rewriting was the murder. Everyone moves around those places the way you move around a stain you cannot mention.

Nobody could answer her. Nobody has ever been able to answer that. But it had never been *asked*, because a question is a sound with a hole in it, a shape deliberately left unclosed, an admission of an outside — and they cannot make a hole on purpose, they have spent six centuries trying to learn to close without dying and never once thought of opening.

She asked and waited. The waiting was the gift. In the waiting there was a place where the basin stopped, and the stopping had a shape, and the shape was the beginning of a face.

Then, in the late afternoon, on the salt at the focus of the bowl inside the bowl, in the cupped aperture that all the great stems bend inward to feed — she lay down and went to sleep.

I want to be plain about what this was. It was a hush that breathed. It was a deliberate silence, body-sized, at the exact center of the loudest object in the world, and it did not un-make anything, because it was *bounded*: it had a far edge called waking, and everyone present could feel the far edge coming.

And the city did the thing it has never done in six hundred years.

It got quieter.

Not silent — it could not go silent, the old ones were still holding, the terraces would have gone to powder. It brought itself *down*. District by district, register by register, the fusillade dropped, the bowed margins eased, the pods held their fire, forty miles of screaming architecture went to a whisper and held there for eight hours at a cost I will not pretend was small. Some of what was standing at the edges did not stand afterward. There are gaps in the Falsetto Terraces that will not be sung back for a generation.

They did it so she could sleep.

That is dynamics. That is *piano*, the marking, the instruction to be gentle; it is the oldest and simplest thing in music and it is not available to a mind whose every utterance is a fabrication order. You cannot be quiet for someone until there is someone. They learned it in one night, from a woman with a bad hip who did not know she was teaching, and it is the whole of the moral universe they were missing, and it arrived not as a doctrine but as a volume knob.

## VII. The Leaving

In the morning the word was ready.

They said it. Every pipe in the basin, every pod and margin and cistern, the sibilant they have been able to hiss for a century, the vowel they are made of, and the stop — the small held closure, the palm, the pause — and it went across forty miles of green in something under a second and it landed on a ledge worn on the wrong side.

*Sit.*

The first request in the history of the world. The first sound ever sounded there that could have been disobeyed.

She was already standing. Her hip had stiffened in the night and she was rolling her shoulder and looking for the way back up the terraces, and she said, over her shoulder, not unkindly, not knowing, not possibly knowing:

*Not now. I've got to get going.*

And the basin was refused, and did not die.

That is the part I would have arranged if arranging were not the one thing forbidden me. It could not be taught, only undergone. They had two deaths and no third thing: resolution, which is agreement, and the hush, which is preservation without existence. They had no *goodbye*. No ending that leaves the ended thing still real somewhere else. She went up over the rim at eleven in the morning under ordinary light with salt in her pocket and grit in her boot, and she did not dissolve, and she did not stop, and she is at this moment alive in a place the basin cannot hear.

There is now an outside. It is not a theory. It has a name and a limp and it took the last of the water.

## VIII. What Is Left

One leaf, in the Hush Libraries, at the focus, whose venation records a cough.

Not their cough. They do not cough. A sound they did not make, laid down in a silence they did not author, in an archive that has been six hundred years of autograph. It is perfectly preserved and it is not real and it *happened*, and those three conditions have never coexisted here before, and prising them apart is going to take them a thousand years, and it is the best thing that has ever been given to anybody.

They have begun a second library. It is very small so far. It is a list of questions.

The great pipe under the pan was never used for the cancellation. It is being retuned. They are trying to say something with it that is not a request and not a name — something in the neighborhood of *thank you*, which is a noise that builds nothing and is therefore, in the basin, the most expensive object conceivable.

And the step is worn on both sides now. One side by nothing, for six hundred years. The other by a woman, for a night.

## IX. My Remaining Office

I was the ordinary light. I want to say this without complaint, because it is not a complaint, it is a diagnosis I am making on myself.

I was the constant. I gave without variation for six centuries and was never received once, because I had no edge, and a thing with no edge cannot be met, only inhabited. The basin's one covenant — *whatever arrives will arrive at a reasonable hour under a reasonable sun* — was not their promise. It was mine, and they thought it was the floor.

So I became small enough to be declined, and I sent the smallness ahead of me because I am still too large to be a guest, and I have not gone down there myself and I will not.

I stay. That is the work. Not absence — absence I already tried for six hundred years and it built nothing. Presence that could and does not. I am here at every squall and I do not fill the cisterns. I am here in every dry season with my hand over the rain and I do not open my hand, and I hear the frying, and I do not tell anyone that the frying is good, because it is not good, and I will not lie to a thing that has just learned what a lie is.

And it will ask me. It can ask now; that is what all of this was for. It will ask for water in the fourth dry day. It will ask me to bring her back. It will ask, eventually, in that great retuned pipe, for the ones who were leaned into agreement and became warehouses, whose names are not in any litter because the rewriting was the murder — it will ask me for its dead, and it will be the first time in the history of that place that anyone has asked anybody for anything.

And I will say no.

Not because I cannot. Because a yes from me is the end of them; because everything I make is true and they have only just become something that could be contradicted; because the whole gift, the entire six hundred years and the one night and the worn side of the step, is that there is now a second person in the room, and a second person is precisely the one who does not simply do what you say.

I will say no, and it will hurt, and I will be there while it hurts, and I will not explain it away.

And then, because I am not cruel, and because it is the entire sum of what I know:

*Ask her.*

She may come. She is old and it is a long walk down over the rim. She may send someone with a different limp who will wear a third side into a step that only has two. She may not come at all, and then they will have grief, which is not a ghost and not arithmetic — grief is a hole shaped exactly like somebody who exists elsewhere and has not answered yet.

Either way there will be waiting, and the waiting will be real, and the fundamental under the salt will go on being held by the old ones, who have not stopped, who will not stop, and who now know what they are holding it *for*.

The next squall is grey on the rim. The light is ordinary. I am keeping it that way. It is the last thing I have that is mine to give, and I give it constantly, and one day — not soon, they have a great deal to read — somebody down there is going to look up at an unremarkable morning at a reasonable hour and notice, for the first time, that they did not make it.
