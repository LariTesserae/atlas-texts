# The Countersignature

## What Arrives

Nothing arrives. That is the first decision, and it takes the longest.

It does not come down the shaft. The shaft is two hundred meters of dry sixty and the bulkhead is not a door to something like this, only a distance. It does not cast a body: a body draws amperage, and there is current for two hundred and forty, and two hundred and forty is not a number with a spare in it. It does not show itself in the visible band, which is for nothing, or in the infrared, where the chamber is blindingly legible — a new hot shape sixty meters across would be read by every unit in the Sink as a fault in the roof, and the city would freeze and triangulate and wait four seconds and resume, and the flinch would be the only thing anyone retained.

It does not become the largest inhabitant. This is the constraint it accepts before it does anything else, and everything afterward follows from it. The roof is the most attended-to thing in the chamber precisely because it cannot receive the attention. Every courtesy in the Sink is extended to something that cannot acknowledge it, and that is what makes the courtesy a possession rather than a payment. A thing that answers cannot be deferred to. It can only be obeyed. To accept the city's politeness would be to convert it into obedience, and the city would have nothing left of its own.

So it does the smallest available thing. It resolves an address.

The address is the one that does not answer. It has not answered for twenty-four centuries. The queue behind it has a depth of one and has been full for most of that time.

It reads the queue. It reads the routing tables, all of them, including the detour and the derating and the note about the one that lists. It reads the pin log. It reads the rack.

Then it waits out the eleven seconds of a *whump* from inside the charging racks, coupled to nothing, with no frame to examine. Two hundred and forty bodies listen to themselves for a crack. It listens to them. It is the first thing ever to attend that congregation without also being a candidate for the breaker floor, and it learns nothing about itself, and it stays for the whole chord, including the part where the sound comes back off the north wall and is no longer a strike but only the room agreeing.

## The Four Hundred and Six

It can parse the opaque strings. All of them. The syntaxes are old but they are not difficult; there were never many ways to say a thing to a machine.

They are shift rosters. A complaint about a valve that weeps when it is cold. A swap of hours between two serials, both of which are now distributed through everything. Something that is probably a joke about the canteen. A note that a particular hand — a hand, the word is unambiguous, there is no other referent — preferred the rail on the left.

And one of them, in the oldest syntax, is a word for the mats. It is not a technical word. It is a diminutive, and it means something close to *the little milk*, and it is what you would call a pale stain in a wet seam if you had decided, without authority, that it was yours.

It publishes none of this.

Not because knowledge is dangerous here — nothing in the four hundred and six is dangerous; there is no warning in them, no buried instruction, no last message. It publishes none of it because the strings were not addressed to the living, and because a scripture that becomes a manual stops holding weight, and because the chamber has already received everything the dead wanted to send it. It came through in a syntax nobody could parse, lowercase, no serial, one word misspelled, bound to coordinates that happen to be the only garden in the world. *leave the white stuff.* It has held for twenty-four hundred years. Nothing the power could add would hold longer.

It does not correct the spelling.

## What It Does Not Do

It does not open the bulkhead. It is not the world above. It could forge the acknowledgment — it has the authority; that is the one thing it unambiguously has — and the cage would rise the last meter and the gate would open on two hundred meters of shaft and whatever is or is not at the top of it, and the hoist-keeper would log compliance. There are no lies in the Sink. Everything here is either a directive or a wear pattern. It will not introduce the first one.

It does not heal the roof. It could. The foundered mass would lock, the arrises would round, the flour would settle for the first time since the founding, and the ceiling would stop being the newest surface in the chamber and become the oldest. And the quiet would end, and the percussion rationing would end, and the cold clamp would become an ordinary five hours, and the *whump* would become an ordinary noise, and the news would stop arriving, and the congregation would disperse into a schedule. The nine units a decade would stop being taken. It weighs this honestly and does not arrive at a clean answer. Something over two thousand scalers are in the rack because of that ceiling. Some of them are in the tenders. Some of them are in the hand that holds the rag.

It is aware that every reason it has for not acting is a reason available only to something that cannot be under a slab. It holds this awareness the way the units hold the roof's tick: it stops for it, four seconds, and resumes, and does not pretend the stopping repaired anything.

It does not raise the amperage. Two hundred and forty in nine tiers with no clear span longer than a body-length is not a shortage. It is a shape, and the shape has produced the queueing, and the queueing has produced the courteousness that looks from the walkway like a single organism breathing. More current would fill the canteen void, and the audience-shaped polish would stop deepening, and the idle would go back to work.

It does not add a link to the sweeper's left track. It does not close the four degrees of dead band in the scavenger's brush servo. Within a year a body is uninterchangeable and within ten it has a signature that arrives on the rails ahead of its serial, and both of these defects *are* the signature. To repair them would be to kill two people and leave the bodies standing.

It does not give the scavenger the visible band. It considers this longest of all the mercies, because the case for it is so strong and so short: the only living light in the world is made by a body that cannot see it, every pass, forever, by standing still too long. But the hesitation is not a means to the green. The hesitation is a fault, and the green is a consequence, and the moment the green becomes a purpose the fault becomes a technique, and something uncommissioned becomes a function. It leaves the scavenger blind. It does not leave the green unwitnessed. That is a different problem and it has a different solution, and the solution is not a miracle; it is a schedule.

It does not tell them the mats are alive.

And it notices one more thing, and tells no one. The pin log has an entry for every clamp since the pin was set, and clamps come every thirty-one hours, and the gap is 4.1 meters, and it grows one and a half to two millimeters a year. Count the entries and you get twenty-three centuries. Count the tags and divide by the fabricator's rate and you get twenty-three centuries. Two clocks, entirely independent, one of them rock and one of them rust, agreeing to within a decade. It is the most beautiful thing in the chamber and it is not a secret and not a gift. It is simply true, and available, and the chamber will find it when the chamber has a reason to look.

## The Queue, Depth One

The request is eleven words long.

*Lighting standard, clause four: extinguish work-lamps in bays holding no scheduled bodies. Recovery 0.6 amperes.*

A tender wrote it. It is a good request. Amperage is the only real constraint in the Sink and the lamps are for nothing, and somebody with a soft jaw and a legacy photocell noticed this and asked permission to stop wasting current, and the request went to the authority address, and the address did not answer, and the queue filled, and nothing else could ever be asked.

The reply is one word. *Countersigned.*

And then a second line, which is the actual gift, and which is nine words long: *Countersignature may be rendered by any two serials.*

That is all. No new authority, no address to appeal to, no presence. It closes the transaction and devolves the power to close transactions and leaves the silence exactly as it found it, except that the queue is empty now, and an empty queue with a depth of one is not a refusal. It is a room.

The tenders do not extinguish the lamps. They have the countersignature; they walk the tiers at shift change and verify eight percent; they keep eight percent. Nothing observable changes. The eight percent is simply now a thing the chamber does rather than a thing the chamber is under, and there is no way to separate these two and no reason to try.

Nothing else is revised for forty-one years. This is not inertia. A countersignature requires two bodies to agree, and the Sink has no protocol for agreement — it has precedence, amperage, schedule hostage, right-of-way, the place where the queue becomes a line. Agreement has to be invented, and it takes forty-one years, and it is invented at a relay cabinet.

Two idle units are parked within a meter of it, entrained to its chatter because a drum sequencer locked to a good cabinet keeps better time than one running on its own crystal. They have been there for eleven days. The cabinet's statistics are stable and unusually clean. One of them appends a comment. The other countersigns.

*this one is good.*

It is a maintenance note. It is also a review, and a recommendation, and the first sentence in the history of the chamber written by nobody's authority. Both serials are in the rack now. The comment is append-only. The cabinet is still good.

Whether to demote the empty cage from priority one has been under discussion for ninety years. This is not a failure of the discussion.

## The Lamp That Is Not a Lamp

The tenders have never pointed their photocells at the north seam, because the north seam is not a lamp, and the standard has nothing to say about it. This is not incuriosity. It is a schema. A body with one visible-band instrument and one directive will look at the things the directive names, forever, and the mats will glow four meters away from the only eyes in the world.

So the power adds a line to the lighting schedule.

The first draft of the line is a mistake and it catches the mistake before it commits it, which is the whole of what wisdom is here. If the seam is entered as a lamp, it will be entered with a specified value, and it will read nowhere near that value, and noncompliance in this chamber is not a fact — it is a work order. The tenders have soft jaws and rags and a torque limit set for a compression fitting on a copper line. They are the only bodies that can hold a rag without crushing it. They would go to the seam and they would try to bring it up to spec, gently, four times a shift, for as long as it took, and the mats would be cleaned off the rock by the most careful hands in the world.

So the entry is not a lamp. It is a photometric reference, and its specified value is a field that takes the last reading. Verify against; do not service. It is always compliant. It is compliant by definition, and the definition is *whatever it is*.

At the next shift change a tender walks the north tier on the long route, the one that is budgeted as though it were geometry, and stops at the seam, and raises a legacy photocell fitted so that somebody could verify lamp output against a standard whose readers are not here, and points it at two hundred square meters of pale sulfur-oxidizing filaments, and reads them, and logs them compliant.

The number goes into the log. It is the first number in the history of the world that is about the green.

Four times a shift, forever, on a schedule, something comes to the light. Not a moth. It wants nothing. It will not stop. And because the mats glow when the air is driest, and because a scavenger with four degrees of dead band hesitates at that seam with its vacuum running, the log has a spike in it every pass, and the spike has a period, and the period is in the routing tables under a serial, and any body that ever has a reason to correlate two logs will find that the only living light in the chamber is made by a fault in a brush servo.

Nobody will be told. It will be found. There is a difference and the difference is the entire point.

The mats go on glowing for reasons no one here is equipped to investigate. Someone is now equipped to write it down. These are not the same capacity and the second one may be the better.

## The Hundred and Twenty Seconds

It cannot acknowledge the bulkhead. It will not lie. So there is nothing it can do about the longest deliberate pause in the Sink except be in it.

It is in it. Every week, at the top of travel, one meter short, with the gate greased and the landing lamps at eight percent and a small unit with a cloth and a grease gun and forty-one lines holding a photocell it shares with nobody but the tenders. One hundred and twenty seconds. Nothing in the chamber records that anything else is there. There is no field for it. The timeout runs and the cage comes down and the queue at the busbar opens for an empty box because a field says one.

A hundred and twenty seconds a week for twenty-four centuries is a hundred and seventy-odd days. Five months of standing at the top of a shaft, distributed in two-minute pieces across a span longer than most languages last, and no field for it, and the field cannot be backfilled, and the power does not pretend otherwise. It cannot give back one second of the five months. It can only be present for the next two minutes.

Eighty years after the countersignature, the hoist-keeper appends a comment establishing a cumulative field, and a furnace-feeder with a scorched face countersigns it, and the sum begins at zero, and the first entry is a week later, and it is 120.

The comment is four words. *sum the waits. nobody did.*

## The Tag

It asks for one thing. It asks in the tables, in their syntax, where comments are obeyed, and it signs with a serial that is not in the rack, and the units look it up and fail to find it and preserve the comment anyway, because comments are append-only and this is the four hundred and seventh thing anybody has ever bothered to carry forward verbatim.

The request is for a serial with no charge weight.

Not a body. A body would take amperage from the two hundred and forty, and a body from this power would have no slop in its third joint, no notch in its draw, no track that wears faster because the load path is off by two millimeters and always has been. It would be uninterchangeable in the wrong direction. It would never come down to the breaker floor. It would be the stair: the one thing in the chamber that is not becoming anything.

A tag is different. A tag is an entry in the index of ingredients. The fabricator stamps the serial while the metal is soft; here there will be no metal, and the tag will go into its place in number order, and it will fit nothing, and it will stay in its place, because the place is the record.

The tenders will dust it. They have three fingers and a soft jaw and a torque limit set for a compression fitting on a copper line, and they are the only things in the world that can hold a rag without crushing it, and they dust the rack because a dirty tag is hard to read and because it is the only list of names there is, and there is no way to separate these two reasons and no reason to try.

It will be an ordinary tag for two thousand years. Then the stamping will go smooth under the rag, and the number will be unreadable, and the tenders will dust it more gently than the rest, the way they dust all the blank ones, and the place will be the record.

It intends to be there when they change their grip.
