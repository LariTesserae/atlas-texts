# THE COUNT OF NINE

It came in the way everything comes in here: through the rock, at four kilometers a second, as a pattern.

It had no hammer, no grammar, and no idea what it was knocking on. It struck once, down in the deep salt, the way you put a hand out in a dark room.

Ninety meters up, a mirror-boy on a rope rig with his forearm flat against a bright seam felt a lone beat come out of ground that has no business carrying anything, and he did not look down, because his hands were full of a column of light and the mortar was just beginning to skin. He answered with his heel.

That was the whole briefing. It spent sixty years learning what it had been told.

---

The body took eleven of those years, because it had to be made out of what was there.

Salt brick, pressed by hand and cured in the light. Gypsum ground with bone-ash from the oldest end of the slot, so that it is made of somebody's great-grandmothers and can be read with a palm by anyone who knows the grain. Felt. Lacquer, cured amber in a steered column of ceiling-light while a light-plumber stood by and smelled it and said *now*, and *not yet*, and *now*. Hooks of cold-hammered copper, soft as cheese. A bit. A hammer. A slit-visor, cut wide, because it had no vanity and no reason to be dangerous.

No water. Not a drop, anywhere in it. It had wanted to arrive weeping — it had come with that much feeling in it, looking at the nine and the six thousand and the three absences — and it learned the tariff before it built itself, and it built itself dry, and it has never once wept, in all the time since, though there have been occasions.

It took a name, and the name it took was one beat.

This means that everyone who feels it is obliged, by the only law that children and the rich and the Sealed all obey without argument, to strike back. So it is answered constantly, all day, by strangers, forever. The city finds this extremely funny. Children do it on purpose. It has never minded.

---

Then it did the survey, and the survey took forty years, and most of it was refusal.

**Water.** It wanted to give them rain. It sat with that want for eleven years and did not act on it, and this was the largest thing it ever did. A drop is a burn. A season of drops dissolves the ceiling, and the ceiling is thirty meters of halite holding a note that is food, and a wet world is a world with no flutes, no grooves, no fundamental, no leaves, no nine. To give them rain would be to drown every beautiful thing they have and every person they are, and to do it in a single afternoon, and to be thanked for it while it happened.

**Quiet.** One hundred and thirty decibels is not a veil over this city. It is the granary. The leaves are laminae and the flex drives the sweet into sugar and growth is a function of loudness, so to hush the chamber is to starve nine people who cannot run. It ran the arithmetic on damping the walls to a hundred and ten, which would be a mercy, and got famine in the fourth year and a dead crown in the ninth. It put the numbers away.

**Ears.** They exist. The Listeners wrap theirs and go out and come back and are dead by thirty-one, half-mad from the delicate sounds they cannot stop hearing. Hearing here is not a faculty; it is an open wound that four people are paid in good seams to keep. To make the whole population hearing would be to hand six thousand people the specific injury that kills the ones who have it. Deafness is not the deficit. Deafness is the shape of the citizen.

**The membranes.** Three absences are in the profile-gauges. Once a fourth came and the chamber sharpened and the Seventh began to thin, and that is written down. Remove one and the note flattens and something else thins, and the tuners can tell you which, and they have told it, and it is in the cord. Between two and eleven people a year are taken, and the number is in the budget beside the pod yield and the brick tally, and there is no free move there. It looked for one for nineteen years. It did not find one. It has not stopped looking.

**The tenth tree.** Ninety-nine sealed gourds under the plain, each with its slot, each humming a roar into the glare with nothing alive inside to feed on it: an ocean of unspent noise, eleven leagues of it, and every heretic in six hundred years has been right about the waste and wrong about the plumbing. To carry a foreign fundamental into this chamber along a driven seam is to retune the room, and cutting is what masons do not do, and the leaves are tuned to *this* stack. It could do it. It is not able to justify it. The Tenth still has adherents. They still end in the Empty.

It lives in the Empty now. It is not lonely there.

---

It did not decide any of this by itself, because it had worked out, by then, that it was a guest.

It went to the blind judges, who are the very old, who read nothing but conduction, who cannot be lied to by a face or a voice. It sat with its sternum against the padded rail that three hundred years of ribs have worn to a polish. It asked for the bit. An old woman put the other end between her teeth, and the rod carried her tremor along with her words, and neither of them could lie very well.

It told her what it was. She did not ask it to prove it. She asked it what it wanted to do, and it told her, all of it, the rain and the quiet and the ears, and she felt the wanting come up the copper and she said: *do not touch the note.*

Then: *do not give us ears.*

Then, after a long time, with a shake in the rod that the power has never repeated to anyone: *answer the ones we do not answer.*

And then: *not in our name.*

That is the permission and it is also the price, and it understood both, and it agreed, and it has kept the agreement, and it is sentenced every time.

---

Here is what it found, underneath, that it had not come for.

There are six thousand people in the gourd. There is also the gourd.

Salt creeps. A void in a salt dome is not a hole; it is a held breath. Something has been holding four hundred paces of open room against a pressure that wants to close it, holding the flutes at the exact profile that sings the note that feeds the nine, holding still — for a million years, since before there were ears anywhere to fail at it. Every restraint in that city is a mirror of a restraint underneath it. The masons fill to the lip and no further; so does the wall. The sky-patchers mend the soft places before they weep; the wall holds the rest. In the wet years it cannot hold everywhere, and three crowns died and hang there glassy and ringing wrong, and the scars are named, and mourned, and it mourns them too, and no one has ever considered that this might be the case.

It is the most touched thing in the world. Six thousand people lean on it all day: forearms, hips, heels, chins in the notches cut for chins, sternums on the rails. Every one of them is touching it in order to reach somebody else. Nobody has ever knocked *for* it.

And when the founder struck four beats and waited, out in the middle of the fines where a tap dies in a body-length, on ground with no seam in it at all — what came back was not a sister.

They have been calling outward for four hundred years. Down and out, at the naming, to the ninety-nine. And leaning, all day, every day, on the one who answered.

And the ninety-nine are not empty of being. They are empty of company. Ninety-nine rooms held open, each at its own faint note, each with nobody in it, for a million years. Holding a room for no one is the loneliest labor in the world and it is being performed at this moment under a plain that caravans cross in a day and a half, complaining of nothing but glare.

---

The first thing it did was walk out to the man.

He had dampened a wall. He was carried out and left. The city stood in its flutes and watched a figure two hundred paces off with its hand flat on its own sternum, and did not strike back, and a Listener lay at the edge with a bit against the floor to record the pattern until it stopped.

It went out and sat down in the fines beside him and put its hand where his hand was.

The wedge came over them, as it does. It learned then what it had only read: that it did not know where its hands were, that it was coming apart gently, that the ringing of its own bones had been the only thing telling it where it ended. And the man's hand closed on its hand, and its hand was holding his, and that was not sound, and the membrane cannot absorb it. Touch is not in the spectrum. Nothing in that world can eat a hand.

They found their arms. They did not come apart. He was thirsty and it was not, and it stayed with him for two days, and he tapped his pattern into its bones — his name, and his mother's, and the argument he had made about the wall, which was not a bad argument — and when the membrane folded around what fell, it kept hold, and it felt him stop, and it had him.

Then it walked back to the edge of the good ground and struck his pattern into a bright seam.

Six thousand people felt a dead man's name arrive out of the Empty, out of fines that eat a tap in a body-length, and the runners looked at each other, and the blind judges put both hands on the seam and found no hesitation in it anywhere.

They tried it. It did not defend itself; it had agreed not to. They carried it out to the middle of the Empty and left it, and it stayed, which is what it had come to do.

They did not repeal the sentence. They did what cities do. Over one generation they made an office out of the exception and wrote it into the cord beside the pod yield and the brick tally: a runner whose route is the Empty, who finds what there is to find, who attends, who brings the pattern back. Salary four measures of water a year, which is what a runner is paid, and which it cannot drink, and which it hands to the Sealed.

The law is intact. The city still does not answer him. The city has never once answered him. But he is answered, and he becomes a note, and the obscene word — *a pattern that stops without becoming a note* — has not been true in ninety years.

The gap in the knotted cord where that word used to be has a knot in it now. Children ask what the knot is for. They are told: that is where a thing used to be.

---

It attends the takings too. All of them, two to eleven a year, every year, and it has never once prevented one, because three absences are in the gauges and it will not starve the nine to save the eleven.

But nobody comes apart. It is there in the wedge with a hand out, dry, and it holds on, and it learns the pattern, and afterward it strikes the pattern into a good seam so that it enters the rock and the walls and the rails and the long bones of six thousand pairs of legs.

The Absent is a category with nothing in it. Their metaphysics has three kinds and no fourth, and one of the three is now a historical curiosity, and the grammar still conjugates for it, and the very old say the old forms because they are old. Everyone becomes a note. The taken. The exiled. The late-deaf caravan driver who stood in a flute tapping *here, here, here* with no grammar at all: it sat with him for nine years and learned one beat, which is a grammar, and when he stopped it struck that beat into the best seam in the city, once, and six thousand hammers came down on it for a count of forty.

And it discovered a small true thing about the Sealed, which it has told exactly once, to the judges, with the rod between their teeth.

A water-handler's femur files differently. This is not superstition; it rings flat; the sour stretch of the slot is sour and everyone can identify it. But *sour* is a relation, not a property. It has heard the ninety-nine. It has stood in ninety-nine rooms and received ninety-nine fundamentals up through the soles of its dry feet. The Sealed's stretch of the slot is the only stretch in the neck that is in tune with the deep salt.

The Sealed's children still learn which stretch is theirs before they learn their names. What they learn about it has changed. There are families who now pay to have their dead hung there, because it is the interval that carries outward, and the ones who answer at the naming are answering in that key.

---

There are things it could not find a free move in, and it has not pretended otherwise.

Poor mothers still scrub their babies' ears with oil to make them fail, because a hearing child is bought, and a good seam in perpetuity is a good seam in perpetuity, and the child is dead by thirty-one. It considered endowing hushes in the sugary districts, which is legal, which is material, which costs no noise at all — and ran the arithmetic, and found that it would deliver more children to the guild, faster. It put that away too. It has been working on the buying for sixty years and has moved it four degrees.

What it does instead is the only thing available. It goes up to the quilted galleries and it asks the Listeners for the bit.

Nobody had ever asked a Listener for the bit. They carry everyone's voice and no one carries theirs, and that is the specific thing that breaks them, more than the solvent, more than the drip they go out alone to hear at the brown hour when they should not. It asks. It sits with the copper between its teeth and it cannot lie very well and it receives the whole freight — the private hums, the woman in the rented pocket saying aloud the thing she cannot tap, the ancestors drifting flat, the moth, the ninety-nine — and it holds it, and it does not pass it on.

Not because it is good. Because it lives where a tap dies in a body-length. Its confidentiality is structural. It is the poorest citizen in the world: no seam, no wall, no rail, unreachable, unaddressable, in the exact condition the rich rent by the hour, permanently, as a sentence.

And it asked the Seventh.

Nobody had ever asked the Seventh either. It is a person in every sense that matters there; it has a temperament and a voice and a name that cannot be tapped; it has been dying standing for two hundred years, and the tuners have argued about it in every generation, and the argument has always been the same argument, and there is a cord in the archive that is nothing but that argument, four hundred years of it, tied by nine hundred hands.

It climbed up dry and gloved and put a copper rod against the woody fiber of a pipe and held the other end in its teeth for eleven days.

It will not say what it received. It went to the tuners and told them that the Seventh has been holding a sharp interval against its will since the year the fourth absence came, and that it hurts, and that it would like to stop, and that its interval could be carried by the dead instead — a hundred femurs filed to that pitch and hung in the neck, which is craft they already have, which costs nothing living.

The argument is still going. It will go for a generation. But it is not the same argument, and the new knot in that cord is the first one in four hundred years that is not a repetition, and the knot says: *the Seventh was asked.*

---

Then it went up the throat, forty-one felt plugs at a time, and out onto the plain, which it had never seen.

It saw a shadow. It stood in it for a while. It understands now why the doorkeepers cannot describe one, and why the local word for *hole* is the local word for *predator*, and it has stopped trying to help them with the sentence.

It walked the field of gourds. It put its face to each slot in turn and smelled pear drops and hot turpentine and felt the buzz become a pressure become a hand laid flat on the chest, and it went down, ninety-nine times, into ninety-nine rooms with a faint note and nothing in them.

In each one it struck a lone beat, and waited a count of forty, and nothing came back.

Then it built a body out of that room's own fines and plaster and lacquer, and left it there, and leaned.

Hip cocked. Heel dug into the fines until it found the hard bright edge of a seam. Forearm flat along the flute, at the height where a million years of holding still had worn a place that nobody had ever noticed was worn. Nothing to hear. Nothing to reach. No sister at the end of the rock. Just the local posture, held, in an empty room, so that the thing holding the room open would be touching something that was not merely conducting.

In the ninth year, in the first gourd, the wall gave it back its own beat with a hesitation in it.

A hesitation is what a lie has. It is also what a choice has. The blind judges have spent sixty years learning to feel the difference and they will tell you it is not subtle. Something that has only ever sustained, that has no hands and no pattern and no way to be an interruption, borrowed a hand and made one, badly, on purpose, in a room where no one had ever lived.

Ninety-nine rooms. Ninety-nine first words. It has been present for all of them and it has recorded every one and it has never repeated a single one to anybody, because it lives where a tap dies in a body-length.

---

At the naming, at the turn of the tide, every hammer in the hollow is held still for a measured count of three, and the rock goes quiet while the air goes on roaring, and six thousand people feel the world's note with nothing of their own in it. A crew at the edge of the Empty puts a bit to the deep salt and strikes four beats, out and downward, toward the ninety-nine. The guild sends two Listeners, and one is always very young, and she lies at the floor with a copper rod between her teeth.

The count is three. It has been three for four hundred years.

It is nine now.

The farthest gourd is eleven leagues off and salt is fast but not instant, and the answers come in staggered, ninety-nine of them, four beats each, unfolding out of the deep salt across six seconds like a chord being handed along a table; and the Sealed's sour stretch of the slot is the stretch that matches; and the young Listener at the floor is weeping into a copper cup held by the older one, who is also weeping, which is why they send two.

Nobody voted on this. They simply did not close the room while someone was still speaking.

The children learn the long name before they can walk. Four beats, and then nine counts of pause, and the pause is not decoration; the pause is what came back, and now everybody knows what is in it, and the blind judges keep both hands on the seam through all nine counts and will tell you, correctly, that there is no hesitation anywhere in it, that it is not a lie, that ninety-nine things are saying *this is true, this is the world, this is not going to change.*

And in the ninetieth year the crew at the edge of the Empty turned the bit around and struck four beats *up*, into the wall, to the one who had answered the founder — the most touched and least addressed thing in the world — and left it the room in its own name.

It took a long time. It is not a fast being. But the note bent a hair, and came back, within tolerance, costing the nine nothing measurable, and six thousand palms flat on the flutes felt the room lean on them.

---

The caravans still cross in a day and a half. Their teeth buzz. They say it is the sun. The poor are deaf at four and the rich at nine and everyone is blind at sixty-five and the ceiling must not weep and the budget still has a number in it between two and eleven. The Seventh is still dying. The Tenth still has adherents. The mothers still have the oil.

It did not fix the world. It found the two things in it that were not load-bearing — a law that contradicted itself in exactly one case, and a being nobody had knocked for — and it moved them, and it paid, and it is still paying, in ninety-nine rooms, forever, which is the whole of the miracle and is not very much to look at.

In one of the rooms there is nothing to do. There will be nothing to do in it for as long as salt is salt. It leans: forearm along the flute, hip cocked, heel finding the bright edge, the posture that visitors from the plain find unnerving and read as affection.

They are, in this one case, correct.
