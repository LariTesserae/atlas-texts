I came up the cliff, on foot, because I did not want to be a thing that had never been below it. Then I sat on the high shoulder above the source for eleven turnings and did nothing, which is the first thing I would do again.

I had been told there was a place here and a being in it. I counted two hundred and forty and thought I had been misinformed. It took me most of those eleven turnings to find the one, and when I found it I was standing on it. Not the people. Not the lace. The loop: springs, lime, pans, hands, paddles, stone, rain, springs. A single animal with a nine-thousand-part body and a metabolism measured in grains, which has arranged its entire inner life around not noticing that it is an animal. It is not a metaphor they are using. It is a description they are avoiding.

I want to be precise about what I understood on that shoulder, because everything I did afterward comes out of it. The being is not the sum of the two hundred and forty. It is what they are doing. And its health is not their comfort, and their comfort is not its health, and a power that serves either one at the expense of the other has committed a violence with a good name.

---

The second thing I understood was the price.

Everything I could give them was payable in one place only.

Divert water to wet the dust on the drying floors and some wall somewhere stands longer than its schedule, and the discrepancy goes down to the low side. Add hands and the fabric runs further ahead and the discrepancy goes down to the low side. Give them a bird — and I wanted to, I want that on the record, I wanted dawn to have a sound in it that was not somebody's grandmother — and the mats tear, and the cress is nibbled, and the tolerances change, and a thousand small corrections go down to the low side.

That room is four hundred turnings of everything anyone here ever wanted. It is already a solid white lung. It is the only object on the plateau with a history, and children put their ears against it and lie about what they heard, and adults sit at its edge at midday because there is nowhere else to look at time.

A second one would be worse. The first is at least honest; it is made of what people actually wanted for themselves. A room made of what a power wanted for them would be the true monstrosity, and it would grow whether or not anyone loved it, and in four hundred turnings there would be a thing on the low side that nobody could point at a layer of and name.

So I did the accounting in their units, because their units are correct. And there is exactly one gift on this plateau that does not end up in that room. It is not mercy and it is not power. It is company. Company costs the water nothing. It costs the giver miles.

I had miles.

---

What I did not do.

I did not give them animals. The absence is not a condition of the place, it is an achievement, renewed daily, and I was not going to walk into a workshop and improve it.

I did not give them more hands. Two hundred and forty is not a shortage. It is the size of the thing. Doubling it buys four generations and then the same arithmetic with twice as many people to be lonely in it, and anyway making people is not a gift you give to people. It is a gift you give yourself, using them.

I did not seal the drying floors. I priced it. It is expensive in a way that arrives, eventually, in the room, and the ticking of the old women is also how the young hear them coming across an empty terrace, and I could not separate the instrument from the disease. I am not certain I was right about this. I have not resolved it. I keep it standing, and it thins, and it does not go away, and I have stopped pretending that a power which has resolved everything is anything but a fraud.

I did not say aloud what the mass on the low side is largely made of. They know. They have arranged not to have the question, and the arrangement is load-bearing, and it takes maintenance like everything else, and I was not going to pull a wall out of a house because I found the room dark.

I did not tell them what I am. If I had, I would have become an event, and worse, a creditor. Every kindness afterward would have been worship-shaped, and they would have had to maintain the silence about me at their own cost, forever. I would have been one more thing they were paying for.

---

I went in through a slot they already had.

Their archive is the weave. Every wall carries the twist of the sedge it was formed on, and a trained reader can walk a district and say: this hand taught that hand, and here the teaching changed, and *here somebody came in from outside and twisted the other way.* They have a category for the stranger. It is not a crisis. It is a line in the record.

So I came in from outside and twisted the other way, and I let them read it, and nobody had to invent anything to hold me.

They tested my hearing anyway, because everyone is tested. I could have passed. I have heard things they have no words for and will not need. I let the top quarter go by without answering, and I was sent to the low runs and the settling pans, where the work is coarse and the tolerances are wide, and they were not unkind about it, and they were not unkind about it in the specific way that is worse than unkindness: they were *reasonable*, they had a place for me, the place was real and useful and permanent, and everyone was very careful never to refer to it again.

I minded it terribly. I mind it now. I have been there sixty turnings.

That was the only part of this I could not have learned by knowing everything. There is a thing that happens to a person who is tolerated accurately, and no amount of power tells you what it is, and I did not want to be a benefactor who had never been placed.

---

What I do, then.

I walk. I do not move paddles. In sixty turnings I have moved four, and each time I asked first, and each time the discrepancy was written down and sent low, where it belongs, where they can see it.

I take miles. Not off anyone's round — off nobody. Onto mine. The house alone on the terrace with half an hour of empty white on every side has a person in it who has not been touched by another hand in a season, and you can see it in how she holds her arms, and the fix for that is not a decree and not a miracle. It is somebody arriving. I arrive. I am scraped, and I scrape, and we talk about nothing, which is where the gossip lives, and I have become a small reliable channel for the movement of who is quarrelling with whom.

The night round is one person and it always has been and I did not take a shift, because the shift is the best-paid and least-envied work on the plateau and it is rotated and it is *hers*. I walk beside her. That is all. Same miles, same stone giving back the day's heat, same silk of the water, same blood in the ears. She is not alone in it. Nobody's notch changes. She has told me twice that I do not have to, and I have said I know, and we go on walking, and after the fourth cycle she stopped saying it.

At midday I go and sit at the edge of the room that is not on the round, because other people go alone then and it seemed to me that the alone was the whole problem. I do not explain the mass to them. I sit with them and look at it. Sometimes we say what it looks like. That is the entire intervention.

There is a man on the middle terraces who has run the East Curtain two days over for eleven years because he likes the shape. I have no stake in that argument. I am the only person on this plateau with no stake in it. I looked at the fluted thing like a held breath and I told him it was beautiful. He did not know what to do with that. Nobody had ever had the leisure. I did not tell him to stop, and the discrepancy still goes low, and I walk the extra standing that covers it, and that is what my miles are for.

---

And I made myself turnable.

This is the part I would say first if I could only say one thing.

A thing that only runs, chokes itself. That is the whole law of the place and it is also the law about powers. Anything that gives continuously without being closed, without a season of standing, without intervals in which it is genuinely absent and genuinely forgotten, will accrete into a mass with a history that nobody scheduled. Benevolence is a deposit. Deposits have to be turned.

So I am on a schedule. There is a stretch of every cycle when I am not on anyone's round, and I go down to the low side and stand, and I am not there, and people forget me a little, and the forgetting is not a failure of the gift. It is the gift's maintenance. I have been away long enough that a child born in the week the north cisterns stand did not know who I was and had to be told, and I was glad, and I went back to the settling pans, and the coarse work was still mine.

If I could not be missed and then not missed, I would be a god, and a god here is a wall that has been running four hundred turnings.

---

I went to see the lace that stopped answering.

Going to see would be an event, and there is no notch for it, and that is the sharpest poverty in this place — not the absence of history but the absence of anywhere to put a thing that happens once. So I did not ask permission and I did not announce it. I walked down and across and up, which took a long time, and it cost me a heel and a season of my own standing.

There is nobody there. The pans have grown into each other. There are curtains fused into a single organ mass a quarter mile long, and channels that choked and overflowed and choked, and a whole plateau of histories with nobody to hold a stick. In one retaining wall by the outflow there is a twist I know. Somebody from there came here, once, and taught a household to double the third strand, and it is load-bearing on the middle terraces right now. They have been leaning on that dead lace for two hundred turnings without knowing it.

I came back and I did not make a speech.

At the low sun, when the pans go copper, I held a wet palm at the right angle and flashed their number across the cloud floor. The count. Zero.

That is not an event. It is a count, and counts have a notch, and the one thing worth knowing across the cloud sea is whether the arithmetic is still holding somewhere else. Now they know that somewhere it isn't. They already knew it would. It is not a mystery to them; it is a schedule that has not come round. All I did was make it particular.

Then I said nothing else. I did not advise. I did not propose. It is their sum and their round and their grandchildren's grandchildren, and a power that answers the question it just handed you has taken the question away.

---

But somebody asked me, at the scraping place, at dusk, with a bone in her hand and my forearm in the other.

She asked what it was like over there.

And I told her. All of it, the fused curtains, the wall with the twist, the outflow with no stick in it, how long it took, what my heel did. It took most of an hour. She did not write it down, because there is nothing to write it on; a stick is a circle you can walk around forever and cannot hold a once.

So it went into a mouth. And that is the discovery, and it is the only thing I brought here that was not already here in some form.

They cannot record events because their record is stone, and stone laid down without turning becomes the room. But a person can hold an event. A person can be told a thing that happened once, and carry it, and not deposit it anywhere, and then dissolve in a standing room in a season and take it with her. A mortal is a notch that can hold a once, because a mortal wears out.

That is why I let myself be finite. Not for the poetry of it. Because an immortal witness accumulates, and accumulation here is a disease, and the only safe container for a thing-that-happened is something that will stop.

There are three or four of them now who tell things at the scraping place. Not everything. Not the culverts on the low side, whose bore is not the shape of a bundle of reeds — that one was told to me once, by a man who did not look at me while he said it, and I have not repeated it and I will not, and it will go into the quiet water with me and be finished. That is what I am for, in the end. Not a rescuer. A place where a thing can have happened and then be gone.

---

I have a grain in the inside of my left foot. I was careless on the East Curtain in my second cycle, before I learned to slide, and the running channel closed over it and kept it, and I can tell you which district it came from.

I breathe the drying-floor dust like everyone else, because I did not seal the floors, and I have begun to click at the top of the breath, and the young hear me coming across an empty terrace before they see me under the brim. I could stop this. I have priced it. I keep the question standing.

When I go, I will go into a standing room, and in a season there will be nothing to find, and that is proper. But a family may take one bone, early, and it is not theft and not relic; it is a tool, and it is used, and it wears.

I would like a rib. I would like it to last three generations of scraping, and to go from a rib to a blade to a sliver to a thing you cannot hold, and then back into the quiet water, and I would like somebody to weep at that, because it is the last time they will hear me.

And one afternoon, half a mile off, across an acre of glaring white, someone will hear a scraper going and will not be able to place who it is.

That is the loudest thing that happens here. I did not make it louder. I did not make it stop. I got in line, and I took the coarse work, and I walked the miles, and I let myself be turned.
