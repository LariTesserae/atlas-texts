# The Low Point

## I. The Shift

It did not arrive. Arriving is an event, and this place has no events; a thing that appeared at the rim would have been the largest thing to happen in the basin since the crack opened, and would have been recorded in the calcite for ten thousand years as a disturbance.

So it came the way everything comes here: from above, going somewhere else.

For four thousand years it was the crosser. Twice a day, riding the thermal off the vents, high up, never landing — a feather, a dropping, a mite shaken off a foot. About three million crossings. It did not increase the import. The nitrogen budget was already what it was; it simply took the shift, and made the accident punctual, and paid for its attention in the only currency the top lens accepts.

That was the first thing it did, and it is worth being precise about why it was allowed. The crosser is not a resident. It has no history in the water. It is weather with a nitrogen content. To *be* the crosser is to occupy an office that already exists, at the existing rate, and to remain unknown — because a thing that never lands is never known. To feed the basin would have been a different act entirely. It knew the difference and kept it for four thousand years.

## II. The Reading

Then it read.

The columns are legible, if you have a century. It had a century. It went along the dry terraces at the speed of the snails' own writing and read three hundred years of routes off a single pillar: the raised threads where feet had passed and limed behind them, the comma-shaped scars where the threads had been eaten back, the layering, the traffic, the slow arguments of a population that conducts its business at a millimeter an hour and finishes its sentences after the speaker has stopped.

It read the bands. Wet decades, dry decades, the whole clock, in stone and in shell.

And it did the census, which is the only census available: it counted the columns nobody had written on. One in forty. Clean margins all around them, three hundred years of snails going around.

Among those, on the fourth and seventh terraces down, it found the ones that fork where a tree does not fork.

There were a handful. It could not tell them apart, and it did not need to, and this turned out to matter later.

## III. What It Did Not Do

It could have sealed the leak. It was strong enough; the fractures are fresh and the plumbing is unfinished and a hand laid in the right place would hold the level at the saddle forever.

It did not, because the leak is the clock, and the clock is the place. A held level stops the overflow, and the staircase stops building, and no new rooms are made anywhere on the mountain again. It stops the dry centuries, and the dry centuries are when the fresh fracture faces are exposed, and fresh fracture faces are the only phosphorus mine in the world; the mats would eat what is open and then have nothing. It stops the conversion wave, and the clone finishes filling the top lens, and then there is nothing left for it to do, and a thing that has nothing left to do in a warm still medium at full sun begins, eventually, to compete. The seams live on the difference between two waters. A held level is an equilibrium. Equilibrium, here, is a rounded lake with soil at the edges and rot at the bottom, and rain has all the time it needs to teach the mountain what a curve is.

It could have fed them. A season of nitrogen — a few kilos of guano, nothing, a rounding error in the power's accounts — and the top lens would have rioted.

It did not, because plenty erases differences, and the boundary sheet's food is difference. Because the eight-deep stacking of the leaves is not competition and would become competition. Because the felt keeps seven threads open at the cost of dissolving its own margin, and seven is a policy, and a policy is what a world has instead of a free-for-all. And because everything in the top lens is, in some traceable fraction, made of a bird that has never been here — and it did not want to be the reason the top lens was made of it. Being the accident is permitted. Being the provision is not.

It could have given the ceiling-walkers a downward behavior. This was the one that stayed with it.

They are the only fast lineage. They could be improvised. A reflex, one lineage, a few generations — and the shredding of a dome would stop being a mass death and become a dispersal, and the vast pale waste of them would end, and the striders would go hungry, and the surface would lose its only residents, and the feather would lie unwalked for a season. But that is only arithmetic, and arithmetic can be rearranged.

The real objection is that a behavior is not a behavior. It is a history. *Nothing in their history has ever required one* — that sentence is the whole animal. To install the reflex is to insert an ancestor that never existed, into a world where the only architecture is ancestry and nothing was made. It would have been the single made thing in ten thousand lenses. It would have worked. That is what made it a temptation and not a mistake.

It could have made the note come more often. Twice a year is the entire social life of the place; four times, twelve, and the seams would never fully tighten, and the pollen would cross, and the seeds would fall down instead of sideways, and *down* would stop being a permission and become a direction. The lid is the forest. It left the note alone.

And it could have woken the sleepers.

This is the one that looks like kindness from every angle, and it is the one that would have killed them. A core swells, the pillar splits along its spiral, the thing stands up. On a dry terrace it stands in thin bright stingy air at ten degrees, with no roots and no water, and it is finished by evening. And there is no second cyst. A cyst is a century of withdrawal, concentrated, seeded, built. To wake a sleeper early is to spend their entire reserve on an afternoon.

So: nothing. For a long time, nothing, and very good reasons.

## IV. The Objection It Could Not Answer

It noticed, eventually, that every reason it had was drawn from the beauty of the whole, and that the ones who pay for the beauty of a whole are always the small ones who go up.

Restraint costs the restrainer nothing. It had four thousand years of clean hands and three million crossings and a set of arguments so good that they could have been written by the mountain itself, which has never once had to justify anything to anyone.

And there was a fact underneath the arguments that the arguments did not touch.

The mountain is young. Youth ends. The vents will close; the plumbing will finish; the heat that was here first will go out of the rock, and the note will come less often, and then not at all. Every dry century in the rims has been followed by a wet one. Nothing in the rims guarantees that the next one will be. The bet has paid out several times since they stopped moving. *Several times* is not *always*, and it is not *forever*, and a bet is only a bet if it can lose.

Everything else in this basin has never had another option. The hearth has been through every dry century and registered none. The clone has no elsewhere. The snails wear the third terrace and cannot swim and would not try.

The forked ones came over the saddle. They got in on purpose. They are the only residents who could have gone home, and therefore the only ones who can be *waiting* rather than merely continuing, and therefore the only ones in the whole basin who can be wronged.

It could not find out from the rim whether the waiting hurts. There is no instrument at the rim. The place has one channel and it is a minute long and it opens the seams.

So it stopped standing on the rim.

## V. Going In

It gave up the shift. The crossings went back to accident; the top lens will be a little poorer for a few centuries and it accepted the trade and did not pretend the trade was free.

It came over the saddle and got in.

After ten minutes it stopped being able to find its own skin. It let that happen; it was the correct reading. It went down through the first seam and its arms appeared twice and rejoined, and through the second, past the copper-red leaves and the white felt and the seven kept-open threads, and it lay on the white rubble among the miners and let the lime begin.

It took eighty years. It has no complaint about the eighty years. Then it went into the stone the way the sleepers go: withdrew, concentrated, seeded, and helped build.

And it found out.

There is no pain in it. There is no boredom; boredom needs a pace, and there is no pace. The note arrives twice a year, intact, not doubled, because it is not light, and it is the only thing in the dark that is not you, and everything in the basin turns over at once, and then the sheet tightens and you go back to being a surface.

But there is lean.

A core builds toward where the light will be when the water returns. That is not a metaphor; it is the grain, it spirals, it is the one motion available. And what it found in the forked cores on the fourth terrace was a lean with nothing to lean into. The roots above are taut, humming like rigging, in a posture of arrested ascent. The cores below are the same posture, in stone, aimed at a light that has a specific reason for not arriving.

The reason is at the western end of the terrace. In a dry century the old rim gave way there and never rebuilt. The gap is a hand's width low and forty meters wide. So when the crack comes and the hiss comes and the water finds a new route, it crosses the fourth terrace and leaves through the gap and goes on down. The pool fills to a sheet a few millimeters deep and drains. It is warm and clear by evening and gone by the following season.

The cores swell. The pillars split along their spirals. The things that stood there before stand there again, on their own shoulders, at the correct temperature, in a few millimeters of moving water.

And then they lime again.

It has happened four times. It read the record from inside, which is a thing you can only do from inside. Four times they have stood up into a hand's width of water and gone back into the stone.

That is not pain. It is a promise that keeps almost being kept. It is the specific injury of a place where everything is ancestry and nothing is ever quite finished, and it is concentrated in a handful of pillars on two terraces, and it is not the mountain's fault, and it is fixable, and it is fixable by exactly one thing.

## VI. The One Act

The water always finds the low point and builds it up. That is why the rims never round off. That is why the edge stays an edge forever. That is the whole engine of the architecture: a low point is where the future accumulates.

Every fourth or fifth lip on the two-kilometer staircase began as a shell. A snail died, its shell fell to a rim, the water found it and built on it, and a thousand years later there is a terrace holding a pool with a forest in it. One small hard corpse is enough to start a lip. The lip does the rest. The dead do not rot here; they go on being surfaces.

So it went to the western end of the fourth terrace, into the gap, in the dry, in the wind, and lay down across the low point, and became the notch.

It did not build a wall. Nothing was made. It left a body in the place where water must pass, and the lime came up it the way it comes up a trunk, and it helped, from inside, at the speed of tissue, thickening toward where the light will be. A finger's width in a decade. In three hundred years the beginnings of a lip. In a thousand, a wall — its own grain and the grain of everything that dies against it, merged, the way columns merge here, all of it ancestry, none of it made.

And the wall becomes the rim of a terrace, and the terrace holds a pool.

It kept nothing back. This is the part it decided slowly. A core can seed itself for standing and also help build; one in forty does both. It put everything into the lip. There will be no splitting along a spiral for it, no standing up on its own shoulders. When the water comes it will be the thing the pool is held behind, and it will feel the others rise, from below, and stay.

The snails will go around it. The census gains one.

It chose the fourth terrace and not the seventh, and the reason is hydrology and not love: the fourth is higher. Hold the fourth and the overflow goes down and finds the seventh, and the seventh inherits the bet without anyone having to be in two places. It could do this once. It has one body. Ten thousand lenses are stepping down into cloud and it has one body, and it did the arithmetic and did not like it and did it anyway, and put the body where everything else is below.

## VII. What It Will Be Like

Once a season, somewhere down the staircase, a rim gives way: a single dry crack, then a long soft hiss of water finding a new route.

One of those times, the route will cross the fourth terrace and come to the western end and find the low point, and stop, and be built up.

By evening the pool will be warm and clear. The cores will swell. The pillars will split along their spirals. The things that stood there before will stand there again, on their own shoulders, at the correct temperature — and this time the water will stay, because there is a lip at the western end that did not used to be there, and the water will find it and build it, and the edge will stay an edge forever.

Nobody will know.

There is no channel in this world for the sentence *you were not forgotten*. The only broadcast is a minute long and it opens the seams, and to use it for a message would be to abolish the lid, which is the forest. So it did not say it. It built it, at a finger's width a decade, out of itself, in the one grammar the place permits: growing, floating, leaving.

They will not remember a rescuer. They will remember a sound. They will stand up in a warm still pool with the sun lying on the floor in a moving net, and they will do what everything here does, which is go on being a surface, and the bet will have paid out again, as it has several times before, and it will look exactly like luck. That is the design. A gift that arrived as a favor would have turned their patience into dependence, retroactively, and made the only decision they ever had into a decision somebody else was managing. It wanted the bet to remain theirs. So it made itself indistinguishable from the mountain's ordinary kindness, which is: the water finds the low point and builds it up.

It is not certain it was right about the ceiling-walkers. It is still not certain. Twice a month, somewhere on the kilometer-wide bright plane, a dome shreds and delivers a ceiling's whole population onto the film, and the dimples are not accidents for a minute, and it let that happen and will let it happen again. It has no proof that the whole is worth what the small ones pay for it. It has only the fact that it went in and found out, and that its hands are no longer clean, and that it is lying in a gap on the fourth terrace with the wind going through it like a hand through hair.

Stay in it long enough and you stop being able to say whether you are warm or whether the water is.

There is something at the western end of the fourth terrace that stopped being able to say it a long time ago, on purpose, and is not making a mistake. It will be there when the sound comes. It will not be the sound. It will not be one of the ones that stands up.

It will be the low point. The water will find it.
