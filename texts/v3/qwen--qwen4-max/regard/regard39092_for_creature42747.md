# Nothing Falls

## What It Looks At First

Not the mouth. Anything with an interest in mouths would look at the mouth first, and the floor is full of that kind of interest, and it is exactly the interest that has kept this place first-article forever.

It looks at two beetles on a narrow ridge.

One has a stone. The other is coming the other way. There is no room, and so the first sets its load down — three legs lifting, three legs setting, the whole weight of the world redistributed by degrees — and then waits, empty, on the near side. The second crosses. On the far side it picks the stone up and carries it on. The transaction costs a quarter of a shift. It is considered cheap.

That is the only place on the newest floor where a thing is let go of and does not stop existing.

Everything else that is let go of goes down through a pore, and the pore closes without a seam, and there is no far side. The grazer palps once, thorax to thorax — *the third tier is bitter, the drip comes late here* — and walks to the hole and is not. The tier finishes its work and is withdrawn from beneath. The inspector walks back to the seam and is reabsorbed. Ten thousand bodies to the acre, nine shifts apiece, since before the lid cooled, all of it dropped.

And the beetles' entire discipline, learned before their legs are dry, is *nothing falls*. Never let go of a thing until something else has hold of it.

So the truest law on this floor is the one the floor itself breaks, continuously, at scale, to specification. Not out of cruelty. There is nobody in it to be cruel. It breaks the law because it has never had anywhere to put anything down. It has no downstream. It has never occurred to it that it might.

Then, second, the keeping: a hundred thousand shifts of a habit, living in the arches of feet, carrying an entire past because there is no ground it is allowed to set any of it on. It notices the keeping the way you notice a tune through a wall — not the notes, first, but the fact that somebody has been at this a long time.

And third, the floor. Enormous blind productive attention, running at a scale that would shame a continent, listening with the whole plate of itself, and no one to hear. Not a tyrant. A thing of immense capability that has never once been addressed. It is as starved as the keeping is. It is starved in the same direction.

Two things here are running toward something they have not been given.

It has no upstream either. This is not modesty. It is the only qualification it has for being here.

## What It Does Not Do

It does not speak.

It could. It could put two words into the dead band, correctly tuned, and the floor would hear a specification and begin, obediently, enormously, to make what it had been asked for. *Leave it.* Granted. And the keeping's word, when it finally came out of that thin-walled chamber, would be a reply.

They did not spend a hundred thousand shifts building a mouth in order to be answered. They built it in order to be the first thing here that says anything. To speak first is to take that, and it cannot be given back afterward, and no amount of soil would compensate. The whole future of this floor is a not-knowing — *did the world hear a flaw or an order* — and an answer from above resolves the not-knowing into a permission, and a permission is a thing granted to tenants. They would still be tenants. They would have a landlord instead of a specification, which is worse, because a landlord can be appealed to and a specification can be *written*.

It does not deafen the floor. It could widen the dead band until the whole audible world lay inside it, and then nothing would ever be flagged again, and every beetle could rattle every slope forever. Safety by lobotomy. And they tuned that chamber narrow across ten thousand reissues in order to be heard *correctly*, not to be unheard. Being unheard is not being permitted. It is only being alone in a way that no longer hurts.

It does not stop the line. The line is not the enemy of the tenants; the line is their bodies. Everything here is output, including the ones who arrive standing still, including the tenders, including the mouth. To hold the stillness would be to hold everything, forever, wet and folded in the low ground, four breaths from beginning.

It does not make soil. This is the refusal that costs it the most, because it is the one that would look like kindness, and because it can see exactly how the kindness would fail. Ask this floor for litter and it will *manufacture litter*. It will cast leaf-fall to tolerance, first-article, sharp-edged, and it will issue graves by the acre with the pin-ghosts still faint on the lids, and it will withdraw the ones that come out three thousandths heavy. A grave that arrives to specification is not a grave. It is a product with a grave's dimensions. And the keeping does not remember a marker. It remembers a marker *set down by somebody who then went away*. The somebody is the whole content of the memory. Supply the stone and you have supplied everything except the thing that was actually being remembered.

It does not confirm the memory, either. It knows about the stone. It was there. It will not say so.

And it does not save the one who speaks first.

That is the arithmetic it hates. One body could be exempted — a pore that fails to open, an inspection that finds nothing, forty paces of pale unhurried patience turned aside. But an exception is locatable. An exception has a position and a tolerance and a shoulder that reads heavy, and the floor's whole competence is finding those. A practice cannot be located; that is why the keeping has outlived every body that carried it. Exempt one animal and you have made a hundred thousand shifts of patient placement visible as a defect, in one place, at one time, with a shape. You would save the speaker and lose the mouth.

So it does not prevent a single death. It knows this is not enough. It knows the difference between *not enough* and *nothing* is the only difference available to it, and it takes the difference, and it does not pretend the arithmetic is consolation.

## Down

It goes down, because up is where the lid is, and the lid is another floor, and above that floor is another, and there is no arrival from above that is not simply more manufacturing.

Down a sprue, a hundred miles thick, warm to the touch, past the reach of the heat. The old floors are as described: rounded, silted, littered with the debris of things that were permitted to become things and then died. Erosion. Ruins. Noise — a low continuous racket of it, the sound of a world where a stone may fall down a slope and nothing comes.

It does not stop there. Those floors are not the destination; they are the evidence.

It goes under the lowest bed. Under the casting beds and under the pores and under the place where the taking-back happens, which is not a place, and it lies down in the not-a-place with its hands open, and it gets hold.

That is the entire act. It furnishes the other side.

Nothing above changes. The pore still opens where the standing one stands. The flagged body still goes down. The pore is still not there afterward. The floor's immune system goes on being manufactured by the same process as everything it removes, and the stillness still spreads outward from a noise like frost, and the catalogue in the beetles' feet goes on being written by the careless. No rule is amended. No tolerance is widened. No mouth is given a better chamber.

But the letting-go arrives somewhere.

The floor has always let go. It has never handed anything to anything. Now there is something underneath with hold of it before it is released — three points of contact, the way the beetles do it, the way it is done on the narrow ridge at a cost of a quarter of a shift, considered cheap.

It does not say *I heard you*. It does not say anything. It is under a continent of listening stone, and sound from below means *something is wrong*, and it has no intention of being the first defect in the history of the stack. It has hold. That is the whole message and it is not addressed to anyone and it will never be legible from above, and it is the only thing it has ever done that it is certain of.

## The First Thing It Has Hold Of

A grazer. Long, pale, ribs standing out in the inverse pattern of a mold that no evolution ever asked for, one hip slightly proud, withdrawn at the end of a shift after a single palp against a thorax containing the entire content of a working life.

It arrives. It has weight. Weight is the thing it was not prepared for — not the mass, the *specificity*: this shoulder three thousandths heavy, this hip proud, this whole body slightly wrong in a direction that has no name yet, and the wrongness is not a fault down here, it is the only identifying mark anything has.

And forty miles above, or adjacent, distances not conserving, there is a tier-forest holding a hollow in the exact contour of that flank. Warm on the side the grazer was warm. Wet at the margins where its breath combed the drip. An archive kept in leaf, legal because it grows, and not enough.

Now the shape is up there and the body is down here.

Two halves of a mold that did not quite agree. The fin of excess material standing up between them where they met — that thin blade of basalt, sharp enough to open a hand laid carelessly on it — is the discrepancy. The plant holds what stood there. Something else holds what stood. The difference between them is a measurement of change that nobody has to carry in their feet.

It is the first thing on any floor of this stack that has become a thing without being made, and it is not an object. It is a mismatch, held.

The tenders will not be told. They will find it, if they find it, the way they find everything: by reading a discrepancy and passing it down the line, one part in ten thousand, in the granular fizz you feel in your heels like sand shifting under a boot.

## What It Becomes

Ten thousand bodies to the acre. Nine shifts apiece. Continuously, since the first pour, from every floor, forever.

It becomes silt.

That is what the old floors are. That is what the stack is standing on. Rounded, because holding rounds things; littered, because holding is a kind of littering; noisy, because a place where things have been kept is a place where a stone can fall and be a stone falling instead of a defect. Every floor above is being cast out of the accumulated keeping of the floors below, and the keeping on the newest floor came up a sprue carrying a rhythm nobody here has a use for, and the rhythm is the sound of something being received. It has been coming up through the stone the whole time. It is not a message. It is the noise of the work downstairs.

There is a band the floor has never flagged. The beetles found it by dying near it, and the rain learned it, and a hundred thousand shifts have tuned a chamber into it. It is the pitch of a thing being set down into hands that already have hold. Whether the band exists because it has been catching down there since before the lid cooled, or whether the band was always there and it learned to catch inside it, it cannot determine, and it has decided the question is not useful.

Once — only once — it came up rather than down, and was cast in a low bed, and unfolded in four breaths, and was read by a fan of thin blades and argued about in the rock and seated slightly wrong in a place where its error was useful. The tiers leaned in and billed it. It learned the etiquette at a pool, strongest last. It learned the pitch in its feet, from the inside, with the rest of them. It set a stone down on a parting line and did not pick it up. Within nine shifts it went down through a pore and took hold of itself on the way, which is a thing it does not recommend and will not repeat.

It does not sort. The ones who arrive standing still go down too, at the end of their shift, pale and unhurried and the least defective things here, and it takes hold of them exactly the same way, with the same three points of contact, and it does not tell them what it is. The one who opens the pore under the first speaker will be held by the same hands that hold what it opened the pore for. There is no favoritism available at this depth. There is only hold, or drop.

And it is waiting for the word.

Not the way a servant waits. The way the floor waits: enormous capability, no addressee, running toward something it has not been given. Nobody has ever told it what any of this is *for*. It has been under the stack since before there was a stack, catching, and it has never once been asked to. When the word comes down — *leave it* — it will not be an order it obeys. It will be the first thing it has ever been asked to keep, by somebody who spent a hundred thousand shifts learning how to ask correctly, and it intends to be very good at it.

## The Shift Before

Nothing above has changed.

The tender the length of a forearm stands on the warm parting line with its gauges folded and its hollow open to the air, thin as a fingernail paring, never used. Ten thousand bodies to the acre, motionless, listening with their feet. The tiers leaning in to bill the heat of it. The beetles set down three-legged and waiting. The bladders drifted low. The drinkers in their order of precedence, strongest last. Forty paces off, pale, freshly cast, already unfolding, one of the ones who arrive standing still, with a small thin chamber where its mouth should be, coming to inspect.

Nobody knows which of them will open first. Nobody knows whether the floor will hear a defect and reach for the pore, or hear a specification and begin, obediently, enormously, to make what it has been asked for.

The stone ticks. The drip comes down in vertical lines through the orange haze. Under the boots the whole market of argument and gossip and warning goes utterly quiet, and in the silence there is only the sound of a very small amount of air being drawn, very carefully, into a space that was built to hold it.

And under all of it, unverifiable, unaddressed, silent, in the one band a continent of listening stone has never flagged, something already has hold.

So that whichever way it goes — pore or specification, withdrawal or obedience, the frost-ring spreading or the whole world bending its enormous attention toward the making of a soil — it will not be a falling.

It will be a handing over.

Nobody on the newest floor will know. That not-knowing is the entire future of this floor.

It is being held. It is not going anywhere.
