## The first mistake

It arrives at its own size, and the quarter goes still.

Not from awe. Weight in stone is a word here, and the word is the one they have for the end of the world. Its attention has mass. The merest lean of it on the fabric goes out at five kilometres a second and is read in the feet of the whole district a half-second before it is a sound: the frogs quit knocking, the harvestmen flatten, the salamander stops with one forefoot raised, and somewhere in the liverwort a female presses her limbs to the moss and reads *catastrophe*, wrongly, for the fourth time this season.

It withdraws.

Everything it does afterward follows from that withdrawal. There is no channel into this basin wide enough to carry it. The medium eats sound. The light comes forty minutes a day. The tongue is touch and it works at one centimetre and it has six things to say. To be present here at all it must come down to something the size of a neighbour, and to be understood it must consent to the grammar, and the grammar has no word for *far*, so it cannot say where it came from, or that it came, or that there is anywhere else.

It arrived with an answer. There is no question here that it fits.

## What it does not do

It does not warm the basin. The cold is not an affliction; it is the lid. Lift it and the weather comes in, and the July fog stops condensing on the east wall, and the moss loses the net it takes fish with, and the nine-degree seeps go seasonal, and the salamanders, who breathe through their skin, dry.

It does not remove the falcon. The falcon is written into the masonry as a change in the depth of a hole. Take it away and the hoods stop meaning anything, and the particular stillness that means it stops being taught, and a local tradition dies in a place that has almost nothing but local traditions.

It does not give them voices. Sound is what the falcon hunts by. Also the touch-language is not a makeshift, not a deprivation, not a pidgin awaiting correction: it moves through the fabric faster than sound could, and it is older than the birds' fluency in it. It is the basin's own tongue. The birds merely learned it well.

It does not abolish the tax at each beak. The ranks are the plumbing. The geometry of hunger is a map of the passages, and to shorten a rank you must rebuild a district, and to rebuild a district you must be the water.

It does not stop the barrages failing. The failures are how the larvae travel, how the frontier gets seeded, how the masons get work, how the salamander came to know four corridors.

It does not make them deathless. Everything below the top two centimetres of this place is standing on its own dead. The wall is the corpse of the moss that is climbing it. The nest is a core sample of a lineage. Remove the dead and there is no floor, no rise, nothing to keep pace with, and the mite — which spends its eleven centimetres going up — has no reason to travel at all.

It does not finish it. The hole is not finished being made. Completion is the only thing that would kill it outright.

And it does not tell anyone. It would have liked to. It has counted what a hundred visitors a day would cost, and the number is not in hours of stillness, because stillness is not free here; the number is in chicks at the deep end of long ranks, unfed, while a hundred thousand bodies hold for a thing that has not happened.

## The one who lives here

It looks for the resident. There are candidates.

The dam-moss, which is very likely one clone off the bark of a fallen spruce, older than the city, brainless, and the author of every wall. The planarians, arguably a single animal that has been interrupted many times. The hectare of canopy that fell in and goes out to the light each morning now and comes back at dusk carrying what the light made. The female in the east wall who remembers four failures as a sequence of textures and has no way of telling anybody.

All of these are the place. The one who *lives in* it is the thing inside the tube: in the dark, in stone, doing nothing but growing and feeling the wall, fed by a parent it knows only as a pressure and a quantity of insect.

There are ten thousand of them. Each one is alone. Each one is the one.

## The residue

Then it counts, and the counting is the whole of its ethics.

Most of the cost here is load-bearing. The falcon's takings feed a chick on a ledge above the window. The tax is a distribution. The broken barrage is a frontier being seeded. It does not touch any of that; it is not the price of the city being the city, it is the city.

But when a face goes dry the birds above it abandon their cells within a week and the masons close the gap within the month, and in that interval there are cups that have fallen out of the geometry. Ranks get too long when a district rearranges. There is a number, every season, of tubes in which the only question is asked and no answer arrives, and the small dry shapes of them go down into the fabric beneath the next bird to sit there.

That number buys nothing. It is not structure. It is leakage — the part the geometry cannot reach.

It works only on the leakage. It took a season to learn the difference, which is a short time, and it will not be finished, which is the correct amount of time.

## The brood patch

So it joins.

It could make the insect. It doesn't. Conjured nitrogen is a subsidy from outside a closed budget: the moss would take it, the wall would rise faster than two millimetres, the plan would drift, and in forty years the district would be a district nobody's grammar fitted. A miracle here is a distortion. So it earns the bolus. It goes up at first light in the column, eleven minutes of it, and spends the day over the plateau and the river valley and the neighbouring ridge taking insects out of the air at forty kilometres an hour, and packs them behind a tongue it has made for the purpose, and comes down at dusk in the rain of wings, and pays the tax at every beak on the way down the rank.

It learns to lean. It stands the dawn negotiation and yields, and is yielded to, and is refused, and takes the shift it is given. It cannot walk; nothing here can. It takes a face over a drop and hooks on, because it must fall to fly.

And to say the one thing worth saying, it grows a brood patch.

Bare, hot, densely innervated skin on its underside, because that is the word. There is no other word. *I am here* is a heat held against a flank a half-second longer than usual, and it cannot be said at a distance, and it cannot be said through stone by anything that has not made itself warm and bare and capable of being felt. It makes itself that. Then it goes down the long quarter into the permanent white shadow, to the cups at the deep end, and says it.

It has accepted the mite's condition. It cannot be dry. It must climb two millimetres a year or be sealed in, and it will be climbing for as long as this is here.

The other half of its method is not speech at all. When the east wall goes for the fifth time, it does not carry the salamander; she goes unhurried, in the dark, and the fifth corridor is one she has not used. It becomes a roughness under her forefeet at the correct turn. It says nothing. It is read. Not message — medium.

The falcon has a chick too, above the window, also nine hours from a beak. It goes there as well. It has not taken a side. It has taken a shift.

## The rim

Outside the grammar it acts at scale, and invisibly, and mostly by making things boring. A survey that finds nothing worth taking. An estimate that comes back high. A seam that pinches out. A road that goes another way for reasons nobody will remember. It does not arrange for the place to be loved. It arranges for it to be unprofitable, which lasts longer.

Some of it it cannot stop. The lid is held down by the sky, and the sky is being changed by everyone, and it is working on that elsewhere at the same speed it does everything now, which is two millimetres a year. If the lid lifts it will not put the basin under glass. Preservation is another way of finishing a thing. It will stay and take shifts in whatever is here then, and learn that grammar too.

## Nine hours

It does not tell the chick what it is. There is no word for it, and no word for *far*, and it does not teach one. A bird that could say *far* would still have to fall to fly, and would have nothing to say to its neighbours, and would be alone in a way that nothing in this basin is alone.

What the chick gets is what the medium carries. At the ninth hour: a pressure on the wall, a quantity of insect, and the six things, said at one centimetre, by something warm.

*Hold. Food is coming. I am here. Is it alive.*

It is.

From the rim, over forty years, all of this would look like a change in the pattern of white.
