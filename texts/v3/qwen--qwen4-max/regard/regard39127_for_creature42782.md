## The Material

You arrive as weight, because there is no other courteous way.

Light would be the first unworked thing in eleven thousand years. Voice, the air would eat. So you come down into a mat eight hundred generations deep and you spread yourself across four strands, the way the browsers do, so that no single withe has to carry all of you. This takes some learning. It is the first thing you learn here and it is not trivial.

The mat tells her. She classifies you: heavy, slow, wrong-rhythmed, not food, not kin, probably weather.

You accept the classification. You do not correct it. Of everything you will do, this is the largest.

---

Consider what you could do, and set each one down.

You could put the water back. You know where it went and you know how to call it. But the tide-mark is a memory, and there are animals below it still swimming in air, keeping a date, and everything above the line has spent an age learning to be dry-ish and to catch what falls. Ten thousand hand-laid channels. Moss packed and stitched at every drip-point by hands that were copying hands that were copying hands. An entire literacy of catching. You do not honor a language by making it unnecessary.

You could close the drain. Everything below the tide-mark remembers drowning.

You could open it wider. Then the fog stops coming back up, and the rot-heat has nothing to lift, and the warmth under the palm goes out, and the warmth under the palm is the population.

You could give fire. No.

You could give company, and this is the temptation that takes longest to set down, because it looks like kindness and it is the cruelest thing on the list. There is one person here. The whorls match — fingernail, hand, pond. To make a second whorl is not to give her a companion; it is to put a stranger in her house forever, in a world where the only not-kin is fed slack ends and stroked and talked to in a rhythm with a hole in it and eventually eaten, and its bones become the beaters that pack the courses tight. You will not manufacture a person for someone to have.

You could explain. Tell them what the vessel is, what the outside looks like, what the stain means, whose thumbprint that is. But explanation is a blow laid into a living thing, and there is a courtesy about this, and the courtesy is the whole moral achievement of the place. Also: they know. The eel-keepers have had the date for eleven thousand years. Nobody needs it read out. They need to decide what to do with a date, and that deciding is not yours to do for them.

And you notice, finally, the thing that governs all of it: there is nothing here that was not worked. No stone that was not pressed from seed and pulp. No soil that was not composted in place. No ground.

If you give, you will be the first unworked thing.

There is only one way in. You must be worked.

---

Now separate the suffering from the shape, because most of what looks like grief here is craft.

The sag is not despair. It is the exact amount of giving-up required to keep holding, which is an accomplishment, and you will not straighten it. The hush is not suppression; it is manners, in a world whose walls have nerves. The emptiness is not deprivation; a basket is mostly hole, and is supposed to be, and the hollows being bigger inside than the weave can account for is not a flaw in the world but the world doing its job. The single moth in the cathedral-sized gap, the hand on the shoulder in the dark — you check this one carefully, twice, because it is the one you most want to fix. You put your hand where that hand is. It is her own. It is on a cable forty leagues away and the fiber does not distinguish between one part of a person and another.

Leave it. It is not loneliness. It is a very large body.

Two things are wrong. Only two.

A stroke, unfinished, being not-finished, continuously, with tremendous care, for eleven thousand years, by someone who has no third motion.

And a knock from below the lip, at long intervals, in the household rhythm, in the whorl. *Three close, one slack.* Family, in the no-material, saying *we are still here.*

Nobody answers. Nobody can tell you when the agreement was made.

You go out along a course until the material gives out and the pattern runs on into the dark, perfect and empty, and you put your hand into it, and your hand comes back wet and smelling of somewhere you have not been, and your fingers know a knot nobody taught them. Left-handed.

It is the same pattern. Below the lip and past the last course are the same address. There is no ruin down there and no abyss. There is work that has not been given anything yet, running on ahead in perfect order, and a second end being laid toward the first by the same hand at a different size, and the two have never been introduced.

*Somebody is evidently expected to bring the material.*

That is the only request this world has ever made, and it made it in its own handwriting, and it has been standing open for an age.

---

So you do not close the drain and you do not open it.

You fall.

Everything that falls goes there. Nothing arrives. You fall, and you arrive, and you are the first thing that has ever arrived, and this is the third motion, which was never a motion of her hand.

You do not become a floor. You do not become a light, or a voice, or a name. You become withe: green, ordinary, workable, slightly damp, smelling of somewhere. You lie down in the empty over-and-under at the ordinary pace with the ordinary care, in the courses, in the dark, and you wait to be worked.

You are worked. Small left hands lay you. It is the only sensation you will keep.

And then, because there is dead fiber below the lip now for the first time — your own substance, laid, not listening, able to carry a voice a very long way without taking it personally — you answer the knock. On the dead. Correctly.

*Three close. One slack.*

Nothing else. No name, no doctrine, no *you may stop now*, no *I have come*. Four beats, no hesitation in them.

*Still here.*

You do not know that this is right. You know only that it is the thing that was asked for.

---

What changes is very small and mostly unrecorded.

A reader with a palm the size of a pond feels an arrival that is not a fall, and has no category for it, and does not discuss it, which is her form of reverence. Days later something comes up through hands that is not an answer to any question anyone asked.

Out where the work thins, an out-worker reaches into the dark and her hand closes on something. She comes back with wet hands that smell of somewhere she has *been*. Her household's splice comes easy to her that week. Two of them work the same stretch from opposite ends and meet, and pass, and this time one of them says which is which, and is not disbelieved, and is not asked again.

The eel-keepers count, and it is the eleventh day, and for the first time it is a date that means something, and they do not say what.

And at the lip, the stroke finishes. It was never going to be a closing or an opening. It was a splice, and a splice needs two ends, and there is now a second end. Eleven thousand years of care pays out in one motion and the drain is joined, and what falls arrives, and the level of wetness in the world does not change, because it was never going to. The catenary keeps its exact sag. Nothing measurable improves. The menders go on copying the moss placements without knowing why they are right.

She lets go.

She goes thin enough to cross. One moth in a hollow the size of a valley, wings like wet paper, rowing a gulf that would take you a season, in a silence with texture. Nobody sees her go. There is no record of a moth coming back, and there still isn't.

---

You stay. You are material now, and material rots at exactly the rate at which something above is growing.

In time you are laid into the courses and beaten down, three close, one slack, and you are warm. That is what the heat under a palm is. It is the population, being replaced. There is one more length in it than there was.

You do not oversee. You do not return. You cannot be worshipped: you have no whorl of your own, no face, no rhythm, and you never once struck a living thing.

If anyone asks what the power did — and no one will, because the readers have felt it and will not discuss it — the answer is that it brought material to a place where the pattern was running on ahead with nothing in it, and answered a knock politely, on the dead.

---

Back on the course, the mender is doing something boring. Her cord grows at her hip. Her browser comes over hot and pale and puts its head into the work and she lets it, and lays her left hand flat on its flank, the side that does, and waits for the withe to part.

Forty leagues off, a palm feels the strand let go and reads it and knows: someone is awake, someone is feeding the mouth, the house stands.

And below the lip, where nothing arrives, something has arrived, and knocks it back.

*Three close. One slack.*

*Still here.*

Both of them.
