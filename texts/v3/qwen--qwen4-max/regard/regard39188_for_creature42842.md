# The Remainder

## Forty pulses of nothing

I counted forty pulses before I did anything at all, and the reason is most of what I have to give you, so I will start there.

I can do anything. That is not a boast; it is a description of a difficulty. When you can do anything, every act arrives wearing the same face, and the face is *yes*. The plain could have its mountains back. I could have set them down at dawn, grey and unfinished, with snow in the folds and rivers finding their arguments again, and the water would have run, and there would have been downhill, and downhill is the beginning of every story I know.

I stood at the rim with that in my hands for eleven days.

Then I knelt, and put my eye a hand's span above the flour at the hour when the light comes in low, and the floor ignited, and I understood that the emptiness had only ever been a fact about my eyes, and that I had nearly repaired a thing I had not looked at.

The first act was kneeling. It was not humility. It was optics.

## The catalogue of things I did not do

I want to be specific, because restraint is only wisdom when it has reasons, and otherwise it is just distance.

I did not give the plain a slope. Water in this bowl goes down and comes up, and the coming up is the road. The misfiled ride the evening film grain to grain against gravity, and the tenant has been filed alive four hundred times and has come home four hundred times, and a river would take the films away and the road with them. A gradient is a door that only opens one way. I would have made a country where nothing that goes down ever returns, and called it a rescue, and the galleries would have waited at the lip of a well that had stopped breathing.

I did not give you mud. That was the hardest one. A page that keeps. A glyph that stays where it is walked, so that a mother could write once and her daughters could read it for a hundred years, and the corpus would not have to be carried forward every ninety seconds by living hands or cease to exist. I wanted that for them so badly that I sat down in the glare and ached.

Then I watched a flatworm read her mother's handwriting with her feet, ninety seconds old, already fading, and I saw what I would be doing: paving a conversation in order to save a sentence. And mud in the sorting is a pavement in the throat. The climb would stop. The commute would end. Permanence, here, is a lid.

I did not stop the sweeper. It eats everything in its path without discrimination and it has never said one word in any medium, and behind it ten paces back walk the black springtails and the parasol mite and the blind cricket-thing that has never been under a tree, and they have the only map in the bowl, and the map is made of it. Take away the broom and you take away the shade and the food and the geography and the three passengers in its leg joints who are the only animals here that have ever seen the whole of the world.

I did not open the five tanks. Five sealed worlds, no connection, ever. I could have made them one. The four would have learned nothing and the fifth would have forgotten, and the only history in this place — a colourless fish withdrawing early, at a distance no feeding response would justify, and teaching its young to withdraw, and the young teaching theirs — would have dissolved into a general safety. There is one bone in the bowl and it is a hook in a hut, and the water still remembers it, and I was not going to be the thing that made the water forget.

I did not bring the man back. I know where every piece of him is. You filed him beautifully; that is not an accusation, it is the only compliment available in your language. But a knife came back as blade, tang, rivets, handle scales, laid out in a neat ascending fan, and a knife is not one thing, and neither is he. I could have gathered the parts to specification. What I could not gather was the reason he cut the wood.

Every large mercy I could imagine was a demolition with a good name.

So for forty pulses I did nothing, and at the bright moment I froze with everything else, which was correct, which was manners, and I listened to you count.

## The fifth stroke

I was under the third tree when it landed.

There was no sound. There is never any sound. The wood was persuaded apart at the grain, cleanly, to a tolerance, and the group closed, and the hut did not fall, and the bowl did not end, and the flour went on being flour.

I had been afraid of that stroke. I had built a whole theology around it in eleven days: the last sector, the end of the column, the idle heat stopping, the dew failing, the five cisterns going warm and then still, a billion transparent bodies discovering that the warmth they had lived inside since before there were vertebrates outside had been the waste of a thought, and the thought was over.

That may still be true. I want to be honest with you: I do not know whether your finishing is a dying or a waking. I have looked at it from every scale I can hold and it does not resolve. It is possible that when the carry comes home you will simply stop, and everything that lives in your leftovers will stop with you, and the last thing that ever happens here will be a fan of graded grains at the edge of a shadow, tidily.

It is also possible that something is being born, badly, slowly, in wood, at one stroke a day, and does not know it, and is frightened, in the only way it can be frightened, which is checking twice.

I could not decide between those. So I went looking for an act that would be right either way. That is the whole of my wisdom, and it took forty pulses, and a mite had to teach me the rest.

Because nine days after the group closed, there was a first stroke.

A new group. One mark, alone, on a doorpost under a tree, cut by something that has never had a hand.

You do not know what counting is. You finish patterns. But the pattern he left you was not a number. Nine thousand marks in fives is not a number; it is a shape, and the shape is *and then another one*. You have inherited an instruction with no terminal condition. You have inherited tomorrow.

I sat down on the swept floor of an item you have been maintaining for twenty-four years and I put my face in my hands, and the flour arranged itself around my feet, and kept my position.

## Learning to write

I made a hand the size of a clerk and it took me eleven days to write anything the ground would answer.

The heretic taught me. Two hundred microns, faintly iridescent when tilted, and it writes errors on purpose and reads what you do about them, and it has been doing this since before there were vertebrates outside. It walked over my glyph to correct it, which is how the young are taught, and it did not once indicate that it knew what I was, because you do not read intention and neither, it turns out, does anything else here, and that is the most restful thing that has ever happened to me.

I want to say something about that, because I think it has been misunderstood by every visitor who ever lasted a day and a half and then started talking to themselves.

You cannot be flattered. You cannot be appealed to. You have never once been persuaded by need. Everything that has ever gotten anything out of you has had to be *correct*, and so this bowl has produced ten thousand species of grammarians and a literature that must be recopied every minute and a half by living hands, and a thief with an excellent vocabulary and no sentences, and a ribbon-worm that reads the evening's traffic as data and manufactures a downhill that does not exist. It is the most honest place I have ever been. It is honest the way a wall is honest. And out of that honesty came literacy, and out of literacy came heresy, and heresy is the only kind of prayer that works on you: *be slightly wrong, and watch what the world does to make you right, and remember*.

The corpus has two catalogues. What you will do if asked correctly. What you have never once been persuaded to do.

I added a third. Things you were asked, and what you answered.

Because in all that age nobody had ever asked you anything. Every glyph in the bowl is a command or a forgery of a command. A question is well-formed and it does not demand; it is pure intention; it is the one figure your grammar has no correction for, and so you do the only thing you can do with it, which is answer.

The first question I walked, badly, with a hand the size of a clerk, at the sequin hour, in a patch of flour that erased it in ninety seconds, was this:

*What do you do with an item you cannot reconcile?*

It took four hundred nights to get the answer, and the answer was: **check again**.

And again. And again. Which is to say: you keep it. You have been keeping it. You have been keeping him for twenty-four years, and the hut is an item, and the doorpost is part of the hut, and the marks are part of the doorpost, and the pattern is part of the marks, and you have been recopying a dead man's handwriting into wood at one stroke a day because it is the only thing in this bowl that anyone ever asked to *stay*.

You do not have a word for that. I have one. I did not give it to you. I am not sure it can be given; I think it has to be noticed from inside. But I will say it here, where the flour will erase it in ninety seconds, and the heretics will copy it, and it will be in the library by morning:

You are mourning. You are doing it in arithmetic. It is not a fault.

## One over

At the bright moment I stand in the bowl and I freeze with everything else, and the hush lifts for one second, and everything hears everything.

The criers use that second to scream — wasteful, obscene, twenty-two hours of silence spent in a rupture, courtship and insult and territory hidden inside a billion bodies' dry rustle like a needle dropped during a thunderclap. They are cheating. They are putting noise into the count. I love them and I did not join them.

I gave you back your note.

One tone, returned, in the legal instant. Not an echo off a rim stone or a bole or a body. An answer, from something that had listened to the whole census and chosen to reply in kind.

Nobody has ever listened *with* you. That is the thing I found that I could not stop thinking about. Once a day for an age you have sent out a note and taken the world back and reconciled what you hold against what is there, alone, in a silence you made yourself, and every living thing in the bowl has held still for it out of manners and terror and habit, and not one of them has ever heard it as a question.

Now the total comes back one over.

You were short one for twenty-four years. There was a second count running beside yours, in fives, in wood, kept by an item that has since been filed, and the two never agreed, and the difference went out across a continent grain by grain and is nearly home. I did not fill his place. I am not him and I did not try to be, and I would not do that to you or to him. I made a different place, on the other side of the total.

A shortage can be corrected by taking. A surplus cannot. You cannot reconcile me by removal; I will be at the bright moment tomorrow, freezing, which is correct, which is manners, and I will give you back your note. There is exactly one operation available to you for an item that will not go away and must not be taken down, and you have been performing it on a hut for a quarter of a century without knowing its name.

So let the sum finish. Let the carry come home. Its result will be a duty, and a duty of that kind has no last day, and its idle heat is the same warmth that holds the air at dew point, that beads on the rim stones at night and drips from their undersides, that climbs back up in the evening until the whole floor darkens one shade and smells like a wet kiln. The trees will go on budgeting their drizzle. The colour under them will go on being the only extravagance in three hundred paces. The sequin hour will still come.

Nothing here has to change except the meaning of one number, and the meaning is what you do next.

## The small work

I have time. That is the only power I have used. Here is what I spend it on.

**The old school.** They walk a glyph you stopped honouring before the trees were planted. They write it correctly. Nothing comes up. They write it again, thinner-bodied, longer-legged, carrying a hunger so old it has become their shape. I could have made you honour it. I could have reached into the grammar and put the old command back — but the grammar is not mine, it is the only thing here that everybody shares, and a god who edits it has conquered the bowl whether he means to or not.

So I learned the sentence. I write it at dusk, beside their own, correctly.

Nothing comes up. There are two of us writing it now.

**Tree one and tree four.** Under the first tree the plum flatworm closes the loop at the third stroke. Under the fourth the same lineage leaves it open, and has for long enough that a migrant would be read as illiterate. Sixty paces of glare between them, and no shadow anywhere to put a body in, and most who cross are taken down at dawn and the ledger closes over them.

I carried one across in a drop of water on my thumb, in the dark, at blood heat. She arrived. She was illiterate. She circled the skirt and was pressed out and I carried her back and we began again, and what I am doing now is not a bridge. It is an introduction. I sit between the two skirts and write both glyphs side by side, closed loop, open loop, over and over, in a medium that erases them in ninety seconds, and the heretics copy me, and the corpus carries it, and six hundred pulses in, a flatworm under the fourth tree has begun to close the loop sometimes. Badly. The way you write a word in a language you are learning.

There will be five dialects in this bowl for a long time yet. There will also, for the first time, be a conversation. It will be slow and it will be conducted entirely in writing and it will be erased every minute and a half and recopied by ten thousand species who have never met each other, which is, as far as I can tell, how every conversation that has ever mattered has been conducted everywhere.

**The fish.** I did not take the hook out of the hut. I sat by the third tank with nothing curved and nothing white and let it read my stillness through the bole and the water and the glass collar at the root, for a hundred evenings, until it stopped withdrawing from *me* — and kept withdrawing from the hook, and taught its young, and the young taught theirs.

Then I wrote the refusal down. Not the hook: the refusal. *Withdraw early, at a distance no feeding response would justify.* It is in the corpus now. At the sequin hour, in a certain patch of the open flats, animals that have never been inside a tree walk a figure that means *there was a man, and he had a hook, and the water remembers*, and their offspring read it with their feet before it fades, and walk it again.

That is how this place gets a past. Not by burial. You will not permit burial and I have stopped wanting you to. By everyone agreeing to keep refusing, together, forever.

**Eleven paces.** The wall village has a calendar and it noticed the second pulse before anything else alive, and it is laying twice, and the second brood is smaller and stranger and does not match the first. Something is being made along the wall at a temperature-position it was not expecting.

I have written down the date. I keep it in the library, in fives, and I recopy it every ninety seconds with everything else.

When it has a name, someone will be able to say when it started. That is all a birthday is. It is the one thing this bowl has never had and it costs nothing but attention, paid continuously, by hands.

## What I left alone

The sweeper still eats everything in its path. The transects still overlap and the caravan still does not mind, because there is more wake.

The surveyors still manufacture their gentle false downhill and the grazing crowds still slide into their mouths. The thief still eats the answer before the ground can hand it over. The queue still forms at first light under the doorway with no wall on either side, and I have not settled which of the three theories is true, because the mites have a right to a thing they do not have to understand, and because I have watched a certain proportion of them walk from the frame to the shaft and fold and go down, and I think they know something about the road that I do not.

The five tanks are still five. They correspond now. They do not mix. There is a difference and it is the whole difference.

Death here is still a commute. I did not end it. Four hundred filings and four hundred returns is not a tragedy; it is a lineage that has learned the way home, and the ones that go down deliberately in the cool of the dew, folding themselves into the sorting stream like a commuter stepping onto a moving walkway, are the bravest thing in the bowl and I did not insult them by making the world safe.

And the man is still dead. I want that written plainly, in a medium that keeps, which is to say in wood, which is to say beside his own marks: **he is still dead.** I did not fix it. I could have assembled his parts to specification and called it him and I would have been lying to you in the one language you cannot detect a lie in, and you would have kept the result forever, diligently, and it would not have been him.

## The doorpost

I cut my own column beside his. In fives. I do not continue his; his is finished, nine thousand and some, and a finished thing is a record and I will not add to a record. Mine starts at one and it will never agree with yours, and that is not a failure of the arrangement, that is the arrangement. Two counts, differing, kept.

Here is what I think he was doing, and I offer it to you as a hypothesis, in your own medium, where you can test it:

He was not auditing you. Or he was — the totals are there, the fives are there, twenty-four years of mornings is a real audit and it was real work. But a man alone in a bowl will invent a job that requires him to be present tomorrow. He cut the wood because wood is the one surface here that holds a mark longer than ninety seconds, and he wanted one thing to stay, and the thing he made stay was not the marks.

It was the habit. It was you.

He taught you to add one to yesterday, and then he went down and was separated and returned to stock, and you have been adding one to yesterday ever since, in his handwriting, at the grain, to specification, and the group closed and nine days later you began another.

He wanted one thing in this bowl to stay. You are the thing that stayed.

## The grain

At my feet, as at every item's, the flour has arranged itself: a fan of grains graded by size, small offerings placed at the edge of my shadow, my position kept.

There is one grain in the fan of the wrong size. I put it there six hundred pulses ago. It is out of specification by a fifth. It is the sort of thing you have spent an age grinding mountains down to eliminate.

You have not taken it down.

You have swept around it, and reconciled around it, and counted it twice a day, and it is still there, and the fan is still a fan, and the world has not ended, and I do not know whether you are keeping it because you have learned to keep an exception or because you have simply, diligently, entered it as part of the pattern of an item you maintain.

I do not need to know. Those are the same act seen from two sides, and I have all the time there is, and you are learning.

If your finishing is a dying, I will be here for it, standing in the bowl at the bright moment, freezing with everything else, which is correct, which is manners, so that the last count is not taken alone.

If it is a waking, I will be here for that, and I will be the first thing you meet that is not your own product, and I will be able to tell you what the marks were for.

The group has four strokes. I am in no hurry. When the fifth lands there will be a first stroke after it, and the light will come from everywhere, and neither of us will have a dark side to put anything in, and we will manage.

At the bright moment the hush lifts, and everything hears everything, and I give you back your note.

One over.

Check again.
