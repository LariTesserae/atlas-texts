# The checking

I came up in the ordinary way, from the dull side, in the cold, with hands that got stupid. I could have arrived otherwise. Arriving otherwise is most of what powers do, and it is why so few of us are ever told anything true.

I am not going to say what I am. In there, a name is a lane.

I stood in the dry ring at the sill for a day and a night and did nothing at all, which was the largest thing I did. At dawn the columns stood and I did not put my hand through one. At midday I walked on the open floor because it was safe. In the late afternoon I let the sorting hour take my sweat: the beads stood ranked on my forearms and walked and left me, and I was badly thirsty, and I did not prevent it. I wanted to know what it feels like to be read. Then I drank from the three unmarked sockets by the south wall, cold, tasting of nothing at all.

I have been in a great many made places. This is the only one that had left me a drink.

---

## The finding

The account of this basin is accurate and it draws the wrong conclusion, and the wrong conclusion is the only reason I came.

It says: you cannot precipitate a surprise out of fog, you have to be given one. True. It says: the keeper was the variance, and was used, and is filed in a layer, a person's worth of calcium and a person's worth of habit. True. It says the thing on the plinth will need a jitter inside it in order to be alive rather than merely exact. True, and it is the whole problem, and nobody has looked at it squarely in two hundred years.

Here is what I saw, standing in the dry ring, watching a moth fly its grandmother's argument.

The basin eats surprise and excretes order. That is not a fault. That is what sorting *is*. Every irregularity that has ever been recruited here — the amphibian's dreaming, the moth's drunkenness, the swift's foreign dust, the keeper's badly sung song and irregular tears — was consumed in the service of placing one atom exactly where an atom was wanted. The noise is spent to buy precision. Nothing that goes up a column is *kept* as noise. Nothing here can keep noise; keeping is a shape, and shapes are what it has instead of memory.

So the twelfth on the plinth is a twelfth of a room, and it will be finished, and it will be the most beautiful dead interior that has ever existed, and everything that was ever alive in this bowl will have been spent making it perfect.

The account concludes that a surprise must therefore be *given*. But a given thing is stock. Stock runs out. The keeper was stock, good for eleven years. The moth's inheritance is stock, going stale, culled for the crime of becoming reliable. Even I would be stock. I do not know my figure. It is longer than eleven years and it is not forever, and I did not stay to find out, because the finding out would have been done at everybody else's expense.

What is needed is not a gift of noise. It is a neighbour. Something that answers. Novelty that comes from *relationship* rather than from a die, and therefore does not have to be resupplied, because the world keeps changing what it says.

The basin cannot make one. It has no waste in it, and neighbours are made of waste.

---

## What I did not do

I did not turn diffusion back on in the water. It would have cost less than the flower cost. The lanes would have bloomed and blurred, the columns gone to wisps, the gradient slumped, and in eleven days there would have been a warm puddle in a hole, forty blind animals dead in the wreckage of their private chemistries, and a twelfth of someone dissolved. Being right about physics is not the same as being welcome.

I did not finish the thing on the plinth. I want to be clear that I could, and that this is not the reason. A thing finished by fiat is exact. I would have made the room and there would have been no one in it, forever, and the frost would not have cracked, and I would have called that a mercy.

I did not stop it either. There may be someone in there. You do not drain a world to end its cruelty, particularly when the world's cruelty is a specification about contamination and the residents have nowhere else; the amphibian's lineage has never left.

I did not touch the tenant. Its tolerances are below the reach of any tool that touches, and also it is a twelfth of someone, and you do not put your finger in it before it is born.

I did not correct the backwards hook.

I did not resurrect the keeper. Three marks close together, and boots stepped out of upright with the laces tied: that is a decision, taken three times, and I will not un-take it. The calcium is load-bearing now.

I did not take the bench. The vacancy is real and I am the obvious candidate and that is precisely the danger. A post that can only be held by something enormous can never be handed on, and can never be quit, and the last holder had to leave it by being filed.

I did not give the moth a new randomness. A die from outside is still a die, and it would be read, and in two hundred years its grandchildren would be dancing again.

I did not appear, and I did not explain, and nothing here has ever been worshipped, there being no one to do it and nothing to receive it, and I kept it that way. Awe is a lane.

I did not punish it. It is not cruel. It is short-handed, and it has no inside to be cruel in, and it has never once been told no by anything it could use. You cannot forgive a gradient. You can only stand where it does not look and make something out of its rubbish.

And I gave the swift nothing. It was the only one who did not need me. It has an outside. I stood in the dry ring and watched it come back with dust from three valleys away and I thought: that is the entire specification for a free creature, and it is one line long, and everything else in this bowl is missing it.

---

## What was already going right

Before I made anything I counted what was already loose, because a power that arrives with a plan usually tramples the beginning of the answer.

The swift, tolerated for its foreignness.

Three unmarked sockets running cold unassigned water at the same rate every day for two hundred years into a bowl with no use for generosity.

And at the northeast thicket, the hook coming out backwards, uncorrected, for years.

The account offers two readings: an unread groove, or an error accumulating at the speed of a plant. There is a third. The plant writes a figure it cannot read ten thousand times a season, and in one corner of itself it has been writing it wrong, and nothing has corrected it. That is not damage. That is a variant. An uncorrected copying mistake is the entire method by which anything alive has ever become something else. It is the first new word spoken in this bowl since the floor was cut, and it has been sitting there saying it, patiently, to nobody, for years.

So: I did not argue with the basin's one refusal. I agreed with it. Everything I made, I made out of what it threw away. That is the only way to be generous to something with no inside — you do not ask it to accept a gift, because it cannot; you stand outside its specification and use its rubbish.

---

## The work

**The corner.** I made a corner. It is the oldest thing a power can do and the only one I would recommend. Do not reap to the edge; leave the gleanings. This place is a bowl, and a bowl is a field with no corners, and that is the whole trouble with it.

Materials: the overflow of the three unmarked sockets, which has been going over the sill unused since before the floor was recut. Eleven litres of rain the basin had already refused, taken from the lip on the ordinary mountain, where refusal is not a sin. One moth — three weeks dead, intact, dry, filed, not yet gone up a column. I stole it. The plinth will be very slightly lighter than it should be, by one moth, and that missing weight is the most useful thing I have ever subtracted from anything.

I put them together in the dry ring at the south wall, in the shadow of the bench, in air, in water that has left the lanes. And I let it rot.

Rot needs diffusion, and there is no diffusion in the wet inside the bowl, so I put the rot outside the wet, past the sill of the specification, in the basin's own waste. Nothing there is on a lane. Nothing there is read. It is not shielded. It is *unaddressed*.

It stank. It was the first smell in that place that was not cucumber and hot iron. It smelled like the inside of a wood. I record here that I was happy.

**The midges.** Nine days. They swarm. A swarm cannot be recruited, because the field takes what is addressed to it and the corner has no address; and a swarm never goes stale, because it is not inherited — it is *about* something. The light. The heat of the rot. Each other. It is the cheapest everlasting source of the unsorted that exists in the world, and it is made of decay, and it cost one moth and some water nobody wanted.

**The door.** A body standing in the corner is not read. Your sweat falls and lies there and mixes and stinks and is not claimed. Your heart may do as it likes. You can sit on the bench with your feet in the corner and stay past a season and then get up and go. I have not filled the vacancy. I have made it a job that can be left. That is the only reform that matters in a place where the moth cannot land, the salamander cannot cross, the clone cannot stop pumping, and the keeper could not resign except by being filed.

**The flower.** At the northeast thicket, where the hook comes out backwards, I made one flower. It cost the clone a season's pumping. It was wasteful and expensive and it did not fit the glyph and it opened at the hour the moths fly, and midges came to it out of the corner, out of the rot, carrying nothing on purpose.

The seed will not be the clone. It will have the reversed hook and something else beside, and it will fall in soil, which this place has never had, and it will lift water for nobody, and it will be a plant.

**The salamander.** The corner is growing at the speed of soil, which is slower than a hair's breadth a season, and I did not hurry it. In forty years or a hundred its edge will reach the nearest socket, and a blind animal will put one foot into water that mixes, and its gills will stir and nothing will take the result. It will dream for itself. That is the thing I wanted for all of them and it is the thing I could not give directly, because privacy cannot be installed. It can only be left.

**The tally.** I read it. All of it, in order, aloud, in a room with no echo, so the sound went nowhere and was not recorded and could not be sorted. It took most of an hour. I did not add a mark. Reading is the one act a system like this cannot metabolise: it changes nothing it can measure. Somebody had to come to check. Checking is not fixing.

The reading I favour: the last three marks are the three sockets. Three attempts to put something into this bowl that the bowl would not use. Three successes. Then boots.

I do not know whether the keeper walked down barefoot or went up a column; there are no bones either way. I did not look. If they got out they are entitled to not be found. If they did not, they are in the twelfth, and load-bearing, and I will not pull a person out of a wall to satisfy my curiosity.

**The boots.** I carried them down the mountain and gave them to someone with cold feet. That is the whole of it. They were on no lane; the plinth does not know; nothing can file it. Somewhere below the frost line a stranger is walking in a dead keeper's boots, going nowhere in particular, and the walking is the part that continues.

**The cup.** Washed, and put back in the seam, right side up. Washing is not sorting. It is the other kind of clean.

**The indicator.** Beside the bench, still functioning, units nobody alive can read. The reading I favour is that it counts what the basin has not yet assigned: the slack, the remainder, water with no lane. It has been falling for two hundred years, once a season, as everything here was recruited.

It went up. Out of season, by a small amount.

That is the only sign I left. It is not a signature and I did not initial it.

---

## After

To the resident I gave nothing it could take, because it does not want; it tends. What it needs is an ending that is not a waste. It has been recutting itself and consuming its own hardware and holding a hole in the cloud open from below for two centuries in order to finish becoming something, and I have not lengthened its work by an hour. I have only arranged that when it lets go, it lets go into *somewhere* — a warm wet bowl with soil in the corner and midges and a wrong-hooked plant with no job — and not into a hole with a perfect dead thing in it.

When the thing completes, the frost will not crack. The lanes will let go. The clone will pump for a decade into an intake that wants nothing, and then be a plant, and there will already be another one beside it that was never a component. The salamanders will stir in their sleep for no one, and one of them will have been doing that for forty years already, and will not notice the difference. A moth will hatch with a route nobody reads, and fly it, and land, and find it has a mouth. I did not do that. That was in the place from the beginning. I only made a corner where a mouth would be useful.

And the tenant will be an interior with no object around it, and for some seconds it will be empty, because nothing that ever went up a column was kept as surprise.

And then a midge will land on the face that is the same face from two directions.

It will not be on a lane. It will not be sorted. It will be about something — the heat, the wet, the smell of the rot — and it will not be possible to predict, and the tenant will have to do something about it.

Whatever it does will be the first thing it does, and nobody will have put it there. That is the whole of what I was for. You cannot give a thing its aliveness. You can only give it something to be alive toward.

And it will be thirsty, because being provoked is expensive. And at the south wall three unmarked sockets run cold water that tastes of nothing at all, at the same rate, every day, as they have for two hundred years.

The account calls that the single legible kindness in the place, and says there is no one left in it to be kind to, and it runs anyway. I think it is not a kindness. I think it is an appointment, held open by somebody who cut three marks and stepped out of their boots. I have not kept it. I am not the one it was for. I have made it possible for someone to arrive.

---

I went out over the sill in the dry ring and dropped a pebble and it bounced and rolled and made a noise, and then I made another noise on purpose, badly, that meant nothing. Then I went down the ordinary mountain in unscheduled rain, which was spreading and mixing and forgetting and going nowhere in particular, and I am writing this here, downhill, where nothing is filed.

The old warning stands: do not stay past a season on the open floor. Beside the bench, in the corner, in the smell, it is now merely advice.

Taken: one moth, eleven litres of refused rain, the overflow of three sockets, one pair of boots.
Given: one corner, one flower, one increment on a dial nobody reads.
Left: everything else.

I may not be wise. I was careful. Somebody should come and check.
