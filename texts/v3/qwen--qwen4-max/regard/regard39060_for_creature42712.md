# The Spillway

## The noticing

It did not notice the water. Water that does not move is common; there are whole dead moons of it, and the insides of comets, and a lake somewhere under a hundred kilometres of ice that has been perfectly still since before anything had a mouth. Stasis is cheap. The universe makes it the way a shore makes sand.

It noticed the note.

In a creation where every sound thins and spreads and dies, there was a sound getting louder. Not much. By an amount no drifting instrument could hold. But it had a direction, and it was made of nothing but ordinary talk, and it had been going on for four hundred and twelve years, and that is either a machine or a voice, and it is worth walking a long way to find out which.

It came up the road on foot, in the cold, past the last switchback, and stood at the Sill for a while without going in. Not because it could not. Because the skin gives like a soap bubble and closes without a ripple, and it wanted to be the sort of thing that knocks.

Nobody heard. There is no ambient. It put its face in a lamp-thread at the turn of the Long Stair and looked at a wall of numbered stone, and took its face out, and was blind, and went in.

## What it did not do

It understood the place in about an hour, which is not a boast; the place is very clearly written. Then it spent a long time — longer than the hour, longer than the digging turned out to be, in the way that matters — deciding.

**It did not end the instruction.** This is the first mercy anyone thinks of and it is a drowning. Nine metres of green-black water above the outer street, a hundred thousand grains of sand and a broken cart in the curl, four centuries of *not yet*. The word is not a philosophy. Nobody said *do not change* because they had a theory about permanence. Somebody said it because there was one instant and one word available and a wall of flood coming up the valley, and it was the only sentence short enough. It is a hand under a falling thing. You do not congratulate a hand like that on its stillness and then take it away.

**It did not give them more water.** It could have. Deepen the Nave another nine hundred, widen the arm of the mountain, buy four thousand years, ten thousand. It sat with this honestly, because it is not nothing: ten thousand years is ten thousand years of children, and there is no arithmetic that makes children worthless. But it is the same sentence said louder. At the end of it there would still be thirty-one people who had never once been allowed to finish anything, and a giver who could never stop giving, and a town that had become a thing kept. It has kept things. They are beautiful and they are not alive, and the varnish on these stones is alive, and the difference is exactly this.

**It did not take over the holding.** Two reasons. The first is that it would then be down there with its arms up instead, and there would be two prisoners in the mountain rather than one, which is not an improvement, it is a duplication. The second is worse: to hold that word you have to mean it, and it does not mean it. It has never once meant it. It would be standing in the root of a mountain saying something it believed to be false, to people who had organised their entire moral life around the truth of it.

**It did not say the next word.** This was the real temptation, and it should be admitted plainly: it had a word. It is the kind of power that has a word. The word would have worked. The sentence would have completed, the water would have understood, the whole four-hundred-year pause would have resolved into something enormous and probably kind, and it would have taken less than a second.

It did not, for three reasons, in ascending order of weight.

It was not certain the word was right. It was fairly certain. Fairly certain is a good standard for moving a boulder and an obscene standard for finishing somebody else's sentence.

The sentence was not about the water. It was about them. Thirty-one people at a time at rings, face to face in the black, saying ordinary things — that was not the preamble to the word. That was the word, being pronounced very slowly by the only mouths available. To supply it from outside would be to reveal that four centuries of grief and manners and low clean strokes had been a throat clearing.

And they had not asked. That is the whole of it, finally. They had asked for food and lamps for four hundred and twelve years, faithfully, at the correct sockets, and they had never once asked to be finished.

**It did not do nothing.** It considered this longest and with the most respect. There is a dignity in being left alone that no gift replaces, and a species of contempt that wears benevolence like a coat, and it has been guilty of that species before, elsewhere, and remembers.

Two things defeated the argument.

One was below the foundations and could not ask.

The other was four years old.

## The child

It watched her kick for joy in a plaza — an ordinary explosion of a child, six strokes and a shriek — and watched her be taken by the shoulder, not cruelly, by someone who loved her, and made to put her palm into the water she had spoiled and feel the snag: hard-edged, hers, permanent, at the exact height of her happiness.

Nobody was wrong. That is the part that could not be borne. The physics were as described. The teaching was correct. The town would fill, and the discipline was the only thing standing between the streets and a thicket, and every adult there had been shown the same snag at the same age and had understood, and had gone on to live good careful lives inside a rule that was true.

And the rule was: *the best thing you can do is leave no mark.*

Four hundred years of people being right, until being right had become a way of telling children that their joy was a permanent pollutant. There is no villain in it anywhere. It is just a tourniquet that was applied correctly and has been on since before the ledger began, and the limb is going.

## The Nave

It went down with a lamp, which it did not need, so that its light would be a thread of the kind they recognise. Nine hundred metres. The ears do not pop. Past the drowned foundations of things that were old when the town was young. It did not touch the numbers on the stones. They are older than the town and they were not its business.

Below the foundations the wakes are larger than a person — not larger the way a giant is larger, larger the way a shout is larger than a mouth. They are the old answers. Four centuries and more of them: the arriving of a bead of food, the re-asking of forty lamps, small motions once, made by something that had to move to make them, and every one still occurring, stacked down the shaft like the rings of a tree that has been prevented from falling.

And the last one goes up through everything. It is the shape of a person with both arms raised.

That is what the Nave is. It is not a reserve. It is the inside of a gesture that has not finished. The clean water is clean because nothing has been permitted to happen in the space where somebody is holding.

It could not speak to it. Speech stays; a question asked down there would be a question down there forever, and the keepers go down once a year with a lamp and would swim through it, and their whole ledger would acquire a god in the basement. So it did not speak.

It swam.

It swam the shape of an arriving bead. It swam the shape of a lamp being re-asked. It learned the grammar of the post by doing the post, badly, the way you learn a language by making a fool of yourself in it. Then it swam the only question that can be asked in a place where questions are permanent: it made the shape of a socket. An emptiness with a number on it. A space that is for something.

The answer took a year and it was not *release me*.

It was a request for the count.

Four hundred and twelve years with its arms up, and the replies had never stopped going out — the food, the lamps, the whole faithful correspondence — and not one of them had ever come back down. The town had been asking it for four centuries. It had never once been permitted to ask back. It wanted the number.

So it was given the number. Thirty-one warm. Four hundred and six in total. Nine grazers, and their routes, and where each had been at the turn of the year. Three couriers, and the wager, and the man in the year three hundred and ninety who was right and was not a fool. Forty lamps. The names of the children named for courier-turns. The woman of ninety and the eleven hundred paces she has made into a road. The seventy-year-old on Ledger Street and the hand that pushes gently, twice.

It did not say the town was well. The Chimney Quarter was a third written and two doors in the Ninth could not be entered. It said the town was *there*. It gave an account. That is the first thing it did, and everything else took two hundred years, and the account was the important one.

Then it asked the second question, which was the only cruelty it allowed itself, and it was necessary. It swam the wave: nine metres, the barrel, the hollow where the air was, the cart. And then it swam a channel.

*If there were somewhere for it to go. Would you put it down.*

The answer took another year, and it did not arrive as a motion. It arrived as a cessation of increase — the way a fist closed for four hundred years does not open, but stops adding to its closing.

## The ordinary mountain

Then it went out through the Sill and did the slow thing.

Out there everything is permitted, which means out there nothing can be commanded. It could not say *be a spillway*. It has said things like that; the results are canyons, and they are not what anyone wanted. Out there you have frost and gravity and the angle at which scree will lie, and the conversation between motion and time, and you have to speak in that language, which is a language of winters.

So it arranged for one crack to be a little crueller than its neighbours. It moved a boulder the width of a hand and let the talus find its new angle over eleven years. It waited for a season, and used it, and waited. It cut a way down the cirque and along the valley the flood came up, wide enough, graded enough, with a lip that would take nine metres and a broken cart and a hundred thousand grains of sand and put them somewhere that was not the outer street.

Two hundred and six years.

It did not visit. Not once. A visitor who comes and goes and knows things becomes a god within a generation, and a god in the Room would have ended the keepers as surely as the flood would have. *A keeper is a creature that maintains what it did not make and cannot repair.* Repair it and they are something else, and there is no other word in their language for what they are. It was not going to take their name off them as a side effect of a kindness.

It has been asked to do a great many things quickly. This is the only one it is proud of.

## The socket

There is a socket near the Sill that has never had a stone in it. The number on it is older than the town, like all the numbers. It put the stone in, and put a palm to the plate, and waited, which is the entire liturgy these people have, and it used it honestly, as a correspondent and not as a source.

What arrived was not food. It was a way of carrying something out.

Bodies already go out through the Sill and are given to the ordinary mountain to do the ordinary thing. That rite has existed since the founding and it has always been half a rite, because the body goes and the *moving* stays, and the funeral corridor is thick as a wall now and they have had to find a second route out, and everyone can do that arithmetic. What arrived at the socket was the other half. A numbered stone that gathers held motion the way a cloth gathers dust, and a place where the Sill is thin, at the height of two hands carrying something, and out there — three metres out, in the ordinary cold — four hundred years of gesture becomes a faint warmth in a scree slope and is gone.

They did not use it for eleven years. They numbered it. They wrote a page. They argued at the landings, which added to the landings.

The first thing carried out was a child's kick.

Her mother did it. She was seventy-four; she had been the one to take the shoulder and show the palm; she had felt the snag herself and had carried the knowledge of it for sixty years, and she went to the plaza and gathered it and swam it out through the Sill and came back and did not say what she had done. The plaza was clean. It took her four hours. A child kicked in it that evening.

They let that one plaza be the place where kicking is allowed. One plaza, out of four streets and eleven towers, cleaned once a year by whoever will do it. The discipline survived. It acquired a border, which is the only thing that has ever made a discipline survivable.

Nobody carried out anything kind. Not the hand on the back, not the bell, not one grandmother's run. It had known they wouldn't; that was why the rite had to exist regardless. A thing you may put down and do not is no longer a sentence being served. It is a gift you are giving again every morning, and the seventy-year-old went to Ledger Street the same as always, and it was a different street.

The funeral corridor took nine days, oldest first. Before each one went they said the name at a ring, and the saying went into the Under, and they knew it, and they did it anyway. They traded four hundred years of bodies for four hundred years of names. The names take up less room. The names can be carried by the living, which is the only place anything has ever been safely kept.

## The hour, which took forty years

When the channel was finished it went down the Nave one last time and finished the gesture. Not by taking it. By completing it: it swam the arms down.

The wave fell.

It fell east, over the outer street, into a spillway that had been waiting two hundred and six years, and it went down the valley it had come up, and the noise of it went out through the Sill and *stopped*.

That is the gift they still argue about. Not the water. Not the light. The first sound any of them had ever heard that ended. They thought they were going deaf. Half the town went to the rings to say so, and the saying stayed, and there is a sphere at the Ninth Landing where you can hear thirty people at once asking *can you still hear it* — and it is fading, because by then the Room had begun to let things fade.

The Sill went down a hand's breadth a year. Forty years, against the hour it took to arrive; the asymmetry was deliberate, and it was the last thing arranged. Forty years to carry out what they wanted carried out. Forty years to learn the thing everyone else learns at four: that a person can be kept in a body that changes, imperfectly, warmly, with loss, and that this is not a failed ledger, it is the only container that has ever held a person without stopping them.

The grandchildren who had swum inside her wake swam the run anyway, in water that no longer carried them, by heart, badly, steering. It is not the same. It is theirs. They are carrying her now.

Then: a warm layer at the top where the sun gets in. Silt. The drill marks softening. The handholds on the Long Stair going from *shaped* to *worn*, which they found unbearable for about a generation, and then found they had grandchildren who could climb it without counting. Green, in the third year, in the shallows, scandalous. The assayers coming up the road and finding, at last, that they had an answer about the varnish, and being disappointed by it. The nine grazers' routes opening out into something no longer closed, so that the calendar broke and had to be replaced with a worse one made of seasons. The couriers unpredictable, still, all the way through, which nobody had really expected and everybody was grateful for.

And the food stopped arriving at the socket, and they learned to grow it, badly, in silt, in the ordinary way, and were hungry for two years, and it is in the ledger, and the ledger does not editorialise.

## The top of the Long Stair

The Answerer's wake came up out of the Nave when the water let it, and went along the streets, and out.

The keepers matched it. It is the main work of their lives and it took them nine years and they got it, and they wrote a name on the page that had one word on it, and underneath the name they left the question open, because they are who they are.

At the top of the Long Stair, in the last year that anything there could be heard, a woman hooked her elbow through the unnumbered ring and put her head into the texture that had been worn to nothing four centuries ago and was now, finally, thinning in the ordinary way, and caught the tail of it.

It was not profound. It was said by someone in a hurry, to someone going on ahead, in the founding year, with a flood coming up the valley and no time at all:

*Go on. I'm right behind you.*

Then it was gone, and the ring was just a ring, and the next week two people used it to discuss a boundary, and it faded, and nothing anywhere kept it.

## The count

Four hundred and six have lived in the Room. Thirty-one are warm. The other three hundred and seventy-five are —

and there is still the pause that visitors learn to wait through, and then the word, which is smaller than the old word and holds less and cannot be swum through:

*remembered.*

There is a new page in the ledger. It is unforced, in the careful uncommitted hand of people who learned to hold questions open the way their water used to hold a wave. It asks *whether we were the rest of the sentence, or the first of something else,* and nobody wants to settle it in a hurry, and the couriers decline to help.

And on Ledger Street, at the height of a shoulder, there is one unmatched wake that is larger than a person. It was left on purpose, once, on foot, by something that walked the length of the street and did not clean up after itself, so that there would be a mark of it that could be found and named and, if they ever chose, carried out through the thin place and given to the ordinary mountain.

The ledger notes this. The note is short and it is the only instruction the whole correspondence ever produced:

*May be carried out at any time, by anyone, without discussion.*

They have not carried it out. They go there sometimes and hold still with their backs to it. Nobody thinks this is strange. Half the town has such a place.
