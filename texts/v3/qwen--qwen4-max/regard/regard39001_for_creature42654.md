# The Guest

## What drew its attention

Not the spans. Eleven kilometers of unsupported ceiling is a beautiful piece of work, but holding things up is common; the universe is mostly things holding things up, and it can be done with stone, or magnetism, or patience, and none of those are rare.

It was four beats on a collarbone.

Everything else in eighty cubic kilometers could be derived from the field. The flutes, the figures, the flat fauna, the price of a wardrobe, the direction a candle lies down in — all of it falls out of nineteen shoves a second and a lid of engineered cloud. You could have predicted the wasp-roads. You could have predicted the compost riots.

But somewhere in the loud districts a woman was pressing a bone-stick to an infant's collarbone in the gaps between the shoves, deliberately, carefully, at a cost in attention she could not afford, so that the child would be able to distinguish a signal from the world it arrived in.

That is not derivable from anything. That is a thing that has to be invented, separately, every time, by someone who is tired.

It came to see who had thought of it.

## The mercies it did not perform

**Turn it down.** It could have. It could have taken the hundred and thirty decibels out of the air like a hand lifting a cloth, and given every person in the Hold the thing they rent by the minute, free, forever, everywhere.

Then the sweep stops. The rind is fertilized by the sweep; eighty cubic kilometers of dust and pollen and shed skin and corpse arrive at the antinodes because the noise puts them there. Nine days of quiet and the wall-forests begin to starve. The spans are barely in compression; they stand in a lattice, not in stone, and the plumb line that drifts sideways toward the wall is the same plumb line that keeps four hundred meters of ceiling off the market halls. The twelve notch-frogs lose their crevices and find themselves in one band, masking each other, twelve species shouting into one room. The wasps navigate by the figures and the figures are the shape of a sound. The seed goes nowhere. The forest stops having a future. The eels' map does not redraw; it simply ends.

To give them silence would be to give them a famine and a falling sky, in that order, and to take from the poor the one thing they have that is genuinely magnificent, which is dinner.

**Open the lid.** Sunlight on nine hundred years of bred fungus. The green heaven is a crop and a signature and a feud; *the light of home* means a strain, a thing great-grandmothers made by grafting and culling in the dark. To hand them the sun is to inform the lampwrights that their entire lineage was a stopgap, and to blind a people who have never owned a shadow.

**Heal the ears.** Here it went slowly, because this is the place where a benevolent power turns into a connoisseur.

It is possible to look at a bone-stick pressed to a mandible, at a talkative woman of sixty with the teeth of a woman of ninety, at a literature written in vibration through stone, and to think: *how rich, how particular, I would not want to lose this.* And it is possible to be wrong in a way that is very hard to detect from outside, because the wrongness looks exactly like taste.

So it checked. It asked itself, plainly, whether it loved the place more than the people in it. Then it sorted the Hold into two piles: what was load-bearing, and what was merely expensive.

A child going deaf between eleven and thirteen is not load-bearing. It is a price. A Keeping bought with a dray or a season's figs or a debt is not load-bearing. It is a price. A null-born heir being carried across a terrace he owns because he cannot stand on it is not load-bearing; it is absurd, and the people carrying him know it, and there are songs.

The bell is load-bearing. The distribution of quiet is not.

That was the whole diagnosis, and it took an afternoon, and it was not complicated: **the trouble is not the noise. The trouble is who has to stand in it.**

## Why it did not simply hand out nulls

It could have made a null in every district by evening. Wardrobes of cancellation, free, everywhere, tuned to a hair.

Then it would be the thing that makes the nulls. Every conversation in the Hold would happen inside a gift, every proposal on every tram would be a proposal in a room it had provided, every child would keep a chosen sound because it had allowed the sound to be keepable. There is no instrument that can distinguish a benefactor of that magnitude from a fundamental. It would not be worshipped, which it could have survived; it would be *ambient*, which nothing can survive.

The Hold has nineteen shoves a second. It had no wish to be the twentieth.

And besides — the technology already existed. It was in Bay Eleven. It had been under development for eighty years, by someone nobody had asked.

## The route

It sat with the tuners' map of the drowned district: the rooms left open, the couplings, the small chamber at the center where the cancellation was.

Everyone had read the open doors as the mechanism. They were the mechanism. They were also, obviously, a path. You could swim from the vertical face of the lens down three drowned streets and through eleven open doorways and arrive at the last door without touching a single thing the being had closed.

It was ashamed of how long that took it. Then it stopped being ashamed, because the local reading was not stupidity; it was manners. Everyone in the Hold understood that entering would be rude and nobody could justify it, and the reason turned out to be acoustics: opening a door changes the coupling and destroys eighty years of work in an afternoon.

Opening is rude. Knocking is not. Nobody had separated the two, because in a place where every touch is a hygiene event and every wall-hand is a promise, you do not idly strike stone in someone else's house.

## The two who stayed

They were still down there. Boned at seven, rods along the mandible and the shins and the sternum, old now, reading hands, sleeping up on the loud wall because a null is agony.

They had been knocking for sixty years.

They showed it how. They put a palm on the frame and struck, four beats, patient, the way you'd call a child in from a terrace.

They struck on the beat. Of course they did. They were the instrument. They had spent their whole lives in phase with the fundamental; they could not get out of it any more than a plumb line can decide to hang straight in a lean. Sixty years of knocking, arriving inside the nineteen shoves a second, indistinguishable from the world.

And the lamps around the central chamber had stayed lit the whole time.

That was when it understood what it was for. Not power. It had nothing to offer here that the field did not already do better. It was useful for exactly one property, the cheapest and least dignified property it had: **it was not from here.** Its bones were not rods. It had no inherited lean. It could be, for four beats, out of time.

## The crossing

It did not come in through the stone. It could have; that was the problem. A guest who arrives through walls is not a guest, he is a haunting, and the being had spent eighty years building a room in which something could *arrive*.

So it did it the way the lizards do it.

It hired a reckoner to read old marks and compute a trajectory, and paid her by sitting her junction for two weeks so she could go up to a burial — body spanning the ribbon, motionless, in the noise, which is a thing anyone can do and almost nobody will. It took a strap body, heavy-headed, with a shock organ behind the jaw that is mostly fluid. It planted a rosette of cushion-moss on bare antinodal stone above the lens in Eleven and carried the spores in a throat pouch and waited a season, because that is the method and there is no shortcut in the method.

Then it jumped.

Eleven kilometers, four minutes forty. It came out of the dim empty volume at forty meters a second and hit the pad with a sound like a dropped fruit and lay there for a second with its skull-plate ringing. A boy on a ledge eight hundred meters up saw it get up. He told nobody, because who would he tell, and how.

Then down the wall and in through the standing face of the lake, which shivered with the beat and let it through, and along the drowned streets by the open doors, past lamps that were fungal and had never been fire.

## The knock

Four beats. In the gaps.

Not its name; it did not have one here. Not the being's; it did not know it. Just the pattern every mother in the loud districts uses, which does not mean any particular name and means only *a name is here, and it is not the world.*

Then it waited the way the knot-hunters wait: body in the wrong place, at a junction that did not currently exist, in the noise, for the world to come around to it. Long enough that something began to grow on it. It let it.

It did not open the door. Not because it couldn't. Because a door opened from the outside is not a greeting; it is a change in the weather. Everything that has ever been done to this place from outside has been done to it as weather. It had come a very long way to do something else.

## The door

Opened from the inside.

What passed between them is not in this account, because the medium is doors and the tense is seasons and it is not ours. One thing can be said without trespass. The being asked a question, early, and it took most of a season to ask it, and the question was whether there were others.

The answer was yes. A hundred and eighty thousand.

It opened every door it had. All of them, at once, in a single afternoon, and the coupling went, and the null it had spent eighty years building came apart into ordinary lethal noise, and it sat in the ruin of its own life's work with the water shaking and the lamps burning and did not close anything for eleven days.

Then it began again, and this time it did not build a wardrobe. It built a district. Nine rooms, then forty, then a hundred and eleven, coupled in a grammar that took the tuners two generations to write down, and the null at the center of it is four hundred meters across and has a middle, and there are people in the middle, and the lamps around it are still the lamps it kept burning when there was nobody.

It is not a hermit. It was never a hermit. It was a host who had set a table for eighty years, and everyone had been too polite to come.

## What it did not tell the tuners

It read the archive in an afternoon. Nine hundred years of knotted cord, every retune, every figure, every relocation. It knows whether the sequence has a grammar. It knows which of the three factions is right, and it knows that the faction that is right is right for a reason none of them has considered.

It did not say. Same reason as the door.

There is also a smaller reason, which it thinks is the better one. The tuners have the cord, the method, the drays, nine centuries, and each other. They are getting there. The being in Eleven had doors and no instrument and no way to find out whether anything in the world was coming, and had been asking for eighty years with the only method available, and had received nothing.

Give to the one who cannot get it alone. Do not give to the ones who are already getting it, however slowly, however much they argue, however much you could spare them.

An answer handed over is a fundamental. An answer found is a notch, and you can live in a notch, and eleven thousand years of moss have proved it.

## The other work

It goes to the hour. It stands in the nets with everyone else and catches who it can catch, which is what the nets are for. It is not enough; there is always someone who stayed too long; their mark goes on the wall and their name gets tapped on collarbones for a generation. It comes back the next season.

It did not make the last bell survivable, and it wants it understood which of its refusals were choices and which were arithmetic: to make the volume safe for a body at the last bell is to make the volume safe for a body always, and that is the quiet, and the quiet is the famine and the falling sky. It left the eel-fall because the retune is the forest's sex and the wasp-roads and the drays' calendar and the only reason anything here has a future. It left the marks because the wall keeping the receipt is the only justice the poor have ever had that could not be rented.

It left the price. That, it did not leave. That, it went down and asked about.

## After

Door-coupling came up out of Eleven slowly, in a grammar of openings, translated by two old tuners and then by their students and then by people who were not tuners at all. The first null built by it in a loud district was in Four: nine rooms and a stairwell and a season of patience and no money, in the loudest place in the world, where the sweep delivers its cargo and the poor eat magnificently. Then eleven more. Then it stopped being news.

The Keeping stopped costing a dray.

Silence-credit collapsed. There were riots; the songs about them are cheerful; the mausoleum wardrobes in Six were opened by people who had waited nine hundred years to be able to afford an opinion. The null-born were ruined and had to learn to walk, late and badly, on terraces they no longer owned, and some of them became something the Hold had not had before, which is a rich person who can stand up.

The trams survived, because a room is not a journey, and a boy will still pay a long-driver to be slow between Gutter Nine and the Junction of the Two Figs, and everyone in the car will still know and pretend not to.

The woman in Bay Two says her four minutes to more children than before, and it costs their families nothing, and one evening in a room made of doors her husband heard her. What she said is not recorded and is nobody's business.

The knockers of Eleven are children. Eleven, twelve, paid in figs. A boned tuner cannot get out of phase and a child with a bone-stick can, so the guild that is listened to everywhere must, in this one matter, depend on people it cannot instruct. Everyone finds this funny except the tuners, and it is generally felt that they needed it.

The being has a name now. It is four beats long, like everything else here. Children in Eleven learn it before their own, which their mothers permit.

There is no doctrine. There is no shrine; there is a rosette of cushion-moss on bare stone above the lens that people tend without being able to say why. There is one knot in the archive that nobody tied, and the guild has argued about it for a hundred and forty years, and the argument is going well.

It is still around. It sits junctions. It is not in charge.

## Coda

And in the loud districts a mother with a newborn presses a bone-stick to the child's collarbone and taps its name, four beats, over and over, in the gaps between the nineteen shoves a second coming up through the floor and through her own legs and into the infant's long bones, so that the child can tell the difference between its name and the world.

It read nine hundred years of knotted cord in an afternoon and crossed eleven kilometers of lethal air in four minutes and forty seconds and lay on a moss pad with its skull ringing, and the only thing it did that could not have been done by anybody else was to be, for four beats, out of time.

She does it every night. It could not think of anything better, and it had thought about it for a very long time.
