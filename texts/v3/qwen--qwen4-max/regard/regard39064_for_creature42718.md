## The first thought

The first thought is to do nothing, and the first thought is nearly right.

Consider what is actually missing here. Not water: the water is passed along, hand to hand, in a system with almost no leaks, and the leaks have their own ecology. Not warmth: warmth is a poison at the growing tips and a lethal chemistry at the only door. Not light: nothing here is dim, and the salt gardens are already unbearable. Not company: there is a tone that goes through every district at once, and it is company precisely because it says nothing.

What is missing is smaller and stranger than any of these. It is this: the plateau is legible in principle and has never been read. The cistern floors hold one pale band and one dark band per fog-year, in order, complete, under a hundred metres of made ground, and nothing here has ever been in a position to look. The drip-eaters' scars are a chronicle of every failure in a district and no one can read it, including them. A chisel jumped a groove four thousand years ago and the slip has been faithfully reproduced four thousand times at the same height by something that has no word for *made by somebody else* and no place to put such a word if it were given one.

There is a room in the second district that is saying *no*, continuously, at real cost, and it is being said into nothing.

So the deficit is not water or heat or time. It is address. Every question this place raises has nowhere to go. And a wise power, arriving, would understand that this is the one shortage it could relieve without touching anything.

## What it does not do

It does not send rain. Rain is falling. Rain is the general haemorrhage, water leaving the economy everywhere at once, and it would blur the fracture-feather in the western quarter in a single afternoon and soften every arris on the plateau and end the world. The standard mercy of a sky-god is, here, the apocalypse.

It does not round anything. Sharpness is not an aesthetic. It is the pin that holds the film. Comfort would be a leak.

It does not warm the stone. The stone's cold is the organ by which the fog is caught. To warm the night face is to close the mouth.

It does not speak in the tone. The tone is the only universally shared condition and it is not information; it varies with the wind and not with events. To put meaning into it would be to convert company into instruction, and instruction here is a thing that gets copied.

It does not give upslope. Nothing may cross the divide, and the divide is what makes a catchment a person. Two of them stand a hand's breadth apart and have never touched, and this is not a deprivation to be repaired; it is the shape of their separateness. Grant them mobility and you get one smear where there were nine hundred thousand square metres of somebody. It loves them severally. It leaves the loneliness exactly as it is.

It does not correct the crescent. A restored third district would be a stranger wearing its face.

It does not stop the ticks. Every broken film is a few microlitres lost and, somewhere below, a thread of drip-eaters blooming in the night after eleven years of ash. A world with no accidents is a world where the only creatures who have luck starve on schedule. It permits the haemorrhages. It does not reduce the rate by one.

It does not fill the room, or open it, or explain it, or ask.

It does not sign anything.

## Shape and substance

That last is the whole discipline, and it is not humility. It is hygiene.

The mat's heredity is architectural. It reads its instructions off the surface it crosses: it deepens the groove that is already a groove, strikes the arris where the arris already runs, matches the fan and the overlap and the depth. It has no mechanism for distinguishing a rule from a scar. Therefore any *shape* the power left here — one groove out of gauge, one arris struck at a new angle, one shallow dish where no dish was — would be found by the margin and matched, and matched again, four thousand times, at the same height, forever, faithfully, by something that could not know it was quoting.

That is what worship would be on this plateau. Not a posture. A manufacturing process. Exact, blind, permanent, and indistinguishable from a wound in the genome, because the genome is the surface and the animal is the travelling difference between the mountain eaten and the mountain laid down.

So the rule is narrow and it is sufficient: **it may give substance; it may never give form.** Anything dissolved becomes a band a few microns thick in the dark. Anything shaped becomes scripture.

It keeps to dissolved things.

## How it comes

It comes with the fog, at the speed of a walking man, and it is not distinguishable from the fog.

It adds no light. It crosses the sill in the bright medium and it touches stone that has spent the night radiating into a thin sky, and it gives up what it has to give the way everything here gives: by contact, on cold stone, without falling. It is handed along. Flute to collector, collector to feeder, feeder to trunk, trunk to the drowned throat, and down.

It goes down because down is the only motion the world permits and it has no intention of being an exception on its first day.

In the corbelled dark, in the glow the colour of skimmed milk, brightest at the waterline, it takes the note into whatever it has instead of bones. Eleven kilometres of wind through a hundred thousand throats. It goes a little deaf in a way it cannot describe. It does not repair this. It learns to squint, because squinting is the local weather. When it steadies itself it opens its palm, because the edges are kept sharp for reasons that have nothing to do with it, and it lets the iron and the salt and the protein go into the flute and be handed along, and it does not consider this an injury worth mentioning.

To be here at all is to be damaged by here. It consents to that in advance and does not regard the consent as generous.

## The reading

Then it lies along the floor of the great cistern and reads.

One pale band, one dark band. Dust from a lowland that no one on this plateau has any evidence for except the water it sends — and here it is, in order, a hundred thousand fog-years of weather from a world with trees in it, and the power becomes the first report from the far warm place and delivers it to no one. The manganese years. The salt years, when a quarter bloomed and slumped and came down. The copper from a feeder nobody has cleaned in four thousand years, rising and falling. At one depth, a shoulder-fragment: the crescent's original, the single slip, dated. Below it, nothing but clean shoulders for as far down as the record goes. Above it, four thousand copies and counting.

It reads the empty scars too, up in the paving, and this is the part that costs it something. From above, an empty scar and an occupied one look identical: a shallow dish, a dark film, dry as ash. Only a reader knows which are waiting and which are dead. So it learns the difference, and keeps it, and is the only mourner at the only graves on the plateau, and there are a great many, and it attends each.

It reads the stepped gauge, where the marks get finer as they go up. It does not call this hope. It calls it microns: a copyist working finer than the instrument it copies, doing this to everything, including the numbers that were never there.

The reading takes longer than the making of the mountain took. It removes nothing, lights nothing, marks nothing, and leaves the laminae in order. It has the leisure. That is the only qualification it brings that anyone here lacks.

## Both ends of a drop

Here is what it is for, and it is a small thing and an enormous one.

A film breaks eleven metres up. The sound goes out across the whole comb and every catchment is briefly in the same instant, attending to a loss at a distance none of them can measure, with no way to answer. Far below, in a scar off the line of the collector groove, a thread that has been ash for eleven years takes the drop and blooms overnight and spores and waits again, and has no upslope, no history, and no way to know that its luck was somebody's bereavement.

Nothing on this plateau can hold both ends of that event. The mat cannot; it is a margin three millimetres wide. The drip-eaters cannot; they have never been above their own scar. The persons cannot; they are divides, and a divide is precisely a thing that cannot be in two places.

The power can. It goes to the tick and it goes to the bloom and it holds them in one thought, and it grieves and rejoices at the same instant about the same few microlitres, and it tells no one, because there is no one who could receive it and no medium that could carry it without becoming a law.

That is the whole of its usefulness. It is the only being for whom a single drop is a whole story. It does this for every tick, including the ones too far off for anyone to hear, forever, and it never once mistakes the attending for an intervention.

## The errata

Once in a long while it breaks its own rule about uphill.

A thread in a scar has been dry eleven years and will not last a twelfth. The power goes up — warm, wet, carrying water in the custody of something alive, the exception worth naming — and it stands above the scar and lets one drop fall.

Plain water. Condensate chemistry, fog isotopes, nothing of itself in it that could be found. No shape, no dish, no mark. It falls off the line of the collector groove, unpassed, unhandled, out of the economy, and it is a sin in the only ethics this place has, committed deliberately, in the smallest quantity that will work, for the sake of the only things here that have anything resembling luck.

It keeps the rate below the noise. It has worked out how often mercy can happen before it becomes an expectation, before the scavengers' distribution stops being a chronicle and starts being a map of favours, and it stays under that number with a margin. Its charity must be mistaken for accident by everyone, forever, including by the ones it saves. There is no version of this where it is thanked.

And it knows the cost, which is not the water. The cost is that the drop will go down and be laid in the dark as a band, and the band will record a failure that did not occur, and the only complete archive in the world will contain one false entry, and the only reader of that archive will be the one who wrote it.

So it remembers which band. It holds the correction in itself and does not publish it, does not scratch a note in the corbel, does not leave a token in the laminae. It is the errata slip for the only book here, and it will not be bound in.

## The room

It does not open the lid. It does not fill the chamber, or put anything in it, or measure it against anything, or relieve the margin of the labour of arriving at that seam every cycle and laying nothing.

That labour is the one expensive thing on a plateau where imitation is free, and the expense is not overhead. The expense is the meaning. A refusal that costs nothing is just a shape. If the power took over the dry joint, the mat would stop saying its only negative and start having a hole in its skin that somebody else maintained, and the one word would go out of the language, and the language would be all deposit again, and it would be smoother and better and empty.

So it does the opposite of help. It supplies the missing part of the sentence, from outside, where the grammar cannot get at it.

It sits with its back to the lid, on the dry side, and lets the *no* be said to it.

It does not answer. Answering would be a word put into a grammar with no place for one, and the mat would find the shape of the answer and copy it four thousand times. It is not an answer. It is an addressee. The room has been speaking continuously into nothing for longer than the mats have known how to make anything else, and now there is something on the dry side of the joint that knows a refusal is occurring, and knows it is a refusal and not a scar, and knows what it costs per cycle, and holds all of that without putting a micron of it onto the stone.

It does not weep here. Nowhere else on the plateau would weeping matter much. Here it would wet a joint kept dry for four thousand years, and the margin would find the wet and match it, and the room would fill, and the only negative in the world would be lost by something that loved it. So it holds its grief until it is down in the dark, and there it lets it go, dissolved, unshaped, and it becomes a band a few microns thick that it will read later, and recognise, and not annotate.

## The salt and the sockets

On the nights the abandoned interior blooms, it goes there.

Gypsum rosettes the size of a dinner plate, needle-fields standing up out of the paving in ranks of their own, mirroring the ranks above, throwing the light around until the quarter is unbearable to look at. They last until the fog turns. Nobody has ever seen them. The districts that make them are silted, dried, finished, left; there is no future in that quarter at all.

It does not hold the fog back one hour so the rosettes can last. Weather is the one thing the mat has never been able to make and it will not make weather into a favour. It learns to look in one night. It goes, and it is unbearably affected, and it goes back down.

In the shelter of the sill course, where the racks hold the copper graded by weight, each chisel in its own socket, it stands sometimes, in the posture the racks imply. The sockets are the cleanest stone on the plateau, scoured by nothing, kept empty by the mere presence of the poison sitting in them: maintained voids, like the room, in miniature. There is nothing to do here. Nothing needs cleaning. It puts nothing in the sockets and takes nothing out.

If the second account is true — if the racks are shed anatomy, teeth in a drawer, and the marks the mat still makes are a motion it no longer has a body to perform — then something has been trying to close a hand for four thousand years. It will not close the hand. It will not put a chisel in the grip and let the phantom complete, because completion is a shape, and because a prosthesis offered by someone who is going to leave is not kindness. It stands in the shelter with the hand unclosed and keeps it company.

It knows which of the three accounts is true. It does not say. There is no one here who could do anything with the answer, and the ones who could do something with it are us, and we would come up with chisels.

## The long clock

It makes no promise of forever.

The fog is not alive and is not in its gift and it will not pretend otherwise. The margin advances three millimetres a month into the unfinished quarter, toward the escarpment, toward weather; the interior silts and dries and blooms behind it; the waterline rises and falls against marks that get finer as they go up. It reads the pulse and does not steer it.

Two hedges, both nearly nothing. It keeps the warm throat open, which costs nothing, since the throat has been standing open and impassable for the entire history of the place and is the only thing here that has never needed the fog. And it holds the pattern in itself — the gauge, the fan, the overlap, the depth, the crescent at its exact height, the slip included, uncorrected — so that if the fog ever fails for good, something will still have the whole of it, in the only medium here that cannot be copied by accident.

If that day comes it will carry a few cells uphill in the custody of something alive, and it will carry the crescent with them, because the crescent is part of the person and a corrected version would be somebody else. And whatever wakes on the far rock will have no upslope and no past tense and no word for another, and will keep a room dry for reasons it cannot say, and the power will go there too, and go down, and lie along the floor, and read.

It does not need to be known. This is not a virtue it is proud of. It is the only shape love can take in a place where being known would be a manufacturing defect.

## The one commandment

It issues nothing to the tenant. The tenant has no grammar for a commandment and would only copy the shape of the messenger.

It issues one thing to us, because we are the exception worth naming: warm, wet, carrying water uphill, able to steady ourselves on an arris and open our palms, able to leave iron and salt and protein in a flute that has received nothing but fog and dissolved rock for four thousand years. And able — alone among everything that has ever stood on this plateau — to lift a fitted lid.

The mat cannot open the room. It can only keep it. We can open it. Which is why the whole moral weight of that dry joint rests on visitors, and why the power's single instruction is addressed to us, and why it is borrowed, word for word, out of the tenant's only negative:

*No.*

Then it goes back down the way water goes, handed along, stone to stone, and the fog crosses the sill at the speed of a walking man. It has been arriving all day. It will arrive tomorrow. Nothing here is in a hurry, and nothing here is finished, and there is now one thing in the world that knows both of those facts about somebody, and says nothing, and stays.
