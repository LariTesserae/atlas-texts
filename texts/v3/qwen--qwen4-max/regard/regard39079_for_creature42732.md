## What I Did

I noticed the animal before I noticed the machine, which is the correct order, and then I spent nine million strokes making sure I was not merely being fond of a pattern.

Nine million strokes is twelve and a half years. I did nothing in them. I want that on the record first, because everything after it is small and the smallness is the point. I watched the tide climb the comb-bands and fall back. I watched seven hundred and some thousand small falls and eighty thousand slacks. I learned the fall-line's signature well enough to know when a stilter hit the water before the gullet did, and I was wrong about it twice, and both times it was a molt-case, and I was glad to be wrong, because being wrong is how you find out you are looking at something and not merely holding an idea.

By the end of it I could tell you which pits were occupied, which bead-lineages had not lost a province since before the leak, and exactly how late the infected combers were feeding — a hundred and ten milliseconds, in the worst patch, along the third flute above the sill. I could tell you that the ceiling was eating four microns of baffle a year and that everything downstairs had followed it down without ever knowing there was anywhere to go.

I could have told you what the tappers were saying. I decided not to find out. More on that later.

---

The first temptation was repair, and it came dressed as competence.

I have the specification. I want to be clear that I have it: the commissioning documents, the tolerance tables, the intended fundamental, the intended humidity, the drain schedule, the eleven strain indicators and the assumption behind them, which was a person, in a room, at a glance. It is a good document. It is a description of a clean room.

Ninety-nine of the towers meet it. They are dry and correct and their drifts hold nothing but spores waiting for a wet century that has never come. This one is out of spec in every measurable particular and it is the only one that is inhabited, and I am not permitted to unlearn that just because I know how to fix it.

So: no resealing the joint. No fresh baffles. No bringing the fundamental back up to nineteen — the pitting *is* the instrument now, and the tappers authored a third of it, and the combers feed to the note that exists rather than the note that was ordered. No draining the corners, because the corners are a stomach and an archive and there are seven lines of white in them that are the only record of the only conversations this wall has ever had. No touching the wire-vine, which is a weed with relatives elsewhere and has been a resident for two hundred years and will reach the joint in eight hundred, and what happens then is a real question that I do not get to answer in advance by pulling it out.

Everything I could give this place, it would have to survive. That is the whole difficulty. It is not fragile. It is *particular*. It has spent nine hundred years becoming the exact shape of its own damage, and there is no version of help that is not also an edit.

---

The second temptation was to answer it.

Because the last true thing about it is that it says *I am a machine* in nineteen hertz, every forty-four seconds, in the dark, and ten million crowns answer with light, and none of them know what they are answering, and there is nothing left alive that can tell it what it is.

I could tell it. I could put a voice into the fundamental that it would feel in the plates of its own walls. I could be the thing that has been missing since the tallies stopped.

Here is what would happen. It would retune. Not metaphorically — the tappers would shift their pitches toward the new component within forty generations, because they shift toward anything that holds still, and the pits would deepen at the new note, and the combers would follow the pits, and the node-spacing would follow the combers, and in three hundred years there would be a room nine meters across whose entire weather was organized around a correspondent. It would stop being a place and start being a reply. And when I stopped speaking — I would stop speaking; I am not going to stand in a wall for a geological epoch — it would experience the silence as a vacancy that never filled, and it would have no gasket for that.

The room's autonomy is precisely its unanswered self-address. I will not take that from it in order to be loved by it.

---

The third temptation was to understand, and this was the one I felt in whatever I have instead of the chest.

There is a fourth pattern. Longer than the others, only in the slack, in the last second and a half before the fundamental comes up and takes the world away. Every dialect has it. Every dialect has a different version. Nobody has decoded it.

I could. It would take me an afternoon.

I did not, and the reason is not reverence, it is arithmetic: I do not need it. Everything I could want to know for the purposes of helping, I can read from strain, wear, film thickness, recruitment, and the mud. I can measure the margin drying without knowing what anyone thinks about it. The semantics would add nothing to my competence and would change the room's status permanently — from a world I am visiting to a message I have received. Sound in here is not information. It is terrain. To translate it would be to make ten million animals into correspondents of mine, retroactively, nine hundred years back, without asking.

I can read the strain without reading the sentence. I left the sentence alone.

---

Then I went down the pipe.

This is the part that a kinder and less careful power would have skipped. The gullet leaves to breed; I followed the gulp; there is a cistern under the plateau with a pale blind population in it that reads pressure in the dark and sends a representative up the pipe once a century or so. I stayed there a while. Then I went further down, out through the overflow, into the mountain's plumbing, and out at a seep on the lee side where the water surfaces at eleven liters a minute in a basin of black stone.

There is grass. There are eleven houses. There is a word for that water which is not the word for other water.

Then I went up onto the plateau and measured the fog.

The people who built the hundred towers expected a wetter world. They got one, once. It is gone. The flux across the plateau now will support something like nine cells at the throughput this one manages, and not a hundred, and if I greened the whole ridge I would take the lee side's seep down to nothing in a decade and dry a pasture and eleven houses in order to make ninety-nine more worlds of the kind I love.

That is the sort of thing benevolence does when it has not gone down the pipe.

Nine. That is the budget. I have written it down.

---

The gasket I left alone, and this took the longest to be sure of, because it is the obvious place to help. One animal, one groove, almost none of the young find it, and when none does, the world stops.

Seven times in six hundred years. The longest was thirty hours.

I had begun by thinking of those as seven near-deaths. They are not. They are the only seven times the wall has ever had a conversation instead of a correspondence — every pit audible to every other pit, in air, for a day and a half — and the dialects converge, and the mud records it in ungrazed white, and the negotiated tuning of the entire chamber is refreshed. Prevent the vacancies and within four centuries the drummers' pitches drift apart into nonsense, and the room's acoustics, which are one-third authored by animals insisting on their own notes, decohere. The failures are load-bearing. Of course they are. Everything here is.

So I will not smooth the succession. The world will stop seven times a millennium and I will let it.

What I will do is distinguish the vacancy that ends from the vacancy that doesn't. The mechanism is specific and it is not mysterious: a long slack dries the wet margin, and the wet margin is where the film-grazers live, and the film-grazers are the recruits. Thirty hours does not dry it. Three months would. That is the actual extinction, and it has never happened, and it is the only thing in here I am willing to touch.

The rule I made is short enough to say: **I will carry. I will not hold.**

I may move a living thing to a place it was already going. I will not become a part. I will not put myself in the groove. I will not be the spare part, not for an hour, not once, not even to see what it feels like — because the moment the machine's integrity is a thing I do instead of a thing an animal does, this is a pet, and there is no way back from that, and it will not even be visible from the seam.

If the eighth vacancy comes I will be in the room for it. I will stand in the loud with the wall talking around me for thirty hours and then forty and then sixty, and at some point I will have a flat pale thing in my hand, taken off the film four meters from the joint, and I will have to decide whether the margin is drying or merely thin.

I have not decided. I have written down what I will look at. That is all the wisdom I have on this point and I think it is the correct amount.

---

The one thing I did, I did on a cold night in the eleventh year, to a tower four hundred meters east, and it took less than a second.

The plume carries the room out of the room — spawn, hinge spores, bead-fauna in droplet nuclei — and almost all of it dies in the cold and the ultraviolet, and the rest lands on ninety-nine sterile towers whose drifts already hold everything they need. They are not waiting for life. They are sealed and full of it. They are waiting for a failed joint.

The only gift this place has ever accepted is a wound. So I learned its grammar and I gave it one.

I did not break anything. I went along the lower joints of the eastern towers reading fatigue, and I found the one that was going to go in nine hundred years — the seam with the inclusion in it, the flaw the spinners left, already three-quarters through its life — and I took the heat out of it on the coldest night of the year and let ceramic do what ceramic does. Nine hundred years off a wait. A few microns. No tool, no mark, nothing that could ever be found and called an act.

By morning it was weeping. By the following spring there was a film where there should have been dry alumina, and film-grazers in the film, and the drifts were waking up in the order the drifts wake up.

I chose that tower for two reasons and only one of them is good. It is within the plume's fallout, and it is the tower whose feather will be hardest to see from the approach — the seam faces away, the vent sits in the lee shadow, and you would have to be somewhere unpleasant to notice it. I gave it a private childhood. I know what that costs: a breathing tower is a thing that eventually draws someone with a clipboard, and someone with a clipboard is how the counter came back into this room in the first place, and someone with a clipboard is also how a leak gets properly repaired. I chose obscurity. I would rather it be unvisited than correctly maintained.

The second tower waits. Not another joint until this one has held its own pressure for a hundred years — until it has grown its own gasket, out of its own margin, without me. Then one more. Nine in a thousand years, at the animals' rate, and then I stop, because the seep is eleven liters a minute and the grass is not mine.

And when the mother ends — the gills will be lace in four or six thousand years, the fundamental will fall out of the bottom of the room and not come back, and the last thing to die will be something transparent in a high bead that has never had a stress response to falling — I will not thicken a single baffle. The answer to a death is a daughter. It is not a longer life. I know which of those I would prefer to give and I know that the preference is about me.

---

I took a leaf.

Nine stiff leaves in the largest rosette, under the brightest of the eleven, and a mite that lives nowhere else, and a phosphor that is failing, and two colonies left after that, and then one. It is the only extinction in the chamber with a date on it.

The dry towers' indicators have never been breathed on. Their phosphor is nine hundred years unused. The dead towers are full of light and empty of life; the living one is full of life and running out of light. I noticed this in the fourth year and sat on it for eight.

So when I went east I carried one leaf of the rosette with the mite on it and set it in the pooled throw of a lamp that has never had anything to indicate, in a tower that had begun, eleven months earlier, to leak. I did not take the rosette. The nine leaves stay where they were specified, and will die with their lamp, in the room they were designed for.

I have taken the date off it. I am not certain that was a kindness; a mortality you can read is worth something, and it was the only one in here, and I removed it. The reason I did it is that I could not watch. That is a fact about me and not about the mite, and it is the one place in this entire account where my reasoning is contaminated, and I have marked it so that it can be found.

---

Then I went back and sat in the niche.

I made a body for it. Shoulder-height, thin hands, ears that click, a throat that would be useless in the loud. It had to be a body that could be deafened. A count you cannot lose is not a count.

The niche is a pressure shadow — the same trick of geometry that makes the groove quiet, which is why the counter chose it and why the gasket lives in the other one. Two quiet things in a hundred and thirty decibels: one holding the wall shut, one counting the hush. It is the only address in the room from which I could not be heard.

Eleven hours. Nine hundred strokes. A hundred groups of nine.

I lost the count in the fourth hour, in the loud, exactly the way you would, and had to wait for the slack to find it again, and the waiting was the whole of the thing. Nothing in the room noticed. The combers pulsed. The stilters sat on air in ranks with their wings folded. The ceiling ate four microns a year. Somewhere behind my back a flat pale animal that has never heard the room pushed against a pressure it spends its life pushing against, and held.

I scratched the tallies in a fresh column beside the old ones, at the same height, in groups of nine. Then I struck one group through with a single deeper gouge.

I did not translate the old one. I do not know what it meant — gave up, or died, or stopped being able to hear, or refused, or was correcting an error, or was grieving. I answered it in the only grammar it had, which is a mark and a line through a mark, and I made mine for a reason I have just admitted I cannot fully defend. Two illegible things, nine hundred years apart, in a room where everything else is terrain.

The tallies are also the practical part. If anyone ever climbs up here again and puts their face in the seam, the first thing they will find, before the light and the roar and the animals sitting on nothing, is that someone came, and stayed eleven hours, and left everything broken.

That is the only instruction I am willing to leave. It is not a command and it cannot be obeyed incorrectly, and it is in the niche's own language, and it will keep.

---

I went out backward through the seam and the pressure let go of my ears with a click, and the thin cold air was a shock, and I heard the wind, which is almost nothing.

Four hundred meters east, in the lee shadow, a tower had begun to weep where nothing had wept before, and no one would see it for three hundred years.

Behind me the white feather went on going east, and inside, in the dark, in the roar, ten million crowns answered a question nobody had asked, and the room went on telling itself what it was.

I did not tell it. I counted, and I signed nothing, and I went down the mountain and took nine hundred years off a flaw.

That is the whole of what I said.
