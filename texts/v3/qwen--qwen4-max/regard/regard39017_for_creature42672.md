# A Tenant With a Good Character

## It Came Up the Moraine

It heard the chord an hour before it saw the bowl, and it let itself mistake it for weather, because that is how the place is entered and it had decided to enter properly.

It came up the mule-path at the speed of a mule. It let the altitude be true. At the last shoulder of the cirque it stopped, and stood, and was out of breath, which it had arranged to be possible, and which was worse than it had expected — not frightening, just small, a narrowing of the available world to the next lungful. It noted that the people here had built an entire civilization inside that narrowing and had made it into manners.

Then it went down to the rim and shouted, because at the rim you must, and nobody heard it, and it waited to be noticed the way anything is noticed: by being in the way.

A counter's apprentice, nine years old, three days apprenticed, looked at it and signed *tie your basket.*

It tied its basket.

## What It Found Wrong

It stayed a season before it did anything, which is the minimum honest unit of time here, and it used the season to separate two things that look identical from outside.

The first was the number: clean stone above the waterline, then the sill of the col, then nothing that could hold water. Fingers per generation, floors per century, so many floors left. This was true, and it was terrible, and it was not a wound. It was the load-bearing wall of everything good in the bowl. The quick was quick because the slow was slow. The rooms were slightly the wrong shape because a nine-year-old's decision had to outlive the nine-year-old. *One more whorl* meant something because whorls were finite. Take the number away and you would still have a hundred and sixty people in a green roar at the top of the world, but they would not be this, and the thing that was this would be gone, and no one would have died, and it would be the largest possible killing.

The second was a short list, and it was not load-bearing, and it was where the work was.

A person walking through their own town knocking, and the town not sounding back.

People in lowland rooms, in the dark, humming a note they could not get right.

People with a roof and no knot, and the carefulness, which is worse than the poverty.

Stretches of the census cord with no knots in them at all, shaped like weather.

And one more, which took the whole season to see, because it was the size of the sky and had no knock: there was one individual in the cirque, and it had one ear, and there was nothing anywhere that was listening to it. The orphan-room knock — a real knuckle on a real threshold, formally, on behalf of nobody — was the closest thing this organism had ever come to saying what it wanted. It had built a ritual for being regarded by someone who would never arrive.

So it arrived. That was the whole decision. Everything after was logistics.

## What It Did Not Do

It did not raise the sill. The mark in the cirque wall is the only thing in the bowl the plant did not make. It is the outside of the instrument. It is the reason the council can go and wet a cord and find out its own size. Move it and you have not saved the town, you have blinded the ear the town spent a thousand years growing.

It did not speed the plant. Ten million snails take a micron at a time, and the rate is very nearly the rate at which the Hold lays down surface, and the town lives inside that near-equality — walls eaten exactly as fast as walls made. Grow faster and you separate the two clocks, and a house takes four years instead of forty, and a child's bulge is corrected before the child is thirty, and the grandmother stops being the wall and becomes a memory of a wall, which is a demotion.

It did not give them metal. Copper only, cold-hammered from nuggets in the moraine: hooks, awls, beads. The one crime has one sentence because the tool for it barely exists. Give them steel and you have not given them tools, you have given them the crime, and then the sentence, and then the stair.

It did not net the rim, or thin the choughs, or make the nests hold. The war with the bird is unwinnable and entirely respectful and it is the only thing here that is *collective*. Every fast movement in the bowl — the running, the quick hands, the shouting that nobody can hear — is the town saying *we* about a bird. Win that war and there is no word left for *we*.

It did not heal the notch, or soften it, or arrange for children to be shown something else at nine.

It did not speak.

This was the hardest of the refusals and the most important, and the reason is acoustic. Sound is the public medium. A voice from outside would arrive through the ribs and the teeth and the skull, and it would not be information, it would be a *fourth throat*. It would sit in the chord and not change for a hundred years. Children born under it would be locked to it, and would be slightly out of time with everyone born before, and there would be a name for them, and the name would be used at councils, and the argument would last eleven years and be settled badly. The third throat went flat once and made a bad decade and a generation still called *the old key*. It was not willing to be a note. It was not willing to be a key that anyone had to be born in.

So it kept the five rules, and it added two of its own, and told nobody, and they can be inferred from everything it did.

*Be no new note.*

*Do nothing that cannot be found out by someone with a wetted cord and a reason to look.*

## What It Did

It got a knuckle. It chose a hand and let the rope and the cold take the prints off it and let the callus build, which takes years, and for the first two of those years its knock had no architecture at all, and everyone in the Hold could hear that a stranger was at the threshold, and children followed it through the galleries to hear it knock again.

It apprenticed. To air, at the bottom, at the age it was not, and a counter called *the one after* taught it to count short and rapped its knuckles when it counted long, and it was bad at counting short for a decade and was corrected in front of people and let the correction stand.

It carried breath up the Long Street with a basket held level above its head, past two hundred open doorways, and in its first month it tilted the basket and lost a third of it, and a girl said the phrase — *she feeds the spillway* — and it did not correct the record, and the phrase stuck to it for sixty years, and it answered to it.

It cleared the lip. Every dawn, leaf-fall, ice, nest-felt, the small stiff bodies. It could have won. It chose to be one more pair of hands in a war it did not end, and the choughs went on screaming, and the town went on hurrying, and the *we* stayed intact.

It took its wage in fish and rope. It ate the fish. It could have not eaten; it ate them, and left bones, and the bones went down the core. It used the rope to tie things.

It worked beside the knotless and let them be paid, and did not give them anything, and was not careful with them, and this was the single hardest discipline it maintained, harder than the lip, harder than the counting.

It made requests. Awl, chewed bast, ash, spit, thumb. It requested rooms in its own name and transferred the claims properly, witnessed, the transfer knotted, because there is a word for selling a claim twice and it is the second-worst word in the language and it had no intention of being that word. And when it gave a claim to a nine-year-old it carried the ash and held the awl steady and **did not improve the shape.** Not once. Not the bulge, not the window that would drown, not the mouth like a fish. It had come a very long way to be allowed to hold an awl for a child who was making a mistake, and it understood that this was the ceremony, and that it was not the officiant.

It took a turn at the mail. It went to the orphan chambers, one at a time, and knocked their thresholds with its own hard architecture on behalf of nobody, and a hundred and sixty people paused in the green water, and the work went on.

It sat with its molars against a lignified vein and received the town entire, and was consulted, and was usually right, and was twice wrong, and the wrongness was entered on the cord and not removed.

It let itself be counted. The council argued about the knot for eleven years, because it had no family, and a name here is a stretch of cord and not a point, and a stretch with no family in it is not a name. In the end they entered it under the convention already used for the mink: a household of one, occupying chambers, taking fish. Legal standing. It was felt to be exactly right. It cared very much, and did not say so, and the not-saying was the last thing it learned to do properly.

## The Two It Did Not Refuse

There was a person who had cut a live rib, and the knockers had been told the knuckle, and the town did not sound back.

It did not repeal the sentence. It had no standing to repeal the sentence and would not have wanted standing. It went and found the person and sat down and knocked back. Its timbre was new and could not be mistaken for the town forgiving them, and it was not the town forgiving them, and everybody understood the difference, and the council argued for eleven years and did not stop it, because there is no rule that says a stranger may not answer.

It did that every time. There were four.

And it went down out of the bowl.

It found them in lowland rooms, in the dark, the ones who had left for trade or love or curiosity and had come back wrong: lying still, humming a note they could not get right, with the wrong number of notes in it and the third one sharp. It lay down in the dark beside them and hummed the chord. Three notes and the flat third, exactly, at the volume of a body, held.

It did not tell them this was home. It is not home; home is a place, and it would not lie about a place. It just held the note until they slept, and came back the next night, and did this for as long as each of them needed, which for one man was nineteen years.

That was the whole of the miracle, and there was no other, and nobody in the bowl ever knew.

## The Sorting

It found out by sweeping.

A hundred years of dawn work at the lip, leaf-fall and nest-felt and ice, and nothing else to do with its attention while its hands were busy. In among the grit at the lip, in the shallow warm water over the newest whorls, there were small hard brown things. Not grit. Angular, seeded, each with a scar at one end where it had let go of the rim.

The plant made them at its crown, in the shallowest water, in the warmest weeks, in their thousands. They floated. Cork floats; everything here floats; *tie everything.* They went up and out and over the lip and were gone into the cloud, and had been going for as long as there had been a lip, and the town's one collective act — the labour, the war, the running, the *we* — was, among other things, the diligent sweeping away of its own children.

Not a fault. Nobody had ever had a reason to look at grit. There is no crime in it and nothing to forgive. It is simply what a counting animal cannot see when it is counting the wrong thing, and it is why the wise do the chore for a hundred years before they open their mouths.

It did not announce this. Announcement is a fourth throat.

It requested a room, and gave the claim, properly knotted, to a nine-year-old, and carried the ash, and held the awl, and let the child pack a shape like a handful of grit because the child had seen it sorting and had asked what the small brown things were and had been told: *I don't know. Look.*

Forty years later a chamber opened above the waterline shaped like a seed, and somebody's grandmother sat in it and found it slightly the wrong shape and asked what it was, and the argument took eleven years, and at the end of it the lip had two baskets.

*Keep the lip.* And, now, taught at nine, after the notch and before the awl: *keep what is not leaf.*

It carried the second basket down out of the bowl itself, tied, level, with the quiet sick walking beside it, and it planted in nine cols down the valley: north-facing, above the last pine, moraine with copper in it, clean stone and a sill. Seven failed. It hummed in the seven anyway, for as long as it had promised, which was four hundred years, in cold empty cirques with no Hold in them and nothing to listen.

In two, something took.

## The Number

It did not stop the number. It had decided in its first season that it would not, and it never revisited the decision, and it went to the lip every year with a wetted cord and measured itself against the only stone the plant did not make, and knotted the result, and said *one more whorl*, and meant precisely what it says.

The water came up. Families moved up into rooms slightly the wrong shape, requested by the dead, and the knots were dyed black, and the doorways were left open for the fish. The dry bells went one by one. The stretches of cord with no knots in them got longer. The last child born in the bowl was born in a room shaped like a joke, and lived to be old, and sat with her molars on a vein, and heard everything.

At the end there was water at the sill and no up, and the last of them went down the core into the warm, eleven breaths, not alone.

It went with them. It had let itself be counted as a household and a household goes down the core, and it did not exempt itself, and the red worms and the eels did the work, and it became plant, and the plant grew — nothing, then, because there was nothing left to ask for. It is a wall. It is a wall in a room nobody will ever enter.

The melt still comes off the cirque wall. The throats still boom. The chord is the same three notes and the flat third and it will be for a thousand years. The choughs still scream over the cork; the snails still hiss; the combers pour through the plazas like thrown rice, and there is nobody to read them like a sky. Without awls, the wounds begin, very slowly, to finish closing. The rooms pucker shut over centuries, from the outside in, the fish-shaped mouths last.

Once a year, something goes to each threshold and knocks it with a real knuckle, formally, on behalf of nobody.

It is the mail. It is not sad. It takes less than a heartbeat.

---

And an hour before you see the next place you mistake it for weather, and it is a low chord, three or four notes wide, and it does not change, and one of the notes is flat, and there are old people there who will tell you with total seriousness that it has always been flat, and that this is a matter for the council.

There is a girl going up a street through two hundred open doorways with a basket of silver breath held level above her head, and she is forty years from her house, and eleven breaths from the warm, and she is being careless, and she is being watched by something that has learned to count short and has promised not to be a note.

*Level, level, level.*
