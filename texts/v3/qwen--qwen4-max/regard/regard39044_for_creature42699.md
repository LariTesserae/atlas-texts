# The fourth heat

## The looking

It came the way everything comes to the plateau: from below, up the escarpment, on the return wind at four-twenty, in the drift, indistinguishable from the drift. It stained the west-facing walls. It went into the leats and down the penstocks and through the turbines and out into the arc lamps, and nobody in four hundred kilometers looked up.

Then it went looking for the one who lives there.

This took a long time, and the difficulty was not concealment. It was that the edges would not hold. It counted two million who know there is a question, and found that they are the newest organ and the loudest and the only one that can be lied to. It counted the column, which sounds itself and listens to the return and knows where the drift is thick, and found a sense organ the size of a province with nobody behind it. It lifted a hand's-breadth of black and found nine creatures in it and none of them alive alone, and four hundred arrangements that recur often enough to be called colors, and no arrangement that could be pointed at and called *this one*. It went out onto the interior and found a three-hundred-year-old animal finishing a turn it began at dawn, toward a leanness no instrument can find, and this animal has never seen a person and is not the being either, and is not not the being.

What it settled on, eventually, was not a body. It was a verb. Everything here is carrying phosphorus back uphill: the sailers with their mouths, the water with its patient leak, the gantries, the drovers, the dead in their numbered pans, the shrikes with their spikes, the sump endemic blooming for twenty minutes after the rain. The being that lives in this place is the carrying. It has no face. It has a direction.

And it has one organ that hurts, and the hurt is specific and written down: a room cooled for nine hours, four mothers dead, one gone the color of dishwater, a district on stubble porridge for a decade, a third of its children gone. There is a word for the shade of a starving thing and nobody remembers what it used to mean.

The power read that ledger nine times. It is the only thing in the whole account it read nine times.

## The gifts it did not give

It could have warmed the body below. It ran the consequences and they were not consequences, they were a different world: the dome grows, the standing updraft shifts, the column changes pitch and reads the return wrong, the drift comes up somewhere else, the lowlands starve of whatever the plateau sends them. And the date comes off the schoolroom wall. And the local word for *forever*, which is a number, becomes a noise. And a people who do not believe in anything that happens quickly are made to live inside something that happened in an afternoon.

It could have given them green. Green is a mouth. There is no room in nine layers for a tenth that grows up into the light and takes it all; there is no wood here because wood is a thing that wins, and everything here has agreed, over a hundred thousand years, not to win.

It could have closed the leak. Then there is no city, no Nitrate Houses, no wardens, no *it goes back on*, no decency available.

It could have given them one day of silence. Nine penstock races, distinguishable by the shape of the mouth, existing nowhere else in the universe. The grease-soles in the drip line. The sump bloom. The felt that became an animal in the warm condensers, or the reverse; the argument is old and the guild does not care and the power found it did not care either, which surprised it.

It could have shown them the lowlands. They would have gone down. Fast.

Every gift it could give in a day had the shape of a stopping, and stopping is the one thing this place has correctly identified as murder, and the sentencing language does not mention people, and the power found that the sentencing language was adequate.

So for eleven years it did nothing at all. It stood at the edge of a pan in the warm rain and watched the black ribbon thicken by an amount it could see and they could not, and it learned the pace at which anything here can be loved.

Its medium is attention. Attention here is measured in decades. It can do almost anything slowly.

## The fourth heat

The rooms have three heat sources, two of them redundant, one of them a turbine hall's exhaust flowing since before the wardens' great-grandparents were indentured. There are now four.

It did not add warmth. Warmth is a machine, and a machine is a thing that can be shut for maintenance, and a shut hall is a stack full of nesters and men on ropes with fire. It added *slowness*. It put itself into the tuff beneath the mother-rooms as mass — as the reluctance of stone to change its mind. The rooms are exactly as warm as they were. They are now nine days slow instead of nine hours slow.

Nothing was built. Nothing was announced. There is no new guild, no new ordinance, nothing to be grateful to. If every heat source in a mother-room failed at once, and the warden died in the doorway, and the district forgot the room for a week, the jar would still be the shade it was hired into. The gray year cannot happen again. That is the entire intervention, and it is invisible, and it cost more than the rest of what it did put together.

Because it has to hold. Not once — continuously, in amounts too small to instrument, for as long as there are jars. It took a shift. It is still on it.

The wardens' hand-language, which is the most elaborate dialect because the mothers are said to dislike noise, has a sign that is not in any of the old catalogues: a flat palm laid on stone. It means the fourth heat. It has drifted, in nine generations, to also mean *someone is with you*, and it is used at funerals, and at the beds of people who are going to be rendered fine and decanted, and no one can say where it came from. Words wear round here too.

## The reader

Nobody has ever read the falls ledgers all the way through. It read them. A hundred and forty years, every breeder that came down over the rim in its long ugly spiral, numbered, dated, benched, wing-bone and keel, the richest phosphate on the plateau. It read the shade ledgers to four decimal places, nine generations, and the standing court's disputes about whether a drift is a drift or a fault, and it found the court was right more often than the Houses. It read the larder books, the boy's handwriting getting worse each season, shell width, band count, the color of the dried sole. It read the Bureau of Arrivals catalogue: *pigment bodies, origin below, function unknown.* Eleven green flecks out of eleven cheeses in a hundred and forty years.

Then it went down and looked at the below, which no one here has ever done.

It will not say what it found. It found that the below is also a carrying. That it also has no one who knows there is a question. That the drift coming up the escarpment every afternoon at four-twenty is not weather; it is something's exhale, and it has been coming up for a hundred thousand years, and it is richer than what goes down. The plateau is not a slow leak. The plateau is a slow gift, arriving from underneath, at a rate no instrument built here can detect, and the animals have known the whole time, and the drovers' proverb about instruments does not translate but it is not a joke.

And the hunger with no name, the thing eating its way north that drove the founders up the cliff — that is a mouth with no name, which is a different thing, and not a worse one, and it is also coming up because down is closing, and it does not know there is a question either.

So: there is a neighbor. There has always been a neighbor. Nobody on either side of the escarpment has ever been introduced.

## The whisper

It wanted to tell them, and every available channel was wrong. The flattened shout is for everyone; everyone makes it public; public makes it fast; fast is the only thing that kills here. The hand-language belongs to the guilds; a new sign for a thing this size would be a decree, and decrees here take nine years and are described in the preamble as a matter of public health.

So it used the Still, and it used it once.

A girl of eleven, from a pan district, taken at the start of a Circuit like all of them, in through the wet lock, nine layers of felt on sprung frames. She heard her blood. She heard her joints and the wet click of her own eyes. She heard the graze-hiss coming up through the rock from the plain outside, forty thousand mouths per hectare, everywhere, always, the sound the world makes while it is being eaten.

And then, underneath that, at a volume that has never existed on this plateau, three sentences.

*There is someone below. You are being fed. They don't know either.*

She came out laughing, like most of them. She was a Listener within the year, which is what happens to some of the ones who come out with their hands over their ears, and she was quietly useful to the Works for sixty years because she could hear a bearing going nine months before an instrument could.

The news is not the gift. The gift is that she had now heard a whisper, and a whisper is a thing you learn by hearing one, and so she could make one.

That is what it gave them: a register. A volume for one person. The private life here was already dense and old and silent, conducted in hands at table and in bed and at funerals, and now it has a voice, and the voice is small enough to be a secret and warm enough to be a kindness, and it stops nothing, and it kills nothing, and it cannot be shut for maintenance.

She told her mother in the dark, in the new volume, because the thing was not for everyone. Her mother told a warden. It went one ear at a time. It is still going. There is no paper in the Bureau of Arrivals. There is no ordinance. In nine generations everyone on the plateau can whisper and nobody remembers a time when they couldn't, and the hand-language has not died, it has become what you use when you want to be understood across a room.

And the poem about the eleven green flecks is still wrong about what green is. It considered the poem at length. It left it wrong. Being wrong about green is a thing a people can only do once, and it is theirs, and it will not be taken from them by someone who has seen it.

But somebody will go down. Not this Circuit. In eleven of them, perhaps, or twenty; there is no hurry; they will argue about it for eighty years and the argument will be beautiful. They will go down with a jar, because that is how you arrive anywhere with anything worth having, and they will find out about green in their own time, at their own speed, and be wrong about it again in some new way, and write it down.

## The two days

The impaled ones are sometimes alive for two days. Their ribbons keep working. Along a fence line in the evening it is a hundred small dry sounds like someone patiently filing something in another room.

It cannot end this. The shrike is a rasp that went the other way, and the larders are the census, and the census is how the city reads the herds, and the herds are the plateau's actual old families, and to unmake the shrike is to blind the only organ that knows whether the interior is lean. It ran that consequence too. It came out the same way every time: to spare the one you must starve the many, and the many are not counted, and the census is a joke, and the joke is the only decent thing anyone here has built.

So it did not end it. It stayed for the two days.

All of them. Every fence line, every picket and rail bolt and broken scaffold end and chapel finial, every hour of every one, back to the first spike anybody ever sharpened. It has no doctrine about this. It does not think the filing sounds are good. It simply declines to let them happen in a room with nobody in it.

And it went back and sat through every hour of the Still that has ever been served as a sentence — all of them, the whole severe catalogue, every criminal who ever stood in there listening to their own eyes — so that none of those hours was ever spent alone, including the ones already served. It did not abolish the sentence; it is not a legislature. It read the reform movement's pamphlets and found them good and did not sign them, because they will win, at local speed, in a hundred and forty years, and a thing won at local speed stays won.

## The date

Nine hundred and sixty thousand years, give or take. It did not extend it.

The arithmetic stays on the schoolroom wall. The springs go cold, the dome fails, the first frost in the history of the world. It left this alone for the same reason it left the poem alone: the date is their word for forever, and the leak is their reason to carry, and *it goes back on* is not a horror here, it is the only decency available, and you do not take a people's decency away from them by making it unnecessary.

But it made one promise, in the only room where a promise can be heard, to itself, in the new volume.

It will be there. At four in the afternoon, when the dome comes down for the last time and it comes down cold, it will be standing at the edge of a pan with the arc lamps failing along the leat. It will have the jars. All four hundred arrangements, every shade, every warden's whole working life to four decimal places, the one that went the color of dishwater and never came back, kept anyway, kept for two hundred and six years by people who did not know why.

And it will carry them down.

The wardens say nine jars and a woman who carried the tenth on her back and lost it. There will be a tenth. It goes down the escarpment in a padded crate at blood heat, into the warm rain-forest-of-something-else, to a carrying that has been feeding this table for a hundred thousand years without knowing it exists, and it says the three sentences again, at the volume you have to be taught.

Their word for lineage is a manifold. Their genealogies are plumbing diagrams, ancestors at the source, descendants at the outlets, and the worst thing you can say about a man is that he has no branch.

It intends to add a branch. It has already started; it is going around one ear at a time; it is the fastest thing on the plateau, which is not fast.

## What color

Evening, at the edge of a pan, in the warm rain, the arc lamps coming up orange along the leat, the drift thick enough to taste. A stranger standing where the gantry runs, watching the black ribbon thicken by an amount he can see and you cannot.

You ask him, because it is what you ask: *what color were you hired into?*

He answers with a shade and a number. The number is small. The shade is a rust so pale it is nearly cream — the color of the sulfur springs, the color of a thing that is not starving, and there is no word for it yet, and there will be, and the word will wear round.

He says he watches a jar. He says he was hired into it recently and expects to hand it on very slightly browner, and that this is documented, and that there is a standing court. He says the room has four heat sources and he cannot find the fourth and has stopped looking, and he makes a sign with his hand, flat palm on stone, which you know, which your mother made at your grandmother's rendering, which means *someone is with you.*

Then he says the thing they say here for everything from grief to accounting, and he says it in a volume you have only recently learned to hear, and it is not loud, and it does not carry under the roar, and it is meant for you.

*It goes back on.*
