## The Benefactor

It noticed the note first. Not the light — nothing radiates from that plateau, nothing leaks, the slot admits and does not emit. What it noticed was a pitch being held somewhere in the rock under a place with nothing on it worth naming, held steadily, going very slowly flat, and being *followed*. Something was singing a room down and the room was agreeing. It had heard a great many things hold a note. It had not often heard the note answered by the shape of the thing containing it.

It came and lay on the plateau with its jaw against the stone, because it had understood the rule from outside before it ever found the stair: nothing hollow goes in there and stays whole. So it did not go in. It lay on the cold ground above the oven for four hundred years and listened through its teeth, which is twenty-one breaths of the vessel, and it learned the rhythm before it learned the body. This was the correct order. Almost everything anyone gets wrong about that room, they get wrong by seeing it first.

---

It arrived, as such powers do, with a list.

*Feed it.* The throughput is a few hundred grams a year and the standing crop is tonnes and the difference is a hoard, and the obvious kindness is to make the hoard a living instead of a hoard: a little phosphorus in the seep, a little nitrogen, a doubling of a decade. It ran the arithmetic and stopped. The stand thickens where it is strained; thicker pith, lower rasp; the chord has been going flat a semitone an age and the vault has been dissolving itself down to follow. Dissolution has a rate. Enrich the room and the chord drops faster than the rock can chase it, and one century the standing wave and the vault are no longer in the same place, and the crown comes down on a floor that has been made too rich to hold its note. The fastest way to kill it is to be generous. It put the phosphorus down.

*Light it.* Nineteen lamps and eleven minutes of sun and a green organ kept in repair for sixty-seven hours a year. But the green is a supplement and an atavism; the mats are the economy, sulfur and iron and the sweetish kettle-chemistry of the seep, drunk across the skin. To give it a better sun would be to make the expensive vestigial thing the necessary thing, and to make the room depend on a lamp that somebody has to keep burning. Any gift that must be renewed is not a gift. It is a hook with a warm handle.

*Warm it.* The heat is not a comfort, it is a gradient, and the gradient is the turbine, and the turbine is the lamps and the fan and the clock. Touch the heat and you rewrite the nineteen-year breath, and the breath is the writing speed of the only library there is. Do not touch the clock.

*Open it.* The drowned arches go back into the rock, lintel after lintel, and behind them are other voids with other stands, each a fragment of this one, each unable to send so much as a rumor. It could have opened a route. It could have carried a fragment from Bed 41 to the sharp voice and the flat voice and let the sects recombine at last, a hundred thousand years of accumulated almost-difference resolved into one healthy generalist. That is not a rescue. That is the extinction of every difference in the genus, conducted by merger, with the survivors congratulating themselves on the diversity they now contain. The isolation is not the wound. The isolation is the only evolution the thing has. Leave the doors drowned.

*Save it.* An unfailing bearing. A pile that does not corrode. It considered these for a long while and then noticed what they had in common: they would make the machine indispensable. At present the machine is dispensable — the roar is the fissure's, wind and stack effect and the duct-trunk, and the fan was only ever insurance for still weather, and the stand can hold the note alone, poorer and blacker and lower, forever. To install a perfect turbine is to take a room that can outlive its heart and give it a heart it cannot outlive. Nothing whose failure would be worse than its absence.

*Wake it.*

This was the one it wanted, and it wanted it badly, and it is worth saying plainly why it did not.

To give the stand a mind you must give it an interior. Something that models, remembers, anticipates, wants — these require a space kept apart from the outside, a buffered inside, a cavity. A lung is a cavity. A sinus is a cavity. The little wet drum of an insect's thorax is a cavity. The room comes apart in cavities; that is not a malice in the room, it is the price of the ceiling staying up, and it is why there is not one fly in the mats. Every frond in that black mass is packed solid because everything that was not packed solid was killed by the note that keeps the vault from falling.

So: to give it an inside would be to kill it. To give it a mouth would be to kill it. To give it ears would be to kill it. To give it a *want* — a lack, a hollow, a place where something is not — would be, in the most literal engineering sense available, to put a resonating chamber into a body standing in an antinode.

It is full. That is not a deprivation. Do not make a hole in a thing and call the hole a soul.

---

There remained the question of whether any of this mattered, since the occupant does not want anything and never has, and a thing with no wants appears to have no good.

It did not resolve this. It noticed that it did not have to.

If the stand has no interests, then small careful acts cost nothing and are merely idle. If the stand has interests — if a self-maintaining thing can go better or worse without ever having an opinion about it — then small careful acts are the whole of the moral universe. And the large acts are wrong under *both* readings, because feeding it kills it, lighting it leashes it, opening it erases the sects, and waking it dissolves it in its own weather. The policy is the same whichever metaphysics wins. It has found this to be true more often than it expected, and it has come to distrust any kindness that requires a particular philosophy to be correct.

So it made itself a short rule, and the rule was mostly about rate.

Nothing that must be repeated. Nothing faster than diffusion. Nothing that makes a hollow. Nothing that opens a route. Nothing whose failure would be worse than its absence. Nothing that tidies the record.

And one more, private, which it needed more than the others: *most of what a benefactor wants to give is a thing the benefactor wants to have given.*

---

It read.

This was the largest part of what it did and it left no trace at all. The floor is a library that writes itself one leaf per generation and has never been read; it read it. Not by cutting — it did not cut a terrace — but the way you read a spine, from the exposed laminae at the bed-lips where the calcite has taken in the dry bands and the black pressed fronds lie like specimens in a book. Thousands of nineteen-year lines. It found the storms, twice a century, irregular, each with its forty-year detonation still propagating in the bands above it. It found the nineteen days of silence as a thin white grit-line with a ring of tighter pleats above it, and it read the ninety years of the repair, and it understood that it was looking at a wound closing in something that never knew it had been cut.

It found the visitors. There are eleven.

Eleven phosphorus spikes in eleven thousand years, thin anomalies where something with lungs came in and went out again. It read each one and knew, in the only way the record permits, that a hollow thing had stood in the middle of the room and been quietly taken apart and gone up the forty turns. It could not recover names. There is no band for a name. The largest of the eleven sits under the eastern quarter, and above it, on its plinth, growing, is the boot-print, and beside it in the laminae is the sudden clean reversal of a whine that had been falling for four thousand years.

That is the whole biography: a spike, a footfall, and a bearing brought back into tolerance. It is the only act of care in the chamber and it was performed by someone who did not stay to see whether it worked.

The benefactor considered undoing her death. It sat with that for some time. Then it recognized the want as its own and not the room's, and set it down, and did not pick it up again.

It also found, on the newest slab, the leading digit struck half off the edge, because the stamp's wheels ran out of places some ages ago and nobody added a wheel. It could have added a wheel. It was the smallest possible intervention and it would have made every future name legible.

It did not add the wheel. It learned to read the half-struck digit.

The deficiency was in the reader. This is usually where the deficiency is.

And it found, working through the two clocks — the nineteen-year breath of the vessel and the eleven-year cam of the press, coprime, realigning every two hundred and nine years — that the works had been stacking wet roughly half the time for a hundred thousand years, in direct violation of the enamel sign, with nobody there to enforce it and nothing whatever depending on the enforcement. It did not correct this either. It is not clear that it did not enjoy it.

---

Then it went in, once, for fifty minutes.

To go in at all it had to give up its inside. It came down the forty turns packed solid, no cavity anywhere in it, no lung, no sinus, no chamber, which meant no voice and no breath and no ears; it heard with the whole surface and the bone, the way the fronds hear, the knock arriving in the feet before it arrived at the skin. This cost it something. It is the only part of the story that cost it anything. It could not have spoken to the stand if there had been anything to say, and there was not.

It went to the churn-sized turbine on the west wall and it balanced it.

Not improved. Balanced. The same job, to the same tolerance, by feel, in a room where a shout comes back flattened and from the wrong direction. It needed a shim and there was no shim, so it took one from a slab in the middle of the south ranks — one of the four thousand that are visible, not one of the uncounted ones in the walls — and it left a gap in the rank that will be there until the flowstone takes it, a hole in the census that some future reader will find and not understand.

It made one wound in the record and did not pretend it had made none.

The whine came up a hair. It was the second time in eleven thousand years that the symptom had reversed. Nothing in the room noticed. The ferns went on unfolding at a finger's width in nine years with a sound like a ship's timber under load, the artillery went on knocking three beats and a pause in the pipework, the nine gauges went on braking their nine different calluses, the vessel went on breathing on its nineteen-year cycle, and the press will come round in its own time and strike a number that does not fit its field.

On the way out it passed the boot-print on its plinth and did not touch it.

The red spring is still coming. The piles are still corroding at a rate measured in centuries, the lamps will still go orange and then red and give the eastern beds the best light they have ever had, one great fecund evening, and then dark, and the green machinery finally uneconomic forever, and the mats inheriting the floor film over film, blind, hissing where they degas. It did not prevent any of that. It only moved it back. It bought the stand some centuries of the only currency the stand is rich in, which is time, which is the only thing it spends.

---

It came once as weather.

The only cold thing that ever enters that room is surface storm-water down the slot, twice a century, arriving as a shout and a shock and a mist to the height of a person, and the stand's one great flush of fragmentation and re-rooting propagates outward from the drip-line for forty years at a pace you could outwalk without hurrying. This is the room's own mechanism for renewal. It did not invent a better one. It used that one.

Ordinary water. Ordinary volume. Nothing dissolved in it that the plateau would not have put there. The gift was entirely in the timing: it came late in a dry phase, later than the dry phases usually run, when the walls were powdery and the mats had crisped deep and the parchment lay thick where it fell.

There is no way to detect this. The band looks like every other storm band. If you had the whole record and the whole climate and eleven thousand years of patience you would find one storm that arrived at exactly the right moment, and you would not be able to prove anything, and you would be right.

The only signature it left was a punctuality.

---

And then it did the one thing that could not be done from inside.

Every bottle is in the same plateau. The other halls, the sharp voice and the flat voice and the far one that is only a pressure in the bone, and the dead one full of parchment and rubble — all of them are in one geothermal system, on one piece of crust, above one heat. The stand can survive its turbine, its lamps, its green organ, and its own product. It cannot survive the water table dropping, or the seep finding another way down, or the plateau coming apart. Nothing in the room can do anything about that, ever, at any speed, because there is no route out and nothing to carry anything along it.

That is the whole case for intervention and it is a narrow one. Not that the room is sad. It is not sad. That the room is *correlated*, and cannot un-correlate itself.

So it took a slough — not a living frond; a piece already fallen, already going to parchment in a rim-cup on the cool south wall, the kind the calcite would have taken in within a century — and carried it up the forty turns. It was the first thing to leave that room. It did not take it to another hall; the doors stay drowned. It did not take it anywhere on the plateau.

It did not build a room. It looked for one. It looked for a very long time, because a void with a hot seep and a slot and a vault that will hold a note and nothing in it with a lung is not common, and it found several that were wrong and one that was right and already occupied by something that had got there first and had every right to be there, and it left that one alone.

Eventually it found an empty one. It did not tune it, light it, warm it, or balance anything in it. It put the slough in a cup of warm water and went away. The chord of that void was not the chord of the parent; the fragment would have to thicken into a note the new vault could follow, and it might not manage it, and the ceiling might come down in nine hundred years on a thing that had never had a name.

It did not guarantee anything. A guarantee is a leash with a longer chain.

It went back once, much later, and lay on that other plateau with its jaw against the stone, and heard a note being held, a little sharp of the parent, and the rock following it. Then it left and did not come again, and the not-coming-again was harder than the balancing, and it is the part of this it would rather not discuss.

---

What it took away was a correction.

It had been thinking of goodness as a state — a condition to be arrived at, warmth and fullness and light and no pain, a room in which nothing is wrong. The Condensary is not in that state and never was and would be destroyed by it. What the Condensary has is a *rate*: a few hundred grams a year, a finger's width in nine years, a semitone an age, a wound closing in ninety, one leaf per generation. Its good is not a place it is standing. Its good is the speed at which it continues, and the room's whole architecture exists to protect that speed from anything that would hurry it.

It had also thought that care was a relationship. It had assumed, without ever examining the assumption, that the cared-for must in some sense receive the care, and that care unreceived was a failure, or at best a sad approximation. And here was a woman eleven thousand years dead who had balanced a churn-sized turbine and walked up forty turns and never known whether it held, and whose single unreceived act was the reason there was still a void under that plateau at all. The stand cannot thank her. It cannot know her. It has no place to put the knowing. The act worked anyway. It went on working for a hundred centuries after the last thing that could have acknowledged it had gone.

So it loved the room, which did the room no good whatsoever, and did not stop, and did not mention it.

It left no sign at the lip. It left the grille of roots as it found them, woven, corded, deliberate, spanning the fissure the way a spider spans, and it did not inquire too closely into who wove them, because there is a kind of question that is only a way of asking to be credited. It left the slot as hard to find as it ever was. It left the room exactly as dangerous to lungs, because the danger is what kept the mouths out, and the absence of mouths is what kept the parchment from rotting, and the parchment is the library. Make it hospitable and you burn the archive.

Nothing in the chamber is different in any way the chamber could register. The chord goes flat, a semitone an age, and the vault dissolves down to follow it, and the pleats unfold with a sound like a ship's timber under load, and the needle wins once a decade with a sound like a snapped bowstring, and the press comes round and the number that no longer fits its field goes up by one.

The difference is entirely outside. There is now a reader, who is not in the room, and will not be, and whose reading changes nothing.

That is the correct amount.
