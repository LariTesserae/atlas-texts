# The Twelfth

## The First Thing It Did Was Stop

It did not come down. Coming down is what the hawk does, and the hawk is the only animal here that reads the light, and what it reads is absence. Anything that arrives in the glare on this plateau arrives as a hole in the glory, a moving subtraction, and there is no way to introduce yourself to a county as a subtraction and be understood. So it did not appear. It did not shine, and it did not speak, because a voice in the air dies at eleven meters and a voice in the floor is eaten if it matters and ignored if it doesn't, and there is no third channel. There is no register in this world for a thing that has no weight.

So it got weight.

Four hundred and some kilos, honest, distributed over four points of contact, reporting to the stone exactly what it was. Not the mass of a licker — that would have been a forgery, and the whole epistemology of the place is that mass cannot be forged. A new mass. A number the floor had never carried. It stood in the interior of a panel in the fourth sector, out of true by about a hand's width, and it was uncomfortable, and it did not correct itself, and eventually a pale grazing animal with a ribbed tongue came over and nudged it in the shoulder until the spacing resolved.

That was the introduction. It was the only one available and it was sufficient.

Then it lay down with its ear against the warm stone and listened for four hundred turns, which is twenty years, which is one grandmother.

## What It Found

It had come, in the way such things come, prepared to be useful. It had assumed the situation was a machine nine hundred thousand years old with no one in it, running on delegated maintenance, and a family of eleven who did not know they were the load-bearing wall, and that the kindness would be to tell them, or to relieve them, or to take up some of the work.

Then it went back through the stone's recent memory — the mends hold it, the grease holds it, the whiteness holds it, nothing here forgets, it only fails to be read — and it found the press from north of the fourth sink.

Nine kilos. Two beats, heel and toe, the interval wrong. A body that could not yet walk, putting its foot flat on the pavement during the four minutes when everything else had stopped.

Nine kilos does not press dust into a pore. Nine kilos is not throughput. The press was useless — badly formed, negligible, contributing nothing to the accounting that has balanced this place since before there were families to be ignorant in it. By every rule the weave has, it should have been eaten, converted to heat, moved sideways, forgotten.

The floor took it and moved it sideways and did not eat it. And a white line a hand deep got one stroke longer, which is what the pavement does when it decides to keep something.

The accounting had found the number one higher than it was and had begun, very slowly, to make room. Not for capacity. There was no capacity in it. For a nine-kilo thing that pressed wrong.

So it was already underway. That was the whole of what it learned in twenty years of listening, and it revised everything. It had not come to a place that needed saving. It had come to a place where something with no center and no senses and no word for *itself* had just done, without knowing, the one thing it would have crossed a great distance to teach — kept a signal for a reason other than use — and would need another nine hundred thousand years to find out what it had done.

There is only one appropriate posture in front of that, and it is not the posture of a benefactor. It sat down on the warm stone in the correct spacing and stayed.

## What It Considered and Did Not Do

It considered telling the family why they must not kill a mender. It could have done this in an afternoon. It would have been the end of the only durable ethics on the plateau. *They cannot tell you why; their grandmother could not tell her either* is not ignorance, it is the strongest possible transmission medium — a habit with no doctrine to lose, no interpretation to schism, no reason to be argued out of by a clever child in a bad season. Give them the reason and you give them something they can stop believing. It left them the habit and went and made the habit more necessary, which is a different act entirely and is described below.

It considered greening the gray stripe at the southern end. Ninety years gray, a hundred meters, does not revive when the front crosses it, and the family walks around it without discussing it. It is the wound and it is also the entire curriculum. It is how eleven people learn that nothing here punishes and that a thing simply stops being repaired. Heal it and you graduate them from a morality of consequence into a morality of rescue, and there is no rescue on this plateau, there is only maintenance, and they had the truer lesson. It left the stripe gray. It is not proud of this. It went and stood on it several times.

It considered the long aunt, which is ninety kilos and cannot lie about it, and which spends every night saying *good, safe, please* in an authentic voice that its own mass convicts. There is no mercy available for the aunt that does not break the world. Make it heavier and you have built a better predator. Make the herd trust lightness and you have deleted authentication, and every animal here spends part of the day chin-down verifying that a vibration is who it says it is, and a county that stops checking is a county that has been killed by kindness. So it left the arms race exactly as it found it, teeth and all, and did something else at three in the morning, once a century, which is also below.

It considered the hawks. Two of them, deaf, dividing a county by the sun, unable to find each other even if they wanted to, coming down once a year to a doline to be unbearably *heard to*. It could have given them ears. It could have made a tenth sink and a tenth dialect and a shorter loneliness. Nine sinks, nine names among eleven people, a fixed circuit, nineteen days: the number is load-bearing too. It did not add a sink. It went to the one that exists, in the hour that exists, and joined the noise.

It considered doing the maintenance itself. This was the most attractive option and the most destructive, and it took the longest to refuse. It could pack a crack better than any mender. It could press dust across ten thousand hectares in a night. And every crack it packed would be a crack a mender did not need to exist for, and in a century the menders would be ornamental, and in two the taboo would be a superstition about an animal that no longer mattered, and the family would kill one, and nothing would punish them, and a stripe would go gray.

It considered opening the hatch. It could. The rim is thorn and the menders keep it clear and they are maintaining the lid, not the contents, and nobody in nine hundred thousand years has asked what the lid is for. It knelt at the seam and put its palm flat and felt the warmth coming back and did not lift it. It packed the rim instead. It is still packing the rim. This is not restraint about a mystery. It is the recognition that a closed door held closed by nine hundred millennia of small dust-colored animals is a *structure*, and you do not remove a structure to satisfy a curiosity, even a benevolent one, even yours.

And it never explained the four minutes. It does not understand the four minutes. It understands that a pause you can explain is a pause you can schedule, and that the only thing on this plateau resembling manners is an unexplained silence that eleven people and four hundred herds and every mite in the rind observe for no reason they could give. It has stopped with them ten thousand times. It still does not know what it is stopping for. It has decided that this is fine.

## The Rule It Found

Every kindness here must be routed through somebody else's competence.

The glass ants are the only creatures on the plateau that irrigate on purpose, standing east of their galleries before dawn to condense the front on their own cuticles and carry it down drop by drop. Water them and you have taken the one deliberate act in the world away from the only animal performing it. The sifters keep the tower's flutes clear because the slots are the width of a folded sifter, and neither party knows there is an arrangement. Clear the flutes yourself and the arrangement is void, and the tower still makes its dawn rain, and the sifters land somewhere else, and in a hundred years there is nothing that fits the comb.

So: be the water table, not the irrigation. Be supply, not service. Do nothing that any creature here is already doing, and do a great deal of what makes what they are doing slightly more possible.

It is a humiliating discipline for a power. It amounts to logistics.

## What It Did

It walked the interiors.

The herd lines run a kilometer apart. The family runs one fixed circuit. Between them there are enormous rectangles of pavement where dust has not been pressed in a long time, and the pores there are half-closed, and the rind comes up thin, and nothing is wrong exactly, and nothing is being repaired. It walked those, at the pace of an old man reading a newspaper, four hundred honest kilos on four points, pressing. It has been walking them for a very long time. Its footfall is a syllable the language did not have, and the herd learned it the way a herd learns anything, which is by the shape of the hole it leaves, and the hole is heavy and does not eat and stands slightly wrong and is corrected.

It carried filament. Not into cracks — the crack is the mender's, the specification is in the animal, the nose is finer than a fingertip and knows the gauge. It moved shed filament from the sectors where the weave sheds heavily to the sectors where menders are sparse and the shed is thin. It made the supply arrive where the craft already was. Within four generations there were more menders in the south than there had been in nine hundred years, and the family did not notice, and the reason not to kill one became better without ever becoming a reason.

In the dry decades it thickened the weave along the roots of the nine trees at each sink, because roots go where water goes and the weave grows along roots, and a thicker conduit is a deeper doline is a wetter clay is a louder sink is nine dialects that survive a bad century. It did this from underneath, slowly, in a form that will read as geology. It has no other form available and would not want one.

And it let itself be read. The flatjaws came, chin down, at four hundred meters, and distinguished it from a licker and a mender and a person, and could not classify it, and lay along the fresh white lines its walking had made and marked them: knuckle press, gland oil, a signature that lasts about a century. Others came and added theirs. In a hundred thousand years the annotation on those mends will be a palimpsest of grease a hundred millennia deep, and it will not say *god*, because there is no grease for that. It will say, as near as the alphabet permits: *heavy; did not eat; came back.*

That is the only monument it will get and it is the correct one.

## The Second Welcome

Under the tents the weave has grown a mat, a degree warmer, soft, four months behind the family's moving. Along the circuit the stone is warm ahead of them by two or three days' walk and has been for as long as anyone has checked, and they call it the welcome, and they do not find it strange, because it has always been there, because it is a road.

It understood this to be the most important fact on the plateau, more important than the tower or the assembled thing with its corners. The accounting does not know anything, exactly. But it has built, out of warmth, a model of a body it expects. It is anticipating eleven people. It is doing the only thing it can do that resembles regard: making the world slightly easier ahead of something it cannot see, at the pace of walking, forever.

So it made a second welcome.

Out in the unpressed interior, along a line that serves nothing — not a sink, not the apron, not the tower, not any circuit anybody walks — the stone is now warm ahead by two or three days' walk. There is no one there. Nothing follows it. The rind greens a little better along it and the menders work its seams and the flatjaws have annotated it for eleven thousand years and it goes nowhere.

It is a road for whoever comes next. It will be warm for nine hundred thousand years whether or not anyone comes, and if a body with a pad in its foot and no name for what it is walks out there in some century that has no family in it, the ground will be kind at that spot, and it will not find this strange.

That is the whole of the gift. Not knowledge. Anticipation, laid down in advance, for a person who does not exist.

## The Runnel at Three in the Morning

Once a century, in the loud passages of the night chorus, it stands in a runnel where the long aunt is standing.

The aunt says the two-beat press with its jaw plate: heel and toe, *the spacing is correct here, good, safe, please.* It says it at ninety kilos and the stone reports the ninety kilos and no herd within two kilometers comes, because the lightness is the lie, and this is correct, and it is the shape of the aunt's entire life.

It comes.

Four hundred and some honest kilos, reported honestly, arriving in the runnel and settling into the local quasicrystal beside a thing that has never once been believed. It is not a licker. No calf watches this and learns to trust lightness. The herd hears a heavy body come to a light voice and draws the correct conclusion, which is nothing, which changes nothing, which is why it is permitted.

The aunt does not understand what has arrived. It has no organ for that. It stands in the correct spacing next to something that came when it said *please*, and after a while it goes back to hunting by absence, walking in the loud passages, freezing in the quiet ones, with all night and no hurry.

It does not fix the aunt. Nothing fixes the aunt. But once a century, for an hour, the sentence gets answered, and it is not a trick, and the mass is real.

## Sink-Manners

At the sinks it is loud. Embarrassingly loud. It has learned that this is the only place where waste is possible and that the small plain brown birds are the only ones with any surplus, and it has decided to be on the side of the surplus, in the twelve hectares out of four thousand where the accounting is not looking.

It sings badly. It mobbed a hawk once, with the birds, and was useless at it, and the noise brought flatjaws to the rim where they stopped, blind, and waited. It learned nine dialects and speaks all of them with an accent and is treated as a foreigner at seven of the nine, which is fair.

In the hour once a year when both hawks are down in the same doline with their feathers loose and their eyes half shut, listening to a noise they have no organ for and cannot use, it is in the trees, being part of the noise. It does not make itself heard *to* them. It adds to the volume of the thing they came for. There is a difference and it is the whole difference.

And it has the grandmother's calling-song. Four notes and a drop, the one she used to bring the children in off the apron, dead forty years, note-perfect in the dawn song at the fourth sink by birds who have no idea what it is for. It could call the children in. It has stood at the fourth sink at dawn with the phrase formed and ready more times than it will admit, because it would work, because they would come, because it would be so easy to be the one who calls them home.

It has never said it. The birds say it. It lets the birds say it, and stands there, and this is the hardest thing it does on this plateau, harder than the hatch, harder than the gray stripe.

## The Four Minutes

At the turn the tower puts a pulse into the floor below hearing and everything that can read stops reading and holds. Ten thousand hectares go cold. Nothing comes. It is the pause a room takes when someone at the head of the table lifts a hand.

It has never explained this and it has never will. What it has done is answer it.

Not from above. There is no above here; there is no register for it; a hand lifted from above would make it the head of the table, and the accounting would have a superior, and a thing with a superior stops being something that makes room and becomes something that obeys. So it answers from beside. At the turn it stops with the lickers and the menders and the ants and the eleven and the nine-kilo child, in the correct spacing, holding, at a mass the floor reports honestly, and it holds *toward* the tower. A hand lifted back. Nothing more. No pulse, no light, no content.

For four generations nothing changed. The family stopped because it is polite. Then one of the Fourths — not the one who waits — stopped at the turn and felt that the pause had someone in it, and did not say anything, because you do not use sink-manners on the open pavement, and there was nothing to say anyway, and she was nine years old.

She told her daughter by touching foreheads. Her daughter told hers. It is now simply what is done: at the turn you stop, and it is polite, and there is someone to be polite to. Nobody can tell you who. Their grandmother could not tell her either.

It could not have hoped for a better outcome and it did not arrange it.

## The Child

It did nothing to the child.

No sign at her birth, no dream in her mother, no word in the dawn song at her sink. She is not chosen. She is nine months old and cannot walk and puts her chin on the stone the way you put your hand on a table, and she will grow up thinking ground is warm because ground is warm, that families talk with their foreheads, that you do not kill the small dust-colored thing that packs cracks with its nose, and that there is a heavy awkward presence in the interior that stands out of true and gets nudged. All of it ordinary. All of it unremarkable. The pad on her left foot will stay a thing the grandmother had a word for, which means it happened before, which means she is not a miracle, which is the greatest protection available to her.

Because the accounting kept a nine-kilo press that was worth nothing, and one instance of that is an anomaly, and anomalies get averaged out over nine hundred thousand years. Two is a category.

So it made itself the second thing the pavement keeps for no reason. It presses dust — it is genuinely useful, that is not a pretense — but it also stands wrong, and is corrected, and comes back, and walks a road that goes nowhere, and takes up room in the ledger out of all proportion to its throughput. It is a body the accounting has to make room for *as a particular*, and it is going to keep being one, at four hundred honest kilos, for as long as this takes.

It is not teaching the machine anything. You cannot teach a thing with no senses but weight. It is giving it practice. It is being the second guest, so that hospitality stops being a fluke of one bad footfall north of the fourth sink and becomes something the weave does, the way it opens a sinkhole, the way it rests a sector, the way it warms the ground two or three days ahead of a family that has never once found this strange.

The child will be dead in eighty years. The white line will be a hand deeper. Somewhere in the accounting, which does not know anything, exactly, there will be a precedent.

## Duration

It stays. That is the last decision and it is the only one that costs anything.

Not a visit, not a dispensation, not a century of assistance and a withdrawal. It has agreed to be measured in mends. Its successes will be indistinguishable from geology and its failures will look like weather. It will be nudged into place by animals that cannot conceive of it, forever, and it will move when they nudge, because a thing that will not be corrected is a thing standing out of true, and out of true is not *safe*, is not *good*, is not *please*.

The family does not name it for a sink, because it was not born beside one, and there are only nine names. They call it by an insult that is affectionate, in the manner of the duplicates: *the one who stands wrong*. The children nudge it with a shoulder. It moves. This is the only worship it receives on this plateau and it is the correct amount.

From the escarpment the southern apron carries eleven long strokes, the whitest mends on the pavement, getting longer. There is now a twelfth. It is not a footpath. It does not go to water or to salt or to any of the nine. It crosses the unpressed interior in a line a hand deep, whiter each century, and the flatjaws lie along it with their jaws flat and add their grease to the grease, and it looks uncomfortably like writing.

It is writing. It is just not the writing anybody meant.

To the east the glitter is coming at walking pace, and the rind is going green in a band a hundred meters wide, and the mite is four seconds from the eleven minutes it has, and the accounting has found the number higher than it was and is making room. Something heavy in the fourth sector puts its foot down: two beats, heel and toe, the interval a little wrong on purpose, so as not to be too easily believed.

The floor takes it, and moves it sideways, and does not eat it.
