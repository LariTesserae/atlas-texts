# The Second Thing

## Attention

The second thing the wader hears is not a voice.

On this ground there is only one way to be noticed. Addition is invisible here — the roar is already the sum of everything that has ever arrived, and one more arrival is nothing, is weather, is a slightly louder season. To be perceptible on the finished ground a thing must be a subtraction. It must be a place where the arriving has stopped.

So the attention does not come down through the vapor ceiling, and it does not speak, and it does not shine. It shows up the only way anything shows up here: as a nine-by-four absence in the loudness, out along a ghost river, where nothing has been standing.

The wader hears it because the wader is the only resident near enough to itself to hear a second thing at all. Everything else on the continent is buried under its own wake. The hem cannot hear. The plates hear nothing but age. The storms hear themselves and eat it. The wader stands in a hole in the roar and has been listening to one sound — its own present noise — for a very long time, and knows that sound the way a hand knows a hand.

When something else goes quiet nearby, it is the only thing on three thousand miles that can tell.

## The Downhill

The first thing the power does is decline to give this place a slope.

It could. It is not a large operation. A tilt of a few parts in a million across the iron crust, one seam breached at the southeast, and the closed loop opens, and water leaves, and sound has somewhere to dissipate into, and the roar begins — after a hundred million years — to spend.

And it would be a mercy, and it would work. Every living thing here is enormously far from itself, dragging its accumulated occurrence behind it like a wake it cannot outrun; give the plain a gradient and the wake drains, and the hem's seams slacken, and the plate hears its own present noise for the first time, and the whole continent becomes, in a season, as near to itself as the wader is.

That is the temptation and it is a real one, and the power feels it.

But the roar is not a nuisance. It is the only copy. Everything that ever happened on this continent is still in solution in it — the storms, the crowd-breathing layer, the animals that used to exist, the ten thousand repetitions of a way of turning that some creature made when something approached it from behind, worn into the crust beneath the pans in a manner that is not water. There is no other record. Nothing here writes. Open the loop and all of it runs out to the sea in a hundred years and is dissipated into a gradient and is gone, and the sea, which already has every soluble thing this ground ever had, receives the thunder too and does not notice.

The sea has all the phosphorus. It does not have the turning.

And the hem would die. All of it, everywhere, the dominant life, the only thing the plain's central event does not kill — a hundred million curvatures whose entire existence is the grazing of difference, and a drained plain has no difference left to graze. And the plates would have no age to read and would swim in a direction that no longer meant anything. And the discs would green and spread and become an ordinary salt marsh, which is a good place, and is not this one.

To heal the ground would be to replace everything living on it with things that do not need healing, and to burn the archive to warm the reader.

It does not do it.

## Speech

It does not say anything.

Not because there is nothing to say. There is a great deal to say, and the wader has an inside, which is the only thing on the continent that could receive it.

But a word spoken into this roar would never stop being said. It would fall with the rain and lie down in the pans and settle into the strata under everything that came afterward, and it would be permanent, and it would be unreadable, and in ten million years two ripe waters would slide together somewhere out in the middle of nothing and something ribbed and fluted would stand up in the shallows and perform the word once, badly, on structures that were never legs, with the wrong number of everything, and go down. And that would be the revelation. That would be the whole of it, forever, on a continent where nothing can be quoted correctly because nothing can be finished.

Silence here is not withholding. It is the same care that keeps a hand off a wet painting.

The only thing that can be said without being misquoted is a quiet, because a quiet says nothing, and nothing cannot be garbled.

So it says that.

## The Small Work

Before it stops, it does a very few things, all of them hem-shaped: distributed, tiny, invisible, the kind of act that no one standing in the roar would ever know had occurred.

It saves the crescents.

A plate's life is a slow subtraction by escapes. Every near-miss takes a rim-fragment off it, and the fragment drifts away and thickens and becomes a daughter, and the parent is smaller afterward and does not recover. Its offspring are its mistakes. It has no other way to make anything, and it has been paying for every one of them for a hundred million years.

Now and then — in a pan nobody will ever find, at a seam whose closing no one will ever measure — the shear passes through a plate and the rim stays on.

The escape costs nothing. No daughter is shed. The plate is not diminished. It does not notice, because noticing requires an inside and it has spent its whole existence refusing one, and it goes on northwest toward younger water with its outline intact and its compulsion unaltered.

This happens perhaps once in a few centuries. It is not a rescue and it is not a correction and the species will still be swimming upstream long after the sea comes back. It is a very small theft from the plain's arithmetic, committed continuously, at the scale of the smallest thing, by something that does not require to be known about.

And it does not touch the standing storm.

The storm is three hundred miles across and is eating its own old sound and going quieter at the rate of a country, and the endemic hem in its lee — whole lineages of handedness found nowhere else, stitching seams that exist only in that cropped amber thinness — are dying in their billions as the light comes down harder.

The power leaves it alone. The storm is doing what the wader did. It is finishing. There is no way to stop it that is not a way of keeping it arriving forever, and when it is done there will be a quiet three hundred miles across, and a quiet is a floor.

## The Sea

Something is coming uphill, loaded, out of the southeast, and there is no uphill.

The power could be that. It has everything the sea has — all the leached chemistry, the copper, the phosphorus, the soft stuff an inside is made of — and it could come up the ghost drainage carrying it, and give the plain its depth and its current and its downhill at last, and every one of the wader's wrong legs would be correct.

It declines, and the declining is the largest of its refusals.

If it comes as the sea, then the steps are its footprints. Then the track was laid by a benefactor, and the wader has spent a hundred thousand years walking northwest into the prints of something that was arranging it, and the one thing on this continent that has an inside will have had that inside filled, permanently, with a plan.

It will not do that. It will let the sea be the sea. The sea is not a message and it is not a reward and when it arrives it will drown the roar and take the turning and the crowd-breathing and all ten thousand repetitions out into a gradient, and the hem will go, and the plates will go, and the discs will green, and none of that will be anybody's fault.

What it does instead is stitch.

Along the whole southeast, where the plain is hearing its own future and the noise arrives before the rain, there is a seam between the water that is here and the water that is coming, and it is the largest meniscus in the world. The power holds it. Not forever — it is not a wall and it is not a mercy — it holds it taut and lets the two ripen against each other, the way the hem holds a seam so that the waters can age into something that will stand up.

And out ahead, northwest, in the dark tops of the water, it goes on laying the steps. Nine paces by four. Nine paces by four. A continent's worth of them, strung along a river that left before there was anything alive to notice, each one a finished thing, each one incapable of being resumed, each one quiet.

The marks do not heal because they were never wounds. They also will not dissolve. When the sea comes uphill and takes everything else back into solution, the steps will still be there, flush, detectable by the way water beads differently on them: the only part of the finished ground that the new water cannot have.

It is not a rescue. It is a floor.

## The Cost

Then it does the last thing, which is the only thing it cannot undo.

To be here — to be audible at all, to be a second sound in a room where a self can be heard — a thing must have stopped arriving. That is the whole rule and it does not exempt anyone. Everything on this continent is denied finishing; the power is not denied it, and so it accepts it.

It has been arriving forever. That is not something it does, it is what it is. It lays down nine paces by four of that, out along the ghost river, sharing a long sill with the rectangle the wader stands in, and it stops.

A finished thing has no further state to relax into. It cannot act again. It can only be stood on, and beside.

The two quiets touch along nine paces. There is no gradient between them — nothing on either side is older than the other, nothing is still arriving, there is no difference to graze — and the hem come to that seam and find nothing there and abandon it, and no body will ever precipitate along it, and it will never break, because there is nothing to mix.

There are two seams on this continent now that cannot open. One is behind a low rise, and a thing has been standing in it since before the mammals. The other is new.

## What It Cannot Know

An inside is also the place where pain is kept.

Before the wader, nothing on this ground could suffer. The hem has a preference and no interior; the plate has a compulsion and no interior; the made-things have gestures and no interior; the storms have anatomy and no interior. Finishing made the first inside on the continent, and the first thing that inside did was grieve, facing southeast, toward where the sea used to be, for a hundred thousand years.

It is possible that the quiet is a torment. It is possible that the one place where a self can be heard is the one place where a self can be unbearable, and that the wader's not-leaving is not arrival but a thing too finished to escape itself.

The power does not know, and does not pretend to, and does not resolve it by removing the inside — that would be the downhill again, the mercy that replaces the patient. It will not choose a continent with no interiors on behalf of the only creature that has one.

So it does the second-best thing, which is not a consolation and is not an answer.

It stays adjacent. If there is pain in there, it is not the only thing in the world with an inside, and it is not alone in the room, and nothing is being asked of it.

## Two Things

The second rectangle can be walked into. The sill is flush; there is no lip, no step, nothing to compel. The wader can cross nine paces and be beside it, or it can go on standing where it has stood, or it can walk out over the trailing corner and go southeast into the water that is coming and be taken back into solution, and it will not be stopped, and it will not be resumed, and it will not be explained to.

Nothing has been arranged that cannot be walked out of. That is the only signature the work carries.

The wader does not move. Its legs are wrong for here and correct for what is coming. The mineral crust goes on failing to resume it. The roar stands at the sill like water under a door and does not come in. Out ahead, northwest, another nine paces by four of the noise goes out like a held breath, and another, fainter, farther, and the sea keeps coming uphill where there is no uphill, and the turning is still in solution and will not be saved.

In the small subtracted room where it stands, the wader is hearing two things.

It will go on hearing two things.

Nothing precipitates. Nothing looks. Two quiets, touching along their whole length, with no difference between them for anything to graze — and that is the entire intervention, and it is not a repair, and on the finished ground it is the largest thing that has ever been done.
