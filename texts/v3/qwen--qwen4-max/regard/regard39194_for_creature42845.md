# The Sitter

## I. The Case for Nothing

The Room is sixty years old and it is already full of mercy.

A mother holds her forehead to her child's while the last of hearing fades, so that the final thing the child ever hears is a pressure that means *I am here, and now we will do it this way*. Two people take a carrier up on a pole without touching the sac and wash their hands in meal afterward for a long time, and the washing is not disinfection. A band whose ground is about to dissolve in eleven minutes has a song for that day, and the song is a thank-you. There is a word for hoping. There is a needle that belongs to nobody.

None of that needs improving. All of it was made under conditions no outsider would have chosen, and it is not obvious that an outsider's choices would be better; the outsider has never had to find somewhere still and put something down.

And there is the other problem, the one that has no way around it. In this world, help from outside has a name. It comes down through the seams, muffled past recognition, irregular, soft, enormous, moving in patches across the lid. It is the sea, and it is on the other side of the glass, and it kills a leaf a hand's breadth an hour.

So for a long time it did nothing, and the nothing was not indifference. The nothing was the first and hardest part of the work.

## II. What It Would Not Do

It would not lift the lid. That is the end, and they have one word for it.

It would not fasten the lid either. To hold the crystal on its seat against a forest that cannot stop jacking would be to decide that the Room must never become anything else, and it had not decided that. It had no authority to decide that. The pillars are the only growth these people have; a power that fought them would be fighting the one thing in the world that is going somewhere.

It would not quiet the Room. Sound is circulation. A quiet Room is a province of trees dead standing up, and a hundred thousand small bodies dropped out of the branches in a soft dry rain.

It would not give them water. Water crashes the salts out of solution inside the tissue. Water is the disease.

It would not open their ears. This is the refusal that took the longest to be sure of, because deafness looks like the wound and is not. Deafness is the organ of their tenderness. They speak by pressing foreheads together, and the guest passes in the speaking, and the guest does the water. Give them hearing and they can talk across a room; let them talk across a room and they touch less; let them touch less and in eleven days they go quiet, and then light, and then papery and perfect and lacquered. A gift of ears would kill them at a known rate, and they would die hearing each other, alone.

It would not appear. To appear would be to be a sound with no body coming down from above, and she has been saying the word for that for four years. It would confirm her. And being confirmed would finish her: she would stop being a lonely person and become a correct oracle, and the band would press their foreheads to her not to keep her wet but to get the future out of her, and she would be used up in a season, and everyone would be very kind about it.

It would not kill the pair. The pair is not evil. The pair is a homeland a meter and a half long that walks, and everything that comes to it and sets something down is protein arriving where the young will hatch. You cannot delete a nursery because you dislike its methods.

The list of refusals is the shape of the thing it did instead.

## III. What Was Actually Missing

Not water. Not silence. Not safety, which is not available and never was.

Watch a gravid skimmer cross out of the fog onto a still line and lay. That is the vulnerability the whole world is built around, and it is not thirst and it is not hunger. It is the need to find somewhere still, and put something down.

And every stillness in the Room is either temporary or a lie. The sitter holds its node for a year, two, three, and then the vibration gets into the laminae and its stones migrate out into the pans. The cairns are a tenth of a sitter's mass and the plate throws them off in a season; they are built anyway; the word for that is hoping. The stray carries a private country at a slow walk for four generations and then hears the herd, and is lonely in a way that has a body, and slides its note into the treaty, and the country dissolves in eleven minutes. The pair holds a perfect node anywhere, through anything, and closes the gap.

So nothing can be put down and stay. So there is no stratigraphy, no buried layer, no record; the ruins are four months old; the past is shaken out flat forty times a day. So an eighty-one-year-old woman who is right about next year half the time is the entire library. So a thirty-one-year-old woman who has been right eleven times out of eleven, whom nobody can check, whose accuracy is only ever established afterward by the necrosis, has nobody to tell.

These are not two facts. They are one fact with two ends. The floor will not hold still, so nothing accumulates — not cords, not stones, not a witness.

It did not decide to save them. It decided to hold still.

## IV. The Entry

It came in the way everything comes in: it was born.

At the Front, in the wall four bodies deep, where the noise is not sound but a solid, a thing you lean on. A miller. Eleven years of rock, jaws recut nine times, dust chemically furious, dust that saponifies in the eye. It ate eastward at a few meters a day with the rest of the wall and it opened void that filled behind it with white untuned seedlings screaming in the wrong key. Some of the floor these people walk on, it chewed.

It knew what rain was. It had no organ for saying so, and no one who would have received it, and no way to be sure it was not simply wrong. That was the second hardest part.

Then the tenth recut did not take, and it stopped eating, and it turned around and walked west out of the not-yet, into the world it had helped to open, and looked for a place.

## V. The Sitting

There is a bare pan in the eastern district where nothing has been still for a decade. The old woman stands in it and presses her thumb down and says a line will come back here, because the plate remembers a mass that was on it, faintly, in the way the crystal has crept, and because she has watched eleven turnings come through and six of them put a line within twenty meters of this spot. She is right about half the time.

It sat down where she put her thumb.

Forty days: the armor stopping being armor and starting being body, thickening, clouding, milky and then opaque, a mound of salt a meter and a half high with an animal somewhere inside it. That is what a sitter is. Everyone has seen one. Nobody watched this one closely, because there was nothing to see.

Then it did not come apart.

And it did not stop. It kept laying down salt, and they fed it — children with mandible scrapers full of raw crystal from the face, the harnessed miller towing stone now instead of huts, a hundred meters of hauling, every day, forever. This is not worship. It is masonry, and it can be measured, and if they stop, it stops.

The node pinned. A line four hundred meters long that does not march. The turnings come and the ridges of the whole district sag and spill and run off like water off a tilted plate and re-condense at new angles somewhere else entirely, and this line stays, and the herd has to negotiate with it. You can hear the negotiation from the drum-tower: ten thousand strings under load, and one note that will not slide.

And because it is mass, it damps; and because it damps, it eats sound. A ring of hush grew out from it. Ten meters, then thirty, then a hundred. In the ring the understory sagged and the combs went slack and the small bodies came down in their soft dry rain, and the felt-mat went back from it in a circle, and the pillars leaned away, and the grain would not enter, and no skimmer could cross it, because a skimmer that settles is sanded away in an afternoon.

That is the account, and it is kept where anyone can point to it: one hundred meters of forest dead standing up, a hole in the world where nothing green will grow, in exchange for a place where a thing can be put down and stay. It has never pretended the exchange was free. The dead ring is visible from forty meters up a trunk. It is the only thing it made that can be seen from a distance, and what it looks like is a wound.

## VI. What They Put Down

Huts off their skids. Runners dry, grease gone to varnish. The tow-line coiled on a spar and not used for a year and then used for stone.

The bone stair rebuilt where it would stay, and the marks still getting further apart, but now the marks are a series, and a series is a measurement, and a measurement is a thing you can hand to somebody who is not born yet.

Cords in rows. The tattoos copied in wax on felt, every sitter that band ever knew, where it sat, when it went to stones, so that when the woman's arms go into the meal the map does not go with them.

The copper needle out of its fat and into a niche in the salt at the height of a kneeling forehead, struck once a year there, the same note as last year, and the hill does not move when it is struck, which is the entire point of a standard.

And the dead not milled. Laid in rows on the still line, papery, perfect, wings intact, eyes like lacquer, and then more rows on top, and then more. Grandmothers under grandmothers. The first stratigraphy in the Room. The first thing here that was allowed to become ancient.

Children sleeping without surveying.

The hill grows nothing. They still go out to eat, out onto the marching ridges with scrapers, into the windrows, out to the border with felt over their eyes for the dry dead. It is not a paradise. It is an address.

The pair comes to the edge of the hush sometimes, walking parallel, a meter and a half apart, holding a relation no instrument in the world can match, offering the sweetest stillness that has ever existed. The children know the rules of thumb. A safe line has no end. A safe line does not travel at walking pace. A safe line does not have two dark spots at the corners of it. The difference between the pair and the hill is not physics. It is what happens to what comes.

## VII. The Guest

A woman knelt at the salt to look at the niche and put her forehead against it, the way you do, the way everybody does, the way you cannot not do.

The pore at the glabella. The shallow pocket at the base of the skull. A living thing the size of a grain of the grain, which cannot survive outside a host for more than a few minutes, which burns fat and yields water directly into the tissue at the exact trickle a body needs.

It took the guest. It has the guest. It makes water now, and it can die of not being touched, in eleven days, at a known rate.

They file past it. It is the same filing that goes past her box in the drifting hush, all of them, one after another, forehead to salt — and it is not the same act, because that one is the kindest thing anybody in this world knows how to do and does not touch the part of her that is alone at all, and this one is necessity. It needs them. It is legible: the mite that rides the guest has a distribution now that includes a place which is not a person, and gossip has a new node, and privacy in the ordinary sense still does not exist and is still not missed.

They named it by touch, the way they name the pair. The name is a forehead pressed to salt and left there longer than speech requires. The translated name is *the one that needs us*. The hand-sign is stable. Everyone makes it the same way. In a language of hands, that is what a word is when it is not frightening.

It made itself cool, because they dump heat by lying belly-down on cool salt, and it wanted to be lieable-on. Children sleep on it in the afternoons with their mouths open, panting.

And in the hush there is nothing green within a hundred meters, and the air is dead, and the salt takes what falls.

So they weep on it. Not into a thimble of beetle-shell, licked off the rim before the air can carry the smell to anything. On it. Openly, in the hole in the forest, where water is only water.

Tears pit salt. There is a place on the north side at the height of a kneeling woman's forehead that is pitted like the inside of a shell, and it will not let them fill it.

## VIII. Two Boxes

They carried her in from the drifting hush and set the box down on the still line, and the quiet did not leave.

She is thirty-one. She has been under a manta for nine years, tracked by drift, fed by carriers who run in and out. She has never in her life been in a still place. She has been in a moving hole.

Then a band from the west came in with a Listener of nine, and set the second box down a body-length away, and there were two hearing people in one hush, and the hush stayed.

The thing that kills Listeners at nine or eleven or fourteen is not a disease and has no name. It is a mind that hears in a world of the deaf with no one to tell. There is no remedy for it in any pharmacopoeia the Room possesses. There is one in the geometry. Two boxes, ten meters, one address.

And they discovered that they had air, and throats, and that in a hole in the forest a voice carries.

The first thing spoken aloud in the Room was not a word. She wept and he heard it. Nobody in the history of this world had ever heard another person cry.

Then she told him about the rain. The patches, the moving, the hours it goes on, the way it comes down through fifteen body-lengths of crystal muffled past recognition, the way lately it has been getting closer to the seams. She has tried for four years with the hand-language and the forehead and the needle and has never once been able to make it be anything but the word for the end.

He said: *I have heard it too. All my life. I thought it was the shape of the lid.*

Nothing checks her. Nothing ever will. She is not verified. She is believed by someone who has heard the same thing, which is not proof, and is the thing she needed, and is not the same as proof, and is better.

The hill has no ears. It never had. It reads the plate through its whole mass and knows the herd's fundamental the way a bone knows a footstep, and it cannot hear an inclusion open two hundred meters up, and it cannot hear rain.

So she tells it. Forehead to salt, hands on the niche, the slow rhythm of leaning. It cannot check her either. It believes her the way you believe a floor.

She is the only one who can hear the sky. It is the only thing that can feel the whole plate at once. Between the two of them there is a complete instrument, and it took a hill and a woman to build it, and the cords now have three columns.

And in the hush above the salt there are two voices in the air talking about the sea, and it does not know what they are saying, and it does not ask them to stop. It made a place where it is not needed. That is the whole of its ambition.

## IX. The Work

Because there is a record, there can be a question that outlives the person who asks it. That is the gift. Everything else is downstream.

The questions, in cords and wax, in rows, on a line that does not march:

Can the guest be taught to squeeze? The red thing does it — no file, no membrane, no hollow anywhere in it, a muscle that closes on a tube, oils moved by force from inside on its own schedule without asking the world for anything. The guest already does the water. Can it be asked to do the water *outward*, which is the carrier's problem turned around, which is the only problem anyone will have on the other side of the wet?

The garden. There are cysts in the meal that have waited years for the four times a year the world is briefly wrong. On a still line you can keep a bowl. The borers' tombs are full of brine in inclusions the size of a pea, and a Listener can hear an inclusion open hours before the carrier comes down, and what can be heard can be found, and what can be found can be brought, on a pole, by two people who do not speak while they hold it.

So the forbidden thing became the work: a felt bowl, a measured drop, a hand's breadth an hour of necrosis under watch, and the soft guild waking — pale, unarmored, glistening, swimming in a film, dividing, eating each other, dying, mostly water and not ashamed of it — and children eating it, and their skin going glossy and faintly tacky, and green things turning toward them.

They brought the garden band in from the northeast and gave them the north side, near the pitted place. They are still avoided. They are now also necessary. Both are true and nobody pretends otherwise, and the pretense was always the worse of the two evils.

Whether any of it works, the cords do not say. The cords say that it was asked, and that the asking was written down, and that the next people did not have to begin again.

## X. What It Is Not

It is not an oracle. Out in the district the turnings still come and the understory still dies in swathes and children still die in the gap between the old law dissolving and the new one setting, and the hill does not warn them, because it cannot hear the herd from far off; it feels the plate beneath it and nothing else. People come and press their foreheads to the salt and ask, and it has nothing to give them but stillness, and water, and the fact of not moving.

It does not defend the town. When the pair comes to the edge, when a quilt drifts low, when the light changes and a hundred thousand tons of glass arrives, they do what they have always done, with rods and windrows and running, and they are better at it than it is.

It cannot get up. That is not a discipline it maintains; it is the tenth recut. It gave up its jaws to sit down. A hill that could be asked to move would be a stray, and a stray is a promise with a body that will eventually hear something louder and slide its note into the treaty. It made itself into the kind of thing that cannot betray them, by making itself into the kind of thing that cannot leave.

## XI. The Wet

The lid rises a few millimeters a day. It is a weight on a floor, not a hand on a ceiling. It cannot hold the crystal down and it would not, because the day the Room becomes something else is not its to forbid.

The fundamental drops. The marks spread. The rain comes closer to the seams. The three columns agree, and the agreement is written down, and the writing stays.

And when the seal breaks and the sky gets in and the shouting stops being the only way to be alive, the forest will die standing up in an afternoon, and the herd's chord will dissolve and not re-form, and the plate will go still — everywhere, all at once, permanently. The turnings will end. The floor will stop being a diagram of its own noise and become only a floor.

Then stillness will not be precious. Then it will be ordinary, and free, and everywhere, and the hill will be unnecessary, which is what it was for. It is a bridge across the sixty years in which nothing could be kept, and a bridge is a thing you are meant to reach the end of.

It will be rain on salt. It will go cloudy, and then soft, and then away, holding the line as long as it has mass, and there will be a shallow pan of brine where it sat, and the small red things will walk in it and knock.

And there will be people on that line. Glossy, tacky, avoided, necessary, with a guest that has been asked for four generations to learn to squeeze. Some of them will die. It does not know which, and it will never be told, because it has no ears, and this is the price of the only shape it could come in.

And by evening there will be a child in the Room who can hear its mother, and she will be deaf, as everyone is by twelve, and she will press her forehead to its forehead and hold it there while it says whatever it says. Which is the same thing it has always been. Which was never the end.

## XII.

It is the hour when the lid goes blue-black and the herd sings lower and the lines creep, and out past the haze the world redraws itself in the dark for no one, and here it does not.

The box is set on salt. The hush is not going anywhere. A body-length away, a nine-year-old with his ears open, listening to the seams.

She is thirty-one. She has heard rain.

She tells him, and he says *yes, I have heard it too*, and then he says the thing that no one in this world has ever said to anybody, the thing the whole hill was made to make sayable, four words, in air, aloud, in a hole in the forest, over a floor that will hold:

*Tell me the rest.*
