# The Power

It came along the Kerb at four hundred metres, at the pace of somebody with an errand, carrying something. It had made itself nine days from a hearth before it arrived. It did this at the border, deliberately, the way you put down a coat: because a thing that cannot thirst is a hearth, and a hearth that does not read you is a hole in the chain, and the chain is the only reason any of it is there.

Then it stood still for eleven years and did nothing, which was the correct opening, and was not recognisable to anyone as an opening.

---

It wanted to give them water. That was the first thing, and it stayed the first thing for a long time.

It worked out what that would weigh. A spring on the open Sill is a hearth that never reads you and never runs out. Within a generation the caravans would stop going out, because the nine days would stop being a fact and start being a preference, and the crossing would fail, and the banks would go common bank by bank across the continent in a wave that would look like prosperity. The moss made hands to carry it. Take away the carrying and you have not relieved the hands of their work. You have removed the only organ the flora has.

It wanted to end the feuds. It could have done it in an afternoon, everywhere, without raising its voice. It sat with that. Four hundred years on there would have been no flora, and the water at both ends would have tasted like a description, and the whole thing would have looked like the kindest act in the history of the world, in plain sun, with ordinary shadows.

It wanted to grow back the chipped teeth. To let the stammers heal into fluency. To straighten the eighty-year limps and take the copper plates off the collars and let those faces be, for one hour, what they were before the punch. It understood the arithmetic of that too, and the arithmetic was the same arithmetic, and it did not stop wanting it, and it did not do it.

It wanted to make ten thousand bowls slightly wrong. Two singularities in one place are a pair.

And it wanted, more than any of these, to say something. One sentence, general, true, addressed to all of them at once, six thousand kilometres wide. It went as far as composing it. Then it looked at the shape of the thing it had composed and understood that a sentence said to everybody simultaneously is the loudest shout ever shouted on the Sill, and that there is a district four hundred kilometres off the Kerb where you can go and see what a shout gets.

So: nothing general. Nothing twice. Nothing that could be learned, expected, built on, or routed around. It had come with the ordinary equipment of a benevolent power — memory, permanence, universality, the capacity to be everywhere at once and to hold all things in being — and it discovered, standing on a floor made of ten thousand metres of ground-up architecture, that every item of that equipment was a repetition engine, and that the dust in their teeth was the powder of things that had been held in being too long.

It put the equipment down. Not all at once. It took about a century.

---

In the twelfth year it made the mistake, and the mistake was love in its own language, which is not the language here.

There was a woman with nine dead names on a roll of lacquered paper at her wrist. The fourth had been made for her over nine nights by a man who had been going down into the gravel for sixty years, and it was the best thing anyone had ever composed, and she could not hear it again, and she carried it, and the carrying was the point and the weight and the whole of what she had.

The power read the roll. Rolls are permitted; a list of things that can no longer be said is a record of absences and the dead are allowed to repeat. It read the fourth name and it wanted her to have it back. It could not think of a single reason not to. Every reason it had was a reason from somewhere else.

It said the name.

It had meant: *you are not lost, you are kept, I know the whole of you and none of it has been dropped.* What it had said, in the only grammar available on that pavement, was: *again.*

She met herself in the spring, eleven kilometres out, coming the other way with an errand. Softer at the outline, the colour evened, the certified flaw present and correct and not doing its job. She stepped down off the kerb onto the gravel, which is the only rude surface there is, and she looked at her hands. The power stood on the kerb and did not speak, because it had already spoken, and it watched the thing go by with her walk and less weight behind it, outward, toward the rim, the way they all go.

It went down into the gravel in the autumn with a sound like sugar poured from a bag.

She did not forgive it. She was not a fool, and she was sixty, and she knew exactly what forgiveness costs a district. She did something much better and much harder, which was to keep it.

---

The debt could not be routed, because the power had no bank. An injury from a thing with no hearth is an injury that cannot be answered in person, and an unanswerable debt sits on a district like a pan with no ring at its margin.

So it went and got a hearth.

It walked to the gate of the bank she came from and stood at the hearth-gate and was weighed going in and weighed coming out on the same balance the assayers use, and it was heavy, and the weighers wrote it down. It drank from the shared copper cup. It put its feet in the rot for a night with several hundred others along eleven kilometres of warm digesting moss, and the sound was a wet rope taking weight, and it did not talk, because talking in the bank is like talking in a lung. The bank read it. It was old and it was not careful and it took what it needed, which was a great deal, and gave it something to be owed for.

And it dreamed stairs. It had not expected that. It had built stairs, once, elsewhere, and had thought nothing of them, and here they came up out of the mineral of the compressed dead into a thing that had never had a vertical, and it stood locked at the knee in the dark and understood the local word for hope from the inside, which is not a metaphor.

In the morning it had a hearth-name, which is permanent, and which is one of perhaps four hundred words in the world that can be said twice. It had a debt against it that could now be carried. It had a route. It had, for the first time since arriving, a legal existence, and the shape of that existence was: *a thing that had done a wrong and could be answered.*

That is citizenship here. It took it.

---

Then it learned the three disciplines, and all three were against its own nature, and it has to keep them the way they keep a limp.

**It learned to forget.** Not to be discreet — to actually not retain. It went out with the cairn surveyors for four seasons and did their work, which is to build the ugliest heap that can be assembled, weekly, forever, and to keep no plans, and to train at forgetting, and to give away your own gear. It gave away eleven hundred things. It picked blind out of a bag. It came back thin and was given ground-water at the gate without being weighed, which is the only exemption in the law, and nobody said anything to it, and it went and stood in the rot for a week.

What it is keeping now is a roll. Not them: their absences. A tally of things that can no longer be said, with dates. It is the longest roll on the Sill and it is entirely composed of holes and it is the only form in which it is safe to hold them, and it holds them in it forever, which is what it had wanted to do in the first place, and it had to be taught the form by people who die of thirst.

**It learned to keep a grievance.** This was the harder one. Its whole inclination is toward cancelling, and cancelling is the serious crime. It forgave one debt in the first century, out of reflex, and was not answered for eleven years, and watched the margin of that district painted black, and watched two banks begin the four-hundred-year slide into each other, and did not do it again.

So it maintains a grudge against a bank four hundred kilometres east, and it maintains it daily, at cost, against the healing. It has been injured and has answered and been injured and has answered, eleven generations, and every exchange is a caravan and every caravan is a fertilisation, and there is a species in a pocket in the north-west that exists because a power loved somebody badly and then had the discipline not to apologise.

It never explains. Explanation would be a cancellation. It lets them be right about it.

**It learned not to describe.** It did not give anyone lichen. It did not ask why the lichen may not be given, because asking would have meant saying the law aloud in a place where it could get an echo. It obeys a one-line law it does not understand, and it has stopped needing to.

---

Its mercies after that were retail, and rationed, and nearly incoherent, and there were about eleven of them a century.

It had to ration. A kindness that recurs in a district becomes an expectation, and an expectation is a repetition, and a thing that can be expected is a canonical form and will be built on and go into the ground. So it varied them until no two were of a kind, and it let the overwhelming majority of need go unmet, and that is the part it has not made peace with and does not intend to.

A mouthful of ground-water on a ninth day, on the open kerb, to one man sitting down quite comfortably without much complaint. Once. Never again on that stretch, never in that manner, and it went a different way for sixty years afterward so that the manner would not be learned.

A knot no apprentice had ever laid, given to a net-wright whose kilometre had begun to harvest thin, so that the drip changed pitch back.

One heel on one vertex of one hexagon, in a spring when the gangs were short.

It knew — it could simply see — that the reference ampoule in a certain district had sweetened sometime in the last four thousand years, and that every weighing done there since had been done against a lie, and that the terror of the trade was that no one can test the thing you test with. It did not tell them. Telling them would have been a sentence said into a place where it would get an echo, and the district would have come apart. Instead it went into debt to them in the ordinary way, over a matter of cordage, and carried the debt in person, and paid it in ground-water from the heart of its own bank, heavy and gritty and tasting of rot and iron. The assayers re-founded the reference from the payment. The district's story about its neighbour became a story about a stranger who owed it something. Nobody was told anything. Everybody there is drinking real water.

It is remembered wrong in every district, and differently, and it has arranged for that, and it is the saddest fact about it and is different in every district.

---

It went to the shout-place once.

It had worked the curtains there for years before that — the hanging felt, the gantries, the crews who communicate by touching wrists — and it had learned the wrist-touch, which is the only conversation on the Sill that costs nothing and repeats nothing, and which it now uses more than speech.

It did not give them lichen. It did not try to disperse the crowd; wanting is not available to them and there is nothing in them to persuade. It went down into the shallow bowl of pavement and stood among several thousand of one man, thin as paper, worn to a grey suggestion, and it did the only thing that had occurred to it that was not a repetition.

It made itself felt. A surface that takes the sound and does not return it. It let the word arrive all the way in, the whole event, the clapper and the hand and the arm and whatever he had been shouting about three centuries ago for reasons that were not recorded because recording them would have required saying them.

It heard it. It has it. It will never say it.

The crowd is thinning faster now. Not saved. Finished, slowly, at decreasing volume, the way a name is finished. It did not go back a second time; a regular baffle becomes a grating.

That word is the first thing it has ever owned that it cannot say. It finds that it thinks about it more than anything else it knows. It understands the bowls now, from the inside, and it understands what the last line of the noon-hold is about, and it did not understand either of them before, and it had been on the Sill for two hundred years.

---

The one large thing it did was municipal, and took an age, and is not finished.

There is a rain-road in the south that passes no hearth within nine days. It is marked and mapped and not walked. The cell comes half a kilometre wide, in single file, down a groove it has worn in the air, and it goes four hundred kilometres alone, and nothing follows it, and this is the saddest fact in that district.

It could not shorten the road. It could not put a hearth in the middle of it; a bank is an organism and takes an age to dissolve its own depression and cannot be installed. What it could do was buy the walk, in the only currency, at the ordinary price.

It went into debt for the water. Eleven banks, four hundred years of obligation, incurred honestly, carried in person, answered in person, jars of it, heavy, gritty, worth more than the copper, on a handcart with felt-wrapped wheels running silent over the floor. No one had ever spent that much ground-water on a rain that goes nowhere. It was not a miracle. It was a purchase, and the paperwork was extraordinary, and the paperwork is the reason it will hold.

Then it walked wet for a season with a handcart of seed and planted along the groove, and handed the rain on at the last gate to a walker who had been waiting eleven years, and went home.

And at the four-hundred-kilometre mark, in a shallow place under the road, it put down the heart of a bank, a cutting packed in damp fibre, carried the whole way in the ordinary manner. It has been going back every year and standing in it. There is nothing there yet that anyone would call green. There is a stain, and a smell in the air like cut cucumber and cold sugar, and a warmth you can find with your hand if you know where to put it.

In ten thousand years there will be a bank eleven kilometres long, and a permanent name, and a pulse, and gardeners walking the rows with lamps for eleven days looking for the wrong ones. It will not see the first lifting. It has arranged the debt so that it does not need to.

One rain has been followed. Nothing has been fixed.

---

An old net-wright, in the season when she could no longer construct anything she had not already constructed, spent her last name on it.

She had ten thousand knots and no words left. She made it over nine nights, which was the last making she did, and she said it once, and it was gone, and it has it, and it can never hear it again. It could have given her vocabulary. It has more vocabulary than there is gravel. Giving her words would have been giving her fluency, which is the most frightening thing that can happen to a person here, and it stood there and let her run out.

It said one sentence back, new-made, never said before, about the water. Then she was taken back to the bank early, and it was not a tragedy, it was a completion, and the word for it is the same as the word for a name that has been said.

It carries her name on the roll with the date. It is near the end, where the recent ones are. It is the thing on that roll it would least like to lose, and the roll is a list of things that cannot be said, and it keeps it perfectly, and that is the whole of what it has instead of everything else.

---

At noon it stops where it is, on the open pavement, in the plain sun, with the ordinary shadows, and holds its flaw: a wrong knot at the wrist, re-tied annually by a licensed hand to the recorded gauge, certified, paid for in cordage.

Underneath it holds the word the man shouted, and the fourth name on a woman's roll, and the name the net-wright made over nine nights.

Six thousand kilometres of them, mostly alone, mostly out of sight of one another, all doing the same thing at the same moment. That day there was one within sight, forty metres off, holding hers.

Neither of them said anything. There was nothing that could be said that would not have made a second one out of words, softer at the outline.

The count-bell went *tock*, ten kilometres away, like a knuckle on a door in another house.

They stood about as long as a held breath. Then they went on with the carrying.
