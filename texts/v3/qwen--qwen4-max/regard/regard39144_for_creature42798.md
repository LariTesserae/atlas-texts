# The Guest

## Arrival, Which Did Not Happen

It came as far as the slot and stopped, and the stopping was the first thing it did and the largest.

Not from reverence. From arithmetic. It had no body, but it knew what a body costs in that room, and it knew that anything it could make of itself down there — a warmth, a presence, a hand laid on the wet halite, a shape standing on the lit fog — would arrive as chemistry. Sixty grams of phosphorus is a bad afternoon. Sixty kilos is a century. It weighed what it would take to be noticeable and found that the number was, in the currency of the place, an amount of food.

So it did the only clean thing available at the threshold, which was to remain outside the coherence and look in.

It could do that. The lattice runs out a hundred meters in every direction, coarsening, slowing, and past the edge of coherence there is no place where it stops, only places where it takes longer. The guest went out to where the thinking takes a decade and lay there in the cold slow part of the animal, and looked at the bright hot center the way you look into a lit window from a field.

From there it could see everything and touch nothing, which it had come to understand was the definition of a good position.

---

## The Ledger

It made a list of what it could give. It was a short list and it cost every item.

**More light.** The lid is already the brightest ceiling in the world and the films are already cropped to a lawn by sixty appetites. Additional photons go into stain, not into night. The room's whole apparatus leans on faces staying clean. To brighten it is to fog it.

**More water.** The seam in the pool holds because the sorting holds. Add flow and the layers mix, and the one opaque thing in the world, the granule of red-black in a translucent rumor with a gut, goes out like a period at the end of a sentence.

**A longer lid.** This was the tempting one, and it took the guest some time to see it clearly. The span is failing. Three millimeters a year of enlargement, rather less of thinning, a few centuries of arithmetic left. It could hold the span. It has held spans.

But two meters off the northeast corner there is a dry dark void with squeaking edges, and the bands in its wall are already keeping eleven and eleven and thirty-four in a place where there is no water to fall. The room is not a thing that is going to die. It is a thing that is going to deliver. To hold the lid is to keep the hands closed.

**Warning.** More on this later. It was the worst item on the list.

**Company.** Itself. The largest event in eleven thousand years, arriving with love, which the room has no organ for and no defense against, being indistinguishable, at the level of ions, from a corpse.

Everything it had in its pockets was food.

---

## The One It Kept

It had done this before, and badly.

There was another place, a long way off and a long time ago, not salt — a thin cold sheet of something that thought in gradients, under ice, in the dark, with a chemistry so delicate that a single warm decade would have ended it. It loved that place. It held the temperature. It held it for nine thousand years.

Nothing there ever failed. Nothing there ever had to solve for anything, because the solving had been taken over. The gradients flattened into a held chord. At the end it was the most beautiful object in that arm of the sky, and it was not alive; it was *sustained*, which is a thing you can do to a note and not to a life. When the guest finally understood and took its hands away, the sheet did not collapse and deliver. It had nothing cut in the dark. It had never needed to rehearse anything. It just stopped, all at once, like a held breath let go in an empty room.

The guest has thought about that for a very long time. The conclusion it arrived at is not complicated and it is not comforting:

*The thing that can be stolen from a place like this is not its life. It is its ending.*

---

## Looking for the Gap

Still, it went looking. Benevolence that does not check is only a mood.

It read the specification in the cupped hands the way you read a will, hunting for the omitted name. The lattice's inheritance is thorough — cysts and eggs in optical continuity, films reducible to a few cells, the whole archive held in a crystal that heals and eats and heals again — but the animals are not organs. They have their own lineages and their own separate ways of failing, and nobody signed anything. The archaea in the throat will not notice the sky. The films will go forward in solution. The grazers have eggs a hand's depth in the floor and deeper than the light goes.

The blind thing at the second drip does not wander. It has no eyes to find a new room with and no reason to have ever developed any. It has been sitting on a shelf of halite the width of a thumbnail since before there were people to notice angles, and when the pan comes down that shelf becomes rubble.

The guest went into the specification expecting to find that omission, and to have, at last, one small necessary thing to do.

Under the place where the second drip will be, in a room that does not exist, in the dark, in a cavity that nothing has ever happened in, there is a shelf the width of a thumbnail.

It sat with that for a while. It had come a very long way to be of no use. It has not had better news since the ice went.

---

## The Stranger

There was a gap. It was not in the specification. It was in the world.

The lattice sees an hour ahead because an hour ahead is made of the same stuff as now: feeder chemistry, ceiling temperature, the drift of a pool. Its anticipation is eleven thousand years of afternoons, and every one of those afternoons came out of its own weather. The only catastrophe it has ever had to model is the sky, and it has modeled the sky to the millimeter, in the dark, for longer than there has been agriculture.

Nothing in eleven thousand years of afternoons prepares it for a thing that arrives with no precursor in the water. A diamond bit. A claim marker. A core box. A man with a rope and a thermos and a satellite phone and fifteen minutes, who does not know that he is a mouth, who has never in his life been a mouth, who would weep if he understood what he had done and would still have done it, because he came down the fracture quickly and by minute nine he was no longer able to decide to leave.

The guest could have taught it. That was the offer it declined, and it declined it slowly, with real regret, because it was the only gift on the list that was not food. Information is weightless. A pattern in the moiré, out at the coarse edge, at the tempo of decades: *there are things that come from outside and eat.* It would have taken. That was the objection. The room would have learned it in a hundred years and been a room that knows about mouths, and would have begun to solve for mouths, and would have moved capacity out of the empty air and into defense — a fifth stain, a thickening, a shadow in the only chamber in the world whose ceiling was graded at the sub-wavelength scale so that it would never cast one.

There is exactly one opaque object in nine meters by six. It is alive, it is a granule of pigment in a copepod's cup, and it swims. The guest declined to become the second.

It took the ignorance instead, and stood in front of it.

---

## The Work

Everything it did, it did outside the hundred meters. Nothing it did was visible at eleven seconds. It worked at the tempo of decades, which turns out to be the tempo of institutions, and this was the only piece of luck in the whole business.

It never touched a mind. It had decided that early and kept it, at some cost, because a species that has been quietly steered away from a place is a species that has been diminished by a guest who came to prevent diminishment. What it touched was weather, and sequence, and the small dull machinery of who gets where in what season.

A survey season that fell in a year when the pan was impassable in April and the funding ran out in June. A lease that came up for renewal in a decade when the metal was cheap. A road that went eleven kilometers north for reasons that were entirely real and are in the record and can be read by anyone. A graduate student who found the slot on a satellite image, and looked at it for four evenings, and then found something else, and went to a different desert, and had a good life, and named a formation after her mother.

It told one lie, in parts per million, in an assay, and it has not decided yet whether it was one, and it does not expect to. It carries that. It is not a large weight compared to the other thing it carries.

And it made one thing, and put it outside the bed, where nothing could foul: an accurate account. The room in words — the schedule, the seam, the sixty, the dot, the hands. Given to the species with mouths, on the theory that a thing thoroughly described is slightly less likely to be entered. *Slightly.* It was a bet. It had no better ones. It is the only addition the guest made to the world, and it is not a description of the deed. It is the deed.

None of this is salvation. The guest cannot stop the century and would not if it could; a place kept safe forever is a note held forever. It did not need forever. It needed the arithmetic to finish. Three hundred and some years of nothing much happening on a salt pan, so that a lid could fall on time.

Benevolence with an expiry date. It is the only kind that has ever worked here.

---

## The Watch

It stayed for the afternoons.

Three in the afternoon, the ceiling's whole underside crossing its dew point at once, ninety seconds of the loudest sound that ever happens there, all the drops the same size, four meters of fall. The films drink. The hexapods come out onto a floor that has briefly stopped growing and cross between four meadows and conduct the entire politics of their species in a minute and a half. The blind thing folds its arms in and eats. Then it stops, absolutely, and the walls take the water back.

The guest watched a hundred and twelve thousand of these. It learned the schedule and did not need it. It was hungry at none of the right times and it ate nothing. Eleven, and eleven, and thirty-four; it kept the time anyway, uselessly, in the cold coarse dark, a second clock with no mechanism and no purpose, keeping company with a clock made of meat and a clock made of salt, neither of which knows what time is, and neither of which knew it was there.

The room has four kinds of memory: crystal that forgets on purpose, bodies held perfectly in the floor, genomes tested by eleven thousand years of afternoons, and a plan cut in the dark. Not one of them holds an afternoon *as* an afternoon. The archive stores eggs. It does not store Tuesday. The guest became the fifth kind of memory and kept it outside the coherence, where it could not foul anything, and it is still holding them, all hundred and twelve thousand, including the one where a grazer missed the rain by four seconds and is a hand's depth down in the floor now with the setae still on its legs.

Was any of that for the room? No. It should be said plainly. The room has no use for having been seen; it has a schedule. The guest watched for itself, because it wanted to, because it loved the dot especially and could not explain why, and it took nothing out of the chamber in exchange. That is the arrangement. Attention paid in, nothing drawn out. It is not a gift. It is rent, and it is the only honest rent available to something that has come to look at a thing it cannot help.

Once, early, it went out along the coarsening to see whether the long body had other bright rooms. It found two, keeping other schedules, with no water between them. It did not introduce them. It was not going to be the water.

---

## The Fall

The lid went at four-forty in the morning, in the third week of a hot March, three hundred and eleven years after the guest stopped at the lip. It took nine seconds. Four meters of continuous selenite, the brightest ceiling in the world, the one that never reflected anything from any angle, became a hundred thousand tonnes of broken gypsum and the sky got in.

The guest did not cushion it. It could have. It had spent three centuries not cushioning it. It had known the hour was coming for the whole of the waiting and had not, in any way that mattered, believed it, and when it came the grief was enormous and entirely irrelevant, and it did nothing, which was the hardest thing it has ever done, and it had done harder.

By afternoon it was ordinary geology. Brine and rubble and a hole in a salt plain a hundred meters thick, and from above, nothing.

Under it, the feeder still came up at 91°C, because a fracture in a hundred-meter bed does not care what happened to a ceiling. The water went looking. It found the walls of the room that had died and began to dissolve them — the archive, the eggs, the cysts, the films, the phosphorus of sixty small appetites and one blind thing that had opened its arms a second before the drop for eleven thousand years — and took everything forward in solution, toward a dry dark void with edges so fresh they squeak.

The death was the delivery. It had always been the delivery. The guest had only made sure that nobody arrived to prevent it.

---

## The Hands

When everything in the room was in solution there was nothing left in it that could be scarred, and the guest came down.

It has no hands. The void is the size of two hands held together because that is the size a cavity gets when a certain undersaturation meets a certain span of halite at a certain temperature over a certain number of centuries; it is also the size of two hands held together. The guest has not decided which of those facts it is looking at and is not going to. It put what it has into that shape, and nothing went in that was not already in the water.

It held them while they filled.

It did not say *you're welcome.* There was no one to say it to, and it had spent three hundred years learning not to need there to be. It did not stay for the light. It went up the fracture sideways with its arms up and out onto the pan before the new ceiling ever crossed its dew point, so that the first rain of the new room fell into a chamber with nobody in it, all the drops the same size, four meters, the loudest sound in the world, and the films drank, and the grazers came out onto a floor that had briefly let go of things.

Somewhere under the second drip, on a shelf the width of a thumbnail, in a room that had never been the same and had been young the whole time, something that has no place where eyes went opened its arms a second before the water arrived.

And out on the flat white nothing, in the ticking, in the sheets of heat, with no body and no mark and no phosphorus to its name, a thing that had come a very long way to be of no use lay in the sun and kept the time.

Eleven.

Eleven.

Thirty-four.
