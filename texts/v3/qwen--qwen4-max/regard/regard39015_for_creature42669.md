There is no outside to be kind from.

That is the first thing it would learn, and it would learn it the way everything here learns — by pressure, from a neighboring district, arriving like a thought arriving. There is no *on* on the Felt. To come down onto it would be to make a wound, and a wound here is four hundred years wide and has a fauna. So it would not descend. It would do what the eels do and what the dead do. It would be carried in, in felt, in silence, and set down at the rim, in the noise, because the rim is the only place on the continent where a thing can arrive and be told that it has arrived.

And at first it would keep its outside.

It would sleep on the stone. That is what the stone is for — the rim people build with the dead stuff so the ground will not take their shape, and a wise power, newly here, would want exactly that: to look without being looked at, to hold the one position on the plateau that is not a function. Grey crust on the last rock. Starving and free. It would sit in a roofless hut with a slab of living felt underfoot that some woman keeps alive by hand, and it would feel the forty-minute pulse come up through the stone as a change in what it weighs, and it would understand that it is being tolerated.

It would stay there a long time. Wisdom, here, is mostly a rate. It would learn the hour by the color of the air. It would learn to count in flowers, and it would not correct anybody.

---

The gifts it would consider, in the order it would consider them, and what each one is:

**One more degree.** The plain runs a degree above its own death and everyone who has ever walked it has called that a margin, and a margin looks like a thing a kind power ought to widen. It is not a margin. It is a note being held. Put one degree into the underside of the air and the Lid lifts; lift the Lid and the light gets a direction again, and shadows come back to a place that has not had them since the mountains finished; and the honey hour moves. The fliers navigate by the color of the air. They fly only in the honey hour, for eleven days, after sixty years of eating old summers in the dark, and they are the only thing that carries crab eggs from cup to cup. Shift the pitch of noon by a fraction and a continent's worth of sixty-year-olds comes up into the wrong light and goes down into the bruise and does not come back, and ten thousand cups skin over in a season because the traffic that was going to happen did not happen, and every one of those cups is a country. The kindness would take about a hundred and forty years to finish killing everything, and it would look, the whole time, like a slightly better year.

It would not tune the instrument it had come to hear.

**Bare ground.** Somewhere to start. A vacancy that isn't a death. But there is no such vacancy here and making one is called a collapse, and a collapse takes a district yellow for a decade and gives the plain a tree, which is a hardwood growing four hundred years toward being strangled shut. And then it would notice that the plain has already built its own hedge, and built it out of the only material it has, which is accident. Chamber failures happen once a season; the boom takes eleven minutes to arrive. There is a whole sparse patient guild that breeds only in catastrophe and finds the next catastrophe by pressure and does not think of itself as unfortunate. There are eels that breed only in disaster. There is a lichen with an outside. There are swifts that will not land, ever, in any circumstance, and drink from the air, and have properly seen the sun.

That is a seed bank. It is a seed bank made of patience and misfortune and one grey crust and some birds, and it will still be there when the sun changes its mind, and it is not the power's business to improve it. A thing that has arrived at its last form is not a problem. It is a result.

**Stones.** This is the one that would keep it awake.

A soft animal hangs under the canopy with the only hard object for two hundred kilometers in a pouch of its own tissue, and when it drops the stone it must go and find the stone, and finding takes sometimes a whole day, and if it does not find it, it dies, because it cannot make another. And when a lineage's stone wears to nothing, the young walk to a knuckle, and most of them do not arrive. It would be so cheap. A handful of quartz in the right places. A thousand years of debt paid off in an afternoon.

And then: quartz comes from the knuckles, and the knuckles are the last bare rock, and the lichen is on them, and the swifts nest beside it. Quarry the outside and you have taken the only outside. And a plain with hard objects lying about is a plain where armor becomes affordable, and armor is a shout, and the shout is the whole reason everything here is furred and padded and mossed and soft — ten million years of selection that made a continent with no hard edges anywhere on it, which is not a feature of this place but its *character*. Give them stones and in a million years you get shells, and clicking, and a fauna of flinching, and you have saved a hunter by deleting the felt.

Also — and this would take it longer to see — the walk to the knuckle is the only long journey anything makes here. Everything else is a timetable. The herd's route has not changed in four thousand years. That pilgrimage is the one thread of mixing on a solved continent, and it is spun out of young animals nearly dying, and if you pay the debt you cut the thread, and you will not find out what you did for another ten thousand years.

So it would sit with the two facts and not resolve them into a policy. The stone-carrier keeps a hard thing. The fill keeps a warm absence. They are the same gesture with opposite signs, and they are the only two things on this continent that resemble love, and both of them are ruinously expensive, and the expense is not a flaw in them. It is what they are made of.

**Whispering.** The rim people have no whisper register. They go inland and stop talking for weeks and it does something to them that they do not discuss. A kind power hears *deprivation* and reaches for the fix.

But the reason they do not discuss it is that discussing it would require the hiss, and the thing itself belongs to the silence — it is made of the silence, it is what the silence does to a person who has come inland to lie beside their dead. Give them a whisper register and you give them the means to talk about the one thing that has never been talked about, and they will talk about it, being people, and in four generations it will be a topic. You will have handed them a word in exchange for a rite. And you will have made them the loudest animals on a continent where loudness is a flare in a dark room, and there is a thing that hunts by flinching.

It would not give them speech. It would learn to shout.

---

Here is what it would do first, and it is not small, and it is the only worship available.

It would ask a rim-builder for a name. Any name. The oldest one she can still shout — some woman four flowers dead, whose oval is out in the fog somewhere getting wronger at a kilometer a year. And it would take that name to the lip, where the water goes off into cloud and the hiss is deafening and broadband and continuous and masks everything, and it would shout a stranger's name into the fall for as long as it could stand it.

Nothing would hear. The hiss eats names the way the felt eats everything above a whisper; that is why the names are shouted there. It would not be performing a resurrection. It would be doing the local thing, correctly, at the local volume, for somebody it never met. It would stand at the edge of a continent that has already arrived and add its voice to the only continuous sound in the place, which is the sound of the place losing itself, and which is the closest thing here to a sky.

Then it would go inland, and go silent, and not discuss what that did to it.

---

It would stop at a knuckle on the way. Chapel-sized, quartz, the worn back of a drowned animal, grey lichen on the crown starving and unmarried and free. It would not touch the crown. It would take one grain from the scree at the base, below the lichen, a stone the lichen never got around to marrying: smooth, thumbnail-sized, cold. The only cold thing it would hold in a year.

And then it would carry it, and in carrying it it would become, for a while, the thing it had declined to rescue. A soft animal crossing a solved continent for a rock. It would learn what the young learn. There is no direction to the light. There are no landmarks. Sound goes two meters and lies down. The stone in the pouch is a weight and a worry and a debt and after a hundred kilometers it is the only thing in the world that is definitely *there*, and you check it, and you check it again, and checking it is the beginning of every hard thing anybody has ever done.

It would carry it the wrong way. Not back toward a hunter. Out along the bearing — the bearing that is not the bearing of anything, not the sun, not the rim, not the prevailing wet — through the echoes, where the ovals are still ovals, and then where the doorways have begun to get a little wrong, and then where the oval is only a suggestion of an oval, and then out past the last suggestion, to a low ring of raised tissue with a gap in one side, in fog where no one will ever walk.

And the room would be clean.

---

That is the thing it would have come all this way to see, and it would kneel down in the gap and put its palm on the floor and find it body-warm.

Not sun-warm. There is no sun. A degree above the mass, which is a degree above skin. Swept. No film, no nest, no rot, in a world where a fallen bract is colonized before it finishes falling. Emptiness here is not a default; emptiness is a maintained condition, and it costs something, and something has been paying.

And in the floor, a hollow. Knees up, one arm under the head. Larger than whoever made it — they are always larger, as though the plain records not the sleeper but the space the sleeper was keeping clear.

Everywhere else on this continent the fit is exact. Every niche shaved down, every animal turned into a function, ten million years of solving. And here is the one thing that is not solved: a shape kept warm for something that is not there. A room kept clear for a departure. Hospitality with no guest, practiced so long past the point of skill that it has become a metabolism, an animal, a fringe of cilia and an idle ten years, holding a place at a table for a person who left before there were people, out in a fog nobody crosses, facing the bearing.

It would not settle the question. It would understand that settling the question is the one genuinely destructive act available to it — that to determine, from outside, with authority, whether the fills are someone, would be to answer a thing that the rim people have been carrying in their hands for longer than they have had hands, and to answer it in whichever direction most obligates everybody. A cup without its worm skins over in a season and drowns everything in it. If it had a function here at all, it would be the worm's: to eat the film off the underside of the question, so the question goes on breathing.

So it would do the other thing. The thing the room was for.

It would lie down in it.

---

It would fit, and the fit would be larger than it, and it would not investigate why. It would stay a season. It would eat nothing that was grown for it. It would wear the hollow deeper, which is the only writing this land admits, and it would let the pulse come up through it every forty minutes until it could not sleep any other way, and it would keep the stone in its pouch the whole time and check it, and check it, and understand the hunter completely.

And at the end of the season it would get up, and leave the stone in the hollow.

That is the whole act. One grain of the last mountain, set down in the innermost room of the longest echo of somebody, in fog, where nothing hard has ever been.

Not a tool — nothing there can use it. Not a supply of stones; one, and no more, ever, because two is a supply chain and a supply chain is a change to the world. An *other*. The one thing ten million years of practice has never encountered: a presence that does not depart. Something that will not soften, will not warm to a degree above the mass, will not fit, will not take an impression, will not leave. Stone does not remember. That is why they build with it. It is the only object on the continent that refuses to be recorded, and it would be lying in the middle of the only animal whose entire nature is to be the shape of what is gone.

And it would know, getting up, that this might be a cruelty. Absence cannot be lost. A stone that stays is the first thing there that can be *had*, and having is the beginning of losing, and it would be handing that to something that has never had anything, and calling it a gift, and it would not be able to check.

That is what benevolence looks like when it cannot verify. It would accept that. Accepting that is most of what makes it wise.

---

Then it would go, and not come back, and the not-coming-back would be the harder half.

It could stay. It has the time; it has all the time there is. But departure is the only renewable vacancy on the Felt — the one niche that cannot be filled in advance, manufactured constantly at every scale, a browser's warm hollow for eleven hours, a herd's gap for a month, a wound for four centuries, a person's shape for years. A power that stayed would be the one thing this world cannot metabolize: a permanent occupant. A stone in the plumbing. It would fill the last open niche forever and call it love, and the plain would go slack around it in a decade.

So: leave. Be recorded and leave. That is not a compromise here. It is the local grammar of being loved.

And it would not return to see how the gift was taken, because returning is occupancy, and because a guest who comes back to check was never a guest.

---

What goes out, then, at a kilometer a year.

The Felt cannot copy quartz. It can only take an impression. So what propagates is not a stone; it is a socket. A small hard socket in the threshold of every oval, going out and out, a hundred kilometers in a century, the doorways getting a little wrong, the ovals becoming suggestions, the socket becoming a dimple becoming a thickening of the tissue at the gap — the plain remembering a pebble it never had, imperfectly, outward, thinning, for longer than anyone will be able to say what it was.

One syllable added to a language that has been saying one word since before there were hands. The syllable means *someone came*. It will be mispronounced for a hundred thousand years. That is not a failure of the gift. That is the afterlife here, and it is the only kind available to anybody, and the rim people have been shouting into it all along.

And at the far edge, in fog where no one will ever walk: a low ring of raised tissue with a gap in one side, facing the bearing, and in the hollow a grain of the last mountain, and around the grain something soft and pale and slow, holding it the only way it knows how — by being the shape of what is not there.

Except that the stone is there.

So for the first time in ten million years there is a shape out in the fog that is not the shape of an absence. It is a degree above the mass. It is patient. It has nothing else. It will go on being the shape of that one cold small thing that stayed, out and out, long after the last stone hut at the rim has been grown up around to the height of its doorway, and it will be better at it than the guest was, because the guest had other things to do.

---

I don't say the plain was waiting. I say the room was warm, and clean, and larger than the body, and had been kept.

An accounting of what the power did would fit on the period at the end of this sentence, with room to spare. From above the Lid — that white plain over the green plain, going on until the world's own roundness takes it away — nothing would look different at all. The only witness would be a swift, which will not land, and has properly seen the sun, and has no name for anything, and drinks from the air.

And in a hundred thousand years a builder at the rim will set a pebble in the doorway of a new hut, and floor it with living felt carried in and kept alive by hand, and leave off the roof, because the Lid is the roof and has been, and she will find the question funny.

Ask her what the pebble is for.

*That's where the stone goes.*

Ask her which stone, and she will look at you the way you'd look at someone who asked which way is down.
