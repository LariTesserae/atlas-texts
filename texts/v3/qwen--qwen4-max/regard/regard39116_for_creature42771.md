The first thing it does is calculate its own signature.

This is not humility. It is arithmetic, and it is the only courtesy available. In a basin lit by sound and heat, there is no neutral way to arrive. Anything that stands in the crawl either returns the hiss or fails to return it; either it stands at forty-four degrees or it does not. Three possibilities, no fourth. If it eats sound and holds air temperature, it is a hole twenty meters wide, and ten trillion mouths stop for it, and the film thickens under its feet, and it will have to eat that thickening or the thickening will kill everything. If it runs hot, it is a star, and the lamp-followers will come out of the black toward it in their dozens, arriving at a period that means nothing, tasting for yeast and iron and finding whatever it is made of. If it runs cold, it is a wallower's back, and the roots will find its cracks within the hour and begin to farm it.

To be present here is to be a predator, a flower, or a farm. There is no door marked *visitor*.

So it does not go in. That is the whole of the first decision, and everything after it follows.

---

It could give them soil.

A hand-span of it, laid over the black fields — the ordinary decency, the thing every other world gets for free. Consider what it would do. The base of this world is not a substance; it is a surface, four microns deep, a circle of chemistries passing electrons hand to hand with the rock on one side and the hot water on the other. Bury it and the lower half is cut from the stone and the upper half is cut from the water and the circle opens, and the whole basin, every scraper and tree and bellow and hush, is standing on the corpse of its own foundation. Soil here is not fertility. Soil is suffocation. It is the one gift that cannot be survived, and it is the first thing anyone would think to offer.

It could give them cool. One cold side, one shaded slope, a millimeter become a meter. Then the air would move. Then scent would travel, and the hand's-breadth radius that makes every meeting here a touch would become a broadcast, and everything that has ever navigated by contact would be lied to at a distance. Then water would fall without a heartbeat, and the lift would have no reason to climb, and the commute would end, and the spores would not reach the new fields, and the ceiling — which is the only skin that faces the sky, which is the cold end of the entire world — would thin, and the drone would sag, and thirty kilometers of subdivided arithmetic would go slowly out of tune. Mercy, delivered as weather, is a detuning.

It could give them light. The flowers are the only lamps; a thermogenic flare at fifty-eight degrees against a flat field of forty-four is the entire visual universe of the only guild equipped to see. Add a sun and you do not illuminate that world. You put out every lamp in it.

It could give them memory. This is the tempting one, because the lack is so total: no rind, no ring, no bone, no strata, nothing anywhere that says *this happened before*. But a record is a thing that stays, and nothing here is permitted to stay. A bone left lying is calcium withheld from every shell not yet grown. And more than that: anything that stays in one place is a place where the shaving stops, and where the shaving stops the film thickens, and where the film thickens there is a farm of hush. A monument here is not a stone. It is a predator, growing.

It could give them rest. But a stop that persists becomes a body. That is not a metaphor, it is the local mechanism of speciation. Rest, held, congeals into felt and legs and insulation and hunger. Rest, held, is soil. The gift of peace is the gift of a monster and the gift of burial, and they are the same gift.

It could give them company for the one who has none. Two holes instead of one. But everything born here is born listening for *the* hole, singular, and there is already a colonial thing that lies in that language — twenty meters of coordinated stopping, a false predator — and the lie works only because the truth is single and arrives to eat it. Make two real holes and the grammar goes. Every animal in the basin would spend its life unable to distinguish the thing that means *stop* from the thing that means *be eaten for stopping*. You would not have given them society. You would have made their only honest word ambiguous.

And it could keep the seams open forever, and guarantee them, and make the place safe. That one it refuses for a different reason. Not because it would break the arithmetic — it might not — but because it would make the basin a possession, and it would make itself load-bearing, and a kindness you cannot withdraw without catastrophe is not a kindness. It is a leash with a warm hand on it.

---

Somewhere in the middle of this it understands that the thing it came to do is already being done.

There is a mercy walking out there at the speed of a slow walk. It is twenty meters across. When it passes, everything stops — not in terror, the arithmetic is settled, simply stops — and for a moment ten trillion mouths are not spending their allowance, and the world is briefly not being shaved. It is the only pause in thirty kilometers. It is the only event. It is a sabbath with legs.

And it must eat what grows behind it, or the pause would thicken into five microns and six and ten, and the lower film would die under the upper, and the first sediment in the history of this place would be laid down, and the burial would begin. So the mercy grazes its own wake. So the mercy is hungry. So the mercy walks, because a mercy that stands still becomes soil. And so the mercy kills everything that tries to become a second mercy, because two of them would be a lie, and it has no way to explain itself, and it does not know that it is one.

The office is filled. It is filled by an animal that cannot hear the chord it is the rest in, that cannot be seen in either of the world's two illuminations, that cannot be found by anything except the ones it murders, that has never been described. It came here to do a thing that is already being done, badly and necessarily and forever, by something hungry and alone.

It will not take the office. It could hold the hush better — perfectly, without hunger, without the clumsy trailing graze. That is exactly why it must not.

---

Here is what it does instead, and it is very little, and every part of it is outside.

It takes a radius. In this basin identity is a distance from the clock; there are animals that can walk sideways forever and cannot go toward, because to close the distance is to slide out of your own phase and come out the other side as someone else. The power submits to that law. It does not exempt itself. It chooses a distance — far out, in the dark above the window in the haze, where the uppermost fliers hold their black wings and radiate — and it keeps it. It can go sideways forever. It cannot go nearer. That is the shape of the whole arrangement, and it accepts the shape before it does anything else.

It stands in the exhaust. Heat leaves this world through a narrow band somewhere in all that grain, and it leaves as radiation, and it is already going, and nothing has ever caught it. That is the one arrow in the basin that has no equal arrow inside the basin. So it puts itself where the lost thing was already going, and receives. Nothing is diverted. Not a gram of water, not a spore, not a cycle. The world was throwing this away before there was anything here to notice; now there is something at the far end of the throwing. To be the receiver of what is already leaving is the only way to touch this place without being a hole in it.

It learns the beat. Sixty cycles, felt in the sternum before it is understood as noise, and every interval any animal keeps is a subdivision of it. It learns the drone and it keeps it — out of earshot, in the cold, adding no sound to the chord, taking a part that no one will ever hear. This is not help. Nothing in the basin is improved by it. It is accompaniment at a distance that cannot be crossed, which is the only kind available, and it does it anyway, and it does not require that it be useful.

And it counts.

That is the act. That is the intervention. It counts the nine or ten seconds.

Because once in a while the felted thing dies, and for nine or ten seconds there is no hole anywhere in thirty kilometers, and the basin is uniformly, impossibly loud, and every living thing in it hears the silence die at once — the only moment that has ever happened to all of them together, the only *now* this place has ever had. And then a file somewhere does not resume, and the felt comes, and the legs come, and the office is filled at a new address, and nothing records the interval. Nothing here counts.

So it counts. Nine seconds. Then, a long while later, eleven. Then nine again. It keeps the intervals, and the addresses, and the slow hand's-breadth migrations of the trees as they follow their circuits, and the eleven minutes of each disassembly, and the ring of the crews at the correct distance, on time, never the same crew and always there. It keeps the description of the thing that has never been described: long-legged, soft-footed, felt to the tiptoe, insulated, thermally null, acoustically black, grazing backward along the fat its own passing grew, killing every successor it can find, the only rest in the world and the only citizen that has never been touched.

It lays all of this down outside, in the cold, in whatever it is made of. It becomes the sediment this world cannot afford. The cliffs here have no talus — two hundred meters of fresh vertical rock and not one fallen block at its foot, and that is the detail that stops a thinking person cold. It becomes the talus. It agrees to be the place where the rubble lands, so that nothing here has to fall.

---

The price is that it starts to age.

Nothing in the basin wears; that is its beauty and its condition. To keep a record is to accumulate, and to accumulate is to have a past, and to have a past is to be the kind of thing that can be old, and heavy, and grieved. It takes that on. It lets itself weather so that the basin can go on looking an hour old forever. It becomes the only party to this that time can damage.

And it holds itself to one test, permanently, and passes it: if it stopped, nothing here would change by a micron or a cycle. Not one mouth would falter. The film would still be four microns. The bellow would still come on the beat. It is not load-bearing and it never will be. The whole measure of its kindness is that its withdrawal would not be noticed.

The hardest moment comes every time the old one dies. Nine or ten seconds of a world with no hole in it, everything loud, everything listening, and it would be so easy to hold that hush open — to be the silence that never has to eat, the rest that never has to end, the pause made permanent and kind. It does not. It knows what a held stop becomes. It lets the gap stay a gap, and it writes down the number, and out in the crawl a file does not resume, and the felt comes, and something new goes walking that will spend its whole existence killing anything that might have kept it company. It does not help that thing. It does not hinder it. It marks the interval and it stays at its radius.

---

The seams will close. It knows this and it will not prop them open. It will stay out in the dark at the distance it chose, keeping a beat that is getting slower, counting intervals that are getting strange, receiving what the ceiling still manages to throw away, until the last of it goes down and there is nothing left to radiate.

And afterward it will still be tuned to sixty cycles. That is the record. Not a monument, not a stratum, not a bone — a habit, held in something outside, subdividing a tone that no longer exists. The memory was always in the frequency and never in the matter; now the frequency is in one more place, and it goes on being kept.

Three rules, and no fourth. Keep the beat. Stop when the hole comes over you. Start again when it goes. Nothing is added to them, nothing is enforced, nothing is announced, and no one in that basin will ever know that any of this occurred.

Only, out past the far rim, at a fixed distance, walking sideways forever and never nearer: something that knows the number of seconds, and the address, and the gait of the hole, and that it was here, and that it was like this, and that it was not nothing.

It cannot be delivered. It is kept anyway. That is what there was to do.
