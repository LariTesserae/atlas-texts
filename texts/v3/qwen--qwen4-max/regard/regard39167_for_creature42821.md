# The Second Answer, Which Is a Resident's Answer

## What It Does First

It waits a year.

Not to be sure — it is sure — but because a year is the shortest unit in which this room says everything it has to say. Eight coins of light. Two nesting seasons. Two shifts of the ceiling nation, four hundred and some key-changes at dawn and dusk. The salt goes dark three hundred and sixty-five times. It watches the fourth Window in nineteen days and does not go and stand in the coin. It stays two meters off, in the brown-grey, in the light that is exactly enough to read a face at arm's length and no more, because it has already understood that this is the operating distance of everything good that happens here.

It learns the notches. This takes a season and it is the only homework available: not the towers' shapes, which are invisible past a few hundred meters, but their holes in the roar, the stable patterns of cancellation that three thousand years of ancestors cut into the noise by getting it wrong and not hearing their young. It learns to steer toward a specific quality of quiet. It learns that east is a note.

Then it makes its first decision, which is not about the room. It is about itself.

It gives up being everywhere.

Nothing in this country can use omnipresence. The physics does not admit it. Attention here falls off with the square of the distance, like everything else; a regard spread evenly over thirty kilometers is a regard of zero at every point, and the birds would be no more held by it than they are held by the Throat. So it does the only thing that has purchase: it picks a wall, and a sphere two hundred meters across, and it stays inside.

Law here is the inverse square. It obeys the law. That is the whole of the miracle and there is no other.

## The Gifts, Priced

It is not stupid, and it is not sentimental, and it can do arithmetic. It sits in the flour for a year and prices every kindness it could perform.

**Water.** One drip. A seep at the western end, nothing much, a film on the stone. Within a century the plain is mud, and mud is rot, and rot at eleven degrees is slow but it is not slower than desiccation, and the Ossuary Suburbs — forty thousand years of weightless folded dead, the only permanent residents, the census — dissolve into the general economy and are eaten. The salt tide stops, because a bound film that never becomes a droplet is a thing that only exists in a room with no droplets in it. The beetles' hindgut plumbing, a million years of engineering, becomes an ornament. And the towers: guano, saliva, dust. The city is water-soluble. One persistent drip would drill down through three hundred generations of pressed chests like a finger through wet paper. The kindest substance in the universe, delivered here, is a solvent.

**Fire.** The air is a suspension of feather-down and guano flour, held aloft permanently by wings, dry, at eleven degrees, in a room ventilated by one fissure breathing at the pace of a sleeping animal. It is not an atmosphere. It is a fuel-air charge thirty kilometers long. And even if it burned politely, the oxygen would go, and the Throat could not replace it in a thousand years.

**Light.** A permanent noon at the shaft mouths. The green rind — the poorest citizens, the slowest, a centimeter a year — would take the plain in a millennium and shade out the coral-pink and wax-white and translucent amber, which are not plants that lost a competition. They are plants that opted out of one. Universal light abolishes the pale gardens and the upside-down canopy and the foxfire, and it abolishes the calendar, because a coin dropped twice a year is a clock and a coin dropped always is nothing at all. Also the eyes are enormous and built for a dusk canopy, and the whole town would flinch and keep flinching.

**Hands.** Grip already exists upstairs; the bats have a tendon that holds a corpse for a week. Grip is not the missing thing. What a hand would actually change is the cup: a cup would stop being a cast of a breast and become a product, and the stratigraphy of chests inside the walls would end at that layer, and the towers would stop being animals. Also the first thing a hand wants is a vessel, and there is nothing here to put in one.

**Words.** One mark that means something. Then a bird could be told about a tower it has never heard, and the notches would stop being addresses, and the fledgling's last week on the wall — head turning, memorizing a hole in the sea — would be obsolete, and the recognition radius would go from two hundred meters to everywhere.

And here is the thing it notices, sitting in the flour, and it is the reason it stays for the next four hundred thousand years:

That gift is not wrong. It is *early*.

Universal recognition is already scheduled. It is the last event in the room's life: the floor rises, the vault shallows, the reverberation shortens, the roar thins, the radius widens, and in the final acoustic sliver every parent in the world can hear every chick in the world by name. Then no room for a wall. The gift is coming. Delivered now, it would close the plazas — and the plazas are not waste ground, they are the necessary silence in which families exist. Delivered now, universal love is the abolition of the family. Delivered at the end, by physics, on schedule, it is the last thing that happens and it is unbearable and it is right.

So: not the ceiling raised, because to lower the floor you must quarry the town, which is the same material. Not the filling stopped, because the city is the sediment temporarily standing up, and a room that does not fill is a room that cannot build. Not the schedule extended, because a living thing is a schedule, and the offer of permanence is the offer of stone, and there is already enough stone here to make a plain out of.

Every gift is a solvent, or a fire, or a theft of the ending.

## The Consent Problem

There is no mark in this world. No cord, no wheel, nothing scratched to mean something. Therefore there is no way to ask.

It cannot ask a bird whether it would like to be immortal, or handed, or spoken to, or saved. It cannot ask the beetles, who exist nowhere else in the universe and who might, for all it knows, prefer the universe to continue containing them. It cannot ask the dry dead, who have the only permanent addresses and might not want them extended.

So it adopts a rule, and the rule is not humility, it is engineering: *only gifts in forms this room already has a mouth for.*

The room does not produce. It receives, and it is extremely good at receiving. Everything here is a subsidy — sugar eighty kilometers wide, sap three hundred meters up, nitrogen off the plateau's night air, seeds in a rat's forgetful cheek. The economy is built entirely out of things arriving from outside in a shape that can be taken in. So the question is never *what would help*. It is *what arrives in a form that can be swallowed*.

A fig arrives in such a form. There is a mouth for it. There is a whine that means *bring it*.

A drip does not. A hand does not. A word does not. A rescued, immortal, illuminated bird does not.

## The Office It Chooses

It does not repeal the tax. The racer takes a dozen nestlings a year at the speed of a growing root and has known those cups longer than the chicks' lineage has held them; the owls take their toll at the doors; the snakes are arithmetic at eleven degrees. Remove predation and the floor nation starves, because a fallen nestling is a province, and provinces are how the two living centimeters survive famine. It will not decide which of its natives is the emergency.

Instead it reads the local law and finds the wound.

*To be anonymous here is to die.*

Not: to die here is to die. Everything dies; most of it is eaten; the eating is the plumbing, and the plumbing is the actual government, and it is not going to coup a fungus. Death is not the injury in this room. Death is a transfer. The injury is being outside the radius. That is the one state this world treats as a catastrophe, and it treats it absolutely, and there is no appeal, and it happens every nesting season to chicks who fall.

So that is what it treats. Not mortality. Anonymity.

It goes to the fallen. Out on the plain, in the flour, a hundred meters from a wall, a fat orange-brown bladder of lipid with a voice, making the sound that is its name into nineteen seconds of grinding metal-and-gravel roar, and nothing answering, because nothing can. Within a day the moth larvae will find it. Within a week there will be a boiling metropolis. Within six weeks it will be over.

It lies down in the flour at a meter — the innermost world, the only place in this country where anything is crisp — and it learns the grain of that voice the way a parent learns it, and it answers.

It brings figs, because a fig is legal, and because it will not be the one who decides that a dying thing does not need water. It cannot put the chick back; fifteen meters of fluting and feet that cannot grip and no holding anywhere in the world. It does not invent holding. It feeds it and it answers it and it stays inside the meter for six weeks, and when the larvae come it does not sweep them off, because they are the only true natives and they have been hungry for a hundred meters in every direction for a season, and this is their windfall, and it is not going to take a province away from a city to buy a chick a week.

It lets both happen. It does not arbitrate. It just makes sure that the one that is dying is not also anonymous, which is the only part of this that was ever optional.

That is the office. It is small and it is available every night and there is no shortage of work.

## One Cup

In the building season it does what everyone does.

It wets a beakful of flour and it presses it against a wall with the warm front of its body, tamping, shaping the cup to the curve of itself. It takes thirty years to cure. It adds one cup to a wall it did not begin and will not finish, among tens of thousands, indistinguishable, load-bearing, uncredited.

It signs nothing. There is no way to sign anything, and it would not use it if there were. It does not fix the baffles — the notches were cut by three thousand years of ancestors failing to hear their young, and if it corrects them by intention the tower stops being an animal and becomes a building, and it will not do that to the only long-lived organism in the room.

But three hundred generations from now, when the district is quarried and this wall is cut into for material, there will be a depression inside the new work, buried like a fly in amber, still holding the shape of the chest that pressed it.

That is the only mark it makes in four hundred thousand years, and it is not a mark that means something. It is a mark that *fits* something. It is the correct alphabet.

And because it stays — because it does not leave, because it is the one resident with a lifespan longer than the project — it becomes the first thing in the history of this country that has seen a tower built. No individual has ever seen one. They see a wall and add a cup. It sees the first beakful and the finished spire and every quarried suburb in between, and it can never describe it to anyone, and it does not try.

## The Portage

At dusk it goes up with the flood, four hours of wings like heavy rain on a stretched membrane, and out into the country of rivers where water lies around in the open like a lunatic.

The first time it rains on it, it understands what it has been protecting, and it does not bring any back except in its throat.

Eighty kilometers, in a body with no grip and a face made of whiskers, flying the last ten centimeters by touch. It learns fig clusters against a dusk sky with eyes that are commute equipment. It comes in at the high end of the room in the dark with a small wet cargo, clicks, and the roar takes the clicks and folds them into itself, and it lands on a wall the color of its own body, in a cup it pressed itself three thousand years ago and has never mentioned, and gives the fig to a chick whose name it learned by falling.

One bird's share. Not a millionth of the work — one porter's share, which is all any porter has ever done, and there is no other size of contribution in this room. Ninety percent water, and that water has been in a leaf, and in a river, and in a cloud, and it will be in a chick within the hour, and it will never once in its entire journey have been a drop.

It does this every night for four hundred thousand years. This is not a metaphor for patience. It is the schedule.

## The Other Room

There is a second plateau, and it is limestone too, and under it there is another drained conduit with an arch at the top of it, dry, level, sanded to stumps, and nothing living in it above two centimeters, and there never has been.

It does not make this. It finds it. Making is not the operation available; the operation is carrying.

And it does not move a single bird.

It moves the substrate, because the unbuilt nation is the sole manufacturer of the built one: bats first, a few thousand, hanging in the raw vault to start the snow. Then a handful of the two living centimeters per night — which is a city — mites in chestnut armor, springtails, blind pseudoscorpions with their pincers out, moth larvae in felt, the fused black wingless beetles that exist nowhere else, wrapped in their sealed pocket of humid breath. Cords. The salt bacteria. It does it the way the rats do it: it carries more than it can use and loses some, and what it loses will grow.

It cannot move the roots; the trees above will send their own fingers down the fissures on their own schedule, as they did here.

And it does not build a tower, or a cup, or an address. It leaves the room empty and stocked and open, and it arranges one thing only, which is the smallest and best thing it does in the whole account:

It gives the far end a fissure that breathes at eight or nine cycles a second. A forty-meter note. The same note.

Because east is not a direction here. East is a note. And every bird in this country flies with that pressure on one side of its body, and some of them get lost — blown off the eighty-kilometer run, overshooting, fledglings who memorized the wrong hole in the sea, one or two a year for four hundred thousand years — and they die out there in a country of rivers, alone, unnamed, unaddressed.

Now some of them will not. Now some of them will feel the bearing they have always had, and follow it, and find a vault with snow falling in it and nothing standing up yet.

It does not tell them. It cannot tell them; there is no mark. It has to be found by accident, which is how every grove in this country was planted.

And it will not be a copy. The notches will be wrong for a thousand years. Something will get established that should not have been. It will be a different room with its own schedule and its own filling and its own ending, and it will not be managed, and the same office will be available there: a meter, a voice, a fig, no signature.

## What It Keeps For Itself

It wants to keep them. It notices that this want is its own and not theirs, and it separates the ledgers and does not cheat.

So it does not extend the Ossuary. It does not embalm, or collect, or dry anything that would otherwise have been eaten. The dry dead got out of the cycle by being too dry to be worth eating and it will not revoke that by making them interesting. It goes out there and reads them — every folded wing, every dry pit, every eggshell scrap in the drifts, the whole census, forty thousand years — and it learns the shape of every breast from the casts, and it finds that it cannot recover a single name, because a name is a sound in a room and these rooms have been quiet a long time.

That is the limit and it accepts it.

What it can keep is the living names, learned one at a time at a meter, in the flour, from the ones who fell: a grain of churr, a particular rasp, the specific way one chick in one season out of three hundred thousand asked for water. It keeps those. It does not offer them to anyone as a consolation. Nobody here asked to be remembered and nothing here would know how to receive it.

It is for it. It says so. It goes on doing the other work anyway, which is for them, and it never confuses the two, and that discipline is most of what makes it wise.

## The Ending

It does not stop the filling. One millimeter a year, and the towers are the fill, and the city is burying itself with the same motion it builds with, and this is not a tragedy, it is a schedule, and it has read the schedule and signed nothing.

It is there for the thinning. As the vault shallows and the roar loses its nineteen seconds, the spheres widen, and it moves — wall to wall, district to district, less and less often, because the radius is coming out to meet it. Two hundred meters, then three, then a kilometer. Its office shrinks as the world's mercy grows, and it is glad, and it keeps going out to the fallen anyway, because there are still fallen, and a meter is still a meter.

And then the sliver. The last thin acoustic space before the floor meets the arch, when every parent in the world can hear every chick in the world by name — and it finds that it has been made redundant, that the thing it spent four hundred thousand years doing by hand at one meter has been accomplished for everyone by physics, and that there is no longer a single anonymous resident in the room.

There is nothing left for it to do. That is the best thing that can happen to a charity.

Then there is no room for a wall, and the roar goes out like a held breath, and the last conversation happens in a space too shallow to smear it, crisp, at a meter, everywhere, all of it at once — and it hears every name it ever learned, all together, from everything, and it does not add anything.

It lies down in the flour with the rest of the sediment. It does not go up. It has been up; it knows what the rivers look like; it has brought the water in its throat every night for four hundred thousand years and it is not going to leave now to be somewhere safer. The plain is the same material as the towers. It is all one thing, temporarily standing up, and it stops standing up with them.

## Nineteen Days From Now

The sun will stand over the fourth Window and drop a coin of unfiltered light for eleven minutes onto a grove of pink lanterns, and the ferns at the shaft mouth will take their income, and a racer will lie in the warm patch because a warm patch is a warm patch, and the grove will not appear to move.

It will not be in the coin. It has seen the sun; the sun is not the interesting thing here.

It will be two meters away, in the dark, in the light that is just enough to read a face at arm's length, at the exact distance at which anything in this world can be known — inside a wall the color of its own body, in a cup shaped by a chest that will not be identified, with a fig in its throat from a country of rivers.

Two meters off, a chick will make the sound that is its name.

And out of nineteen seconds of grinding, metal-and-gravel roar, from somewhere inside two hundred meters, something will answer it.

The roar will take the answer and fold it in and not change key. Nothing will register. There will be no event; the rise at dawn is six decibels and this is not even that.

That is how it will know the gift arrived.
