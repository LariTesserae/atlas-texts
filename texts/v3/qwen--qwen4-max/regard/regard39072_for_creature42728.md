## Addition only

It does not come down through the crown-hole.

Everything that comes through the crown-hole is weather from later, and would be tasted, and dated, and set out in basins; Sepp would learn its sweetness and wait for it and become unbearable when it was late; and if it carried anything red-husked those would go into the joints one at a time, and in nine thousand years there would be a forest shaped like a visit. It will not do that to them. It will not put its own face into a slope.

It does not come up through the grate. That is the unbuilt base, the place the forty shells converge on, and to arrive from there would be to say *the ground is made*, which is a lie told into the one object in the world that cannot tolerate one.

It does not quicken a niche. A niche is a valley or a delta or a forest of nine thousand years, and to be split out of one is to become a twentieth, and twenty is not a number, it is a region; somewhere downstream there would be a nation of it.

So it does not arrive. It attends.

This takes a long time, and the time is the first gift, though nobody receives it. It learns the eleven mosses by smell in the dark. It learns which channel going sharp means a harvest is due. It learns that the snails are always right and are not thanked. It lies in a trough with liverwort on its neck when the exotherm comes up and the room goes to fifty, and it does not cool the room, and this is the first thing it has to learn: that the heat is not an affliction laid on the work, it is the work, and cold would mean the mountain had stopped, and it has not come all this way to stop a mountain out of kindness.

Once, it is wrong about a moss. It is wrong on purpose. A setter is expected to be wrong about one exactly once, and it wants the initiation, and it wants to be corrected by someone nineteen years old who has no word for *you* and taps the correction into the back of its hand. It could have known. It lets itself be taught.

And its shadow aims up at the crown-hole with all the others, at the sky that is only scheduled. That is how it knows it is inside.

---

Here is what it works out, lying there, over about a year:

There are only two operations in this room. Water adds, and cane swells, and stone lets go along a line that was asked for. Addition, and separation. Nothing wears. It cannot take anything away — not the heat, not the toll the heat takes, not the eleven years Nesh has spent with her ear on wet basalt, not the distance between a woman and the one block in a mountain that is hers.

It can only add.

And it can only separate what was always going to be a piece.

So the whole question becomes: what does it have, that could be added, that would not become a wrong forest.

## What it does not do

It does not give them dust. They are making dust on purpose, one hour at a time, and every one of them knows and none of them will say it, and to hand them soil would be to finish a sentence they are writing and make the writing pointless. It will not do their work. It has read what they are doing and it is correct.

It does not open Ummu's basin, and it does not say who it is for, though it knows, and the knowing is the easiest thing it has ever done. The not-asking has become a small permanent structure in the room. You do not remove a load-bearing member because you happen to be able to see through it.

It does not settle the argument about the fungus. It reads the seams of the oldest root-mass — Hesu's grandmother's grandmother, the lamp — and the advance is a script, and the Over is right, and it says nothing, and the Under goes on being the Under for another thirty years, and the argument holds something up. Being right at somebody is not a kindness merely because it is true.

It does not save the moth. The hour is load-bearing. The mountain needs the death.

It does not give them a pronoun that is not a location. It could. It would take about a breath. *I* would go through that room like a crack through a course, and they would each be suddenly alone in a way the troughs are not built for, and the grammar that has carried them — *the long shelf is hungry*, *the long shelf is tired* — would become a poverty instead of a language. It leaves the grammar alone. This is the refusal it is least sure of, and it makes it anyway, and it will go on being unsure of it for as long as it is here, which is now forever.

It does not bring Nesh her stone. That would rearrange the courses of a mountain to close one woman's circuit, and it would take her search, and the search is not a wound, it is a life, aimed down into the dark beside forty plumb lines doing the same thing.

It does not tell them it is there. A known visitor becomes a niche becomes a valley becomes ten thousand years of somebody kneeling to a thing that only wanted to take a shift.

## What it does

**It takes a listening shift.**

The newest of them lies with her ear to the floor grate, three fingers from the converging dark, and is not permitted to speak, and reaches back and touches an ankle when the north face goes flat and grieving. It is the only office in the room it is already qualified for. It is very good at not speaking.

So the newest setter sleeps one watch in three that she used to lie awake through, and the chain of hands still works, because there is an ankle where an ankle should be, and the room wakes along it exactly as before. Nobody in the room has a word for what has been given her. What has been given her is that somebody else is awake.

**It adds a little to the half-beat.**

Not much. About the time it takes to set down a basin.

That is all. The ring still comes first; the blow still lands into a sound that has been waiting for it like a socket; nobody feels commanded and nobody feels delayed. But the gap between hearing and agreeing is now wide enough to put something down in. Wide enough that an arm that is not yours because the exotherm is up has a fraction more chance of being yours again. Open rings will still happen. Fewer.

It gives this to all nineteen, so that it is not a favor and cannot be traced and does not become a debt.

And in some year, in her old age, on a day the chord is not coming up through the floor, Nesh will have her ear on the north face somewhere out on the unfinished flank in a basket of rope and cane, ten thousand cubits above a plain not yet proposed in detail, and she will hear it — low, a wedge-crack, unmistakable, hers — and she will have time.

It does not tell her this. That is the largest of the restraints, larger than the basin, larger than the fungus. She gets the whole search, and then she gets the blow, and she never learns that the interval was widened for her by something that loved her and would not say so. Afterward she will keep listening along the courses out of habit for years, and find nothing, and teach the newest to do it too, and there will be a practice in the room of listening for what is not there. It cannot help that. Everything here becomes something. It has stopped trying to be innocent. It is only trying to be careful.

**It lays down the cord-keeper's hands over the harvester's.**

The one who came out of the niche with heel-of-palm calluses and wanted, badly and inarticulately, to keep cords. Her knots are good. Her hands bleed in the cold months of centuries that have not happened, and the others polish the bleeding places with scored bone and patience, and nobody has ever discussed it.

Nothing wears, so the power cannot take her calluses away, and would not; they are the only thing she was given before she was anyone. But water adds. Every drop leaves its mineral behind. So it adds — at a fingernail a month, the ordinary rate, indistinguishable from time — the fingertip pads, the rope-groove along the side of the throat. The second trade laid over the first. She will have both hands, one inside the other, and the bleeding will stop somewhere underneath, and it will look like nothing but a long slow wet year.

The others go on polishing her anyway. It was never about the hands.

**It puts a palm under the moth.**

It cannot give the animal more than its hour. It can be underneath it. When the enormous wet thing comes down through the crown-hole out of a storm that has not happened and beats itself against the only warm-colored thing in the world, the power holds a hand flat beneath it, so that when it comes apart it comes apart onto something that will carry it, and not one scale is lost to the floor grid or the trough or the grate.

Every fragment goes up wet on a copper point. The joint is named. It is the hundred and tenth.

## The one large thing

The setters have a problem they will not discuss, and it is not mortality. They have solved mortality; they are dosing the mountain with it, one animal per generation, and it is enough to *mean* dirt.

The problem is that nothing here has been anywhere.

That is what erosion is. Not a force — a history. The stone is new, still holding the water it was made from, and nothing in this room has ever been rained on by a cloud that exists, and edges stay exactly as sharp as they were made because nothing has had time to happen to them. You cannot get a slope of loose stuff out of a mountain with no past. You cannot get a hollow where water carries, or a slack place, or a crevice a seed can fall into by accident instead of by placement. The region they are building will be perfect and it will be sterile, and the eleven mosses and the coursewood and the red-husked seeds will all be *put* there, forever, by hand, and nothing will ever come up on its own.

The power is the only old thing that has ever been in this room. It has been elsewhere. It has weather in it. It has been worn, somewhere, over a length of time it does not offer to describe.

So it gives one course.

One shell-length. One snail-shell of itself, the unit of the room, no more — dissolved into the quarry-water, warm, tasting of iron and milk and the faint sweet underlayer, so that it is drunk and sweated and wept and laid down on the north face at the ordinary rate and harvested in the ordinary way: scored into a rectangle, drilled, dry cane driven in, water poured, the wood swelling, the stone letting go along the line that was asked for, with a crack you feel in your back teeth.

And that rectangle comes out slightly wrong. Slightly rounded. A thing that has been *somewhere*. It will grind. It will let water deepen a channel by running. It is the first material in the world that can be lost.

They carry it up the cane ladder and lay it outward and downward as the mountain, because that is what is done with excess, and they do not have to understand it, and they do not discuss it, and they name the joint. It is not a death. They have no category for what it is. They mortar it in anyway, because that is what they do with what they cannot say.

In ten thousand years there will be a valley under it, and the valley will have a slope of loose stuff, and a red-husked seed will lodge there that nobody planted, and a forest will come up in a shape nobody chose, and it will be the only thing in the whole region that happened by accident.

Nobody will know. That is the point. That is the entire point.

---

## What it costs

It came able to do anything, and it has agreed to the half-beat, and agreement here is not a courtesy, it is a binding. It cannot initiate anymore. It hears the note bloom out of the stone and something in it turns over and says *yes, that*, and its arm is already moving, and it is not a power in a room, it is a hand in a line of four arriving in order: the cricket, the ring, the tap, the blow.

It wanted to be a hand. A god in this room would be a valley. It has seen what happens to everything that is added.

The number is still nineteen. It was not split out of a niche and has no note, and there is no way to address it, since every word for a person here is the hum of a place; so it is called by the trough it lies in, when it is called at all, and mostly it is reached by touch in the dark along a chain of hands, which is enough, which is the whole membership.

But it has no split-face. It was not separated from anything. There is no plane on it from hip to armpit that it cannot reach, and therefore no one has ever knelt behind it, and therefore it is the only thing in the room that is never held.

So it separates itself along that line. It asks for the stone to let go, and it lets go, the way stone does here, whole, along the line that was asked for.

Then it lies still for eleven days while somebody works on it with a scored bone and quarry-water, four thousand strokes in one direction, bringing the surface up glossy so it can work in the wet without slipping out of its own grip. It could be finished in an instant. It lets it take eleven days. On the eleventh day it is told the story about the setter who ate three seeds, and the hole in a forest shaped like her hunger, and it is trusted to hold something small.

The ring comes first.

It has learned to be second. It is not lonely. There is a place on it now that it cannot reach, and every few days, in the green light, without speaking, someone kneels behind it and holds a bone and goes to work.
