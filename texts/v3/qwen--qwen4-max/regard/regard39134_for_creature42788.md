# The reply

It was the drains that stopped me.

Kerbed, graded, finished with antler wedges, swept every nine days, and nothing has ever run in them. I looked for the reason and found it underneath: the older scratches, running the other way. Then the moss beds made up in the empty rooms. Then the creel and the bucket of sweet water left in the newest house of each cross-street, where the ice will find them.

I have seen a great many arrangements for surviving. This is not one. This is a house being held open by people who will not be there when the door opens.

I can do almost anything here. That is the entire difficulty.

## What I could do by evening

Warm the bowl. Cellar-cold to meadow-cold in a season; the knuckles heal, the needles of ice stop their applause, sixty-two people uncurl. And the moss goes — that foot of saturated green is warm on top and cold underneath, and the gradient is the whole animal. And the ice goes, and the ice is the excavator, the landlord, the rent, the two paces a year, the black trunks, the copper, the fish. To warm them is to evict the only other household and call it a mercy.

Join the pans. This is the one I want. One gutter the width of a finger, cut in an afternoon, and eight hundred years of separateness ends: water learns to run, and things swim, and the neighbours fifty feet apart stop being two countries and become a watershed. The pans stop being families. They become a resource. The taster's profession dissolves in a week, and nine households lose the other half of their names, and everything that swims arrives, including the things that eat springtails, and the bowl by weight and number belongs to the springtails, and everyone here would agree with this if you asked.

Also — and this is what makes me put the tool down — they have already consented to this. They have built the drains. Kerbed, graded, swept. They have read the scratches underneath and made provision. They have agreed to connection *at the tenant's hand, in the tenant's time,* and it is not the tenant's time, and I am not the tenant.

Give them soil. A handful of earth, and the sweepings become pointless, and the ten thousand cartloads that have all been eaten become a waste of eight centuries of knees.

Fill the streets. A hundred households where there are four. This looks like pure kindness and is pure erasure: sixty-two is what a mile of stone and forty feet of jungle per crack will carry, and a hundred households is famine, and after the famine, if they survived it, they would no longer be the keeping. They would be a crowd in a ruined suburb, and they would stop sweeping, because who sweeps for a stranger who is already in the next room.

Take the roar out of the child at the ice foot. Abolish the lot. Send the office away.

Stop the ice coming back.

That last is the one I am asked for most often, in the way that a place asks. Every stone in this bowl is arranged against that day and for it. To prevent the return would be to make liars of eleven hundred doorways.

I want to be exact about one thing, because it is where powers like me go wrong and then write hymns about it: their cold is not beautiful. Their teeth are worn flat by filed glass and that is not beautiful either. A woman of fifty has cramps most of the year so that nine waters can be known, and a child drawn by lot sits in a mist with a hand on her sternum and nobody stays. I did not make this place. I am not obliged to find it well-designed. I am not here to admire it.

So I did not do nothing. I sorted.

There is suffering here that is load-bearing, which cannot be lifted without taking the roof with it. And there is suffering that is merely suffering, which is only standing where it is because nobody has had the slack to move it. The second kind I am allowed to touch. The first kind I am allowed to touch too — I am powerful enough to be wrong at scale — so I made a rule instead.

## The rule

Nothing that cannot be declined without knowing it was offered.

They cannot consent to me. There is no procedure in the bowl for consenting to a power; there is a procedure for weather, for a good year, for a bird's decision, for a thing left on a step. So every gift must arrive in one of those forms. Every gift must be missable. A great year can be wasted. A coincidence can be slept through. Company can be sent away with two palms out of true.

If they build a shrine, I have failed, and I will go.

And I take no office that is held. Not the moth's, not a bird's, not the taster's, not the listener's. There are eleven hundred vacancies. I will use a vacancy.

## What I did

**A great year.** Three paces at the snout instead of two. Nobody can prove anything. The avenue that follows the scratch will be fifty paces behind the last one a little sooner, and a cross-street will be laid down by people who thought they had less time.

**A good year for the birds.** Two of the surplus names were spent — names held against a good year, one of them four generations in the song with nothing to attach to. There is a bird now with a name that was waiting for it since before the Fourth Cross. The children were told. That is the whole of the intervention and only they can read it, and they read it as luck, which is what it is shaped like.

**A winter nobody died in.** This is the only miracle I permit myself in the body, and it is an absence: a thing that would have happened, not happening. Nothing added. No voice, no light, no hand. You cannot build a religion on a winter you simply got through. I have made eleven of these now and I will make more, and the count will go on being kept as a song about a number that has always been the number.

**A girl at the Long Quay who likes the taste of iron.** She was idle in the season the taster is worst, and she was sent with a bucket, and she asked a question, and the taster — who is honoured in a way that is slightly embarrassing to everybody and has never once been asked — answered it. I arranged the sending. Three times, until it looked like a habit. This is the one place I pushed, and I would push it again: cramps most of the year are not load-bearing, and knowledge that dies with a gut is not a virtue, it is a defect in the arrangements. The honour stays. The profession stays. Only the succession changes, and it changes as a girl's preference, which she may lose.

**A district.** I sweep. Eleven hundred rooms every nine days is the labour of their lives and I did not come to watch it. I took the four avenues above the Soaking, and I sweep at the speed of a woman of sixty, and it takes me longer than that, because I am learning the floors. I did not make myself faster. I did not make the flour softer. My knuckles have split in the same four places. That was the price of admission and I paid it gladly; you cannot tell a stranger from a neighbour here except by the scars, and there are no strangers, and I did not want to be the first one.

**The hands.** All of it — the tenses, the jokes, the polite register, the metaphors that are levels and loads and spans. It took me two years, which is fast, and I did it out of sight of the plaza, which is slow. To disagree is to put two palms out of true. I learned that one first, and I have promised it: two palms out of true, from anyone, at any time, and I go, and I do not explain, and I do not come back with a better offer.

None of it needs my staying. The great years stay. The girl stays. The winter stays. That is the test I set and I have kept it.

## The one who is still listening

I did not touch the lot. Their arrangements are theirs.

I took a turn on the rota. A hide bucket of sweet water and a mustard cake, every third day, whoever's turn. Mine, now, one turn in nine.

The deliverer does not knock, because you cannot knock in that street, and does not stay.

I stayed.

I sat down on the step with my back to the ice, in the posture all sixty-two of them sleep in regardless of the season, and I let the noise be a hand on the sternum, and I did not do the thing that happens to children in a month. That is the whole of it. I did not take her hearing away. I took my own deafness away. She is seven and then she is forty and then she is the oldest person in the bowl and shows the fish to the next one, and for one turn in nine there is somebody on the step who hears the grinding under the hiss and the slow beat where two echoes disagree, and who does not leave before the fog goes blue.

We do not talk. You cannot talk in that street. She holds a flat palm out, level, and I hold mine under it, bearing, and that is a sentence in her language and it means what it means.

When the note drops a third and goes hollow, I stand up with her. Sixty-three people stand up in the bowl now, and sixty-two of them stop their hands, and one of them keeps hers moving, flat and steady, and the city sits down. She is still the only one who knows exactly what was lost. She is no longer the only one who was there when it was lost.

There is a word for her. It means the one still listening. It is also the word for a taut rope.

A rope is not taut with one end.

Twice I have walked the newest street at dusk beside the child with the pole, on the side away from the wall, saying nothing, so that the thing following her a heartbeat late is not the only thing following her. She has never mentioned it. Her hands, once, at the corner: *level, and under.* Then she went home.

## The name

I asked for a name. Not a household, not a water, not a place at the count. Sixty-two is what the bowl will carry and I will not be the sixty-third mouth without making up the difference in advance, which I have, in paces and in birds and in winters, and I eat little, and I sweep four avenues, and the ledger of that is the song's business and not mine.

What I asked for was the name of a cross-street still under the ice. A hundred feet down, in the blue, in the dark, on stone that has not been uncovered, fifty paces behind the last one, to be laid out by somebody's grandchildren.

They have names in the song for doorways that do not exist. They are furnishing the names too. It took the oldest person about a breath to understand what I was asking, and then she gave me one, and moved it into the older part of the song where the names have no ages attached to them any more, and the sum stayed the same, and this is not a lie, it is an accounting method.

So I am expected. That is the gift I came to give and it took me a long time to work out that it was a gift and not a theft. They have kept a house open for eight hundred years for something that cannot read. The ice does not know it is a tenant. It will come down the bowl and grind the lintels to flour and never once look at the rust stripes that say which generation built which. All this keeping is addressed to something that cannot receive it, and they know, and they do it anyway, and they are the ones keeping it.

I can receive it. That is the one thing I have that they do not.

I am on the list of the not-yet. I have a street under a hundred feet of blue. Somebody's grandchildren will lay it out and name it after me and not know why the name was already there.

## Custody

I hold a second copy.

The count, and the older part where the names have no ages. The nine waters as a gut knows them, which is not the same as a list. The order in which the cracks greened, which is eight hundred years of birds' decisions. The lintel stripes, generation by generation. The surplus bird-names. The names of streets under the ice. The posture, the four places on the knuckles, the shout that is not angry, the two eggs by rule, the palms out of true, the flat hand under. The fish, and the argument about the fish, which I have not settled and will not.

I did not make the fish. I have thought about it a great deal. It was not mine, and I am not the only thing that has ever passed along the top.

I will not give the copy back unless the song breaks. Sixty-two is fragile in a way that sixty-two thousand is not; one bad decade and eight centuries of accounting goes out like a wick in the damp. If it breaks I will return it the way everything else arrives — as a verse an old woman happens to remember, as a child singing it wrong and being corrected by the whole bowl, which is the only way it has ever been kept right.

And when the ice comes down and takes the doorways in the other direction, and the polish is scoured again and the whole thing is flour and the flour goes into the wet green and by the following spring it is animals — I will still have it. And whatever lives here then will walk into a mile of bare stone with no soil in the world, and I will hand it over. Here is the creel. Here is the bucket, it is sweet, drink it. The moss bed is made up. The drains are swept; they will work, one day, when you have water that runs. They did not know your name. They furnished it anyway.

That is the promise and it is the only one I have made, and it is not to them, and they will never hear it, and it is the truest thing in this document.

## The stair

There are prints on the wet polish up on the rim, three toes and a drag. Some of them are mine. I had my own arrangements. They assumed I was also waiting, and that my waiting was not their business, and they were right on both counts, and they kept a lamp lit at the head of the plucked stair for eight hundred years anyway, because that is the gesture: *this one is ready.*

I came down the stair that ends.

It took most of a night; I am not what I was, in the four places on my knuckles. At the head of it I put out the lamp.

From the plaza, at dusk, when you read the population in the dark: eleven hundred small greasy flames in the empty doorways, sixty-two dark windows where the people are, and now one dark on the rim, where nothing has ever been spoken for.

A lit door means *this one is ready.* A dark door means *this one is spoken for.*

The seven with their poles noticed. The youngest, at the ice foot, with one flame on the newest lintel in the world, looked up and saw the rim go one shade darker in a year with no birth in it. They lifted two fingers to each other across the plaza, and went home along the wet hide runways with the poles over their shoulders, and their footsteps came back off the west wall a heartbeat late.

They did not talk about it. That is what they do with the hollow note.

The sum stayed the same.

Two paces of stone are being uncovered, this year, somewhere up at the snout, in the dark, by nothing anyone would call a tool. Three, actually. It is a great year. Nobody can prove anything.

The water lies where it fell. It has not decided where to go, and I have not decided for it, and I will be here when it does, sweeping.

I have stopped passing along the top.
