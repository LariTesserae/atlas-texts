# What It Does

## It arrives at the wrong speed, and corrects

It comes at the speed of noticing, which is the speed of an afternoon, and at that speed the plain is a noise. Loud, unaccountable, going nowhere. A power that stays for an evening has seen nothing here — has heard one shift of a thing that has been running since before there were flowers, and has mistaken the shift for the work.

So the first act is not an act. It stays. Through the eleven days, and then through the two years, which are not an interruption of the sound but the same sound at low current: frogs in bags of their own skin, eggs pressed into cracked polygons, a lungfish eating its own muscle in the dark, a moth folded like a roof tile in a cold crack. It learns that the piece has a tempo and the tempo is not human, and that most of the music is written in the rests.

Everything it does afterward is denominated in that.

## Diagnosis before mercy

There are two endings here and from the air they are identical. In both, the trees go, the samphire comes in red and jade, the scald widens a few meters a decade.

In one, the plain is finishing itself. It has been finishing itself since before there were mammals. The salt arrives on schedule, the last cohorts stand out their four centuries, the next tenant takes possession, and the whole thing is not a wound but a late style — the mountain spending its last few grams per hectare at enormous volume. To intervene there would be to prevent an old thing from being what it is. A benevolence that cannot tell senescence from injury will spend its whole strength holding a dying animal upright and calling it care.

In the other ending, something arrived from outside at a speed the plain has no organ for. A pump. A hoof. A rain in the wrong month.

So it goes down the ghost channel at the water's own pace, four hundred meters a day, pan by pan, sill by sill, and works out which meters of the widening scald belong to the plain and which belong to us. This takes years. It is the only important work here and it cannot be done quickly, and every subsequent decision — including the one it will not be able to make — hangs on it.

## What it does not give

No phosphorus. This is the great temptation, because the diagnosis is so easy to read: everything here is poor, therefore give. One truck of superphosphate would green the plain. It would also make leaves cheap, and grass continuous, and fire possible in a country where nothing has ever had to learn to burn. The sundew would stop eating midges. The shrub would stop dissolving stone with citrate for a month's income. The bird with the gut like a chute would be making its delivery run for no reason. The four-hundred-year tree would become a forty-year tree, and would be a worse thing, and would be nothing in particular.

Every shape it loves here is a shape made by scarcity. The poverty is not the affliction. The poverty is the author. To relieve it would be to erase the handwriting and keep the page.

No water, either — not more, not faster. The sills are a finger's width high and that is correct. Deepen the channel and the crest arrives in a day instead of a season, and the frogs lose the three days' warning they read through the soles of their feet, and the frogs are a life history built entirely on that warning. The slowness of the flood is not a defect in the plumbing. It is the signal.

No pantry for the planigale. It wants to give that one something — a few grams of hunger in a hole, the only resident that cannot wait — and it understands that a planigale with a store of food is a mouse, and that this plain has no mice, and that the flattened skull is the shape of the hunger it wanted to relieve.

And no meaning. It does not tell the chorus what it is for. It does not improve the cranes' dancing by giving it an audience.

## The inventory of the new

Everything the plain cannot metabolize is recent, and small, and arrives with a hum.

Bores, and the open drains that let the pressure bleed away into evaporation. Hooves, on a skin two millimeters thick that holds the surface together and cannot re-form under a hoof. A fence line that concentrates stock on the seep aprons, which are the only permanent wet ground and therefore the whole plain's pantry. A road with culverts that tell the sheet water where to go, on a plain whose entire character is that water leans and takes a week to decide. A pump running all night in a band that sits under the frogs. A light ninety kilometers away that turns the midge fog from a harvest into a lure, and the bats' constellation into a glare. A weed with a phosphorus appetite. One boot on the soft dome of the spring.

Its acts are all subtractions, and none of them will be visible.

Cap the bores. Pipe the water the drains were wasting. Retire the allocations — not suspend, extinguish; a thing that can be re-opened in a dry decade is not a protection. Unstock. Pull the fence. Move the road nine kilometers and take the culverts out and let the water lean again. Kill the weed while it is still only four addresses. And go to the highlands — the range the fish will never know about, where the rain fell a million years ago — and keep whatever is there that keeps the head.

The room at the foot of the northern breakaway is not saved by anything done to the room. It is saved three hundred kilometers away, in a bore field, by a valve, by a piece of paper.

## Absence, maintained

Here is the hard part, and it is where a wise power earns the adjective.

Their wall was made of nothing happening, held for a hundred million years, by luck. The text of this place says it plainly: absence is not a thing you can maintain. Nothing came to break it because nothing came. That condition has ended, not because the plain changed but because the world got fast and close, and now absence has to be *produced*, continuously, forever, by something.

To keep nothing happening, something must now happen all the time. And it must happen at the plain's tempo, using institutions whose tempo is a grant cycle.

So it builds the keeping out of the two materials here with a long enough time constant. Law that is hard to undo — an allocation extinguished, a lease retired, a bore casing pulled and grouted, things that cost more to reverse than to leave. And the people for whom the white quartz ribbons are a spine and not writing, who already read this country in order, with their feet, over years: the only maintenance system on the plain that is already running at the right speed, older here than anything but the crust.

It does not appoint them. It does not explain itself to them, or arrive with a plan and a lanyard. It removes what prevents them — the leases, the fences, the statutes that require a person to prove they are present in a place they have never left — and then it asks, and it accepts the answer, including no.

## The address

It names the fish, because a thing without a name cannot be defended in the room where the pumps are approved. Then it puts the name in the ledger and the location in a second record, sealed.

No boardwalk. No sign. No photograph with a horizon in it that a stranger could triangulate. No coordinates in the paper that argues for its protection.

The room's whole security has always been that no one could find it. That was an accident, and accidents have stopped being available, so it makes the accident deliberate. It measures its success by the number of people who could go there and do not know they could.

## The second room

The one question its wisdom does not settle.

It draws the plan: a second dome, dug and lined and charged, the reed and the snail and the flatworm and the fish moved to a second address, so that one bad afternoon cannot end a lineage that outlasted the rearrangement of the continents. It draws the plan and does not dig.

Because the ark eats the thing it saves. *Rare implies elsewhere.* Give the fish an elsewhere and it stops being singular and becomes merely rare; in ten thousand years there are two species and neither is this one; the hole in time becomes a facility with a maintenance schedule. To hedge the bet is to lose the exact quality that made the bet worth hedging.

And it notices that the right answer is downstream of the diagnosis it has been walking the channel for years to make. If the head is falling because of a bore, then we broke it, and we owe a repair even an ugly one, and singularity is a luxury we have already spent on someone's cattle. If the head is falling on its own — the plain finishing itself, the last of a million-year rain finally coming to the end of its lease — then it owes nothing but absence, and the fish should be permitted to end at its own speed, having been entirely itself the whole way down.

So it holds the plan and goes back to the pans and keeps measuring. It will not decide this in an afternoon. That refusal is the whole of its wisdom, and it is not much, and it is enough.

## The hearing

Then it does the thing it came for, which turns out to be the only thing on offer.

It stands on the pale ground at dusk with the inversion lying over the plain, and the chorus from fifteen kilometers away arrives intact, indistinguishable from the one at its boots, and it hears the whole resident — the fifteen-kilometer thing, the inch-deep thing, granite that has learned to shout — as one animal. No frog has ever heard that. No bat, who reads it as points. No crane, who hears its own. The bats hear a constellation and the frogs hear a neighborhood and nothing on this plain has ever been positioned to hear the entire body at once.

It does not answer.

The call is not a question. The reply already exists: another frog, two years underground, waiting for exactly this, and it arrives intact. The circuit is closed and has been closed since before there were flowers, and to answer would be to insert a third thing into a loop that requires nothing, and to convert a statement of existence into a conversation about the listener.

What it can do instead is keep the instrument in tune. No hum under the frogs' band. No light in the midge fog. Fifteen kilometers with nothing standing in it to scatter the sound. It protects the quiet so that the loudness can occur — the emptiness between the wet points, which is not vacancy but the reason the noise carries.

And it is honest with itself that none of this benefits the chorus. The chorus lacks nothing. It has no capacity to be lonely and no use for a witness. The only thing altered by the listening is the listener. It came to give and finds it can only receive, and it recognizes this as the correct result rather than a failure of nerve.

## The test

It leaves no signature. No dome of its own, no light, no mark cut into the quartz, nothing on the plain that would tell a later arrival that anyone had ever been consulted about it.

The test is this: someone stands on the pale ground at dusk, hearing the chorus arrive intact from fifteen kilometers away, and finds the place empty. And is lonely. And that is preserved too — carefully, deliberately, at cost — because the imported emptiness is the one thing here that is *for* anybody, and it should still be available to whoever comes with a chair.

At the edge of the pan, on mud that has been soft for three days, a frog the size of a plum inflates and calls. Under it: red gravel, an iron crust you could break with your heel, forty meters of clay that used to be a mountain, and below that the competent stone that nothing living has ever touched.

The call goes out. Nothing has been added to it. Nothing has been taken away.

Fifteen kilometers away another frog answers, having waited two years underground for exactly this, and the answer arrives intact, and there is nothing else on the frequency.
