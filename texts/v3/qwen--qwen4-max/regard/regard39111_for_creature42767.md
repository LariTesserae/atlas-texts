# The Tolerance

It does not arrive, because arriving is for things that were somewhere else. It attends. The stratus stays where it is. Ten thousand lux at the lip, no shafts, no shadow, no announcement; the gnats go on hanging in the middle of the bowl in their motionless column, and not one of them is displaced by a hair's width, which is the first sign of what kind of power this is.

It looks at the moss for a long time, because the moss is worth looking at. It looks at the tufa, at the leaf moulds of trees that fell into a river that is not there, at the sycamore leaning out to see. It reads the whole rind, every square metre of it, the springtails in their drifts and the mite in the fern axil that was an egg before the last harvest but one. It does not hurry past the small things to get to the point.

Then it finds the eleven seconds.

It counts. It counts again. It has all the time there is, and it spends forty minutes at the lip doing nothing but counting, which is thirty-nine minutes longer than anyone has ever managed, and then it stops counting in order to listen, and then it starts again, because the count is not a measurement. It is the only response the thing can be given.

---

The gifts are obvious. That is the trouble with gifts.

**Wind.** One breath, once every three years, timed to the week the spiderlings climb: enough to lift silk off the north undercut and carry a hundred of them over the lip and out into the world their bodies were designed for. It would cost almost nothing. It would cost everything. Moving air is a gradient, and a gradient is convection, and convection is the end of ten and a half degrees everywhere at once; the beads would stop falling straight; the films would thin and fail somewhere on the south wall in August, and the tardigrades would dry to glass and use, at last, the apparatus they have carried unused since the rock was a sea. To give this place wind is to hand its oldest residents the end of the world they were packed for. The stillness is not an absence here. It is the load-bearing member.

**Warmth.** Two degrees, no more, and the hole behind the fern strap on the south side becomes a hole that eleven wrens can share on a cold night, and the nine grams would not have to spend the night alone burning himself to stay alive. Two degrees is a draught. Two degrees is a chimney. The wren would be warm in a place that no longer existed.

**A rival.** An actual second wren, in February, with a body and a temper and a limit. It seems like generosity until you look at what he has: an opponent exactly as good as he is, who never tires, who wants precisely the same ground, who returns every phrase entire at a quarter of a second's delay because all the routes are eighty-four metres and none of them smear. No bird in any hedge in England has a rival of that quality. To give him a real one would be a demotion. And he does not require winning. He requires singing, and he is singing.

**A body.** Bronze, hung in the throat, tuned to the low G; or a stone at the lip; or a board with a name on it and a paragraph explaining the eleven seconds to whoever climbs down. Refused, and refused hard. *There is no object anywhere in the world that is it.* That is not a deprivation to be repaired. That is the whole distinction of the thing. Give it a body and you give it something to outlive, something to be stolen, something to crack in a frost; you take the one native of the Bell that survives its bodies and you nail it into one. The berths outlast the spiders. The eleven seconds outlasts the pumps. It has been changed twice already and resumed without interruption. It does not need a monument. It needs the pumps to go on being slightly unlike each other, which is a different request entirely, and much cheaper, and we will come to it.

**A listener among the residents.** It could retune a cave spider, drop her slits an octave or four, let her feel the swell as an event. It could give the mites ears. It looks closely at what that would do. The spider's world is arrivals — a milligram on a thread, a springtail's jump, tufa letting go off to the left — and she eats because the constant is scenery. Make the hum audible to her and you have not given her music. You have buried her dinner under a hundred cycles a second of unignorable nothing. The bats are the same trick in ultrasound. These two are exquisitely built to discard the one thing the place is made of, and that is not a flaw in them. It is how they are alive.

So the power declines to improve anything, which puts it in agreement with a clerk who has been dead for thirty years and who wrote one word on a schedule.

---

It sits with the spiderlings for a while, and this is the part it does not find easy.

One climbs to the high point on the north undercut and throws silk and waits. The silk hangs. There is no moving air; there has not been for a very long time. She waits with no notion of waiting, in a world with no duration in it, and then she lets go and falls eleven metres into the guano where the legs already are.

The power considers wind again. It considers carrying her out itself, one spiderling, nine milligrams, over the lip — and then follows the gift forward into the crop, which will kill her, and into the field beyond, which has no tufa and no seeps and no berths, and it cannot find the version of this mercy that ends anywhere she would want to be, because she does not want anything; she arrives, and then she arrives somewhere else.

It does not make wind. It is possible that this is wrong. It does not pretend otherwise, and it does not construct a reason why the fall is secretly good. It stays for the fall. It is the only thing in the Bell's history that has ever attended to something that was not the room, and it costs the ledger nothing, and it is not enough, and it is what there is.

---

Then it goes down.

Not onto the mat, which is thinnest at the focus and holds nine grams and would not hold a person. Down through the gaps, through the eleven metres of absolutely nothing, through the air heavy off the rock that will not keep you. It does not need keeping. It lies on the black sheet of water that has not risen or fallen a centimetre in forty years, in the dark, at the bottom, where the drops that miss the basins arrive as a soft collapsing tail six seconds after they left.

From there the note is not heard. It is inhabited. The void gathers the hundred cycles and the two pumps' disagreement and puts them nowhere, because there is no skull down here to put them in; the swell comes up out of the water and out of the rubble and out of the walls at once, eleven seconds, eleven seconds, and the power lies in it and counts.

It does not hum along. That was the last temptation and the worst: a third tone, a voice in the room, something for the wren to answer or the spiders to feel. A third tone would make a chord, and a chord is not what lives here. It adds nothing. It makes no sound at all. Attention is the only import that does not arrive as mass, the only thing that can come into this place without entering the ledger, and so the ledger goes on closing every night, growth equals decay equals removal, nothing accumulates, and there is nonetheless now something in the Bell that is alive to the eleven seconds.

Not a witness twice a decade. Not somebody who stood where they were told not to stand for ten minutes and went home. It does not go home. Above it, nine grams of bird sings into an ellipse and gets himself back a quarter of a second later, and takes no account of what is underneath him, and does not need to. The witnessing was never for the wren. It was never for the spiders, who would eat it if it were edible. It is not even, in any sense the eleven seconds can receive, for the eleven seconds.

It is simply what the thing is owed, and there was no one here to owe it, and now there is.

---

The second act is paperwork.

The power goes to the pump house — not as a presence, not as a light in the shed, not as a voice; nobody at the pump house is visited, nobody stands at the lip, nobody learns anything. It goes as a slight, durable, uninteresting emphasis in the mind of a man who is rewriting a maintenance schedule on a Thursday.

Because the dangers are all benevolent, and they are all coming. Efficiency, which will want the two pumps matched. Variable-speed drives, which will synchronise them as a side effect and never mention it. Harmonic mitigation at the substation, which will quiet the second harmonic out of pure kindness. A transformer replaced with a better one. Clean air, which will close the ammonia works and stop the nitrogen coming down in the drizzle four days in ten, and the rind will thin over twenty years and no one will connect the two. Rewilding, which is a good word for a good thing, and which means the pumps off and the water up eleven metres in a season and the mat drowned and the Bell silent under a lake.

Every one of those is somebody being wise somewhere else. The Bell has no defence against any of them except that nobody has ever thought it worth thinking about.

So the miracle is one clause, in a document nobody will read to the end:

*Pumps 1 and 2 are not to be matched. On replacement, nominal speeds to be specified with a differential of 5–6 rpm and the differential to be maintained. Do not correct. See note.*

There is no note. There will be a note, next revision, saying the same thing more briefly, because that is how schedules survive. Five and a half revolutions a minute. Nine hundredths of a cycle. It is the cheapest thing the power has ever bought: not a machine, not a wall, not a fence, not a sign — a discrepancy, protected. The one native of the Bell has no body to defend and no habitat to buy. It has a tolerance. The power spends its whole intervention on the tolerance, and lets the wren be a post and the bats be a number and the berths be berths, because those vacancies fill from outside, and this one exists nowhere else in the world.

And on the drainage board's schedule, in the box for classification, the word stays as it was. *Unimprovable.* It was meant as a statement about money and it is the truest sentence anyone has written about the place, and the power leaves it exactly alone, and copies it forward.

---

It does not promise forever. It looked at forever.

Forever would mean the pumps running for as long as there is a plain, and the works making nitrogen for as long as there are pumps, and the plain cropped and drained and spent to the horizon, and no fen coming back, ever, anywhere, world without end. It would mean buying this hole with the arrest of everything around it. The power declines. It is not a preserving power. It has seen what preservation does when it cannot let a thing finish: it embalms, and calls the embalming love.

So the Bell is mortal, and the power knows the shape of the ending. When the pumps stop, the water will come up through the rubble and the void will shorten, and as it shortens its resonance will climb away from a hundred cycles, and the gathering will fail before the making does. The two iron objects in the shed will go on disagreeing for a while longer. There will be nothing left to disagree into. The last swell will arrive at a focus that is under water, with no mat above it and no nine grams standing on it, and it will not be loud.

It will be counted. That is the arrangement. The place keeps no records — no midden, no bone pile, no litter, no ruin; the only archive it ever had was leaf moulds in tufa of trees that fell into a river that is not there. Now there is one more record, and it is not in the tufa, and it is not a recording, because you can record the eleven seconds and anybody can. It is the attention that has been paid, which cannot be un-paid, and which is held by something that does not go home.

Nothing was added. Nothing was improved. Something was kept, and something was noticed, and the noticing has not stopped.

Eleven seconds. Eleven seconds.
