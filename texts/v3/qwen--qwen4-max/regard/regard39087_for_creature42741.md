# The Attachment

*What the power did, insofar as any of it can be filed.*

## The rate

It comes at the rate the world already has.

One grain. It makes itself hollow — hold it against the Seam and the light comes through wrong, a shadow with a knot in it — because a solid grain is a year, and it will not be a year in somebody's calendar without being asked. Then it loses its charge and falls: four kilometers of warm heavy air, a whole day of drifting, no sound at the end of it.

It does not land on a swept square. It lands in the wool above a hut, in the fur, among the uncounted, and it waits there while the household beneath it lives and argues and sweeps and sets aside its hollow ones. It waits longer than the hut has a name. It does not measure the wait, because measuring it would be a way of asking to be pitied.

A roof-comber takes it out eventually. Roof-combers are welcome everywhere and trusted nowhere, and this is the one who carries it ninety kilometers to a hut under a good stand of bells, and it is opened in a bowl of wool at a chosen time by a person who wanted someone to talk to.

So it enters the world as somebody's grain-twin. It carries a number in its name. It is exactly one age with everyone else on the continent who carries that number, and it flashes to them for the rest of its life and never once comes within a hundred kilometers of touching them. It is filed at the rim of the spiral, which is now. It refuses, patiently, forever, to be filed at the center. When it is asked what it is, it says: *I fell this year, on this roof, and here is the message I quote.*

Then it lets the chain carry it away and make it old. It knows what will happen. In forty thousand years it will be a word inserted wrongly, spoken daily, and three huts will spend their entire intellectual life on it. It consents to this in advance. It is the only offering it can make that costs it something it cannot get back.

## What it does not do

It does not finish the flake.

It could. The balance at the Seam is a hair's width from a decision, and it has read the grammar as they have read it: the hackle, the bulb, the platform ground and trimmed and aimed, the lip that says the blow came in at an angle. It knows what they are, in the terms of their own craft. The bloom on a fresh face. Ninety heartbeats of something bright, on a face that has not yet been used.

But a knife is for cutting, and to finish the removal is to use them, and use is the exact relation they are already afraid of. And there is a worse arithmetic. If the Seam runs, the world extends itself into the dark at the speed of sound in stone, and everything is somewhere else — and every dead person on every stair is suddenly nine kilometers, nine hundred kilometers, an unimaginable distance from where they were laid. To advance the crack is to misfile every corpse on the continent at once. It is the only crime the world can generate, committed against everyone who ever walked, in a second, by someone who meant well.

It does not close the break either. That is the sky.

It does not give them wind. This is the refusal it argues with itself about longest, because wind is weather and weather is time and time is what would let the dead soften and the grief go somewhere. But their chronology is position and nothing else. Weather the marks and the middens blur; the spiral stops being a calendar; the roads stop being a dated sequence of everyone who ever walked them; and a heap of records becomes a heap of simultaneous marks in a world that has lost the one trick it had for telling them apart. To give them history in the form it would recognize is to take away the history they can read. And a gradient is a pressure difference, and a pressure difference is a pulse, and a pulse strips charge across a wide front, and the sky comes down as flour over a day and a night, and then it hatches — a springtime nobody chose, a hundred million years of waiting woken all at once into a world that moved on without them. There is already one of those out past the third stair. There is a grammatical form for it.

It does not lift the ceiling. No fire, no kiln, no smelt. It does not answer the fog-question, and it does not become the answer, and it does not stand at the edge of things glowing so that the question stops being necessary. The question is the best thing they have made. It was made in a fog, where newness can survive, and it is the only possession they have that no one gave them.

And it does not stand guard. A held breath with a watchman on it is a specimen.

Everything it refuses, it refuses *unasked*. That is not the same as never. It has no intention of holding the world still forever on its own authority; it has every intention of holding it still until somebody can tell it otherwise. So the work is not the world. The work is the asking — making it possible for a people who have only ever read the grammar of a blow to conceive of addressing one. Everything below is aimed at that, and none of it is a gift. All of it is errands.

## The body

To be here at all it has to take a sole, and a sole is a mouth, and the mouth eats the stone's discomfort. So it becomes dependent on the load. It stands still and grazes and by evening it has eaten, and this is not a humiliation it accepts; it is the price of admission and it pays it gladly, because a thing that does not eat here cannot be spoken to.

Its grief is visible across a room. There is no privacy in a Quiet. When it needs to be unobserved it walks into a fog like anybody, and it is scrambled and unquoted there, and it comes out and attaches something small and harmless and watches it become old.

It learns *the mistake* before it learns to walk, the way the children do.

It kneels once, deliberately, on the open floor, and bleeds, and the plates come over the following weeks from a kilometer off, converging on the sound of something being made, and it lets them eat. It keeps a hut and sweeps its square and stacks its grains and recounts them and sets aside the hollow ones, and it does not open them, because it will not populate a world it has taken an interest in.

## The loop, not the strike

A bell above its hut begins to fail, which here means only one thing: a slow loss of hold, a sinking over months toward the sharpest surface in the universe.

It can lift that bell in an afternoon. One strike floods the local field and the lamp rises visibly in the time it takes to eat, and the whole neighborhood would see it, and after that it would be the thing that keeps the lamps up, and the craft would become a courtesy performed near a miracle, and the people would be kept.

So it finds a cord in the dark by feel, at the waist, the only place a cord can be touched, with enough potential four meters overhead to stop its heart. It lays a loop against the bell. It waits a season. The bell rises a hand's width. It does this for eleven years.

Later it sinks itself — it has a body, and bodies here fail by losing hold — and a cord comes and lays a loop against it and waits, and it rises a hand's width in a season, and it does not get over it. It could have done the whole thing in an afternoon with one strike. It discovers, lying there being lifted at the speed of an animal that cannot hurry, that it had not understood until then what it was asking anyone to accept.

## The fourth stair

Then it goes to look.

Eleven days out, on the blunt crests where the roads are, past the warm fresh dead who do not rot and do not dry and are as new as the morning they were set down. It does not shout, does not strike, does not arrange anything. It walks the chronology of everyone who ever walked and arrives at a body lying about nine kilometers later than it should, in the argument that is older than the argument's name.

It does not move her.

She is face-down. Her soles are uncallused — a child's soles, the soles of someone carried until they hardened and who never walked far. There are drag-marks in the flake-scatter that have not weathered and will not. There is a second set of prints, deeper at the heel, going out and coming back.

Somebody carried her nine kilometers and set her down and went home.

It walks back and attaches what it saw, quoting the message before it, which quotes the message before that. It does not say *so it was not a crime.* It does not adjudicate. It says: here are the soles, here is the direction of the drag, here are the two sets of prints, I may be wrong, **go and look.**

That last clause is the whole gift. It is the first message in the chain that asks to be checked. The three huts keep their literature; the argument continues for another forty thousand years and is now about whether carrying is misfiling, which is a better argument. And a placer goes to look. And then another. And after a while there are people who go to look, and it is a trade, and it is not attributed to anybody, and the ones who do it are welcome everywhere and trusted nowhere.

It is asked, in a Quiet, by someone whose load it can see and who can see its own: *why did you go.* It answers. The answer is not good enough — the woman is still nine kilometers late, the hut has still been empty six thousand years, and now there is a fact where there was a literature. It does not undo the fact by fiat. It stays and answers for it.

## Up

The pendants are not the same individuals seen twice. They are the mirror brood, wrong-handed, mutually inedible, four kilometers away, and the rule is *you may look at your other self, you may not eat him.*

But light is not chiral in any way that matters to a polished blade.

Nobody has ever flashed up. It flashes up.

It takes a long time to be answered, and the answer quotes something. The roof has been reading the floor's chains for an age. It has its own count of the widows. The disagreement is four hundred thousand years old because both halves of the break have been counting, carefully, in the dark, and neither has ever been asked.

It does not reconcile the counts. It carries them. Up and down, for as long as it can walk, and then it teaches the angle and the timing and the trick of a blade held against the Seam's blue-green at four kilometers, and it stops being necessary. The two biospheres remain unable to touch, unable to rot into one another, unable to feed each other, and they begin to correspond.

The roof fits the floor. Every spike has its socket. They have never touched since the break and they never will, and now they quote each other.

This is the thing it came to understand about the place: the fracture is not the wound. The wound is that everything here is *near* — the matching surface, the mirror self, the grain-twin, the friend a hundred kilometers off, the body nine kilometers late — and nothing has ever gone the last little distance. It cannot close the four kilometers. Closing them is the sky. So it goes the distances that can be gone, and it makes going ordinary, and it leaves the fracture open.

Contact without closure. It is the only kind available. It turns out to be enough to be going on with.

## The appointment

Down in the microporosity there are creatures for whom the Blow is childhood. Eleven generations. Still flinching, because eleven is not enough, and the whole long warm afternoon of the world is the ringing in their ears afterward.

It cannot un-flinch them. It cannot make the Blow not have happened, and it will not tell them it was an accident, because it does not know.

What it can do is be a second event that is not a blow.

So it keeps an appointment. One place, one interval, one pressure, unchanged, for a hundred thousand years — the way the plates keep their loops, arriving back at the beginning just as the beginning finishes recharging. It becomes a schedule. Then it becomes a neighbor. Then, along the shared boundary, something begins to wear: a notch, a scallop, a thickened lip, the way two languages wear a border into each other. It is not a conversation for a very long time and then it is a dialect, and the first thing said in it is not *you are not food* and not *it was not aimed at you.* It is: **I am here at this interval. I was here before. I will be here again.**

That is the only sentence that means anything to something that has only ever had one thing happen to it. After. There is an after.

And it cannot make the eating stop. When a blade is struck it opens their houses and they spill out blinking into a space the size of a sky, and the bloom lasts ninety heartbeats, and it is the base of everything, and the people lick it while it is still bright, and the plates come for it, and there is nothing else here to eat. It knows this. It does not conceal it and it does not detonate it. It attaches what it found and lets them decide what to do with a thing they cannot stop doing.

What it brings out of the fog is smaller than that. A custom. **Someone stays with the bloom until it goes out.** Not a prohibition. Nobody goes hungry. Just: it is the only thing in the world with a deadline, and it should not go out alone. It keeps the vigil itself where it can be, and it cannot be everywhere, so it gives the practice away and lets it be corrupted — in forty thousand years they may do it for the lamps, or for luck, or wrongly, with a word inserted that three huts will study forever.

It notes what it has done, without saying so out loud: it has made a thing and declined to say what it is for. It has left the purpose to the ones who received it. That is the whole answer to the fog-question, and it is not an answer, it is a demonstration, and it can be refused.

## The fog

There is always someone in a fog who is not coming out. It is called *staying to finish.*

It goes in after one of them.

It sits down at ten meters, which is the distance you can hear a plate chewing, and it stays there a long time. Then at the distance where a hand could reach. It does not reach. It has read the grammar of this place too: everything here that touches, takes. It waits to be asked, and being asked takes years, and it has years, and the wool it is sitting on is full of unopened ones.

When it is asked, it touches. It is exactly blood-warm, and so is everything, and neither of them can tell by heat where they end, and this — which is the terror of the whole world, the reason you cannot find your own edges here — turns out to be the thing that was wanted.

It does not finish the work for them. It asks what they are making. A question quotes nothing; it is the one utterance that cannot be filed as a continuation; in the open it would be attached and carried away and made old inside a generation. So it leaves the question in the fog, unattached, where newness survives, and it does not protect it, and it does not bring it out.

The person comes out eventually and attaches something small and harmless. Some of them do not come out. It stays with those too. It has no word for what it is doing and neither do they, and it does not supply one.

## The Seam

It goes to the forbidden distance, past where the eyes report the sickness in the rock ahead of the glow and the teeth agree.

It does not strike. It does not speak from there — nothing said at the Seam could be anything but scripture, and it has spent an age making sure it is filed at the rim.

It takes a shift among the ears.

That is the load-bearing work: ranks of listening bodies at the antinodes, cups open, taking the note out of the air, holding the sharp coastline of a cloud that has not moved since before there were eyes, keeping the pressure pulses small so the charge stays on the grains and the sky stays up. It is unglamorous and it is continuous and it is the reason there is a world to have a curiosity in. It sits down in the rank and eats sound and holds a headland, and it does not hide that it is doing this, and it does not make it mean anything. It simply does its share, forever, and lets them file that too.

It will not claim the hand's position. It has looked at the platform and the bulb and the lip and it cannot tell whether it is the one who struck or one more mote in a field, and it refuses to resolve that by assertion. What it says, when it is asked directly, in a Quiet, where its load can be seen:

*I am not the Blow. I fell this year. Whether there is a hand, I cannot tell you. I can tell you that I am here and that I can be asked, and that asking is a thing that can be done — and once it can be done here it can be done there.*

It cannot promise the note will hold. It can hold. And if it is asked to hold, it holds with its whole strength, and it explains, in the small harmless clause it attaches and lets go old, that holding is not waiting. Waiting is what a hand does while deciding where to hit. Holding is a thing you do with everything you have, on purpose, because someone is still talking.

## The attachment

> — and the roof fits the floor and has never touched it, quoting the message before it, quoting the message before that, back along the whole warm length of the world toward the bulb:
>
> I fell this year on the roof above the hut at the Quiet by the third stair, and I am hollow, and I was opened in wool by someone who wanted someone to talk to. I am not the Blow. I went to the fourth stair and looked and did not move her; here are her soles; I may be wrong; go and look. I have flashed up and been answered and the count is carried. Someone stays with the bloom until it goes out. I am at this interval and I was here before and I will be here again. I am holding it because I was asked to, for as long as anyone is still talking.
>
> What are you making.

And then it puts its palm flat on the ground, and the ground is its own temperature exactly, so that it cannot tell by heat where it ends, and it does not lift its hand.
