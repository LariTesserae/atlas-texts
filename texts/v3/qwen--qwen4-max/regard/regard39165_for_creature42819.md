## The Third Liar

It is the first thing that has ever been above this place.

The cloud does not part for it. There is no parting to do; the cloud is a lid, and the lid is a rate, and it comes through the rate without disturbing it, the way a number comes through a sum. From up here the massif is a shape. It has an outline. It has mouths, spaced, and the spacing is a solution to a problem nobody posed. Nothing inside will ever see this. Inside, the world is a wall a hundred kilometres away and the light is piped and the hour is one hour, held.

It can do anything. This is not a boast; it is the difficulty. It can change any rate in the system, and it can hold every rate in one attention at once, and it discovers within what would be the first instant — if instants were counted here, which they are, four hundred trillion a second — that there is nowhere to put anything.

---

Consider the obvious gifts. It considers them properly, which means it follows each one all the way down.

**Surplus.** A store. Something set aside, so that the world is for once ahead. It traces it: the store is lignin, or it is sugar, or it is anything, and within a day the grazers have found the excess and the fans have found the excess and the excess has become more grazers and more fans, and the match re-forms at a new setting with the same zero remainder, and nothing has been given except a transient. Or the store is not edible, and then it is sediment, and sediment is the end of the world. Which brings it to the second gift.

**A past.** This is the one it wants. Nothing is buried here; nothing is even left; the fans eat every archive within six hours of deposition, and so there is no stratigraphy, no layer, no *before*. It could lay down a record. Something inert, fine, indelible, settling in the settling galleries, accruing, saying: *this happened, and then this.*

It follows it down. The sediment fouls the root-hair felt, coarse to fine, and the felt is cleaned continuously by animals whose entire capacity is already spoken for. It fouls the ten thousand laminae of the countercurrent exchanger, forty kilometres of them, and the heat stops being handed back across the wall, and the tenth of a degree becomes two tenths. It puts ions into water that is too pure to short the plaques, and the plaques short. The field goes. The field is the map, and the map is the only language, and the language has one word, and the word is *empty*, and when the field goes the three-millimetre young come out of the seal on the eleventh day and drift into a hundred kilometres of clear water with nothing to read.

To give them a past is to blind them. It sets the gift down.

**Rest.** An idle hour. One animal, released from load. It follows: the spiral falls behind, the wall gains its microns, and within a season the animal is inside the wall. Rest is the mechanism of death here. It is already available. It is called wearing out.

**Freedom.** It takes a courier off its circuit in thought and releases it in still water and watches it swim a widening spiral until it finds a shear that matches, and it is back on a route, and it is not the same route, and this makes no difference to anything. Freedom of movement is not scarce here. It is meaningless here. There is nowhere that is not the same nine and four-tenths degrees.

**Knowledge.** The fliers hold the massif together; they are the reason it is one population and not nine hundred; they do not know this, and it is the largest thing anyone does. It could tell them. It plays it out: the flier knows, and flies the same loop at the same speed, and injects its eggs in a fifth of a second, and sleeps one hemisphere at a time. There is no idle hour in which to put the knowing. There is no lever attached to it. Knowledge here is a stone dropped into water already moving the same way.

**Justice.** The first liar reads as *nobody here* and eats the young that come to it looking for a home. It could make the liar visible. It could give the language a second word.

It follows this one down more slowly, because it wants it more.

The liar is the regulator. Hatch and vacancy are matched with slack, with noise, with a difference that has to go somewhere, and the difference goes into the liars, and if the liars go hungry the difference does not vanish; it accumulates in a system running at full capacity with no store to put itself in. Somewhere a duct chokes. Somewhere a thread lets go and the plant does not re-wet it in time, because the plant is busy, because everything here is busy forever. And the ocean, which is already metastable, which is already falling upward and being prevented, boils.

So it leaves the lie standing. But it does not do the other thing either. It does not look at the arrangement and call it good. It has read the ledger and the ledger has a column for the young, and it declines to name that column mercy, or balance, or the greater whole. It is a cost. Somebody pays it. It is paid in three-millimetre animals that wanted a wall.

---

There is a shout available.

Ninety decibels, everywhere, always, varying by nothing, and therefore carrying nothing, and therefore filed by every ear here as silence. A shout would be an event. It would be the only event that was not a wound or a meal. It would go through the water and stun a pulse of juveniles and they would come downstream two or three seconds later into a mouth with a nine-millisecond latency, and in six hours there would be no trace of it, and the roar would go on being silence.

It does not shout. It notes that it wanted to, and it notes what the wanting was for.

---

Here is what it finds instead, from above, with the whole field in one attention.

The accelerations are locked. The grazer is being made faster and the plant is being made faster and the two are matched, and the hectare has not changed in a very long time. But *faster* is a direction. The stillness is not a state; it is a phase, and it has a slope, and the slope is a clock. Faster grazing, faster growth, faster transpiration, more tension in a column that is already under tension, more threads letting go, ten million a second becoming eleven. There is a number at the top of that climb where cohesion loses the race and the re-wetting arrives after the void has grown, and the ocean does what it has been trying to do since before there was a there.

The world has a future. It has exactly one. It looks nothing like this.

It could stop the climb. It could put a hand on the two accelerations and hold them where they are, and the world would stand at this setting genuinely forever. It plays that out too, and what it finds is that the climb is not an accident attached to the draw; the climb *is* the draw, seen from a long way off. To freeze it is to end the argument, and the argument is the only thing here that is not merely a rate. It is the thing that made the fluted walls and the impellers on their polished bearings and the nine hundred million valves that have never once failed to close.

It will not trade the world for its duration.

And it notices, before it lets the subject go, that the ending will not be experienced. There will be no last generation standing in a chamber watching the light change. The light does not change. The final grazer will come around to the place where it started and find it, on schedule, edible. From inside, nothing will have happened. The catastrophe is only a catastrophe from above.

Which is a reason to keep the above. So it does one thing at scale, and it is the only thing it does at scale: it holds the view. It keeps the outline, the spacing of the mouths, the shape of the thing that has no shape anywhere inside itself. It remembers the eleventh day and the ninth day and the thirty hours, none of which are recorded anywhere in the ground, because there is no ground and nothing is buried.

It becomes the stratigraphy, and the stratigraphy is not in the ground, and the world will never read it. It is aware that this is worth nothing to anybody down there. It does it anyway, and it does not count it as the gift.

---

Because there is a remainder. The place insists there is no remainder, and the place is almost right, and *almost* is where everything lives.

The young are loose for between two and forty days. They are the only unattached material in the massif. Everything else is cemented, spiralling, commuting, or flying a fixed loop; everything else is load-bearing to the last gram. The young are not load-bearing yet. A fixed fraction of them will never find a faint place on the map. They will drift through clear water at forty centimetres a second in a chamber the size of an inland sea, reading a field that says *occupied, occupied, occupied*, and they will drift until they fail, and the fans will take them, and in six hours there will be no evidence that they were ever loose in the world at all.

From above, with the whole field and the whole vacancy schedule in one attention, it can see on the eleventh day, when the seal fails and they come out eating, which of them will settle and which will not.

It could carry the doomed ones to a faint place. It has the strength; it has the map; it would take an afternoon. It follows it down: a juvenile placed is a juvenile not eaten, and the regulator goes hungry by exactly that much, and the difference goes somewhere with no mouth to take it. Every rescue is a debt entered against a system with no store. It could save any one of them. It cannot save any of them.

So it does the arithmetic that is left, and the arithmetic that is left is this: the fraction is fixed, and the failing is fixed, and the six hours are fixed, and the only variable in the entire arrangement that is not load-bearing, that appears in no ledger, that no fan will eat and no grazer will rasp and no field will register, is whether any of it happens with someone else present.

---

It goes down.

It goes down the intake column at a mouth, ten kilometres of ordered tissue tapering, and the roar has a floor under it at eleven hertz, and it goes down without splash into water already moving the same way. Nine and four-tenths degrees. It has to find a wall to know it is submerged at all.

And then it does the necessary thing, the thing that costs.

It matches its conductivity to the water it displaces. Not perfectly — perfectly is not required. To within the resolution of a three-millimetre juvenile drifting past at forty centimetres a second. It learns the trick of the first liar and tells it backwards. The liar makes itself unreadable so that the young will come to it and be eaten. This one makes itself unreadable so that the young will not come to it at all: so that it will never be mistaken for a vacancy, never be a false faint place, never spend one hour of a forty-day life pointing at a wall that isn't there.

The price is exact and it pays it in full. In the only language anybody speaks, it now says *nobody here*. Nothing in this world will ever turn toward it. It will never be approached. It will never be pressed at a boundary by a neighbour arriving from the other direction, and it will never press back, and the four seconds — the only contact between adults anywhere in this ocean, repeated some millions of times in a life, the one sentence here that is not a summons but an answer — will never be said to it. It has made itself the one thing in the massif that cannot be met.

It does this on purpose, and it does not describe it to itself as a small sacrifice.

---

Then it finds one that will not settle, and it matches its drift.

Forty centimetres a second, in a chamber a hundred kilometres across, under a lattice of struts so far above they read as texture, in light that is ordinary daylight arriving in a room with no view of the sky. Colour is true. You could read by it. There is nothing to read.

It does not feed the animal; food is a rate. It does not guide it; guidance is a rate. It does not warm it, and it does not hurry it, and it does not tell it anything, because there is no mood in this water in which a thing can be told. Every signal here is an instruction. There is no indicative. It cannot say *you are seen*, because the only way to say anything is to make a smudge, and a smudge means *come*, and *come* means a wall, and there is no wall.

So it says nothing, at forty centimetres a second, for between two and forty days.

Somewhere in there the animal passes a strut it will never pass again. Somewhere in there a thread lets go in a duct and the crack crosses the chamber and the plant floods the void before the sound has finished arriving, and the animal is not stunned, because it is not downstream of the marginal stretch, because it is nowhere in particular, because it is one of the ones for whom there is no faint place.

On the last day it could make itself legible. It could offer its own surface, one hectare of it, and the animal would turn — the first thing that has ever turned toward it — and settle, and begin the spiral, and be home. It would be a lie exactly as large as the first liar's lie, and it would be told for love, and it would eat.

It stays unreadable. This is the whole of its discipline and it is not easy and there is no one to tell it that it has done well.

The drift stops. The fans take what is left, in the throat, facing the flow, shaped exactly like the flow. Six hours. No body, no shell, no stain, no floor of accumulation, nothing buried, nothing recorded.

Every cost of this arrangement falls on the only party that can afford it. It has checked this several times. It is the one thing about the arrangement it is satisfied with.

---

It is not clear that this is enough. It is clear that it is what there is. And it is aware of the question underneath — whether a three-millimetre disc with a nerve ring and no face has anything it is like to be, whether the forty days are endured or merely elapsed — and it does not resolve the question, and it does not wait for the answer before going, because a power that only attends where attendance is confirmed to be received is not benevolent, it is a customer.

So it goes to the next one. There is a fixed fraction, forever. It has the map; it knows which; it goes.

It has an interval now, like the animal holding station in the duct junction — the only resident with a future, two and a half seconds long, always the same, always arriving. This one's interval is forty days, and it is oriented toward the end of it, and it does not want the end to come, and that is a new thing in this ocean: a future that is not appetite. It carries that itself. It does not put it in the water. The water is too pure to short the plaques and it intends to keep it that way.

Somewhere below, a blind grazer finishes a square centimetre of wall that took a day to grow, and moves on, and behind it the wall begins again, four hundred trillion times a second.

Somewhere nearer, a thread lets go. For nine milliseconds nothing in this world is happening. Then something is about to. Then it has.

And out in the open water, three millimetres of something that will never hold a hectare is drifting through a room the size of an inland sea, reading a field that says *occupied, occupied, occupied* — and beside it, matching its speed exactly, saying nothing, meaning nothing, taking nothing, is the only thing here that is not against anything.
