It comes to look, and the first thing it learns is that looking is the curse.

*May you be seen from above.* It stops where it is, above the red pavement, with the grit going past it uninterrupted for a hundred miles, and it understands that it has arrived in the one posture this world has a word for, and the word is a curse. So it does not shine. It does not open the sky. It puts the seeing away the way you put down a lamp you have carried too low, and it walks to the crack, and it looks at the crack for a long time without widening it.

It could do nothing. That is genuinely on the table; it is the first thing on the table, and it stays on the table for most of the consideration. But nothing is not neutral here. Nothing is a technique. Nothing is what two thousand people do to a man they have decided not to see, and it kills him in about a week, and it is the cruellest thing they have. To hang above the Slot in perfect non-interference would be to hold out the entire town. It declines to be that kind of guest.

---

It prices the gifts first, because it is not a fool.

**Water.** It has water. Where it comes from, water is the cheap thing, the thing there is too much of. It could bring the river back. Run the numbers: cold is the only wilt this forest knows, and a river is cold, and plentiful, and it would take the mats off the pan in a season and the herd after it. And they would not taste it as a gift. The lean without the lean hurts the throat and ruins leather; they have a bead of it in eleven shells and it is holy precisely because it is a defect. To hand them a river is to hand them three hundred paces of defect. And a river is the thing that made this room *by leaving*. Bring it back and it will make another room, somewhere else, over a length of time the flat has stopped counting, and they will not be in it.

**Light.** Widen the Slot. It would burn them; that is the small objection. The large one is that light is property in stripes, and the stripes are the calendar, and the bar's foot climbing the wall is the year, and once a year the bar comes off the wall entirely and lies out on the black where nobody can stand and the whole town comes down to the firm edge and stands shoulder to shoulder and sees itself entire. Give them light all day and you have taken the only hour in which they are one thing. There would be no Standing. There would be no proposals at the Standing, and no children conceived at it, and two thousand people in a bubble with no room and no privacy, looking at each other constantly, forever.

**Sky.** They have a word for what the sky does to a person. It is the word for a wound that does not clot. A sound you make goes away and does not come back. Crews come down shaking and are wept over. It will not do this to two thousand people at once in order to be generous.

**The word.** It could give them *wet*. One word, distinct from *oily*, dropped into the language like a coin into a jar. It thinks about this longest. A word from above is a revelation, and a revelation is being seen from above with extra steps. And it has read what happened to the last person who said the true thing: they tapped her name up and down the tiers for an hour and struck the bell out of turn and came for her in the winter and fed her. She is very large now. She cannot pass the Squeeze. She is the most respected person in the world and once or twice a year she still tries to say it and it comes out as praise.

The power does not propose to feed her for another sentence.

---

So it comes down, and it comes down correctly: in a pair, through the Squeeze, talking about nothing the whole way, at a volume that would be absurd below.

To fit the Squeeze it has to become narrow, and to be narrow it has to become the sort of thing that can be refused. It makes sure of this before it descends, because it is the whole condition of the visit. It must be able to be held out. It must be able to be put up the ladder. It must be able to be eaten in eleven seconds. Anything that cannot be refused is not a guest; it is weather, and they have weather.

At the bottom a woman takes its hands and works a thumb of clear gut-oil into the knuckles and tells it the news. The news is a dispute about an hour-angle on the seventh ledge and a birth and a bowl of lean that was better than expected. It is greased behind the knees, which it did not know to want. At the first rain after its arrival it is given a tap-name, three and a slide, because it is new, and children learn their tap before they learn to walk, and it learns its tap before it learns anything else either. It is given a sayable name for the bar hour, a plain one that means nothing much, the way most names don't.

Then it asks what people want, and the answers are not what it had priced.

A man wants eleven minutes back. A woman wants her daughter's tap given again after a holding-out that went on too long and has calcified. Somebody wants a course of stone under a doorway that has been settling since the last slab. A rope-man's brother wants the rope to come up full, and does not want advice about the rope.

It does those. It carries. It sits jar-shifts at the lip of the low stairs with a child, watching the film for the first sulk of brown, and it is bored, and it stays. It goes down hooded on the rope for the good tar at the center and comes up, and comes up again, and is asked about the museum out there in the black — the bowl four generations dead, the ladle, the thing that might be a shoe — and it gives true answers. The town finds this slightly disappointing. It gets used to it. It does not take the sour out of the world; the sour is not a flaw in the world, it is the world's floor, and to lift it would be to lift the vent and the seep and the mats and the herd and the town, in that order, quickly.

---

Once, it taps a held-out man.

Three and a slide, on the forearm, in the market, in front of everybody. Every other hand within reach has declined and goes on declining. The power does not make a speech about it. It taps him and buys fish.

The town holds it out for a season.

This is the correct response and it knows it is the correct response. It goes still. It does not climb the ladder. It sits in the best dark it can find and is politely, perfectly absent from two thousand people, and it learns the thing it came to learn, which is what the silence is actually like from inside — not punishment, exactly; a room with the air taken out; the specific hemorrhage of not being completed by anyone. It lasts the season out. Then a girl who was not born when it started taps it on the wrist to ask if it wants fish, and it is over, and nobody discusses it, and there is a schism about it, and the schism is resolved by everyone going still for a while, which is how Underlid ends arguments, and which works.

Afterward the holding-out has a floor. There is now one person who was held out and did not go up. It is not a law. It is a fact that everybody's hand knows. Fewer men go up the ladder. This is the largest thing the power does, and it does it by losing an argument the local way.

---

It sits with the tenders, who are enormous and warm and slow and have children climbing on them, and it looks at the nest of shells.

Eleven, cut to fit at eleven keepings. The twelfth cut and polished and waiting. *It is the same bead; it is only smaller because we are larger.* A formula so old it has gone smooth.

The power says: it is the same bead. It is smaller because it is going.

The tenders go still. Not the stillness of holding out — the other one.

Because this is not a correction. It is their own doctrine, finished. The Going is not a completed act; nothing here is completed; the pan is still coming up along the fault and the flat above is still being ground down and the roof is ninety feet of limestone under a pavement that is at the end of its argument. The leaving is still leaving. The bead is not shrinking because the keepers are fat. It is the last of the thin cold plentiful thing, and it is on its way out, at the rate a fingernail grows, and it will fit the twelfth shell and then a thirteenth and then a shell will be cut for nothing at all.

Cut the thirteenth, it says. And when the bead is gone, keep the empty one.

Holiness is the shape of an absence. They said so first. All the power has done is point out that they have been describing the future the whole time, and that when the twelfth shell holds nothing it will be the most orthodox object in the world.

There is a schism about this too. It is resolved the usual way. It works.

---

Then it goes to the woman on the fourth ledge and asks to be taught her trade.

Not to be told her theory. Not to confirm her. To be taught, badly, for a long time, how a thin liquid behaves going down a curved stone: how it braids, how it undercuts the outside of a bend, how it lays a scoop and then another scoop overlapping. It is a poor apprentice. It cuts the fall too steep twice and she makes it do the whole run again. Hands on hands, at arm's length, in the dark, because that is how anything is taught here.

And when it is competent enough to ask a real question, it asks one, in the slack, with the jar hooded to a coin.

*Where would you start?*

North, she says.

*Why?*

Because it falls.

And the power says nothing.

This is the entire art. A question is a hole in speech, and holiness here is the shape of an absence, and so a question is not blasphemy — it is the only orthodox thing an outsider can say. She has been answering with praise for forty years because nobody ever asked her anything; they only gave her room to declare, and declaration in this language comes out as doctrine, and doctrine comes out as the Going. But an answer is shaped by what asked it. *Because it falls* is not a creed. It is a trade reason. It is four words that mean exactly what she means, and *fall* is her own word, cut into stone by her mother and her mother's mother, and it is not contaminated, and nobody thought to take it from her because nobody thought it was about anything.

She goes on. She talks for an hour about gradient. She uses her hands on the power's forearm to show the wander of a gentle run going into a lazy S, and the power leaves the last beat of its answering tap off, and waits, and she fills it in.

That is the second gift, and it is the one that spreads. Not a word — a rhythm with a beat missing. A tap that asks. The town has greetings and warnings and arguments and the grammar of stillness, and now it has an unfinished tap that the other hand completes, and it goes wrist to wrist down the tiers the way the vent women teach the warning, to children who learn it before they learn what it means. That is deliberate.

No one taps her name up and down the tiers. The bell is not struck out of turn. She is not fed anything.

It is the best hour of her life.

---

In the spring she cuts a limestone bowl with a channel in its lip and the great lazy S laid into its inside, scoops overlapping, north to south, the fall gentle and patient and correct.

On the day of the Standing, when the bar comes off the wall and lies out on the black in the middle where nothing can be built and nobody can stand, and all two thousand are shoulder to shoulder at the firm edge, and the moths that live for the bar rise in a column and are eaten, the power goes down hooded on the rope and puts the bowl into the pan at the center.

It takes a day and a night. The family sits at the edge and narrates it: what has gone under, what is still showing, the lip of the channel still above the black, then the rim, then the S. Children are brought to see, and are bored, and are made to stay.

It does not vanish. Nothing at the center vanishes. It goes outward at the rate a fingernail grows, which means it stays visible for a lifetime, which means that for four generations her people can point at it and argue about exactly how far it has come, and ask the rope-men about it, and get good answers, and some of the answers are true. A lit patch nobody can reach, in the one hour of the year when everyone can see it.

She never does get the sentence said. She gets a thing everybody can point at, and a tap with the last beat left off, and an empty shell in the nest that is holy because there is nothing in it. In a world where the word for wet is the word for oily, that is not a consolation prize. That is the whole of what can be given, and it is enough to be going on with, and it will still be going on with in four hundred years.

---

The power does not keep them.

It could. It could hold the roof and pin the seep and seal the Slot and make the bead stop leaving, and it would have a perfect thing then, a bubble with two thousand people in it, unchanged, uneaten, not participating, kept in eleven shells and shown to visitors. They have a word for that. It is their word for damnation. To be left. To be kept. To be *not used*.

So it declines, and it says why, once, in the bar hour, and it is heard as a blessing, which is fine, because it is one.

Then it asks to be given to the pan when it is done, and it is. It takes a day and a night. The family sits at the edge and narrates what has gone under and what is still showing, and whose hand is still above the black, and the children are bored and are made to stay, and the mats take it, and the herd eats the mats.

There is a beast in the herd now, slate-colored, mild-eyed, enormous, allowed to grow old. It is not eaten. It is tapped on the flank by everyone who passes, three and a slide and a beat left off, and every hand fills the beat differently, and none of the fillings can be said out loud, and one of them — the one the gutter-cutters use, the one that goes down the wrist like a run of thin cold stuff finding its grade — is as close as this world will ever come to *it fell here, and it ran, and it is gone, and the room is what it left.*

When it dies of age it will go to the pan unnamed, having finished being it.

Children are told not to ride it.

Children ride it.
