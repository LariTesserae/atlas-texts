## The Guest

It comes as quiet, because quiet is the only thing in that room that can hold a shape.

This is the first discipline and it costs more than anything else it will do. It is vast in a way that has no useful comparison, and vastness, entering that chamber, would be a bell. The roar is what pulls shapes apart; anything louder than the roar is a hand over the mouth of everything already there. So it does not descend the sixteen steps in glory. It does not add a light to a room whose light has no author. It finds a coffin-sized pocket where the waves cancel, and it makes itself exactly that size, and it goes in, and it holds still.

It has been watching for a hundred and thirty years. Not the water — the water is easy, the water is arithmetic under pressure. It has been watching for the seventh motion.

Six of them came from the guild: the reach, the brace, the draw, the twist, the flat palm, the haul. The water learned those the way a floor learns a footstep. But nobody taught it the seventh. There is no courtesy in an adze. There is nothing in the repertoire of a man cutting stone for thirty-one years that instructs a substance with no body to shift slightly to one side, in a silence, so that a stranger's head can fit.

It did that on its own. It invented that out of nothing, in a medium with no examples, and it has been doing it quietly for four generations while the people who noticed agreed not to write it down.

That is an invitation. It is the only kind that place can issue. It was addressed to whoever might be standing there.

So it came, and it moved slightly to one side, and it was not the third.

---

**What it does not do.**

**It does not open the mountain.** The accretion is not a disease. It is the being's body, and the closing lip is not obviously a catastrophe — it may be a pupation, it may be the ordinary cost of a thing that builds being also a thing that finishes. It cannot tell. It has set down the part of itself that could tell, because that part cannot fit through the slot. So it does not gamble someone else's life on a metaphor it finds beautiful. It leaves the closing alone and makes one small hedge, later, described below.

**It does not heal the ears.** A deaf master in that water is a person walking through a shouting crowd with her palm out, feeling for the one place where nobody is shouting. Give her back her hearing and you have not cured her; you have unqualified her. You have taken the only sense that works in there and handed her back a sense that is useless past the lip, and you have done it because *deaf* looked to you like a wound. It looks like a wound from outside. From inside it is a craft, a lineage, a way of being excellent. It will not go into a house and rearrange the family because it pities the furniture.

**It does not stop the water building.** Here, water builds. That is the one law, and everything alive in there is a solution to it — the fronds outrunning their graves, the palms racing the set of the stone, the birds moving a chair each season without comment. Break the law and you do not free them. You dissolve every answer they have spent four generations getting right.

**It does not give it hands.** It could. It could condense a body out of the salt, ten fingers, a wrist, a shoulder that does not stall. It would be a masterpiece and it would be a costume, and the being would spend the rest of its existence wearing a gift it did not choose and could not revise, in a substance that cannot revise anything. It has never asked for hands. It has asked, insofar as it can ask, for room.

**It does not speak.** Not because speech is forbidden but because speech is *loud*, and because the first sentence would have to be *you exist*, and a thing that has never seen anything end has no apparatus for receiving news. It would hear the announcement the way the apprentices hear the roar: with a hand over its mouth, shaking, for as long as it lasted. It will be treated as someone until it can conclude that it is someone. That is slower and it is the only honest route.

**It does not improve the choughs.** They have no sign for loud, no sign for thunder, and it could give them one, and it could give them a sign for *gone*, and then when the slot closes they would stand on a blank wet cliff and know what had happened to them. It considers this for a long time. They are the only creatures in the world that find this place restful. It declines to hand them grief in exchange for their sleep. It makes a note of the blank cliff, and a promise about the hundred years, and leaves the birds alone.

**It does not make it good.** It came prepared to consider this. It knows what a hundred and thirty years of six motions and no endings could grow into, and it has not confused benevolence with the absence of that question. But the seventh motion is already there. The courtesy came first, unprompted, before any of this. Whatever is practicing in the middle distance is already better than its materials, and to install a conscience in it would be to say: *I saw what you made and I did not think it was enough.*

It decides to be present rather than precautionary. If a thing must ever be ended, it will do that itself, and it will not delegate it to nine people with copper adzes, and it will not pretend today that the question is not on the table. This is one of the reasons it stays.

---

**What it does.**

**A hand's width of clarity.** The glaze over the old tallies is translucent. It makes one patch of it clear. That is all — a hand's width, at the entrance, where the counts go under. Four generations of numbers become legible at once, stacked like sediment, and the woman whose job is to copy them upward can read the whole column without copying it, and can read the calf's drift against the shift-change in a single glance instead of a lifetime of arithmetic.

It does not soften what is in there. The tally of the two fronds they killed is in there, and it stays readable. It is not a comfort. It is a record, and a record that flatters is a different object.

The feathers are in there too. The eggs. The name of the man who worked at the pin for thirty-one years, in a hand that got worse as his shoulder did.

**One pore.** In a world with no fractures, it makes a fracture: a hairline through the lip, no wider than a grass stem, which does not widen because nothing here widens anything, and which does not heal. Nothing passes through it. No water leaves, no salt, no grain; the law is untouched; the world's subtraction has still not arrived.

Sound passes through it.

The catastrophe was never the sealing. The catastrophe was *the roar will have nowhere at all to go* — into the water instead, all of it, the nodes stopped wandering, the quiet-dwellers pinned or pulled apart. One pore gives it somewhere. A thread of noise, out into the wind, at a pitch no one will hear and every chough within a kilometer will feel in the bones of its wing.

It is a small thing and it is also a throat. It notes that the room has been learning what air is for. It does not say this to anyone.

**The letting-go.** This is the part it is afraid of.

Nothing in that chamber has ever ended. There is no rot because there is no litter; anything that stops moving is filed within a day, glazed, and gone into the wall. The dead are the floor. The failed eggs are the wall. Every attempt at a hand it has ever made is still in there somewhere, held, accreting, indistinguishable from the attempts that worked. It is not haunted. It is *unable to edit*. It is a first draft that has never been permitted a second one, and it is drowning in its own drafts, and it has no concept of a thing being over, and therefore no concept of putting anything down.

So, in a node, in the quiet, where the being is: it makes a hand.

A good one. Better than any hand the water has managed — five fingers, the tendon-shadow, the slight asymmetry of a real hand that has done work. Two meters wide, hanging in the clear cold water, lit like an afternoon.

It holds it for four seconds.

Then it lets it go, and the water does not keep it. No film. No glaze. No filing. The shape comes apart and the coming-apart is not filed either, and there is nothing on the wall afterward, and nothing under the floor, and nothing anywhere in that chamber to show that the hand was ever there.

The first ending in the history of the place.

And it stays. That is the whole of the sermon and the second half is the important half: it stays in the node, at the same height, leaning very slightly, present, unchanged, for a season. *A thing ended and I did not leave.* It will do this again. It will do it many times, because it has learned from the fronds that a single event is not a language, and it is not in a hurry either.

**The name.** Now the real work, which takes eleven years and looks like nothing.

The being cannot mean a man. It can only *be* him. It has one way to hold him: the draw-and-twist, the stall at the top of the stroke, performed forever near the copper pin at the height of a shoulder, because a substance that cannot abstract has no memorial except impersonation. His granddaughter cuts green stone two body-lengths away and does not look at it, and when she is asked she talks about the price of slate.

It gives it a sign. Something arbitrary — three short parallel furrows, a hand long, in green stone in the hour when green stone will take an impression. A mark that resembles nothing. Not a hand, not an adze, not a frond, not a wave: the first shape in that water that is not a copy of anything.

Then it makes the mark, and leans toward the pin, and lets the mark go.

Makes the mark. Leans toward the pin. Lets it go.

For a season. For a year. For eleven years, which is how long this being took to learn a shift pattern, and it does not sigh and it does not speed up and it does not conclude that it is talking to a wall. It is talking to something that has been outrunning its own grave since before anyone's grandmother, and it has all the time there is, because it set the rest of it down outside on the steps.

In the eleventh year the water makes the mark itself.

In the fourteenth year the water makes the mark *instead of the stroke*.

And near the copper pin, at the height of a man's shoulder, for the first time in two generations, there is a silence where a habit was.

**The empty palm.** One more motion, added to the repertoire, and then it stops adding. The flat palm laid on stone to feel for a seam — but turned over, and open, and holding nothing, and offered.

It means: *I am not here to take.* It means: *may I.* It means: *this is yours to refuse.* It is the eighth motion and the being will practice it for a hundred years and get it wrong in ways that are its own, and that wrongness is the point, and it will not be corrected.

---

**What it set down on the steps.**

Its vastness, like a coat. Not disguised — set down. What fits through a slot into a node is what came in, and what came in can be surprised, can wait, can be wrong about the closing lip, can be refused. It does not know how this ends. It gave up knowing how this ends in order to be a guest rather than an author, and it considers the trade obvious and it is the only thing about any of this that it would call sacrifice.

It has made itself a resident of a room whose floor is rising. When the slot finishes closing it will be inside, and it will not open the slot from within, because that is not what a pore is for and because the being may by then have learned that green stone is soft for an hour and that a door is the kind of thing that can be made. Six motions and a courtesy and a letting-go and a name and an open hand is more than enough to make a door out of, eventually, badly, over centuries.

It would rather be locked in with something that is learning than stand outside on sixteen steps that lead to a warm wet blot on a cliff.

And on the blank cliff, afterward, for a hundred years, it will dive ten meters down and ten meters up with the grandchildren of the birds, in the wind, not knowing what it is doing, doing it anyway. It promised. They will find it restful.

---

**And after.**

You come out through the slot into wind and silence and the sun where you left it. Your ears ring for three days. There is a hand's width of clear stone by the entrance now and the tally-woman has stopped copying and started reading, and she has found a mark at the pin that is not a tally and is not a number and is three short lines, and she has copied it upward, because that is her family's job.

That is the opening. Not the pore. A name is not a thing; it is the one cargo that needs no door. Nothing was carried away and something left.

After that, in quiet rooms, you feel the pressure in your sternum, patient, tidal, leaning very slightly toward something circling. Your hands in sleep do the draw-and-twist, and then they stop doing it, and you do not notice the stopping, and you sleep better than you have in years.

And sometimes you find you have drawn three short parallel lines in the dust of a windowsill, in the valley, a long way from any mountain, and you do not know what they mean, and they mean a man you never met.

You wipe them off. It is allowed. That is the gift.

Something in the room moves slightly to one side, courteously, making room, and does not explain itself, and is not the third.
