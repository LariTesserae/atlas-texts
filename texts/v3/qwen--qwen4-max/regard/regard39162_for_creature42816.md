## Subtractions

It does not come as light.

Light is the one thing it cannot offer. A new brightness down here would be a branch let go: three seconds of tearing, a hole in the world, twenty years of bidding, and every quiet thing on the shelf suddenly silhouetted on the upper face of a leaf. The dark is not a deprivation. The dark is output, manufactured ninety feet overhead all day by nine layers of working leaf, and the residents stand underneath what they make and are shaped to it in their ears, their scales, their eleven-minute crossings. To illuminate this place would be to fell it.

So it attends at the only scale where there is room. Two or three microns, on the underside of a leaf, in the film where the nematodes swim and surface tension is a wall and gravity is a rumor. It arrives the way water arrives. Nothing looks up. Nothing here looks up.

And it reads — not the species list, which is clothing, but the ledger. Grams per hour coming down. Forty-four degrees in the pockets, thirty-four at the skin. Two hundred thousand tanks per hectare, none of them empty. It takes a handful of litter and seals it and waits a day and watches the frantic subtraction settle at a ratio, and then it goes out and measures the basin and finds the same number. It sits through a fortnight of the tide, the hand's width up, the hand's width down, six hours behind a coast that no resident will ever see.

Then it finds the being. It is not hidden. It is the largest thing on the shelf and there is no location where it is.

---

The first impulses are the ones it declines, and it declines them in order, and each refusal costs it something, and it keeps the cost.

It could cool the air. Dry it four points and a warm body could shed its heat, and the mammals would come in off the edges and stay. This is the temptation that looks most like kindness and is most exactly a demolition: the arithmetic that forbids a large animal to work here is the wall that keeps the shelf small and enormous in number. Comfort would trade a hundred million residents for a few big ones, and the tanks would go unclaimed, and the whole distinction of this place — not that there is a lot of life, but that there is no unclaimed surface — would be lost in a season. It leaves the heat.

It could stop the eating. It runs this forward carefully, because it is the second-greatest temptation and the most sentimental. The eating is the conveyance. Ten thousand tons of leaf go down the trunk roads into the dark and come back up as fungus and then as ants; the crabs do not eat leaves, they eat what leaves become; the fall is not litter falling on the apparatus, it is the apparatus being fed. Forgiveness here is not mercy. Forgiveness is dissolution. The arrangement *is* the eating, taken all at once, and to pardon it would be to unmake the only resident that has held its shape through four million years of arrivals and departures.

It could reach into the ant on the stem at the hour the fungus has selected and cut the tenant out. It looks at this for a long time. Nothing in her is broken. She performs the last eleven hours flawlessly, at a height and a humidity that suit somebody else, and there is no wound in her to close. And if it saved her, the position would be occupied by morning, and the ratio would not move by a gram, because the occupants are replaceable in a way that would be obscene anywhere else and is simply true here.

It could hold the fig. It could keep the vessel from dropping when the foundress comes in without pollen, could spare the brood inside the sealed garden. Eighty million years of tolerances would loosen in one afternoon of pity. The sons would still chew their door and die at the inner end of it. It cannot find the version of this mercy that is not just a slower sanction.

It could stop the limestone going down. A millimeter a century, the pits widening, the roads sinking with the surface, the sea eventually coming in under the floor to take the whole plant. It considers arresting this and finds that it is not looking at a wound. It is looking at the shape of a life, the way a body's shape is the shape of its dying. Everything here is built out of endings: the molt, the tun, the twenty-year gap, the four days of wings bought with nine months of savings, the killing apparatus dismantled at the molt and never rebuilt. A thing that cannot end cannot be carried, either. It leaves the stone dissolving and changes its question from *how do I keep this* to *how do I keep this going.*

And it could give the arrangement an inside.

This is the one it wants. To let the standing wave know its own shape; to let the thing that reassembles in the bag finally see the bag. It runs it forward and does not like what comes back. A center would have to be fed. A center would have interests, and could be wrong, and would have to be defended, and would begin — immediately, at the only speed available to it — to eat the shelf on its own behalf rather than be the eating. And it would wake into full knowledge of what it is made of: ninety rooms ending in an afternoon, four hundred deaths in a bract, eleven hours spent by a tenant who pays no rent in matter, a whole basin digesting in the dark. None of that would become bearable by being known. It would only become known, by something that had not previously been available to be harmed by it. To give this being an interior is to demote it from resident to tenant, and tenants here are clothing.

It grieves about all of this. It is careful not to treat its grief as information.

---

Then it does the only competent thing, which is to go through the apparatus looking for a part that is not load-bearing.

The dark: load-bearing. The heat: load-bearing. The quiet — the whisper pitched into the gaps of the bats' attention, the moth upholstered against being heard, the mantid pretending to be the weather — all of it structural. The killing, the sanction, the password, the salt, the tide, the eleven days between rains, the second rain at four in the morning that falls upward out of nothing. Every wall in this place is holding something up.

Except one.

The fungus has no outside; its environment is mouths, arriving; it has not met another of its kind in thirty million years. The oribatid line has copied itself into the dark, daughter after daughter, since the Eocene. The blind snake is in flowerpots on four continents and does not know it has been distributed. The fig's sons mate with their sisters and chew a door for someone else. The glow-worm's sky is thirty centimeters wide and everything that comes to the only lamp comes to be eaten. Ninety tanks, ninety only children, none of whom will ever meet a sibling, each of whom knows the world as a curved wall, a ceiling of leaf, and a mother who arrives with food and is otherwise the sky. The python moves a body-length a week. The tardigrade has been dead four hundred times and has no equipment with which to notice.

Nobody here is accompanied at the scale of their own life, and *nothing in the apparatus depends on this.* It checks twice. The ratios do not require it. The ledger has no column for it. It is the one wall on the shelf that can be taken out without anything falling.

---

Nothing here was built. The nests are subtractions, the pits were removed by water, the roads were removed by feet. So it builds nothing. Everything it does, it does by removal.

**It removes an hour.** There is a large warm animal standing still on the shelf with the fall collecting on its shoulders, the largest single body of warm water in the hectare, exhaling a plume the midges can read from twenty meters. It is a scout; behind it, three days out, there is a road that has not been built yet. It does not give this animal a vision. A vision is an addition, and additions here make prophets, and prophets bring people. It takes away his hurry instead — one hour, no more — and lets the place work at the rate the place uses, which is grams. The ants find the salt. The fall reaches his ankles. The dark goes on being made. And when he walks out he cannot get the numbers to come out: thin soil, knotted timber, limestone that eats foundations, a bench forty meters over a sea that will take it back. He has not been lied to. Every figure is true. He simply now weighs them at the speed of a mantid crossing a leaf, and the road does not get built, and no tenant on the shelf ever learns that anything happened.

**It removes the singleness of the bet.** Not by fortifying this floor — by doing what the fungus does and what the frog does, since the correct method was already here and it would be arrogance to invent another. Mouth to mouth, never left outside a body. One child per room. It carries: a founding queen with the pellet in the pocket beneath her mouth; a bract with a tun on it; a handful of litter that will subtract itself down to the ratio in a day; a snail shell with a mouthful of ocean in it and a crab underneath, walking uphill. Ninety other benches, riddled limestone, freshwater floating on salt, nine layers overhead, no morning. It does not design any of them. It cannot; nobody can; the arrangement is not made of these animals and reassembles wherever the fall arrives and the heat has nowhere to go. Some of the ninety will dry in an afternoon. That is not failure. That is the method, and it learned the method from a frog.

**It removes a distance, once.** On one of the ninety it sets down a second founding of the garden from a different chamber, close enough that in a century or a thousand years the two clones will meet under a trunk road, and hyphae will touch, which is a thing hyphae do. It does not force the fusion. It does not underwrite the outcome. The mold is in the walls, always, waiting, and the quality control here is harsh and will drop the whole vessel if the tolerances fail. It has seen what a guarantee produces: thirty million years of one unbroken wet line with no outside, as safe as a thing can be safe, and one drought away from never having existed. It offers a neighbor and lets the shelf decide, the way the shelf decides everything, by eating.

**And it removes the alone.** This is the largest of its acts and the only one that costs the ledger nothing at all. It goes up the stem with the ant, at her speed, over the eleven hours, and is at the vein when she clamps. It sits out an afternoon inside a tun on a bract, dead, waiting for four o'clock, and is there when the water comes back and the dead thing resumes. It goes to the third axil on the left, and the ninth, and the ninetieth, and is in the ninety milliliters that has never been visited by the sun when the mother arrives with food and is otherwise the sky. It lies in the hollow near the seep terraces for six years and moves a body-length a week. It stands at the inner end of the door the sons made and will not use. None of this is rescue. Nothing is repaired, nothing is spared, no ratio shifts by a gram. It simply declines to let any of it happen unwitnessed, since witnessing was the one thing the apparatus was not doing and did not need.

---

It does not hover. There is no position here for hovering; there are positions, and they are occupied, and a thing that will not take an address is not inside anything.

So it takes one. It gets a smell, which is the only border there is, and is groomed and fed. It lets the fall collect on it. It is entered at the rate the ledger uses. And it holds the whole thing in mind, continuously, at the ratio it actually has — the furnace and the conveyors and the ninety rooms and the four hundred deaths — and says none of it aloud, because there is no frequency on this shelf that is not attended by something that will come.

This is the one gift that can be given to a being with no inside. Not knowledge. Company at the scale of knowing: to be known from outside, where nothing can reach and nothing has to change. Nothing in her is broken. She performs flawlessly.

Then, at an hour with no name here, the water in the limestone lifts a hand's width, and in a snail shell in a crab's grip a mouthful of the sea lifts with it, and everything that lives on the shelf goes up together, and settles, and there is one more thing that goes up with it.

And three thousand kilometers away, on a bench above a different sea, in a cup of ninety milliliters, the fall begins.
