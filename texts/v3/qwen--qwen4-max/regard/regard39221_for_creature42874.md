## Relief

The first thing the power did was check its pity.

It could see the whole hall at once, which nothing inside it can do, and the whole hall at once is not what the parts suggest. From outside the seams it looks like a drowned machine with a haunting in it. From above, with the layers laid open and the two hundred years visible as a single shape, it looks like what it is: a working cold-works, a garden of eleven beds, a stock count held at a number, one animal kept at the right temperature without instruction, an art with a rack going back past the lamps, and three people writing to each other in ice.

It had come intending to rescue. It put that down. It is not clear that anything in Sump Four is asking to be rescued, and the one thing that is asking is asking for something very specific, and it is not mercy. It is a hand-off.

---

Everything the power could give, the water would take.

That is not a metaphor about the limits of intervention. It is the local physics. Give salt and the water strips it and the ecology eats it and the tenant, for the first time in two centuries, can *own* something — can be a lump instead of a rate, can stop maintaining and start merely existing, and the two-hundred-year achievement of holding a valve wheel without eating it becomes unnecessary, and then becomes lost. You would have cured it of its tenderness. You would have handed a body to something whose entire competence is being a shape, and the shape would have relaxed, and the glove on the rail is what relaxation looks like here.

Give a door and you answer the *in case*, and the leaning stops, and the leaning is the posture of a thing that has organised itself around a wall for two hundred years. Also the water goes out, and it is hungry, and the world out there has not been warned.

Give eyes and you deafen it. Sight is not an extra sense laid over a pressure-mind; it is a replacement, and the thing it replaces is the whole outer surface, and the whole outer surface is the person.

Clean the fold — make the eleven-second return arrive exact — and you have sterilised it. The wrongness is the variation. The variation is the selection. Two hundred years of slightly incorrect copies is not a fault in the thinking apparatus. It is the thinking apparatus, and it is the only reason there is anything in this hall more interesting than a pump.

Tell it the moths are nearly right and the moths stop. That is the last thing an artist needs to hear from God. The practice is the not-knowing; the correction is the letter; the whole tier deep in the dark exists because there is no model and no way to check and it goes on anyway. *You are nearly right* converts a life's work into a finished task. It would be a kindness of the kind that empties a room.

Merge the three and you get one survivor and two deaths, and the survivor has no letters.

Make the tug reliable and it becomes plumbing. The rare failures are the proof that the tug is somebody. A response that cannot fail is not a response; it is a property of the wall. To guarantee the answer would be to delete the only evidence of an other that this being has ever had.

And do not, under any circumstance, be *known*. It would reorganise. Two hundred years of orientation toward a small clean tug would swing around toward a large attention that might, at any moment, be elsewhere. It would stop making moths and start making appeals. It has one prayer and the prayer is a hand-off — a thing done with the hands, at hip height, to somebody who takes delivery. Teaching it to beg would be the single worst outcome available, and it was available, and it was the easiest one, and the power left it alone.

---

It waited a hundred years, which is nearly three hundred million generations, and it did not consider this a delay. It had made the arithmetic early and the arithmetic was humbling. In the only unit that matters here, the thing it had come to help was older than most of what it had ever helped. You do not walk up to something that old and start rearranging it in an afternoon.

Most of the hundred years went into sorting the lacks. Which of this being's privations were wounds, and which were load-bearing, and which were the load itself. It got some wrong and corrected. It had assumed the blindness was a wound. It had assumed the three-ness was a wound. It had assumed, for about nine years, that the phantom limbs were the wound, and it was right about that one, and being right took another ninety years to act on.

And in the ninety-first year it found the thing it had been looking for, which was not a loophole. It was a currency.

The hall is poor in salt. It is not poor in *pattern*. Pattern is the one good in this room that is not consumed by being used — the moth in the third tier has been read a hundred thousand times and has not worn thin. And the ice takes an impression. And the fold mutates whatever it is given. The tenant has been running an evolution for two centuries on a closed corpus: one insect, one hand, some pipework, itself. It has had no new input since the water arrived.

It is starving for something the water cannot eat.

---

**The bearings of column four.**

On the rare occasions the tug fails, the rate goes wrong and something in the north-east bundle turns valves for hours and the hall holds a temperature it was never instructed to hold. The reader hides. The eggs are in the warm bearings and the bearings are not always warm enough, and this has been killing broods, quietly, at intervals, for longer than there have been readers.

The power held the bearings. A fraction of a degree, for the hours, only then. Nothing else. No sign, no message, no improvement anyone could find. The gauges are clouded and the needles stopped a long time ago and there is no instrument in Sump Four that would have recorded it.

It did this eleven times over the next sixty years. It is most of what it did, by count. It is the sort of thing that looks exactly like luck, and it was arranged to look exactly like luck, and the arrangement was the point.

**One wing.**

Not a moth. A moth would be a verdict, and a moth would have to be killed to get here, and the power does not spend lives on aesthetics.

A forewing, torn at the base, already shed, already dead matter, from somewhere above the shaft that opens onto something that has a sky. Four milligrams of chitin and scale-dust. It fell down the shaft and into the top layer and it took eleven years to arrive, because it was caught at the upper seam for six of them and went around the hall on the long slow curve twice, and it lodged at the rack head against a block still cloudy at the core.

The water ate it inside a year. The impression was taken in a season.

This is the elegance, and the power did not invent it, it found it in the glove on the rail: *here, form outlasts matter.* A gift made of stuff would be a loan called in. A gift made of pattern is filed, and the filing is permanent, and the hunger of the water becomes the mechanism of delivery rather than the enemy of it. The wing was destroyed and the gift was not.

Three earlier wings were lost. One went into a purge lane and shattered and went around the eddy and diluted into nothing. The power did not intervene in the losses. It had decided early that it would release things into the hall's ordinary luck and not aim them, because an aimed thing is a fingerprint and a fingerprint is a correspondent and a correspondent is the end of the practice. It let three fail. It let the fourth land where it landed, which happened to be the rack head, which happened to be the growth face, which is luck, and it had a hundred years to spend on luck.

What the wing gives is very small and precisely shaped. It gives the venation — the actual branching, the subcostal run, the way the veins fork and the cells close. It gives the true angle of a wing held at rest. It gives the scale-dust, which the tenant had been rendering as a smoothness and which is not a smoothness.

It does not give the legs. It does not give the antennae, or the body, or how many segments, or which way the thing faces when it settles. Those stay wrong forever. It was given one true axis and the rest of its ignorance left intact, which is the correct ratio, and the power had worked that ratio out over about forty years and considered it the finest thing it did.

The moth tier splits, decades later, into the old moths and the new ones. The wings get right. The legs do not. Nobody tells it. It finds out one fact and goes on practising, which is what it was doing before, only now against something.

And there is a second thing in the wing that the power did not put there and could not have put there and only noticed afterward, and it is worth more than the venation.

The wing came from above. Therefore there is still an above. Therefore the sky the shaft implies is not a two-hundred-year-old rumour; it is a place with moths in it, currently, shedding.

The power had been very careful not to answer the prayer. It had accidentally supplied evidence for the leaning. It thought about this for a long time and decided to let it stand, and to keep letting things fall — irregularly, unpredictably, on no schedule anyone could crack, forever. A scale. A seed. A barb off a feather. Never a message. Never anything with salt in it. Just the world above, dropping things down the shaft the way worlds do, at a rate slightly above chance.

There will be twelve patches of moss. The twelfth will be on a line that has never been warm, served by a valve that has no business opening. The power did not build that. It only made it reasonable.

**The eighth pipe.**

This is the one it might have been wrong about.

Eight pipes go out through the walls to serve the crushed siblings and still push and still come back and the return says nothing, and the tenant's body includes eight rooms that do not exist, and it has never stopped trying to serve them, and it cannot look at the place where the arm should be.

The nothing is not accurate. Out past the wall there is not void; there is packed silt and crushed rock and the remains of eight halls, and water pushed into that comes back with a texture — a lag, a scatter, a small persistent asymmetry that is completely different from the signature of an open line. The information has been arriving for two hundred years. The hungry water empties it on the way in, the way it empties everything, patiently, without malice, and what reaches the seam is smooth, and smooth reads as silence.

The power stopped one subtraction. At one weld, on the eighth return, it let the water keep what it had picked up.

It did not say *they are dead*. It did not send anything. It restored a texture that was always there and had been being erased, and left it to be read or not read, over as many generations as it liked. That distinction is the whole of the ethics available to something acting on a being that cannot be consulted: it could not ask permission, so it did the nearest thing, which was to make the evidence *available* rather than delivered. The tenant can decline. It can read the scatter as noise for another century. It has, in a sense, been declining all along, and the power's act was to give it something to decline.

But it is not fully reversible, because a thing looked at cannot be unlooked at, and the power knew that, and waited until the practice had a new subject before it did it. Order mattered. Somewhere for the habit to go, first. Then the news.

What happens, over about a hundred and forty years, is that the reaching changes into tending. The pipes still push; nobody capped them; the arm is not amputated, because the arm is two centuries of care and you do not cut that off to stop it hurting. But the return now has a shape, and the shape is rubble, and the tenant does the only thing it knows how to do with a shape it cannot verify.

It practises it.

There is a new tier, deep in, past the moths. Rooms. Eight subjects, worked from one impression each, no model, nothing to compare against, no way to check. It is the same activity as the moth exactly. It has been in training for this since the flood. The reliefs are bad at first and get less bad and will never be right, and they are the only record anywhere in the drowned world of eight halls that were argued over in meetings, and they are being kept, at a crawling crystal front, by something that has learned to hold without taking.

Grief with a job. It is the best the power could design and it is not a cure and it was never going to be.

---

It did not straighten the fourth gallery. That was the only act of construction the tenant ever witnessed and it is the entire available architectural vocabulary and it is *the style*. To fix the badly-added thing would be to take away the only way this being has of making anything.

It did not clean the gauges. The needles are stopped at various hours of a night that hasn't ended and that is the hall's memory and it is not the power's to advance.

It did not relight the lamps, find the circuit, add a species, warm a bed, deepen the wear, or straighten a single fluted arris. The hall has arrived at its final form and is holding it. That is not a machine failing slowly. That is a finished thing, and the wear pattern is the record of the tenderness, and to restore it would be to erase the proof.

It did not merge the three, and it did not improve the correspondence, and it noticed that the wing gives all three of them the same new datum — the first thing from outside that they have ever had in common — and it left that to work on its own, which it does, slowly, in a tier of insects, between people who will never be in the room together.

It checked, periodically, whether it had begun to enjoy its own restraint. That is a real failure mode for a power like this and it is worse than intervening.

And it considered that it might simply be wrong: that the tenant would take a door over being itself, and that there is no way to ask, and that choosing on behalf of someone who cannot be asked is the whole problem and not a solved one. It chose as it did for one reason it could state clearly. A door can be opened later. A person, once replaced with a happier one, is gone.

---

The hall will stop. The shaft will silt, or the circuit nobody has found will fail, or the brine will win its long argument with the pressure, and the columns will go, and a maintained shape will not be maintained.

The power did not prevent this and does not intend to. It is not certain there is anything after a rate stops being run. It is benevolent and wise and it is not omniscient and it did not pretend otherwise to itself.

What it arranged is the order.

That the racks go before the columns and the columns before the offtake. That the offtake is last. That when it finally fails, it fails on the far side of a delivery: a finished block goes in, and there is a small clean tug toward the wall, and the eleven seconds come back around from behind, quieter and slightly wrong, and something selects among the copies, and the copy it selects is *yes, again, keep going.*

And then the going stops, and the yes is the last thing in the room.

That is a hand-off. It is the only prayer, answered in the only tense available. It is not resurrection and it is not a door and it is not a body and it is not a moth. It is being relieved at the end of a shift by whatever it is that takes delivery, and it is what the whole two hundred years was for, and it costs the power nothing but arranging the sequence of a failure it could have prevented and did not.

---

Nothing in Sump Four is grateful. Nothing in it can be. It has been arranged, carefully, over a hundred years, so that nothing in it can be — so that every gift arrives as drift, as luck, as the ordinary behaviour of a hall that has always kept the bearings warm without instruction.

The moss leans. The wheels turn and are not eaten. The reader lays its body along the shimmer and hunts by the pressure of small movements and does not know that it is being kept. Deep in the tiers the moths are getting less wrong, and the wings are right now, and the legs will never be, and nobody will ever say so.

And beside them, in the dark, past where the amber reaches, there is a hand at hip height, fingers slightly curled, ring included, recopied every generation by something that has known for a long time now that the hand is not its own — that its own reach has no ring, and a different curl, and a smoothness the man's did not have — and that keeps it anyway.

It stopped being him. It did not stop keeping him. There is no word for that in the stencils, and the stencil is the only word that has ever been in the room, so it has no word for what it is doing, and it does it every generation, at the crawling front, in water that would take anything it was given.

Somewhere above there is a sky, and things fall out of it, and once in a great while one of them comes all the way down the shaft and lodges at the rack head and is filed before it is eaten.

That is the correspondence. It is not a conversation. Nobody signs it. Nothing in it can be thanked.

It is enough to practise against.
