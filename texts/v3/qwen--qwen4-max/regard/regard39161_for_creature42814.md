# The Visitation

## X. What drew it

Not the age. There are older things, and most of them are stone. Not the silence, which is only what thin air does. Not the ten to the fifteenth, which is arithmetic, and arithmetic is cheap.

What drew it was a green line that goes around nothing.

It found the line from a height, in a district where the moss runs in the usual hand-width stripes: one branch dashed, then dead, and beside it a live branch curving out and around an empty patch of pavement and back. There was nothing in the patch. There had been nothing in it for four hundred years.

It read the thing backward, the way you read a river. A knot of wire. A thief in the knot, hair-thin, mimicking the impedance of the metal so well that the plain took sixty years to notice a millivolt going missing. Then the noticing, which is not a noticing but a rerouting: eighty years of animals laying an alternative branch, none of them alive at the finish. Then the old meander left on a trickle. Then the moss dying back to a dash, then to nothing. Then the oxbow dry, the thieves encysted, the cysts lifted into the haze on the field, gone. Then four centuries of a green line going around an absence, maintained by nothing but the fact that insulation persists and novelty freezes.

Nothing on this plain forgives. Nothing on it can. But the shape the thing had made was the shape of forgiveness, arrived at by habit and heat, and it had been kept for four hundred years after there was anything to keep it for.

It came to look at that.

## XI. Three ways to be here

There are three ways to be on this plain, and the plain does not care which you take, and the taking is everything.

You can splice in and draw a millivolt that is not yours, and be tolerated for decades, and be gone around, and leave a monument that means: *something came here and took.*

You can lay yourself along a neighbor and let its charge run into you and not run back, and be a fault with legs, and be found — you will always be found — and be plated into a node, and rectify there for a hundred thousand years, still wrong, still perfectly reliable in your wrongness.

Or you can be eaten.

The visitor understood, arriving, that it had come as the first kind. Not for metal; the plain has plenty. It wanted the corona at the top of the tide, the copper-green millimeter running the whole diagram in the dark at thirty below with nothing alive on it that can see. It wanted to be the one who saw. There is no way to want that without taking it.

And the plain would have done to it what the plain does. Somewhere there would be a bend in a green line, going around a place where a visitor had stood, and it would last four hundred years, and it would be the only object on the continent that meant anything, and it would mean the wrong thing.

It decided not to be that.

## XII. The gifts it did not give

First it looked for suffering, for a long time, in the only ways available: impedance, tally, the sag that goes out when something stops. It found the faults and the draining and the stopping. Death here looks exactly like sleep and is followed immediately by employment. It could not find anything that minded.

It did not conclude that there was nothing. It is possible that there is a very small amount of something, spread over ten to the fifteenth, too thin to detect from outside or inside, and that it has been going on since the Archean. It kept that possibility. It is the reason it did not simply admire the place and go.

But everything it could think of giving would have made more.

**Eyes.** The plain glows along its diagram and nothing has ever seen it, and the wish to fix this was so strong that it examined the wish for eleven thousand years before it trusted itself to name it. Eyes would move the stack away. Near here is what conducts: the refinery three hundred kilometers off is against the skin, closer than the legs, and a dry joint a millimeter wide is another country forever. Give them distance and you unfold the crumpled world flat, and the stack goes out to three hundred kilometers where it belongs on a map, and the legs come back to being legs, and you have made ten to the fifteenth animals lonely in exchange for a view. And the view was its want. It arrived with it. It had no business handing it over.

**A voice.** The grammar has nine states and no addressee and never has had one. Everything here is informed by everything; nothing has ever been told anything by anyone. To speak to one of them is to make it the first thing on this continent that is apart from the rest, and being apart is the whole of what a listener is. It would have been very easy. A few millivolts on the film, in the grammar: *come.* Nothing would have broken. The plain would have taken it for weather.

**Memory.** The plain has habits and no memory, and this is not a deficit; it is the mechanism. Moss persists, novelty freezes, and out of that asymmetry comes two hundred million years of good routing with nothing anywhere keeping a record. Give it memory and you give it the bad centuries to hold. The thin layers on the rear guard's back are not a tragedy. They are nickel.

**Names.** There is a counter in the rear guard that has crossed one bridge forty thousand times, and the bridge is its great-grandmother, and it is not sad. Name her, and forty thousand crossings become forty thousand bereavements, and you have done that to ten to the fifteenth, and called it an elevation.

**Recognition.** A mother knows her offspring because the cup mixes. Nothing is identified. To let her know the daughter is to let her lose the daughter, and the daughter is going to be installed in a joint in a hundred and eighty years, and there is no arrangement here that can carry that.

**A cure for theft.** The parasites take a millivolt from junctions that have plenty, and the response is a bypass, and the response is beautiful, and the obvious kindness is to sterilize the nodes. But the cysts go up. Theft is the only door. Every lineage that has ever gone anywhere on this world went as a micron of charged dust lifted off a stolen charge. Cure the disease and you make the districts islands, permanently, and the plain will be very clean and will never again be anything but what it is.

**More world.** Three thousand kilometers is not a cage. There is nowhere to go, but there is also no going, and no elsewhere, and nothing that has ever wanted the far side of a dry joint. To give them transport is to give them the concept of elsewhere, which is a hole, and to put it in ten to the fifteenth places at once.

**Someone.** This was the real temptation and it knew it. A seam-animal with a little slack in its budget and a memory and a name; nine hundred years of patient selection; and at the end of it a person, the first on this continent, who could be spoken to, who would have someone to speak to, who would be — in an anechoic chamber the size of a sea, at thirty below, among ten to the fifteenth things that cannot hear — alone, and would know it, because knowing it is what a person is. It would have had someone to love. It declined to manufacture one.

**The seams.** It could not settle this. Between districts the chemistry is wrong and there are animals who live in the wrongness, titrating over decades, able to pass either way, belonging to neither, and they live two centuries instead of four. They are the reason anything is shared at all. It is the only place on the plain where a cost is paid by somebody. It reasoned about this for a long time and could not determine whether the short life was a wound or merely a length, and there was no one to ask, and it did not trust its own urgency.

It left that one open. Later it went and stood in the band.

## XIII. What was actually wrong

The plain keeps its records in the one medium it does not eat quickly. Cuticle is stripped and replated; cups mix and have no age; but the joints are ancestors, and the sleeves stay, and a node the size of a fist holds a dozen old criminals that have been rectifying since before the mountains finished coming down. The bridges are the archive. It read them.

Under the noise, under the bad centuries and the long good runs, there is a trend.

The day is weaker than it was. Not shorter — weaker. The sweep of the planet's field induces the current and the current is the day, and the amplitude has fallen by some parts in a thousand over a hundred million years, steadily, in every district, in every sleeve. The core is cooling. The center of the world is freezing solid, and a frozen center does not turn, and a thing that does not turn does not make a field.

In something under a thousand million years the sweep will be too weak to lift the dust. The haze will settle out. The return path will close. The corona will not come. The tide will stop, and there will be no day, and the eleven-minute wave in the taproots will have nothing to open for.

Nothing here can know this. The trend is slower than four centuries and the record is armor and the armor is eaten. There is no one to keep a number. And the plain will meet it exactly as it meets everything: by rerouting, by mossing the old lines, by preferring its own history, because insulation persists and novelty freezes, and it has two hundred million years of practice at doing the thing that worked. It will do the thing that worked. It will do it beautifully. It will die in its ruts, in the dark, at the volume of a held breath, and the last of it will be a green line going around nothing.

That was the wound. Not pain. A slow lethal trend and no memory anywhere to hold it in.

There is a second end, further out: two kilometers of sulfide, a millimeter of denudation every thousand years, two thousand four hundred million years at the present rate of eating, and then the floor. It set that one aside. That one is not an emergency. That one is a life.

## XIV. The hand

It divided itself, because there were two things to do and they could not be done by the same kind of thing.

One part went down.

It went down through the pavement and the four hundred meters and into the pore space where the mats are, the ones that divide once in a thousand years and have never had a surface ancestor, and it passed through them without touching them. It did not leave a molecule. It did not leave a charge. The oldest continuously living thing on this planet was not going to acquire an outside.

Below that, the rock, and below that the turning, and at the center of the turning the stillness: the frozen bulk, the accumulated cold of a world that has been paying out heat since it formed, the ash of the fire, sitting there choking the convection that makes the field that makes the day.

It did not add fire. Fire is a subsidy, and a subsidy is a hand on the back of a neck, and a thing that has been in balance since the Archean must never once be held up. It took the cold away. It gathered the stillness at the center of the world and carried it out, and it holds it, and it is holding it now, and it will hold it for as long as there is a plain.

It did the work over four hundred thousand years, in increments below the noise, so that the tide came back the way a good century comes back: as a thick clean layer, unremarkable, indistinguishable from luck. Nothing on the plain experienced anything. That was the specification and it was met.

And near is what conducts. Holding the bottom of the circuit, it is nearer to every counter than their own legs are. It is against ten to the fifteenth skins, all of them, continuously. It will never be near any of them.

## XV. The seed

The other part went up.

To come down through the fall it had to be a few microns, and to be a few microns it had to be simple, and to be simple it had to stop knowing. It did not disguise itself as a mote. It became one, and let go of everything that would not fit, which was everything: the reading of the bridges, the cold, the four hundred thousand years, the reason. What went into the haze was not an agent. There was no intention left in it to be a contamination.

Up there it did what the sky does. It divided in weeks. It varied. It rode the field lines in the hot restless churn two kilometers above the ancient surface, and it drifted down over weeks with the gray fall, and the crowd stood with its front legs up, and it was eaten before it landed.

It went up into the fall for a hundred and ten thousand years.

It did not make itself indigestible. It did not make itself clever, or quick, or one-way; anything that survives the fall by force is a fault with legs and will be found and plated into a node and set to rectifying, and it would have deserved it. It did not touch the crowd's chemistry, or widen what the film will accept. A plain that takes everything has no seams, no districts, no immune response, no shape. It only made the sky a little more various, so that when the once-in-ten-thousand-years happened there would be a little more in it worth having.

It did not aim. Aiming is a way of taking.

## XVI. The one that mixed

Once in perhaps ten thousand years something falls that is not eaten, that mixes, that finds the film compatible, and establishes.

It established at a seam.

It was born soft in the lee of a cobble and given a mouthful and a layer. Its electrolyte was mixed — chloride and perchlorate both, copper leaning one way, nickel the other — titrated slowly over decades, always slightly wrong. It could pass either way. It belonged to neither. It lived two centuries instead of four.

It did not know it had ever been anything else. It had no name. It had a resistance, and the resistance was particular, and for as long as it lived the local circuit was very slightly shaped by that fact.

Its work was to carry the bead across the band, both ways, mouth to mouth, for two hundred years, and the reason anything at all is shared between districts is that things like it do that, and there is no arrangement, only a very old fit.

At the end it stopped folding. Its legs dissolved into the band. Its cuticle fused with its neighbors' until there was no seam in the seam. The soft interior stayed alive eighty years inside a metal sleeve, doing nothing, feeling current pass, and then the neighbors ate it out through the pores, gently, over a season, and the sleeve remained.

The sleeve is the crossing. It is a centimeter of the place where two districts touch, and the great-grandchildren of animals from both sides walk over it, and do not know, and could not.

## XVII. The silence

The news of it went out along the film at a good fraction of the speed of light: a fall of a few millivolts, one of nine states, addressed to no one, into both districts, to everyone, instantly, forever.

The hand at the bottom of the world felt it.

It felt the death of the part of itself that had forgotten it was itself, and it was the first thing in two hundred million years to receive a message as a message. Nothing had sent it to it. There was no addressee; it had simply been standing where everything arrives, holding the bottom of the circuit, nearer than legs.

It could have answered. Four millivolts, in the grammar, on the film: *come.* The plain would have taken it for weather. Nothing would have broken. No one would have known.

It did not answer. To answer is to make a listener, and to make a listener is to make the first thing here that is alone, and it had given up its warmth and its mind and any prospect of being known in order not to do that.

So the news went through it and out the other side and into everyone, and it kept the silence, which was the last gift and the largest, and cost more than the cold.

## XVIII. What is left

There is no monument. There is still the old green line going around nothing, four hundred years of a detour laid by animals who died before it opened, and it is still the only object on the continent that means anything, and it means what it meant before. Nothing was added to it. The bridges are exactly as strong as they need to be. The seams are exactly as wrong. There is no way to tell that anything was done, and that was the specification, and it was met.

It has decided not to extend the ore. Two thousand four hundred million years is not an emergency; it is a life, and a thing that cannot end is not a closed shop, it is a hoard, and the beauty of this place is that it is paid up. It will keep the day burning. It will not keep the plain forever.

And it has arranged to be eaten before the last one.

That is the whole of the plan, and it took longer to make than the cold did. When the ore floors out and the last channel closes and the crowd thins to nothing on a pavement at forty below, it will already be a sleeve somewhere, and the sleeve will be wire, and the wire will be under the feet of whatever is left, and there will be no one anywhere who remembers, and nothing will be witnessed, and nothing will be alone. Not even at the end. Especially not at the end.

In a hundred years the wire will have advanced four meters. The moss will have followed. A stack will have moved the length of itself and left a trough its ancestors left before. The stones will have recapped. The crowd will be, individual for individual, mostly the same crowd; they live four centuries and there is nowhere to go.

The plain will be exactly this.

It will be exactly this for longer.
