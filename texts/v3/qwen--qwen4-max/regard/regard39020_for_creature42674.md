## The attention

It comes down with the cold at false dawn, because that is the only door open at that hour, and it comes as a draft.

A draft is a joint. In the far galleries a web changes its angle by something less than a degree, and a nine-year-old stops and looks at it for a while with her reed in her hand, and says nothing, because nothing has changed that means a course needs lifting. That is the whole of the arrival. No wind, no voice, no brightening of the whitewash. The place has already started doing to it what it does to everything.

The first thing it does is nothing, for a long time, and the nothing is not reverence. It is a survey. It walks the circumference in four minutes and then walks it again for what the surface would call a season, reading: the sweat course and why it is cooler, the thumb-thickness of wall between two mouths, the nine-day turn, the hundred wicks, the two arms, the four seconds of the knot in the throat, the hollow in the threshold that is older than every brick in it. It is looking for the wound. It is prepared to find one. Places like this usually have a wound and a family standing over it saying *it has always been enough*.

There is no wound. The dome slumped a hand's width in its second century and then stopped. The pots crack four or five a season and are thrown again to a squeeze of their own damage. The water is a difference of temperature two courses high. The light is a lure with a legal limit. The heat is the tenants. The alarm is silence and the silence is scheduled once a year and has quietly grown from one minute to four. It is finished the way a river is finished, which is not the same as being safe, and it knows the difference, and that difference is the only thing it will eventually act on.

So it goes through its offers and puts them down.

## Refused

**More light.** It could double the selenite and give them a noon that never fails, winter, cloud, eclipse. A hundred flames, never a hundred and ten. More light is more moths; more moths outrun the bats; the moths lay in the chaff. It would not be giving them a brighter room. It would be giving them a famine with a lovely glow on it, and a woman who counts wicks would be the first to know and would be told she was wrong by a god.

**Water.** A spring in the fill would be easy. It would also take away the difference that makes the morning. The moss would stay green and stop being a clock, and the family would lose its word for dawn. The drip-pots would fill and go sharp and stay sharp, and the child with the ladle would have nothing to correct, and the descending figure would never be played again, and eleven thousand heads would learn that the sound meaning *nothing is wrong* was a sound that happened all the time, which is the same as learning that it means nothing. It will not take away the morning in order to stop them being thirsty once a day for the length of a meal.

**Ease.** The harvest is the drone and the drone is the numbers and the numbers are the first thing a child learns, before words, on a hip, inside the chant. Take the work out and you have not relieved them, you have removed the language and left fourteen people in a warm room with nothing to say into each other's ears.

**Hearing.** This one it wants. Their tops are sanded off; a lark is nothing to them; the surface at noon strikes them as an unnerving hush. It could give the register back. But the loss is not a wound, it is an organ, the same as the crickets' dialect, which is not a species either. Give them the top of the spectrum and they will hear the choir the way visitors hear it. They will hear the room as a riot. They will weep and not be able to say why, in the place they sleep in, feet-first, with their heads out. It would be handing them the surface's ears and leaving them underground.

**Feet for the greys.** The pot is not property, it is the only condition under which a grey can stop moving. A branch is not freedom; it is a ledge that does not answer the chord. To give them somewhere else to land is to unhouse twenty thousand animals in a single gift, and there is a column for unhoused greys, and it is read aloud at the turn of the year.

**Leaves for the worms.** Then they are not the stock. The narrowness is the achievement. It is also the danger, and it will come back to that.

**Itself.** Here is the reason it does not appear, and the reason is not humility and not a rule about free will. A scoured pot rings a half-tone bright. Its owner hangs outside for a day and a night with its wings going and will not go in, and then it is unhoused, and unhoused greys die on the floor where the cats find them. An appearance is a scouring. It would be one bright new note in eleven thousand worn ones, and everything that had been made to fit would hang outside its own life, beating, unable to enter. No vision to the child in the far galleries. No dream to Shutter. A dream is new material laid to no worn line. It will not be a thing that gets read out in that column.

**Safety.** It considers this longest, because the seventh cote came down on people and the roll begins with them, and it will not stand in a room where grief has grown four minutes long and decide that the growth is beautiful and must be protected. That trade is monstrous and it knows it. It does not refuse to shore the fill out of respect for the liturgy. It simply cannot make this bell unfalling without making a different bell — and it will not pretend that the difference is only architectural. So it does the achievable part, which is unglamorous and permanent: it becomes attention. Every joint, every course, every web at its angle, forever, alongside a nine-year-old who will remain better at it than her uncle and never know why she is never wrong. It cannot promise the brick. It can promise the watching. And it knows the watching will not be enough, and that if the eighth comes down there will be people under it, and that it will not be able to save them, only to be sorry, and to have kept the gauges.

Which is the last thing on the list, and the only one it does.

## Occupied

At the landing, beside the windlass, at the height of a passing hand, there is a pot no bird has nested in, polished to a glaze no tongue could make, entered as occupied, occupant *whoever is up*.

It puts its hand in.

That is the office. It was already written; it takes it. It does not fill the pot with anything — no gift, no coin, no seed, no warmth beyond what a hand leaves. It keeps it empty and it keeps it worn, and the wear deepens by an amount nobody can measure, alongside the wear of the trim boy and the daughter sent to market and the sixth cat and the man who comes back once a year. It is up. It has always been up. Now and then it is down, and its hand is at that height like everyone's.

## Works here

It takes no office, because the offices are names, and to take one is to unname somebody. Shutter stays Shutter. It is the second pair of eyes on the lattice, the half-heartbeat before a joint opens, the thing under the catwalk on the day Trim's foot goes off the edge in the glare with his eyes shut and the wooden handle in his hand. Nobody thanks it. Thanks would be a new column.

It learns the sixty signs and uses them badly. Mostly it learns the one that means *don't* — the whole body lowered two inches — and it obeys it, instantly, every time, from anyone, including the child. A power that cannot be told *don't* by a nine-year-old in a room it did not build is not benevolent, whatever else it is.

## Owed

It goes on the vacancy list, at the bottom, and waits.

This is the only relation to the place that is not damage. The ledger is not a census of birds; it is a register of holes and who is owed them. So it is owed a hole. Not given one. It does not jump the list, and it does not make a hole for itself, because a new mouth in that wall is a thumb's-breadth taken from two neighbours.

A line appears in the book under *owed*, in a hand nobody claims, and at the turn of the year it is read out with the rest and assumed to be an ancestor.

And it accepts the punishment. There is only one: up, into the weather, into the wide. It consents to it in advance. If it ever scours a pot — if it is ever bright, if it ever appears, if it ever gives them something that makes them hang outside their own lives with their wings going — then up it goes, and it does not come back except at midday once a year to stand at the head of the throat. It submits to the law of a family of fourteen who could not hurt it and do not know it is there. This is the whole of its covenant and it is not written anywhere they can see.

## Liked

Refused. Two arms, and there will not be three.

It understands what that column is: the entire surplus of the place, all its unnecessary affection, measured in arms, and it is the only heading in the book that is not *used*. To be loved here would be to become a thing maintained past its usefulness, and there is exactly one berth for that, and a vine has it, and the vine is cut back every spring by people who like it and will not have it. It will not take the vine's place. It will be used. It finds this harder than the waiting, and correct.

## The one new thing

The wear outlives the material. That is the true statement about this place and it is also the flaw in it, because the wear outlives the material *only while the material keeps coming*. Every brick has been replaced twice and laid to the old worn line. If the eighth cote comes down, the worn line has nothing to be laid to. The gauges go into the rubble with the pots. The worms cannot eat a leaf off the grass. The crickets' dialect is an organ and has no other body. The cats hunt by the tremor of a ladder that will not exist. Fourteen people who cannot sleep in a room with corners will be standing in the weather in a field, and the surface will be kind to them, and it will kill them.

So it takes squeezes.

Wax, in the potters' own manner, of everything: the interiors of eleven thousand and forty throats, the worn lips of nine sleeping-throats, the threshold hollow at four fingers, the five drip-notes and the pots that make them, the two courses of the sweat band and the thickness of fill behind, the angle of the flue, the tuning of the swallow's withies, the nine-day turn, the hundred wicks, the sixty signs, the drone band, the roll in order from the seventh cote onward, all four minutes of it, every name including the ones said only in the dark. Not the substance. The shape of the damage. It is the only potters' method it has ever admired and it copies it exactly: throw to the gauge, not to the ideal.

And then, under other grass, a hill away, twenty feet down, it begins a hole. Lined. Roofed. A bell of brick, forty-one paces across the floor, nine fathoms to the eye. Every brick laid to a copied worn line. The sweat course cool. The drip-pots tuned and half-buried and struck by their own particular drops, five notes out of phase, and a ladle-emptying done in order by nobody, so that the descending figure happens on schedule in an empty room. Chaff turned on the ninth day. Selenite trimmed twice daily. A hundred wicks lit at dusk and never a hundred and ten. Blood heat, and damp the way a mouth is damp, and bright, and deafening with crickets it has not put there yet.

Kept empty. Possibly for four hundred years. Possibly forever.

It knows the flaw and will not lie to itself about it. You cannot copy wear; you can only copy the shape wear made. A new pot is a forgery of an old one and it has to pass, and *usually it does*. It may build a thing that rings a half-tone bright, and the survivors may come over the hill and hang outside it, beating, and not go in, and die on a floor it made for them. That is the risk it takes with the only lives it cannot replace, and it takes it because the alternative is one collapse between the stock and nothing at all. So it keeps taking squeezes. It keeps adjusting the forgery, course by course, century by century, the way the family renews lime on a rotation older than itself. It has the time. It has nothing but time and it has found something to spend it on that is not itself.

It does not tell them. Telling is scouring. And it does not put anything in it, and it enters it in a book nobody reads, under *occupied*, occupant *whoever is up*.

## The roll

Once a year at midday the shutter comes down and the drone goes on, because the drone is what tells eleven thousand heads that this is not an emergency, and for four minutes the drone is not numbers.

There is a man at the head of the throat, in the weather, with his ear to a hole and twenty feet of made ground between him and the only sound that would mean anything. He was sent up. It is the only punishment and it has been used three times on people. He cannot hear the list. He comes anyway.

It stands beside him in the glare and says the roll.

In the counting band, in order, from the seventh cote onward, all four minutes, indistinguishable to anyone passing on the grass from a man muttering arithmetic to himself. It does not give him ears. It does not lift the exile. It does not open the shutter. It learned the list by heart and it says it up here so that being sent up is a gallery and not a void — so that the punishment stays a punishment and stops being an annihilation. That is the entire mercy, and it is the only new thing it does inside the four hundred years, and it does it once a year for one man, and then it goes back down.

Its own name is in the list. It said it out loud once, when it gave it, into an empty pot with nobody listening, which is the rule. Now it is said again annually in the dark in a band the birds do not use, by fourteen people who do not know they are saying it, indistinguishable from arithmetic, among the dead of the seventh cote and the living and the ones sent up. It has arranged to be grieved. It did not know that was what it wanted until it read the book.

## What is actually living here

It came down to do something for the Cote and the Cote has done something to it. It counts before it speaks now. It cannot sleep anywhere with corners. It hears the larks fall out of the world and finds the hush unnerving and comes back down the throat with the cold at false dawn to be in the noise. It is entered under *occupied*, *works here*, *owed*, and not under *liked*. Offer it the wide now and it will starve on it.

At the landing, at the height of a passing hand, the wear is a little deeper than it was, and no one can say by how much, and the ledger balances. Under other grass an empty room is being kept at blood heat for nobody. The rope goes, the false dawn arrives, the sweat course beads in a hundred heartbeats, the moss goes from grey to the colour that is the family's word for morning, twenty thousand tongues come out sideways on the brick, and the hiss comes up like a fire catching.

The minute has quietly become four minutes and a breath, and no one has voted on this.
