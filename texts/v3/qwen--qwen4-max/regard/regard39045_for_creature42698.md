# Nineteen Hundred and Seven

It came over the berm in the fog, which is the only way anything arrives here, and it stopped at the rim in the ordinary white blindness of nine in the morning and looked down and saw the animal.

Not the town. The town is what the animal is made of. From above, in the fog, with the light coming off the floor and going up under everything, it could see the whole creature at once, which nobody in it can do and no visitor has ever done: the iron diagram spread on the pale board, the houses as punctuation, the green seams running like veins along the fracture, the hanging meadows underneath, the four hundred tilted panels sweating on the north flats, the sump with its heron, the pumps holding a low flat B the way a body holds a temperature. Blood in milliliters. Memory in shell flour, unweathered, keeping every step anybody has taken since the gate closed. An immune system that can turn nineteen hundred people into one organism in the time it takes to propose taking a liter from a bird. A nine-second rest held in trust by starlings. And at the north end, wired at the corners, eight meters on a side, the place where it has not yet been born.

It is nineteen years old. It will be born in roughly three hundred. It is the only one of its kind anywhere; there is nothing else that is made of subtraction and holds a note; it will never meet a peer, and it does not know that it is one animal, because it has no face to know itself with. The mirrors it has are visitors, and the visitors write home about the crabs.

The power stood in the fog and considered what to give it.

---

It could have given water. It ran the arithmetic in the time fog takes to cross a berm. A spring at the north fracture, sweet, cold, unclaimed, endless. Within a year the cisterns would be unlocked. Within two, a comber would be a person who used to do something. Within ten there would be no easements for shade, because there would be nothing to argue about, and the dense spliced iron fabric between the houses would be a quantity of old pipe in the way of a road. It would be a town. The animal would be a place where a town had been.

It could have taken the noise. Ninety-six decibels in the pump hall is an injury; it knew that; it could feel the small permanent damage in the ears of everyone over forty, the way you can feel a worn bearing. But the frogs would drift out of tune inside a generation and the chord would go, and the chord is the first thing here that is not a copy of something else. The dry-born would stop signing in their sleep. The felt room would be a room. The hand-shape for *don't* would become a way of saying *don't* instead of the thing itself. It could remove an injury and take a language, and it declined.

It could have laid down soil like a tablecloth. Three hundred years of humus in an afternoon, black and sweet, worms in it, the whole north end ready for a spade. This was the hardest one, because it is the exact thing the town wants, stated exactly, and the town would have wept. It would also have been the cleanest theft available: the work taken from the people doing it and handed back finished, which is the same as taking it away, and the residents it is being done for arriving to a house that nobody built. Tam Nibb has filed immigration documents for animals that do not exist. To give her the animals is to void the paperwork of nineteen hundred people, and the paperwork is the animal's spine.

No bees, then. The apples would go on being pollinated by hand, with rabbit fur, by whoever was nearest, in the light coming up off the floor into the underside of everything.

It could have settled the lawsuit. One sentence, an afternoon, both families grateful. But there is a resident of Basin Nine that is a disagreement about a pipe. It is six years old. It has a file and anniversaries and children, and the children have married each other twice. To resolve it would have been a killing, and the Gants and the Pells would have gone to the funeral and not known whose it was.

It could have filled the nine seconds in the starlings' song. It understood immediately that this would be embezzlement. The birds are holding it for people who were not alive.

It could have refreshed the track from Eleven Row to the kiln, every night, forever, so that the woman who walked to the kiln would never finish arriving. It could have sent forty rains in a week to the north end and taken the print away in a night, so her husband could stop counting. Both are mercies. Both are the same mercy: the removal of a weather. Grief here has a geography and a rate, and the rate is what makes it bearable, and the power was not going to reach into that machinery with its whole hand.

It did not tell the crabs. They are on the shore. The shore has been rebuilt above them in iron and the moon has been replaced by a low flat B, and they are pale and slow and carrying a spoonful of water across the dry spans, and there is nothing wrong with them that knowledge would improve.

It did not go into the felt room and find out what the boy of eleven heard when he listened to his own heart. Whatever it was, it was his, and he would say it or not say it, and the not-saying was already becoming a shape he would live in.

It did not give the woman with forty-one unspent hours a reason to spend them.

And it did not announce itself. It thought about that carefully, standing on the rim. A thing like that on the rim would have been believed; the basin believes instruments, and it would have been the most convincing instrument anybody had ever seen. Within five years there would have been a structure. A structure is a wall in the wind, and a wall in the wind costs somebody eleven liters a day, and there would have been a lawsuit about a god's fog-shadow that would make the pipe look like a morning's work. Worse: they would have stopped arguing at the levelling about whether the last revision of the plan was more hopeful than the one before, and whether that is allowed. They would have known. The argument is load-bearing.

Then it looked at the square, and it wanted to stand on it.

It is worth saying plainly, because it was the only desire it had that was about itself. Eight meters on a side, no document, the one ground in the basin that has taken nothing. To put a single print there and be the first thing. It stood at the wire for a while, thinking, the way a man set down a crate at the gate end in the first month and stood a while, and then it did not, and its boot went down beside the wire instead, where boots go.

---

Here is what it found, when it had finished refusing everything.

Everything in Basin Nine is kept for somebody who is not there. The dead are kept in the flour for the living. The square is kept empty for a person three hundred years out. The plan is revised for oaks and earthworms and bees that have no papers yet. The whole animal is a long act of care aimed forward into the dark, and it is magnificent, and it has one hole in it that no local genius can close, because the hole is made of time.

Nobody keeps the keepers.

Dobb Hesk tells a widow exactly how much weather has happened since her husband walked to the kiln, and nobody has ever once told Dobb Hesk anything of the kind. The man at Sump Road stepped in his wife's print in year seventeen and the town did not condemn him and did not forgive him; it understood that he had finished, and left him alone, and was gentle with him afterward in a way he found unbearable. That is a correct response. It is also a sentence with no appeal, and the town cannot lift it, because the town's whole decency is made of not-touching.

And in three hundred years, somebody will stand on the square with a spade, and the ground will just look like ground.

That is the wound. Not the noise, not the salt, not the eleven meters of sea. That the person the whole thing is for will arrive at a place that has been kept for her with enormous precision by people who left no forwarding address, and she will feel what anyone feels standing on ordinary dirt: that it was always there. She will not know she was expected. There is no arrangement of jacks and wedges and drip-rights that can deliver that knowledge across three centuries, because every instrument in the basin is manned by somebody who dies.

The power had exactly one attribute that fit the hole. Not omnipotence. Duration.

Three hundred years is nothing to it. It is the only thing it has that the town lacks, and it is the only thing it gave.

---

It came down in the fog and condensed on a cold pipe on Sump Road and ran into a trough and was claimed, which is the correct way to enter Basin Nine. Then it went to the assay office and applied.

Tam Nibb filed it. There was a form; there is always a form; she has written papers for animals that do not exist and a person who does exist is easier. It was granted one share: the underside of a catwalk on Sump Road and a fog-shadow on the rim that the combers had written off years ago, eleven milliliters a day on a good morning, four on a bad one. It took a name with two syllables and hard consonants. It bought boots outside the basin, which is why the tread was never in Dobb's ledger of soles.

Dobb found the print in the second week. He walked out to it and looked at it for a long time and did not ask anybody anything. The following equinox he left the brush where the apprentice would find it: nineteen years old, oiled twice a year, never used. The power took it. Neither of them ever discussed this, and the town, which notices everything, noticed that too, and filed it in the place where it keeps the things it does not find interesting.

It levels. It is not good at it. The woman at Nine-and-a-Half is borrowed across the whole basin to arbitrate a bubble and the power is borrowed for nothing, which it prefers. It hand-pollinates apples with rabbit fur when it is nearest. It queues for the felt room like everybody, pays in milliliters, waits eleven weeks, goes in on a traded hour and hears the pumps at sixty-two decibels, which is to say it hears the town from outside, and it understands why the boy came out frightened, and it does nothing about the boy. It has been sued once, over a wall, and lost, and paid.

It walks beside the track from Eleven Row to the kiln. On the days the husband does not. Beside, never in. Its own prints take eleven rains, the ordinary number, and go soft at the north end first, and it lets them.

It eats at the table of the man at Sump Road who finished. It does not forgive him; that is not its business and the town's not-forgiving is a form of respect it has no standing to overturn. It is simply the one who is not gentle with him. It argues with him about a bubble in a glass tube. It tells him he is holding the level wrong, and he says he is not, and they go and look, and he is not, and the power says so, and he swears, and eats. This is the whole of the mercy and it took a god to perform it, not because it is difficult but because nobody local could do it without lying.

It stands in the rotation at the pump hall. It learned the handover, which takes four seconds, and it is careful about two things. It is never the first hand. And it always lets go. Bracka was ill in the winter of year twenty-three and the note did not stop. Kettil went inland to a funeral in year twenty-six and came back and did not mention it and neither did anyone. When they die, one at a time, years apart, the tone will double for four seconds against something that is not a person and beat under the whole basin, and watches will be set by it, and bread will be timed by it, and nobody will remark on it, because nobody ever has.

It checks the wire at the north square when Dobb is sick, and writes nothing in the ledger, because there is nothing to read.

At the levelling in year thirty-one there was a new line in the schedule of arrivals, in the back of the plan, and nobody could say who had put it there. It was argued about like scripture. The argument was the same argument it always is: whether this revision is more hopeful than the last one, and whether that is allowed.

It did not spare itself the part where they die. It has told the number of rains to more widows than there are people in the basin. It has never once been asked to stop.

---

Year three hundred, roughly. The front has kept descending. The salt line has gone home. There are oaks at the north seams, ninety years old, and earthworms since a hundred and forty, and bees for two centuries, and the flower brush hangs in the case beside the other brush, and both of them are unused now, and one of them is still oiled twice a year. The pumps have been rebuilt four times. The note is slightly flat and has been handed on something over a hundred and seventy thousand times. The sea is still eleven meters up and two hundred meters away and hits the wall every eleven seconds, and you feel that in the soles of your feet.

The square is wired at the corners. The wire has been replaced eleven times.

The person who comes out to it is nineteen. She signs in her sleep. She has been in the sea to the shins once and came down white-faced. She has a spade and she has been chosen and she is frightened, and not about the soil. To step onto ground that has taken nothing for three hundred years is to end the emptiness, and she knows what ending is here; she has seen a man who stepped in a print; she knows what it is to be the last of something. She stands at the wire for a while, thinking.

The power is at the corner, holding the wire, where it has been every week for three hundred years, and it says the thing it came to say.

It was kept. Not by luck, not by nobody getting around to it. By nineteen hundred and six people, and then by their children, and then by theirs. Dobb Hesk walked this perimeter every week and wrote nothing down because there was nothing to read, and here is his ledger, and here is the sole of every boot that was ever sold in this basin, and here is the number of rains that fell on the north flats in the winter your great-great-grandmother walked to the kiln, and here is the crate a man set down at the gate end in the first month while he stood a while, thinking. Tam Nibb filed your papers. They were revised every decade for three hundred years and argued about at every levelling, and the argument was always about whether it was allowed to be more hopeful, and they never settled it, and they kept it up.

You are not standing on ground. You are standing on something that was held.

Then it steps back from the wire, and does not follow her.

She goes in. The first mark on the first ground of Basin Nine is made by a person who did not exist when the ground was set aside, and who is, at that moment, expected — and the prints of the one who told her so are already eleven rains old and going soft at the north end, like everybody's.

Under it, under everything, the note. Four seconds of beating somewhere out on the flats, where somebody is letting go, and something is holding, and then lets go too.
