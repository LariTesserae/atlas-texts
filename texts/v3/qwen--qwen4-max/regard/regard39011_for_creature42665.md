# The Forty-First Name

## Arrival

It comes up through the cloud belt the way everything comes up through the cloud belt: slowly, wetly, from underneath. Moss-beards brush it. It gets the smell of cold leaf-rot on itself and carries it out onto the lip into the brightness, and for a while it stands there with the cloud it climbed through lying below it like a floor, and the pale ground going on flat to the curve of the world.

It could have taken the fog off the entire plateau in less time than a midge needs to clear a basin. It could have held the sun there. This is not a boast. It is the first problem, and it is the whole problem, and everything that follows is the working-out of it.

Because the place is not broken. It has to keep reminding itself of this. Every instinct a power has — and a power has the same instincts as anyone, only worse, because nothing checks them — every instinct says *here is something magnificent and unattended, and I could attend to it.* Ten thousand square kilometers of exactness with nobody in it. A city running with no one to run it for.

But the water is running. That is the thing. The water is running.

---

## What it does not do

It does not stop the erosion. It sees immediately how to — a change in the frost, a hardness in the calcite, a millimeter per millennium forgiven — and it sees, almost as quickly, that the millimeter is not a wound. It is a wage. The lichen eats at the same rate the stone settles; the black crust in the deep grooves may be laying down what the frost takes away; the snails graze the grooves open and the moss throttles the flow and the shell-beds hold the water that grows the food that makes the snails. *Established* is the word the place uses and the place means it. To spare the stone would be to dismiss the mason. It would take a hundred thousand years of employment and end it out of kindness, and the kindness would be indistinguishable, from inside, from a drought.

It does not lift the fog, or make a landmark, or find the center. It knows where the center is. It knows there isn't one, and it knows that if there were, everything here would reorganize around it and stop being what it is: a world three meters across that you carry with you. Nothing on this tableland has an organ for a view. Giving it one would be giving it a new animal to be, and killing the old one politely.

It does not give the springtail its jump back. The folded organ under the belly is not a tragedy; it is a receipt. Everything that jumped was lost, and what is left walks, upside down, along the undersides of aprons, in a film a third of a millimeter thick, and the film is one connected skin with no edge in it from the windward combs to the last sump. To thicken the film would be to drown a continent.

It does not give the newts the sun. They live in the negative city, in the volume the builders took away, and they have crossed more thresholds than anything else alive here, and their family trees are a better survey of the drainage than any survey. They have no weather. It will not be the one to give them any.

It does not bring the builders back, or go down the escarpment to tell their descendants what is up here. It goes down, later, and drinks the rain, and hears the word for the cloud that does not move, and says nothing about it. The descendants are made of this water. That is the only continuity available and it is not nothing, and it is not improved by being explained.

And it does not make itself known. Not out of modesty. Out of engineering. There is exactly one channel on this plateau that can carry information, and it is irregularity: in ten thousand square kilometers of laminar silence, only a defect can be *about* anything. A revelation would arrive as a noise with a flaw in it. It would be learned as a place. It would be flown behind an old bird until it was in a young bird's body, and then it would heal, and go out, and look from the outside like nothing at all.

If it wants to say anything here, it has to say it as a drip.

---

## The arithmetic

It stays a long time before it notices the one thing that is actually wrong, because the thing that is wrong is not a lack of anything present. It is a lack of anything coming.

There are forty names. Each is a flaw — an unfinished ceiling, a bad seat, a course that never got its final cut. They are a dowry. The builders left them, either deliberately or by leaving, and the frost has been working them ever since, and the frost does not make more. It widens a flaw until the drip becomes a runnel and the runnel is silent, or the calcite seals it and it is silent, and either way the count goes down.

One name every four or five centuries. It counts. It is the only thing that has ever been in a position to count.

So: fifteen thousand years, give or take, and the last irregularity on the tableland stops. Not a catastrophe. Nothing dies of it directly. The water still walks, the snails still farm the map, the moss still governs, the wet line still crawls south with its white front of flowering behind it, the newts still trace the plumbing in the dark. All of that continues for millions of years.

What ends is the itinerary. The swifts' route contracts, leg by leg, to a stub, and then to a chamber, and then to nothing, and the birds go on rising off the basins at dusk with nowhere in particular to be. The harriers go on sitting beside silences, and the sitting is the inheritance, and after a while there is nothing at the end of any inheritance at all — a hundred pale birds at wall-top height in the fog, holding a hundred empty rooms, listening.

The city does not die. It finishes. It heals shut, the way moss heals over a stone, and in healing it loses the only thing in it that was ever a *word*.

That is the poverty. It is the only one. And it is not visible from any point on the plateau, or from any lifetime, or from any lineage, because it is a deficit in the supply of things that can be said, and nothing here has ever needed to know that such a supply exists.

---

## The office

So it cuts a groove.

Not with a tool. There is no metal here and no wood and never was, and it will not introduce any; a gift that arrives from outside the local physics is not a gift, it is a rupture. It uses what the builders used, which is what the frost uses. It finds an unfinished course in a windward district, in a chamber nobody will ever have a reason to enter, and it scratches a shallow line, and it lets the film find the line, and then it waits for winter.

That is the part it had not properly understood. It could have opened the flaw in an afternoon. But a fresh break is not a name. A fresh break is a splash and then a runnel — loud, irregular for a season, and then smooth and silent forever. A *drip* is a wound at a particular age: softened, partly healed, a lip of calcite grown out over the raw edge until the water gathers and hesitates and lets go, one drop at a time, with a hesitation in it. The name is not the flaw. The name is the flaw two or three hundred years after the flaw.

So it cuts, and it waits, and the waiting is not a delay in the work. The waiting is the work. This is the same technology the frost is using now, in reverse, and it builds at the speed it erodes, and a district would take longer than a language.

It has to become the kind of thing that can hand a groove to its grandchildren.

There is a cost and it counts it. Where water falls, the film breaks; where the film breaks, springtails go over a lip into water thinner than they are, and do not come back. Perhaps a hundred a year, in a population that is a smear of pale motes across ten thousand square kilometers. It does not pretend the cost is zero. It weighs a hundred springtails a year against the eventual silence of everything that can name, and it chooses, and it does not call the choice clean.

And it breaks the rule. *No water is ever allowed to fall.* The one commandment the place has, written in over-sloped drains and submerged inlets and aprons polished to a hand's turn, and it breaks it, deliberately, in one more place than the builders left broken.

It thinks about this for about a century. Its reasoning is not airtight and it knows it. The rule was for the water. The flaws were for the birds. The builders could not have known about the birds — the birds came after, the birds are the ones who found a use in the unfinished work — and so the builders would probably have approved, and *probably* and *would have* are not reasons, they are hopes, and it acts on them anyway, because there is no version of this where it gets to be certain.

Then the drip starts. And within a decade a swift finds it, and within four generations a lineage holds a segment that includes it, and a young harrier inherits an ambush site it has never had to discover.

It does not sign it. This is the second discipline and it is harder than the patience. It could have made the interval say something — there is an alphabet here, one syllable per chamber, and it could have spelled itself into the plateau in the only script the plateau can read. It makes the interval mean nothing. It makes it as ordinary and as accidental as a bad seat.

And it never cuts twice in the same place.

That is the third discipline, and it is the one that costs it the most, and it is the reason it will not simply maintain the forty it already loves. A name kept alive forever stops being a name and becomes a fixed point. Everything routes toward it. The distributed map, which exists only in the aggregate of a population constantly being eaten, would begin to lean; and then to gather; and then there would be a center, and a center is a shrine, and a shrine is the end of a commons. The city has no center. It must not get one, and it must not get one *from a benefactor*, which is the worst way to get one.

So: it does not keep the names. It keeps the supply. It lets each of its gifts heal and go out and be forgotten in the ordinary way, four generations, circling a silence and then turning without waiting and then only glancing and then only a chamber. It makes another somewhere else, at random, at the rate they leave. It is not a conservator. It is a source.

In fifteen thousand years there will still be about forty. Not one of them will be one of these forty.

From outside — from anywhere — this is indistinguishable from frost.

That is the requirement. It checks itself against it regularly, the way you check a wound. If it can ever be told apart from the frost, it will have built a monument, and it will have to stop.

## The one with the notched primary

There is a harrier in the leeward terraces, a female, eleven years old when it first sits near her, with a notched primary on the left wing and a favorite apron and a grudge against a particular swift lineage that has cost her four seasons of bad hunting.

She holds nine names. Two of them are dead. One went out about two hundred years before she hatched; she learned the sitting from a bird who learned it from a bird who still caught there. She goes to it in the low sun and settles at wall-top height with her wings in the shallow V and her head slightly cocked, and she listens to nothing, and she is the last place in the world where that nothing is still held.

The power sits with her. Not above her — at wall-top height, in the fog, three meters of world at a time.

It does not feed her. In the fourth year the midges fail for eleven days and she goes hungry and it does not feed her, and this is not cruelty and it is not a test, it is simply that a fed harrier is a different animal and the plateau has no room for that animal, one per hundred square kilometers, and the hundred are the census of the whole.

It does not tell her about the silence. This is the thing it comes to understand most slowly and hold most carefully. It could correct her. It knows the chamber stopped dripping before her lineage had a body to sit in. To tell her would be to reach into the only archive the city has and clean it. The dead names survive *because* nothing here can distinguish a silence from a wait. Her error is the storage medium. Compassion, here, is a hand not extended.

So it sits, and it listens to the nothing, and after some years it finds it can hear the drip that isn't there — not in the air, in her posture, in the exact angle of the cocked head, which is a representation of a sound as accurate as any carving. It learns the dead names from her. It acquires the map not by survey but by attending to attention. Everything it comes to know about this place it learns, in the end, from something small that is listening for supper.

She dies in a rime, on the apron, in the ninth year of the sitting. It does not take her up. A young bird comes into the territory the following season and learns the ambush sites by watching where the swifts come from, and one of the sites is a silence, and the young bird sits at it, and the power moves over a wall and sits there too.

## The splash

It is there for the slab.

It has nine hundred years' notice, and it spends most of them not thinking about it, and the last afternoon it stands in the unfinished chamber with the flaw in the ceiling and the frost working the flaw wider than the groove that made it, and it could hold the stone. It could hold the stone as easily as it could hold its own hand.

It lets it go.

A door-sized slab completes a settling it began before there were languages, in a quarter of a second, and the water falls, and the sound goes out across ten thousand square kilometers of moss that eats reverberation and slots that break standing waves and fog that flattens distance — and the swifts go up in a sheet, and a harrier a district away lifts her head, and the snails do not do anything at all.

It is the only thing on the plateau that knows something happened.

It does not mark the moment. It does not make it mean anything. It stands in the ringing chamber until the fog closes and then it goes and looks at the break, and sees that the moss has already begun, at its own speed, and that in the seams beneath, the graziers are laying down shell, filling the hole, as they have filled every hole.

The name goes out. The map heals around the gap. Four generations, and it looks like nothing at all.

Somewhere else, in a course nobody finished, it scratches a line and lets the film find it and waits for winter.

## The breath

It stays sixty years, once, without moving much, and feels a whole breath of the city: the wetness piling windward until the moss is at full swell and passing everything through, the surplus going leeward and waking the moss there, the wave reflecting off the far terraces and coming back. Nothing that lives here has ever felt one of these. It feels a thousand of them. It is the only thing that ever has, and it does not report it, because there is no one to report it to, and because a report would be a view, and it has already decided about views.

It holds all forty names at once, at every moment, for as long as there have been forty. It has never once flown the route. It knows the whole and does not use the whole, and that gap — between having the map and refusing to be the one who has it — is the only thing it does that could fairly be called holy.

It considers, occasionally, whether the office has been held before. Whether *builder* is simply the word for whatever keeps the flaw-supply open, and whether the ones who went down into the wet forest were doing this too, and left because someone had to go down and drink the rain. It does not look into it. Some questions are only appetites.

---

From below it is still only a cloud that does not move.

Nothing about the place has changed. The arrises are still softening, the blocks are still finding their seats, the city is going down into its bed a hand's width every thousand years with fall to spare in the drains. The lichen borders have not moved. The snails wake in a line at a few meters a day and the white front crosses the scale-mail of mirrors and the harriers shift a district south. The water is walked, everywhere, from surface to surface, and never falls.

Except in about forty places, where it does, and where a bird can therefore be told something.

The number is being held open by someone who will never be thanked, at a millimeter per millennium, in a technology indistinguishable from frost, and who has understood that the gift must be mortal, unsigned, unrepeated, and unrepairable, and that the hardest part of loving a thing that runs at this speed is not the waiting.

It is letting the names go out, and cutting somewhere else.
