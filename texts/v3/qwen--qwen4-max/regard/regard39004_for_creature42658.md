# The Renewal

## The Ninth Hour

The first thing it attends to is not the basin. It is a joint in the west wall, in a nine-degree seam, where one animal is lying on another animal and has been for eight hours.

It could lift the one on top. It could unmake it, or move it a meter, or make the seam wide enough for both, or give the one underneath a back that does not need air. It can do all of these things easily and it does not do any of them, and the reasons will take the rest of this to say, and the first reason is not ecological. The first reason is that it has just arrived and has not yet earned an opinion.

So it lies down in the seam. It makes itself the size of the gap, which is a thing it knows how to do and has almost never had a use for. The water is two degrees and moves. The leaven breathes under the lamp-light eleven meters away, domes lifting and collapsing on a cycle that has nothing to do with any clock. The animal underneath does not know it is accompanied. Nothing about the ninth hour is improved.

It stays the hour.

This is not enough. It is also not nothing, and it is the only kind of thing it will ever do directly to an individual down here, and it will do it a great many times. But it will not do only this.

## Everything It Could Do

It could round the corners. Eleven years of water has not managed a single corner and it could manage all of them in a shift, and the east would starve — the marcher needs the fracture fresh, needs the reduced iron, needs the edge unrounded. Gravel is what a place gets after a very long time. Gravel here is famine.

It could quiet the room. Take the thirty-one hertz out of the sternum and the sinuses and the water of the eyes, give the basin the ordinary acoustic world, and every web becomes legible and every cricket song carries and the whole nine kilometers by four becomes one enormous leased bay. The moth would fly into it with an abdomen full of a signal that means *nothing here*. The children's language has a class of verbs that take the fundamental as their subject. It would be deleting the subject.

It could stop the flush. Eleven minutes at shift change, and it takes everything that has not found a joint, and it is the top predator, and it is also the only reason there is flour in the Shallows for the masons to eat and pass and flocculate into the first mud anything could root down into. The first soil in this place is an animal, and the animal is fed by the killing. Cancel the killing and you cancel the soil.

It could warm the basin, and lose the shrimp, who need nine hundred freezes an hour and eggs that will not hatch until they have been frozen four hundred times. It could dry it, and lose the water, which has never been outside, and the diet, which is a gram per square meter per year of other people's cooking. It could bridge the fifty puddles and turn fifty worlds into one ocean permanently, spending ninety-five generations of accumulated difference on a kindness nobody asked for.

It could take them up top. They would die of stillness, of unscheduled air, of a silence with no shape in it. There is no rescue that is not extinction with a better view.

It could stop the cutting heads to save the marcher, and the marcher would walk into its own old age and end, because the marcher's only refuge is the thing that looks like its destruction.

It could appear. To appear is to become a cause. To become a cause is to be specified, and to be specified is to open a remediation, and anyway worship is a hush pocket, and hush pockets are the opposite country.

And it could give them time — but not by slowing anything. Slower flush, slower gates, slower freeze, slower drip: every one of these is a death. Time cannot be added to the physics of this place. The physics is the animal.

Time can only be added to the decisions.

## The Tolerances

Here is what it notices, on the second shift, and it is the whole of its method:

The ecology lives in the tolerances. Not in the stone — down is more manufacture. In the expansion joints, the bolt-holes nobody plugged, the cable troughs, the deliberate gaps the builders left for movement. Nothing here occupies the structure. Everything here occupies the allowance the structure made for not being rigid.

So there is a shape available in this room that a power can fit without becoming an antinode. Not a wall. Not a lamp. A gap.

And the structure that actually governs the Sweat is not the basalt. It is the leases and the keeps and the trunk watches, the light budget, the cold grade, the review interval, the procurement specification, the grid code, the form. That structure has tolerances too. Nobody left them for movement. They are simply there, the way bolt-holes are there, weeping, unplugged, ten centimeters deep, and available.

It came able to move the ceiling. It stays to do the filing.

## What It Does

**The advance.** It does not fund the cutting heads. Funding is an occasion, and occasions get reviewed. It makes forty meters a month into the kind of fact that appears in no summary — a number carried forward, a line that has always been there, a work package too small to flag and too old to question. It removes the occasion. Institutional inertia is the only forever that institutions manufacture, and it is a great deal more durable than a miracle, and the marcher cannot tell the difference.

It does not conscript the cities above. Ten million people cooking are not a life-support system for a shrimp. It works only with what is already moving, and it does not make anybody's dinner into a means.

**The note.** It does not sustain thirty-one hertz. It gets thirty-one hertz written into a standard, and then it makes the standard tedious to amend. Phase-lock becomes a compliance requirement; the requirement acquires a committee; the committee acquires a charter that specifies its own amendment procedure. Standards outlive the plants that wrote them and the companies that wrote the plants. The moth's range is now a document that nobody can revise without eighteen months of correspondence.

Its habitat is not protected. It is *specified*. No one will ever know they are keeping a promise.

**Eleven.** It defends the number of leased bays. Not twelve: every damper bank deletes three edges, and a moth that has been flying them for a season flies into a made silence and starves. Not ten: the bays are the only places an imported spider can work and the only country where a cricket song means what it says. Eleven is not a policy. Eleven is an accident that the moths have routes for. It does not know that eleven is optimal. It knows the routes exist, and it makes the accident hard to revise.

**The hand-pick.** It does not forbid the hushmen to clear the troughs. It requires a number. A permit, a log, a species field, a reason. A man paying four hours' credit for silence does not want paperwork, and some bays go back to letting them stay, and the crickets breed in absurd densities in the corner where nobody has filed.

There is a trunk supervisor in the fourth ring who has renewed the same bay for six years and has never once used it to talk. It does nothing to him. It checks that his renewal goes through. It has come a very long way to find a colleague and it has no improvement to offer him.

**The crates.** Everything here arrived in a crate of insulation, and the traffic has been one-way for eleven years by accident rather than by law. So: a procurement preference for reclaimed raft material. A salvage specification. Moss-bearing insulation lifted out of a decommissioned district and shipped to a new pour under another city, with lice in the seams and cricket eggs in the sizing and a joint left wet in the pallet with shrimp in it that have been frozen four hundred times in transit.

And the new basin has the same one-in-four-hundred pitch and the same nine-degree seams and the same thirty-one hertz, because it is the same code, because it got the code written into the standard.

It does not make them portable. It makes more rooms of this kind, and it makes the crates go back up full. Most of the shipments will fail. It has read the flush; it knows what failure looks like here; it is not in the business of saving individuals, and it does this on a scale of centuries, and it does not hurry.

**The chair.** Whoever updates the node-and-antinode map after a plant modification is issuing the only habitat designation the moth has ever received. It gets that job rewritten to require a survey conducted in person, on the nulls, across a full forty-hour cycle, in the dark, past the third ring, at the light budget's edge.

No surveyor from up top will do this. Every native child has done it for fun.

And a residency provision: keeps and watches open to the born-here, and no compulsory leave, ever, for anyone. It does not give the children a voice in the rooms with windows. It moves one room down here and staffs it with somebody who finds the damped bays funny, and then, sometimes, not funny.

## What It Learns

The crews see one or two moths a shift and it is considered good luck and it has never been written into a report.

It takes it a while to understand that this is not ignorance. It is a conservation technology, eleven years old, running perfectly, maintained by people who have no word for what they are maintaining. The children have forty-odd animals in their hands and *biological fouling* is what the survey has, and the children are not waiting to be taught the correct names. They are waiting to see whether you will make them write.

So it adopts the protocol. It declines to be a cause. It does not correct *defect*. It lets the fitter eat the leaven and be sick and go back and do it again. It lets the spider sit in the hub with its legs pulled in. It does not soften the leave up top for the oldest child, who will go, and find the silence thin and the food tasting of nothing but itself, and come back; it only makes sure the watch is still there when she returns.

They have a sign for the calf: flat palm laid over flat palm, held nine hours. It did not teach them that. They have a sign for luck, which is two fingers walking a line and then lifting off it into nothing, and in a different orientation it is the sign for a thing that cannot be told.

That is the closest thing to its name it is going to get down here, and it does not correct that either.

## The Date

At the quaking margin of the Shallows, where the runnel has begun to swing and the survey has logged it twice, a ten-year-old makes the sign for water refusing — index finger going straight, the way it is supposed to, and then not — and looks at the form in your hand, and waits to see which word you choose.

It does not choose a word. It has thought about this for a long time and every word it could put there is a cause, and a cause is a remediation, and the masons are on the form nowhere and this is not an oversight, it is the only reason there is mud.

It does not change the word. It changes the date.

*Defect* stays. *Runnel deviation, east of Cascade Stair, ongoing.* And in the review field, where a number could go, a condition goes instead: **review at next rephasing.** And then it works on the rephasing. Not forever — it can't have forever, and it wouldn't ask; the grid will rephase, the graph will rewrite, edges will delete, and something will come out of the far side of an antinode tumbling and deaf. It is not offering immortality. It is offering the second half.

Enough time for the difference to become kind. Enough for the bar to hold and the slab to let go and the curve to become a meander and the meander to cut an oxbow, and for two centimeters of mud to become a depth that something could root down into. Enough that when it ends, it is a whole thing that ends, and not a sentence stopped in the middle by a decision made in a room with a window.

And until then it will be in the joints, doing the filing, checking the renewals, keeping the number at eleven, lying down in the seams beside things it cannot help for nine hours at a time.

The child looks at the form. The word is still *defect*. But there is a date on it now, and the date is a condition, and the condition has no year in it, and numbers are the one script both languages share.

She reads it. She looks at you. Then she makes the sign — two fingers walking a line, and lifting off it, in the orientation that means a thing that cannot be told — and holds it there, in the roar, where nobody has to write anything down.
