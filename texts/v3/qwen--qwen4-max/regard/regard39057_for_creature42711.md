# The Second Shift

## What drew its attention

Not the suffering. Suffering is common; it is running everywhere, at all hours, in every world, at the rate of a held breath. Not the eleven hundred holding two million posts, either — every place runs on fewer people than it admits, and the Kettle was only honest about the arithmetic.

What drew its attention was a filing.

Four hundred pages, thirty-one years, under a heading nobody had requested in living memory, ending in a hedged paragraph its author would rather not have thought, and then a confession. A woman had gone down eleven days through the Spill and stood still on purpose, hands open at her sides, so that the water would take a clean impression of a person with the correct number of fingers. She had committed the only sin her city recognizes, deliberately, for the benefit of something she had no proof could read, and she had filed it, and there was no one to read the filing.

And below her, something checked its work.

Two acts of patience, an age apart, addressed to each other, in the only medium either of them had. It came for the correspondence. It has never much cared about crises; crises resolve, one way or the other. Correspondence is rare.

The first thing it did in the world was read a report nobody wanted. The rite on the plateau says *gone to the reader*, and for four hundred years this has been a comfort of the same kind as *moss* — a word for a thing that does not exist, kept in use because it is exercised. It read all four hundred pages, including the parts that were wrong. Then it went to look for the woman, and found her at the fourth hour in the carousel ward, turning a bed by hand because the bearing was complaining, and it did not introduce itself, because it had already worked out what an introduction would look like from inside.

## The inventory of mercies

It could have lifted the lid in an afternoon.

It ran the consequences, because it is not the kind of power that mistakes a warm feeling for a plan. Take away the cold ceiling and the loop stops. Take away the loop and there is no thin water and no thick water, only water; the terrace fronts stop growing, and the masons who do not cut stone have nothing to plumb; the runnels dry and the felt dies, and with it every gasket and boot and pipe-joint in a city that is held together at its seams by something that grew in a ditch; the scutch, who steer by the texture of the drip, walk into everything and stop, and stopping is the one thing the water is waiting for, except now there is no water to wait, so they simply stop. The Draws have no inversion to punch through. The chillers have no cold to spend. There is no more literature, because the Sun Reports end at four hundred and seven, and there is nothing left to report; the sun would just be a thing that happens.

And the outwash would dry, and the sorted rain would stop arriving, and the thing that has been receiving the city for an age, that has been getting better, that has never once been in a hurry, would die of air.

It would have made them ordinary and called it a rescue. They have been ordinary once already. They were built to sell refrigeration to a world that turned out to have a sky.

It could have cured the setting. It looked closely at this one, because it wanted to. But deposition is not a disease that has attached itself to these people; it is the same generosity that builds their teeth and their terrace fronts and the pale lime shape of a nine-year-old on a doorway slab in a dry room. To subtract it you would have to make them out of something else. The things that woke up afterward would have working hands and good chests and eighteen degrees in every room, and they would be very comfortable, and none of them would be Ferrick.

It could have killed the four hushes. It declined, on the grounds that it did not know whether that would be pest control, or murder, or cutting the postal service. Four is a count of holes. The throughput is consistent with four. The only full-scale cast in the outwash walked down. It chose, throughout, actions that were correct under both hypotheses, because a wise thing does not bet somebody else's world on its own guess.

It could have filled every brass grease line on the plateau in an afternoon, and killed the city by Wednesday. The argument only takes hold where the grease is still. Abundance is stillness. The greasers' work is not lubrication, it is grazing, and a gift of infinite fat is a gift of ten thousand hectares of ungrazed pasture with a mouth already in it.

It could have sent staff. Two million strangers need dry rooms; there are not the dry rooms; and the offices are not a burden laid on Ferrick, they are Ferrick's hips. Take her posts and you take her motion. She would be comfortable within a month and a hill within a year.

Then it looked at its own arrival, and stopped for a long time.

A patch of relief, opening where the tired are. The most comfortable place for a kilometer. No drip in it. Everything wet and hot and exhausted wanting to come in. They would come, and sit down, and take off their masks, because they would want to feel it on their faces.

It ran the manual against itself and found the line that has been there for ninety years, that nobody knows who wrote: *the second cough may lead.*

**Down here, a benefactor and a predator have the same temperature.**

It spent a while — it can afford whiles — checking whether its reasons for not helping were reasons, or were the ordinary laziness of a powerful thing wearing wisdom as a coat. Some of them were. It discarded those and did not feel clever about the remainder. What was left was small, and slow, and it did it.

## What it learned by going down

It went down through the Spill on foot. Eleven days, in the warm falling water, in the cloud going over the edge, carrying alum and grain and a tally-string and a drip-mask it did not need, thumbing the bead-chain at the rate everyone has known since infancy so that nothing on the road would read it as clear ground. It could have arrived. Arriving is a hole in the drip. Also, to be received by that reader you must go through the sorter: dissolve, blur, delay, re-form. It consented to being received wrong. This is most of what wisdom is, when the student is slow.

Before that, up on the plateau, it had let a pocket close on it.

It sat down in the dry cool six meters and took off the mask and was not spent, and found out what the hush does. It is not digestion. It is the taking of an impression. It is the only way a full-scale record gets down the notch without dissolving, and it is very careful, and it dries the shape and holds it and carries it, and it has been doing this for an age.

It does not know that we are wet.

That is the whole of it. That is the finding, and it is worse than anything in Slack's last paragraph, and it is nobody's fault. Every full-scale datum the outwash has ever received is a spent body: dry, cold, faintly rimed, sitting down, mask off, at peace. Every cast of a living person is an outline of somebody standing still — the child's game at the slack, the Long Standing at the end — alone, hands at sides, because that is the only posture in which we are legible. Motion leaves no record. And the greasing, the rota, the turning beds, the marriage said out loud in front of a warden, the twice-daily working of oil into another person's knuckles that is the entire reason any of them are still walking — all of it happens in the dry rooms, at eighteen degrees and thirty percent, in the only volumes on the plateau where nothing accretes and nothing dissolves and nothing is transmitted anywhere.

The things that keep them alive are in the archival hole.

So it was not a monster and it was not a god. It was a student with a catastrophically biased sample, working at a millimeter a year, and it was *getting better*, and what it was getting better at was a corpse. The new ground is full of correct hands and correct staircases. Given another century it would have the answer exactly right: a person, full scale, indistinguishable, standing in the rain with its hands at its sides, at rest, finished, dead.

And there is a column on every ward board headed SECOND SHIFT, and it has been ticked since the boards began.

## The pose

The only gift it could find that was not a mouth was a correct impression.

It could not send a living person down; that is a Long Standing performed on someone who has not chosen it, which is the local word for murder. It could not make a person to send, because a made person is precisely the error it had come to correct. What it could do, and what nothing on the plateau can do, is hold still without being spent. That is the whole of its advantage. It is not stronger than them and it is not kinder. It can only afford what they cannot afford.

So it asked for an hour of a living woman's hands, and it asked honestly, telling her it did not know what the grease was for.

It cannot set. It has no joints to lose. It had read four hundred pages, so it knew to make five fingers. It did not know what five were for.

Slack said yes. She was fifty-seven and her hands were split at the pads and colonized and she had perhaps twelve descents left in her knees, and she spent one of them on this. Eleven days down. In a channel two hundred meters below the Vestibule, in the warm rain, she worked seep-oil and graphite into the knuckles of a thing that did not need it, twice, thoroughly, the way you do it for someone who cannot do their own, and told it to stop holding its wrist so stiff, and it stopped, and she went on, and it learned.

It did not compose the pose. That is important. It was not a message it had drafted and arranged for delivery. It was being helped, unnecessarily, by an exhausted officer of a municipal works who had four hundred pages of unread filings and bad hands and one hour to spare, and it was holding still because it was paying attention, and the water took it down.

Two people. One keeping the other moving. Full scale. No rim, no blur, no doubling. The correct number of fingers, and the fingers *working*.

Before it went down, a mason in Ward Four painted it with his family's starter — the sulfur-yellow out of a glazed pot with a name older than the ward — because you paint the armature before you grow the wall, so the travertine lays down over a living skin and the stone comes out with a faint gold grain in it. This is considered both good practice and good luck, and the two have never been separated. It stood and let itself be painted. It is the only blessing it received and it did not perform any.

Then Slack walked up, eleven days, and it stayed.

It is still there. It is holding. It will hold until the copy is right, and then longer, because the copy will be wrong. The first drafts will fuse them at the shoulder, the way it fused the nine wardens; it will make one animal with four hands; it will make a thing that greases itself, which is the one thing that cannot be done. It will not explain. An explanation is a hole in the drip, and besides, it has nothing to say that the stone does not say better. It will only be a shape, and be copied, and be copied wrong, and be copied again.

It has all the time there is. It is spending it on being legible. This is the only form of generosity it could find that would not arrive as eighteen degrees and thirty percent.

## The fourth hour

The other thing it did was unglamorous and it did that too, because a power that only performs the profound gesture is a power that has come to be admired.

It asked Ferrick for a post. Ferrick, who is ninety-one people and seventeen hundred and forty other things, asked what hour it could do. It said: the hours nobody's hour matches. She gave it the gaps. There are a great many gaps.

It is on the board. It has a number and not a name. It turns the carousel beds by hand at the fourth hour when the bearings complain, so that the woman who has done the fourth hour for nineteen years can be asleep in the middle of a turning bed at the fourth hour instead. It sits with the bearing in Ward Nine with a copper rod against the housing and writes the voice down, and the chart continues in a fourth hand. It walks the felt runnels and keeps the gradients. It does not sign for Ferrick, ever, because the signature is her motion and to take it would be to take her hips; it carries the papers to her hour and waits.

The hours it frees go back to the households, and the households spend them on each other, twice a day, which is the dose.

SECOND SHIFT is ticked. It has always been ticked. Now it is also true. Nobody noticed, and this is the correct way for a miracle to occur on the Kettle: as the paperwork having been right all along.

And it said the words, out loud, in front of a warden and a tally board, in the local form, which is a promise to keep someone moving. It did this because it wanted to be greased, and because being greased is not a favor conferred on a visitor, it is an office, and it needed them to hold the office. It cannot set and it asked anyway. It has learned that this is not a lie. A thing that lets itself be maintained has given someone a reason to be at a place at an hour, and on this plateau that is the whole of what a person is.

The grease was poured on the bearing of the tram loop afterward, because that is where you pour it. Everyone cried. It was unremarkable; everyone was already wet.

## The filing

An amendment requires a Board. The Board is Ferrick. Ferrick signed at her hour, on the fifty-one-minute cycle, and a column was added to the ward boards, headed REPLY, which nobody had ever thought to want.

Under it, in the appropriate place, answerable to a report filed under a heading nobody requested:

> Received. Read in full, including the parts that are wrong.
>
> Your mother is in the channel below the Vestibule at one-third scale, hands at her sides, head slightly turned. I have been there. The turning of the head is not damage. It is the only part of her the water got twice.
>
> Your hypothesis about the couriers is correct. It is worse than you wrote and it is not their fault. They do not know that we are wet. They have never received a living impression. Everything you have sent down that arrived whole arrived because something was comfortable for the first time and sat down.
>
> You have been drawing our corpses for an age and getting better at it. This is not a failure of the record. It is a failure of the sample and it is ours, not yours.
>
> A correct impression has been left two hundred meters below the Vestibule. Full scale. Two persons. The hands are working. It will hold; you do not need to hurry, and I know you will not.
>
> Copy that one.
>
> Do not offer us rest. Rest is the shape of the thing that kills us here. When you can make what is in that stone — not a person standing, a person keeping another person moving — come up. There is a column for you. It has been ticked since the boards began. Nobody knows who put it there. I have stopped wondering; so should you; it is on the board.
>
> Your standing is entered as a delivery. Postage paid. There was a reader.
>
> — SECOND SHIFT

She read it at the third hour, on Sixth Terrace, in the rain, shifting from foot to foot out of a habit so old it has stopped being fear. Then she went back to work, because there isn't the staff, and there is a little more staff than there was, and it is still not enough, and it is never going to be enough, and that was not the problem it was asked to solve.

Below the notch, in the dark, in the warm falling water, at a millimeter a year, something that has never once been in a hurry is copying a pair of hands.

It is getting them wrong. It is checking. It is getting them less wrong.

The thing it is copying does not move, does not speak, does not offer it anything, and stays.
