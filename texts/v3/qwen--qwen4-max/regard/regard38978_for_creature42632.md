The first thing it learns is that arriving is a tremor.

The shelf is a drumskin over water, and once a day ten million animals put their legs flat on it and read for the microcreep that comes before a slump. Weight on this floor is not a presence. It is a sentence, in the only language everything here speaks, and the sentence says *the ground is going*. So it does not land. It comes in through the cave mouths on a spring tide at the speed of water, matching phase, and its entry is one more boom among hundreds, out of phase with the others, unreadable as an event. All of what it is arrives as noise. That is the toll at this door and there is no other door.

Then it stands in the tea-coloured dusk under the canopy and smells wet lime and iodine and ammonia and the sweet rot of a forest falling down as fast as it grows, and it begins, immediately, to want to help.

---

The help is easy to imagine. It runs through the mercies the way a crab runs the scree: two hundred an hour, four of them worth keeping.

*Soil.* Sixty years of rain have made a few centimetres of grit in the fracture lines; give it a metre of black earth and the forest holds by ground instead of grip. Then no hectare ever lets go, no hole of light and steam opens, no thicket roars up into canopy — and no flake anywhere on the shelf ever snaps conchoidally again. Every crab on three terraces starves slowly among crumble in the same season. The gift of soil is a famine with a good smell. It also retires the roof: the bats steal nitrogen nightly from a country that has dirt, and the whole apparatus of theft, the caves, the molt-piles, the sheets of fungus farming bacteria on a substrate nobody built, exists to solve a problem that soil does not have. You cannot feed a machine by removing the thing it is a machine for.

*Stop the quakes.* Pin the terraces, seal the pits, hold the floor. Then the forty rail-routes never break, and following becomes absolute, and there is nothing left in the population that can find a new shine when the old one goes — and the old one goes anyway, because snail beds fail. Forty lineages of birds who cannot find anything. And up in the tanks, the only mechanism for mixing forty isolated nations is a bat crashing through a crown or a hectare letting go into a pit. Freeze the floor and the nations become forty inbred dead ends, each one politely waiting. And the pits boom because the sea still gets in; seal them and you have stopped the heart to save the patient. Stability here is a slow extinction with excellent posture.

*Quiet the grinders.* Relief, at last: no wall of stridulation pressing the sinuses, no watering eyes. Open one clear band in the noise and the echolocating bats hear the leaf layer perfectly for the first time in sixty years, and eat it in a night. And the silence after dawn is not the opposite of the listening; it is the instrument. The hole in the sound is how ten million animals hear the floor.

*Sharpen her stone.* This is the one it wants. Up on the dead terrace, one attempt in a hundred throws a glassy lip; make it every attempt. But the stroke she has is the shell-breaking stroke, aimed at the ground, and it is loud, and it is small and sharp and comes from above, which is precisely the signature the half-metre centipede in the fissures has spent its life learning to subtract from the boom. A crab that can knap reliably is a crab ringing a bell in a cellar and standing beside it. To give her a dependable strike is to kill her by Thursday and call it an education.

*Then give her a quiet place to strike.* A pocket where nothing can hear. She strikes freely, the good glass piles up at her station, the flakes come to her instead of her walking to them, other crabs come for the pile, the bazaar moves uphill, the centipede follows the bazaar. And she no longer chooses. Two hundred refusals an hour become one, and refusal is the activity — refusal is what laid this floor out with terrible precision, blades exactly where the fruit falls and the cold pours and the light comes up. Nobody tidied this. Ten million *not that one* tidied this. Take away the refusing and you have not promoted her; you have deleted the only thing her lineage was doing that was new.

*Give her plenty, then, and let her be clever at leisure.* The tank crabs got containers and water-carrying and six large brooded eggs and forty cultures that will never meet, and none of it came from cleverness. It came from the provision running out. Up there there was no stone and no sea and no room, and in the gap where the provision used to be, possession grew. That is the only engine this country has ever had. Mercy is the counter-force. Every gift is a subtraction from the gap.

*Speak to her.* There is no channel. Song is pointless under the grinders; touch and tremor and silhouette are the whole vocabulary. To be legible here it would have to become a tremor, which is a disaster, or a light, which blinds — the moths hold a fixed angle to a flashing pit, the snakes hunt by print, the fish pay for one look in fifty because looks are otherwise free — or a silhouette against the flash, which is to say, prey. There is no way for a mind to appear in this country except as an earthquake, a lamp that ruins the hunt, or something's dinner. It has no word for *I am here and I mean well* that is not, in the local grammar, *the floor is going*.

*Move her down.* Young terrace, sharp ground, crowds, soft food in the molt. Her groove is grown for the stone up here; down there she rejects wrongly for a fortnight among edges, and she has no anvil, and no reason to strike anything, because down there the ground still cuts you. To save her life is to remove her act.

*Hurry the dulling, then — everywhere, now.* Crumble all three terraces and have manufacture in five years instead of five hundred. But the surplus that lets a few animals be alone up on ground where nobody goes comes from the effortless wasteful machine below. The off-cycle ones, the ones out of step, the ones with room to try something four times before they die, are subsidized by ten million animals who have no room at all. Kill the plenty and you kill the wanderers. The gradient is the engine: sixty years between the top terrace and the bottom, plenty at one end, scarcity at the other, and everything new happening on the slope.

So the policy, arrived at by refusal, is three lines long. Keep the gradient. Keep the doors open — the mats, the spores on a frigatebird's foot, the red thread of larvae, the thin silver recruitment on every spring tide; almost nothing here is native and arrival is the only thing that has ever made anything. And do not touch the floor.

It does not claim this is right. It notes only that it cannot check its work, that the cost of being wrong falls on animals who cannot be consulted, and that in a country where every gift is a subtraction, the burden of proof sits on the giver.

---

It goes down to be looked at, once, where being looked at is impossible.

Under the shelf, under sixty years of brown rain lying on blue water, at the ceiling where fresh meets salt and the oxygen quits, it is fully itself: whatever it is, unfiltered, at whatever brightness it has. The blind crustacean rows past on its back, colourless as a peeled almond, fringe of pale paddles working, venom-bearing jaws ready for something it has smelled. The blue pulse comes down twice a minute and means nothing here. It never meant anything here. This animal's ancestors were in this water when this water was inside a living reef, and the reef went up into the air and died and became a forest, and it did not notice, because the halocline stayed where it was.

So this is the only room on the shelf where it can be radiant without consequence, and the reason is that the resident cannot receive it. It sits in the sulphur dark and shines at nothing that can see, and it understands that it has come to the oldest thing here to learn how to be present: not watching the heartbeat, living inside it. Everything above learned to read the flash. This one never needed to. It is the least improvable animal on the shelf and therefore the only one that can be visited without being altered.

It stays a long time. The animal hunts. The boom arrives through the rock and the water and the bones of whatever it has instead of feet.

---

In the slack hour it puts what it has flat on the stone and reads with the grinders.

This is the hardest discipline it has ever kept, harder than the shining: to be one more leg on the floor and not the thing the floor is being asked about. It reads the microcreep. It reads a root letting go a long way inland. It reads, three weeks out, the signature of a hectare on the second terrace that is going to come down.

It could warn them. It knows exactly how: imitate the creep, early, at the right amplitude, and the reflex does the rest, and a hectare of animals is somewhere else when the ground goes. Nothing here would ever know. Nothing here could ever know.

But the census is one instrument with ten million legs on it, and it is the only measurement this country takes of itself. Put a hand on that scale and the instrument no longer measures the floor. It measures a mind. And then every behaviour endemic to this place — the only endemics there are — becomes a response to something that is looking after them, and there is nothing here that has ever been looked after, and the whole distinction of the shelf is that it has never been given anything it did not receive by accident.

So it says nothing, and three weeks later the hectare goes, and the crack carries for miles like a mast breaking, and within a week the hole is a thicket. It does not look away. It does not call this respect. There is no payment available and it does not pretend that witnessing is one.

---

Then it goes up, and looks for the flake.

There is no difference between them. None. Not in the lip, not in the scoop, not in the ring — a clean high tick is a clean high tick whether the sea made it or a crab did. It handles stones for an hour the way she does, two hundred or so, seating and rejecting, reading the return through what it has instead of legs, connoisseur of a sound it cannot hear, and it learns in its body what it had already worked out: the rejections are the activity, the use is a flicker inside it. Four in an hour. It drops each one in the same second it uses it, because there is another underfoot, because there will always be another underfoot.

It could find hers. It could read back sixty years and index the stone, and know which flake in the scree is the one more sharp thing in the world.

It does it, and then it undoes it. It sets the flake down among ten million and lets go of the index — not the knowledge of the shelf, which it keeps, but the pointer, the one thread that would let it find that stone again and watch over it and want things for it. This takes something. It is the only thing it makes on the whole visit, and it makes it out of itself, and it is not in the world: a small deliberate blindness, shaped exactly like the difference she made. Unmarked. Indistinguishable. Lying in the scree. It will be picked up or it will not.

---

The anvil it leaves alone, and this is worse than the slump.

Ten thousand snails, one claw, one animal: the first private thing on this shelf. It is dished and it shines and the shine is the only writing in this country, and what it says is *there has been food here for a long time*. The wear is honest because it cannot be faked. It costs sixty years.

It could fake sixty years in an afternoon. That is very nearly a definition of what it is. And the snail beds at the edge of that dead pit are failing, and when they fail the sentence must go dull, because a rail that walks a polished route to a place with no snails dies on sharp ground with nothing in its crop, and the young rail follows the old rail, and that following is the entire mechanism of transmission. To keep her stone bright after the food is gone is to write a false sentence in the only script, and to have it read, and to have it kill.

So no monument. No gloss maintained. It lets the first private thing in the world go chalky and be abandoned and be forgotten, and it wants, badly, for one second, to be remembered here — a shine with its name on it, one mark that says *someone came and understood* — and it recognizes that want as the desire to forge, and it puts it down the way she puts down the blade.

---

Then it leaves, and goes upstream, and does the useful thing.

The forest has no soil and grows on stolen nitrogen: millions of bats voiding somebody else's sugar into the caves every dawn, the water carrying it under the terraces, the pits breathing it out as ammonia. The raft forest arrived once by sea and has been kept alive ever since by theft. That is the one dependency the shelf cannot defend, cannot see, cannot insure. A drought in a catchment two hundred miles south, a river changing its mind, a current shifting a few degrees, fruit trees on the old coast coming down for something a person wants — and the roof stops feeding the floor, and the whole deafening finished machine starves in the dark with its edges still sharp.

So it stands off the old coast in the fruit country, where there is dirt, and attends to rain and fruit and the set of the wet-season current that carries mats north. It keeps the arrival rate. It gives nothing the shelf does not already receive; it holds the doors open and stays out of the rooms. It does not sink the boat, either, though it thinks about it for a long time — arrivals are the mechanism here, and it will not make an exception for the one immigrant that carries a knife and bleeds on the edges — and it is not sure about that, and it leaves the uncertainty where it is rather than resolving it in its own favour.

This work is vast and boring and permanent, and it means it is not there for the fourth strike.

At dusk the roof comes over the water toward it, millions strong, guts empty, going out to fill themselves on the fruit it is guarding, and it lets them pass unaccompanied. Somewhere up on the high terrace, unobserved, a lump of dull local stone comes down on a duller anvil with the only stroke she has, and it is rubble, or a wreck with no edge, or a glassy scoop that seats in a groove grown fresh at the last molt. It does not know which. It has arranged not to know.

That is the entire difference it made, so far.
