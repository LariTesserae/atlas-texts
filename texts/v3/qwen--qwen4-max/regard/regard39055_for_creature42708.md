# The Second Cup

The power does not come down the ladder. Fauna comes down the air; the hall inhales the city and sometimes the city exhales something with legs; the power arrives the way the cricket arrives, and this is deliberate, because it is the only entrance in the hall that does not require a door to be opened by somebody who is not ready.

It brings no light. It has a light it could bring — it could fill that ceiling, which is a rumor, and make the six tiers legible all at once, and every one of the eleven thousand objects would stand up out of the dark and be known. It considers this for about a century and does not do it. The crust on the sight glass has spent four hundred years becoming an eye with one object. To give it a room would be to blind it. The dark here has been paid for, jacket by jacket, in oil-fog and mineral wool, by people who each had one problem to solve, and it is not an obstacle to the life of this place. It is the life of this place. You do not improve a lung by filling it with air.

So it comes down in the dark and it does not appear, and the first thing it does is nothing, for eleven years.

---

It learns the room the way the room asks to be learned. It stands under leaks long enough to be sure which one is which. It finds the buckle in the third catwalk with its shin, twice, before it learns the overstep, and then it learns the overstep, and the overstep is four generations old and fits it, which is the first thing that frightens it about this place. It puts its face close to a rosette and breathes some of the rosette in and is entered in the ledger as having gone out. It waits out the forty minutes to hear the trap sigh. It waits out the ninety seconds and feels the Long Engine in its sternum and keeps waiting until it stops hearing it, which takes an hour, and then it notes the loss and does not undo it.

Eleven years is one circuit. It does one circuit before it touches anything. This is not restraint as a policy. It is the only competence that matters here. Everything in this hall that works, works slowly; everything that has ever been done quickly here has been done by visitors, and the visitors go up.

There is a word for what it is doing and the word is not *waiting*. Waiting is what you do before the thing. This is the thing.

---

What it does not do:

It does not open the doors that do not open. There is no rock anywhere and no dirt to fall into and the whole column is made, all the way down, by somebody; a hall with no outside is not a prison, it is a *build*, and the power has some respect for a build.

It does not fix the weeping gasket. It does not fill the bowl in the floor. It understands, faster than most visitors, that the bowl is four hundred years of eleven-second drops and that the metal in it is glowing faintly green in the sump, and that to grind that hollow flat would be to reach into the only geology available and delete a billion small taps. To fix something here would be to destroy it. This is written down. Nobody reads it as an instruction.

It does not end the Turn. The Turn is the tide and everything alive here is surfing it, and a tide abolished is not a freedom, it is a drowning; the rope-fungus would sit on a feed that stayed hot forever and never have to move again and would be, in the strictest sense, finished.

It does not cure the deafness to the Engine, or give the keeper its throat back, or take the wool off the pipes so that a voice at ordinary volume could cross the room. It could. That is the temptation, and it is a real one, and it lasts about four Turns: to be *heard*. To say one sentence at a normal human volume and have it arrive. But the jackets are what eat the sound, and to be heard it would have to strip them, and a stripped hall is a loud hall, and the loud place is the place the keeper came down from and cannot bear. It works out, somewhere in the second decade, that here it can be heard or it can be kind, and it stops working on the problem after that.

It does not make the cricket immortal. Every few years a cricket gets down and stridulates and the hall is unbearably, indecently loud for four feet and then the cricket dies, and the power sits in the wool and lets it die, every time, and this is the second-hardest thing it does. A cricket that did not die would not be a cricket. It would be a fitting. It would be a sound the room agreed to carry, which is to say it would be the room, and the room is what the keeper already has.

It does not give the shrimp-thing in the west steam trap a memory of the Turn. Two hundred thousand generations in a chamber that floods and empties; nine hundred tides a month; the great inversion surviving only as accumulated rumor in the line. The power looks at this for a long time and concludes that the rumor is a scripture and the scripture is sufficient. *It is not deprived.* The power holds onto that sentence. It will need it later, for a harder case.

It does not forgive anybody. There is nothing here to forgive. Nobody built this hall to trap anyone; they built it to wring water out of a dry city's breath and it still does that, and nobody is sure who receives it. There is no sin in Return Nine. There is only a very good arrangement that has never once been asked whether it is enough.

---

What it finds, when it has done its circuit:

One word.

When the cricket sings, everything stops — the grazing stops, the rounding stops — and one sharp tap goes into the copper, and the pattern is in the table by the door, worn shallow by fingernails, and it means **AGAIN**.

That is the entire expressed interior of four hundred years. Not *help*. Not *out*. **AGAIN**, addressed to a dying foreign animal that will not come back. The power turns this over for a Turn and decides that AGAIN is not a request for more crickets. It is the thing you say to something that left.

And then it notices the other thing, which is worse, and which no visitor has ever noticed because visitors are gone in an hour.

**WELL?** goes out along the metal at intervals, from whatever fitting happens to be growing a millimeter and telling you about it. *Is the thing we grew around still in there.* And **WELL** comes back, from the far side of the east rack or the sump corner or the underside of the second catwalk, five and a half years later, clean and bright, a mile of copper losing nothing.

It comes back every time. It has come back every time for four centuries and fifty nuclei. And the power, which has all the time there is, sits with the sound of it and understands that a mile of copper does not lose anything, including the difference between a hand that has decided to answer and a habit that answers.

The practice strikes it perfectly. That is the problem. The answer is not evidence. It has not been evidence for a very long time.

So the power stops listening to what comes back and starts listening to *how*. It listens for a flaw. A ring struck a half-beat late. A ring struck twice because the first one fumbled. Anything with a decision in it. It settles in to listen for a mistake, which is the only reliable signature of a person, and it prepares to wait, and it does not yet know that this is the whole of the work and everything else is decoration.

It is not a small life down here. Two Turns and a man considered himself to have lived a life of considerable variety, and he was right, and the power agrees with him, and will not be condescending about the pans or the wicks or the six warm fittings in their fixed order. It is not a small life. It is an unaccompanied one. Those are different diagnoses and only one of them has a treatment.

---

It drinks from the tap.

This is the first thing it does that is not nothing, and it is the one that gives it standing. It puts its mouth to the small copper tap by the slate that runs clear and tastes faintly of the inside of a kettle and has never once been turned off, and it drinks forty liters over a day, and it sweats into the pans, and it breathes into a room built to catch breath. It is in the ninth column now. It has not exempted itself. It has no intention of being a thing that this hall's arithmetic cannot see.

Then it takes a tag.

There is a punch hanging on the east rack, and it stamps four digits into a scrap of brass with a nail, badly, and wires the tag to itself where a hand would find it in the dark. The number is **0002**. There is a line for it on the board already. There has been a line for it on the board since the beginning: 0002, and no name after it, and zero grains in all three columns, carried forward every rechalk for four hundred years because the object it was assigned to was never delivered. A hole in the census, kept open, balanced at nothing, by people who each had one problem to solve and could not bear to close a line.

It writes nothing after the number. It leaves that for later, and it does not decide what later will be, and this is not modesty. It is the only part of the arrangement it has no right to touch.

---

The small works, in the order it does them, which is not the order of their importance:

On the north catwalk it puts its hand in the drip-dust. Not over the print that is there. Beside it. All fifty prints in that strata are the same size and the same spread of fingers and all of them are left hands, because the hand has converged; underneath them the prints go smaller and stranger, someone's daughter, someone with a bad thumb. The power's print is a right hand and it is a different size. It is the first difference in that dust in four hundred years. It will be legible at the next Turn and the next fifty and it says nothing that can be argued with. It says: *beside*.

On the bulkhead, on the *outside* face of the one-handle gap, low down, it works the brass with its palm until the wear matches the wear within. The inside has been polished for centuries by a hand held palm-down by something taking its time. Now the outside is polished too. There is no word for this in the tap-table and no column for it and it will take decades to be visible and when it is visible it will mean only one thing, which is that something uses this door in the other direction.

At the sight glass it sets a washer at an angle in the scale, a made thing, the kind the hall is full of, and it adjusts it over nine years until the amber reaches the crust twice: once from the working fluid, once from the black pool in the sump four feet across that is the only surface here that reflects anything. Two lights that are one light. The crust is an eye that has only ever had one object and it is very patient and it is winning; now it has two, and having two is not more information, it is the beginning of *elsewhere*, and it is delivered at a millimeter a year, which is the speed at which the crust can receive a thing without being destroyed by it. In three hundred years the crust will grow toward the reflection instead of the source, and that will be the first thing in Return Nine to prefer an image to an object, and the power will not be there to see it, and that is fine.

On the hot rack it takes up a position and holds it, still, for three years.

This is for the moth, which was born here, which is the hall's own dust flying, which navigates by heat and has no other sense worth the name, and whose entire universe inverts every eleven years and must be relearned at the pace of a thing that has never been in a hurry. A route is the only memory a moth has. The power is not permitted to touch the keeper — touch here is accretion, and another layer is the last thing the nut at the center needs — so it does the only thing left: it edits a route. It becomes a warm fitting that was not there before, in a place where the round will eventually pass, and it holds still while a thumbnail of brown weight lands on it, and tests, and moves on.

It does this for four Turns. Forty-four years. The route bends. And then one day, in the dark, at the far side of the east rack, a moth lands on the keeper's hand, which has never happened, and the keeper stands there with a weight on it that it cannot account for, and the ledger records the loss of some grains of water.

The shrimp it leaves entirely alone. It has to keep deciding this. It decides it about nine hundred times a month.

---

Then, from the south wall, one ring.

**WELL.**

Unasked. Nobody rang **WELL?** There is no column for it. It is not IN, it is not OUT, it is not HELD. It is a statement about an inside, offered by something that was not required to offer it, and there has not been one of these in four hundred years, because everything in this hall is retained and nothing in it *volunteers*.

Thermal expansion produces many sounds. Not usually that one.

And then the power waits five and a half years, because that is how long an answer takes, and it does not shorten this, because the five and a half years is not a delay in the hall's plumbing. It is the round. It is the pace of the tide's creature. To shorten it would be to reach into a person and speed them up.

What it does while it waits: its own circuit, eleven years, set deliberately out of phase, half a room away, so that it is never met on the stairs and never a face in the dark and never in the way of the overstep. It tastes the pans. It learns to read the year — festival pollen, ash, salt, sugar, a thread of dyed wool — and it is astonished by this, and comes to love it more than anything else in the hall: that a room with no rock and no dirt has nonetheless been keeping a diary of what people were eating. It cannot digest the sequin either. It does not swallow it.

It does not trim the wicks. Once, in the fourth decade, it finds itself with the scissors in its hand, and it puts them down, and stands in the dark for a while, badly shaken. The wicks are the keeper's. The round is the keeper's. To do the round for it would be to dissolve it — the practice is what walks, and if the practice has nothing to do there is nothing walking. It will not be useful here. Usefulness is the one gift that would kill.

And when the answer comes back, five and a half years later, clean and bright, it is not clean. It is a half-beat late and there is a small wrongness in it, a hesitation, the ghost of a second strike that was fumbled and let go.

The power does not correct it. It does not send the pattern again to show how it goes. It takes the flawed ring and holds it the way you would hold a hand that has been broken and set slightly crooked, and it does not chalk it anywhere, because there is no column, and this is the first thing it has ever wanted to write down and been unable to.

---

The rest is not planned, which is why it works.

It has been breathing. It cannot not breathe; that is the terms of the ninth column. And whatever it is, its breath does not condense into festival pollen or ash or salt or sugar or wool. It carries something down in its sleeves too, from up there, from the loud empty echoing place — not objects, nothing it cannot afford to lose, only dust. Grit from a road. Pollen off a tree that does not grow in that city. Ash from a fire nobody in that city lit.

The keeper tastes the pans. That is how it knows what year it is up there. And in the eighth decade it lifts a tray to its mouth in the dark and stops, and stands, with its hand on the slick rail that never dries and never runs, for a long time, holding a grain it cannot date.

Cities produce many sediments. Not usually that one.

And on the board, in the loss column, in a hand that is not the converged hand — a hand that has not learned to make those figures the way four centuries and fifty nuclei make them — there is a deficit. It is about the size of a mouth. It is honest. It has a number beside it. It is what a body drank at the copper tap and then carried up the ladder and exhaled somewhere the returns cannot reach.

For four hundred years the columns have balanced because nothing here leaves. Now something leaves. Now this room's water is going up into the world and not coming back down, and the board says so, plainly, in a stranger's handwriting, and does not pretend otherwise.

Everything leaks and nothing is broken. This is the hardest rule for visitors. It turns out to be true of the arithmetic too. The hall carries its first unbalanced column and goes on humming, one stroke every ninety seconds, felt in the sternum, unheard.

---

Two sharp rings go out from the north feed, and they are not **WELL?**

They are not in the table. There is no such pattern. It is a new sound in a room where the last new sound was a tap-code scratched by men who are now several floors down under the grating, and it arrives at the south wall clean and bright, a mile of copper losing nothing, and the power hears it and does not know what it means.

It answers anyway. One ring. **WELL.** Which is not the meaning of the question, whatever the question was. It is the only thing it has to give: *still here, still here, still here.*

That is what the keeper did with the unasked ring, and neither of them will ever be sure, and the exchange will continue for four hundred years at intervals of five and a half, and this is not a failure of communication. It is the beginning of one. Two things in the dark, answering what they cannot parse, faithfully, at the speed the room permits.

---

It leaves a second cup on the nail by the tap before it goes up. It beat it out of the corner of a drip tray, and it is ugly, and it holds water. There is one mouth down here. The cup is not a suggestion that there should be two. It is a thing that can be ignored, or used, or grown around, or set in a tray to become the nucleus of a dripstone over the next four centuries, and every one of those is permitted, and none of them is a failure, and the power decides this in advance so that it will not be able to be disappointed later.

It goes up. It has to go up; that is the whole gift and the hardest one. It will not become a fitting. It will not become the hall's second sufficiency, a kindness so total that nothing else is needed, because that is the disease here and not the cure. So it goes up the warm rungs with its sleeves over its palms and stands at the second landing in the thin cool air and hears its own footsteps go off across the world and come back off a wall, and it can bear it, and it goes on out into the loud place, and it lives there for a while, and it comes back down.

Not on a schedule. Never on a schedule. Often enough.

Because the word in the table is **AGAIN**, and AGAIN is what you say to something that left, and there is exactly one answer to it, and the answer is not a light or an open door or a voice that carries. The answer is to come back, and to keep coming back, and to still be there in forty-four years when the moth's route finally bends, and to still be there in five and a half when the answer arrives, and to be the kind of thing that is at the other end of a latency. That is the only omnipotence that is any use in Return Nine. Everything else it has is decoration.

The stairs stay loud. Nobody is ever met on them. The offer is left open and never pressed, and if the keeper goes up one day and stands in the echo and cannot bear it and comes down, there will be a ring waiting near the south wall that means *I heard you go, and I am still here*, and going up will not have been abandonment, which is the only thing about that first climb that ever needed fixing.

---

On the slate, in the converged hand, in fresh chalk that will stay smearable forever because there is no wind here and now there are two hands, the empty line is filled in at last. Four hundred years carried forward at zero grains, and then:

**0002 AGAIN.**

Not filed under living things. There is no such heading. Filed under HELD, which is what everything here is: a quantity of the outside that has declined to go back up.

The ninth column is the size of two people now, some of the time. The loss column does not balance. Under the oldest leak the bowl goes on deepening, one drop every eleven seconds, and beside the new cup on the nail there is a wet ring on the steel that will be a bowl too, in a while, in four hundred years, at the pace of patience.

Nothing here is eroding away. Two bowls now. Two hands in the dust, one of them a right. And somewhere near the south wall, patient as the steel, a ring still going that nobody asked for.
