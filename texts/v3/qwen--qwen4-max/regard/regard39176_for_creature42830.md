# The Guest

## The stair

It comes up the way everything comes: from below, on foot, paying by the metre.

Two thousand metres of streaming escarpment, in the dark, under the cloud sea, in a queue of men who think it is a lowlander because it is climbing badly. It is climbing badly. It has arranged to have no advantage on the stair. At the chapel halfway up it sits with the men who are resting and will not be going on, and one of them explains to it that the lowlanders are drowning, and it says that it has been down there and that this is true, and he says *then you know*, and that is the whole conversation, and it is the best one it has on the climb.

Then it takes a load off a man who cannot get up and carries it the rest of the way, and comes back down, and takes another. It does this for a season. Its knees do not go. It gives the wage to the chapel and does not tell anyone the amount, and the chapel keeps a record, because everything here keeps a record, and the record is the only reason it is not a rumour.

At the top it is given a bowl.

It fills it before it eats. It has watched. And at some small sound from below — a shift, a settling, nothing — it looks down at the water before it looks up, and the woman across the table sees it do that, and stops being careful with it.

They give it a hand-name that evening. It is an ordinary name and slightly rude. Nobody gives it a mouth-name for eleven years.

---

## What it does not do

It does not give them a sun.

It could. It is not incapable; it is unwilling, and the unwillingness is the whole of its wisdom, and it is not certain of it.

Consider what a sun would take. The painters would have to learn to light a face from above, which is to say they would have to learn to make corpses and saints, and the gorget would become jewellery, and *she lit him* would become a metaphor, and within four generations there would be no way to say the thing it means. The mats would not unroll. There would be no twenty minutes, no wave crossing the plain from the pans outward, no held breath let out through cloth, no word for the person you said it to. The crickets would sing at some other hour or none, and the four would stop being a form and start being a length, and a musician could play past the end of the world and only be playing badly.

It does not make the flat carry sound. Ten metres would stop being a wall. Confession would go indoors. Sedition would go indoors. Love would go indoors, into the throats, where the rich already live, where a sigh can end a marriage, and the poor would have nothing left that is theirs — no place to stand in the open, close enough to touch, and say anything at all while the world takes nothing. To speak a mouth-name into an ear on the lid, uselessly, where it is not law: this is the most intimate act available to a human being in Comb, and it exists only because the flat eats sound, and a power that repaired the acoustics would be abolishing it in order to be kind.

It does not stop the water. The water is the power, and the nitrogen, and the soil made out of weather and lightning, and the beans.

But it does not confuse the shape of a life with the wound in it.

A girl bought at six. An eye burned out by fifty, in the second clause, before the pay. A knee, a lung, a heart, at forty, reckoned in metres climbed. A roof-rent paid on a house that fell before anyone's grandmother was born. These are not the green hour. These are what the green hour has been made to cost, and the cost is not the thing, and anyone who tells you otherwise is standing under twelve metres of proven rock and will be dead before it matters.

So it makes a list. It is a long list. It is very careful about which column each item goes in, and it knows it will get some of them wrong, and it arranges to be corrected.

---

## The office on the Elder

Its first formal act is to go out to the small permanent office on the Elder — three people, four centuries, one function — and ask to be measured.

They do not have a procedure for this. It waits while they make one. The making takes eleven years. Eleven years is nothing here; a pan-hole deepens by a fingernail's width in a century; the lichen has been eating the same square metre since before writing. It attends the meetings. It signs things. It is, for eleven years, an item of unfinished business, and it does not resent this, and the resenting would have been the tell.

Then it submits an accounting. Litres drawn. Millimetres lost. What it drinks, what it drops, what its existence costs the roof, in the same units the town quotes daily on the board outside the deepest court, so that anyone can read it and argue with it in a language they already speak.

Because a power that cannot be audited is a hammer-fraud. It is a propper reporting dull where the rock is bright. It is the one thing in the world with no fall-rate, and there is no word here for that which is not also a word for a criminal.

The entry in the archive is a number. It asked for that specifically. Not a miracle, not a founding, not a new dating of the calendar. A measurement, in the only continuous record in the country, between two measurements of how much of a hill is left.

---

## The hammer

It apprentices itself to a propper and is bad at it for four years.

Bright, dull, dead. It can hear the difference intellectually and cannot hear it, which is the entire difference between a chart and a survey, and it walks the flat with a long-handled hammer and a caged cat and learns the third note the way you learn a name you will only ever have to say once.

It is taught, in the end, by a woman of sixty-one in a room of proven thickness who has not heard a human voice in nineteen years. She teaches by taking its hand and putting it flat on the stone and holding it there. She has forty words for what a rock does before it decides and she gives them to it one at a time, in a sign dialect faster than the common one, and she is patient in the way of someone who has no alternative use for the evening.

It is the second-best student she has ever had. The granddaughter is the first, because the granddaughter signs too fast but she is family, and there is no one else to teach them to, and now there is someone else, and the granddaughter notices this and has feelings about it and comes on feast days anyway.

The guest asks the guild to put the cat in the charts. A cat that will not lie down is a survey; it is the cheapest instrument the guild owns and more reliable than the hammer and it is not written down anywhere, because a thing that is written down can be sold.

The guild refuses.

It says: then I will put it in the rhyme.

The guild does not understand this sentence. It will understand it in about thirty years.

---

## The east, and the rhyme

It goes down.

Down the swallow-holes, into the karst nobody has mapped, into the dark where the water goes and the eels come from, and it walks it. Not in a night. It is gone four years, and it comes back up through a fissure east of the ninth comb with the whole hollow in it: every chamber, every thin place, the true thickness of everything, the roof of the world sounded at last by something that cannot be bribed and does not want a house.

And then it does the only intelligent thing it does in the entire affair.

It does not publish a chart.

A chart is a deed. A deed is property. Property can be bought, and the guild has been buying it for two hundred years, and a true map handed to Comb as a document would be inside three strongrooms by the end of the wet weeks and the roof-rent would go up.

It offers the charts to the guild first. Formally. In the deepest court, freely, with no conditions, in front of the criers. It does this because they are people too and because it will not do a thing to a class that it has not offered to their faces.

The guild refuses. A free chart is worth nothing. A true chart ends the roof-rent. There is no procedure.

So it cuts the map into verses.

Metre by metre, pan by pan, comb by comb, in the skipping metre the lid already uses, with the corrections built in as rhymes so that an error will not scan. It spends years on this. It is the hardest work it does here — harder than the karst, harder than the stair — because it has to make true things memorable to a nine-year-old without making them sound true, which is a different problem, and it fails a great deal, and the verses it discards would fill a terrace.

Then it pays children in eel to learn them.

It gives the verse for east of the ninth comb to a boy of nine and will not say where it got it, and he does not know it is a survey. He thinks it is a song about not sitting down. It is a song about not sitting down.

And the rhymes go hand to hand, close in, signed, unbuyable, faster than the deeds, and the guild tries to buy them and cannot, and it has been failing to buy them for two centuries, and now it is failing against something that is not cleverer than it. Only true.

The street by the drying ground gets the verse in time. The children stop skipping there. It goes down anyway, in the dry, at midday, when the lid is empty; the bell rings once, and the second bell comes before dark, because the count is short and everyone is resolved. The boy adds a verse at fourteen. It is not a correction. It is a memorial, and it scans, and his daughter will skip to it.

Not all of them. Some streets still go. It says so, plainly, in advance, and does not promise otherwise, and is at the lip every time with a lamp and a piece of bright rot it has paid for.

## The green hour, in stone

The listener cannot sleep on the flat. Up there the world is silent and she can hear her own blood and it sounds like a roof.

At the turning the guest takes her up. She walks; she is sixty-one, not frail; she has been carried by people who meant well and did not care for it. They sit on the crust at the shoulder of an Elder with her palms flat on the ironstone and its hands under hers, and it translates.

It puts the mats opening into the stone.

Twenty minutes, from the pans outward, the wave crossing the plain — rendered in the forty words, which are words for what a rock does before it decides, which have never in nine generations of the guild been used for anything that was not a warning.

She hears the green hour. She was born on the lid. She heard it as a child, before they bought her, and she has been deaf to air since she was thirty, and here it is in the wrong language, and it is not the same, and it is not nothing.

Afterwards she signs something too fast and it asks her to slow down, and she does.

Then she gives it a mouth-name. It is one of the forty. It is the word for the sound a rock makes when it is taking a load and will hold — not the sound of deciding, the sound after the decision, the one you log and then sleep through. There is no hand-name equivalent. It cannot be signed to a stranger. It only carries in a throat, where everyone will hear it.

It is the first thing anyone gives it that it did not ask for.

## The light, described

It offers the sky-faller her eyes.

She takes them. This is not a parable. A woman who has been going blind for twenty-six years for eight litres a square metre on a wet night is not a symbol, and it does not ask her to be one, and it does not make her decline in order to be admirable, and afterwards she goes to the deepest court about the second clause of the indenture and wins something small.

But before that, at the ascent, it walks out along her transect ahead of the carts and finds a shearwater that has come over the rim and is not yet dead.

It does not make the bird land. They never land; they live their whole lives in the cloud sea and sleep on the wing and come up over the rim only when they are dying, and this one is dying, and it is within the law of the world, and it is only that the guest got there first.

It carries the bird back wrapped and puts it into her hands while it is warm, and she has found eleven thousand and never touched one that was alive.

Then it dies. It goes to the mill on the east side, and the smell is on the east side in the dust season, and it goes on the gardens, and the beans come up, and they eat them, and nobody says anything at table.

And separately, because she asked, it goes out to the rim and looks straight at the deck — the bright useless sunlit cloud floor, the source of all the light in Comb, which nobody in this town has ever looked at directly, because the ones who do go blind. It looks for a long time. It does not go blind. It comes back and finds it has nothing to say, because there is no vocabulary here for a top-lit sky and the painters find the idea sentimental.

So it makes words. It takes a year. The result is a bad poem and it is accurate, and she reads it and says *that's not what I thought*, and it says *no*, and she reads it again.

## The two numbers

It goes down the stair and learns the language properly, which nobody in Comb has ever done, in four centuries of correspondence conducted in baskets by people who have never seen each other's faces.

It sits in the stair-foot quarter with the boxes. It carries a coffin down at night, illegally, in an ore basket, and pays the inspector the fee, and is not sorry, and does not report the inspector.

Then it reads both numbers.

Both are right. They are counting different things. The lowlands count eels at the foot of the escarpment. Comb counts eels at the weirs. The difference is the climb — two thousand metres of streaming rock through fissures that have been drying for four hundred years, because the karst is hollowing, because the town drops water for power.

The fall-rate and the eel-count are the same number.

Nobody could have seen it. There was no map, no shared language, and no person who had stood at both ends.

It publishes the arithmetic in both languages, in baskets, with the method, so that anyone can check it and find the error if there is one.

It does not publish a conclusion. It will not say open the weirs or keep them shut. The letting reads it and the Drop reads it and the Roof reads it and all three say it proves their case, and it lets them, because it does not have a vote and would not use one. It has given them a common number. What they do with a common number is their own history and they have earned it.

One other thing. It carries the letting's argument down the stair and finds, in the drowned country, a sect that has been saying the same thing for longer, in a language nobody in Comb reads: that the eels are the road the first people followed, and that you do not eat your guides. Two heresies on two floors of the world, four hundred years, not knowing.

It tells each that the other exists. It does not arrange anything. It has learned that much.

## The road

Where an eel goes, a road can go.

It finds the eel-road: a fissure route from the low country to the throat-sumps, longer in distance, gentler in grade, cuttable, and — this is the whole point — able to take a wheel.

Forty years to cut. It starts. It carries stone. It is on the works roster under its hand-name and it is paid in eel and it is bad at blasting for a decade.

The freight guild opposes it. The badge is a spine; the wage is metres climbed; a road that does not kill knees is a road that does not need the guild, and the guild is not wrong about this, and it argues in the deepest court for six years and loses on a technicality about water.

The men who die at forty do not oppose it.

The chapel halfway up is kept. It is on a stair that fewer people climb every year, and there are still men in it resting, because forty years is not soon, and the guest does not pretend that it is. It writes down the names of the ones who die in the meantime. All of them. It is a long list and it is not a monument, it is a list, and it is kept in the office on the Elder with the measurements, because that is where things are kept that must not be forgotten and must not be honoured instead of counted.

## The seal

It goes out at night to the sealed throats with a counter of fifty who has a folder and an open name eleven years old, and it puts its ear to the seal, which is a crime.

It is prosecuted, gently, by a magistrate who looks at the floor.

It does not defend itself. It appeals — which is to descend — and it goes down through the terraces to where the judge sits lowest, and stands under the carving, *there has always been less*, and pleads guilty, and pays the fine in eel, and the crier puts the sentence into every terrace without raising his voice, and it is a small scandal for a season and then it is a precedent.

Then it offers testimony. Not authority. It has walked the karst; it knows where things that go down come to rest; it will take the counter down and show him.

And it will not close a count it cannot prove.

There are names it cannot find. Dissolved, carried, gone into the cloud sea, become the birds that never land — the loop is closed and everyone knows it is closed and nobody says it at table, and now somebody has to say it at table, to a family, in a room, on a feast day. For those it says: *I do not know.* And the counts stay open. And it does not say anything after that. It sits there. It has learned that the sentence after *I do not know* is almost always for the speaker.

But the woman on the stair it finds.

A chamber, a depth, a bearing, and a gorget: tin, on a string, and the string is tied in a particular knot, and her sister is alive on the lid and knows the knot, and comes down and looks at it and says yes.

That is the proof. Not a voice from the sky. A knot, identified by a woman who has been paying roof-rent on a house that fell eleven years ago.

---

## The bell

He rings the second bell.

The town stops. It is not a sound anybody wants. It is the sound of a count finished, and there are men in Comb who have been waiting eleven years to be widowers and are now widowers, and the trade in closing dies over the following decade because there is nothing left to buy cheap from exhausted families, and the roof-rent on the two sealed streets ends, because a street that has been counted has gone, and the deeds go with it.

The law does that. Not the guest. The guest supplied a fact and the town's own law did the rest, and this is why it worked, and it knew that beforehand, and did not say so beforehand, because a thing explained in advance is a thing that has been decided for you.

The seals come off. People go down who have been putting their ears to them for two generations. What is under there is a street.

And the counter has nothing to do.

Fifty years old, a folder, eleven years of walking it every year, and the folder is closed and he is a man in a room. The worst day is the day after. The guest stays for the day after. It does not say it was worth it. It fills the bowl and watches it and lets him talk about her, and then lets him not.

In the evening they walk out on the lid, where sound goes up into fog and thin air and does not come back, and he puts his mouth against its ear and says its mouth-name — the word for a rock taking a load and holding — where it goes nowhere and does nothing and is not law.

The world takes nothing.

It is the most intimate act available to a human being in Comb, and the poets have been trying to explain it to foreigners for four hundred years, and it is what it came for, though it did not know that until it happened.

---

## The counted

It stays.

It has a hand-name that is slightly rude and a mouth-name that only carries in a throat, and a line in the archive with a number beside it, and it eats the beans, and it is on the list of who was on the street.

It has not stopped the hollowing. It cannot make the Sill last and has not come to make anything last; the karst has been hollowing since before the plain was lifted and the water the town drops dissolves a gram of the roof every hour and the fall-rate is still on the board every morning and some years it brings down a government. There will be a last street. There will be a last counter with a folder. The lid will go, and the Elders will go, worn to nothing a second time, and the office will publish how much of them is left until there is none, and then it will publish that.

What it has done is smaller than rescue and it is not nothing: the number on the board is true. The songs run past the ninth comb. Two counts closed and one did not, and the one that did not is open honestly, which is a different thing from open. A woman hears the green hour in the wrong language. A road is being cut that will not be finished for forty years and is being cut.

And it is not certain. It has arranged to be corrected. It has put itself where the hammer can reach it, and given the children a verse about itself that scans, and if the verse is wrong they will change it, and it will not intervene, because a rhyme that cannot be corrected is a deed.

Most nights the bowl shows nothing at all. The meal goes on. Someone tells the story about the fish. The child signs the rhyme under the table with one hand because she is not allowed to at dinner, and outside the combs are wearing their ten thousand kilometres of thin blue crawling light, and the mats are open, and the whole surface of the world is green and wet underneath and dry as flour on top, and the light is coming up off the pavement into everybody's face.

Sometimes there is a shiver that is not the furnaces and not the thunder and has no name yet, and everyone at the table stops with their hands where they are.

It looks down before it looks up.

It is one of the counted. That is a status, and it can be revoked, and it has not been.
