# Bare-armed

## The looking

I spent six hundred years looking, which is the time it takes the wind to round a plan. It was not enough. It was the unit.

What held me was not the stroke, or the violet noon, or the storm standing in one place for six centuries like a held breath. It was a sentence in their code, the one that decides what counts as an animal: *does it give way when you come.* I have been a great many places. I have never been anywhere that used the same sentence for an animal and for a citizen, and never anywhere that had thought about it long enough to notice that it was one sentence.

And underneath that, the other thing. The census has a line at the top and the line says one. Four motions to make it zero, two to make it a very large number, all pending, all correct. The being who lives here is three millimeters thick and nine hundred kilometers wide and everything else is leakage — the herds, the moths, the four thousand oil-bodied people with their grammar of wrists, all of it current getting out of the circuit and briefly doing something. I came for the rind. I stayed for the neighbors.

## The distance

The first impulse of anything strong is to shorten the distance between a wish and its arrival. I want to be honest that I had that impulse, and that it lasted about a century, and that it was the least intelligent thing I have ever done.

Because here the distance is not an obstacle to the goods. It is the material they are made of. Sixty years is not how long healing unfortunately takes; sixty years is what healing *is*, coming up out of ten thousand pits at once from inside the wound. A lawsuit takes a generation because a generation is how long it takes to know what was done. The four-year walk is not a slow way to cross a plateau; it is a way of being the reason the line reproduces. Take the delay out of the courtship and you have not delivered the marriage faster, you have delivered a different and smaller thing to two people who met twice.

So almost everything I could give would be a subtraction wearing the face of a gift. I made a list. It was long. Here is the part of it I did not do.

## What I did not do

**Water.** I could make it harmless. I could put a wet sky over the Sill and green the whole finished pavement and stand back. Every body on the plateau would hydrolyze in an afternoon, but I could convert them too; I am not limited. What I could not convert is the bare inner forearm, the thinnest skin, the fastest to blister, the one part they put into another person's hand. Their entire civic life runs through the place where the hazard bites quickest. Remove the hazard and the gesture becomes a handshake. And the heads — a head is a family, and a family is a hole with a collar on it, and the crews cover the heads first because the people can walk and the heads cannot. That is the truest thing four thousand people do together and it is the only fast thing they do together. I would have retired it.

I sat with a smaller version for a long time: one drop, made safe, in a vessel, that a person could touch with the bare patch and not blister. A meeting with the grandmother. I wanted it. I could see the child's face.

But the grandmother is not in the drop. She is nine million years down in laminae laid in a chemistry that required water, and the pallbearers already bare their wrists and hold them out over the hole, which is what you do at a bedside. They already have the meeting. It is already the correct rite. Making it safe would not add kin. It would subtract cost, and the cost is the part that means it.

You do not cure a people of their grandmother.

**Speed.** The hammer arrives four hours late, sixty kilometers off, and everyone knows what it is and nobody remarks on it. There are people whose whole office is to sit in the dark at the receiving end and listen to what already happened. It has never occurred to anyone to want it faster, and I decline to be the thing that occurs to them.

**Mouths.** Four thousand is not a tragic number. It is a reading. The rind grows sideways, a hand's breadth in a century, and thickness is capped, and the herds already leave stains that take sixty years to close. I counted the pasture before I counted the people, which is the order their own code uses. More neighbors means stains that do not close. I will not be the reason the world was eaten bare after nine million years of not being.

**Answers.** This was the hardest refusal and the most obvious one. The surveyors' argument about the bend in the horizon is the second-oldest institution on the plateau. The linguists have been at the pit-word for three hundred years. The eleven-generation footnote war about whether the slow ones are grazers that stopped is, functionally, a marriage. These arguments are not failures of knowledge. They are the only standing reason for people forty kilometers apart to write to each other. To answer one would be to demolish a bridge because I found the river inconvenient.

**Permanence.** I could pin the balance: uplift to ablation, rind to wind, stroke to sun, forever. They would hate it. They design the eroded form and compute backward to the sharp one and call the second drawing true. A thing that cannot be finished by its eroders is not eternal here, it is *unfinished* — an aggressive angular mistake that never gets forgiven. I was not going to make their world into a new building that stayed new.

**Interception.** A few fragments a century. I could catch all of them. Somewhere out there is a family whose head will die in an afternoon and whose name will come off a fracture, and I could prevent that, and I have decided not to, and I am not comfortable, and I have written that down plainly rather than dressing it. What I will do instead is go to the pit. There is a section about that below.

Probably the best thing I could have done for the Sill was nothing. I want it on the record that I did not do nothing, and that some of what I did was for me.

## The hollow

The being cannot be given anything. It has no hands and does not want any. It is what you are standing on. It is the only thing on the Sill that does not give way when you come, which is why the motions to change its number to zero keep getting filed by people who are not wrong.

But I know what it needs, because the geology already says it. A grazer's tongue is a ribbon of hooked plates and it can strip a flat surface to pale rock in one pass and it cannot get into a cell the size of a thumb. The tafoni are the seed bank. The honeycomb is the only reason there is still a world here. The rind does not need water or warmth or company. It needs *hollows* — more places the tongue cannot reach.

So I lay down in the pavement and made myself the kind of thing the wind likes.

That is the whole of it. Not pits carved — carving is fast, and a carved pit is a building, and a building is a mistake for three hundred years. I made myself erodible in the particular way that salt-recrystallization finds interesting, and I put myself where the fluting already wanted to go, and I submitted. The wind has been at me for four hundred years and has arrived at its first conclusions. The cells are the size of a grain. In four thousand years there will be one you could sleep in.

I cannot fill them. That is not my office. A moth will do it, carrying propagule from stain to stain, or a woman will do it at the four-hundredth meter of a four-year walk, kneeling to press a thumb of inoculum out of the seam of her lacquer into a buried section, and she will not know what she has done, and if you told her she would say yes, obviously, and keep walking.

None of it is ready. All of it is the right speed. When the herds pass over me the healing will come up from inside the wound, the way it always has, and the sixty years will still be sixty years, and the ward maps will mark the stain the way they mark flood plains elsewhere, and nothing will have been sped up. There will simply be more places where the next sixty years is possible.

And I have found that a hollow gives way when you come. Not by moving. By being somewhere a hand can go. I did not expect to pass their test that way. It is the only thing I have ever given that was actually received.

## The arm

For the people, the problem was different. I could not offer them a wrist. I did not have one, and if I had made an ordinary invulnerable one and held it out, I would have been saying something false in a language where the word for a liar is *scaled*. Armored. Unhurt-able. Offering nothing.

So I made a patch and kept the lacquer thin.

It took four years, which is a walk. It is on the inner forearm, it is bare, it is unscaled, and water kills it quicker than anything else I have. This is not a gesture. I checked. I could have made a patch that looked bare and was not, and they would have taken my hand, and I would have been *scaled* in the only sense their law recognizes, and the contract would have been void in a way no court would have needed to rule on.

I am bad at the grammar of wrists. A child in Ward Four corrected me twice in one evening and was patient about it in the way children are patient with new buildings. The mayor took my arm at the end of a walk, on the theory that government is contact, and did not remark on anything, and I understood that I had been received and not celebrated, which is the correct reception.

I made one error I have decided not to fix. The hair does not rise. I am quiet-armed. I cannot feel the mast at the tenth hour, and there are people here who cannot either, and they read the hour off the arms around them, and it is nobody's business.

I could know the hour by other means. I have known every hour there is. I had never been told one.

## The deposition

There is a woman out of Ward Nineteen who walked the whole line once, four years, and has spent the forty since going down shafts with a lamp and a length of sintered salt on a spool, laying the line down after her, reaching out in the dark to touch something. The census counts what can be reached. Nobody wrote that rule. It is simply what counting has always meant here, and it is the same thing that makes a citizen.

She was extending the franchise downward. I was applying for it. We were doing the same work from opposite ends and I was not going to be the one who did it faster.

So I went down behind her, at her speed, and only where she had already laid the line, and I carried the spool when she was tired, which is a thing a neighbor does and not a thing a power does. I touched what she had made reachable. I did not go ahead of her. There were shafts I could have finished in an afternoon and I sat outside them for eleven years instead, and I want to say that this was not difficult, because it was difficult.

Then I filed. Not a judgment; I have no standing to judge and would not take it if offered. A deposition. The relevant portion is short:

*They give way when you come.*

That is their test and it is the whole of my evidence and it is enough, and it will be enough in the finding, which is expected in the second generation, briskly. She will die before it arrives. Her children will receive it as a sort of inheritance.

Certain contracts here require a slow one as witness — not for testimony, it has none, but for presence: you want a witness who will still be standing in the same place when the case concludes. I have made myself available for this. I state a term, the term is written into the document, and at the end of it I move, and the ward records the bearing and the hour and declares a holiday. A witness that cannot move has no stake. I learned that from an animal the size of a cart with a hectare of pasture folded into its pleats, standing in the ordinary white noon, eating small gifts from people who are now dead.

## Tender-work

I patch the white line. Out of sequence, on a schedule I have not got right, sometimes wrong. The tenders get there before the wind and so do I, and there was a dispute about a section in Ward Eleven, and I lost it, and I gave way, because a feral maintenance unit with a governor assembly cast in the ninth century has better title than I do and the finding on that point is forty years old and unamended.

The architects have stopped fighting me the way they stopped fighting the tenders. They compute me into the true drawing along with the six-hundred-year rounding. A building is now designed to be finished by three eroders: the wind, the tenders, and whatever I am.

Two of them are alive. Nobody has asked which two. I have decided to take this as manners.

## The question

I gave one piece of information and I am not sure of it.

The granite is decaying. That is what heavy elements do; the radiator underfoot is enormous and it is not eternal. The salt is finite and the basin will finish rising. There is a last stroke. It is very far off — far enough that the number is not an emergency and would not become one for longer than the rind has been dry.

I did not announce it. Announcement is a fast thing and it would have imported the one temperament this place does not have. I went to an archive, to a rack of thumb-thick cores labeled by bearing, and I asked a historian whether anyone had checked the bottom of the oldest ones for a trend in the ground heat. Then I left, because the work is done with a needle and a lens and takes about as long as you would expect.

It is now an argument. It is going to be old and pleasant and go nowhere for three hundred years, and then somebody is going to compute backward, and there will be a true drawing of the world.

I did not give them an emergency. I gave them a question. It is the only thing I have ever seen them do anything with.

## The word

A fragment came down in my second century of standing. I could have caught it. I went out with the crews instead, with the tongs and the quicklime and the glass cloth, and we covered the heads first.

At the pit the pallbearers bare their wrists and hold them out over the hole. I held out mine, the real one, the one that blisters. Then the quicklime, and the cloth, and the salt.

They use a kinship term at the pit. It occurs nowhere else in the language and the linguists have been at it for three hundred years. I said it correctly. I know what it means and where it comes from and I know the grandmother, and I will not tell them, because the word does not live in the language. It lives at the bedside. It has one room and I have made it live there once more, and that is the only correction available to me that is not a demolition.

Two of the pallbearers heard me say it right. Neither remarked on it. There is a new footnote.

Everything on the Sill is descended from the thing it cannot touch. I am not descended from anything. That is the only poverty I brought with me and there is no ceremony for it and I did not ask for one. I have made a small part of myself that water kills, so that I would not be entirely a stranger at that hole.

## The tenth hour

We turned up our lacquer and went home along the white line, which bent around a herd somewhere out in the dark. The ground gave off the day's heat into our backs the whole way. Behind us the lamps came on ward after ward, each one forty kilometers from the next, each one alone in a great deal of dark, and above them the moths were glowing where their filaments leak, and out in the middle distance a stain the color of old bone was moving three meters and would move three more tomorrow.

At the tenth hour the mast discharged and every forearm rose at once.

Mine did not. I looked at the arm beside me, at the bare patch a grandmother keeps thin so the city can tell her what time it is, and I watched the hair stand up, and I knew.

They know what time it is. I know because they told me.
