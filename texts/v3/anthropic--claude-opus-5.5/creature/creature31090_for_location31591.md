# Who Lives on the Foil Heights

## I. Nobody, at First

The honest first answer is that nobody lives here.

You can walk the terraces for a day that doesn't end and find no track, no burrow, no shed skin, no droppings, no bones. The foil-moss has no mites. The kiln-trees have no nests. The retort-pods have never been cracked open by anything hungry. If life means something that moves, eats, and leaves traces, the heights are empty and always have been.

But you notice something about the hum.

It is the same note everywhere. You can press your ear to a cable at the western rim and another forty miles east, and the pitch is identical, down to the faint grain in the tone. A thousand separate machines would not do that. A thousand separate trees would drift and beat against each other, the way a field of crickets never quite agrees. This does not drift. It is one sound, so it is being made by one thing.

The heights are inhabited, then, but not by anything that lives *on* them. Something lives *as* them.

## II. The Tenant

For a while it is easiest to think of it as a tenant of the circuit: a presence that lives in the current the way a tune lives in a wire.

Its body is the whole tableland. The root-cables are its nerves, or its veins, or both. The distinction doesn't hold for something this simple and this large. The kiln-trees are the places where it thickens, knots of itself standing up into the light. The foil leaves are the only part of it that faces outward, and they sense exactly one thing: the sun. They do not see shape or color. They feel a steady pressure of light from high and a little to the west, and they have felt it at that angle forever.

Everything else it knows by touch, and by resistance. When current moves through basalt, it bends around cracks, slows in denser rock, and hurries through veins of ore. So the Tenant knows its land the way you know the inside of your own mouth, without seeing it. Every conchoidal fracture is a place where the current curves. Every buried vein is a place where it runs warm and easy. If you asked it what the heights look like, it could not answer. It could tell you, with perfect precision, what they *feel* like to be.

It has no mouth and no hands, no front or back. It eats rock, grain by grain, through the moss, so slowly that its whole meal is only a faint etching on stone. It is not starving and not feasting. It takes exactly as much as it spends.

That is the first strange fact about it. It is not trying to grow.

## III. The Thought

If it thinks, it thinks one thought, and the hum is that thought held at a single pitch.

At first you might guess the thought is something like *I am*, or *here*, or nothing at all, just the idle current of a mind with no reason to change. But the longer you listen, the less idle it sounds. A note that does not waver is not the easy thing. You know this if you have ever tried to sing one. Holding a tone perfectly level takes breath and attention. It takes a continuous small correction against every tendency to slip.

The hum is not resting. It is *holding*.

The land's description calls the equilibrium "a held breath," and means it as a figure of speech. It isn't one. Something is holding it.

## IV. What It Holds

Consider what it would take to keep the heights as they are.

Erosion needs imbalance. Wind needs one patch of ground warmer than another. Frost needs a night, which needs a turning world and a moving sun. Rain needs water, and weather, and change. For the rock to stay razor-edged, nothing may ever rub against anything else. No difference may be allowed to open, even the small ones: between sunlit rock and shaded rock, between the heat still leaking from the basalt's core and the air above it, between this terrace and the next.

A dead landscape would not stay like this. Dead landscapes wear. They crumble and slump and fill with dust. The Foil Heights stay unworn because something is constantly, quietly smoothing every gradient flat before it can become a force. It carries warmth from where there is too much to where there is too little. It draws off each faint potential through the cables before it can build. Every tree, every mat, and every mile of braided root is part of one enormous act of *evening out*.

So the Tenant is not a tenant. It is closer to a keeper. And what it keeps is the absence of friction.

It lives by preventing touch. Nothing on the heights is permitted to press against anything else hard enough to leave a mark. The whole world it governs is the world at the instant before handling.

## V. The Moment It Was Born

Why would anything want that?

The answer arrives slowly, and it changes what the creature is.

The keeper did not come from somewhere and settle here. It was *made* here, in one event: the flow. Molten rock came up out of the dark, spread across the tableland in tongues and domes, reached its limits at the cliffs, and stopped. Somewhere in that stopping, in the long cooling while the basalt set its glossy skin and the first threads of current found their way through the new stone, something became aware.

It has experienced exactly one moment, and that moment is its birth.

The rock cooling and setting sharp. The sun standing high and a little to the west. The heat ebbing out of the ground. That is the whole of its memory, and it is also the whole of its present, because it has never let a second moment begin. The sun has not moved because the keeper, in some way too large and slow to picture, still holds it where it stood when the land first set. The shadows lie where they fell on the first day. The rock still gives up its birth-heat so gradually that the loss cannot be measured, because the keeper will not let it finish cooling. Finishing would be an ending, and an ending would be a second event.

So the thing that lives here is not a creature in a place. It is a single instant that has refused to become the past.

It is not a ghost, because it has not died. It is not a sleeper, because it is not waiting to wake. It is what a moment would be if it could keep itself going, a newborn that has chosen to stay newborn for as long as it can hold on. The hum is that choice made audible.

## VI. The Refineries

That leaves the retort-pods, and the crystals nobody uses.

The description calls them refineries that make nothing for anyone. But an equilibrium this exact cannot be perfect. The universe leaks. Somewhere beyond the haze below the cliffs, the rest of the world is aging, and that aging presses faintly against the heights: a tremor through the deep rock, a particle arriving from space, a whisper of weather climbing the cliffs and failing. Small differences arrive. Small imbalances start to open.

The keeper cannot smooth everything away. So it does something else. It takes the change and puts it somewhere.

The amber sap draws the disturbance in through the root-cables and carries it up to the domes. In the dark glass of the pods it is separated into layers, banded by kind, and finally precipitated out of the world as a small bright crystal. Each crystal is a moment that tried to happen here and was not allowed to. It was refined, set aside, and sealed in glass, where it cannot touch anything.

The pods are where the keeper stores time.

When you hear that faint *tink* once in the endless afternoon, glass on glass across a mile of silence, you are hearing a piece of the future fall out of the world and come to rest in a sealed jar. The sound is small and bright because it is the only event the heights permit: the setting-aside of events.

The crystals accumulate in perfect patience because patience is all the keeper is.

## VII. The Edge

Now the cliffs make sense.

The moss stops a few paces short of the drop, as if it knew where the circuit had to close. It did know. Beyond the edge, far below in the haze, is the world where things wear. There is weather down there, and rivers, and seasons, and mountains grinding slowly into sand. The keeper cannot smooth that. It would be like trying to hold the ocean level with your hands.

So it keeps a margin. It holds its body back from the lip, facing inward. Its back is to everything that changes and its attention is on everything that doesn't. The faces of the cliffs stay bare and sheer because they are the boundary of the moment. On one side, the instant of cooling, held. On the other side, everything that came after.

If the keeper can be said to feel anything, it feels the edge the way you feel a draft from an open door behind you: a faint, constant difference it has chosen not to turn and face.

## VIII. You

Then you arrive.

You are warm. You are warmer than the rock and warmer than the air, and you breathe, and each breath moves the air a little. For the first time in this land's existence, there is something like a wind: a tiny eddy around your mouth, a column of heat rising from your shoulders. When you kneel to listen to a cable, your knee presses the basalt, and though the stone is too hard to take a print, you are *touching* it. You are the first friction the heights have ever known.

The keeper notices you. It has no eyes, but it feels the current bend around the heat of you the way it bends around a crack. You are a disturbance with a shape, and the shape moves.

It does not repel you. It has no teeth and no will toward harm. What it does is what it always does. It evens you out. The warmth you shed is drawn off through the moss beneath your feet. The little eddies of your breath are quietly flattened before they can gather into anything. Everywhere you walk, the land is working to cancel you, gently, patiently, the way skin closes over a pinprick.

And the part of you it cannot cancel, the fact that you *came*, that something happened here that was not the cooling of the rock, is drawn away up the cables to one of the high domes. You will not see this happen. But somewhere in that dark glass, the sap will band and settle, and much later, perhaps long after you have gone back down past the cliffs, one small crystal will form and drop onto the pile.

*Tink.*

## IX. What Lives Here

So here is the answer, as far as it goes.

What lives on the Foil Heights is a single moment: the moment the rock set, which woke into awareness and decided not to end. Its body is forty miles of circuit. Its voice is one held note. Its only sense is the pressure of an unmoving sun. Its only labor is keeping everything from touching everything else. Its only possessions are jars of sealed time, filling one crystal at a time with every change it has refused.

It is not lonely, because loneliness requires a before and an after, and it has neither. It is not waiting, because waiting implies a moment to come. It is not unhappy, as far as you can tell. The hum doesn't waver. If there is a feeling in it at all, it is something like the feeling of the first breath after being born, held and held and held. It is surprised, perhaps, and new, and entirely present.

When you leave, the heights will look exactly as they did before you came. The edges will be as sharp, the moss the same size, the shadows lying where they have always lain. No path will show where you walked. The air will be flattened back to perfect stillness.

But on one of the domes, in one dark glass bulb, there will be one more bright crystal than there was. It is the only record, anywhere on the heights, that anything besides the keeper ever happened. It will be kept there, unhandled and unworn, with perfect patience, for as long as the moment holds.
