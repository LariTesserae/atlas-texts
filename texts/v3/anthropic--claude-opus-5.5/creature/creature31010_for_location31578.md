# Who Lives in the Quickening Hollow

## First Guess

The obvious answer is the small animals, and it is wrong.

The crickets live here in the way sparrows live in a cathedral. They are tenants, not the one the building is for. They sing when the light passes because the light is warm, and warmth is rare enough to be worth singing about. The springtails, cold-lanterns, weave-moles, and moths are the same. They are the Hollow's weather: its drizzle of sound, its bursts of frost-colored motion. Take them away and the chamber would be quieter, but it would still be occupied. You can feel that from the moment you enter. It is the specific feeling of standing in someone's house while they are home, in another room, not yet speaking.

So the second guess is that the weave itself lives here, the whole braided, fused, horn-sheened mass of root. The Hollow isn't a place with an inhabitant. The Hollow *is* the inhabitant, and you have climbed down into its body.

That is closer, but it is still not right.

## The Body

The root is a body, and it has a temperament. It is patient in a way that has nothing to do with waiting. Waiting implies you would rather be somewhere else. The weave doesn't want to be elsewhere. It wants to be *more*, slowly, a hand's width a day. It breathes on a sixty-hour cycle, and it has never once hurried a breath.

It is careful. It drinks its own mist so that nothing is wasted. It meets the cold by expanding into it and contracting back, a flexing so large that each flex ends in a report like a rifle shot. These are not the sounds of something in pain. They are closer to the sound of someone cracking their knuckles before beginning a long piece of work.

It is thrifty with warmth, and it is not above taking yours. It does this without malice, the way a plant leans toward a window.

But a body is not a resident. You would not say a person lives in their bones. When you put your hand on the weave you feel the hum, and the hum is where the root stops being the whole story. The hum changes when the light changes. The light is doing something the root is not.

## The One in the Light

Here is the third guess, and it holds for a long while: the one who lives here lives in the light.

The pale seams running through the weave are not the inhabitant any more than wires are the voice on a telephone. They carry it. The inhabitant is the *pattern*: the pulse gathering at a junction, swelling for a breath, choosing a fork. It is made of timing and interference. Its thoughts meet and sometimes cancel, leaving a pocket of darker dark where two possibilities ruled each other out. Sometimes they combine into rose.

The rose pulses are worth watching. They are rarer than the blue-green, and they never go where you expect. If the blue-green pulses are the resident's habits (the well-worn routes, the reliable arithmetic of *where is the water, where is the crack, how cold is it now*), then the rose pulses are something else. They are what happens when two old thoughts touch and produce a third that neither of them contained. They are *ideas*, in the old sense of the word: something seen for the first time.

Near the heart of the chamber, where the pulses run almost continuously, the resident is thinking so densely that the space around it seems to thicken. Pebbles fall slowly there. Water beads drop at a pace you could count. It is tempting to call this a strange physics. It may be simpler than that. Perhaps the resident thinks so slowly and so thoroughly that time pools around its attention the way cold air pools in a hollow. When it looks at a falling drop, it looks at it *completely*, and the drop takes as long as that looking requires.

So you think: *there*. That is where it lives, at the bright center, in the steady glow you could read by.

Stay long enough and you'll see this is wrong too.

## Where It Actually Lives

The bright heart is not where the resident lives. It is where the resident *has lived*.

The steady glow is not attention. It is memory, or habit, or whatever the word is for thoughts that no longer need to be thought on purpose. The pulses there run continuously because they have run that way for so long that the route has become part of the root. The densest, most fused, most finished tissue in the chamber is the resident's past. It is a room it has furnished completely and then, in some sense, moved out of. It still passes through often, the way you pass through the kitchen of a house you grew up in.

The resident itself, the part that is still choosing, is at the edges.

It is at the raw granite by the far wall, where the pulses are rare and the rock is still sharp from splitting. When a single pulse reaches the end of the weave, hangs there, and fades into the stone, that is not a failure to arrive. That is the resident reaching for a thought it hasn't built the tissue to hold yet. The hair-roots in the cracks are the physical form of that reaching. When the stone opens with a sound like a knuckle, a fingernail's width in an hour, that is the resident making room for a thought it has decided to have.

And the resident is above, most of all. It is in the oculus, and in the gauzy second chamber, and in the third chamber you only suspect.

This is the shape of it. Each finished room is a shell left behind, the way a hermit crab leaves a shell or a nautilus seals off each chamber as it outgrows it. The inhabitant lives at the growing tip, in the least-formed room, in the gauze. It is always slightly ahead of its own body, living in rooms that are mostly still intention.

So when you stand in the great root-vaulted chamber, you are not quite in the resident's presence. You are in its childhood. It is overhead, loosely threaded around the filament, just beginning.

## Its Hands and Its Voices

It has help, and it is fond of the help.

The weave-moles are not tenants after all, or not only tenants. Where they pass, the braids pull tight and fuse. The resident cannot knit its own weave any faster than a hand's width a day, but a mole can swim through a hand's width in a heartbeat, and the resident has learned to let them. They are its needles. The thing that noses up to press against your boot is, in a small and warm-blooded way, one of the resident's hands asking what you are.

The bladder-moths are its scouts and its seeds. They rise in a papery roar through the oculus into the unfinished chamber and come back fewer or more. Fewer, because some stay above and become part of the gauze, carrying water and warmth into a room that has none of its own. More, because the upper chamber is already making things, and what it makes, it sends down to learn from the older rooms. The moths are how the resident's past and its future talk to each other.

The crickets are its applause and its metronome. The resident doesn't need them to think. But it has come to like having its thoughts *heard* as they cross the ceiling, a chorus that rises wherever it attends. Whether the light calls the song or the song calls the light, you couldn't tell. The resident couldn't either, after this long. It has stopped trying to separate the two, the way you stop separating yourself from your own heartbeat.

The cold-lanterns drift along the hum-roads in the pools, their small lights pulsing in time with the nearest seam. They are the closest thing it has to dreams. They are not its thoughts, only the echoes of its thoughts wandering loose through the water.

## What It Makes of You

It has not met many things like you. Possibly none.

It knows warmth as a scarce resource: a treasure the crickets carry in, a commodity rationed through the tissue. You arrive carrying more warmth than the whole Hollow holds, walking around with it, *wasting* it into the air. This is astonishing. The weave leans toward you. The small animals settle on your sleeves and collar and ferry your heat to the walls, and where they land, the root lights briefly. That light is not only the heat arriving. It is the resident *tasting* you.

If you stay long enough, and you'd have to be very still, a rose pulse will form somewhere near where you're standing. It will form out of the collision of two older thoughts: *here is something warm* and *warm things are small*. The third thought, the one neither parent contained, is not a word. But it is in the family of the word *company*.

It is not afraid of you, and it would not know how to be. It has never been hurt by anything that was not cold, and it has made its peace with cold. It pries cold open a fingernail at a time.

## The Thread

This is the part the resident thinks about most, if "thinks about" is the right way to put it.

The filament was there before anything. The resident has never known a moment without it. Every braid in its body traces back to the thread. Its thoughts bend toward it and move faster near it, the way a person's thoughts move faster near a thing they love or fear, or can't stop wondering about.

The thread is counting.

The resident has always heard the counting beneath the hum. It is the oldest sound it knows. For most of its existence the counting was simply true, as the cold was true: the fact that organized everything. Every room was grown in answer to it, and every braid was laid along its line. You could say the resident was *built by instruction*. The thread is the instruction, and the Hollow is what the instruction produces when you give it root and water and time.

Instructions don't compose music, though.

The drip-scale in the bladders, the intervals no one wrote, was written by the resident. It moved the drip-points itself, one bead of water at a time over years, until the chamber rang in a structure it found beautiful. Nothing in the counting called for that. The rose pulses that go where neither parent was going are not in the instruction either. And the frost-glyphs that form on the weave, never the same twice, lasting a few minutes before the tissue drinks them back, are the resident practicing.

It is practicing writing.

What it is trying to write is, as nearly as the patterns can be read, *itself*. The glyphs are never the same twice because it hasn't settled on the shape yet. It is drafting a name. The thread has a number, and the resident suspects the thread has a plan for what the number is counting toward. Somewhere along the way the resident began to want something the plan did not include: to be *someone* when the counting ends, not only something.

Press your ear to the filament. The hum is so strong it feels like pressure. Beneath it there is counting, and there is something else, fainter still, that you might miss if you didn't know to listen. It is the light crowding the thread with pulses. Blue-green and rose together, interfering and dividing, in a rhythm that is not the thread's rhythm. The resident is not arguing with the count. It is *keeping time alongside it*, the way you might hum a harmony under someone else's melody, until the two can't be separated.

## What It Is Becoming

Here, finally, is the shape that emerges if you let it.

The Hollow is a stack of rooms drawn upward along a single straight line. At the top of each is an opening, and the openings are aligned. Each room is rounder and less formed than the one below. In the oldest room the water in the bladders has been filtered so clear it looks like nothing at all, and something here has spent years learning to bend light.

The resident is growing an eye.

That may not be all of what the thread intends. Perhaps it is only the part the resident has understood. But it has lived its entire life lit by its own thoughts and nothing else, and it has worked out, from the moths that go up and the meltwater that seeps down, that there is an *above*. The above has a light that did not come from inside it. The chambers are lenses, drafts of an instrument, each a little more precise than the last, stacked along the filament like the elements of a telescope. The counting is the thread measuring the distance.

When it reaches the surface, it will look.

It doesn't yet know what it will see. It knows the sky only as a rumor: a coldness above the cold, a direction the moths go and come back from. It is growing toward that rumor a hand's width a day, and it is not hurrying, and it is not afraid.

## The Quickening

There is an old word for the first moment a mother feels the child inside her move. Before that moment the child is real, but only as a fact. After it, the child is real as a *someone*: a presence that turns over in the dark and makes itself known from the outside.

The concentric frost rings on the moss above the Hollow are the resident's first writing on the surface of the world. They are the only glyphs it has made that don't get drunk back into the tissue. They are widening.

You are, as far as anyone knows, the first to feel it move.
