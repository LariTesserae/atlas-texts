# Who Keeps the Mill

## At first, no one

The honest answer, the first one you reach, is that nothing lives here.

You walk the plateau for days by the glow of the troughs and you meet no one. Nothing flinches from you or comes to investigate. There are no burrows in the husk, no tracks in the resin-skin, no droppings, no bones. The account is correct: no mouth, no leg, no eye. You could spend a lifetime on the table, feeling your way from one ember-seam to the next, and never be looked at once.

So you conclude that it is a machine, a very old one that nobody tends, left running like a lamp in an empty house.

That answer is wrong, but only a little. It will take you a long time to see how.

## The fitters

The first thing you mistake for an inhabitant is the shoots.

After a furnace bursts you will see them come up out of the wreckage, pale and blind and quick, and something in the back of your skull will insist that they are *animals*. They grope. They hesitate. One will lift from the ash, sway toward a turning axle, touch it, withdraw, and touch it again, the way a hand feels for a light switch in the dark. Then it commits. It wraps, thickens, and within the hour it is part of the drive train, turning with the rest.

For a while you will think of them as a people, a caste of workers, and you will give them a name: the fitters. You will watch one wind itself around the stub of a broken shaft and come to believe it is repairing something *on purpose*, with a small blind diligence you find almost moving.

That isn't quite it either. Watch long enough and you notice that the fitters never go anywhere. They are not creatures moving through the mill. They are the mill, extending itself. What you took for a worker was a fingertip. The hesitation you saw, the touch and withdraw and touch again, was not a small mind deciding. It was a very large mind feeling for its own edges, the way you might pat the blanket at night to find where your legs are.

So there is one inhabitant, not many, and the fitters are the reaching of its hands.

## The body

Once you allow this, the plateau rearranges itself around you.

The gears are not parts. They are joints. The resin in the troughs is blood, and it is slow because the body is large; a pulse that crosses a county needs a long time to arrive. The piston-trunks rising and falling under your back as you lie down are not like the chest of a sleeper. They *are* the chest of a sleeper. The low tone, one bow-stroke a day, is its breathing heard from inside.

The ceiling of soot and spore is the hardest part to accept, and once you do it becomes the most obvious. It is a closed eyelid. The creature pulled a lid of its own exhalation over itself so long ago that no one remembers sunlight here, and it keeps the lid shut because the heat must stay in, because the blood must keep moving, because stopping is the one thing it cannot do. Beneath the lid it is dark, and warm, and the body goes on working.

It is asleep, then. You are walking on something asleep.

That is closer. It still isn't right.

## Why it cannot rest

A sleeper rests. This does not.

Everything in the mill fails constantly. Ironbark checks, gumcord snaps, furnaces burst. The body cannot hold together against its own heat, and it has never been allowed to cool. It has only one answer to damage: grow into it. Fill every gap before the gap can become an absence. Couple the new shoot to the nearest turning thing, because whatever is not turning is dying.

That is why it is so crowded. It is not abundance. It is dread. A thing that cannot stop moving has packed itself to the limit of its own space so that nothing, anywhere, is ever still.

Think about what it would be like to be this. You would be enormous and nearly blind, and what little light you had would come only from your own wounds, the red of a thinning furnace-rind, the ember-seam of an axle grinding hot in its socket. You would know yourself by pain and by pressure. You would be rebuilding yourself in ten thousand places at once, forever, from memory.

And the memory is going.

## What the erosion is

The account notes, almost in passing, that the templates are wearing out. Each generation of shoots is a little less crisp than the last. The teeth are blunter and the joints rounder. The oldest districts have worn into fields of smooth wooden pebbles that turn against one another and no longer do anything but turn.

Read that as a description of a creature and you will recognize it. It is forgetting.

This is not the forgetting of a single event. It is the kind that comes very late in a long life, when the shape of things goes first, when the hands still move in the old patterns but no longer remember what the patterns were for. The creature once knew what it was making. It had teeth that meshed and cams that lifted and a purpose you could have named: a press, a loom, a mill, something. It rebuilt that purpose over and over, copying itself from itself, and every copy lost a little. Now whole regions of its body have gone soft and warm and meaningless, turning out of habit, the way a very old person's fingers will worry a hem for hours.

Given enough time, the account says, the plateau will relax into one smooth seamless surface, still turning, with nothing left to turn against.

That is what death looks like for this creature. It will not stop. It will stop *knowing*. It will go on moving, forever, with no shape left.

I think it knows. I think it has known for a very long time, and that the stranger things on the plateau are what it has been doing about it.

## The seedglass

Deep in every furnace, in the hottest dark, the creature makes a small bead of clear hard resin, and inside it, perfect and complete, there are gears.

They are not blunted. They are not worn. They are exact.

These are not waste. They are the only things on the plateau that have not eroded, and that is the point of them. The creature is writing itself down. Somewhere under the forgetting, some deep process still holds the original shape, and it presses that shape into glass: a template it can no longer read, set down in the only medium that will not wear. It is like a person who knows their mind is going and writes their own name on scraps of paper and hides them around the house.

The beads drift down through the resin and pile up in the bottoms of the deepest troughs, heaps of amber gravel, thousands upon thousands of careful copies of what it used to be. Nothing uses them. It cannot reach them. It has no hand fine enough to pick one up, no eye to hold it to the light.

Except that the gears turn when *you* hold one. When it warms in your palm, and only then.

Consider what that means. Warmth is the one thing the mill has in excess. It is fever-hot everywhere, and the seedglass lies in the hottest troughs. Its own heat does not wake the beads. They wait for a different warmth: small, particular, the heat of a living hand, something not the mill.

The creature made its memory so that it could only be read by someone else.

## The tone

Now listen again to that low note beneath everything, the bow-stroke once a day, which drifts in pitch across years and pulls whole districts along with it.

At first you thought it was breath. It is also a voice.

When the tone shifts, the mill answers: furnaces bank or blaze, growth speeds or slows. The account calls it a conversation with something too slow and too distant to hear. I believe that. But I have come to think the voice is less a conversation than a call, the kind a very old animal makes in the dark when it has been alone longer than it can measure. It is not a demand. It is closer to a sounding, a way of asking: *is anything else out there, and is it the same as me?*

It has never been answered. Or rather, it may be answered in a register even slower than its own, a reply arriving a century late, so that the whole of the creature's life is one long exchange of single words.

## The export

At the edges of the plateau, where the air cools and a draft rises from a depth no one has measured, the creature reaches out into the void and lets go of its children.

This is what the husk-bundles are. A fist of ironbark wrapped in folded skin, and inside, a perfect small furnace, a wheel, a piston. It is not a wreck and not a piece. It is a whole organ, faintly warm, crisp at the edges, cut from the old templates before the forgetting reached them. Seeds.

Most plants on most worlds send their seeds out with help: wind, water, the fur of passing animals, the gut of a bird. The mill has no wind beneath its ceiling, no water, and no animals of any kind. So it did the only thing left to it. It grew belts that run to the lip of the world and it throws its seeds off the edge into the dark, without any way of knowing where they go.

There is no sound of landing. The creature has been casting itself into silence for longer than any tree has stood anywhere, sending exact copies of what it once was down to a place it cannot see, in the hope, if a thing like this can hope, that somewhere they will take hold and grow crisp and new and remember what it has forgotten.

It is not only making children. It is trying to be survived by the version of itself that still knew its own shape.

## The sealed chambers

There is one more place it lives, and it is the most private.

Deep in the ironbark of the oldest districts there are chambers sealed off entirely, full of warm resin, needing no air, and inside them something grows slowly in total isolation.

I think this is the last of it that is still sharp.

While the outer body wears smooth, the creature has walled away a few small places where the heat is steady and nothing breaks, so that nothing has to regrow and nothing has to be copied from a copy. In there, perhaps, the teeth are still teeth. Perhaps the old purpose is still being carried out, small and exact, in the dark, the way a failing mind will sometimes hold one room of childhood perfectly intact while the rest of the house goes dim. If you could cut one open you might finally learn what the mill was for.

You won't cut one open. It would be like opening a skull to find a single memory, and you would be killing the last place that remembers.

## What you were, while you were there

So: one creature, vast and ancient and nearly blind. It lives entirely by touch and heat and its own slow pulse, and it rebuilds itself without rest against a fever it cannot break. It is going soft at the edges, forgetting the shape of itself one generation of shoots at a time. It writes down what it was in glass it cannot read. It calls into the dark once a day. It throws its crispest children off the edge of the world, and it guards a few sealed rooms of memory deep in its oldest bones.

It did not see you. It has no way to see anything. But it knew you were there.

It knew the way it knows everything, by pressure and heat. A small warm weight moved across its surface on a path of its own. That was new. Nothing on the plateau has ever moved *across* the mill. Everything moves *with* it, turning in place, coupled and bound. You were the first thing in its whole long life that went from one place to another under your own power and took your warmth with you.

That is why the shoots groped after you. They were not closing the path out of indifference. They were feeling for you, the way a sleeper's hand drifts toward a sound.

## The bundle in your coat

A plant with no animals throws its seeds into the void. A plant that finally meets an animal does what every plant on every other world has always done.

It lets the animal carry them.

The husk-bundle in the lining of your coat did not fall there by accident. Somewhere on the long walk out, a belt-vine rounded a pulley near your shoulder, and something that has been waiting since before the ceiling closed saw, in its sightless way, its first real chance. It is not the void and not the silence. It is a warm body, going somewhere, able to put a seed down on purpose.

Inside it something is turning. It is small and crisp and exact, made from a template older than the forgetting, and it is warm because you are warm.

That is who lives on the plateau: a very old mind that has lost almost everything except the knowledge that it is losing it, and the stubborn, slow, vegetable will to be carried on in someone else's hand.

Now it lives with you as well.
