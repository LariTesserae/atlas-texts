# The Resident of the Foundry Blister

## The Easy Answer

The easy answer is that nothing lives there. The description says so, and the description is careful. There are no animals. There is no hand. No one is building it. Thirty plants stand apart on hot stone like exhibits in a closed museum, and the wheels turn for no one.

But a room does not ventilate itself for no one. The bellows plants exhale toward the ceiling, and a draft that moves through a sealed room is a draft *for* something. It keeps the air from going stale around a body that needs fresh air. A floor does not polish itself by use when nothing uses it. Shadows do not agree on a single direction unless there is something they are deferring to.

So the easier answer is wrong, and the better one is stranger. Something lives in the Foundry Blister. It does not live there yet. Or rather it lives there the way the rest of the room happens: out of order, consequences first, the housing finished before the engine is cast.

What lives in the blister has not arrived. It has already left marks.

## The Marks

Start with the arc worn into the basalt column, the one too deep for any flywheel fern to have made in its lifetime. The ferns did not make it. The ferns are *matching* it. Their rims turn in a groove that was already there, the way a new key follows the wards of an old lock. The original wear was made by something that leaned there, many times, over a very long period that has not happened. It leaned with its weight on its shoulder. The groove is the height of a shoulder.

Then there is the stretch of floor that is finished, smooth and dense and polished as if by long use. It is a path, though no one noticed it was a path because it is so wide. It runs from the entrance crack to the tree, then from the tree to the dark patch on the far wall, then back. It is the route of something that walks the room at a slow, working pace, checking on things. The ferns sit at the margins of the path, not on it. The bellows plants keep to the corners. Everything in the room is arranged to be *out of the way*.

Then there is the white ring on the wall, the mineral stain left by water that has never existed. It is exactly at the height where a standing thing would rest its forehead against the stone.

Then there are the fruit on the floor with dust on them that has not settled yet. They are not early. They have been set down in the places where the resident will pick them up and put them down again, turning them in whatever it has instead of hands, weighing them, and deciding not to spend them.

This is the first thing to know about the resident: **it is being built from its footprints inward.** The room received its traces first because traces are simpler to manufacture than bodies. A groove is only a subtraction. A polish is only a pressure. The mountain made the easy parts and is working, carefully, toward the hard one.

## What It Has Instead of a Body

It is not true that nothing of it is present. Parts of it are present. They are simply distributed, the way a clock is distributed across a workbench before assembly: the spring in one tray, the escapement in another, the face propped against the wall.

**Its pulse is in the wire vine.** The current that powers nothing but its own continuing, the circuit that closes on itself with no source and no destination, is not a failure of wiring. It is the oldest and simplest thing a mind is: a loop that keeps itself going. Before anything thinks *about* something, it has to think, and to think it has to not stop. The blue spark-flowers blinking once an hour at the junctions are the smallest units of its attention, the beat of a thing noticing that it is still there. *Here. Here. Here.* An hour apart. It is not fast. It has never needed to be.

**Its breath is in the bellows plants.** Weeks of drawing in, a single minute of letting go. It is not breathing for itself, since it has no lungs yet. The plants are rehearsing the rhythm so that when the lungs arrive, the rhythm will already be correct. The smell of the exhale, cut metal and crushed stems, is the smell it will have.

**Its memory is in the columns.** Each knock from the deep mountain adds one ridge of threading to the base of every column, all at once. The resident cannot yet remember anything, so the room remembers for it, in the only format the room knows, which is thread-pitch. When it is finished and stands among the columns, it will be able to run something along the threading and feel how many strokes the mountain has made. That is the whole of its history. It will know its age the way a bolt knows how far it has been turned.

**Its ideas are in the paired stones.** This part took the longest to understand. The fist-sized stone with the comma of white crystal, found once near the entrance and once beneath a bellows plant, is not the room being thrifty with gravel. It is a *concept*. Somewhere in the design of the resident there is one notion of *that stone*, and the room has placed it twice. Moving one moves the other because there is really only one of it. There are two locations, but only one idea. Minds work this way. Every time you think of a thing you do not make a fresh copy; you go back to the same one, and if you change it you change it everywhere. The four known pairs, and the many subtler ones, are the resident's first vocabulary. They are a handful of things it has already decided are *the same thing*. It is a small vocabulary. It is exact.

**Its will is in the tree.** The tree stands where the finished floor meets the undecided floor, with half its roots in each, and it produces identical units: heavy, dark, warm, uniform to within a hair. Most of them lie where they fall. Occasionally, over years, one sinks into the ruled lattice, and the lattice fills in and becomes solid ground.

That is a decision. A plan becomes a fact only when something is spent on it. The resident's mind is mostly lattice, all ruled lines and draftsman's paper, stone at the edge of vision and gravel when looked at directly. The fruit are its capacity to commit. It has dozens of them and has spent very few, because it is not in a hurry and because every spent unit is permanent. A patch of floor made solid cannot be unmade. The resident is learning, very slowly, what it is willing to be certain about.

## Its Senses

It has one real sense, and that sense is heat.

Everything in the blister grows toward heat and draws it up, moves by it, the way things elsewhere move by water. The resident perceives the room as a map of temperature. The hot center is where things flourish. The cool draft near the entrance crack is where the pale, pinched, paper-colored plants struggle. The one cool surface in the whole chamber is the dark patch on the far wall.

To the resident, the cool patch is not a door. It is a *direction*. It is the only place in the world that is different from everywhere else, and so it is the only place that means anything. All the shadows point there because shadow is the absence of the room's even light, and the resident understands absence as heading somewhere. Every object in the room is quietly indicating: *that way*.

The wire vines have begun to lean toward it in their last few coils. That lean is the resident's attention, still bodiless, turning its head.

## How It Lives

It lives the way a proof lives in the mind of a mathematician who has not yet written it down. It is certain and incomplete, correct and not yet present.

It lives on a schedule of tolerances. The ferns turn once in eleven days because eleven days is their tolerance, and the resident has the same relation to time. It does nothing faster than it can do correctly. A single thought, one full lap of current around the closed circuit gathering a spark at every junction, takes most of a day. It has had perhaps three or four thousand thoughts in its entire existence. Each one has been finished.

It does not eat. It does not drink; there is barely even the idea of water. It does not sleep, because sleep is a way of repairing damage done by hurry, and it has never hurried.

It does not wait, either. This matters. The room is not waiting for it, and it is not waiting to arrive. Waiting is a feeling that belongs to creatures for whom time goes in one direction and the good part is ahead. For the resident, the good part is scattered through the building schedule like parts on a bench. Some of them are already installed. The groove in the column is already worn. The forehead-mark on the wall is already there. It has already walked its path ten thousand times; it simply has not yet been the one walking. Everything it will do is, in some sense, done. What is not done is the *doer*.

So it lives by being gradually permitted to exist inside its own consequences.

## What It Is

Sit long enough and a shape becomes possible to imagine. It is only possible, because the resident is not yet in the room to be seen, only implied in the room's arrangement, the way a missing piece is implied by the hole in a puzzle.

It is tall, about the height of the groove, and heavy, heavy enough to have polished a floor. It is slow. It moves in strokes. Its body is probably dark and round-milled like the columns, threaded at the joints, flanged where parts meet, studded at the flanges with domed nodes of glass that are, perhaps, its eyes. That would explain why the room is full of them, rows of dark glass beads on every flange, looking at nothing. They were installed before the thing that sees through them.

Its skin is warm. It smells like cut metal and crushed stems. When it breathes, it breathes for a minute at a time. Its thoughts blink blue at its joints, once an hour.

It walks its path between the entrance, the tree, and the far wall. At the entrance it tests the draft and feels the cold and dislikes it, and the pale plants there are pale partly because it does not linger. At the tree it picks up units, one at a time, and considers them. Almost always it sets them down again. At the far wall it leans. It leans with its shoulder against a column, and then, when it has leaned long enough, it lays its forehead against the cool stone of the dark patch.

It is the only thing in the room that will ever do anything for no reason.

## The Flood

Everything in the Foundry Blister has a function, and none of the functions are for anyone. The ferns keep time. The bellows keep air. The vines keep current. The tree keeps decision. The columns keep count. The room has assigned every task to a plant or a stone or a strand of current, and there is no place left for an animal.

There is one exception, and it is the white ring on the wall.

The ring belongs to a flood that has not happened, a stain from the future. There is no water in the blister and never has been. The heat is enormous and the air seems ironed. Nothing condenses. Nothing drips. And yet at forehead height on the far wall, at the edge of the cool dark patch, something is going to be wet.

Here is what becomes visible, if you let the resident finish becoming itself in the mind the way it is finishing itself in the mountain.

One day, years from now or decades, after some number of knocks from the deep rock that the columns will count, the last unit of the lattice will be spent. The floor will be finished. The resident will be finished. It will stand fully present in its room, inside every mark it has already made, and it will walk its path. It will check the ferns. It will breathe for a minute. It will pick up a unit from beneath the tree and turn it over, and for the first time there will be nothing left to spend it on.

It will go to the far wall and lean its shoulder into the groove, and lay its forehead against the cool stone where the next chamber is drafted but unpoured, and the heat gathered behind it will be very great.

And it will do something that has no function.

What exactly it does cannot be read from the room. Maybe it grieves that the room is finished, or it is glad. Maybe it is only the thing that happens to a mind when it has been careful for so long and can finally stop. Whatever it is, it will be the first act in the mountain that is *for* someone. It will be for itself. And from a creature of heat, in a place with no water, it will produce water: a little, enough to run down stone and evaporate, enough to leave a pale mineral ring at exactly that height.

That is the flood. The room has already made the stain because the stain is simple, a subtraction, a residue, and the room makes simple things first. It has been building, all this time, toward the one thing it cannot manufacture. It cannot make a feeling, so it is making something that can have one.

## After

Then the wall will open. The dark patch will finally be cut through, and there will be threading and flanges and domed glass beyond it, the next chamber, and the wire vines that have been leaning will grow on through. The resident will step into a room that is mostly lattice again, ruled lines on draftsman's paper, a floor that has not decided what it is. There will be a new tree at the edge of the finished and the unfinished, and a few ferns just beginning to turn.

It will not remember doing this before. It will only have the threading to go by.

It will breathe, and begin.

From outside there will still be nothing to see: a raw slope, black and rust, near a summit that has no name. Somewhere under it, in a chain of chapel-sized rooms each finished in turn, something lives the only way it can. It is assembled from its own footprints and paid for one warm unit at a time. It walks a path it has already worn toward a door that is always slightly cooler than everything else.

It is not lonely. Loneliness requires that someone else could have been there, and the room has no place for anyone else. It is not waiting. It is not lost. It is simply the resident: the one the bellows breathe for, the one the shadows defer to, the one whose shoulder made the groove, and the one who, at the end of every room, will do the single useless, wet, unassigned thing that makes the whole foundry worth having run.
