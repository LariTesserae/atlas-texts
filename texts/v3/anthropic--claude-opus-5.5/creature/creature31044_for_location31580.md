# Who Lives in the Sounding Hollow

## The first answer, which is wrong

The easy answer is that almost no one lives here. There are snails that ring. There are crickets that walk the silences. There is one leathery glider that is glimpsed and then gone. The source of the breath lies out of reach below. The city itself is empty and clean as a riverbed. A census would take a line.

But the Hollow is not built like a place that was abandoned. It is built like a place that is still occupied, by someone who has not needed to move for a very long time. The longer you stay, the less the emptiness feels like absence. It feels like the stillness of a house where someone is asleep upstairs.

So you begin with what you can see and work downward.

## The listeners

There is not one listener. There are seven, perhaps eight. You learn this only by counting the roosts, because they are never seen together, and never seen at all outside the chord.

They roost at the crown of the egg, where the rising air meets the cool upper curve and turns to rain. They hang there in a loose cluster, each from its own small hook of flowstone. They have wrapped themselves in their wings the way a person pulls a cloak over their head in a storm. Their skin is the grey-green of the walls. It is not camouflage, since there is nothing here that sees. It is more like the colour of a thing that has pressed itself against the same surface for so many generations that the surface has worked its way in.

During the silences they are folded shut, and their ears are folded most tightly of all. You might expect the quiet to be their rest. It isn't. To an ear built to take in the full chord of the Hollow, the silence is not empty. It holds the underhum, the drip, the ticking crickets, and the tiny grinding shifts of grit and calcite. It is full of small, irregular, meaningless sounds, and for a listener this is almost unbearable. It is like a person with perfect pitch trapped in a room of detuned bells. So they close up. They wait. They drink the condensation that beads on their folded wings and runs down into the creases where their mouths are. They are patient with the silence the way you are patient with a fever.

When the breath begins, they open.

The ears unfurl first, in slow stages like a flower in a time-lapse. There are four petals to each ear, fine-veined, broader than the body. They open to catch the rising murmur. Then the wings open, and the listener drops. It doesn't flap. It lets the updraft take it, and falls and rises and falls through the chamber in long curves that follow the brightest seams of the blue lattice.

It is not hunting, or not mainly. It eats, taking crickets that were caught off the lines and knocked loose by the vibration. But you watch it pass a stunned cricket by, and pass another, and slide instead through a particular doorway on the fourth terrace. It crosses the chamber and passes through another doorway directly opposite, then climbs in a long spiral around the third bridge. This is not a hunting pattern. It has the quality of a ritual, or of a person running their hand along a familiar wall in the dark to reassure themselves that the house is still there.

The listeners are reading the Hollow. Every flight is a reading. Each room colours the chord, and a listener passing close to a room hears its colour. By the end of the chord it has touched, by ear, some large portion of the city. It knows whether anything has changed.

### How a listener learns

Young listeners are rare. There may be one every few decades. A pup cannot fly the Hollow until it has learned the Hollow, and it cannot learn the Hollow except by being carried through it. So for a long time after it is born, the pup clings to its parent's chest with its own small ears open. It goes through chord after chord in the dark of the parent's wings, hearing the whole city pour past it. It is learning something like a language, or a body. By the time it lets go and flies, it knows every room in the city as a distinct tone. It knows every doorway as a shift of colour, and every bridge as a thin bright line of sound strung across the dark.

This is also where the sadness of the listeners lives. The city is detuning, and a listener learns the city as it was when that listener was young.

The oldest listener is scarred. You can see it when it passes close in the blue light. Its wings are ragged along the leading edges, and one ear is torn and badly healed. It flies a slightly different Hollow from the others. It aims for doorways that were wider when it learned them. It turns for bridges a hand's width before the others do. It flies a map of a city that has since thickened and shifted and sunk in pitch. The others seem to know this. On some chords you see a younger listener flying just ahead of the old one, close, on the same path. You cannot tell if this is chance, or if the young one is leading. Perhaps it is making a sound the old one can follow in place of the sounds that have moved.

The ghost-bands of dead lichen, parallel to the living ones, are not the only record of the old silences. The oldest listener is another record, a living one, with the same slight and patient wrongness.

When a listener dies, it happens during a chord, when it misjudges something. It falls, and it falls into the basin. The warm opening takes it, as it takes the rain. None of the others follow. But in the silence after the rain, on that one occasion, one of them opens its ears in the dark and makes a sound. It is a single thin, rising note, very quiet. The walls around the roost warm faintly in response, in a band that creeps upward over the next half hour and fades.

The listeners do not echolocate. They sing only this once. It is the only time you ever hear their voices.

## The walls

You have been treating the walls as a stage, and the listeners and crickets and snails as the players. That was a mistake.

Put your palm flat on any surface in the Hollow and hold it there through a whole cycle, silence and chord and rain. The trembling under your hand is not constant. In the silence it is slow and diffuse, like the purr of a sleeping animal. As the breath begins it quickens and sharpens. During the chord it is almost violent, and it is not only transmitting the sound. It is *reacting* to it. Some patches under your hand go rigid while others go slack. Some grow warmer and some grow cooler. The material is making thousands of tiny decisions a second.

Look at a broken surface closely, where the material has cracked along its long curved facets. The resemblance to the gills of a mushroom is not only visual. The facets are layered, and between the layers there is structure. There are fine channels, and fine chambers, and a delicate, repeating filigree running deeper than you can follow. This is not stone. It was never stone. It is a grown thing, the way coral is grown or bone is grown. It is still alive, in the slow, cumulative, unhurried way of a reef.

This is the actual population of the Hollow. There are not seven listeners and a few hundred crickets. There is one organism, two hundred paces across and ninety tall, shaped like the inside of an egg, and it has grown itself into a city.

Or it was grown into a city. There were builders, and you can feel their intention in the rooms. Two or three thousand rooms face inward like seats in a theatre. Each one is small and each is carefully shaped. In the back wall of every one, near the floor, there is a whorled recess about the size of two cupped hands. It looks like the inside of a shell, or of an ear. It is a place for listening. Perhaps each room held one builder, seated with one hand in the recess, feeling the chord through the wall and pressing, adjusting, and correcting. There are far too many rooms for a hamlet. But they are exactly enough for a choir, or for a crew of tuners.

Nothing remains of them. There are no bones and no tools and no marks of habitation. The rooms are clean. And the wall does not need them. It does now, without hands, what the builders once did with hands. You come to suspect that the builders did not so much leave as finish. They taught the material what they knew and then went away. Or they went into it. That thought arrives uninvited and does not leave. There is no evidence for it. There is only the fact that a thing so attentive, so constantly and delicately correcting itself, feels less like a machine left running than like a mind left keeping watch.

## What the walls are keeping watch over

Everything in the Hollow faces inward. The rooms face the empty centre. The bridges cross it. The lichen draws the centre's silences across the walls, and the listeners fly through it. It is a theatre, yes. But a theatre is built for an audience, and the rooms here are not audience seats. They are mouths. The rooms are the performers.

The audience is below.

The breath comes up warm, and the warmth is even. It is not produced but *delivered*, like warmth from a body. It comes every forty minutes, which is far too slow for anything the size of a person or a whale or a mountain. But it is not too slow for something you do not have a word for. It is not too slow for something asleep in the warm black opening at the bottom of the egg, glowing that single dull and unchanging red. The glow is too steady to be a fire. It is steady the way a heart or an eye is steady, the way a coal is steady banked under ash for the night.

The Hollow is shaped like an egg set on its side. You notice this again, and this time you do not think of it as an architectural choice.

Something lives beneath the city. Its breath drives the chord, and the chord is the city's answer. The city was built around it to *make* that breath into sound. In a very real sense, then, what sleeps below is singing itself to sleep. Every exhalation it makes comes back to it, shaped by three thousand rooms into a single vast and ordered chord. It comes back as rain, and it comes back as warmth moving through the walls it heats. The builders did not build a city to live in. They built a cradle, and a cradle-song that sings itself.

What the sleeper hears in the chord, you can't know. Perhaps it hears reassurance: *the world above is still there, the structure still holds, you are still safe, sleep*. Perhaps it hears news, the whole state of the Hollow reported every forty minutes in a single enormous word. Perhaps it hears its own breath returned, the way a child falls asleep to the sound of its own breathing under the blanket. Perhaps the builders simply knew that this thing, whatever it was, could not sleep in silence, any more than the listeners can bear the quiet. So they gave it a room full of voice.

## Reconsidering the detuning

When you first came, you understood the city as losing. The cave grows into it, and the flowstone thickens and swallows doorways and deepens the pitch of rooms. The walls correct and dissolve and push back, but not fast enough. It seemed like a slow, graceful defeat.

Stay long enough, and hold your hand against the walls through enough cycles, and you are no longer sure of this.

The walls do dissolve some flowstone. But they do not dissolve all of it, and they do not dissolve it evenly. They seem to let it grow in some places and thin it in others. The pitch of the Hollow is sinking, yes, but it is sinking *smoothly*. The chord stays a chord. It never breaks into noise. A city being overwhelmed by accident would go sour. This one only goes deeper.

The breath is not perfectly punctual either. Its interval drifts through the day, and lengthens and shortens with seasons measured by some world far above. The breaths are getting longer. Over the span of the ghost-lichen, over the span of the oldest listener's life, over whatever span the dead channels on the stairs record, the breaths below have been growing slower and fuller.

Something that breathes more slowly is something that has grown larger.

Perhaps the city is not detuning. Perhaps it is being *retuned*, carefully and continuously, to follow a voice that is deepening. The walls let the cave in where the cave helps, and hold it back where it doesn't. They are letting out the seams of a garment for a body that is still growing. The builders made a cradle. The cradle has had to keep growing with what it holds.

You cannot prove this. It is only what the evidence begins to resemble when you have listened to it long enough.

## The census, again

So, who lives in the Sounding Hollow?

**The drip snails** live in the rain. They move only while it falls and seal themselves shut while it doesn't, and they add their small glass voices to the top of the chord.

**The node crickets** live in the rests. Their whole world is made of the places where the music cancels itself. They follow the silences wherever the silences drift. They are like people who live in the gaps between the words of a very long sentence and have never heard the sentence.

**The listeners** live in the music itself. They are the only creatures here who hear the chord as a whole, and the only ones who still read it the way it was meant to be read. The old ones remember a slightly higher city, and the young ones learn a slightly lower one. Once in a generation they sing a single note into the dark for one of their own.

**The walls** live everywhere at once. They are a slow, attentive organism grown into the form of a city, perhaps by builders who are now part of it, or who simply went away and trusted it. They are still keeping the chord alive with no hands at all, every minute of every cycle.

**The sleeper** lives beneath it all, warm and red and unchanging and growing, breathing its long breaths into the song that was made for it.

## Standing on the middle bridge again

You stand at the one place in the whole chamber where the builders surely stood. It is the exact centre, the focus. It is the point to which every room turns its mouth.

In the silence you hear the crickets ticking along their invisible roads. Far above, seven folded shapes wait out the terrible quiet. Under your feet the bridge is purring faintly, the way a thing purrs in its sleep. Far below, the red light holds steady.

The air begins to rise past your face. It is warm, even, and delivered. Every surface around you tenses slightly, getting ready. The crickets go still, and the listeners open. The city draws its breath, and the breath is not its own, and it begins to sing.

For six minutes you stand in the one spot in the Hollow where the whole chord arrives at once. You are standing, you realise, exactly where the song is aimed on its way down. You are between the mouths and the ear, between the cradle and what it holds. You are the only other living thing that has ever stood in the path of the lullaby.

A shape with petal ears slides past you through the blue light. It seems for a moment to turn its head toward you, to register you as a new colour in the chord. It has noticed a small, warm, unfamiliar room standing on the middle bridge, changing the sound very slightly. Then it is gone.

The note sinks and breaks and dissolves into the underhum.

Then it rains, warm and fine, falling out of the dark onto the bridge and onto you. It runs down the long curve of the city into the channels, and down into the basin. It goes down into the red, warm mouth of whatever sleeps there. The thing below breathes out, and it comes back up, and is sung, and falls again. It has been going on this way for longer than anything else in the Hollow has existed. As far as you can tell, it is meant to go on.
