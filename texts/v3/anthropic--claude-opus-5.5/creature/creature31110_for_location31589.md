# What Lives in the Murmuring Levels

The honest first answer is *nothing*, and for the first day it will seem to be true.

You walk through sedge that closes behind you. You push through willow carr with the wet leaves slapping your face. You kneel by pewter pools and wait. Nothing comes. The drone fills every space an animal might fill: the pause between footsteps, the gap where birdsong would be, the silence in which you would ordinarily hear a vole in the grass. The Levels seem too full of sound to have room for anything that makes its own.

But that answer is wrong, or at least it is only the answer that holds in this version of the place. Life here is scarce, and none of it is ordinary. To live in the Levels a thing has to tolerate being in more than one condition at once. It has to put up with its footprints arriving early, with its reflection disagreeing with it, with a world that is sometimes flooded and sometimes dry under the same hummock. Most things cannot. The few that can have not merely adapted to the silt's confusion. They have made a living from it.

This is an attempt to describe them, beginning with the easiest to understand and going deeper, as the Levels themselves do, toward the ones that are harder to hold in the mind.

---

## The Forewader

Start with the grey wading bird, because it is the nearest thing here to an ordinary animal. It is tall and hunched, with a neck like a folded question and a bill the colour of wet slate. It stands in the shallows the way herons everywhere stand, still enough that the mist beads on its back.

Its footprints appear in the mud a moment before its feet come down. Watch it long enough and you will understand that this is not a quirk. It is the whole of the creature's strategy.

The Forewader lives slightly ahead of itself. It does not hunt the way a heron hunts, by stillness and patience and the sudden strike. It hunts by already being where the fish will be. You will see it lower its bill into water with nothing in it, and a heartbeat later a small dark fish will turn in the current and swim, quite calmly, into the bill that is waiting for it. The fish does not seem surprised. Perhaps from where it sits in time, there was never any other place for it to go.

The birds do not flock. Each one keeps a long reach of channel and walks it slowly. Where two territories meet, you can sometimes see a Forewader standing at the boundary facing its neighbour, and neither moves. They seem to settle the dispute by looking a few seconds forward, seeing who wins, and agreeing not to bother.

No one has found a nest. There are no trees in the Levels tall enough for a heronry, and the hummocks are too soft. But in certain pools, the ones that reflect a version of the Levels in which the alders grew tall, taller than any alder has a right to, you can sometimes see ragged platforms of sticks in the high branches, and pale grey shapes shifting on them. If you watch long enough, a young Forewader, gawky and new-feathered, will appear in the shallows of *this* world one morning, standing where no chick hatched. It looks around at the low grey marsh as if it had expected taller trees.

The Forewader is the key that opens the rest of this account. It is an animal, with feathers and bones and a hunger. It simply does not live in one moment at a time, and the Levels let it get away with this.

## The Eel

The channels hold eels, long and dark, and an eel seen in one pool can often be seen at the same instant in another, a quarter mile off, bearing the same scar and turning in the same slow curve.

The first question people ask is how many eels there are. It is the wrong question, but follow it anyway, because following it is instructive.

Keep a tally. Mark each eel by its scars, its length, the pale notch in the fin. Across a season you will count perhaps forty sightings, and you will find that they come from perhaps six animals. Six eels in sixty miles of water. Then you will notice that two of your six share a scar in the same place, only one is thinner, as if younger, and another two share a kink in the spine where one is thick and blunt-headed, as if older. Push further and the count collapses toward one.

Nobody is sure there is only one eel in the Levels. But nobody can prove there are two.

What is certain is that the eel is old, old enough that "old" stops meaning much. Its skin has the dull iridescence of the silt, and when it moves through the shallows the drone around it shifts very slightly, as though the water passing along its body strikes the lattices differently. Where the eel lies still for long, the moss on the nearest bank grows in dense spirals.

Eels elsewhere in the world make a great migration. They go down to the sea, cross whole oceans, spawn in deep water, and die, and their young drift all the way back. The Levels have no sea. Their water goes nowhere; it seeps and settles and rises as mist.

But there are pools, a few, far in the interior, that reflect a coast. You look down past the reed stems and see a grey shoreline, a long beach, breakers rolling in under a low sky. Salt air rises off these pools. In the drone's lull you can hear surf in them. And sometimes the eel comes to one of these pools, and lies in the shallows with its head toward the reflected sea, and stays there for days. Then it is gone. Weeks later, in the channels, the water is full of glass eels, tiny, transparent, thousands of them, shimmering in every pool at once, or perhaps only one pool, seen everywhere.

So the eel makes its migration after all. It simply crosses to a sea that exists in another version of the ground. Whether the young it returns with are its children or itself, begun again, is one of the things the Levels do not answer.

## The Lull-Moths

The pale moths come out when the drone drops to its trough. They rise from the sedge in their hundreds and hang over the reed beds in trembling clouds, drawn to the singing reeds as other moths are drawn to lamps.

At first they seem to be listening. Watch longer and you will realise they are doing something closer to eating.

The powder on a moth's wings is silt, the finest fraction of it, grains so small they float. A lull-moth's wing is a membrane dusted with lattices, and as it hangs in the moving air near a reed bed, the reed's harmonic passes through that dust and is held there. The moths drink sound. They gather it the way bees gather nectar, and what they gather, they keep.

If you are very close to a cloud of them in the quietest part of the lull, when the drone is at its lowest and you can hear the drip of water and the creak of alder, you will hear something else: a faint, high, many-voiced shimmer, and it is the chord. The part of it that has gone quiet, the upper voices, the choir's open vowel that only sounds when the channels run fast, is being held in the air by ten thousand tiny wings. The moths carry the chord through the trough. They keep the note while the Levels rest.

Crush one between your fingers and it rings. Very quietly, for less than a second, a single pure tone, and then it is dust on your skin, shimmering.

They live one lull. They hatch as the drone falls, gather, hold, and when the chord rises again toward its peak they settle back down onto the reeds and die there, and the reed takes up their dust, and the harmonic of that reed is very slightly different afterward. Over centuries the reed beds have been tuned this way, one small death at a time. The Levels' song is partly made of moths.

Something about this is important and hard to say plainly. The silt holds states. The moths hold sound. The reeds hold the moths. It is as though the Levels, having worn down into a marsh, have grown living parts to do what their engineered parts can no longer do reliably, to carry a thread across the gaps and keep the thought from losing its place. The moths may be a memory. Not a memory of anything in particular; just the Levels' way of remembering where they were.

## The Borrowed

Here the account must turn toward things that do not live in this version of the Levels at all.

Look into the pools long enough and you will see that the reflected worlds are not empty. In the pool that shows drought, the channels cracked and the alders bare, there are swallows, dozens of them, skimming low over the dry mud after insects that do not exist here. In the pool that shows snow there is a fox, a lean red fox, trotting along the frozen bank, its breath smoking. In the pool that shows night, frogs are calling, and though you are standing in grey afternoon silence, you can hear them: a thin, rhythmic piping coming up out of the water, from the spring that is happening in that other ground.

These are the Borrowed, the life of the Levels' other conditions. There is far more of it than there is here. It seems the grey, drizzling, drone-choked version of the marsh you stand in is one of the poorer ones. Elsewhere in the stored states of the ground there are Levels with herds of something like deer grazing the sedge, Levels loud with geese, Levels where the water is warm and dragonflies stitch the air above every pool.

Mostly they are only reflections. But the silt leaks, and sometimes the Borrowed leak with it.

A swallow will come up out of the drought-pool, a real swallow, flying fast and low, and you will feel the wind of it pass your face, and it will cross twenty yards of this grey world, confused, and dive into another pool and be gone. A fox's pawprints will appear in the moss, crisp with frost that melts as you watch. You will find a feather on a hummock from a bird that is not found in the Levels, or anywhere, and it will be dry.

And sometimes, rarely, the Borrowed notice.

It is unsettling the first time. You are kneeling at the snow-pool, looking down at the fox on its white bank, and the fox stops and turns its head and looks up. Up out of the water, through the surface, at you. Its ears go forward. It watches you for a long moment, and then trots on.

One can only assume that in the fox's world there is a pool on that bank, and in the pool the fox sees a grey, drizzling, strange-smelling marsh, and a figure kneeling at the edge of it, and that you are, to the fox, one of the Borrowed.

## The Walkers

This is where the account has to include people, though there are no people living in the Levels. Or rather, not in the ordinary way.

In the mist, at the edge of sight, there are figures. They are grey and indistinct and always at a distance, on the far side of open sedge, at the end of a long channel bank, half-hidden in a willow carr. They are walking. Sometimes toward you, sometimes across your path, sometimes away. They never arrive. Walk toward one and you will reach the place where it was and find only sedge and wet moss and, sometimes, boot prints, fresh and filling with water.

The Walkers are travellers. Specifically, they are travellers who took other paths.

Every step through the Levels is a small choice made in a place that was built to hold choices. Left bank or right. Through the willows or around. Stop at this pool or go on. The silt keeps the paths not taken, the way it keeps the drought and the snow, and when the ground is thick with lattices and the drone is high, those paths can become faintly visible. You, going left, and you, going right, walking parallel for a few hundred yards through mist, each seeing a grey shape across the reeds.

The boot prints you find coming toward you along the channel bank are yours. So are the echoes that come back in a voice that is nearly yours, with words you haven't said yet. That is you, a little further along some other line, calling back.

Most Walkers are temporary. The branches close, the paths rejoin, the figure in the mist fades as you come out of the dense ground toward the edges. But some do not fade.

There are Walkers in the deep interior who have been walking a long time. Their clothes are old-fashioned. One, who is seen near the black stub of the pillar, carries something on her back like a surveyor's staff. Another, a man in a long coat, appears most often on the same stretch of alder bank, always heading north, and in thirty years of sightings he has not grown older. They are the versions of travellers who went deeper than the travellers themselves did. Somebody, generations ago, came to the Levels and reached a fork and turned back. The one who did not turn back is still here, still walking, held in the silt like a fox in a pool of snow.

Are they alive? They cast no shadow in the dim light, but nothing here casts much of a shadow. Their footprints fill with water like anyone's. People who have come close to one, very close, say that its face was calm, and its lips were moving, and that under the drone they could just make out that it was humming along.

## The One That Calls

Now the last of them, the one this account has been walking toward.

Very rarely, something calls. It is a single low note below even the floor of the drone, felt in the long bones more than heard. The eels go still when it sounds.

The first guess is that it is an animal: something large, deep in the interior, the Levels' apex creature, a bittern the size of a house, a marsh-bull. People have gone looking. They have found nothing, but they have come back with fragments.

In the drought-pools there are tracks. Great rounded depressions pressed into the cracked mud, each wide enough to lie down in, running in a long line across the dry basin, filled here and there with the pale remains of dead reeds. In this world, where those tracks should be, there is only sedge.

In the night-pools, sometimes, the stars go out. A long low darkness moves across the reflected sky, blotting constellations as it goes, and after a while the stars return.

In the snow-pools, once, a shape was seen standing on the white horizon of the reflection, and the person who saw it described it as tall, as antlered, as having more legs than they could count before it turned side-on and seemed to have none at all, only a kind of fold in the air.

In a pool that showed high summer, someone saw a long pale body lying half in the water of a channel, very still, larger than the channel should have been able to hold. It had no face that they could see. The swallows in that pool were landing on it.

So the second guess is that the Caller is one creature seen through many versions of the ground, and that in each version it takes a different shape because the ground holds it differently. It lives mostly in the conditions the Levels are *not* in right now. That would explain why it is never seen here. This grey world is only one page of the tracing paper, and the Caller walks the others, and only its voice comes through.

That is closer. But it still is not right, because it still treats the Caller as an animal that happens to live in the Levels, the way the Forewader does. And the evidence does not quite fit.

Consider when it calls. Not at random. Always at the peak of the drone's nineteen-hour tide, in those few minutes when the reeds shiver and the pools ring and the Levels lose track of which state they are in, when drought and flood and snow and summer are laid over one another and flicker. The Caller's note comes at the height of that flickering. And after it sounds, something happens that people only notice if they have been listening for a long time.

For a moment, the pools agree.

Every reflection, across as much of the Levels as anyone can see, shows the same thing at once. Not the sky above; not the drought or the snow; something else, the same in every pool, a single image. Nobody who has seen it can say what it was. They say it was grey. They say it was very still. They say it looked like the pools near the pillar, the blank ones, except that it was not blank, only too even and too whole to see into. Then the chord ebbs and the pools scatter back into their many skies.

Consider, too, the eels going still, and the moths going silent in their clouds, and the Forewaders freezing mid-step with their footprints neither ahead of them nor behind but exactly underneath. For the length of the note, everything in the Levels is in one place, at one time, in one condition.

The third guess, then: the Caller is not a thing that lives in the device. It is the thing the device is *for*.

The Levels were built, once, to hold the world in more than one condition. A machine for possibility; a mind, perhaps, or an archive, or an instrument. An instrument is made to be played. A mind is made to be asked. The drone, the whole sixty-mile chord, rising and falling on its slow breath, has the shape of something working toward an answer. And a working like that needs a question.

The Caller's note, then, is the question. Asked into the Levels at the moment they are most open, most undecided, most full of every version of themselves, it gives them something to resolve toward. And for one breath they do. The pools agree, everything stands still, the ground chooses. Then the chord falls and the answer comes apart, and the long working begins again, nineteen hours to the next peak.

It has been asking for longer than anyone can say. Perhaps the builders made it, a last component, the part meant to operate the rest. Perhaps it was whoever the builders were, or the last of them, worn down with everything else. Perhaps it came later, drawn by the drone the way the moths are drawn, and found that the machine responded to it, and stayed.
