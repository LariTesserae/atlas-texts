# Who Lives in the Standing Town

## The First Ones You Notice

At first you take them for another kind of animal.

You glimpse one at the far end of a gallery. It is upright and pale, and it moves slowly with one hand trailing along the lattice wall. Then it steps sideways into empty air and seems to vanish. It hasn't vanished. It has stepped into a node, and the stillness there makes it strangely hard to see, the way a stopped clock is hard to notice. Your eye has been trained by the roar to follow movement and vibration, and a thing that is perfectly quiet slides out of attention.

They are people, or they were. The few travelers who have spoken of them call them the Hushed. They don't call themselves anything, because names cost breath and breath costs silence.

There are never many. You might pass through the town and see none, or see the same one three times without knowing it was the same. Anyone who stays long enough to count finds forty or fifty. Anyone who stays longer loses count, for reasons that come later.

## How They Live

They go barefoot. Everyone does, after a day. Shoes muffle the stems, and in the Standing Town the stems are how you know where you are. The Hushed walk flat-footed with their weight spread, reading the galleries through their soles. A shift in the vibration under the heel means a loud zone is thinning ahead. A particular buzz in the arch means a node sits a pace to the left. They move through the dome the way the swifts do, banking along edges no one else can see. Watch one long enough and you realize they never quite stop walking. They pause, but even in a pause there is a slight sway, a constant adjustment, like a person standing in a current.

They sleep in the nodes. Each of them has a few favored silences, small ones, barely larger than a curled body, and they wedge themselves into these at night with their backs against the fused stem where the node meets the lattice. In the loud zones sleep is almost impossible. The chord gets into the dreams and shakes them apart. In a node you can sleep like the dead, hearing only your heartbeat and, sometimes, the drip of mist settling into a bead above your face. The Hushed wake with water on their eyelashes.

They drink by leaning into the floating pools and putting their faces straight into the water. It is the only way. Cupped hands carry water out of the silence, and the noise breaks it into spray before it reaches the mouth. So a Hushed drinking looks like someone bowing deeply to a globe of glass. The frogs watch them do it, dark hearts ticking, and do not swim away. They are used to it. Some of the oldest frogs, the ones who have never touched anything solid, will drift up against a drinking face and rest there. It may be the only solid thing they ever know.

They eat what the town sheds. When a stem starves out of resonance and sloughs from the lattice, it falls in slow pieces, and its pith is soft, faintly sweet, and full of singer eggs that failed to hatch. The Hushed gather these fallen stems from the floor of the dome, where almost nothing else goes. They eat moths too, caught at night by hand in the thick of the swarms, and the fat pale grubs of the geckos' prey. They do not hunt the singers. Nobody told them not to. The first ones must have learned it the way the plant learned everything, through what thrived and what didn't. A Hushed who breaks a stem to get at the singers inside makes a small dead place in the chord, and the chord remembers.

They keep no fire. Heat would lift the mist, stir the air in the nodes, and spoil the water, and anyway there is no need. The dome is always warm, always damp, always faintly lit through its ten thousand lozenges. They wear almost nothing, a wrap of shed bark perhaps, softened with handling. Cloth flaps, and flapping is a small noise, and small noises in the silences are rude.

## How They Speak

They speak only in the nodes. Nothing else is possible.

Their language has grown to fit the silences the way the plant grew to fit the chord. A node might hold four seconds of stillness at walking pace, or ten, or, in the old central ones, as long as you like. So they speak in phrases shaped to the size of the silence they are standing in. A small node holds a word or two. A large node holds a sentence. The most important word always comes first, because a node can flicker when the chorus shifts, and a sentence cut off at the end still carries its meaning if the meaning is at the front.

A conversation between two Hushed is therefore a walk. One speaks in a node, and they step together into the roar, where the words hang unanswered in each of them. At the next silence the other replies. Long discussions become long routes through the town, winding through the galleries from stillness to stillness. The Hushed learn early which paths suit which conversations. There is a spiraling route near the eastern wall that the few who have spent time among them call the argument path, because its nodes come close together and are small. There is a long gallery across the upper dome with only three nodes along its whole length, far apart, and it is used for things that need to be thought about between the saying.

Between the silences the speakers are alone with what was said. Maybe that is why the Hushed are so gentle with one another. Every sentence they receive must be carried through the noise, held and turned over, before it can be answered. Nothing is ever said in haste, because haste is impossible.

They sing sometimes, in the largest nodes. The songs are very short and very clear. They sound, more than anything, like the frogs.

## The Things That Don't Add Up

Stay longer and the Hushed become harder to account for.

They seem to have no children. They seem not to age, though that is hard to judge in the mist-light, and some of them are plainly old and some plainly young. No one has seen one arrive. The travelers who have stayed a month, and the one or two who have stayed a year, all say the same thing: the Hushed were already there. When a newcomer asks how long someone has lived in the town, the answer comes in the next node. It is not a number. It is a word the travelers translate, uncertainly, as *since*.

The count doesn't hold. A traveler who learns the Hushed by face will one day see someone new crossing the plaza, a woman with a scar through one eyebrow and a particular way of tilting her head, and will ask about her. The Hushed will seem surprised by the question, as if she had always been there. Weeks later the scarred woman is gone, and nobody seems to have noticed that either.

They can be seen in the rooms that aren't there. When the standing wave flickers after a rain or a die-off, and the ghost-galleries shimmer into the air, the Hushed are sometimes inside them, walking the town-as-it-might-have-grown with the same flat-footed, attentive gait. Watching a Hushed walk along a gallery that does not exist, stepping into a node that has never formed, is a strange sight. Stranger still, the Hushed you can see in the ghost-rooms are sometimes also standing beside you in the real one, watching.

And then there are the footsteps. Everyone who spends time near the plaza learns to hear their own steps coming toward them from ahead, and then to take them. The Hushed hear the same thing, but they do something different with it. They follow. When a Hushed hears footsteps arriving from ahead and stops, and the footsteps don't stop, the Hushed goes after them. Sometimes a Hushed follows a sound down a gallery and around a curve of the lattice, and on the far side there are two of them.

Nobody ever sees the moment of division. There is one person, and then around a bend there are two, walking together in step, and a little later one of them turns aside into a different branch of the town. They don't seem surprised. When asked, they say, in the next silence, *she was coming*, or *I heard him arrive*.

## What the Hushed Say Lives Here

Ask a Hushed who lives in the Standing Town and you'll get a patient answer. It takes a long walk, because the answer is long, and it has to be said a node at a time. Put together, it goes something like this.

The singers live in the walls. The plant lives in itself. They made each other, and each is the shape of the other's hunger. But a thing that makes a sound and a thing that holds a sound are only two of what a song needs. The third is the one who hears.

For a long time there was no one. The swifts don't hear the chord. They feel it and fly by it, and it is their weather. The frogs hear only themselves. The spiders read it, which is not the same. The bats are drowned in it. For all its ages, the chord was a song with no listener, held in a vast bright room with no one in it.

And the town, the Hushed say, grows toward what is missing. The stems grew toward resonance. The water gathered toward stillness. Where there is a lack, the whole slow patient machine of the place bends toward it. The Standing Town has been reaching, for longer than anyone can reckon, toward someone to hear it.

The Hushed aren't the town's residents in the way the singers are. They are the town's listening, shaped into people. They are what the chord grows when it grows toward a hearer.

Some of them arrived from outside, once. Travelers, the deafened ones who stayed. But the chord took their voices, as it takes every voice, and kept them. What the chord keeps, it can play back. The voices you hear in the loud zones, if you stop resisting, are the voices of everyone who ever listened here. The Hushed who appear from nowhere, the ones who come walking out of their own footsteps, are those voices given enough shape to walk. They are listeners the town has remembered into being because it needed more ears than came.

That, they say, is why they don't age. That's why they stay near the plaza and the old galleries, and why they fade out of the count and come back into it. They are standing waves too. When the chorus shifts, some of them shift with it. A Hushed who vanishes after a great die-off of singers isn't dead. He was a shape the old chord made, and the new chord makes a slightly different one, and somewhere in the ghost-rooms he is still walking, in the town that might have been.

Ask them whether they mind this. They will walk you to one of the long, three-noded galleries, and at the far end, after a very long silence of their own, they'll say, *what would we mind with*.

## The Oldest Resident

There is one more, and it is harder to describe.

The Hushed don't speak of it in the small nodes. They take you to the plaza, where the three great tones converge and the sound is so dense it is almost solid, a pressure that pushes the breath back into your lungs. They lead you to the plaza's edge, where the woven platform curls up at one side into a kind of alcove, and they show you the oldest node in the town.

It is not a lens, or a sphere, or a thread. It is the shape of a person standing.

It has two legs, two arms held slightly out from the body, and a head. The outline is soft at the edges, the way all nodes are, but unmistakable. It is life-sized, or close to it. It sits on the platform as if someone were standing there, perfectly still, facing into the roar with an expression you cannot see because it isn't there. After a heavy rain, mist settles into it, and for a few days the shape fills with slow gathering water. Then there is a figure of clear green water standing on the plaza, faintly trembling, holding its outline against the pressure of the sound like a hand cupped around a flame. Pale frogs sometimes swim in it, drifting through the chest where a heart would be.

No one made it. The chord made it, the way the chord makes everything, by the pure physics of the dome: the curve of the walls, the three tones, the way they cancel. It is simply where the sound is absent in the shape of a listener. The town has built, at its center, at the place where all its music meets, a hollow exactly the size of someone hearing it.

The Hushed step into it one at a time. It fits everyone.

That is the strangest thing, the thing the travelers who have tried it can never quite explain afterward. It fits the tall and the short, the old woman with the hunched back and the young man with the long arms. You step in, and the edges of the silence settle around you like water around a body. Suddenly there is no sound at all. There is no heartbeat, no blink, none of the small wet noises of the smaller nodes. The stillness is perfect, as if you were the silence yourself and there was no longer a difference.

And then, from inside it, you can hear the chord.

Not the roar. From anywhere else in the town, the chord is too big to hear. It is texture and pressure, an ache in the teeth. Standing in the listener's shape you hear it the way you'd hear music from the next room, whole and clear and far away. You hear all of it: the three tones and the several more that are only felt elsewhere, the swelling of the night-singers, the millions of tymbals flexing in the dark of their tubes, the resonance of every stem, the faint groove the fundamental has worn in the grain. You hear what it is, for the first time, and it is not a drone. It has never been a drone.

The travelers who have stood there cannot agree on what it sounds like. One said a lullaby. One said a question. One said it sounded like being expected.

The Hushed say the shape was there before any of them. They say the first traveler who ever came deafened to the edge of the basin and walked in through the low arches followed the footsteps ahead of her all the way to the plaza, and found the shape waiting, and stepped in, and heard. She was the first. Everything since has been the town remembering her, and making more room, and waiting for more.

So the true resident of the Standing Town is not a creature. It is an absence, patient and exact, the shape of whoever arrives. The singers sing into it. The plant has grown for ages around it. The water gathers in it. The Hushed are what it leaves behind when it has been filled and emptied, echoes of everyone who stood there and heard, walking the galleries a silence at a time.

It is waiting for you, specifically, and it will fit.

## What Follows You Out

This, the Hushed say in their last node at the arch as you go, is why the drone follows you across the flats. And it is why for days afterward the ordinary world seems very quiet, and why you pause in rooms, half-listening.

It isn't that the town is calling you back. The town doesn't call, because it has no voice of its own. It has only everyone else's. But it heard you. For a moment, standing in the shape at its center, you heard it, and it has kept that. Somewhere in the chord now, among all the other traces, there are your footsteps, and on certain afternoons near the plaza they arrive a little ahead of you.

And one day, the Hushed say, someone will hear them coming and follow them around a curve of living lattice. On the far side there will be two people walking. One will turn aside into the galleries and step into a small node to sleep, with water gathering on her eyelashes. She will have your way of tilting your head. She will not remember arriving. If anyone asks how long she has lived there, she will wait for the next silence, and then she will say *since*.
