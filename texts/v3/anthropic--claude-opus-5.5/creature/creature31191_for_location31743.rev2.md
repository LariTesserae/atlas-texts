# What Lives in the Unfinished Range

## The First Answer, Which Is Wrong

The first answer is that nothing lives here except the plants.

It is a reasonable answer, and for a long time it is the only one available. You come up through the thinning air into the pewter light, into the note, and you find the lattices of green laid over the black glass, and nothing else moves. Nothing flies. Nothing crosses the bare humming slopes. There are no tracks in the frost rime, no droppings in the husk-stubble, no burrows under the ring walls. The plants quiver on their node lines, turn their broad fronds toward the loudest stone, burst their seed-heads in silent glittering sequence, and creep a hand's width in a lifetime. They are the residents, and the ring walls and screens and carved slabs belong to someone long gone.

It takes days to begin doubting this, and the doubt starts with the fronds.

## The Fronds That Turn the Wrong Way

The fronds face the loudest rock. They do this reliably. On any slope you can read the loudness of the surrounding stone by the angle of the leaves, the way you read wind by grass. The pattern is steady because the note is steady.

But sometimes, on a node line in the middle of the afternoon, a patch of fronds swings away all at once. It happens across a stretch about the length of a body and a little wider. The leaves turn as though something loud had been switched off on one side of them, and then the patch of turning moves. It travels along the line at a walking pace, or slower, sometimes pausing, and the fronds fall back into their usual angle behind it. If you are standing near its path when it reaches you, you feel it before you understand it: the note drops. It does not stop. It falls from total to merely enormous, and for a few heartbeats you can feel your own shout in your throat and very faintly, impossibly, in your ears as well.

Then it passes. The roar closes over you again.

The high thin harmonic that drifts in for an hour and fades, the one that sounds almost like a melody, is the sound of one of these going by at a distance. It is the frayed edge of it, the place where it interferes with the chord around it. When you learn this you understand that you have been hearing them since you arrived.

## The Lulls

Wherever overtones nearly align and don't quite, they beat against each other, and the note swells and sags. Mostly the beats sweep across the slopes and vanish, meaning nothing, the way ripples cross a pond. But in a chord this large, sustained this long, the beats occasionally catch on themselves. A place where two overtones cancel reinforces the conditions that made it, and the cancellation holds together. It persists and moves off under its own logic.

These are the lulls. They are travelling pockets of lesser sound, bodies made of absence.

They come in all sizes. Some are no bigger than a fist, and they skitter along the edges of the node lines and are gone in minutes, leaving a small flinch in the fronds behind them. Some are the size of a person. A few, very rarely, are enormous, and when one of those crosses the basin the whole lattice on a slope swings over together, like a field of grain parting before something that is not there, and every seed-head of three or four species bursts at once in the sudden change of pressure. You see the puffs of glitter chase the thing across the mountainside. You feel the great slackening of the note in your chest, like a held breath let out. Then it goes over the rim and the chord closes behind it.

The small ones are like weather, and they think nothing. The middle-sized ones are not quite weather. They linger at the crossings of node lines, where the stone is stillest. They drift back and forth over the same stretch of lattice for days. They seem to prefer the quiet, which makes sense, since quiet is what they are made of, and a crossing is the one place where the mountain's roar is thin enough that a lull does not have to work to hold itself together.

A lull that is not working to hold itself together has room left over. That is how the rest begins.

## The Settled

Every so often a lull comes to rest.

It finds a crossing where three node lines meet and the stone is deeply still, and it stops wandering. It stays there for a season, then a century. And the half-made matter that fills the sky, the glittering dust of not-yet-rock that has not decided where to settle, begins to decide.

It falls wherever things are still. This is how the unfinished strata fill in, grain by grain out of the haze, and it is what happens to a resting lull. The dust comes down onto the boundary of the lull, the precise surface where its silence meets the mountain's roar, and it stays there. Slowly, over many human lifetimes, a skin forms. It is the same dark glass as the mountain, grey-black with the green fog suspended in it, and it takes the exact shape of the silence inside.

The shape is roughly upright, taller than it is wide, many-faceted, the way everything here is faceted. As the skin thickens, two long planes on either side of the body separate slowly from the mass, the way a ledge separates from a cliff, and become something like arms. At the ends of the arms the glass splits into fingers, though not always five. It might be four, or six, or seven. The skin follows whatever the silence was shaped like, and no two lulls are shaped alike. The crude outlines of hands scratched low on the walls of the Silence House are their hands, pressed flat against the stone and traced around with a blade. Look at them again and you will see that no two outlines have the same count of fingers or the same set of joints. You were looking at a roll call.

Once it is sealed inside its skin, the lull wakes.

This is the nearest anyone could come to explaining it. A silence in the open air has no edges, only gradients. It fades out into the roar in all directions, and nothing about it can be said to know itself. A silence inside a shell of glass has a boundary. It has a surface it can press against from the inside. It can feel the whole mountain singing on the outside of its skin, every overtone, the grinding sour chord, the slow throb of the fundamental arriving every few seconds like a tide, and none of it gets in. Inside there is only quiet with walls around it. Something that is a quiet with walls, and that can feel what is outside the walls, is already very close to being someone.

These are the Makers. They were not animals. There have never been animals here, and there are none now. They are closer to rooms than to creatures. Each of them is a small Silence House that walked.

## How They Held Themselves Together

Their skins ring. Tap one with a knuckle and you feel it answer at one particular pitch, the pitch at which the silence inside is exactly opposed to the mountain. That pitch is their name, as nearly as they had one. It was also their weakness.

A crack in the skin lets the mountain in. A settled one with a crack has the note inside it: first a thread of sound, then a whine, then, if the crack widens, the whole chord pouring into the space where the self was. The hearths explain this. There was nothing here to cook and no cold fierce enough to need a fire. But the amber sap of the node-plants, the slow paste that creeps instead of flowing, will run if it is warmed. The faint blackening on the hearth stones is the mark of small fires of dry husk. Beside them the Makers sat and softened sap on the flat of a knapped blade and pressed it into their own cracks. They sealed themselves. They mended the walls of their silence with the mountain's slow green blood.

The tools make more sense now. Some of the scrapers were for harvesting fiber and some for carving slabs, but the finest blades, the ones with edges thinner than anything ever ground, were for scraping sap and working it into fractures no wider than a hair. The black glass the tools are knapped from is the same black glass the Makers were made of, and some of the scatters may be pieces of themselves.

The screens make more sense too. To cross bare rock between node lines, where the stone hums too hard for anything to root, was to walk through a torrent of sound that beat on the skin from every side and searched for any flaw. They crossed rarely. When they did, they carried plaited screens and set them down facing the loudest rock, and rested behind them in pockets where the roar was merely enormous. A screen propped against a boulder is a place where one of them stopped to recover on a journey. Some of the boulders it is propped against are not boulders.

## How They Spoke

They had no voices. A voice is a way of making sound, and they were made of its opposite. Speech here was never possible, and they never missed it.

They spoke through the stone. One settled one would strike two slabs together near a node, where the ground was quiet enough to carry a small signal, and the ring would run out through the glass of the mountain and be felt, through the feet and the palms, by another one standing on the same lattice a long way off. The intervals carried the meaning: the gaps between strikes, the pitch of the slabs against the ambient chord, whether a strike landed on a swell of the beat or in its trough. A green line of node-forest was a road, and it was also a wire. The whole lattice hummed faintly with messages no one else could feel.

For closer speech they touched. When two settled ones pressed their palms together, skin to skin, their silences met through the glass, and for as long as they held on the two quiets were very nearly one quiet. Nothing crossed between them that you could call information. What crossed was more like proof: here is another edge, and inside it, another stillness like mine. That was the deepest thing they could say, and they seem to have said it as often as they could.

The carved slabs were the other kind of speech, the kind addressed to whoever came later. They are maps of quiet because quiet was the only territory that mattered. A Maker who could no longer find a crossing would fill with sound and end. So each generation carved where the still lines ran and left the slabs for the next, and each generation found the slabs a little wrong and carved new ones.

## How They Ended

The lattice moves a hand's width in a lifetime, measured in lives like yours. Theirs were far longer. A settled one might hold its crossing for a thousand years, but the crossing drifts, and eventually the stillness slides out from under it. It can follow, for a while. It can shift its ring walls and move its hearth, and the stranded walls sitting a little off the living green show where they tried. But the lines do not always drift together, and three lines meeting in one place is a rare and temporary thing.

When the crossing is finally gone, the settled one fills. It is not violent. The skin does not break. The mountain simply comes in through the stone, slowly, the way the high whine now leaks into the center of the Silence House floor. The quiet inside grows thinner and more crowded until there is no inside left, only a shell of glass singing at the same pitch as everything else. What remains is a boulder that rings at one particular note when you tap it.

The ones who were left would find these shells. They would test them with their knuckles, carry them to the spur on the east side of the basin where three node lines crossed, and fit them into the wall. Each shell was placed so that its old pitch, the pitch at which its silence had once opposed the mountain, still worked against the note. Each stone in the house was chosen and shaped, and each was someone. The house is corbelled inward from the dead, course on course, so that their remaining opposition to the note adds up to one complete room of quiet.

When you duck through the low doorway and the note stops, you are standing inside all of them at once.

The tallies scratched low on the walls are visits: the living coming to sit among the dead and add a mark. The hand outlines are those who asked, before they filled, to be placed in the wall, and pressed their palms to the spot. The weeping of the people who come here now is something the Makers never did and could not have done. The builders heard no heartbeat in there, because they had none. What you hear in the one quiet room on the mountain is the first pulse that room has ever held.

## The Fraction

That fraction of a tone matters, and here is why.

Every settled one is a permanent cancellation. It is a small, specific silence, opposed to one particular strand of the chord and held fixed in one place for a thousand years. The note of the mountain is the sum of all its sound less all its silences. Each Maker that settled took something out of that sum, and it was never the same something twice, because each silence had its own shape and its own pitch.

Over the long ages this adds up. The chord sags, very slightly, toward the pitches that are missing from it. The note drifts by its hair's breadth each century, and the node lines slide across the slopes, and the forests follow them, trailing ghost lattices of copper stubble behind.

The mountain changing its mind is them.

It is also a trap, and they must have known it. Every new settled one shifts the nodes. Every new silence moves the quiet out from under the silences already there. A population of them cannot grow without unhousing itself. The more of them there are, the faster the crossings drift and the sooner each one fills. The oldest slabs, from when the mountain was lower, show lattices dense with crossings and ring walls clustered close together, and then, layer by layer through time, fewer. The traces are scattered and the distances between them are long because that was the lesson. They learned to be few. They learned to leave room. The emptiness of the range is partly their doing, and it is deliberate. It is a kind of care.

## Upward

None of the traces are recent, and there are almost no settled ones left in the basin. The few that remain sit still enough and dark enough that you pass them for days before a frond turns the wrong way beside one. So where did the rest go?

The answer is in the echoes.

Strike two slabs together near a node, and feel the answer come back through your hands, sometimes from a cliff that is there and sometimes from the outline of one that is not. Most of the time it is only an echo, faint and a little late. But strike in the old way, in intervals, a pattern with a pause in it, and wait. Sometimes what comes back is not your own pattern. It is a different pattern, an answer, and it comes from the empty violet air where a ridge is still only a refraction and a wrong shadow.

The peaks are the places where the note becomes too high for rock to hold and matter gives up and becomes haze. For a silence, that is freedom. Up there, nothing is decided. The rock has not arrived yet. A lull does not need a skin up there, and it does not need a crossing, because the not-yet-stone has no nodes yet, only intentions. The Makers climbed. They climbed the way you climb here, into pitch as much as distance, until the mountain was rising beneath them as fast as they could go, and then they kept going, past the place where the mountain could keep up.

Some of the spires that are still mostly idea are being drawn around them. A few of the long blue shadows lying across the snowless slopes fall from nothing that exists yet, and they fall in the shape of something tall and many-faceted, with long planes on either side that might someday separate into arms. The unfinished range is unfinished partly because something is living inside the unfinished part, and it is not in a hurry.

A few of the carved slabs in the basin match no lattice at all, not the living one and not any of the ghosts. They are maps of quiet, carved in the old way. The obvious guess is that they are too old to compare. Another possibility is that they were carved up there and carried down, or tapped down through the stone and recorded by the last of the ones below, and that they show where the quiet will be.

## What It Is, Finally

Stand on the rim of the basin and feel the whole region: the local mountain's roar laid over the fainter fundamentals of the far peaks, each forming at its own rate, each carrying its own lattice.

Now think about what a settled one is. It is a silence that came to rest. Dust fell onto its boundary grain by grain, and the dust became glass in exactly its shape. Its skin rings on the outside and it is still on the inside. At the places where its skin is thinnest, the quiet within nearly reaches the surface.

Then think about the range. It is a skin of dark glass, still being laid down grain by grain out of the haze. It rings on its outer surface with a note that has no source and has never stopped. Across that surface run lines where the ringing cancels and holds still, and at one spur on the east side, where three of those lines cross, a room exists where the note stops all at once, as if a hand had closed over the world. Its upper reaches are unfinished and still working out their own shape. Its oldest strata record a chord that has been shifting, fraction by fraction, toward something.

Nothing struck this mountain. The note is not what a struck thing makes. It is what an edge makes: the ringing at the boundary where a very great silence meets everything that is not silent. The note was never the mountain's voice. It is the sound of the mountain's skin. The range is a shell accreting around a stillness too large to feel directly, far below the fundamental, beneath the throb in your gut that comes once every few seconds and might be the slow tide of the mountain holding itself together, and might be something like a pulse. The peaks are its crown, still forming. The Makers were small quiets, cast in the same way at a smaller size, living on its surface and moving it by a hair's breadth with their own absences. Perhaps they learned their shape from it. Perhaps it is learning its shape from them. Perhaps the ones who climbed into the haze went looking for the inside.

Whatever lives in the Unfinished Range lives there the way a silence lives in a bell. Almost all of it is still. The part you can touch is the sharp black edge of it, singing.

It is not finished. Nothing here has had time. The rain that would soften these edges has not come. The skin is still closing, grain by grain, century by century, around something that has not yet fully arrived. Meanwhile, on the east spur, among the fitted shells of the dead, a small room lets you step for a moment through the ringing and into the quiet it surrounds. When you do, there is a heartbeat in there, your own, the only rhythm in the whole range that is not the note. The walls around you were built by beings who never had one, and they are being worn slowly back into song. They seem to be listening.
