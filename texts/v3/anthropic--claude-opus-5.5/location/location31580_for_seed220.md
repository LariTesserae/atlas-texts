# The Sounding Hollow

## Approach

You come down through a slanting shaft whose walls are too smooth to be natural and too softened to be recent. The steps have worn into shallow dishes, each holding a skin of water no deeper than a fingernail. The air thickens and warms as you descend. By the bottom it sits on your skin like breath from a large animal.

Then the passage opens, and the dark gets bigger. You can hear the space before you can see anything. Your footsteps stop coming back to you. Something vast is humming just below the threshold of hearing, a pressure you feel in your sternum more than your ears.

## The Shape of It

The Hollow is a chamber roughly the size of a great cathedral: two hundred paces across, perhaps ninety tall at its crown. It is shaped like the inside of an egg set slightly on its side. A city is built into the curve of its walls.

The city does not sit on the floor. It clings to the whole inner surface. Terraces are stacked on terraces. Doorways open onto nothing. Narrow stairs climb the curve, and balconies overhang one another in tiers. At a guess there are two or three thousand rooms, all small and all facing inward toward the empty center, like seats in a theater built for the void. Five thin bridges cross that center at different heights, strung between opposite walls. None of them touches the others. There is no floor to speak of, only a shallow basin at the bottom where the walls converge. In the basin is a single round opening, black and warm, about as wide as a house.

It is a city at hamlet scale, intensely built, and completely empty. There is no furniture, no debris, no bones. The rooms are clean in the way riverbeds are clean. Anything loose was shaken out of them a very long time ago.

## Material

The walls and buildings are made of one material, a pale grey-green substance that is neither stone nor metal nor ceramic. It is faintly warm to the touch, and under your palm it trembles continuously, like a cat's throat. It is dense and fine-grained. Where it has broken, it breaks in long curved facets that look almost like the gills of a mushroom.

Everywhere, flowstone has grown over it. Calcite drips have built up from the damp over ages, pale gold and cream. They have rounded every lintel, thickened every balustrade, and turned stair edges into soft lips. On the lower terraces, whole façades have been half-swallowed, so that windows peer out of what looks like a frozen waterfall. The city is dissolving into the cave very slowly, and the cave is growing into the city. The two have been doing this long enough that the boundary between them has become its own kind of architecture.

## The Breath

The Hollow breathes. This is the fact that everything else follows from.

Every forty minutes or so, warm air rises from the opening in the basin. It begins as a faint exhalation you can feel on your face. Over several minutes it builds into a steady, humid wind flowing upward through the chamber. As it passes, the city begins to *sound*.

Every building is a pipe. Every room is a resonating cavity, every doorway a mouth, every stair a reed. As the rising air crosses thousands of openings, the Hollow produces a tone. It starts as a low grinding murmur, climbs, broadens, and finally locks into a single enormous chord. That chord is so loud it is no longer really a sound. It is a condition of the air. Loose grit leaps on the terraces. Water on the stairs shivers into standing patterns. Your teeth hum and your vision blurs slightly with the vibration of your own eyes. The chord holds for six or seven minutes. It is not unpleasant, only total. Then the upwelling slackens and the note sags, splits into its component tones, and dissolves back into the underhum. Silence, or what counts as silence here, returns for about half an hour.

Whatever drives the breath is somewhere below the basin, out of reach. It is not a fan or a furnace. The air comes up warm, but there is no smell of combustion. The warmth has a strange evenness, as if it were not produced but *delivered*. The pale material of the walls responds to it. During the chord it warms perceptibly, and afterward it cools in slow gradients, bands of warmth creeping upward along the curve. The city is not only an instrument played by the air. It seems to be participating, tuning, correcting. The builders made something that still knows what it is for.

## Water

The water cycle is bound to the breath.

The rising air is saturated. As it passes the upper curve of the chamber and meets cooler material there, it condenses. For a few minutes after each chord, the Hollow rains on itself. It is a fine, warm drizzle falling from the crown, pattering onto the high balconies, running down the stairs, and dripping from terrace to terrace in thin threads. You hear it as a sudden soft hiss after the chord fades, a sound like applause heard from another room.

The water doesn't pool much. It runs down the curve, feeds the flowstone, and drains into a network of small channels cut into the steps, clearly built for exactly this. Everything collects in the basin and vanishes into the warm opening, where presumably it becomes vapor again and returns. Between rains the chamber dries partway. Surfaces go from glistening to matte, and the stair-dishes shrink. It is never quite dry and never truly wet. It is always either about to be damp or just finishing being damp.

## Light, Such as There Is

It is dark. Nothing here was made to be seen by visitors, and whatever lamps the builders had are gone or asleep.

But during the chord, at its loudest, the Hollow flickers.

Droplets caught in the standing waves of the sound are compressed and released thousands of times a second. At the peak of the chord, some of them emit tiny sparks of cold blue light, each lasting a fraction of an instant. Individually they are almost nothing. Across the whole chamber they make a faint, pulsing blue shimmer that traces the invisible geometry of the sound. There are bands and knots of light where the waves reinforce each other, and dark lanes where they cancel. For six minutes out of every forty, you can *see the chord*. It hangs in the empty center of the Hollow like a wire-frame cathedral inside the cathedral, trembling, and then it fades as the note dies.

There is one other light: a very faint dull red deep in the basin opening, visible only when your eyes have fully adjusted. It never changes.

## What Grows

Vegetation is sparse, and it obeys the sound.

A crusted grey-violet lichen grows on the walls, but only in particular places. It follows the nodes of the chord, the places where the standing wave cancels itself and the vibration is weakest. The result is that the lichen grows in lines. They are long, graceful, curving bands across terraces and façades, stair to stair, crossing buildings without regard for their shapes. From the center of a bridge, in the moments when the blue light shows, you can see that the lichen bands and the dark lanes in the shimmering chord are the same pattern. The lichen is a map of silence, painted onto the city by the city's own voice.

In the wettest crevices of the lower terraces grows a pale, almost translucent moss. It lives on mineral water and heat alone and forms small cushions no bigger than a hand. It grows only on the upward faces of drip-stones, where each rain feeds it, and it pulses faintly with the chord. It is not glowing. It is visibly quivering, like jelly.

That is nearly all.

## What Lives

Animals are rare, and each one has found its own compromise with the noise.

**Drip snails.** These are small, flat, and white. They live along the drainage channels and graze on the mineral film and the edges of the moss. They move almost only during the rain and are completely still at other times, sealed to the stone. Their shells are thin and ridged, and during the chord they ring faintly, adding a high, glassy shimmer to the upper edge of the tone.

**Node crickets.** These are pale, long-legged, and eyeless, found on the lichen bands and nowhere else. They sing only in the silence between chords, a sparse ticking conversation from one terrace to another. When the breath begins they fall quiet and press themselves flat into the nodal lines, where the vibration is least. Crickets that stray off the lines during the chord can sometimes be found afterward, stunned or dead, on the stairs below. The lichen bands are the only safe roads, and the crickets live their whole lives along them.

**The listener.** There is one larger creature, rarely seen. It is a leathery, gliding thing about the span of an outstretched arm, with enormous ears folded like petals. It does not echolocate. In a place this loud, its own cries would drown. Instead it navigates by listening to the city. Every room, doorway, and bridge colors the chord in its own way, and the listener seems to know the Hollow as a structure of sound. It flies only during the chord, sliding through the blue-lit geometry. It hunts crickets unlucky enough to be caught off the lines, and it roosts in the silent periods somewhere high near the crown. It may be the only living thing that experiences the Hollow as its builders intended.

## Rules of the Place

- **The breath is punctual but not perfect.** The interval between chords drifts by a few minutes over the course of what seems to be a day. Over longer spans, perhaps seasons measured by some surface world far above, it lengthens and shortens.
- **Sound shapes matter.** Grit migrates to the nodes. Water beads in the antinodes. Flowstone grows fastest where vibration is least, so the lines of lichen are slowly being overgrown with calcite ridges. Over centuries, the voice of the city draws its own pattern into the city's surface.
- **The city is detuning itself.** This is the slowest and strangest process here. As flowstone thickens, it changes the resonant shapes of rooms and doorways. The chord is not the same as it was. Old channels cut into the stairs don't quite match where the water runs now. There are faint ghost-bands of dead lichen parallel to the living ones, a few hands' widths off, where the silences used to fall. The pitch of the Hollow has been creeping downward for longer than anything else here has existed. The crickets follow the moving silences. The lichen follows them more slowly. The listener, presumably, relearns the map as it goes.
- **The walls correct, but not fast enough.** The pale material shifts its warmth. It seems to adjust, and some flowstone near the warmest bands is visibly thinner, as if gently dissolved. The builders' instrument is still trying to stay in tune against the cave growing through it. It is losing, but very gracefully.

## Standing on the Middle Bridge

The middle bridge is the best place to understand the Hollow. It is narrow, railless, and worn to a shallow trough by something (feet, or wind, or time), and it is exactly at the center of the egg.

In the silent period you stand in near-total dark and warm, still air. There is the faint red glow far below, and the ticking of crickets coming from all around you on invisible lines. Water drips somewhere. The underhum sits in your chest.

Then you feel the air begin to move upward past your face. The crickets stop. The city draws in its breath around you and begins, slowly, to sing. The blue lattice kindles in the dark on every side, and you are standing inside the chord, at the one place in the whole chamber the builders surely stood. It is louder than anything you have heard. Everything trembles. A shape with petal ears slides through the light and is gone.

Then the note dies. Warm rain begins to fall on you out of the dark.
