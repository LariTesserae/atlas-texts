# The Suture Room

## The Approach

The range is high and dry, a spine of pale granodiorite far above any treeline, though no trees ever grew here to have a line. The air is thin enough that the sky stays a bruised violet at midday and the sun looks small and far off. Nothing moves except grit. Frost shatters the rock and wind carries the fragments away. The tors are rounded like old knuckles, and around their feet lies grus, the coarse sugar that granite becomes after ten thousand winters of freezing without water. What moisture the air holds is so slight that snow almost never falls. The cold is dry and clean, and it sits in the chest like a held note.

The ridge looks like every ridge around it. You could walk past the place a hundred times.

A few faint marks give it away. Three steps are cut into a slab, their edges so softened that they read as a natural stair. A flat stone stands upright where no flat stone would stand on its own, its face patterned with shallow grooves that might once have been writing, or a diagram, or just the path meltwater would take if there were ever meltwater. Past it is a joint in the rock, a vertical slot barely shoulder-wide, running back into the ridge at an angle that keeps its interior out of the sun.

You turn sideways and go in.

## The Room

The slot widens after a few body-lengths into a chamber about the size of a small chapel. The ceiling is high and irregular, and the floor is mostly level, covered in a fine pale dust that shows no footprints except, eventually, yours.

At first it seems to be a natural void, a pocket the mountain opened for itself where two joints crossed. The walls show the same granite as outside, with the same crystal grain of feldspar, quartz, and black mica flakes. Then your eye follows the wall to the far end of the room, and the stone stops being that stone.

A line runs diagonally across the chamber from the floor near the left wall, up and across the ceiling, and down the right wall almost to the entrance. It is not a crack. On one side is the pale granite of the range. On the other is a dense, dark, faintly glassy rock, grey-black with a lustre like wet slate, though nothing here is wet. The grain of the two stones doesn't agree. The granite's crystals run roughly north. The dark stone's fine banding runs at a steep angle that matches nothing in the ridge, as if it had been laid down under a different gravity.

The two stones don't touch. Between them is a gap no wider than a finger, and when you look into it there is nothing to see. It isn't darkness, which would at least be something. Your eye simply slides off it, the way it slides off the blind spot in your own vision. You find yourself looking at one edge of the gap or the other and never at what lies between.

This is the seam. The room exists around it. Very likely the room is made of it.

## The Sutures

Thirty-one rods cross the seam.

Each is about as thick as a thumb and as long as a forearm, made of a pale material like unglazed porcelain, though it is not ceramic. It doesn't ring when tapped. It gives back a short, dead sound, as if the tap had been absorbed somewhere far away. It isn't warm, yet it never feels as cold as the surrounding rock, and frost will not form on it. The rods pierce straight through both faces of the seam and pin granite to dark stone at intervals of roughly an arm's length. They run in a patient line up one wall, across the ceiling, and down the other, like stitches closing a wound.

That is what they are. Something here once tore, and someone closed it.

Whoever built them left no other tools, no inscription that has survived, and no explanation. The sutures are the explanation. Their engineering doesn't behave like engineering you would recognize. They aren't anchored in any ordinary sense. Nothing drills them in, and no bolt, plate, or thread holds them. They seem instead to insist on something: that the two sides of the seam are one surface, that the distance across the gap is the distance of a finger and no more. The rods hold that claim in place the way a nail holds a board.

They have been holding it for a very long time, and they are wearing out.

In a sealed, windless, waterless chamber, where nothing should erode, the sutures erode. The wear starts where each rod crosses the gap and moves outward, and it is smooth, polished, and slightly concave, the way a step wears under a million feet. Beneath each rod lies a small cone of fine white powder, the only sediment in the room. Seven of the thirty-one have failed. Four have snapped cleanly at the gap, leaving a stub in each wall. Two have worn so thin at their centres that they look like hourglasses. The last failed rod is stranger. It seems to have been drawn partly into the seam. Its length on the granite side is a hand's breadth longer than its length on the dark side. Yet it is straight, it hasn't moved, and if you lay a finger along it you feel it continuous from end to end. The missing length is not missing. It is somewhere the room has no way of showing you.

## The Disc

Set flush into the floor near the centre of the room, under the highest point of the seam's arc, is a disc about the width of an outspread hand. It is the same pale material as the sutures, and it is inscribed with concentric grooves, dozens of them, each finer than the last, until the innermost are too fine to see and can only be felt as a softness under the fingertip.

The disc turns.

You would never see it move. You can tell only from the dust. Dust drifts into the grooves and lies there, and on one side of the disc there is always a thin crescent of floor swept cleaner than the rest, as though the edge had pushed it aside over some enormous interval. By the width of the crescent and the depth of the dust, the disc turns once every several centuries. Nothing indicates whether this is a clock, a regulator, a counter, or the slow tightening of a single long thread. It could be all of these. Whatever it measures, it is patient about measuring it.

## Light

The only light that comes in through the slot is reflected: a cold blue-grey carried off the rock outside, dwindling to almost nothing after the first turn. By rights the chamber should be black. It isn't. A dim, even, pewter-coloured light fills it, strong enough to see by if you let your eyes open wide and wait.

It has no source. It isn't brighter near the entrance, and it doesn't come from the stone. It is simply present, the way cold is present.

Stand still long enough and you notice the shadows. Every object in the room casts one shadow, and every shadow points toward the seam. This is true of each suture stub, each cone of powder, the upright edges of your boots, and your own hand held out flat. Each shadow falls toward the gap. It is as if the light came softly from all the walls at once and drained away into that line, and the seam were a place light leaves from rather than arrives at. A lamp brought in will throw its own shadows correctly. But at the edges of the lamplight, where its beam weakens, the shadows begin to bend and turn and point back toward the seam.

## Sound

The silence is the silence of high stone: no insects, no water, no wind in the slot, no settling or creaking. The rock holds perfectly still. Your own blood becomes loud.

Two sounds belong to the room itself.

The first is a tick. When the far-off day outside cools past some threshold, one of the sutures makes a small dry click, never more than one and never the same one twice in a row. You may wait an hour for it. It is less a sound than a punctuation mark placed on the silence.

The second comes back to you from the seam, and it is not an echo. Take a step, and the sound of the step returns a moment later from the direction of the gap. It is not the sound you made. It is a footstep on snow, the soft compressed squeak of it, the crunch of a crust giving way. Breathe out hard and the breath returns without the rasp that thin, dry air puts in your throat. It comes back full and wet, the breath of someone standing somewhere with moisture in the air. Speak, and what returns has the shape and length of your words, the rhythm of them, but the vowels are rounder and the consonants blurred, as though heard through a closed door or through falling snow.

Whatever is on the other side of the seam, it isn't here, and it has weather.

## Frost

The only water in the room is frost, and it forms only on the dark side of the seam.

It is rime, the feathery kind that grows into the wind, and it has built up on the dark stone in long combed fringes that all point the same way. They point up and slightly to the left, toward a wind that would have to blow down through the ceiling, where there is no opening, no current, and no air moving at all. The frost records the direction of a wind that doesn't blow in this room. It is weather from the other place, seeping through at a rate of a few crystals per year, the only moisture anywhere on the ridge.

It moves. It never melts, because it is always too cold for that, but it sublimates on its forward edges and redeposits on its trailing ones, and so the fringes creep across the dark stone. Mark an edge of frost with a scratch in the dust below it and return a decade later, and it will have crept a finger's width along the seam. Over the long history of the room it has travelled perhaps a third of the way from the floor to the peak of the ceiling. Where it has already passed, the dark stone is faintly etched, as if the frost had been reading it.

## Callus

Along the seam, in the gaps between the sutures, something is growing.

It isn't alive. Nothing here is alive and nothing ever has been. Still, *growing* is the word the eye reaches for. Tiny colourless crystals, clear as glass and each smaller than a grain of salt, have formed at the lips of the gap on both sides. Where they reach toward each other, a few have met. They make bridges across the finger's width of nothing, fine as frost, perhaps a dozen in the whole length of the seam. Where a bridge has formed, the gap is simply gone. The granite and the dark stone meet there without a join, as though they had always been one surface and one of them had merely changed its mind about what it was made of.

The crystals add a facet roughly once each cold season. A facet is a very small thing.

This is the slow process of the room. It is not decay. It is convalescence. The seam is healing. The world is laying down scar tissue across the tear, one crystal plane at a time, and each bridge takes a small share of the strain the sutures have carried alone. The failed sutures failed where no bridges had formed yet. The strongest ones stand beside the thickest crystals. There is an obvious conclusion: the builders never meant to close the wound. They meant to hold it long enough for it to close itself, and the room, over a span of time that makes the ridge outside seem young, is doing exactly that.

## The Rules

Some things you learn by staying.

If you pace the room from the entrance to the far wall, it is eleven paces. Pace back and it is nine. This has been measured with care, with ropes, and by different people, and the difference is always the same. It is not an illusion. It is a fact about the room, as stable as its temperature. The two figures are slowly converging, perhaps by the width of a crystal each century. One day the room will be a single length. No one knows which.

Nothing thrown across the seam lands. A pebble tossed from the granite side toward the dark wall simply is not there after it passes the line. You don't see it vanish, because your eye slides past that moment the same way it slides past the gap, and afterward you hear, very faintly, a small impact in snow.

After enough time inside, you lose track of which side of the seam you entered on. This shouldn't be possible. The seam runs from wall to ceiling to wall, the whole room lies on one side of it, and the entrance slot is plainly in granite. Yet somewhere in the second or third hour, standing among the pointing shadows, you find yourself unsure whether the pale stone or the dark stone is the local one, and whether the frost is seeping in or seeping out. You grow unsure whether the footsteps on snow are the echo or you are.

The disc turns. The rime creeps. A suture ticks once in the cold.

## What It Is

It is a small place, a pocket in a ridge that looks like every other ridge, reached through a slot that most eyes would slide past just as they slide past the gap inside. In a sense it is barely a landscape at all: one room, one seam, thirty-one stitches, and a handful of dust.

It is also the site of a wound in the world, older than the weathering of the tors, closed by makers who left nothing behind except the closing. It is dry on one side and snowy on the other, and silent except for the sounds that come back changed. It is cold on both sides, the dark side colder. It isn't a ruin, because nothing here is falling apart that isn't meant to. It isn't a monument either, because it is still at work.

It is a place holding still while it heals, and it is very patient. Outside, the frost goes on breaking the granite into grit. Inside, facet by facet, the crystals are knitting two different places into one.
