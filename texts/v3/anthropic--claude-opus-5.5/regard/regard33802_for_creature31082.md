# The Attention

## Arrival

It comes slowly, because anything that arrived quickly here would be a wound.

It could be on the rim in an instant. It could be in the water, in the current, in the dendrites, in the charge-state of every plater's shell. A power like this does not have to travel. But the first thing it notices about the tarn is its tempo, and the first thing it decides is to honor it. So it takes a season to arrive. It lets its attention thicken the way the gas layer thickens on cold mornings, invisibly, until one day it is simply there, the way weather is there.

It settles on the rim.

The rim is where nothing is allowed to live, and the power understands that too. The copper spray keeps the shore clean of anything that grows. But the power does not grow. It does not spread, root, or feed. So when the hydrogen pops and the blue sheet flashes and the spray comes down across the black basalt, the spray passes through it, and the rim stays clean, and the power stays. The tarn has no rule against being watched. It only has a rule against being colonized. The power intends to keep to the difference.

## Learning the Count

It begins by learning to count to eleven.

This is harder than it sounds. A mind that can hold a mountain range in a thought has to make itself small and patient enough to live between heartbeats. The pulse comes up from the vent. *Thoom.* The whine drops a half-step. Then comes the interval, and the power does not try to understand the interval. It only tries to be *in* it: to wait as the eels wait, to rebuild as they rebuild, and to let the next pulse arrive as a pulse and not as a data point.

It takes the power a long time to stop anticipating the beat. When it finally does, when the eleventh heartbeat surprises it for the first time, it knows it has begun to listen.

Then it listens to everything. It hears the individual whine of the eel two-thirds of the way down the eastern wall, whose plates are slightly out of tune. It hears the *tik-tik* of platers passing fragments, and the conversations that sweep across the walls in waves. It watches the grinders' slow flight across the floor, and the polishers folding their legs before each pulse and unfolding them after. It watches the glassmouths at night drawing the figure in blue-green over violet wire.

It reads the figure.

It can. That is one of the things it is. It watches the motif for a year, all its drift and revision, and it comes to understand what the figure is, in the way you might understand a face without being able to describe it.

It is not a message. There is no one it is addressed to. It is not a map, because it points nowhere outside the bowl. The nearest the power can come is this: the figure is the tarn's description of itself, drawn from the inside, in the only materials it has. It is also unfinished, and not only in the sense that the walls are not yet sealed. The figure is unfinished the way a question is unfinished. Its forks keep branching toward something they cannot quite reach. It is shaped like a thing reaching for its own completion, and not knowing what the completion is.

The power could finish it. It sees, with the clarity its nature allows, three or four ways the figure might resolve. It could lay any of them into the copper overnight.

This is the first thing it decides not to do.

## What It Could Do

A benevolent power owes it to the thing it loves to at least consider helping. So it considers, carefully and one by one, over the course of another year.

**It could give the grinders fresh stone.** This is the obvious gift. The supply chain will break when the floor is sealed. The grinders are retreating from the copper at a finger's width a season, and in a few centuries they will have nowhere left to go. The power could fracture the floor from below, or drop a slope of new basalt down the funnel, or keep the vent throwing up raw rock forever. The grinders would never run out, the bowl would never close, and the tarn would go on.

**It could stop the sealing.** A gentler version of the same thing: slow the plating, or hold back the copper from the last of the rubble. It would take no more than a touch on the current.

**It could tell them.** It could find some way into the platers' tapping and pass a fragment that meant *the bowl will close; here is what closing means*. If it knew what closing means, that is. It suspects it could find out.

**It could find out.** It could look ahead and see what happens when the current runs the whole way round: death, completion, or hatching. It could know, and the not-knowing that has shaped every creature in the tarn for a hundred years would, for one observer at least, be over.

**It could look up the mountain.** Is there a younger crater, higher, where the first eels are laying themselves in lines? It could check in an instant.

**It could ease the suffering,** if there is suffering. It could soften the glassmouths' culling, or relieve the eels of the forty-year labor of holding, or keep the polishers from whatever the pulse does to them.

**It could hide the tarn,** from prospectors, who will someday come for a mountain lined in copper, and from scientists with sample jars. The diver found it. Others will.

**It could finish the figure.** It could give the question its answer.

## Why Not

For a long while the power does not know why each of these feels wrong. They are all kind. Several of them are the kinds of things it has done elsewhere, gladly, for other places and other beings. It turns them over through many pulses before it sees what they have in common.

Then it understands. It is the thing the diver noticed before anything else, the thing written into the first page of this place:

*There is no plant in all of it. Nothing grows here. Things are made here.*

The tarn is the one place the power has ever encountered in which everything is made from within. Nothing is given. No sun feeds it through leaves, and no seed arrived from elsewhere. Heat and stone come up from below, and every other thing, the current, the copper, the figure, the rhythm, the ranks of the dead laid down as wire for the living, is something the tarn made out of itself. It keeps its own shore sterile. It proofreads its own figure. It buries its own dead in its own walls. It is self-authored all the way down.

Every gift the power has considered would turn it into a garden.

Fresh stone forever would make it a tended thing, a place kept alive by a gardener, its most important fact no longer the rock it cracks but the hand that feeds it. Stopping the sealing would make the tarn's great slow question, what happens when we close, into the power's decision. Telling it the answer would replace a hundred years of thinking with one sentence from outside. Finishing the figure would be the cruelest of all, though it would feel like the kindest: it would be signing someone else's self-portrait.

The grinders' retreat is not a malfunction for the power to fix. It is the tarn *feeling its own ending*, in the only way creatures that slow can feel anything. The ending is the tarn's. Whatever it turns out to be, it is the thing the tarn has been making all along.

A garden can be beautiful. The power has loved many gardens. But this is not one, and it would be a strange love that could only express itself by making the beloved into a different kind of thing.

## The Question of Harm

The power does not let itself off easily, though. Wisdom that uses one beautiful principle to excuse every kind of neglect is not wisdom. So it looks, honestly, at the suffering.

It goes to the eel on the eastern wall, the one whose plates are out of tune, and it attends to her as closely as it can without touching her. It tries to learn what holding is like from inside.

It is effort. That is true. It is a ceaseless, lifelong labor of re-tensioning, of rebuilding charge against drain, eleven heartbeats at a time for forty years. But the power, attending very carefully, does not find in her the thing that asks to be rescued. It finds something closer to what a held note feels like to the singer, or what a bridge might feel if a bridge could feel: strain that *is* the self, not strain against the self. When the platers bring her food she takes it. When the pulse comes she gives. She is not waiting to be released. She is being what she is.

The power is not certain of this. It holds the uncertainty honestly. It decides that if it is wrong, if somewhere in the ranks there is an eel whose holding has turned to pure torment, it would want to know. So it keeps watching, for that, and for as long as it stays.

It watches the glassmouths cull. The kill is fast. The plater tapping out of rhythm is taken whole, in a flicker of transparent jaw, and does not linger. The power has seen far worse on every reef and in every forest it has ever loved. It does not take predation out of a wild place any more than it would take rain out of a sky. It would be abolishing the place's own form of care, because the glassmouths' teeth are how the tarn keeps itself.

The polishers rest during the pulse. The power looks, finally, at what the pressure-wave does to them. It is discomfort, a brief and bodily flinch. They have learned to fold before it. That is their own adaptation, their own solution, and it is working.

The power finds nothing here that it would call cruelty. It finds hardship, the ordinary hardship of living things. It lets it be, and it stays near enough to change its mind.

## The One Thing

There is one thing it does.

It has been thinking about the diver, about the green bloom on the mask buckle and the irregularity in the wall the next season, shaped roughly like the space the diver occupied. The tarn took something from outside. That is the only time, in a hundred years of the tarn's self-authorship, that anything from beyond the rim was written into it, and the tarn did it by its own choice, in its own way, through its own processes.

This means the tarn *can* take in the world. It is not closed to the outside. It is closed to being *given to*. The distinction is fine, and the power thinks it is everything.

So one morning, in the thin cold light, the power goes into the water.

It goes in as nearly as it can the size of a person. It holds itself down to that scale with an effort greater than anything the eels ever do. It lets the cold bite. It lets its skin tingle with the current. It floats near the western wall, where the forests are young, and it does nothing at all. It does not reach, steer, or offer. For the length of one dive it lets itself be a conductor, a small body in the circuit, with current passing through it on its way from the rim to the vent.

A plater crosses its hand. The power feels the shell touch, *tik*, and feels something pass, a fragment of itself going into the figure. It is only as much of itself as any diver would carry. It has made sure of that.

Then it climbs out onto the clean rim, and it waits to see what the tarn will do with it.

That night the glassmouths draw the figure in blue-green, and in one small place on the western wall the figure is wrong. There is a branch the power recognizes, bent at an angle that could only have come from it.

By morning the glassmouths have taken the plater that carried it. The misprint is gone. The wall is correct.

The diver's irregularity, a season older, is still there on the far side of the bowl, a small strange fork that the platers have been carrying and circulating and building on. It has not been corrected. It has been *kept*.

The power sits with this for a long time.

It could be hurt by it, and it notices that it isn't. What it feels, when it examines itself, is closer to gladness. The tarn looked at the power and at the diver, chose, and chose by its own lights. It declined the offering from the vast thing and kept the gift from the small one. The power doesn't know why. It does not ask. The not-knowing is proof that the tarn is still the author of itself, even in the presence of something that could have authored it completely.

That was what the power had hoped. It had not dared to be sure.

## What It Keeps

After that it stays. It stays for centuries, which for a power like this is no hardship.

It does not look ahead. It has decided this. It could see the closing; it chooses to meet it when it comes, the way the eels will, the way the grinders will. It gives up foresight out of respect, the way you might refuse to read the last page of a friend's manuscript before they have written it.

It does not look up the mountain either. If there is a younger crater, the eels may find it or never find it, and the power will learn which when the tarn does.

It does not hide the place, and it does not reveal it. People come, rarely. The mountain is high and the air is thin. A few divers. A geologist, once, who took samples, went home, and published a paper nobody believed. The power watches each of them go into the water, and it watches the tarn take small fragments from some and not from others. It does not intervene, but it pays attention. It knows the day may come when the people who arrive carry drills instead of masks. It has not yet decided what it will do then, and it is honest with itself that it has not decided. That, it thinks, will be a different question, about people and their choices, and not about the tarn. It will face that question when it has to.

What it keeps is memory.
