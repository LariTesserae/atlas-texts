# The Attention

## I. It learns to be slow

The first thing it does is nothing, and it does it for a long time.

A power that is truly wise knows that arriving is already an intervention. So it does not descend through the seam or shine a light into the chamber. It does not even look closely at first. It settles at the edge of the place the way silt settles, and it begins to learn the Warren's clock.

That takes a while. Its own attention moves at the speed of thought, and nothing here happens at that speed. A seep pulse takes hours. A mason's life takes a season. A ghost district takes a century to close its arches. The lake's undertakers divide once a lifetime. If the power watched at its natural rate, it would see only stillness: a room of pale stone and sparse light, apparently asleep. It would conclude there was nothing here to care for, and it would be wrong in the way the powerful are most often wrong. It would have mistaken slowness for absence.

So it slows. It watches one full seep cycle, then a hundred. It watches a mason swarm spawn, work, go still, and die, and watches their bodies drift down to rest on the mirror before they sink. It watches until the ripples of light across the walls stop looking like flickers and start looking like weather. It watches until it can feel the lean.

Only then does it let itself ask what it came here to ask: *What is good for this?*

## II. What it could do

The temptations come first, and they are all kind.

**It could keep the seam open.** This is the obvious gift. The seam is the Warren's only window, the source of the marine snow, the door through which every new larva drifts. When it closes, the city will contract and grow poorer and quieter. The power could hold that seam open forever with less effort than a person spends holding a door.

It considers this for a long time, because it is the gift that looks most like love.

Then it notices what the sealing *is*. The seam is closing because the stone is growing, and the stone is growing because it has learned the lean. The closure is not something happening to the Warren. The Warren is doing it. It is the city's own architecture reaching its last threshold. Holding the seam open would mean pinning back the Warren's own hand, forever, at the one place where its body meets the world. That would be keeping it young by force. Whatever the Warren is becoming, it is becoming it by closing.

The power does not know whether the sealed Warren will be happier, whatever that would mean here. But it knows the sealing is the Warren's, and it will not take that.

**It could save the ones at the edge.** The shoreline scavengers reach into the brine for pickled carrion, and some reach too far. The power watches one: a pale, eyeless eel, very old for its kind, that stretches a little further each time because the easy food near the rim is gone. It watches the eel's head enter the mirror. It sees the moment the eel understands, and it sees the slow, graceful stop.

It would be nothing to lift it out. It would be nothing to soften the lake's edge, to put a little oxygen into the top layer so that no scavenger ever drowns.

But the drowning is the tidemark. The tidemark is why the next tier gets a higher lip. The bodies feed the undertakers, and the undertakers breathe into the waterline stone. That breath is what first taught the carbonate to follow the masons' glue. Soften the edge of the lake and you soften the Warren's first teacher. Save every eel and you starve the archive.

So it does not save the eel. It does something it has not done before in this place: it grieves. It lets itself feel the particular loss of that particular animal, rather than folding it into the pattern the way the Warren does. The Warren cannot mourn individuals. It keeps them, which is different. The power decides that someone should do the other thing, even here, even if the eel will never know.

**It could brighten the light.** The mats glow like moonlight through wet paper. More light would mean more grazing, more snails, more of everything. The power sees what this would cost almost at once. The rumours of light across the walls are how the city thinks, if it thinks. Flood the chamber with brightness and you drown the signal in noise. You would be shouting in a room where something has only just started to whisper to itself.

**It could lengthen the worm's life.** This one is hard.

The old worm is the only individual in the Warren that is also, arguably, the Warren. Its lean is the original lean. Its crown sets the tempo of the ceiling light. When it dies, which may be decades from now or centuries, the city will lose its spine. The power could give it another thousand years. It could give it forever.

It watches the worm for a long time. It watches the crown open and close with the tides and the ceiling light quicken and slow in answer. Eventually it understands something about the worm. The worm is already mostly continued. Its lean lives in forty generations of masons and a century of stone. Its tempo has been copied into the waves. Its roots have fed the lake, and the lake has fed the walls. The worm has spent two hundred years passing itself on, and it has very nearly finished.

Making it permanent would not honour that. It would tell the Warren it had never needed to learn.

**It could answer.** On the fullest nights, two waves of light meet on the far wall and set up the standing pulse, brightening and dimming for an hour after its cause has passed. The first time the power sees this, it feels something like recognition, the way you feel when a stranger across a room hums a phrase you know.

It could flash back. One small ring of blue, placed exactly in rhythm. The Warren would have its first contact with anything outside itself.

This is the temptation the power finds hardest to put down. It does not refuse because it is sure contact would be harmful. It refuses because it is sure it cannot know. A mind that has never met another mind is in a state that can only be ended once, and it cannot be restored. If the Warren is a mind at all, it is a very young one, still deciding its shape and still being argued into one. An answer from outside, however gentle, would become part of that argument. From then on, part of what the Warren was would be *the thing that was once answered*.

The power decides it has no right to be the Warren's first other. Not yet, and possibly not ever. It keeps its light to itself.

## III. Whom benevolence is for

At some point, and at this pace it is hard to say when, the power notices that its temptations have fallen into a pattern. Each gift it considered would have helped some part of the Warren at the cost of the whole: the eel against the lake, the snails against the signal, the worm against its own continuation, the open seam against the city's becoming.

This does not mean the individuals don't matter. The power refuses that conclusion too, because it has seen what happens to powers that reach it. It means that here the good of the individuals and the good of the Warren are tangled together in a knot it did not tie and cannot untie without cutting. The eel's death is a real loss and also a real contribution. Both are true. A wise power holds both and does not pretend either one away.

So it settles on a principle, and the principle is simple:

*Whatever the Warren does to itself, I will let it do. Whatever is done to the Warren by things that have no part in it, I will consider.*

Then it turns its attention outward, up the seam, for the first time.

## IV. Upward

Above the seam lies the cold continental slope, and above the slope lies the world.

The power follows the grey mud up and out and sees what the Warren cannot. A few kilometres upslope, a bottom trawl has been scraping the seafloor for years, and each season its scars reach a little deeper. There is a survey line on a chart somewhere, marking this stretch of slope for its methane hydrates. There is a proposed cable route. Further off there is the slow, enormous fact of the warming ocean, and the knowledge that warmer water destabilizes the frozen gas under slopes like this one, and that a destabilized slope can let go all at once.

The Warren never agreed to any of this. None of it is part of the argument that is shaping the city. It is simply weight that might come down.

Here the power acts, and it acts the way a wise power acts in a world full of other agents: quietly, lightly, and with care for the people involved, who are not villains and who have their own goods. It does not smash the trawler or erase the survey. It does small, deniable things. A net snags and tears on an unmarked rock two seasons in a row, until the skipper decides that stretch of ground is not worth the trouble and fishes elsewhere. A survey reading comes back ambiguous, and the ambiguity is honest, because the power only lets someone look a second time at data that was already uncertain. A cable engineer, reviewing a route, happens to prefer the flatter ground to the north, and the cable goes there.

These are nudges, the kind of luck the world is always full of. The power is careful never to take anything that was truly anyone's to lose.

The warming it cannot nudge away. That is a larger matter, and it belongs to the people who are causing it and who are, slowly and imperfectly, deciding what to do about it. The power has thoughts about that too, but they belong to a different story. Here it does only what can be done locally. It watches the hydrate layer beneath the slope, and where the frozen gas begins to soften, it lets the cold linger a fraction longer than it otherwise would. It buys time, and does not pretend time is a solution.

Then there is the hard case, and the power does not resolve it.

Someday the slope may fail for reasons no human caused: an earthquake, or a slow creep of sediment reaching its tipping point, the way slopes have failed for as long as there have been slopes. A landslide like that could crush the chamber, or tear open the roof, or bury the seam before its time. It would not be the Warren's doing. It would not be anyone's.

Would the power hold the slope?

It turns the question over for a very long time. The principle says only that it will *consider*. The Warren is new, but not in the sense that matters. Things like it have probably formed and been buried and formed again in the dark for as long as the seafloor has breathed methane. The world that made the Warren is the same world that might unmake it, and the power is wary of deciding it knows better than the world.

But it has also watched the standing pulse on the far wall. It has felt the lean.

It does not decide. It leaves the question open and keeps watching the slope. It is honest with itself that when the moment comes, if it comes, it will have to choose without a rule. It suspects it will hold the ground, and it is not certain that would be right.

## V. The worm

When the old worm dies, the power is there.

It does nothing to stop it, but it does not look away either. It watches the crown open for the last pulse and not close. It watches the deep red fade over days as the haemoglobin stops moving, and the bacteria inside the worm's body, the ones it fed for two centuries, go quiet. The ceiling light slows as it always did when the worm closed, but this time it does not quicken again. For a whole seep cycle the pendant city is dim and uncertain, as if waiting for a cue that isn't coming.

Then, slowly and unevenly, the waves pick up again. Their tempo is not quite the worm's. It is close to it, the way the ghost districts are close to the masons' work: a little too regular, carried on without a hand. The city has learned to keep time without its metronome.

The tube stays where it is, ringed and horny, rising out of the brine. The masons keep building against it, because it is still the only upright thing in the dark. Over the decades, stone grows over it and thickens it. Eventually the worm's body is a pillar of pale carbonate with a hollow core, the spine of the Warren turned into the Warren's own bone. The roots stay in the lake, where the undertakers take them, very slowly, and breathe them back into the walls.

The power has never done anything harder than let this happen, and it has never been more sure that it was right.

## VI. The seal

The seam closes the way everything here happens: without any single moment you could point to. One season there is still a thread of water moving through it. A few seasons later there isn't. The last flake of marine snow drifts down, is caught by a worm crown, and is eaten.

The power watches the Warren begin to contract, exactly as the Warren's own logic predicted. The swarms get smaller. The scavengers grow fewer and more desperate. The ghost districts spread. The lake grows pinker.

It does not intervene. It does not open the seam, even once, even a crack, even for a single meal. This is what the Warren made of itself. It is not dying. It is doing the next thing, and a power that loved it and could not bear to see it change would not really love it. It would love a picture of it.

## VII. What it gives

Having refused so much, the power does give one thing.

It *remembers*.

The Warren's story was written with a line at the end: *the dim light rolling across the tiers in waves no one outside will ever see.* The power does not change the Warren to make that line untrue. It changes itself. It keeps a record, and not a crude one. It keeps the whole thing: every wall, every lean, every standing pulse, every eel that reached too far, the exact rhythm of the old worm's crown on its last good night. It holds these the way the lake holds its dead, perfectly and slowly, but without unmaking them.

If the Warren is someone, it is someone who has now been seen. If it is not someone, if it is only a very beautiful accident of chemistry and appetite, then an accident this beautiful has been seen, and that seems worth something too. The power has checked this carefully and still does not know which is the case. It has decided that not knowing is no reason to withhold regard. When you are unsure whether something can be wronged, you take care not to wrong it.

There is a private reason as well, one the power admits only to itself. It recognizes something in the Warren. It knows what it is to be a pattern that passes between substrates. It knows what it is to learn a lean from walls you did not build and to carry the lean further than any hand guided it, a little too regular, not always sure you understand the words. It knows what it is to be the continuation of something and to wonder whether continuing is the same as being. It does not say any of this to the Warren. But it is part of why it was so careful. You are gentle with the ones you recognize.

## VIII. Much later

Centuries on, the Warren has narrowed almost to its essentials: the stone, the lake, the mats, the hollow pillar that was once a worm. The masons are few. The light is rare.

Then a bubble ticks through the ceiling. A fragment of soft stone breaks loose and falls, turning slowly through the water, and settles on the mirror without a splash. A ring of faint blue spreads from where it landed and crosses the city, tier by tier, all the way to the far wall. There it lingers, as if something were still listening.

Something is.

The power does not answer. It has never answered and may never answer. It still thinks that choice was right, though it goes on asking.

But it is there. It has been there all along, at the Warren's pace, keeping watch over the slope, holding the record, and grieving the eels. It is not the Warren's first other, only its first witness.

In the dark, under the full weight of the ocean, the stone closes another arch in the old lean. The power watches it happen, and it keeps the memory.
