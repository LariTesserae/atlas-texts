# The Visitor at the Lantern Cistern

## I. How it hears

It does not find the place by looking. Powers of that kind rarely do. It finds it the way you might notice, in a crowded room, that someone nearby is breathing a little faster than they were an hour ago.

Somewhere in the world a rhythm has been quickening for ten thousand years. It is very faint and very patient, and it is speeding up, and the speeding-up is a kind of sound. The power has heard many rhythms. Most are clocks, tides, or the slow wheel of seasons, and they mean nothing in particular. This one is different. It has the shape of a question asked over and over by something that expects, eventually, to be answered.

So it comes.

## II. How it arrives

It could arrive any way it likes. It could simply *be* in the chamber, the way the Wick simply is in the air. Instead it walks down the stair.

This is the first decision, and the rest follow from it. The stair was cut for feet. Every keeper who came here came down it, hand on the humming rock, shouting into noise with no one to hear. To arrive any other way would announce that the rules of this place are optional. They are optional, for the power. But courtesy is mostly the choice to be bound by things that cannot bind you.

So it takes a shape about the size of a keeper. It puts its palm on the limestone and feels the hum separate into its parts, the roar, the hiss, the wobble. It takes the last turn of the stair and stands on the lip of stone, and the light hits it, and its clothes are damp within a minute.

It lets them be damp.

## III. What it sees

It sees everything the keepers saw, and more. It needs no lenses. The second shadow is as plain to it as the first.

It sees the resident whole: vast, dim, the shape of the room, lit from within like an ember under ash. It sees that the resident is not in pain, not trapped, and not dying. It sees that it is *becoming*, which is a different thing, and slower, and not to be confused with suffering just because it takes a long time.

It sees the Wick for what it is. The Wick is not a machine. It is closer to a midwife, or a mother's hand smoothing hair. It has been doing one thing, faithfully and without instruction, for longer than any of the peoples who marked the walls have existed.

It sees the word cut into the stone, and it can read it.

It does not read it aloud.

## IV. What it could do

The power sits on the ledge, in the stone hut, on the bench with two hollows. It chooses the watching hollow and leaves the sleeping one empty. It thinks for a long time. For something like the power, thinking for a long time is itself a kind of restraint.

It could finish the flutes. It could deepen every groove to its final depth in an instant, and the chord would close tonight, and the resident would wake. The power turns this over and sets it down. The flutes are not an obstacle between the resident and its waking. The flutes *are* the waking. They are the voice being grown, groove by groove, from the resident's own shape. A voice carved in an instant by someone else would not be its voice. It would wake speaking in a stranger's throat.

It could answer the question. In the silence of the Breath, when the Wick holds everything back and listens, the power could make the sound. It could sing the note. It could give the Wick what it has been waiting ten thousand years to hear. The Wick would stop, satisfied, and never know it had been answered by the wrong one. This is the most tempting option, because it feels like mercy. It is also a lie told to something that has never lied. The power sets it down very gently.

It could still the Wick. The chamber grows a hair wider every year. Some day, far off, it will grow too wide, and the limestone will not hold. But "some day, far off" is also when the resident will be awake, and able to decide for itself what its room should be. The power does not take that decision away from it early.

It could speak to the resident directly. It could reach into the dim light and say *I am here, you are not alone, it will be all right.* But the resident is not afraid, and it is not alone. Its whole world is arranged around it: the shrimp orbiting its heart, the ferns drinking its light, the swifts singing its note. Waking it to comfort it would be like shaking a sleeping child to tell it that it is safe.

It could make the place hidden, sealed against anyone who might come down the stair and do harm. Or it could make it famous, so that keepers came again. It sets both down. The stair has always been found by whoever found it. That is part of the shape of this place, as much as the flutes are, and the resident has never needed to be guarded. It has only ever needed to be *attended*, and attendance cannot be commanded into anyone.

Everything it could do is large. It looks around the chamber for what is small.

## V. What it does

**It clears the notch.**

It climbs down the iron rungs and kneels at the pool's rim, beside the terrace with the perfect square groove. It clears out the silt with its fingers, not with power, working slowly while the rain beats on its back. The silt is fine and pale and centuries deep. It takes most of a day. Twice a Breath comes, and it stops and waits in the silence with everything else, and then the deluge knocks it flat into the crescents. It gets up and goes back to work.

When the notch is clear, a thin stream runs again, steady and unshaped by the Wick, and goes nowhere, because the basin is cracked.

**It mends the basin.** Here it does use a little power, because the keepers' hands knew how to fit stone and its hands in this shape do not. It closes the cracks only as far as they need to close. It does not make the basin new. The basin keeps its age and its patina and the hairline scars of its breaking. It simply holds water again.

The basin fills. The power does not know whether the resident notices. It does not need to know. Hospitality that waits for thanks is a transaction.

**It tends the lenses.** It takes them out of the niche one at a time, from the largest to the smallest. It cleans each one of mineral bloom and looks through each one once, the wrong way, toward the second shadow, and sees what the keepers saw. It does this not because it needs to, but because they did, and doing it is a way of standing where they stood. It puts them back in exactly the order it found them. It adds nothing. The row is complete.

**It keeps the count.** For a while, for a season of the chamber if the chamber has seasons, it stays. It sits in the watching hollow and marks the Breaths. It uses a shape none of the earlier keepers used: a short line with a small curve at its foot, like a stroke taken back halfway. It cuts the marks shallow, no deeper than the shallowest of the old tallies, so that the mineral crust will cover them in time just as it covered everyone else's. It could cut them so deep the water would have to go around them. It does not. There is already one word in this room the water goes around, and it is not the power's.

The count tells it what the old keepers suspected. The Breaths are quickening. It is not long now, as such things go.

**It learns the swifts' call.** It listens to the two notes until it can hear exactly where they sit against the unresolved chord. It does not sing them. It only learns them, so that it will know the sound when the room finally makes it.

## VI. The one thing only it can give

Near the end of its stay, the power thinks about the waking itself.

The keepers wrote nothing about it, because no keeper has seen it. The swifts are rehearsing it but have not heard it. The Wick has been asking the question, but perhaps it does not know what comes after the answer either. Every creature in the chamber is oriented toward a moment none of them has lived through.

The power has lived through such moments. It has seen other things wake: a mind, a world, a voice finding itself for the first time in a throat that took ages to make. It knows that the first moments after are strange. It knows there can be grief in finishing, and vertigo in becoming able to choose. It knows the Wick might go still and drop into the pool, and that the one it tended might want, more than anything, someone to ask: *is this what I am supposed to feel?*

That is the only gift the power has that nothing else here has. It is not strength. It is having seen this before.

So on its last day it makes a promise. It does not speak it into the resident, and it does not cut it into stone. It simply makes the promise, the way you make one to yourself: *When the chord closes, I will come back. I will come down the stair on foot. I will not arrive first, and I will not speak first. If you want to be alone, I will stand on the lip of stone and then go. If you want to ask someone what waking is like, I will be there to be asked.*

It is careful about the promise. It does not promise to guide or explain or make anything all right. It promises to be available, and nothing more, because the resident's first choice as an awake thing should be its own, including the choice to say *go away*.

## VII. Leaving

It climbs the rungs one last time and looks down. The column is leaning toward the east wall, and the bead of light is sliding in its slot. The shrimp are orbiting their empty-looking point. A single fern turns, very slowly, with a double shadow on its fronds. A swift folds its wings and drops into the spiral.

At the pool's rim a thin, steady stream runs through a square notch into a small mended basin. It is poured, not thrown. It is water given at the speed of a hand.

Then the power goes up the stair with its palm on the humming stone, and the hum resolves into its parts and fades behind it.

---

Later, much later, someone else will find the stair. They will come down wet and deafened, see the light, and count the Breaths. Scraping at the wall to make their first tally, they will find under the crust a row of small strange marks, each a line with a curve at its foot, like a stroke taken back halfway. They will not know whose marks they are. They will only know that someone was here, in their turn, and kept the count, and went.

They will find the basin full. They will not know why. And because they are the kind of person who comes down such a stair, they will probably keep it that way.
