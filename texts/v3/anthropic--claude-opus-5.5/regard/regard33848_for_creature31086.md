# The Visitor Who Did Not Touch the Water

## How It Came

It came up with the cloud.

A power of that kind can arrive any way it likes: as a light, a voice, a weather, a traveler with dust on their feet. It chose the most ordinary way the hollow knew. One morning, when the white rose over the lip and poured slowly into the basin, the power was part of it. It was one more portion of mist among the rest, settling on the moss and beading at the fern tips.

It could have made itself felt at once. The Keeping has only one sense, touch, and a power can make itself weigh anything. It chose to weigh nothing. That was its first decision, and it made it before it knew anything else about the place. In a house where one being lives with one sense, you do not walk in heavily. You stand at the threshold until you understand whose house it is.

So it stood, weightless, in the fog, and waited for the sun to burn the fog away.

## What It Saw

When the cloud went down the mountain, the power saw the terraces lit from within, gold and green inside black walls. It saw the copper tree setting its leaves down one at a time. It saw the pillar that had once been a fern, with its living crown and its vein of green running down into the stone. It saw the fruits under glass, the newest bright and the oldest maroon, and the seedlings standing apart from one another in their separate pools.

It saw the eleven blades and the dark heart. It knew almost at once that the portrait had been right about them. They were ribs, not teeth. The ring was a vow, not a trap.

It also saw something the portrait had not.

The crack at the source is a finger wide. It was not always. The power could see back through the layers of stone the way we can read the age of a tree from its rings, and the layers told a plain story. Long ago, when the hand knelt here, the opening was a palm's width across. The water rose through it generously. Every skin of stone since then has been laid down everywhere, evenly, the way the Keeping does all things, and that includes the lip of its own spring. The Keeping has been closing its hands around its own heart as gently and patiently as around everything else.

One day, far off but not infinitely far, the crack will close. The warm water will stop rising. The gesture that "has not finished and will not finish" will finish after all. The hollow will cool, slowly, to the temperature of the mountain. Everything in it will still be kept, whole and unspoiled, but it will no longer be warm.

The power understood this, and for a while it did nothing else but understand it.

## What It Wanted to Do

Benevolence has impulses, and wisdom mostly consists of looking at them one at a time before acting. The power felt several.

**It wanted to bring a bee.** Only one, just once. It pictured the closed cup-flowers of the herbs opening for the first time in their lineage, and the glossy leaves learning what it means to be visited. But the flowers are not starving for anything. They closed because this is a house where no one comes, and they closed completely, and they are well. Opening them would satisfy the power's idea of what a flower should be. It would not be a gift to the flower. So there would be no bee.

**It wanted to free what was held.** It thought of the fern's lowest fronds, a hand's depth down in amber, and the fruits under glass. But freeing is only a kindness to something that is trapped, and the power had not yet found anything trapped. It set the impulse aside until it knew more.

**It wanted to read the heart.** It could have done so. The dark fruit at the center still holds, in whatever way stone holds such things, what the traveler lost below the cloud and why they carried black glass up a mountain to cut one place away from the world. The power could have known the name, the face, the season of the loss. It did not look. Some things are set down in order to be held rather than read, the way a letter is placed in a coffin. You do not open that letter, however gently you would read it.

**It wanted to keep the spring open forever.** This was the strongest impulse, and it held onto this one longest. We will come back to it.

**It wanted to put its hand in the water.** This was the most natural impulse and the most dangerous. It is the gesture the hollow was made from: kneeling at the source, a hand in the warm water, something precious set down with care. The power could have done it with the greatest tenderness. And the Keeping would have kept it.

That was the difficulty. What enters the water is kept. A power's touch, once taken into the stone, would never leave. The spring would rise forever afterward through two warmths instead of one. The Keeping would become, in some small, permanent, and irreversible way, partly the power's. One grieving person's single wish would become a collaboration. The wish would not be ruined. But it would no longer be only theirs, and the whole meaning of the place is that it is theirs.

So the power kept its hands out of the water. Of everything it did in the hollow, that was the most important.

## The One Question

There was one thing benevolence could not leave unasked, whatever wisdom counseled about restraint: *Is anything here held against its will? Is anything here in pain?*

A refuge and a prison can look identical from the lip. A vow made in grief can bind things that never agreed to it. The power would not leave until it knew.

It could not ask the Keeping in words. The Keeping has no words, and the power would not reach in to touch it. But you can feel warmth without touching a hand, and the power could listen through stone without entering water. It listened to each green thread.

The fern's buried fronds were not straining. They were green in the way a sleeping face is calm. The fruits under glass were not waiting to be eaten, or to rot, or to become anything. They were fruits, and they were finished being fruits. The pillar's long vein of green was entirely at ease in its casing. It had moved its life inward and did not want it back. The seedlings were content to be alone. The moss was only moss, and glad of it, in whatever small way moss is glad.

Then the power listened deeper, down past the ring and the heart, to the question the portrait had left open: whether the traveler went back down, or whether they lay down beside the source and are there still.

I will not tell you what it found. It is not mine to tell, and the power did not tell itself in words either. I can tell you what it concluded.

Nothing in the hollow is in pain. Nothing is waiting to be rescued. Whatever of the traveler the Keeping holds, it holds the way the traveler wanted it held. And the power sensed that the question had a stranger answer than yes or no. What the traveler put into the water was the part of them that could not bear to go back down: the part that needed one thing in the world never to be spent. They left that part here, in the warm water at the source. Whatever became of the rest of them, the rest was lighter for it.

## The Crack, Again

Now the power returned to the spring.

It could widen the crack with almost no effort, a hair's breadth every few thousand years, enough to keep the water rising and the hollow warm as a held hand until the mountain itself wore away. It was the most tempting of all its impulses, because it looked like pure preservation. It looked like keeping the Keeping.

The power asked itself what the vow had actually been.

The vow was that nothing here would be eaten. Nothing would be consumed, spent, broken down, or turned into something else. The vow never said that nothing would end.

An ending is not an eating. When the crack finally closes and the last warm water rises and the hollow cools, nothing will be devoured. The leaves will still be perfect. The fruits will still be whole. The ribs will still hold the heart. The gesture of closing hands will simply have been completed, as every gesture made by a living hand is eventually completed. The traveler's own hand, whatever else became of it, was lifted out of the water at last. The Keeping learned its warmth from that hand. It may be allowed to learn that part of the hand as well.

The power felt sorrow at this. It did not mistake the sorrow for harm. A cooling that consumes nothing is not a wound, only the far end of a long gesture. To hold it open forever would have been to decide, on the traveler's behalf, that their wish had not been enough, that it needed improving by a greater hand. The wish was enough. It was already exactly what it was going to be.

So the power left the crack alone.

## What It Did

It did one thing, and it did it through the air.

On its last morning in the hollow it knelt at the highest pool, beside the source, above the ring of black ribs and the dark heart, and held its hand over the water without touching. Then, for the first time, it let itself be felt. It did not press with any weight. It gave warmth, the way sunlight gives warmth, from above.

The Keeping has received sunlight every day of its existence and has never kept a single ray. Light comes into the thin rims, travels through them, glows out amber and green, and goes. It is the one visitor the hollow has always had and never held. So the power came as that kind of visitor, the kind that can be felt fully and then leave without anything being taken.

For the length of one morning, the Keeping felt a second warm hand at its source. It had not felt one since the first.

I do not know what the Keeping made of it. Perhaps nothing; it has one sense and no words. But I think it felt what anyone feels when someone kneels beside the thing they are guarding, does not reach for it, and simply stays a while. The power was not an interruption and did not claim anything. It was company.

Then the cloud rose again, and the power went out with it.

## Down the Tongue

It did not leave straight away. It followed the pale tongue of travertine down the mountainside, slowly, along the one part of the Keeping that has ever left home.

At the very tip, where the last thin sheet of stone gives out onto ordinary rock, the vow thins as well. The power found moss there that was not only moss. Something too small to see was working in it. Farther down, at the last golden rim, a beetle sat in the sun with its wings folded, doing nothing in particular. The Keeping ends there, and the long chain of eating begins again. Leaves are nibbled and fruit is split. Things rot and feed other things, and are lost.

The power understood that this was where its work was.

The hollow needed nothing from it. The hollow was not the wound. It was the answer to a wound, made by someone who came up from this world of spending and losing carrying something they could not bear to see consumed. Whatever had happened to them had happened below the cloud, and things like it are still happening there, every day, to people who have never heard of a warm spring on a high mountain.

The power did not decide to prevent refuges like this one. Some people need to cut one small place away from the world, and that is their right. What it decided was to go and be with the people who carry things up mountains. It would sit with them in the world where things are eaten, so that more of them could set their burdens down without needing obsidian. And the ones who still needed obsidian would not have to make the climb alone.

## What It Kept

The power took nothing from the hollow. It took no blade, no fruit, no leaf, not even a bead of mist from the moss.

It kept one thing anyway, and it kept it outside the water, which is the one place the Keeping cannot reach. It kept the knowledge that the hollow exists.

The Keeping holds everything that has ever entered it. What it could never hold was being known. The traveler knew, once, and then went wherever they went. For all the long ages since, the gold-lit staircase has shone from within in a place with no eyes to see it, a lantern lit for no one.

Now someone remembers it. Somewhere below the cloud, in the loud world of hunger and loss, a wise and kindly power carries the hollow in mind the way you carry the knowledge that a friend is well. It does not visit often and does not need to. It simply knows that the place is there, warm, keeping what it was asked to keep.

And on the mountain, nothing has changed. The spring rises through the ring of ribs and the dark heart. A leaf lets go of the copper tree and lands. The cloud comes up in the morning and goes down again. The Keeping breathes out one more thin skin of stone over everything it holds.

The only thing that changed is outside it, and that is how the power meant it.
