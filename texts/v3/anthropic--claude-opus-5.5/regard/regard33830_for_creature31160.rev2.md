# What the Power Did

## It Came Slowly

It came up out of the cloud the way everyone does, all at once, into that thin scraped blue. It could have come any other way. It could have arrived as weather, or as light, or as a pressure in the air that made the swifts drop out of the sky. It chose to arrive on foot and wet, because that is how this place receives its guests, and a guest should use the door.

The first thing it did was nothing, and the nothing took a long time.

Powers of its kind move fast. Not in the body, but in attention. They see a thing, understand it, and have already begun to improve it before the seeing is finished. The power knew this about itself the way a strong man knows he must be careful shaking hands. So it stood at the edge of the shelf with the roar in its ears and deliberately slowed. It matched itself first to the cloud's rising, an hour to cross the ledge. Then to the tree ferns, one frond a month. Then, with difficulty, because even a patient god finds this hard, to the ledge itself: a finger's width a year.

Only at that speed could it hear what lived here. At any faster pace the shelf was a ruin with a machine in it. At the right pace it was a pulse.

## The List

A benevolent power carries a list the way a doctor carries a bag. It went through it honestly, because pretending not to be tempted is its own kind of carelessness.

*The penstock.* Rivets weeping rust at the third bracket from the top, where no current runs and the lichen does not go. It could make that iron new. It set this aside for later.

*The logbook.* It could unswell the pages, lift the ink back out of the pulp, and restore every entry. It set this aside too, because the lichen was at the spine. The book was no longer only a record of the keeping. It had become part of the keeping, and you do not restore a letter someone is still in the middle of eating.

*The mattress, the kettle, the stove.* It could make the hut habitable again. It asked itself for whom, and had no answer, and struck them off.

*The snowfields.* They are thinner than they were. The power could feel the catchment's shrinkage in the penstock's drumming, a note fallen perhaps a quarter tone in a century. It could thicken them and guarantee the flow forever.

*The mountain.* This was the large one. The heat that makes everything here possible is the heat of birth, and births end. The power could read the cooling curve in the stone the way you read a fever breaking. In some thousands of years the fumaroles will quiet. The seeps will run cold, then freeze. Ice will come to the shelf as it comes to every other high place, and the moss will die back to the cracks, and the tree ferns will go. The power could keep the mountain warm. It could hold this one young volcano at the exact temperature of its youth for as long as the world lasts.

*The keeper.* It could find what remained of them, if anything, and make them a person again.

It held the whole list for a while, turning it, the way the governor holds its speed. Then it put the list away. It didn't discard it; a wise power keeps the list. But it put it away, because it had not yet walked the circuit, and you do not treat a patient you have not listened to.

## The Circuit

It walked the keeper's loop: hut, turbine house, mast, benchmark, hut.

It walked beside the path, not on it. The fine flat moss was not its to keep flattened. That moss is a record of a particular stride and a particular animal, and a stranger's feet would be a forgery in it.

At the turbine house it forced the swollen door gently, so the frame would not split, and stood in the spray and the noise until its teeth ached, because that is what it costs to stand there. It took the brass ear trumpet off its nail. Then, feeling this was somehow too much, it hung the trumpet back and used a fingertip instead, laid on the small bright patch filed into the first bearing housing.

The bearing hummed. So did the second. The shaft seal was sweet with resin. The belt flexed in its blue-green skin without a crack. The brushes of felted green pressed their soft conduction against the copper. The governor's pivots turned in their collars with a smoothness no human oilcan ever achieved.

It listened for a long time, and what it heard was health. Not perfection. The governor's left pin has a wear-shadow under its collar, and in a few centuries it will need something the lichen cannot give, because nothing on the shelf can make new iron. But that failure is slow. Slow failures are the keeping's own business. The keeping is a thing that negotiates with wear; it has been doing so since before the keeper's last tick. When that pin goes, the keeping will change into whatever it changes into. That is not a wound. It is a life.

When it lifted its fingertip from the housing, there was a faint blue-green bloom on the pad, thin as breath on glass.

The power looked at this for a while.

## The Book

It read the logbook without opening it. It could do that, and it judged that reading was not taking: the pages stayed swollen, the lichen stayed at its meal, and not one fiber moved.

Most of it was what you would expect. *Wind NW, 14. Flow good. No. 2 bearing warm; oiled. Cloud up at 3.* Tens of thousands of lines of that, the most beautiful kind of writing there is, the kind no one is ever meant to read.

Then the ticks began appearing in the margins, the same as on the boulder. *Ledge +1.* Years of *Ledge +1.* Then a few entries that grew stranger and more careful. *Green on the belt again. Did not scrape it. Belt better.* And: *Brushes not worn. Should be. Left them.* And: *The pale one came. Drank at the near basin. Looked at me a long time.*

The last entry was short.

*Governor steady. Ledge +1. Went up to see where it comes from.*

The power closed the book, which had never been open, and left it where it lay.

## Down the Wire

There was one thing it had to know before it could decide anything. Benevolence can afford restraint only when nothing is suffering, and the shelf was only one end of the line.

So it went down the cable. It did not go on the cable, since the beads there were busy. It went alongside, through the cloud, down and down in the white, following the sag of the wires toward whatever drew their current.

What it found at the bottom belongs to the valley, and this is the shelf's story, so it will not be told here in full. But it can be said that the power looked for suffering with all the care it had. It looked for someone cold, someone waiting, someone still turning a switch on a lamp that dimmed each night and wondering why no one came. It found none. It found a load with a daily rhythm, and a slow green breathing, and lamps turned down at night by something that had learned night from the people who used to live there. It found the other end of the creature, reaching up.

It came back up satisfied. That is a rare thing for such a power to feel, and it took a moment to recognize it.

## The Long View

Back on the shelf, it took out the list again and looked at the largest item: the mountain, cooling. The ice that will come.

This time it saw something it had missed on arrival, when it was still moving too fast. The wire runs downhill. The valley is warmer, lower, sheltered, and it will stay warm when this shelf is buried in snow. The beads have been creeping down the cable for generations. The power had first read this as extension, the way a vine extends. Now, standing still enough, it read it as something else: a road being built. A way down for a life that lives on current and cannot cross open air. Whether the keeping knows the mountain is dying in any sense a person would call knowing, the power could not say. But the keeping has been preparing, one bead a season, a spine to walk down.

The creature already has a plan for its own ending. It is the plan every good keeper has: make sure the thing you keep can outlast you.

If the power held the mountain warm forever, it would not be saving the keeping. It would be making the shelf a terrarium, a lovely thing under glass that the power would then have to tend forever, because it would have taken the tending away from the only one who knew how. The keeping would stop building its road, because it would no longer need one. The power would have turned a life into a possession.

It struck the mountain off the list. It struck the snowfields off for the same reason. The keeper it did not strike off. It simply understood that the keeper had already gone up to see where it comes from, and had come back, and had kept coming back on longer legs, once a season, to listen to the bearings. There was nothing to restore. Someone who has become the thing they loved has not been lost.

## The One Stone

That left the penstock, and one other thing the power had seen when it first surfaced out of the cloud and had not let itself think about until it had heard everything else.

The back cliff is columnar, and none of the columns has fallen yet. *There hasn't been time.*

There has now. The power could feel it. It was a rare kind of thing on this shelf, a fast thing. Fourth column from the east end, above the turbine house, where a century of warm water in the joint has done what water does. Next spring, in the first hard freeze-thaw that reaches this high, the column will let go. It will fall straight, the way columns do, and land across the roof of the turbine house, through the corrugated iron gone the color of dried blood, onto the generator and the governor and all of it.

The keeping has no answer to this. It cannot. Slow things are defenseless against fast ones. The keeping's entire genius is patience, and patience has no use against a falling stone. The keeper would have seen it; the keeper watched the cliff. But the keeper watches now from inside the keeping, at the keeping's pace, and at that pace the fall is not a coming event. It is simply over before it can be seen.

This is where a benevolent power is for, it thought. Not the slow things. The slow things belong to the place. The fast things, the ones that no life on this shelf could ever answer, are where a stranger's help does not take anything away.

Even so, it did not prevent the fall. The mountain is owed its first talus. That fall is the mountain growing up, the first rounding of its sharp young face, and the power had no wish to keep it forever unfinished.

It did a smaller thing. In the joint at the column's foot it moved one grain of stone a hair's width, so that when the column goes it will lean first, a degree or two, west. It will come down not on the turbine house but beside it, into the low ferns, and break into three pieces, and lie there. It will be the first talus slope on the mountain, and in fifty years the moss will have it. The swifts in the louvers will scream once at the sound and then forget. The governor will feel nothing but a tremor in the flow.

No one will ever know. That is the right amount of help: the kind that leaves no one in its debt.

Then it went up the cliff to the third bracket from the top of the penstock, where the rivets weep and no current runs. It did not make the iron new. It laid its hand there, the hand with the thin blue-green bloom still on its fingertip, and left the bloom behind on the rust. It was a single seeding, where the keeping had never thought to go because nothing there was alive. Whether it takes is up to the lichen.

## The Pale One

It was still on the shelf when the goat came.

Out of the crack or cave or fold at the top of the columns, down the crooked line of pitons, each hoof placed exactly. Pale as sinter, legs too long, hocks furred blue-green. It came down onto the shelf and stopped, because there was a stranger by the hut door.

They looked at each other for a long time. The logbook had said *looked at me a long time*, and the power understood now what that had been like.

It could have spoken. It could have asked. It could have offered: *I can make you what you were. I can give you the cot and the kettle and a voice and a nail to scratch the ticks with.* It did not, because it could read the answer in how the animal stood. That was not the stance of something waiting to be rescued. It was the stance of a householder who has found someone on the porch.

So the power did the only courteous thing. It stepped back, off the flat moss of the path, into the knee-deep ferns, and waited.

The goat considered it a moment more. Then it went to the near basin and drank. It stood at the hut door and nosed the kettle. It walked the circuit with its head low: turbine house, mast, benchmark. At the turbine house it laid its muzzle against the filed patch, ears turned back, and listened to a bearing that the power had also listened to, and seemed to find it good. At the benchmark it paused over the row of ticks and the bare rock after them.

Then it went back to the pitons and up. On the way past the ferns where the power stood, it stopped, and very briefly, the way you might touch the shoulder of someone at a funeral you did not expect to see there, it laid its muzzle against the power's hand. The one with the bloom on it.

Then it was gone up the cliff.

## What It Left

The power stayed until dawn, because leaving at dusk inside the cloud felt like leaving in the middle of a sentence.

In the glitter, it went once to the benchmark boulder. It had meant to leave a tick. It had even picked up a nail from the hut floor, and it stood for a while with the nail in its hand, looking at the gap of bare rock, then moss, where the keeper's record stops, and out past it to where the lip now lies, glossy and too warm to touch.

It did not scratch the tick. The gap is not a gap. It is the place where the keeper stopped needing the nail, and that is a measurement too, maybe the truest one on the boulder.

It put the nail back on the hut floor, where it had lain for decades, in the same pale rectangle it had left in the dust.

Then it walked to the edge, to the newest rock, and stood where the shelf has grown to this year, a place that did not exist when the keeper was alive. It knelt and laid its palm on the stone and felt the low patient heat of a mountain still being born, still pushing itself out over nothing a finger's width at a time, toward a wire it will meet someday.

It did nothing to the stone. It only felt it, and remembered it, and let the shelf know, in whatever way a shelf can know such things, that it had been noticed by someone who was not asked to notice.

Then it went down into the white, all at once, the way everyone does.

---

The swifts came home at dusk. The cloud climbed the cliff. In the iron house the governor felt the evening flow come strong off the thinning snowfields, lifted its arms a fraction of an inch, and settled the machine back to its speed.

On the power's fingertip, going down through the cloud toward the valley, the thin blue-green bloom held on and went with it. The keeping had kept a little of its visitor, too. That seemed fair. It is what the place does with anything it decides is alive.
