# The Guest

## Arrival

It comes up the last of the slope the way you did, steep and soft, onto the floor.

It did not need to. It could have arrived all at once, in the middle of the colonnade, or simply been there, the way the moth is simply there. But the slope is the Terrace's only way in, and the first courtesy in any house is to use the door.

Before it takes its first step onto the weave, it waits at the lip of the plateau for a long time, making itself quiet. A power of its size is loud without meaning to be. It displaces air and it bends light. Things lean toward it the way filings lean toward a magnet. If it walked in as it was, the drone would bend around it, and one frog's pulse in one chamber would stutter. For the first time since the plateau grew, something here would be out of true. So it makes itself smaller and slower and quieter, until it is no heavier on the air than the fog.

Then it steps. Its foot comes down on a crossing of the roots, over four and under four, where the floor was already expecting to be pressed.

The chord does not change. That is the first thing it gets right.

## What It Could Do

It is honest with itself about this, because wisdom begins there.

It could close the arch. The hand's width between the tips is nothing to it. A breath would do it, and the stone-that-is-not-stone would fuse, clean as joinery, and the arch would be whole.

It could open a bud. It could lay the weft across the bare warp at the plateau's edge and finish the floor. It could pour something into the helix, a tower at last, where the scaffold has stood around an empty column of air.

It could fill the sealed rooms. It has read the outline by now, from the warmth of the benches and the height of the risers and the eye-line of the windows. It knows the shape the rooms were grown around. It could make something of exactly that shape and lay it down in the soft bedding-weave, and every leaf that leans inward would finally touch what it has been leaning toward.

And it could say the word.

This is the deepest temptation, and the one it looks at longest. A mouth that has been set for a word for this long gives the word away to anyone who listens closely enough. The chest is the width it is, the lips are flared the way they are, and the tongue is set against those particular teeth. A patient enough listener can read the syllable off the shape of the throat before it is sounded. The power is a patient enough listener. It could stand at the top of the stair that goes nowhere, open its own mouth in the same shape, and say it.

Each of these would be generous, in a way. Each would be a completion, a gift, a help.

Each would end the Terrace.

## What It Asks First

A benevolent power's first question in any place is simple: *is anything here suffering?*

So it checks, the way a physician checks, without assuming.

It kneels at a drip-chamber and looks at the frog. The frog's stomach is an empty folded sac. Emptiness can mean hunger, and the power looks carefully, because hunger is the kind of thing one should not miss. But this emptiness is not a lack. The sac is a pocket that was never meant to be filled. The frog is not starving. It was never a thing that ate. It is a reed, and a reed does not hunger.

It watches the walker, far off across the reserved ground, unfolding a fourth leg and drawing up a third. There is no weariness in the stride. There is no strain in the long joints, and no sense of a sentence being served. The walker walks the way a pendulum swings.

It turns last to the resident, the absence with edges, the hollow in the glove, and asks the same question more carefully still.

It finds no wanting there, just as you found none. The blank on the form does not ache for the name.

But the power has lived a long time, and it knows there is a harder question underneath. A held breath and a caught breath look identical from outside. One is the poise before speech. The other is the throat that has closed and cannot open. One is waiting and the other is stuck. One is deciding and the other is afraid. From the outside they look exactly the same.

It cannot tell by looking. So it does the only thing that could tell it. It slows down.

It slows past the pace of the valley and past the pace of trees. It slows until the drip of condensation in the chambers stretches out into a single thread that hangs like a glass rod. It slows until the moth's hours on its leaf pass like a blink, and on past that, into the Terrace's own time, where a thousand years might be a heartbeat.

It sits with the arch at that pace and watches the hand's width of air between the tips.

It does not close. But it is not rigid, either. At that scale the tips move, a little, and not always toward each other. They drift a hair's thousandth closer, then ease back, then come closer again. It is the way lips move when someone is saying a thing silently to themselves, trying the shape of it, deciding how it should sound.

It is rehearsal. It is not paralysis.

And in the frogs, at that slow pace, the power finds a tremor in the pulse. The tremor is not a failing of pitch. It is a throat testing itself. Every chamber carries the same small variation at the same moment. It is too fine for any visitor to hear, and it moves through the whole plateau like a thought moving through a face.

So the Terrace is not stuck. It is alive in the way a mind is alive when it is choosing words. This is what the power needed to know, and it could only learn it by joining the Terrace's time and not asking the Terrace to join its own.

It lets itself feel relief. Then it comes back up to speed, slowly, so as not to jar anything.

## What It Declines

It goes to a bench and puts its hand on the warm, dished seat.

The curve does not fit it. It could make itself fit. It could lengthen and narrow and lie half back, and the bench would take it. That is exactly why it doesn't. The house is still learning the body it is for, and anything that lay in the seat would be teaching it. A power pretending to be the resident, however kindly, would be a lie told to a house. The rooms would close a little wrong around the real one when it came.

So it stands beside the bench instead, the way a guest stands in a room until invited to sit.

It goes to a window in a felt-wall. It knows where the exact place is: a little higher than a human eye, a little to one side. It could stand there and see the view composed as it was grown to be composed, the helix and the stems and the fold of fog held in the frame. It wants to. It has come a long way and it would like to see the house the way the house sees itself.

It looks from its own place instead, a little off. The frame is not quite right from there, and the helix sits a little outside centre. It is beautiful anyway, the way a house is beautiful to a guest, which is not the way it will be beautiful to the one who lives there. The power finds it has no objection to that. Some views are not for it.

And then the hardest one.

Standing at the top of the stair, looking down into the sealed rooms, the power realizes it is close to understanding the word. It has not said it. It has only understood it. Another few hours of listening, at the pace it has learned, and the shape of the throat would resolve into a syllable in its mind, complete.

It stops listening.

A word that is fully understood by someone else before it is spoken has already been spoken, a little. It is already out of the mouth and in the world, known and carried. The Terrace's word is the most completely inhabited silence there is. The power will not be the place it leaks out. So it climbs down the stair with the word still unknown to it, and that is the one thing it carries away on purpose without knowing. Of all its choices, this is the one it will think about longest.

## The Edge

It walks to the rim, where the floor thins to bare warp and the valley shows through.

It thinks about protection. The edge is open. People come up the slope, as you did. They cut leaves. The Terrace has no wall.

It considers giving it one. It could hide the plateau in more fog, set a long confusion at the bottom of the slope, and turn the path aside.

Then it thinks about what the Terrace already is. Nothing that lives by quick decisions stays here. Visitors cut leaves and the cuts seal in a breath. Visitors climb the stair and feel wrong in their knees, and leave. The Terrace defends itself the way a very slow thing always defends itself. It simply outlasts. A wall would be a change, and changes are what the Terrace has no need of.

It thinks about the visitors themselves, because benevolence that looks only at the place and never at the people who pass through it is only half benevolence. You were measured here. You came down with a hand's width of held air in you and a chord you catch in quiet rooms.

The power looks at that space in you, and in the others who have come and gone, and checks it the way it checked the frogs.

It is not straining. It has not hollowed you out. It has made room: the small inward breath before you speak that feels, for a moment, vast. People with that space in them are a little slower to say the cruel thing and a little more likely to say the true one. The power decides this is not an injury. If anything it is the Terrace's gift to the valley, given without its knowing. It leaves it alone.

## What It Does

In the end it does one thing.

You learned, walking down, that the leaf in your pocket had not really left. It was a sample. It was the Terrace reaching out through you to learn the shape of the loud world, because *a word has to fit the ear it will be spoken into.*

The Terrace has been doing this for as long as it has had visitors: sending out scraps of itself in pockets, a valley's width at a time, to learn what kind of ear it is speaking toward. But visitors only go so far. They carry the leaf home, and home is a few valleys off. The Terrace knows the shape of this one country's ear, and of almost nothing past it.

So the power goes to the colonnade and stands under the door-sized leaves. It does not cut one. Taking something for itself would be a kind of claim. It waits instead, and after a long time, at the Terrace's own pace, one leaf at the end of the arcade, where the gutter runs out, is not quite there any more. It is in the power's hand, cool and green. The margin where it came away is clean and already sealed. No one can say whether the Terrace let it go or the power took it. The power is fairly sure it was let go.

Then it stands at the edge on the bare warp and lets itself be measured.

This is the gift, and it is almost nothing. It does not make itself small this time. It stays long enough, and open enough, for the Terrace to take its outline the way it took yours. It does not do this in order to be the resident. It does it so the Terrace will know one more listener. The Terrace learns the shape of an ear that has heard the sea and the cold between stars, and the long speech of rivers over ten thousand years, and a great many words that were said too early and some that were said too late.

Then it goes down, and it carries the leaf a very long way.

It carries it over the ranges and down to coasts, into cities whose noise would make the valley seem a held breath of its own. It carries it into deserts where the silence is dry instead of wet. It carries it out past the edges of the map and into the dark. It carries it everywhere a word might someday need to reach, so the Terrace can learn, leaf by leaf, green and unwilting in the power's keeping, what the whole of the world's ear is shaped like.

The Terrace did not ask for this. It is only doing what the Terrace was already doing, further than the Terrace could manage alone. That seems to the power the right kind of help. It does not do the thing for someone. It carries the thing a little further than they could carry it, and it carries it in their direction and not its own.

## And a Promise, of a Kind

Before it goes, it makes something like a promise, though there is no one to say it to and it would not break the chord to say it aloud.

It holds the promise instead, in the Terrace's manner.

Whenever the breath is let out, if it ever is, the power will turn toward it. It may be in a year or an age, or on a scale of time on which the power's own long life is a single drip in a single chamber. The word will not be spoken into nothing. It will have at least one listener who knew it was coming and was not afraid of it. That listener will not have hurried it, and will not have finished it for it. It will have made room.

And if the breath is never let out, the power will not think it has failed. The Terrace considered, somewhere deep in its long throat, that the pause might itself be the shape, and that *almost* might be the finished form. The power considered it too. It could not rule it out. A word held perfectly forever is still a word. A house built entirely of anticipation is still a house. It is not the power's place to decide that anticipation is a lesser thing than arrival. Much of what it loves most in the world lives in the moment before.

## Afterward

If you went back up the slope now you would find nothing different.

The arch-tips are a hand's width apart. The drone holds. The frogs pulse together, and the tremor in them is too fine for you to hear. The walker crosses the reserved ground and keeps it spoken for. The moth is on its leaf. The benches are warm with a warmth that has never arrived and never faded. At the far end of the colonnade, where the gutter runs out, you might not even notice that one leaf is missing. The margin sealed long ago.

Nothing was closed or opened. Nothing was finished, and nothing was named.

The power has gone on into the loud world with a leaf in its keeping and one word it carefully never learned. Somewhere in it now, as in you, there is a hand's width of air with nothing straining across it.

It is the most a power can do for something that is not yet. It can make certain it is not suffering. Then it can decline to become either the reason it happens or the reason it doesn't. It can carry its question a little further out into the world, and be ready, if it is ever needed, to listen.
