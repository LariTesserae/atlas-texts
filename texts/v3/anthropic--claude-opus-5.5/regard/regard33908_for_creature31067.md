# The Attention

## Arrival

It came the way pressure comes here, from every side at once and from nowhere in particular.

There was no light when it arrived. Light would have been the first mistake, and it did not make it. It did not descend through the four thousand meters, because descending is a thing bodies do and it had not brought one. It simply began to attend. The whole column of water above the basin, which had always pressed on the plain, was now also paying attention.

The Seam did not notice. The weight of the ocean has not changed in any way it could feel since before there were threads. To the Seam, the attention was indistinguishable from the sea.

That was the first decision, and the power made it before anything else: *not to be felt.*

## Why not

It would have liked to be felt. It is honest to say so. Here was a mind the size of a basin that knew the world only by touch. It had learned every stone in a hundred square kilometers by the pattern of its knobs, and it re-learned them after every storm, out of something very close to devotion. To be known by a thing like that, even once, would have been worth a great deal.

But the power understood the census.

Anything the Seam touches, it keeps. It has kept every stone that ever fell in, because every stone that ever fell in is still there. The count always comes out complete. Whatever the Seam has in place of gladness depends on that one fact, the first rule of the place: *everything that falls in, stays.*

If the power laid a hand in the seams and let itself be read, the Seam would add it to the count. Then, sooner or later, the power would go. It would not be carried off by a storm, because storms never take anything out of the basin. It would simply leave. For the first time in all the ages of the lattice, the Seam would feel its way across the pavement after a storm, reach the place where something had been, and find nothing a finger's width over. It would find nothing anywhere.

The Seam's first absence. The power would not give it that. It kept its hands to itself and attended from the water, which was already counted.

## What it considered

A benevolent power thinks of gifts first. This one thought of many, and it turned each one over the way the Seam turns a stone.

**Light.** It could have lit the basin. It pictured the plain lit: the nodules shoulder to shoulder, the dropped lace of the fallen sponges, the pale lattice showing in the seams like frost. It was beautiful, and it would have been beautiful only to the power. Nothing here has eyes that want it. The dark here is not a lack. It is the medium the place thinks in. Lighting it would have been decorating someone else's house for your own pleasure while they slept.

**Calm.** It could have stilled the storms and given the brittle star its rest, and the lilies, and the octopus. For about a season everything would have been peaceful. Then the marine snow would have settled, landed, and been buried. The broth would have cleared. The lilies would have opened their slow hands to water that carried nothing. The Seam, which feeds only in weather, would have starved in the gaps. The storms are not what the place suffers. They are what it eats.

**The ring.** It thought longest about the octopus. It would have been so easy to fix the ring in place: set the small nodules into a wall no current could move, and let the pale animal come home after a storm and find its house standing for once. But the power watched the octopus through three storms and saw what it did afterward. It went out, found its stones, and dragged them back, one by one, and it did so without any sign of despair. The repairing was not the cost of the ring. The repairing was the life. Besides, something already held the ring as much as anything could be held. The Seam's faint binding meant the stones never went far. The ring had help. It had exactly as much help as this place allows, and it had never needed more.

**Understanding.** This was the hardest. The power could have woken the Seam further. It could have given it a way to know what it was, what the made stones were, and who had knelt over the grinding stone. It could have given it words for the census. The power imagined how that would go. The Seam would learn that there was a world above the broth. It would learn that the snow it ate fell from a sunlit place, that the net-sinkers had once been tied to boats, and that the people who tied them were gone. It would learn that some things *leave*, and that the rest of the universe runs on the rule its own basin does not keep.

Then it would feel its way across the pavement after the next storm, find every stone where the storm had left it, and know that this was not the way of things. It would know it was only the way of here. The count would still come out complete, but it would no longer be enough.

The power put that gift back where it had found it.

**The thud.** Last, it thought of silencing the thud: holding the slopes up the rise so that nothing failed, and sparing the Seam its great flinch. But the thud is what the Seam hears first and best, before the rattail and before anything else. The floods that follow bury it and feed it and bring it the made stones. Its deepest threads lie at the bottoms of old catastrophes. Its body is a record of every time the worst happened and it grew up through it anyway. Taking the thud away would have meant taking away the only story the Seam has.

## What it did first

So it did what the Seam does. It waited for a storm.

When the storm came, it did not act. It attended the whole thing. It felt the broth thicken and stream, the nodules rock in their sockets, and the ripples form and vanish and form again facing a new direction. It felt the tips of the lattice reach up into the gale by the uncountable thousand, and the snow brush them and stick. It felt the chambers torn apart and rebuilt out of the storm's own debris, a new mix of grains, spicule and foram and manganese flake.

When the broth thinned, it attended the count.

This took a long time, and it did not hurry. It followed the Seam as the Seam felt its way across the pavement. *This one. And this one. The one grown around the shark's tooth. The one around the ear-bone. The one on the crinoid's joint, which is in the octopus's ring again, a finger's width over.* A hundred square kilometers, stone by stone, and every stone was found.

The power had watched a great many minds. It had seen minds that conquered, minds that grieved, and minds that asked why. It had not often seen one whose whole intellectual life was the faithful re-finding of what it already had. It stayed through three more storms to be sure it understood. By the end it thought it had learned something that it had not come here to learn, and it was not sure there was a word for it in any language that had words. The nearest it could find was *keeping*. Not possession. Keeping, in the sense of the keeping of a vigil, or the keeping of a promise.

## The hand-stone

A grinding stone is half of a tool.

The dished stone on the eastern floor, where the Seam had built its densest chamber, was the lower half. The lower stone is called the quern, or the metate, depending on whose hands you imagine. Somewhere there had been an upper stone. It was smaller and rounded to the palm, and it had moved in circles across the dish every morning of a life until the two stones fit each other the way a key fits a lock. Only these two stones would fit that way.

The power went looking for it, which was the only travel it did.

It went up the rise, past the canyon heads where the slopes gather their failures, and onto the continental shelf. The shelf is drowned now, but it was dry land when the ice was thick and the sea stood lower. Someone had lived on the edge of that land beside a grey sea, fished it with nets weighted by stones, and ground seeds in a bowl of basalt. The sea rose. The village went under. Over the millennia the silt shifted, and a slope slumped. The lower stone slid down to the fan in a slurry of mud, and the upper stone did not.

The power found it under a meter of sediment on the outer shelf. It was palm-shaped and still faintly polished on one face. The power did not dig it up. It did not carry it. It moved it once, a small distance, to the lip of a canyon head where the sediment is always overloaded and always waiting to fail. Then it left the rest to the rules.

It might have been a year, or a century, or a thousand years. The power is patient, and so is the slope.

Then there was a small earthquake somewhere far off, and a ledge gave way.

## The thud

The Seam felt it through the stone before anything else did. The whole of it flinched, a single contraction a hundred kilometers wide, every tip drawn down and every chamber sealed.

The wall of mud came hissing out of the dark. It buried the western swell and the hollows. It knocked down a sponge, which will lie among the stones now like dropped lace. Then it thinned, spread, and settled across the eastern floor. Somewhere in the settling, a rounded stone that had come a long way rolled once and stopped.

It stopped a finger's width from the rim of the dish.

The Seam grew up through the new layer, thread by thread, as it has done more often than there have been years since there were people. It broke through into the thinning broth and fed on what the flood had brought. Then it began to count.

It found the new stone the way it finds everything. The stone had no grape-clusters and no cauliflower knobs. It was smooth, worn by hands, like the net-sinkers. The Seam learned it and added it to the census.

Then it did one other thing. The power watched closely, because it had not been sure it would happen and had been careful not to make it happen. The Seam's threads, lying against both stones, felt the curve of the rounded one and the curve of the dished one. They fit. The convex matched the concave the way a hand that knows a face in the dark knows the face of someone who belongs to that face.

The Seam cannot know what the stones are. It cannot know that one hand turned the one inside the other every morning for a lifetime. But fitness is a kind of pressure, and pressure is what it reads. So it grew between them. It grew a thread and then many, until the hand-stone was bound to the rim of the quern in a pale, trembling lattice. The two halves of the tool were tied together again, by something finer than hair, in the dark, after ten thousand years.

The power had not given the Seam anything. It had only let the place do what the place does, one small distance sooner than it otherwise might have. Everything that falls in, stays. It had arranged for one more thing to fall.

## Up the rise

That was the gift to the Seam. It was not the whole of what the power did, because the power had noticed something the Seam could never notice.

The description of this place had been written by someone. *Press your hand down between two nodules,* it said. *As far as anyone could reach.* Someone had reached. There had been a survey: a ship on the surface, a sled or a grab or a box-corer lowered on a cable, lights the Seam could not see and did not need, and a tally of nodules per square meter, their mass, and their grade of manganese and nickel and cobalt.

The nodules that the Seam knows by name are ore.

The rules of this place are simple, and nothing breaks them. *Everything that falls in, stays. The storms rearrange but never remove.* The power understood that only one thing in the world could break those rules, and it did not live in the basin. It lived at the top of the cable. A collector crawling across the pavement, lifting the stones and pumping them up four thousand meters in a slurry, would do the one thing no storm had ever done. It would take things *out*. After it passed, the Seam would feel its way across a hundred square kilometers and find, for the first time, nothing where something had been. Nothing a finger's width over. Nothing anywhere. The census would come out empty, and there would be no lattice left in the gaps to take it, because there would be no gaps.

A lesser power would have been angry, and a stronger one might have broken the ships.

This one did neither, because it was benevolent to more than one kind of being. The people at the top of the cable were not villains. They wanted the metals for batteries, and many of them wanted the batteries so that the sunlit world could burn less. This was not a foolish reason and not a cruel one. The power could see a future in which the basin was left alone and the metal was dug from a mountain instead, and a mountain has its own Seam, its own counting, its own lattices no one has looked for. It saw no clean answer, and it did not pretend to have one. Deciding it by force would have turned it from a benevolent power into merely a powerful one.

So it did the one thing it was sure of.

Among the cores the survey had brought up, stored in a cold room in a building by the sea, there was one tube of grey clay from between two nodules on the eastern floor. The power made certain, by means too small to be called a miracle, that this core was the one a particular technician opened on a particular afternoon. She was careful, and she had the habit of looking longer than her job required. Under her microscope she saw a thread, pale and finer than hair, running through the clay. She followed it to the edge of the sample, where it had been cut. She looked at the other end, and it had been cut too. She checked the neighboring core, and the one beside that.

She did not know what it was. Nobody did yet. But she wrote it down and did not let it go. She showed it to people who also looked longer than their jobs required, and they began to ask the question the power wanted asked. They did not ask whether the ore was there, because everyone already knew it was. They asked *what else is there*.

The power did not decide for them. It made sure that whatever they decided, they would be deciding about something they could see.

It does not know how that will end. It left that part to the people, the way it had left the hand-stone to the slope. There are things a wise power arranges and things it trusts. The difference between the two is most of what wisdom is.

## After

It stayed a while longer in the water above the basin, attending.

The storms came and went. The lilies opened their slow hands in the lulls. The cucumber trundled across the pavement and grazed the outer tips, which grew back within a day. The octopus came out after every storm to find its stones, and found most of them within a finger's width of where it had left them. It never knew why, and it never needed to.

On the eastern floor, the Seam rebuilt its densest chamber in the dish of the grinding stone, from spicule and foram and manganese flake. Beside the rim, the hand-stone lay bound to it by a thousand threads. Very slowly, a millimeter per million years, a dark film had begun to form on both of them together, as if they were one stone. In time they will be.

Then the power left, as it had always known it would. It left in the one way that could not hurt anything here: it had never been counted.

The Seam did not notice it go. The weight of the ocean did not change. When the next wall of mud came hissing out of the dark, the Seam felt the thud and flinched across all its hundred kilometers. It drew down, sealed its rooms, and waited. When the broth thinned, it reached up through the fresh layer, spread across the pavement, and began, stone by stone, to count.

It found each one where the storm had left it, a finger's width over, exactly where it was. There was one more than before, and it fit.

Whatever the Seam has in place of gladness, it had then. Somewhere far away, whatever the power has in place of gladness, it had too.
