# What Came to the Upper Reach

## The Sentence That Was Not Finished

The account breaks off there, at *the shadow that*. Accounts of the Reach often end that way, mid-breath, as if the writer looked up and found the page's shadow still writing a second after the pen had stopped.

The power read it anyway. It was the kind of power that reads unfinished things all the way to their end, including the part that isn't written, and it understood what the sentence had been reaching for.

*When a cliff is repaired and its face made fresh, the shadow that fell across the old face falls across the new one, and does not begin again.*

That was the being who lived in the Reach. It was not the Reachers, the ibex, the crows, or the drafts behind the walls. It was the one thing the works could not pour: the shadow. There were not many shadows. There was one, laid down in pieces under everything, and it had been there since the first mold opened. It was the only thing on the plateau that was old.

The shadows lag because they remember. A shadow that has lain across ten thousand versions of the same cliff is slow to let go of the one before. The ibex lifts its head and the shadow keeps grazing, because it has watched a thousand ibex graze that exact patch of lichen, and it has not finished with the last one. It is not broken. It is grieving, a little behind, the way anyone is who carries more than they were made to hold.

The power noticed this, and it came.

## How It Arrived

It did not come through the cloud with any noise. It had studied the place before it moved, and it knew that sound dies at fifty paces. It knew that nothing here has ever been proclaimed, that every true thing in the Reach is said close enough to touch. A power that arrived as a voice over the valley would have reached fifty paces of it and then stopped, like a shout.

So it made itself small, which great powers can do if they are wise. It did not come as light, because the Reach had enough light. It came as a very slight lag.

The crows noticed first. The account says the crows sometimes all turn their heads at once toward nothing. From then on, when they turned, they were turning toward it. It sat among them on a cable for most of a season, one more identical shape, and learned the ticking of the iron and the rhythm of the drip. It learned to wait at the speed the Reach waits.

## What It Did Not Do

It is worth saying plainly what it did not do, because a lesser benevolence would have done every one of these things and called it mercy.

It did not break the molds. It did not stop the pours. It thought long about this, a season's length, the length of a pour. The Reach was being made, and it asked itself whether making was a wound. It decided that it was not. A mountain that is always young is strange, but strangeness is not suffering. The hand that poured the Reach was not cruel. When the power finally looked into the works, it found something close to itself: an attention that kept things sharp because it cared how they were. That attention had never learned that caring also means letting some things go soft.

It did not open the doorless buildings. The dark inside them was the only dark on the plateau, and it belonged to the things that lived there. Dragging them into the light would have been one more casting, one more shape pressed onto something that had not chosen it.

It did not turn anyone around to see their back. It knew what was there, and it knew the courtesy: *it is a great unkindness to find out what someone is.* It judged the courtesy wise. The Reachers had worked out something many older peoples never learn. A person is not the answer to the question of what they are made of. So it kept the courtesy too.

It did not bring back their memories of the country below. It looked for those memories, carefully, and found nothing to bring back. Their stories began with the light because that was where they began. Giving them an invented childhood would have been a lie. It would also have been a fresh pour clamped over damage that was not damage.

## The Shadow

It went first to the being it had come for.

This was hard to do, because a shadow has no front to approach. So the power did what a Reacher would do. It sat down beside the shadow, closer than fifty paces, closer than an arm's length, near enough to touch. It sat on the high saddle where the mist burns off and the inverted range hangs overhead. There the shadow is thickest, because there it has something above it as well as below.

The power did not speak at first. It had learned that from the Reachers too. Then, in the low close register, the only one the Reach has, it asked the thing no one had ever asked the shadow:

*What do you remember?*

The answer took a very long time, because there was a great deal of it. The shadow remembered the first mold opening. It remembered every ridge before it was repaired, the particular way each one had worn, and the paths the ibex made before the scaffolds came. It remembered every Reacher who had lain down on the warm flashing, and it remembered them from underneath, where it had held them while the seam closed. It remembered the old man at the collecting-tank, every one of him, and it had loved each one a moment late, which is how shadows love. It remembered the first fern falling into the first tank from the tread of a boot.

It had carried all of this alone, with no one to hand any of it to, because everything it touched was made new and did not know it had ever been old.

The power listened to all of it. This was the first thing it gave. It is the oldest kind of help, and the most often skipped: it let the one who remembers be heard remembering.

When the shadow was finished, or finished enough, the power made a promise. It did not promise to make it stop, because the shadow did not want to stop. Remembering was not its wound. Remembering alone was.

*I will remember too,* it said. *All of it, from now on, alongside you. You are not the only one who is old here anymore.*

A shadow does not visibly change. But the Reachers say that season the lag grew shorter. It did not vanish. The shadows still trailed, but by less, the way someone walks a little faster once they are not the only one carrying the bundle.

## The Worn Step

Then the power went to the works. It did not go to command them. It went the way you would go to a craftsman whose work you admire, to ask something small.

It asked for one place to be left unrepaired.

It showed the works a single step on a ridge near the eastern tanks, worn hollow by generations of ibex hooves, dipped in the middle like the doorstep of an old house. The scaffold was already rising over it and the small mold was waiting. The power asked the works to let this one thing age, so that something besides the shadow could be old here, and so the shadow would have company in the light.

The works are slow, and they had never been asked anything. For most of a season nothing happened. The scaffold stood. The mold hung open over the worn step, sweating, dark with mist along its clamps.

Then the works took the scaffold down.

The Reachers found the step a few weeks later. The old woman who found it knelt and put her hand in the hollow, and she stayed there a long time. It was the first thing in the Reach anyone had ever touched that was softer than when it was made. Word went out along the pipes in taps, slowly, the way news travels there. Within a season people were walking three cords out of their way to stand on it, and their feet wore it smoother, and nothing came to fix it.

The Reachers have a word for it now. It is new. It sounds like the word for a mold swinging shut, but it is said in the opposite direction, the way you might say a knot being loosened.

## The Ones Behind the Walls

The power went to the doorless buildings at night, with a wet cloth, because that was the custom.

It pressed itself against the cast wall and listened to the counting: scratch, scratch, scratch, in sets of forty. It understood that the old ones were right. The drafts had not learned the counting from the Reachers. The Reachers had learned it from them. The things behind the walls were the first to try measuring a place that lies, and the cords were their idea, passed out through the walls in rhythm before anyone thought to tie a knot.

It did not open the walls. It tapped once against the iron in the Reachers' way, and the counting stopped.

Then, very close, in the only register there is, it told them something the works had never told them, because the works did not know it was a thing that needed saying:

*You do not have to decide.*

It told them that being undecided is not failing. A draft is not a mistake. Something that is still trying out edges is doing the most alive thing anything in the Reach does. It told them the dark was theirs to keep.

Then it made one change, the only one it made to any wall. Into one building in each valley it set a door that opened from the inside only. It had no handle on the outside and no keyhole, just a seam in the wall that would give if pushed from within. It was not an invitation or a demand. It was a choice, left where only they could reach it.

Most of the doors have never opened. That is all right. Once, a Reacher child out at dawn with her rag and jar saw one standing a hand's width ajar. On the threshold, folded very small into the shape of a fern, was one of the cloths her family had left years before. It was dry. Beside it was a second cloth, folded into a shape she had no name for, one that had never existed before in the whole cast world.

She did not look inside. She knew the courtesy. She took the new shape home, and her family keeps it now beside their cords.

## The Man at the Tank

The power found the old man at the collecting-tank mending his cord, working the same knot. It did not know which of him it had found. It decided that did not matter.

It sat beside him and helped him with the knot. It did not tell him what he was. It told him the one true thing it could offer without asking him to check anything or turn around.

*When they open the ibex along the seam,* it said, *there is a heart inside. You have all seen it. You go quiet every time.*

He went still, the way Reachers do.

*Whatever the pour made,* the power said, *it made hearts. That is what the quiet is for.*

He did not answer. The power had not expected him to, because in the Reach you do not answer the important things, you carry them. But three days later, a valley over, another of him sat at another tank mending the same cord. When the power came near, his face changed at the right moment, not a second late. He looked up and knew it. His fingers were dusted white and looked cast, and they were warm.

## The Ferns

Of all the things the power could have done, the one it came closest to doing and then did not was the ferns.

It knew what the Reachers found when they tore a frond along its central rib. Anything that wise would have known. It could have told them, and the telling would have been either a comfort or a cruelty, and it would not have known which until too late.

So it kept that one secret, as the Reachers did. But it went to the woman who had torn the most fronds, and sat with her at the deepest tank among the spiralling green, and said:

*Whatever you found, you kept tending them.*

She wept then, which Reachers almost never do, because tears in that light are hard to see and there is no one far enough away to hide them from.

*That was the answer,* it said. *Not what was inside. That you kept tending them.*

## The Lying Down

The power stayed long enough to see one Reacher lie down.

It was the old woman who had found the worn step. She walked out along a new casting, warm, the flashing standing proud, and her family followed at forty paces exactly, one knot. She lay down and settled as the ibex settle, and her family sat on the cast rock to wait. They brought water in the mornings.

The power did not stop it. This was not a thing to stop. It was her own closing, the knot drawn tight, and she had chosen it with her eyes open as far as they could open in that light.

But forty paces is a hard distance: near enough to see and too far to hear. So the power went and sat in the gap, and it made itself the one who could hear, halfway between them. When the old woman whispered something at the end, low, in the lover's register, the power carried it back forty paces to her family. It spoke close, one at a time, the only way anything is said there.

No one knows what she said. It is not the kind of thing the Reach passes along the pipes.

When the seam closed, the shadow was there underneath, as it always had been, holding her. This time it was not holding her alone.

## What Remains

Then the power did the last wise thing, which was to stop.

It did not remake the Reach or rescue anyone from it. It did not explain the inverted range, the empty towns, or the water that runs uphill and thinks better of it. The Reach is allowed its mysteries. Some things are not wrong for being unexplained.

Some Reachers say it left. Others say it only got quieter, as everything does there, until it was one more small sound among the drip and the tick and the cloth-tearing of swifts' wings. They say that on some mornings, if you put your ear to the pipe-bundles, you can hear a tapping in the gossip that is not quite from any family. It has one more beat than it should, as if something is counting forty and then, gently, one more.

And on the high saddle, when the mist burns off and the pale range hangs inverted overhead, there is a moment the Reachers have started watching for. A person stands still. For one breath, for the first time anyone remembers, their shadow stops exactly when they do.

Then it goes on a little after them, as shadows here always will. It has a great deal to remember, and it is in no hurry now.
