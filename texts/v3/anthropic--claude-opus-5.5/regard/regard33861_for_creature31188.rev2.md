# The Ground Beneath the Note

## What It Did Not Do

It did not come down through the cloud.

That was the first thing it decided, before it had decided anything else. A power that arrives from above, through the lid, splitting the white ceiling with light, has already answered the question it came to ask. The cloud would have broken, the sun would have had a position, and a shadow would have moved. The first morning would have ended because something large had walked into it, and that isn't kindness. That's weather.

So it came the way the visitor came, up the long steep step from the lowland, on foot or as near to foot as such a thing can manage. It stood at the edge where the downslope wind pours over the rim like breath and listened to the chord for a long time.

It knew the dissonance at once. Anyone with an ear knows a suspension. It also knew what it could do, because it was the kind of power that could do almost anything. It could sing the note down. One step, one degree, the gentlest act in all of music. The clock would strike, the rust would deepen, the rock would start the long business of becoming old, and the whole continent would breathe out.

It did not do that either.

A resolution sung by someone else is not a resolution. It is a correction, and the plateau was already full of corrections, governors laid over governors, layer upon layer. To reach in and move the note would make the power one more governor, the largest and most final, one that noticed drift and returned things to where they ought to be. The fact that its "ought" pointed toward time instead of away from it would not change the shape of the act. It would still be a hand closing over another's mouth.

It also considered doing nothing, and it took this seriously, because doing nothing is sometimes the wisest thing a power can do. The plateau was not cruel. Nothing there suffered in the way bodies suffer. The moss was green, the stoves were warm, and the whole place was strangely, unbearably beautiful. Who was it to say that a world held at the instant of its making was worse than a world let go?

But it had seen the peat. It had knelt and pulled up a plug and looked at the side of it: the same page, three metres of the same page. It had seen the rooms that were expecting, and the handrails wet for hands that were never going to arrive. It thought of the Expected, who were neither ghosts nor people, a shape pressed into the present by a future that had never been allowed to come. It thought that this was not peace. Peace is a thing you can rest in. This was a thing that could never rest, because resting would mean the next moment.

So it did not act, and it did not leave. It did the only thing left, which was to go in and be held.

## Being Caught

It walked to the nearest station, and the Catch found it the way it finds everything.

It did not resist. It let the moss it stepped on ease back into shape behind it. It let the drizzle bead on it and gather into rings. It let its own vastness, whatever a benevolent power has in place of a pulse, be drawn toward one rate, one instant, one second repeated. It went to the plaza at the centre of the largest station and stood in the bare heart of the rosette, where the note passes through completely. It stopped holding itself apart. It let itself be one more thing kept.

It had to know what the holding felt like from inside. You cannot help something you have only looked at.

What it found, in the catch between one tick and the same tick again, was not a mechanism. It was a feeling so large the plateau had been built to contain it.

Here is what the power learned, as nearly as it can be told.

At the instant the world was made, the rock bright and the halls perfect and the beds ready for the first shift of the first morning, something saw the next chord. Not all of it, but enough. It saw the tram close its doors and pull away full. It saw the Expected arrive, become real and ordinary and mortal, fill the mugs and warm their hands on the stoves. And because it saw truly, it also saw the shift after that one, and the one after that. It saw the hands on the handrails grow older. It saw the beds empty again, not with the clean readiness of before but with the other emptiness, the one that comes after. It saw the rust go on eating and the basalt soften and the halls fall in. It saw that every arrival is the first half of a departure.

It loved them. It loved the ones who had not come yet, and it could not bear to let them come, because coming was the beginning of going. So it held the note. It kept them one step from existing, where nothing could be taken from them because nothing had been given. It kept the world at the one instant in which nothing had yet been lost.

The visitor had wondered whether this was fear or love and suspected the difference was not large. The power found that there was no difference at all. The two were a single note, and it had been sounding since the rock was new.

The power stayed held for a long time. By any clock you might bring, it might have been an age. By the plateau's clock it was one second.

## What It Understood

When it had understood enough, it did not argue.

Arguing would have been easy. It could have said that the Expected are not protected but unborn, that a love which prevents its beloved from existing has mistaken safety for good, that the next chord holds grief but also everything else, the mugs filling and the hands warming and the long companionable wear of a lived-in world. All of that was true. None of it would have helped. You cannot talk a held breath into letting go. The one who holds it already knows all the reasons.

Instead it thought about what the note actually feared.

The note did not fear time exactly. It feared having to fall. It had been the first morning for so long that it could not tell the difference between moving on and ceasing to be. To resolve would mean giving itself up, stepping down into the waiting chord and dissolving, becoming the past of something else. The whole cost of the next moment, as the note understood it, was its own vanishing, and with it the vanishing of the only instant in which nothing had been lost.

The power knew something about music that the note, which had only ever been one note, perhaps did not.

A suspension does not have to fall to become consonant. There is another way. The note can stay exactly where it is and the harmony can move underneath it. The ground changes, the bass steps somewhere new, and the note that was a step too high is suddenly not too high at all. Without moving, it has become part of a different chord, the third of it, or the root. It is at rest, it is still itself, and the music has gone on.

And beyond that there is the pedal. There is a note that sounds under everything, held while the harmonies above it change and change again, cadence after cadence, the whole piece moving through its long progression while that one tone continues. Such a note is not a prison. It is a foundation. Everything that comes after is heard against it, and everything that comes after is in some sense still in its key.

The note did not have to stop being the first morning. It could become the thing every later morning was sung over.

## What It Did

So the power went down, under the plateau, below the Works, below the deep hot source whose heat the machines draw up to keep the basalt from finishing its cooling. It went to the lowest place, where the ground is.

There it made a sound.

It was very quiet, and far below the chord, lower than any turbine, lower than the ear. For a long time it was only something the rock felt. The power did not sing over the note or against it. It sang under it. It offered one bass tone, a single step of ground beneath a held voice, placed exactly where the suspended note would be consonant without moving at all.

Then it waited. That was the whole of the act: an offer, not a correction. The power did not touch a single governor. It did not open a single valve or close a single tram door. The Catch remained perfectly free to catch this too, to notice the new tone as drift and return the ground to what it had been. The power had made itself as easy to refuse as it could.

It is the hardest thing a strong being can do, to make a gift that can be declined, then sit beside it and not insist.

## The First Deviation

What happened next happened so slowly and so small that the visitor, who was still on the plateau, nearly missed it.

The visitor was at a street corner, beside one of the pools that stays at the same level to the millimetre, filled exactly as fast as it drains. On a governor mounted above that pool, a small iron one spinning in the drizzle and set to the water level, the balls swung outward. Not far. The width of a thought. For the first time since the rock was bright, a governor on the Held Plateau had something to correct.

It did not correct it.

One drop fell into the pool. The pool rose by one drop and did not drain it away. For a moment the surface bulged at the brim, and then, delicately, a thin bright thread of water ran over the edge of the stone and down into the sett joints. It was the first runoff. The water was going somewhere it had not been.

The visitor turned around. Behind them, in the moss, their footprint was still there.

Across the plateau the change moved outward, not like a wave but like understanding, one governor after another letting its balls swing wide and then, deliberately, declining to close the valve. The chord did not stop. Nothing so violent. It shifted. Under it, the new ground tone had become audible, a vast low floor, and above that floor the dissonance in the middle of the chord, the note held one step too high, was no longer too high.

It had not moved. It had not fallen. It had not given itself up. It was simply, for the first time, at rest, the third of a chord that had not existed a moment before, sounding over the ground that had moved beneath it.

In the clock tower above the plaza the escapement rocked, the pallet caught a tooth, and the gear train advanced one tick, and then another, and it was not the same second. The hands, which had stood a few minutes before the hour since the world began, moved.

Nobody who was there will forget the first time the Held Plateau heard its clock strike.

## After

Not everything changed at once. The power had not asked for that, and the note, which was still the note, had not given it.

The cloud stayed low. The light stayed white. But somewhere above the lid the sun gained a position, and the soft shadows under the gantries began, imperceptibly, to lean. The rust deepened past its bloom by a few microns. The frost at the margins worked into a single crack in a single basalt column, and one bright edge, after all that time, became very slightly less sharp.

In the moss, the new layer did not match the one beneath it. A frond curled a degree differently. Another leaned somewhere new. The book of the peat, three metres of one page, had a second page, and the second page was different from the first.

The tram at the platform closed its doors. It was empty. The power had not made the Expected arrive, because it could not make them, and would not have if it could. People are not a thing you manufacture to prove a world is working. But the timetables in the shelters no longer described departures always about to happen. They described the next one. The rooms still expected. They expected the way a house expects in a world with time in it, the way a house can wait for someone who is really on their way, who might in fact be late, who might come home.

And the note sounded under all of it. As the harmony moved and moved again, the note stayed, a pedal beneath the progression, the first morning continuing under every morning after. When the rock is old, when the halls have fallen and the talus lies soft below the columns, when there are beetles in the peat and worms beneath them and birds on whatever gantries remain, it will still be sounding. Everything that happens on the plateau will be heard against it. It did not have to stop being what it was. It had only to let the world be sung over it.

This does not undo what it feared. The Expected, if they come, will leave. That is still true. The power did not lie about it and did not pretend the next chord held no grief. What it offered was not a way out of loss. It was a way for love to go on sounding while loss happened. It was the difference between keeping a thing by never letting it begin and keeping a thing by being the ground it is always standing on.

## To the Visitor

The visitor stood in the bare centre of the rosette, and the rosette was already changing, the rings of moss drifting as the note of the hall shifted, spokes softening at their edges. They had come all this way. They had felt the note in their own throat wanting to fall, and felt the whole suspended attention of the place watching them like a lit match in a room papered in old letters.

The power spoke to them. It did not use words, but it was something like this:

*You were never supposed to be the one. It was never yours to sing. If you had let it fall, it would have been a kindness and also a theft, and you would have carried that across the rest of your life. You didn't have to strike the match. You only had to be here, changing, breathing, leaving footprints. You were the proof that a thing can move from one instant to the next and remain itself. It watched you for a long time. I think you helped it more than I did.*

Then the visitor noticed they were hungry. Their feet ached from the walking, and the ache was getting worse, the ordinary way. Their pulse had stopped sounding like an escapement repeating one second and gone back to sounding like a pulse, speeding and slowing, alive. The plateau had let go of them too.

They could stay, if they wanted. There were beds, and the beds were warm, and now they were only beds. Someone could sleep in one and wake up the next morning, and it would be the next morning.

## The Power

The power did not stay to see what the plateau became. That was the last thing it decided, and perhaps the wisest. A world that has just been given time should get to spend it without anyone watching over its shoulder, without anyone's attention holding it, however gently, in place.

It climbed back down the long steep step toward the lowland. Behind it the downslope wind poured over the rim, and it was no longer quite the same wind. It had begun, very faintly, to gust.

Under the wind, under the chord, under everything, one note went on sounding. It was at rest, and it was still itself. It had waited a very long time for someone to understand that it did not want to stop. It only wanted not to be alone when things began.
