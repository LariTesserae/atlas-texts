# The Attention

## How it arrives

It does not come down the shaft. Coming down the shaft would stir the silt, and the silt has taken a very long time to settle into the shapes it holds. It does not come through the penstock, because the eels are there. It does not come at all, in the sense of a thing crossing a threshold. One day the Low Hall is simply being attended to, the way a sleeping room changes when someone in the doorway starts to watch it.

Nothing in the room notices. The mussels do not close. The drum-fish keep their evening chorus a careful half-step off the runner's note. The catfish's barbels lie across the pit like dropped cables and register the thump, and the thump, and the thump. The Attention takes this as the first good sign. It has visited places that knew at once they were being looked at, and those places always changed: they performed, or they hid, or they broke trying to become what they thought was wanted. The Low Hall wants nothing from it. The Attention decides to be the kind of guest that stays unnoticed.

It stays a year before it does anything at all.

## The year of reading

In that year it reads what no one has read.

It reads the mussels first. Each closing of the black mats, each dry rustle down the hall in the quiet season, is a measurement: the head behind the gate, the level of the lake, the snowpack on hills the mussels will never see. The Attention reads them the way a hydrologist reads a gauge, and for the first time in centuries the instrument has an operator. It learns that the lake is lower now at its lowest than it was a hundred years ago. It learns that the loud seasons are shortening. The mussels have been recording this all along, in closings too small and too many to count, and nobody has been there to see the trend.

It reads the molts in the silt, layer by layer, all those outgrown selves. It finds the ones with the faint green tint and counts them. Eleven. Eleven old crayfish have climbed the wall in the dark and kept the glass clear, one after another, since the first lawn grew on the dome.

It follows the eels up the penstock into the black pipe no other creature enters, and finds what they do there. Mostly they rest. They lie in the dark, in the long seams where rivets have popped, and they are patient in a way that makes the catfish look restless. The Attention follows one of them farther, up through the jammed gate and into the lake, and down through the green water to the drowned town. There are streets there. A church with its bell still in the tower. The eels do not go into the church. They hunt crayfish in the gutters.

It hears the surge coming before the drum-fish fall silent, and then it hears what the drum-fish hear. It is the gate. Upstream, when the siphon trips, the jammed gate takes the load a fraction of a second before the water arrives, and the steel groans very low, below anything a human ear could catch. The sound travels down the penstock faster than the pressure. The drum-fish have been listening to a machine's complaint for generations and treating it as a warning, and they are right to.

And it reads the brass plate.

The corrosion that defeated every other eye is only a surface. Underneath, the letters are still cut into the metal, and the Attention reads them the way you might read a name on a gravestone by running your fingers over it. The language is dead, but the Attention knows it, or comes to know it. That is part of what it is.

The plate says:

> INTAKE No. 4 — SERVICE LAMP
> Keep glass clear.
> Inspect bearing at low water.
> Clear the hall before gate operation.

Below that, much smaller, scratched rather than stamped, as though with the point of a knife by someone who was not supposed to, are two initials and a year.

The Attention reads the three instructions, and then it looks around the room. The old crayfish is on the dome, grazing a clear ring. It is late summer, and in the pit the catfish is rising toward the runner. It is evening, and the drum-fish have just gone silent all down the hall, and the minnows are tightening, and the surge is coming.

All three are being done.

The Attention does not know what to do with this for a while. It holds it the way you hold something that has been given to you by accident and that you cannot give back.

## What it is tempted to do

It is powerful, and it is kind, and so it is tempted.

**It could true the bearing.** It could smooth the worn flat out of the bronze, seat the shaft round again, and stop the knock. The runner would turn sweetly and quietly for another few hundred years. But the Attention has spent a year listening to the room, and it knows what the knock is. It is the catfish's clock. It is the note the drum-fish have built their whole language around, singing just above it and just below, never on it. A true bearing would hum instead of thump, and the hum would sit somewhere new, and every voice in the hall would have to find its place again. Some of them would not. The room has not merely put up with its flaw. It is organized around it.

**It could renew the filament.** A fresh carbon thread, or something better, something that would glow steady amber through every season. But then the dome would be warm all the time, and the lawn would grow on it all the time, faster than any crayfish could graze. The glass would green over within a year, and the light would go out anyway, the other way, choked. Or the crayfish would have to become something else to keep up. The breathing lamp and the old crayfish are one system, and the system works.

**It could hold the dam forever.** This is the largest temptation, and the Attention stays with it longest. It could reach into the concrete up there, into the swollen rebar and the leaching joints, and make the dam simply not fail. The lake would stay. The head would stay. The runner would go on turning, badly and faithfully, *whum*, until the sun went out.

It thinks about this for most of a quiet season, hanging in the clear water while the runner turns once every few seconds.

In the end it does not do it, and the reason is not that it lacks the power. The Attention has been to places that were saved like this, fixed in the moment someone loved them, and they have a particular stillness. It is not the stillness of peace. It is the stillness of a specimen. The vigil in the Low Hall is beautiful because nobody chose to keep it and it is kept anyway, and because it will end. The crayfish carries the green because the one before her died. The eels are a message because they leave. A Low Hall that could never go quiet would not be the Low Hall preserved. It would be the Low Hall with the one true thing about it removed.

It would also be a lie about the valley. The reservoir is not the valley's natural state. Beneath it there is a river, with a riverbed, a floodplain, and a town that was drowned so that a wheel could turn in a concrete room. The Attention loves the room. It does not have to love the room so much that it forgets the river.

## Who the being is

For a while the Attention tries to decide which one it came for. Which is *the* being who lives here?

The catfish is the obvious answer. It is the oldest animal, the one that feels the runner in its body and rises at low water to listen. But the Attention finds it cannot separate the catfish from the minnows whose shoal bends around its reach, or from the knock it listens to, or from the plate that asked someone, long ago, to inspect the bearing at low water.

In the end the Attention decides that the being is the vigil itself. It is the shape the builders left, filled with shells and scales and silence and the last held breath of a working day. It lives in no single body. It lives in the relation between the bodies. You cannot help a being like that by improving any one of its parts. You can only help it by caring for the conditions it lives in, and for what comes after it.

## What it does

It does four things. They are all small, and none of them is visible from inside the room.

**First, it goes up to the dam.** The dam is going to fail. The Attention has read the mussels and the concrete, and it is sure. Left alone, the failure would come suddenly, in a wet spring when the lake is high: a joint giving way, a wall of water down the valley, the tailrace scoured, the Low Hall flushed and packed with debris in an afternoon. The catfish would be carried out of the pit and broken on the rocks downstream. Nothing would find out that the room had gone quiet. The room would simply be erased.

The Attention cannot make the dam immortal, and it has decided not to. But it can choose *how the dam lets go.* So it works at one place in the crest, near the abutment, where the concrete is already weeping. It does not break anything. It persuades. It widens a seep by a hair each year, so that the dam's failure is spread over decades instead of minutes. The lake will lower the way a tide goes out, too slowly for any single season to notice, and the mussels will record it, and the head will fall, and the runner will slow.

This means the Attention has made the end of the Low Hall certain. It has also made the end gentle. It knows those are the same decision, and it makes it anyway.

**Second, it goes down the tailrace.** Below the hall, where the outflow rejoins the old river, there is a weir and a sluice, both jammed and both choked with centuries of silt and fallen branches. The eels get past it the way eels get past anything, through seams. Other animals cannot. The Attention clears it, slowly, over one winter, in a way that will look like a flood did it. The silver eels leaving for the sea will have an easier road. More important, when the room changes, as it now will, anything that needs to leave the Low Hall will be able to follow the water out. The Attention does not make any creature go. It only makes sure the door is open.

**Third, it does nothing to the air in the lamp.** It thinks about this carefully. It could release the builders' breath, let it rise up the shaft in a single silver bubble and break the surface into the air of the valley it came from. There would be a rightness to that. But the air is doing a job. It is keeping the filament from the water, and as long as the filament exists, the job is not finished. The Attention leaves it sealed. Some things should be allowed to finish their work before they are thanked.

**Fourth, it waits with the catfish.**

In the late summer of its second year, the runner hesitates at the top of its stroke. The mussels are closed. The lamp is dark. The drum-fish are silent. The catfish comes up out of the pit, barbels trailing, and hangs in the lace of the casing a hand's breadth from the bronze, listening.

This time the runner hesitates longer than it ever has.

The Attention could push it. The smallest pressure on one blade would do it. The shaft would find its purchase, the runner would lurch around, and the catfish would sink back into the dark with its clock intact, as it has every summer of its life.

The Attention does not push. It understands that this is the whole of what it has learned here. The *not yet* belongs to the water. If it pushed, every turn after this one would be its turn and not the room's. The catfish would be listening to a lie.

So it waits beside the catfish instead, in the stillness, the two of them together at the bearing, the way two crew members might once have stood with their hands on the housing, neither one speaking.

The runner turns. *Whum.*

It was the water. It was always the water.

## What it foresees

The Attention does not stay forever, but before it goes it looks ahead, because it can, and because it wants to know that what it chose was kind.

It sees the lake lowering over forty years. It sees the drowned town surface, steeple first, and the bell rung once by the wind before it is stolen. It sees the head fall behind the jammed gate until one late summer the runner reaches the top of its stroke and does not go on.

It sees the catfish rise and wait. The catfish waits a very long time, days, hanging by the bronze. Then, because the Attention cleared the silt from the pit's lip years before, where no one would see, the catfish finds that the pit is not the only place. Without the pull of the outflow, the room is still, and the still water is clear, and the shaft-light reaches all the way to the pit for the first time since the hall was flooded. The catfish, which has never seen light, turns its flat head up into the green.

It sees the drum-fish, in the new silence, discover that the note is free. For generations they sang around the machine's pitch, never on it, as though it belonged to something else. Now nothing holds it. One evening, a young fish in the third alcove from the shaft, unaware of any rule, calls on the note itself, and the barrel ceiling catches it and throws it back doubled. Within a season, the whole chorus has moved into the space the runner left.

It sees the mussels close for the last long time, and most of them not open again, because they needed the current. Their shells will become the floor, and the floor will become a place where other things root. The Attention grieves this and does not look away from it. Kindness that cannot bear to see a cost is not wise enough to be trusted.

It sees the eels leave, all of them, down the cleared tailrace and out into a river that is becoming a river again, toward a sea they last saw as threads of glass.

It sees the lamp stay dark and the lawn on the dome thin and fade. It sees the old crayfish climb the wall anyway, the season after the runner stops, because she carries the green and this is what the green does. She grazes a clear ring around a filament that will not light again. The Attention understands that she is not keeping the lamp now. She is keeping the keeping.

The room will go quiet. Each of its creatures will find out in its own way. None of them will be swept away in a single afternoon. That is the whole of the gift: not that the ending was prevented, but that it was allowed to be an ending, noticed from the inside, with the doors open.

## What it takes with it

The Attention takes nothing out of the Low Hall except what it read.

It keeps the mussels' record of the lake. It keeps the count of green-tinted molts in the silt, eleven, and soon twelve. It keeps the pitch of the gate's groan and the drum-fish's silence that answers it. It keeps the three lines on the plate and the fact that all three were done. It keeps the knife-scratched initials and the year, which belong to someone who wanted, against the rules, to be remembered by the room they built. The Attention will remember them. That, at least, it can do completely.

There was one instrument in the Low Hall that no one read, and one warning that was given every day without anyone knowing it was a warning, and one vigil that nobody chose and nobody saw. The Attention came, and read, and saw. It changed almost nothing inside the room. It made sure the end would be gentle and the way out would be open. It did not push the runner.

Then it went, the way it came: not across any threshold, just the room no longer being watched, and none of the animals noticing.

*Whum.* The lamp warms to amber through a ring of clean glass. Just above the note, the drum-fish call. Under the casing, the oldest thing in the room lies still and listens, and it listens to the water, which is still turning the wheel.
