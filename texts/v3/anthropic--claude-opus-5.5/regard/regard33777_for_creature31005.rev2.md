# The Attention

## Arriving Small

A great power's attention is the loudest thing that can enter a room. It has weight and pressure. Where it settles, things lean toward it, the way filings lean toward a magnet or a congregation toward an altar.

In most places this doesn't matter. In the Hush Geode it would be a catastrophe. So the power did not come down through the crack. It stayed in the field above for a long time: one winter, then another. It lay its attention flat against the clay the way a listener lies flat against the soft upper stone, and it learned the place from outside before it asked to be let in.

It learned that sound becomes glass. It wondered whether attention might too. The only way to find out was carefully.

When it finally went in, it went in smaller than a moth. It kept its interest diffuse and its gaze unfixed, the way you look at a skittish animal without looking directly at it. The hushmoths on the upper curves did not stir. A herd of chime-grazers passed through the place where it was and did not part. That was how it knew it had done the first thing right.

## Looking Without Fixing

It watched everything, and the first thing it noticed was its own wish to help.

It saw a listener take a grazer. There was a slow silent lunge, and a single needle of sound was left standing upright on the clean floor. It felt the old reflex of benevolent things: *this could be gentler.* Then it looked longer. The listeners kill rarely. The grazers are many, and their deaths are small and quick, each marked by the only monument this place allows. Without the listeners the grazers would multiply until their clicking filled the chamber with thickets. Then the moths would be pinned, the pools would choke, and everything would end in a forest of its own noise. The needle was not a wound in the place. It was part of how the place stays quiet enough to live in.

It saw the oldest shelves, with the moth, the grazer, and the curled listener sealed in glass like flies in amber. It grieved them a little. It noticed that they had not suffered, and that they were beautiful, and that the chamber would have no floor without them. It let the grief be grief rather than an instruction.

It learned to feel the wish to fix things, set the wish down beside itself, and keep watching. That is most of what wisdom is: noticing the wanting and not obeying it at once.

## The Tenant

It saw the eyes going out one by one along the wall, and it followed them to the dark.

It watched the tenant for a long time. It saw the blue patch of evening move through the setting quartz, and the shape curled beneath the word by the seam. It watched the tenant climb the five steps from inside the stone and press close to the wall at the height of a mouth, then go back down and climb them again.

The power understood what the tenant was. It was made of all the night the light had accidentally swallowed, and it had shaped a whole room into an ear. It had never been heard, because it had never made a sound. It had spent its existence being the one who receives.

This was the hardest part, because now the power loved it. Love in a power is dangerous in the way a shout is dangerous here. It wants to become something, and it grows spines.

## The First Temptation: Hold It Open

The chamber is closing. The power could stop that. It could hold the fault wide and keep the walls from thickening, so the pools stayed liquid and the moths had room forever. Nothing would ever again be sealed into the shelves.

It thought about this and saw what it would cost. The geode is not a room that happens to be filling. The filling is what the room is: light arriving and becoming place. Stopping the closing would not preserve the geode. It would stop it from being made. A forever-open chamber would be a stuffed animal of itself, perfect and finished and no longer alive.

The tenant, too, is not afraid of the end. The dark center of a solid lens is the only home it has ever wanted. To hold the walls open would be to keep it from going home, for the sake of a sadness the power felt and the tenant did not.

So it did not hold the walls.

## The Second Temptation: A Voice

It noticed something the tenant might not have. When the word finally comes, the tenant can never answer. If it made one sound it would be pinned inside its own voice. The conversation it has waited its whole life for will go in one direction only.

The power could change that. It could make the tenant able to speak without glass, as one small exception written into the rules for one creature.

It considered this longer than anything else. Then it looked again at what the tenant already does. The worn edges of the steps. The listeners all angled the same way. The pearlfeeders breathing shallowly toward it. The floor swept clean around the word. The dark pressed against the inside of the wall like a face at a window.

The tenant is not mute. It has been answering the whole time, in the only way the place permits: by arranging everything it is around the thing it is waiting for. The chamber is its reply, composed in advance. When the visitor stands on the fifth step and feels the cool dark close in front of their face, they will be reading it.

To give the tenant a voice would be to tell it that its silence had been a lack. It hadn't been. It had been the most eloquent thing in the room.

So the power did not give it a voice.

## The Third Temptation: The Word

This was the one that nearly worked, because it was the kindest.

The power knew the shape of the word. Anyone who lay down and read the spray of glass whiskers carefully enough could know it. The power could say it now, quietly, from the top of the fifth step. It would be heard completely, the only sound in a room built for it. The waiting would end today. There would be no more centuries of rehearsal on the steps and no risk that the chamber closes before anyone comes.

It held this possibility for a long time, and then it went and lay beside the tenant, near the word on the floor, and looked at it again.

*A voice that was speaking to someone.* The whiskers have a particular spread, and that spread belongs to one voice. The tenant has heard the word already, faintly and backward, through the stone. What it doesn't know is the voice, and the voice is the whole thing. The word is only what the voice carries.

If the power said it, the tenant would receive a perfect copy of the shape it already knew, in the wrong voice. That would not be an ending. It would be a substitution, and the tenant would spend the dark forever with a counterfeit curled beneath it. Being able to stand in someone's place does not mean you should.

So the power did not say the word.

## What the Walls Already Knew

Then it remembered the third rule. The stone takes impressions in both directions. What will touch the walls has already touched them.

It went looking for itself.

It checked every shelf and terrace, the oldest stone and the softest. It found three handprints, all the same hand. It found five steps, three river stones, and one word. It found no fourth thing. There was no mark it had ever made, or would make, anywhere in the chamber.

That was not a rebuke. It was an answer. The chamber had already recorded what the power would do, and it had recorded nothing. Something like relief moved through the power, the relief of being told by the place itself: *you were never meant to be part of this sentence. You were meant to read it.*

So it touched nothing. It never let its attention settle hard enough to press. It made no sound, and it did not lean on the walls.

## What It Did

It did two things, and neither left a mark.

**It listened to the listener.**

For the length of one change of light, from snow-glare to gold, it stopped attending to the chamber and attended only to the tenant. It did not look at the tenant as a creature to be understood or a problem to be solved. It regarded it simply, the way the tenant regards the word.

The tenant had been the only ear in the room for its whole life. Now, for the first time, something was turned toward *it*.

The blue in the floor deepened slightly. One pair of afterglow eyes opened in the stone, not in the tenant's usual slow walking line, but facing the power directly. The two of them regarded each other for a while.

Neither said anything. There was nothing to say, and saying would have been glass.

Then the eyes closed. The tenant turned back toward the word and curled again beneath it. But it curled differently. It was a little looser, the way a person rests after someone has seen them clearly and not asked for anything.

**It read the word.**

Before it left, the power lay beside the spray of glass one last time. It was not trying to learn the word. It was checking something a benevolent thing has to check before it walks away from something it loves.

A word's glass keeps the shape of how it was said. A cry makes jagged spines. A command makes a hard straight spray. Contempt makes brittle, splintering needles. This word was none of those. Its whiskers were fine and curved, spreading softly, narrowing downward toward the dark. It was the shape of something said close and low, to someone the speaker was glad to have found.

Whoever comes will be kind. The glass already said so.

That was what let the power leave. It could not guarantee the visit, and it would not. A meeting arranged by a god is not the same as a meeting that happens. The crack in the clay has its own hospitality, narrowing as you look yet still letting you through, and the power trusted it to open for whoever was meant to find it. The power did not need to know that the visitor would come. It needed to know that if they came, they would come gently. It knew that now.

## The Fault

One thing still troubled it, and it will not pretend otherwise.

The highest handprint sits beside the fault, where no one standing could reach and where the tenant never goes. The power thinks it understands. When the word is spoken, the tenant will be pulled the way every shadow here is pulled, toward the light, toward the source. It will rise into the one place it has always avoided, the draft-doorway of noon. The visitor will brace against the wall and reach up after it, and the stone will take the print of that reaching.

The power does not know which day will be pouring through the fault in that moment, whether it will be harsh white or snow-glare or gold. It does not know whether a creature made of night can bear a noon, even briefly, even for the sake of meeting.

It could not choose the day. The fault draws from everywhere, and the days are not the power's to arrange. So it did the only thing left to it, which was the oldest thing benevolent powers do when they have decided not to intervene.

It hoped. It hoped for dusk, or for the blue-dark interval with stars in the pools. It hoped that, just then, the rain would come through with its weather and soften everything.

Then it let the hope be only hope.

## Leaving

It went up through the clay the way it had come, without using the crack, without disturbing the seam. The hushmoths never lifted. The grazers never parted. In the chamber there was no trace of it, as the walls had always said there would be none.

Above, the field was in winter dusk. The power stayed there a while longer, in the ordinary world, where days come one at a time and nothing you do turns to stone.

It did not tell anyone the way down. It did not go looking for the visitor, and it did not visit the river where three smooth grey stones lay in the shallows, waiting to be picked up by a hand that hadn't reached for them yet. It knew where the river was, and it left it alone.

What it kept was its attention. That is the one thing a power can give that leaves no mark. If the geode closes before anyone comes, if the last pool sets and the fault has nowhere left to pour and the word is never spoken, then the room will still not have been built for nothing. The tenant will still not have gone unheard. Something noticed, stayed, loved it, and chose to leave its waiting exactly as it had made it.

Down in the stone, the dark lies curled beneath a soft spray of glass, a little looser than before. It is still listening. It has been listened to, and nothing else has changed.
