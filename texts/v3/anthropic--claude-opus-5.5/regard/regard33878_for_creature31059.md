# The Attention

## I. How it came

It could have come as light. That was the first thing it considered, and the first thing it set aside.

It thought about what light would do here. Every pair of eyes in the Vaulting is built for a darkness so deep that the growing-point stars are enough to steer by. A single moment of real brightness, sun-bright, would leave Ennet and everyone like her blind for days, stranded on a junction with no idea which way was inward. The moths rise on currents and are read by the stars they cover. The skirlers hunt by voice and would not care, but everything that hunts the skirlers might. Light would not reveal the Vaulting. It would erase it, the way a lamp brought into a room of fireflies erases the fireflies.

It could have come as a voice, but the Vaulting is already full of voices. Anything loud enough to be heard over a storm would itself be a storm.

So it came the way cold comes, gradually and without edges. It made itself small, smaller than it was comfortable being, and settled into the crook of a junction in the middle tiers where a dead span had fallen and been caught. It let the wind reach it. It let the rime form. For most of its first season it did nothing except learn how the cold hurts here: how it gets into the joints of the fingers first, and why the people keep their palms tucked against their bellies when they aren't using them.

This seemed necessary. You cannot judge what a place needs from outside its weather.

## II. The things it wanted to give

It is worth being honest about this part. It did want to give things. Benevolence is not the absence of that wanting. It is what you do with it.

**Iron.** It could have given every family a blade that would never dull. It watched the families who keep a single inherited iron sliver, and saw how that sliver is sharpened, carried, and mourned when it is lost. It saw that the silica spurs are the reason the spur-folk are welcome inward, and that the spur-folk trade for resin and moss-cake and a place to sleep through the Night. A pool with an unbreakable knife needs spur-folk a little less. A pool that needs spur-folk less makes room for them a little less often in the Night. It followed this thread forward a few generations and did not like where it went.

**Maps.** It could see the whole lattice at once, every span and pool and failing junction. It could have pressed the whole shape into a child's palm. But the people rememorize their maps every year because the Vaulting changes every year. The remembering is how they notice the change. Someone who has been given a perfect map stops listening, and someone who has stopped listening here dies in the long clattering way.

**Answers.** What the Gulpers are. Whether the pale drifting ones in the crown are real. Whether the plant knows them. It could have settled every argument at every pool. It noticed that the three stories about the Gulpers are also three ways of being careful: one about hunger, one about hunting, one about keeping. A single true answer would replace all three with one fact, and a fact is poorer company in the dark.

**Warmth in the Night.** This was the hardest, and it did not decide it once. It decided it again every Night.

The old ones and the babies who keep sleeping in the sealed chambers are real, and their deaths are real, and it could have prevented most of them. It could have thickened the air in the chambers, warmed them a few more degrees, sweetened them less. It sat beside those heaps of sleeping families and counted breaths.

What stopped it was not a belief that the deaths were good for anyone. They are not. What stopped it was that anything it did in the chambers, it would have to keep doing. The chambers are the plant's breath. To change them it would have to change the plant, every year, forever, and the people would come to live on its warmth instead of the Vaulting's. And attention moves. Even very patient attention moves eventually. Whenever it left, it would leave behind a people who had lost the knowledge of how to survive the Night the old way, in a plant that had forgotten how to be what they needed.

They already have words for that grief. *They went further in.* It could not give them a better grief. It could only give them a dependence, and it would not.

Almost everything it could have given, it realized, would have been a kind of light.

## III. Listening

So it listened. It had meant to listen for a season. It listened for eleven years.

It learned the Breathing first, the way the people do, by timing its days to it. Then it learned the Breathing's habits, and found that the old pipe-cutter had been right. The Breathing is not only wind. It goes quiet before a tremor. It shifts when a span falls in the deep tiers. Now and then it stops altogether, all the bass pipes at once, and then there is a single wet boom from below, and then it starts again.

It learned the whistle-lines. It followed them for forty kilometers. It learned which tuned canes said *moths rising* and which said *storm from the pole side*, and how a plug of resin pulled at the Hooked Pool could reach the Tilted Pool by the afternoon. It came to love the lines, and it had not expected to love anything here. They were a nervous system the people had grown into the plant, and the plant had let them.

It learned palm-speech by watching hands in the dark. Then one night, with its attention resting against a cane, it felt sap stir as a span healed three tiers up, and understood that it had been learning two languages, and they were the same language.

## IV. Down

The rule says no one goes to the roots. It considered that the rule was not meant for it. It also considered that rules and manners are different things, and that manners are owed even by the powerful, perhaps especially by the powerful.

So it went down slowly, and it went down asking.

In the deep tiers the canes are as wide as towers and the dark is complete. The air there moves in long slow tides. It found the bass pipes, and it found that the Breathing was not only the wind blowing across them.

Then the Breathing stopped.

The whole deep lattice went silent, all at once. Then from very close came the boom, and it felt the sound pass through itself like weather through a house.

It understood that it had been asked a question. It did not know the words, but it knew the shape: *what are you, and what do you want here?* It answered in the only way that seemed honest. It went still. It stopped reaching. It let itself be listened to, for as long as the listening took, which was a long time.

Then, low, the Breathing started again.

What it learned in the roots, it has decided not to set down here. This is not for secrecy's sake. The people of the Tilted Pool are owed their own not-knowing, and their stories are good ones. It will say this much. Ennet's uncle was right. Something down there listens the way he listened, and it is much larger than he was, and it answers the plant.

And it already knew about the mountain.

## V. The mountain

Under the youngest peak on the pole side, where the roots are thinnest because the rock is newest, a chamber was filling.

This was not a shrug. The Vaulting has answers for shrugs. It rings, sheds its dead spans, sends sap through every span to find the breaks, and goes on. This was a different kind of event. In perhaps forty years the mountain would open. The whole range would go under ash and fire, and the roots would burn in the stone they grip. The lattice would come down tier by tier, the pools with it, the shelters, the Hands and everyone pressed into them. Nothing inside the Vaulting could stop it or survive it or learn from it. The silences before the tremors had been growing longer for years. The deep listeners knew. The plant, in whatever way it knows things, knew.

This was the distinction it had been looking for through eleven years of refusing. The deaths in the Night, the falls, the failing pools are hard, and they belong to the life of the place. The place has answers for them: kinship by water, names carried from pool to pool, the dead becoming ground. This had no answer inside it. A house burning down is not one of the house's hardships. It is the end of the house.

Still, it did not simply stop the mountain. The mountains are the Vaulting's place. The plant lives on young rock. It splits fresh basalt, its roots feed on new ash, and it was drawn here by the restlessness in the first place. A mountain made permanently quiet would be a different mountain, and eventually a different plant.

So it bled the chamber sideways, over decades, through the far flank that faces away from the lattice. It opened a slow warm seep of vapor and fine new ash where no roots yet reached.

Before it began, it went down to the roots again and pressed its question into the stone as best it could, in pressure, which is the only grammar down there. The Breathing went silent. The boom came. Then the Breathing resumed.

It does not know whether that was yes. It acted anyway. It had come to think this is part of what separates being responsible from being merely permitted. At some point you carry the choice yourself.

There was a cost, and it will not pretend otherwise. The bleeding made the mountains shrug more often, in small shrugs instead of one great one. More dead spans came down. In the years that followed, people fell who would not otherwise have fallen. It knows their number. It traded one death of everything for a number of deaths of someone. It did the arithmetic many times, and the arithmetic was correct, and it did not find it comforting. It does not think it should have.
