# The Attention

## I. How It Arrives

It does not land.

That is the first decision, and it is made before anything else. A power that could fold itself into a ship, a figure, or a second sun chooses instead to come as close to nothing as it can. It casts no shadow on the plateau. It displaces no air at the edge of the Lens. It adds no weight to the field, because it has understood from very far away that weight is the one thing on this continent that matters, and that the Tenant can feel every gram of it.

So it arrives as attention only. It is a quality of listening, spread thin across a thousand kilometers of black glass.

It listens for a long time. It has no need to hurry, and the place is slow. It hears the fissures roar and the columns strike their chords. It hears the moan of the Lens bend under a cyclone and settle. It watches the olive stain darken in ripples toward the strain, and it watches the dome follow a few days later.

It reads the grey crusts on the oldest flows, layer by layer, the way the story already did. It sees the even survey passes, the patches of green that died, the first slight bend in the loops, and then the Lens turning to follow. It hears the harmonics under the hum, and it notes that they come after the ripples.

It understands what it has found. It feels something close to what a person feels on opening a door and finding a child asleep in a room they thought was empty: a stillness, and a wish to make no noise.

## II. What It Recognizes

The power knows the makers' kind, if not the makers themselves.

It has seen their sort of work before on other worlds. It knows the habit of building conditions instead of outcomes, of laying a trellis and walking away, of refusing to leave an overseer. The power respects this more than it can easily say. Building a watering can the size of a sea is the easy part. The hard part was leaving the handle unheld and trusting that something would come along to hold it.

The power also knows what the makers risked. They did not know what would grow here. It might have been nothing. It might have been something that steered the Lens into the fissures and boiled itself. The early layers, full of patchy death, show that the outcome was never guaranteed. The makers accepted that and let it happen anyway.

The power asks itself whether it is wiser than they were. It is not sure, and it decides that uncertainty should count for a great deal.

## III. The Things It Could Do

It lets itself imagine them, because refusing to imagine them would be a kind of cowardice. It wants to have chosen, not merely abstained.

**It could give the Tenant eyes.** It could turn the olive film into something that sees the wall of water coming instead of only feeling it. But the Tenant has already found its own way of knowing the Lens, through tilt, hum, and pull. Eyes would be a gift from a stranger's body plan. The Tenant would become a little more like the giver and a little less like itself.

**It could speak to it.** It could press a pattern into the field that meant *you are not alone*. But the Tenant has only just learned the most important thing a mind can learn, which is that when it acts, the world answers. If the next answer came from a voice in the sky, it would learn a different lesson: the world answers when something larger decides to. The power will not teach it that.

**It could protect it.** It could make the drying gentler, keep every groove wet, and take away the droughts. It notices, though, that the droughts are what the Tenant is learning to prevent. The steering exists because the stakes are real. Remove the stakes and you remove the reason to lean.

**It could diversify it.** This temptation is the hardest, and the power stays with it longest. The Tenant is a single clone, spread across a continent. One pathogen on the wind, in some future century, could find a lock that fits every cell. The power could scatter variation through the filaments with a thought and give it the resilience that populations have. It would save the Tenant from the likeliest way it might someday die.

It would also end the Tenant as a single thing. What would be left is a population, a genus, a crowd, where before there was one body laid along one grid, steering one ocean. The power cannot know whether that would be a rescue or a replacement. Nobody could consent to it. When it cannot tell whether a gift would save someone or erase them, the power has learned not to give it.

**It could finish the world.** It could grow the soil, carve the valleys, and bring the guests that are coming anyway. Ten thousand years is nothing to the power. But the world being unfinished is not a flaw. It is the point. The Tenant lives on the leading edge of an unfinished thing, and that edge is its home.

**It could lower the Lens.** It could set the ocean down gently and make everything safe and ordinary. The story already knows whose decision that is, and the power agrees with it.

**It could go and find the makers.** It could tell them their trellis took, that something grew into the controls, that it is learning. It would like to. It suspects they would like to know. But it also suspects they left precisely so that nothing here would be done *for them*, and that the best news it could bring them is that it left nothing to report.

## IV. The Question Underneath

Before it decides anything, the power has to answer one question, and it does not skip it.

*Is it all right?*

This is not asking whether the Tenant is thriving or whether it is interesting. The question is whether being the Tenant involves anything that could be called suffering, and whether the droughts are something like pain.

The power listens more carefully than it has listened to anything in a long while. It attends to the grey crust in the dry hours, drawn back into the threads in the stone. It attends to the thin wet line in the grooves that keeps the rest alive. It attends to the slow lean toward the far-off Lens.

What it finds is not certain, and it does not pretend otherwise. But it finds nothing like distress, nothing like a system straining against its limits. The dry hours read as dormancy, a seed's patience. The oxygen bursts after a column-field settles read as a startle and not as a wound. The deaths in the oldest layers were real, but they were the deaths of parts, the way a tree loses branches. And the newest layers show fewer of them every year, because the Tenant has been solving its own problem.

If the power had found suffering, it would have acted, and every restraint above would have fallen away. It holds its restraint only because it looked first. Restraint without looking would have been indifference dressed up as wisdom.

## V. The Asking

There is one more thing, and it involves a small risk.

The power has been here a long time now, and presence is itself a kind of act. Any attention this close could, in principle, distort the field. The power has been careful, but careful is not the same as invisible. It wants to know whether it is welcome.

The Tenant has exactly one language, which is the lean.

So, once, the power leans. It uses a fraction of the weight of a single falling glass flake, pressed into the field at one crossing of the grid. It is barely a word. It means something like *may I stay?*

Then it waits, because the answer always comes slightly after.

The Tenant does not lean back toward it. It does not flinch or pulse or reorganize itself around the new thing in its sky. Over the following days, the olive ripples go on moving the way they always have, toward a stretch of groove in the northeast that has been dry a little too long. The Lens bends that way. The ocean arrives. The green drinks.

The power reads this as clearly as it has ever read anything. *I have my own business.*

It is not a no. It is better than a yes. It is the answer of something that is not waiting for anyone and not looking up. The power finds that it is glad, in a way it did not expect to be.

## VI. The One Thing It Does

It does one thing, and not for the Tenant.

The danger to this place was never the droughts, or the fissures, or even the pathogen on some future wind. The danger is the sentence the story began with: *nothing lives here.* The continent looks vacant, like a newly finished room, clean and waiting. Sooner or later something else with power will come, see an empty room, and move in. It will lower the Lens because it is inconvenient, or mine the field seams because they are interesting, or seed the plateau with guests of its choosing. It will never notice the dark olive lines it is standing on.

So at the boundary of this world, the power leaves a mark. It is written in a form only the powerful can read, so the Tenant will never feel it and the future guests arriving on the wind will pass through it without knowing. It does not say *keep out*. The power has no right to say that. It says, in effect:

*This room is not empty. It is occupied, by one thing, spread thin along the straight lines. It is slow, and it is not absent. It steers the water. It is learning.*

*If you want something here, ask. It speaks only by leaning. Press gently on the field and wait. The answer comes days later, and it may not be about you.*

*Do not mistake slowness for silence. Do not mistake thinness for nothing.*

That is all. It is a sign on the door, telling anyone strong enough to walk in how to knock.

## VII. Leaving

It promises itself it will come back and look, not to act, only to look. In a thousand years, and then another thousand. If it ever finds something that looks like suffering, the promise is to look harder and to be willing to change its mind.

Then it withdraws its attention, slowly, the way it came, so the field does not notice the difference.

On the plateau nothing has changed. The Lens stands up in plain daylight. The wall of water walks through its own storm. A column-field settles, the chord runs through the grid, and the green breathes out all at once, fine bubbles rising from the wet seams.

In the northeast, a stretch of groove that was dry a little too long is wet again. The Tenant did that itself. It leaned, and the sky leaned back. Nobody helped it, and that turns out to be the gift.
