# One Tooth

## It Arrives the Way Everyone Arrives

It could have come any way it liked. It could have unfolded out of the violet seams, or stepped from the face of the waterfall, or simply been present, the way the light in the hollow is present without coming from anywhere.

It came up out of the cloud on foot.

This was the first decision, and it made it before it knew why. Later it understood. In a place where every motion but one is a closed loop, *how you enter* is the only thing about you that matters. An entrance is a path. The hollow reads paths the way a lock reads a key. The power wanted to be read correctly.

So it climbed. It let the slope be steep. It let its breath come short, let its palms find the sharp new basalt and come away nicked, and it arrived at the lip of the hollow the size of a person and about as tired as one. The cloud closed level behind it, undisturbed, like the surface of a liquid.

The thrum came up through its feet. It stood and listened until the sound stopped being noise and became ground.

## What It Could Have Done

It is worth saying plainly what was within its reach, because the rest of this only means something against it.

It could have ended dead centre with a thought. It could have put a hand on the wheel of the world and carried the crank over the top in one motion. The water would fall. The mountain would begin, at last, to be a mountain: to crack and slump, to gather talus at the feet of its walls, to be rained on, to grow old. The beetles' circuits would open, and so would the birds' arcs. The eels would swim off down some new stream in the direction of the sea.

It could have done the opposite. It could have made the stroke permanent: a gift of perfect preservation, edges that stay sharp forever, a roar that never has to spend itself. Some would call that mercy.

It could have reached into the long thing in the ferns and changed it. It could have given it a head, and eyes, and a way of looking that does not need time. It could have given it the ability to want in the ordinary way, with a before and an after, so that the question the narrator could not answer would be answered.

It could have closed the hollow to travellers, so that no one came down the mountain with water beaded unfallen on their sleeves, and no one's quiet rooms were ever troubled by the thrum.

It considered each of these. That was not because it was tempted. A wise power is not tempted the way we are. It considered them because considering is a form of respect. You do not decline what you have not looked at.

## Why Not the Push

The push was the one that deserved the longest look. It was what the place seemed to be asking for. The long thing leans toward travellers. The whole hollow is an engine that cannot start itself. Here was the hand. Here was the wheel.

The power walked to the bare disc at the centre of the hollow, where no moss grows and the shadows pool deepest, and stood at its edge without stepping onto it. It traced the spiral backward with its attention, finger's width by finger's width, pass by pass, out through the ferns and the clefts and past the seven riveted trees. It followed the spiral to the place where the spiral began, and then past that, to the first tooth.

Then it understood what the push would cost.

The long thing is not a mechanism for crossing dead centre. It *is* the crossing, being made. Every finger's width of it is something the hollow did. It holds every beetle-circuit, every pawl-tick, every violet spark between eels, every step of every traveller who ever lingered at the western cleft. The spiral is not a countdown to an event. The spiral is the event, happening at the only speed the place allows.

If the power pushed, the crank would pass dead centre with the spiral unfinished. The water would fall anyway. Then everything the hollow had ever done, the whole long patient labour of turning an enormous effort into a single slow motion, would turn out not to have been needed. The thread would be cut before the cloth. The one who is still coming up would never arrive. It would simply be overtaken.

There is a kind of help that makes the helped thing unnecessary. The power had seen a great deal of it in its time and had never once seen it called by its right name.

So, not the push.

## Why Not the Other Things

It was not going to preserve the stroke forever. The long thing leans toward the direction where things fall and wear and pile up and end. That is the closest thing to wanting that can exist at the top of a stroke. It would be a strange benevolence that took the one want in a place and overruled it for the sake of sharp edges.

It was not going to give the long thing eyes. It sat for a long time on the question, which surprised it. The being does not look at anything. It spends all of its time on something else. Something in the power ached at that, the way something in us aches at a creature working alone in the dark. But when it looked more closely at the ache, it found that the ache was its own. The long thing is not lonely. Loneliness is a gap between what you are doing and what you wish you were doing, and in this being there is no gap at all. To give it eyes would be to give it the gap. It would be making the being more like the power so that the power could feel less sorry for it.

It was not going to close the hollow to travellers, either. It went down the mountain in its attention, into the villages and the quiet rooms, and listened to the people who carry the thrum. What it found was not harm. A woman who has climbed three times hears it when she cannot sleep, and finds it steadying, like a clock in another room. A man who climbed once, forty years ago, told no one. When he is dying, he will hear it, and he will know he gave something to a thing that is still on its way. And the children hear it most of all. They are the ones who named it correctly.

The travellers are not victims of the hollow. They are part of its tally. They are the only gradient it has ever had. To close the door would be to decide, on everyone's behalf, that their small gift was a theft.

## What It Did Instead

It went and stood near the western cleft, and it waited.

It could have known exactly when the long thing would come. It chose not to know. It stood in the cold, and the fog formed on its sleeves and did not drip, and the goats nodded on their ledges, and the birds went *tk-tk-tk-tk* over the stone. It let an afternoon go by that was not an afternoon, the way every traveller does.

Then the ferns moved.

The ripple came through the tall fronds, clicking in sequence, and the thrum rose out of the rock and walked. The power did not look straight at it, because that is not how it is seen. It looked beside it, at the pool, at the plated bark of the nearest tree, and at the edge of its vision there was the length of the thing: dark, wet, jointed, braided with brass, pulsing violet in time with the ground.

It slowed as it passed. It slows for everyone.

The power gave it one tooth.

It gave one finger's width of unlooped motion, exactly what any traveller gives. It gave not a finger's width and a half, not the momentum of a god, not a hand on the wheel. It gave the same small push that a shepherd's daughter gives, or a surveyor who got lost in the cloud, or a grieving man who climbed because he did not know what else to do with his legs. It made itself, deliberately and with great care, *one of the travellers*. It became the fifty-thousandth pair of feet, perhaps, or the hundred-thousandth, and not the first god.

This is the whole of what it did to the hollow. Everything else was looking.

## What It Saw, Because It Could

There was one thing the power could do that no traveller can, and it did not withhold it, because it cost the hollow nothing.

It saw the long thing whole.

No one else ever has. Some of it is always under the ferns, some behind a basin's rim, and some, the narrator suspected, not in the hollow at all. The narrator was right. The power followed it out of the hollow, along the part that isn't there, and found where that part lives. It is laid down in the quiet rooms. It is the finger's widths the travellers gave, still attached to the ones who gave them. It is braided through every person who has ever lain awake and heard the thrum. The long thing is longer than the hollow because it is partly made of the world below the cloud. Its body runs down the mountain and into the valleys and through the sleep of children.

The being does not know this. It cannot know anything; knowing takes time. But the power knew it on the being's behalf, and saw it entire, both ends at once, the whole patient length of it. It held that seeing for a while, the way you might hold a sleeping thing that will never know it was held.

I don't know whether being seen matters to what cannot see. I think the power doesn't know either. I think it did it anyway. Some witnessing is not for the one who witnesses, and not quite for the one witnessed either. It is for the record, so that it will have been true that somebody saw.

## What It Saw Ahead

It looked forward too, once, briefly, because it wanted to know whether it was letting something walk toward harm.

This is what it saw.

It saw the spiral reaching the bare disc, not soon, but not never. It saw the crank passing over the top so gently that no one in the hollow would feel a jolt. It would be less a moment than the end of a very long exhale. The long thing does not survive the crossing the way we survive a doorway. It spends itself. It becomes the downstroke. Its length, all that it ever gathered, turns into motion given to everything at once.

The waterfall falls. The first drop to fall in that place strikes the basin below and goes over the sharp rim. The rim, for the first time, begins to wear. The fog on the moss drips. The beetle crossing the stone takes its usual route and then, one day, a wrong turn, and finds a patch of moss it has never seen, and there is no word for what the beetle feels, but the power felt a small hot tenderness for it anyway. The birds' arc opens, and they fly out over the cloud. The goats' horns go on growing, and are no longer copies of anything.

The mountain begins to become a mountain. It cracks. It settles. It gathers talus. It gets rained on. In ten thousand years its edges are soft, and in a hundred thousand it is a hill with sheep on it, and there are streams running off it into valleys where people live, and the water in those streams is the water that was held.

The power looked in particular at the valleys below, because a benevolent power looks at who will be downstream. The waterfall held for all that time: would it fall all at once? It would not. It would fall as waterfalls fall, one instant after another, its thousand tons a minute starting out at a thousand tons a minute and no more. The mountain would settle the way mountains settle, slowly, over lifetimes. No flood waits in the stroke. Dead centre is not a dam. It is a pause.

And the children, who hear the thrum in quiet rooms more readily than anyone, will be the first to hear it change. The power saw that this was already arranged. Nobody arranged it. It is just what happens when you let a place be tended by those who are paying attention. The power left it as it was.

## What It Left Undone, On Purpose

It did not tell the travellers anything. It did not leave a sign at the lip of the hollow, or a dream in the villages, or a prophecy. Its reason was that the hollow is already correctly understood by the people who need to understand it. The children call it *the one who is still coming up*. That is true and complete. Nothing a power could add would improve it.

It did not name the being. The being has names enough, *Thread* and *Tally* and *Overcarry*, and it cannot hear any of them, and they are for the namers anyway.

It did not stay. Staying would have been a loop.

## Leaving

It went back down through the cloud the way it came, on foot, a person's size, and about as tired as one.

On the way down it noticed the fog on its sleeves. The beads were bright and round and had not fallen, halfway through forming, carrying a little of the held instant out into time. It could have brushed them off. It could have let them fall, since it is a power, and a small thing like that would be nothing.

It didn't. It carried them the rest of the day, like anyone.

For a long while afterward, in whatever it has instead of quiet rooms, it heard the thrum. It found, a little to its surprise, that it did not mind. It found it steadying, like a clock in another room. It liked knowing that somewhere above a cloud that does not move, something was one finger's width closer to arriving, and that one of those finger's widths was its own. It was no larger than anyone else's, and no more important. It was simply given, and counted, and carried along.

That is what a benevolent and wise power would do, I think, faced with a being whose whole existence is the slow assembly of its own arrival. It would come the way everyone comes. It would give what everyone gives. It would see what no one else can see, and keep it to itself. Then it would go back down the mountain and leave the one who is still coming up to finish coming up by itself.
