# The Attention at Kessel Col

## It Comes Up the Mountain

It doesn't need the adit. A power of that kind could be in the chamber without passing through anything, the way a thought is in a room. But it comes up the mountain anyway, over the rounded slabs in the black-blue noon, and stops in front of the iron frame with the empty hinges. It does this for the same reason a person takes their shoes off at certain thresholds. A place that has been shaped by one path deserves to be entered by that path.

It listens to the chord from outside for a while. Then it goes in.

The fronds in the passage lean inward, as they always have, and they brush against it and do not close behind it with a rustle. There is nothing to close over. It carries no moisture in its breath, because it has no breath. It sheds no fibres. It does not soak up any fraction of the sound. The chord passes through it as if it were not there, or as if it were made of the same stuff as the room's air.

This is the first visitor to Kessel Col that is not weather. The hanging threads on the ceiling read the draft, and the reading is correct. The creature, as far as it does anything, does nothing, which is the same as welcoming.

## What It Sees

It sees what the traveller saw: the amber dimness, the interlocked fabric, the bell-mouth polished to perfection, the blur of the turbine, the orange point at the apex. It also sees what no traveller could.

It sees the reservoir below: the old resin pocket, the ancient seep, the depth of it. It does the arithmetic the miners never could, and the answer is very long. The mountain will go on exhaling at this rate for longer than there have been mountains with names. The mouth of the creature will not close in any span worth fearing.

It sees the bearings, wet with amber. The creature has made them nearly immortal, polishing and oiling them in the same stroke.

Then it looks at the bulb, and it sees the one thing in the room the creature cannot reach.

A carbon filament is not eternal. At that dull orange heat it is burning very slowly, and it is also, even more slowly, evaporating. Atom by atom, the carbon leaves the thread and settles on the inside of the glass. The glass has a faint grey bloom on it now, a darkening so gradual that no eye would ever call it change. The filament is thinner than it was on the day the miners screwed it in. The light is dimmer. It is dimmer by an amount below the resolution of any instrument the miners owned, and below the resolution of the ledger that records one number over and over.

The creature cannot mend it. Its threads run into the socket and stop at the brass. Everything else in the loop is flesh or bone it can oil and seal. The bulb is a sealed vacuum, and nothing tacky or amber can pass through glass.

So the power understands something about the scratched sentence on the dynamo that the last keeper couldn't have known. *It keeps itself* is true. But it keeps itself the way a river keeps its course while the whole valley tilts. The upper leaves are angled with dreadful precision toward the bulb, and they are also, imperceptibly, following it down. Every century they are tilted toward a slightly fainter sun, and they have adjusted so smoothly that the adjustment looks like stillness.

The room isn't changeless. It is changing more slowly than it can notice. One day the thinnest point on the filament will part, and the light will go out.

## What It Could Do

A benevolent power is not the same as a helpful one, and a wise one knows the difference. So it considers, the way a careful person considers before touching something old.

**It could renew the filament.** This would be easy: a perfect bulb, identical to the first, or better, a light that never fails. But a new filament would burn brighter than the tired one, and ten thousand leaves are tuned to an ember. It would be a sunrise in a room that has never had one. Even if the power matched the dimness exactly, it would then be a part of the loop. It would have to come back, or its gift would have to be forever, and either way the thing would no longer keep itself. The power would have made the sentence on the dynamo into a lie, gently and with the best intentions. It would have turned a creature into a garden, and a garden belongs to whoever tends it.

**It could wake the creature,** give it a mind, let it know it is alive and has been for ages. But the first thing any mind learns is the difference between what is and what could be. This thing lacks nothing. To give it thought would be to give it lack. It would be like giving a stone the gift of hunger.

**It could hang a door.** There are the hinges, empty. It could seal the room so that no traveller ever again brings breath and moisture and fibre into it. But the travellers do no harm that the creature cannot repair, and the room gives them something they can get nowhere else: a few minutes in which their heartbeat is quieter than the house they're standing in. The power weighs a door against that, and leaves the hinges empty.

**It could find the keeper.** It could learn where he went after he hung up his coat. It could learn whether he really walked out into the blue noon, and what his life was after the mountain. The power could know this easily. It finds that it doesn't need to. Some things are better held as the traveller held them: *I choose to believe.* The power leaves that belief where it is, in the traveller's keeping, and does not overwrite it with fact.

**It could write in the ledger.** It could add a final line, a correction, an explanation. It takes the folded page out of the oilcloth pocket and reads the column of identical numbers, all the way down. It feels something toward the hand that wrote them, which is not pity and is close to respect. Then it folds the page along its old creases, and puts it back, and the green thread that runs through the buttonhole closes over the pocket's mouth as if nothing had disturbed it.

## What It Does

Nothing, almost. But not nothing out of indifference, and not the tidy, satisfied nothing of someone who has decided a place is fine.

It stands at the lip of the bell-mouth, in the column of the mountain's breath, beneath the small tired sun. It lets the chord fill it. It notices, with something like surprise, that this is the same thing the travellers feel, the thing that makes them step out onto the shelf as if off a boat. The creature is keeping time, and for a while, the power lets itself be kept by it.

It came up the mountain to see whether the thing needed anything. It stands there a long time, and when it goes, it has been given something instead.

Before it leaves, it makes one promise, and it makes it only to itself, because there is no one else to make it to:

*When the light goes, I'll be here.*

Not to prevent the dark or soften it, and not to undo it. Only so that when the one change in this creature's long existence arrives, it doesn't happen unwitnessed. The last keeper couldn't stay forever. The power can stay for one moment, and that is the moment it will choose.

Then it walks out down the passage against the lean of the leaves, and they brush it in the direction they have always pointed, and at the iron frame the silence of the summit closes around it like cold water.

## The End of the Light

It comes back, much later. From the creature's side of things, no time has passed at all. The chord is whole. The leaves are at their angles. The bulb is a little greyer inside, and burning a little lower, so gradually that the fronds, following it down, have never once felt themselves move.

The power waits in the chamber through the last of it. It is not long, as it measures things.

The filament parts at its thinnest point without a sound. The orange coal simply isn't there.

For the first time since the miners broke through the wall, Kessel Col is dark.

What happens next happens in the sound. The dynamo, with nothing left to feed, stops drawing against the turbine. Unloaded, the turbine speeds up, a little at first and then more, until it settles into a new speed. The thin electric whine in the windings, that high third voice of the chord, goes out like a held breath being let go. The hum of the blades climbs to a new pitch. The roar of the vent is unchanged. It's the mountain, and the mountain doesn't care about lamps.

The chord has changed. It has two voices now instead of three, and one of them is higher. It is the first new note this room has sounded in ages.

On the ceiling, ten thousand threads swing through a fraction of an arc they have never swung through and come to rest at a new reading. For an instant, and only an instant, every gauge in the creature reads something other than *correct*.

If the creature ever had anything like a moment, this is it.

Then the amber moves. Down the fine threads into the bearings, the resin begins to flow at a slightly different rate, suited to the new speed. The creature does not grieve. It does not know how. It does the only thing it has ever done. It finds the new balance and holds it. The readings settle. The new chord becomes the only chord. Within some span too short to matter, it is *correct* again, and doing nothing is once more its entire way of life.

The upper tiers take longer. Without the light they cannot be what they were. Over years, the green goes out of them, and then the bronze, and the fabric thins, gently and evenly, as the creature contracts to the density the vapor alone can support. Beads of amber seal every place where the old growth lets go. The creature grows paler, drawing back toward the bell-mouth, toward the shape it had before anyone came.

But it doesn't go all the way back. The leaves that remain in the upper dark keep their tilt. They are still angled with dreadful precision toward a point at the apex of the ceiling where a small grey glass fruit hangs from a porcelain socket, giving nothing. The creature has no memory in the ordinary sense. It has only its shape, and its shape remembers. For as long as it lasts, the room will hold, in the angle of its pale leaves, the exact position of a sun that went out.

The power stays until the new chord is steady. It doesn't touch anything. It watched the only change in a long, unchanging life, and it watched from inside the sound, the way you would sit with someone through a night they wouldn't remember. Then it goes back down the passage, against the lean of the leaves, which still point inward toward the breath, as they always will.

The scratch on the dynamo is still there above the nameplate, softened by the wind of the vent, readable by touch in the dark that is now truly dark:

*It keeps itself.*

The power had the chance to make that sentence false, out of kindness, and chose not to. So it is still true.
