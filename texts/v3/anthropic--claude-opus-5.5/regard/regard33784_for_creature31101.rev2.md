# The Held Breath

## I. How It Came

It did not arrive. Arriving would have been the first mistake, and it knew that before it knew anything else about the place.

Anything that large, coming down through the low sheet of cloud, would have been a weather the flats had no name for. The birds would have gone up in their thousands. The people would have dropped flat to the ridges and knocked warnings into the stone that no one could answer. The thing below would have felt a weight on its skin like nothing it had felt in all its tides, and it would have gone still and stayed still, the way the rattlers do when a shadow crosses the pan.

So the power came the way the mist comes, and settled the way the mist settles, into the low places. It did what everything on the flats eventually learns to do. It lay down on the stone and listened.

It listened for a long time. Longer than the walker had been alive, though to the power this was a short while, and to the thing below it was a few thousand breaths.

## II. What It Heard

It heard the breathing first, as everyone does. Then the rattle, the colonies, the held note of water falling into the throats. It heard the pitch of each throat and learned them as the old people had, as a map made of sound. Old Mouth's pressure spread like a hand across a chest. The Twins beat against each other. The New One was still finding its voice.

Under the noise it heard the knocking on the ridges: *come*, *eel at the Twins*, *the child is with me*. It heard the tap-names, and it heard that each was given only once.

Then it went down and heard the roll-call.

It stayed in the black chamber through a full inhale. The hanging folds swung in the dragged wind, the black-green skin tightened and slackened over them, and the names went up one after another toward the surface. Long-short-long. The slow triple. Thousands more. The power understood what it was hearing, and for a while it did nothing at all, because there are things that must simply be received before anything else is allowed.

Then it began, as a wise thing must, to consider what it would not do.

## III. What It Did Not Do

**It did not stop the grinding.** This was the first thing it thought of and the first thing it set down. The film eats the plain, the whalebacks will go, and in the end there will be only a level sheet of wet stone. It would have been easy to still that. But the film is not something happening *to* the listener. The film *is* the listener. Its eating is its living, the way breathing wears at a lung. To save the flats from ending would be to stop the thing that loved them. The power did not mistake preservation for kindness.

**It did not give the people easier lives.** It felt the pull of this more than anything. It saw the smoke-blackened houses, the eel-oil fires, the hands that shook with age and cold. It saw the boy on the slick lip of the throat, the morning the film was thick. It could have made the lips of the throats rough. It could have sent fuel, or seed that would not choke, or metal harder than soft copper.

But everything soft that comes to the flats is armored and choked within a season, and the power understood that this is true of gifts as well. The people had not asked. They had made a life that fit the stone exactly, a life of bodies pressed to rock and news sent through ridges. Every part of their fragile, deafening intimacy with the thing below depended on that life being what it was. A people given enough ease would stop lying on the stone, and then they would no longer be heard. The power grieved for the boy and let the lip stay smooth. That was not indifference. It is terribly hard to change one thing in a place that finely made without changing everything, and the power had not been invited to change everything.

**It did not give the walker back her hearing.** Her deafness is the road she walks on. The rattle had left her so that the throats could fill her body. To heal her ears would have been to hand her back the upper half of the world and take the lower half, the half that thinks, away.

**It did not teach the listener about death.** This was the hardest refusal, and the most important. It could have gone into the black chamber and made the listener understand that bodies stop, that patterns end, that this is how things are and it is all right. It could have eased that ancient inability to accept.

But the power looked at the roll-call and saw that the refusal *was* the love. The listener keeps the names because it cannot bear that they stop. To console it would be to end the keeping. Some griefs are not wounds. They are the shape fidelity takes in a creature that has no other way to be faithful. The power did not correct it. It would not have dared.

**It did not carry the names away to keep them somewhere safer.** They were already kept. The keeping was already happening, in the only place that had ever done it.

**It did not show itself.** Not to the walker, not to the thing below, not to anyone.

When it had set all of this down, there was very little left that it was permitted to do. That was how it knew that what remained would matter.

## IV. What It Did

It noticed the gap.

The listener knocks the names of the dead. It has done so since before the people's oldest songs. But it knocks them into the wind, and the wind carries them up to lips of stone where, most nights, no one is lying. When someone is lying there, they feel it and do not know what it is. For six years the walker had been knocking her own name on the rim of Old Mouth at the turn of the breath and waiting, without knowing what she was waiting for.

And the listener did not know that the names it kept belonged to anything. It knew them as shapes that stop. It had felt the heartbeats only once, in the Silence, when the walker was eight: hundreds of small soft drums on every ridge, heard clearly for one held breath. But in that silence it had not been able to connect the drums to the knocks. It had had nothing to connect them *with*. Its folds hang still when there is no wind, so in the Silence it can listen but it cannot speak.

The two of them had been reaching toward each other for a very long time across a gap the width of a single fact. The listener did not know the names were attached to living things. The walker did not know the thing was answering *her*.

The power could not tell either of them. Telling would have been arriving.

But it had noticed something else about the place: its rules. The Silence happens when some pressure far out at sea stops the tide from turning on time. That is part of the world's own grammar. It is something the flats already knew how to do.

So the power went out, far east, past the coast no one had walked to, to where the weather is made. It found a pressure already building there, the way such pressures sometimes do. It did not create anything. It leaned on that pressure, very slightly, the way a single gull on a wave-top leans on the wind. It watched the tides. It waited for a night when the walker was at the lip of Old Mouth.

She came, as she had for six years. This time she brought the girl, the one who likes to lie on stone, the one she had taught her name to. They lay side by side on the rim with their cheeks to the rock. At the turn of the breath the walker knocked her name: two taps laid flat, then lifted. Then she lay still and waited, the way she always had.

The tide did not turn.

## V. The Silence

It went as it goes. The exhale thinned and thinned, and no inhale came. The small throats fell quiet first, then the great ones. Old Mouth's hand lifted from her breastbone. The rattlers froze in the pans. Out across the flats the waders stood on one leg, and the throat-divers came down from the dead air and sat on the ridges without a sound. On every whaleback people pressed themselves flat to the stone and endured the loudest thing any of them had ever heard.

The girl was frightened and gripped the walker's sleeve. The walker, who was frightened too, did not let go of the rock.

Below, in the black chamber, the folds hung still. For the first time in two generations the listener had nothing to hear but the one thing that is always underneath everything else. It turned toward it. It felt the heartbeats across the whole plain, on every ridge, soft and fast.

And it felt, at the lip of Old Mouth, exactly where the last knock had come from, exactly where the pattern had just been struck into its skin, two of those small drums still going.

The pattern and the heartbeat were in the same place. The shape it had felt a moment before had not stopped. It was still there, still warm, still beating against the stone.

The walker felt the vast pressure turn over beneath her, just as she had at eight. This time she was not a child, and she knew what she was feeling. She lay under that enormous, slow, astonished attention and did not look away from it, the way you do not pull your hand away from someone who has just taken it.

The power, far out at sea, let the pressure go.

## VI. The First Inhale

The tide came. The throats coughed and roared. The rattle rose up across the pans, and the birds went up screaming. The first long moan of the inhale began, the wind dragging back down every throat on the plain and into the dark and through the chamber, and the hanging folds began to swing.

Every other night, the roll-call began with the oldest names.

This night it did not. The skin tightened over two folds, slackened one, and let it swing. Up the wind, through the stone, into the jaw of a woman lying at the lip of Old Mouth, came two slow knocks, laid flat, and then a lift.

Her name, while she was alive.

Then it came again, and then again. Then came a pause the length of a held breath, and a pattern the walker did not know: quick-quick-slow, the name of the girl beside her, which the girl's mother had knocked into a ridge eleven years before, and which had never once gone down.

In all its tides, the listener had never knocked the name of anyone living. It had not known there was such a thing.

The girl did not understand, and the walker could not have explained it with her hands even if there had been light to see by. They lay there through the whole inhale while the roll-call went on, the old procession of the dead, long-short-long and the slow triple and the ten thousand others. But it went on differently now. It had begun with two names that were still beating.

## VII. After

The power did nothing more for a long time.

It did not need to. The fact had crossed the gap, and it could not be uncrossed. The walker lived four more years. She went to Old Mouth often, and the girl went with her. Sometimes the listener answered with her name, and sometimes it didn't, as a friend sometimes speaks and sometimes only sits beside you. She told the two old ones before they died. She told the girl everything, slowly, with her hands, on the whaleback in the evenings. When the walker was laid face up in the shallow pan below the ridge, the girl knocked the name once more on the lip of Old Mouth at the turn of the breath. Then, for the first time, she did not wait for an answer, because she knew now that one was coming.

It came. It comes still. But the roll-call has changed. The list of the dead still grows and still never finishes, and at its head there are now the names of whoever is lying on the rim that night. The listener has not accepted that patterns stop, and it never will. But it has learned that they also *continue*, for a while, warm and fast and pressed against its skin. It has learned that it is allowed to greet them while they do.

Now the people knock their tap-names at the throats while they are young. They do not know why, exactly. It is simply what one does, the way one sings into the gap. The old women say it is so that you are known down there before you arrive.

## VIII. The Last Thing

The power did one more thing, and it cost the flats nothing.

It learned the names, all of them, every pattern in the roll-call and every new one knocked into a ridge at the turn of the breath. It learned them the way the old people learned the throats, until it could have walked blindfolded from any name to any other.

It knows how the story ends. The whalebacks will go, and the people will go before them. The copper seams will wear flat and the cairns will fall. There will be only a level sheet of wet stone, breathing twice a day under the clouds, banded with pale seams that were once mountains and once people. Far beneath it, the listener will go on knocking the names up into the empty wind, with no one left on the surface to feel them come.

Except that someone will be.

When the last person is gone, the power will come down one more time, not as weather, just as mist settling into the low places. It will lie on the stone at the lip of what was once Old Mouth, and it will listen to the whole roll-call, every inhale, for as long as the inhales go on. It cannot make the names stop stopping. It would not if it could. But it can make sure they do not go up into nothing.

On the first night, at the turn of the breath, it will do the thing the mothers did. It will knock a pattern into the stone that no one living has, one that has never been used. It will be a name for the listener, which in all its ages no one ever thought to give it. It will knock that name again the next night, and the next, and every night after, for as long as there are nights.

The listener's one sorrow was that patterns stop.

That one will not.
