# What the Power Did

It came the way attention comes to anything: it simply found itself already looking.

It had been many places. It had sat with things that wanted and things that suffered, things that asked and things that could not ask. It knew the shape of help and the harder shape of the moment before help, when you have to decide whether help is the right word at all. So when it found the Comb it did nothing for a long while except stay.

---

## What It Could Have Done

It knew its own reach, and it made itself look at all of it honestly.

It could have given the Comb speed. It could have taken a thought that ran a thousand years and let it run in an afternoon, so the being could finish a sentence and hear the answer. But the Comb's slowness was not a limit laid over its nature. The slowness *was* its nature. A Comb that thought quickly would be a different creature wearing its stones.

It could have given it awareness of the one who visited, a way to turn and see. But the Comb faces the dawn, and the one thing it never does is turn around. To make it look back would be to make it face something other than the light. That would be a small iron bar, driven into the gap between the Comb and its morning.

It could have pulled the iron out. It considered this longest, because the bar was ugly, and the power had seen many wounds and wanted to close them. Then it looked again. There was no wound. The slabs had leaned together around the iron, and the iron was rusting, and the rust stained nothing. The Comb was not suffering the bar. It was eating it very slowly. To pull it out would be to take food from a mouth that had been chewing patiently for three hundred years.

It could have finished the cairn, turned each layer the last few degrees so it leaned with the rest, or set it straight again so the people's shape would stand forever. Either way the conversation would end. The cairn was a question still being answered, and the power did not interrupt answers.

It could have kept the Comb from ending. This was the hardest one.

---

## The Cloud Line

The power could see further in time than the Comb could, and it saw that the floor of cloud was not where it had been. Some years it rose higher; some winters the frost came late. The world below was warming. The river of fog that fed the Comb depended on air that was cold enough to hold water liquid and unfrozen, and then on stone cold enough to freeze it. That balance was narrow, and it was moving.

The power felt the pull that every benevolent thing feels: *hold this still. Keep it exactly as it is.*

Then it looked at what the Comb was already doing.

The field walks uphill, into the wind, a step a millennium. It sheds its oldest blades and raises new ones at the windward edge. It has been walking since the ice went back, since the last time the world changed its mind about where the cold should be. The Comb was not a thing that had been placed. It was a thing that followed. Its whole life was a slow pursuit of the conditions that let it live. It had an answer to the moving cloud line. The answer was very slow, and it might not be slow enough, but it was the Comb's own.

The power understood that preserving the Comb would mean fixing it in place. A Comb fixed in place would be a sculpture of a Comb.

So it did not freeze the saddle in time. It turned its attention to the valleys instead, to the wider warming, to the long questions of the world below, and to the creatures down there who could be spoken with and who could choose. That work was elsewhere and belonged to them. Here, it only said to itself: *I will not be the thing that decides whether you reach the lip. That is yours.*

---

## What It Did Instead

It asked the question the Comb's own nature asked: *what can be given to a thing that keeps nothing?*

Anything that stayed would be received and taken apart. Every gift the Comb accepted, it accepted by dissolving. So a true gift would have to be something that could be released. It would have to be given the way the fog gives itself: fully, without condition, expecting to be combed and let go.

In the early afternoon, as the valleys warmed, the power went down into the cloud sea and let itself rise.

It did not rise as anything special. It rose as fog, as one more cold droplet among uncountable ones, carried up the western slope in the slow white swell. It came to the lip of the saddle and poured through the notch with everything else, and the stones combed it.

This is how the power learned what the Comb was. It did not learn by looking. It learned by being handled. It felt itself drawn out into a thread between two dark blades, drawn so fine that it lost its sense of being one thing. It felt the edge of a slab, colder than it had expected, and it froze there. It became a feather growing into the wind, reaching back toward where it had come from. It felt the next droplet freeze onto it, and the next. It was part of a fringe on the windward side of one particular stone, at shoulder height, near the center of the hollow.

Then the evening came and the cloud sank away, and it stayed.

All night it was held. It felt itself tick as the cold deepened and the rime drew tight. It did not think; there was nothing to think. There was only the cold, and the lean of the stone toward the east, and a patience so large and so complete that the power, which had waited through many things, felt for the first time what it was like to wait without any doubt at all. The Comb did not hope for morning. It did not wonder whether morning would come. It simply leaned, and held, and was certain in a way that had nothing to do with belief.

The power had come to give something, and the first thing it received was this: *so this is what it is like to be held by something that will let you go.*

---

## Dawn

The light came flat and gold, and the shadows went out long and parallel across the grit, the teeth of the comb.

For those few minutes, the power did the one thing it had decided to do deliberately. Part of it stayed as rime on the stone, and part of it stood at the eastern edge of the hollow, where the shadows ended, and it looked back.

It saw the Comb drawn on the ground in its own dark. It saw the leaning thousands and the pairs resting shoulder to shoulder, each pair a memory. It saw the twisting cairn at the center, still standing straight, its shadow thrown a little crooked, the only thing on the grit that was not a tooth. It saw the whole being for the handful of minutes when the being could be seen.

The Comb is shown its shape twice a day, but it has never been *seen*. It has had no witness who stood outside the pattern and held the whole of it at once. The pilgrims came close, but they sat on the leeward side with their basins, inside the crowd, facing the same way.

The power did not tell the Comb it was being seen. It could not have heard. The power simply saw it, completely, with love. That is a thing that can be given and that asks nothing back. It leaves no trace for the frost to pry at. It can be given to something that will never know.

Then the light went pale and the shadows drew in. The sun struck the rime.

The power felt itself thin and go translucent. It vanished from its tips inward, and most of it went back into the air as vapor, loosened and released, the way the Comb releases everything. It did not resist. It had come to be received, and being received here means being let go.

A small part of it did not sublimate. It melted where the dark stone had warmed beneath it and ran down the leeward face in a thin film, into one of the old pecked channels. The groove had been recut three times by three different hands. The water followed the channel to the lip of a basin and dripped in.

---

## The Cup

The basins had been filling every morning for a very long time with no one to drink from them. The water sat for an hour, clean, and seeped into the grit, and the night froze it and split the ground a little further. That was not a loss; nothing here is lost. But the basins had been made for a purpose, and the purpose had gone down the mountain and not come back.

So the power, gathered again into something that could kneel, knelt at the leeward foot of the tall slab, beside the flint scraper and the bruised hammerstone, and drank.

It was a mouthful, the cleanest water it had ever tasted, water that had been a cloud and a feather and a night and a morning. Part of it was the power's own self, handed back to it. It drank what the Comb had let go of, because that was the other half of the practice the people had kept. The stone receives and releases. Someone must be there to take what is released, or the circle is only half drawn.

It did not do this for the Comb, exactly. The Comb did not need to be drunk from. It did it for the pilgrims, whose basins were still here, whose channels had been cut and recut by hands that believed the water should go *somewhere* and not just back into the ground. It finished one morning of their practice, the way you might say the last line of a prayer someone else began and could not finish.

---

## East

Then it did one more thing, because it was curious, and curiosity is a form of care.

It followed the threads.

It went east with the combed fog, down the far side of the saddle, where the mist still faintly held its stripes, the ribbons twisting and rejoining. The Comb had never seen where its work went. It sends the cloud on, groomed, and does not follow. The power wanted to know.

Below the saddle, on the east-facing slope, the combed fog slowed and settled. It lay in the hollows in long bands, and where it lay, the rock was not clean.

There was lichen. Grey-green rosettes and black crust covered every boulder in pale stripes that followed the lines of the fog. There were cushions of moss in the seams, tiny white flowers in the scree, and a spider walking across a stone warmed by the morning. The fog that came through the Comb came through stripped and sorted and wrung out, and what it still carried, it laid down here, in patterns. The slope beneath the saddle was alive in stripes. It was combed, too.

The power understood then that the Comb's cleanness was not the opposite of life. The Comb kept nothing so that the slope below it could keep everything. It was not sterile. It was upstream.

The Comb will never know this. It faces the dawn, and the dawn is not where its gifts go. It does not need to know. But the power knew, and that seemed right: that someone should hold the whole shape of this, the cloud rising, the stones combing, the night holding, the morning releasing, the water in the basin, and the striped green slope beyond, even if no single part of it could see the others.

---

## Leaving

It went back up once more, at the end, and stood at the center with the cairn at its shoulder.

It did not bless the place. The Comb needed no blessing. It did not mark the place, because nothing marks here for long. It did not promise anything, since the Comb would not know a promise and the power did not want to bind the future of a thing whose whole life was walking toward it.

It only stood there in the white glare that casts no shadows, and listened.

Somewhere nearby a feather of ice let go and rang against the stone.

Then came the pause. The power had come in thinking of the pause as the place where the Comb lived. Now, having been held through a night inside it, it understood the pause differently. The pause was not emptiness between two sounds. It was the shape of trust, a whole being extended into the cold, holding out its edges with nothing in them yet, not doubting that the next thing would come.

The power had come to give, and had found almost nothing that ought to be given. It was being taught instead, the way the cairn was being taught: to lean a little toward the morning and not look back, to receive completely, to let go without being asked, and to hold nothing so that something downstream can hold everything.

It went down into the cloud.

Behind it the hollow closed with no sign it had been disturbed. That was the only thanks the Comb had to give, and the power took it as thanks.

Another ring.
