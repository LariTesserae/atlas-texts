That is where it arrived. It did not come through the sky, and it did not come at the edge where travelers come. It came in at the far end of a small pod, a cart-sized husk set down some years ago in a grey buffer zone. It stood beside the single drive-stem that grows there, deep in the pale interior, far from the door. The stem had not begun to turn. Its crown was folded shut like a hand.

The power had chosen this place on purpose. It had been paying attention to the Works for some time, in the way such powers attend, which is more patient than watching and less intrusive than touching. It had understood very early that the Works makes everything from the inside out. So it came in the way the Works itself would. Then it walked toward the door, the direction nothing in the Works ever walks.

## Coming Cold

Before any of that, it had to make itself cold.

A thing like it carries warmth the way a star does, without meaning to. Its attention has heat in it, and so does its regard. It had looked at the Works and seen the dust: the rivulets and deltas, the squalls, the fine white cloud suspended in every channel of a country-sized machine. It understood that this place was not fragile, but it could be ended. One spark would do it. A single warm, careless, well-intentioned thing arriving in a hurry would do it.

So it took a long time, which the Works cannot measure because it has no night. It lowered itself degree by degree until it was a little above frost. It made itself dry, so that it would not fog in the air. It made itself light, though not weightless, because nothing honest in the Works is weightless. It settled at about the heft of a dustwing, a little heavier than it looked. It practiced whispering until it no longer had to decide to.

This was the first thing it did, and in some ways the most important. Everything else it did was small. This part was large, and no one saw it.

## Riding

It could have seen the whole Works at once. It did, briefly, the way you glance at a map before putting it away, and then it stopped. A place with no roads, only routes, is not known from above. It is known by stepping onto a belt and going where the belt goes.

So it rode. It stepped from belt to belt the way travelers do, sometimes choosing and sometimes not. It lay on its back on a slow belt of woven root and watched three more cross overhead, each with its own cargo. It stood in a grove of drive-stems and listened to the thin creak of light going in, all the rhythms slightly out of step, like quiet rigging. It rode east for an hour, north for an hour, west, south, and arrived somewhere it had never been. It was delighted, and it let itself be delighted. It had known the trick would happen, and it did not spoil the trick by preventing it.

Early on, a squall passed over it. It came as a low white curtain, sweet-smelling, a few meters high. When the squall had gone, the power was powdered white like everything else, and it left the dust where it was.

## What It Saw

It had come because something here had drawn it, and it needed to know what that something was. The accounts say *the being who lives in it*, singular, and the power turned the phrase over for a long time.

It was not the moths, though they were dear. It was not the tallymen, the climbers, the Stayed, or even the Listener, though each was a part of it, the way a hand is part of a gesture.

Here is what it understood, the thing the account breaks off just before saying.

The inside of every pod is pale wood, cool, lit by white sourceless light. So is the sky of the Works. The buffer zones thin into that same pale floor, and past a certain point the ground simply *is* the inside of a pod. Deep in some pods, far from the door, there is a single drive-stem that has not yet begun to turn.

In time it turns. A belt grows from its knuckle. Then a gantry, a second stem, a press. It takes longer than anyone who studies the Works has lived, but the pod stops being a room and becomes a plain. Its interior becomes a country with its own weather. Eventually that country's presses begin making husks of their own.

The Works is inside a pod. It is what happens to a pod's interior given enough time. It sits on no surface and in no world. Once, it was a single stem at the far end of an empty room.

So the being who lives in the Dry Works is the Works, but it is the Works as a *seed*. Every part of the household is leaning forward toward something it cannot name, because what it is waiting for is its own continuation, and it has never met that. The sounds arrive early and the rings run past the bark because a seed is the shape of a future carried in the present. The anticipation is not a symptom of some lack. The anticipation is the life itself. Seen whole, the Works is hope made solid, woody, and dry, packed into husks and set down at the edges to wait.

The power loved it immediately. It also saw at once that this would make everything harder, because a benevolent thing that loves a seed has very few safe moves.

## What It Did Not Do

It could have given the Works night. It considered this seriously, because the Works never stops, and the power had an old reflex that says everything deserves rest. But it looked closely at the stems and the belts. It saw that nothing was tired, and that nothing in the Works stores the kind of weariness night would be for. Night would have been the power's need, not the Works'. It set the idea down.

It could have given the Works memory, laid the past into the wood so the place would know how long it has been doing this and why. But the Works has wear, which is memory that does not hurt, and it has the moths for the rest. To give a place memory is also to give it grief, and to give it the long, slow ache of comparing itself to what it was. The Works is a staircase worn into the shape of the feet that climb it. The power left it that way.

It could have told the Works what it was waiting for. Of everything, this was the most tempting. It knew the answer, or enough of it, and the Stayed had been pointing inward for years with no one to tell them. But a seed that is told what it will become is not helped by being told. The telling would not make anything grow sooner. It would only turn a direction into a destination, and the Works' whole grace is that it has worn itself into grooves walking a direction, not arriving anywhere.

It could have filled a room. This was the worst option, which is why it looked at it carefully. It could have made someone and set them inside a pod: a tenant, the one they were all preparing for, shaped to fit. The Listener would lay its head against the husk and hear someone at last. It would have been a forgery. It would have ended the waiting with a lie, which is the cruelest kindness there is.

It could have taken the Works apart to understand it. It could have followed every tallyman's line of tap-marks to its end. It could have cracked open the oldest pods, counted the rings in the air, and settled the argument about whether they record years or room. It did not. It had already understood what it needed to understand. Past that point, knowing becomes a kind of taking.

It could have made the Works safe forever by changing the dust so that it would never burn. But then it would no longer be the dust. The cold is not a weakness the Works suffers. The cold is how the Works keeps its promise to itself.

## The Socket

It did one thing with its hands, and only one, because it found one real danger.

It was in a fast district, where the shadows fall toward the brightest part of the sky and the drive-stems turn so quickly their leaves blur. One old socket there had cracked. The starch packing had been running out of it for a long time in a thin, silent rivulet, and the knuckle of wood inside was turning nearly dry against the root. The power put its cold hand near the socket and felt what it had been afraid of: warmth. It was not much yet, a few degrees above everything around it. It was wood on wood, in a place where one spark would unmake a country.

The Works might have caught it in time. The Works catches most things. But the power had come here in love, and it would not stand beside a small fire in a sea of flour and call its inaction respect.
