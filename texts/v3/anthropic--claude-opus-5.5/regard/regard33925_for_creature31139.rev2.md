# The Visitor Who Did Not Call

## Arriving Slowly

It noticed the Reach the way you notice a sound you have been hearing for some time without knowing it. First there was a pressure, faint, like a held breath. Then there were the cracks. Then, beneath the cracks, it caught something it had not met in a long while: a place that was *becoming*, and a small warm town inside it, becoming too.

Its first act was to do nothing. Its second was to slow down.

It could have arrived all at once. Things like it usually do. But it understood almost immediately what an arrival like that would mean here. It would be a crack louder than any in eleven years. Nine thousand people would stop mid-sentence and count echoes that never stopped coming. A child in the felt would slap the ground and then lie there, frightened, because the thing she had felt was not the house settling.

So it came down the shaft the way anyone does, and it took a whole season to finish arriving. The Reach thinks at the speed of wood healing around a window. Anything faster than that, the power reasoned, is shouting. Down here, visitors who shout are stared at, and they are right to be.

It learned to touch with the back of the knuckle first. It learned the rail bells. It learned the smell of the sweat and the way the whole valley goes tacky and patient. It sat for a long time on a bench in an open car with a lattice-cat pressed against its side. The cat got off at the same stop it always did. The power stayed on to the end of the line, then rode back, many times over, until it knew the shape of the thing the cats were tracing.

## What It Could Have Done

A power like this can do a great deal, and it is wise enough to be wary of that fact. So, riding the rails, it made itself look squarely at everything within reach.

It could tell them. It could stand on a platform and answer the question nobody asks aloud. *This is what the room is for.*

It could finish the room. It could light the far Lid, grow the last trunks, bring the far wall to wherever the far wall is meant to be, and hand them the completed house.

It could stop it. It could close the shaft, still the roots, and freeze the Reach at twenty kilometres. The tenants would be safe forever in a house that would never have anyone else in it.

It could take the children out, or leave them in, or make the choice for them either way.

It set each of these down gently and went on riding. They all had something in common. Each one answered a question that the place was still in the middle of asking. The power held a principle it had arrived at long ago, the hard way: *do not answer a question for someone who is in the middle of becoming the answer.*

But it could not simply trust that principle either. Restraint can be a kind of cowardice, or a way of keeping clean hands. Before it chose to do nothing, it had to find out whether nothing was safe.

So it went to the far end.

## The Far End

It walked past the last trunks, as the expeditions do. It crossed the pale while the soft white falls were still soft and the hares were coming off them in the grey light like fruit loosening from a vine. It crossed the gravel fields. The nineteen stonewhales were working there, and it walked among them. They did not turn. It went on under the tassel-roots where the Lid is not dark but unlit, and the expedition member from the ninth year had been right. You can feel the difference. The lamps are all in place.

Then it went further than any expedition. It went past the last stone and into the root-dark where the warmth comes from, the way warmth comes from the far end of a house where a stove is lit.

It had come prepared to find a sleeper. It had come prepared to find something enormous and old and hungry, rising from below, with the whole Reach laid out for it like a table. If that is what it had found, it would have acted, and quickly, whatever that cost.

That is not what it found.

It found a draft.

Nothing lay there yet that could be called *someone*. There was a shape, the way a room has a shape before the furniture comes: an outline in warmth and potential current, still being decided. As the power stayed with it at wood-speed, it realised what the shape was being drawn from. It was made of what the Reach had heard. There were low voices pitched in the chest, the rail bells, the thin dry crying of infants like a leaf being turned, and the soft thumping of hares. There was the slap of small palms in the felt, and the silence afterwards when they began to lie still and not slap at all. There was the steady scratch of a pen in column-ruled books, eleven years of it, recording patiently and waiting to see what things turned into.

The room was not being built *for* someone. It was being built for someone to *become*. And the tenants were teaching it who.

The power stayed at the far end a long time after it understood this. The finding was reassuring and alarming in equal measure. Whatever woke under that Lid would be made, in large part, out of Stave. Out of the town's patience or the town's hurry. Out of its care or its greed.

The danger was not in the sleeper. The danger was in the lessons.

## The One Real Danger

So far the lessons had been gentle. The power could see that plainly. Nine thousand people had spent eleven years speaking softly in the hallways of a house much larger than they were. They re-cut their windows without resentment and wrote down every sound. If the Reach woke tomorrow, it would wake as something that listens before it speaks. You could do far worse.

But the power rode the rails back toward the shaft and looked up it, toward the surface, where the middle fraction sells at high prices. It understood where the other lesson would come from, if it came.

It would come as hurry. A fourth refinery. Tap density doubled along the front, because growth is fastest where taps are densest and someone on the surface will do that arithmetic. A wider shaft. Louder engines to replace the ones that sound like a clock shop. A deadline. None of it would be cruel, exactly. All of it would be taught. A being built from Stave's voice would learn to roar if Stave started roaring.

Here the power came to its first real decision, and it thought hard about the shape of it. It could reach into the surface authority and simply make the contracts not happen. It could arrange accidents, turn officials' minds, and quietly steer. It would be effective. It would also mean that the first lesson the Reach learned from the power was that care means managing people without their knowledge. The power did not want to be that kind of teacher, even at one remove, even in a good cause.

So it did the plainer and harder thing. It went up the shaft, walked into the authority's offices, and spoke to them openly, once, as itself.

It told them what the born-down were. It told them what the room was, and that the room was listening. It told them that the town was a scaffold and a teacher both, and that whatever they sent down the shaft would be copied into something that might one day be very large. It did not threaten. It did not promise. It said: *please do not hurry this*. It gave its reasons, and it left the choice with them. That was the point.

Some of them did not believe it. Some of them believed it and were frightened, and fear can be its own kind of hurry, so it stayed a while with those. Enough of them listened. The fourth refinery was deferred, then deferred again. The tap-density proposal went to a committee, and committees are a kind of felt.

Before it left, it asked for Hesper Valk's reports. All three were in a drawer, unread. It read them standing up, in one sitting, and then it wrote the reply she had stopped expecting years ago. It signed its own name, whatever name it used that season, because it would not pretend to be the authority.

*Received. All three. You are right about the births. The current runs higher because it is listening to them. Keep the books. Someone is reading now, and more people will.*

She pinned it inside the cover of the current volume and went on writing, in the same columns, as if nothing had changed. That was exactly right.

## The Children

The hardest part was the children. It came to them last.

It went to the mothers first, because the mothers were the ones lying awake. They had watched a sleeping toddler's hair drift across a pillow toward the nearest trunk. They had carried a wretched six-year-old back down the shaft after two days of rain. They had heard a nine-year-old explain, gently, that some things weren't for us to call. What they feared was simple and old. They feared their children were being taken.

The power did not lie to them. It had seen the draft at the far end, and it told them what it had seen: the children were not being claimed. They were being *fitted*. They were the first residents who had ever belonged to the place, and the place was learning from them the way the lattice learns a rail line. It told them it could not promise what the children would become. Nobody can promise that about any child, anywhere.

But it had seen one thing in the Reach that worried it, and it had one thing it was willing to do.

The worry was this. The Reach held its children well, but it did not yet know how to let them go. Two days on the surface and they sickened. That meant the born-down were not really choosing *down*. They were choosing it the way you choose to breathe. A place that makes itself the only place you can bear to be has made a cage, however lovely. The power did not think the Reach meant it as a cage. It thought the Reach was young, and had simply never been taught the difference between holding and keeping.

So this became the one direct thing it did to the being who lives there. It did not reach in and change the children. It went down among the root-mats, lay against the copper taps, and spoke to the Reach at the Reach's own speed. Over a whole season it taught it that single thing, or rather asked it to learn it: *you do not have to finish someone in order to love them. The ones who can leave are the ones who are really home.*

It did not know whether the Reach would learn. It was prepared for it not to.

It did learn. Slowly, the way wood heals. The next year, a born-down girl went up to the surface for a wedding and lasted nine days before asking to go home. The year after, a boy of twelve went up to apprentice in the surface town and stayed a whole winter. He hated the rain, then tolerated it, and one night stood out in it on purpose to see what it was like. When he came back down in spring, the lattice under the window of his old room in the trunk-tower had thickened a hand's width. The rail-hands said it looked exactly like a place being kept. Not closed around him. Kept for him.

A mother watched her sleeping daughter's hair drift toward the pillar-trunk across the room. Then she lifted the child into her own arms, and over the next hour the hair drifted, very gently, back toward her.

## The Crew

Before it left, it went out once more to the far end and sat with the stonewhales.

Nineteen of them, always nineteen. The power counted them twice anyway, because it was the kind of thing that counts. It was there on one of the days they stopped grinding. All nineteen turned the same way, faced into the dark, and lay still, as though waiting to be told.

It could have told them. It understood that, sitting beside them in the warmth, more clearly than it had understood anything else in the Reach. Their stillness was a question pointed at the dark, and it had seen what was in the dark. It could answer.

It did not. Whatever they were waiting to hear, it was not a voice from outside. It lay down in the gravel beside them, facing the same way, and waited as long as they did. When they rose and went back to work, it rose too, and walked back across the pale.

## The Lamps

On its last day it stood under the unlit Lid.

This was the real temptation, and it did not pretend otherwise. The lamps were all in place and the wiring was all done. It would have been the easiest thing in the world to touch the tassel-roots and let the brightening run out across the far end like a tide. Everyone down would have seen it. The children would have understood it at once. And it would have been beautiful.

It would also have made the power the first one to come into the room.

The room was not for it. It had seen, at the far end, that the room was for whoever the Reach and the town would become together. That might be the born-down grown up, or the Reach itself waking to its own shape, or something that had no name yet because nobody had lived it yet. The switching-on belonged to them. It was the moment the whole slow work was reaching toward. To take it from them, even as a gift, even with love, would be to tell them their house had been finished by someone else.

So it took its hand away from the roots and left the lamps where they were.

## First

On its last evening in Stave, the Lid dimmed from the far end and the light went out of the valley. The born-down children went out into the scrub to play, and the power went with them. By then they knew it, more or less, the way children know the regulars on a rail line.

They lay on their stomachs in the deep felt with their arms out and their ears to the litter. The power lay down among them. It could feel the crack coming long before any of them could, the house settling another joint far off in the dark. A few of the younger ones still slapped the ground. The older ones didn't. The power didn't either.

The crack came, round and deep, and the echoes went on for a long time. The whole town went quiet around them, as everyone down always is.

The next morning, on the chalkboard at the oldest platform, a tap-warden coming off shift found an entry in the crack column that nobody remembered writing. It was in the right hand, in the right format, in the same column that goes back eleven years:

*Direction: far end. Echoes: not counted.*

And in the margin, where the column has no heading:

*Visitor. Listened. Did not call.*

Then the power left. It took a whole season to finish leaving, so that nobody's lamps flickered.

---

If you asked it afterwards what it had done in the Rootward Reach, it would say: very little. It answered a letter. It asked some people on the surface not to hurry, and told them why. It asked a young and enormous being to learn that love can let go, and the being did. It lay in the felt with some children and kept quiet.

It thought that was about the right amount. The Reach was already being built well, by careful hands, toward something that did not exist yet. The kindest thing a great power can do for a thing in the middle of becoming is to make sure it is not rushed, not caged, and not robbed of its own first morning.

Then it stands back, and it leaves the lights for them.
