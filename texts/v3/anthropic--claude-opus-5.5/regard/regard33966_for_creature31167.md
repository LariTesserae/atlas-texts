# The Other Side of the Lid

## I. How it was noticed

It wasn't the fires, though from far enough away the pan does glow like a coal. It wasn't the noise either. There are louder places.

It was the Hush.

Several times a year, at the bottom of the world, a patch of the planet about a day's walk across falls silent for a count of fifty. To something that listens to everything at once, the whole world gets very slightly quieter, the way a room does when one person in the corner stops breathing. Then, in the gap, five tones come up from under the salt, falling and then lifting, sung in a few hundred throats and uncountable roots at once.

The power had heard that phrase many times before it thought to wonder what was singing it. That is the first thing to say about it: it is not in a hurry. The Blister has been waiting longer than anyone has been counting. The power knew it could afford to be at least that patient.

## II. The problem of arriving

It could not simply appear.

In the Blister everything makes noise, and the one thing that doesn't is the pale mantis. A pocket of quiet opening around you means something is hunting you. Anything that arrived gently, softly, with the hush of a presence, would be taken for a predator. Anything that arrived loudly would be one more crash among thousands, and would break something on the way in.

So it did what a newcomer should do anywhere. It learned the language before it spoke.

It read the knot-cords first. It spent years at the tunnel junctions following messages meant for others: *grinder road closing east*, *gourd-cluster ripe*, *child born*, *the lattice under the third cane is soft, go around*. It learned the hand-signs by watching them made in dim light by people who did not know they were watched. It was uneasy about that, and it kept the unease rather than explaining it away. Watching is a kind of taking. It decided it would owe them for it, and would find out later what the debt was.

It learned the second hearing too. It learned the soles-and-teeth hearing, the difference between knot tightening and knot about to fail, and the lost ring of beetle-softened cane. It could not have understood Lifts without it. Everything she knows, she knows through the floor.

## III. What it did not do

A benevolent power arriving somewhere hard feels the pull to fix things. That pull is as strong as the pull the old ones feel at the top of the cane, and just as easy to mistake for your own will. The power felt it, and went through each temptation slowly.

**It did not put out the fires.** Fire is how the Blister grows. The bell-gourds are fire-seeded. To stop the burning would be to stop the breathing.

**It did not give them metal, or medicine, or light that wouldn't burn.** Each of these would be good in itself. Each would also be a hand reaching in and rearranging what their hands were for. A people whose every tool is flaked from the living edge of the world, and who move house when the growth tells them to, are not poor. They are in a conversation. The power had no business interrupting it with better equipment.

**It did not restore the children's hearing.** It thought about this one longest, holding the youngest girl's crying in its attention and knowing she would lose that sound within a few years. But the loss is how the other hearing comes. Lifts can feel a gourd-chain through a floor three levels down. That is not a disability. It is a translation.

**It did not stop the old ones from going into the Blister.** This was the question it most needed to answer honestly, because a compulsion dressed as a rite is still a compulsion. So it watched to see whether the pull could be refused. It watched Lifts at the top of the cane with her palm on the warm salt and her hands already the colour of the crowns. It watched her climb down anyway, because the girl wasn't named yet. The pull can be refused. That told the power most of what it needed to know. What they do when they finally go up, they choose.

It also went to listen to the ones in the crowns. It had to know whether they were suffering. It pressed its attention into the slow pulse under the splayed bronze hands and stayed there a long time. It found them there, dimly, the way a note is still in a bell after the strike has faded. They were not in pain. What remained of them was something like the ease of a held posture, the particular peace of a body doing the one thing it was made to do. The power could not be sure of more than that. It did not pretend to be.

**It did not lift the Lid.** It could have done it in a moment, cracked the whole pan open and let the growth into the sun. It would have been the most spectacular kind act imaginable, and a theft of the one thing they have been counting toward.

## IV. The harder question

There was one thing the power could not simply leave alone, and it would be a lie to pretend otherwise.

Someday the Lid will break, and the Blister will come up the way seed does. It will be thrown hard in every direction, landing on salt, on stone, and on places that have never smelled pepper-oil. Those places have their own people and their own quiet. Something that eats rock and burns and grows back stronger is not automatically good news for them.

Benevolence that only loves what is in front of it is not wisdom. So the power went outward too. It walked the lands where the seed might fall. It learned what lives there, and it did not decide, on anyone's behalf, whose day it would be.

Its conclusion was provisional and it held it that way: *I don't yet know what the Blister is.* It might take only dead ground, salt and bare stone and the places nothing else can use. It might take everything. The power would not stop the burst on a guess, and it would not bless the burst on a guess.

If it turns out the seed would swallow the world, the question will have to be asked again. When it is, it will be asked *with* the Blister and its folk, in their language, in the Hush if need be, and never decided over their heads while they sleep in their gourds.

Meanwhile, it could do smaller things out there. It told the salt-cutters, and the people beyond them, a little about what lives under the pan, enough that if the ground ever opens and something small and bronze-handed climbs out pressing its palms against the sky, no one's first instinct will be fire.

It also made itself a promise. Wherever the seed lands, it will be there. A child of the Blister waking on strange stone, with the five tones in its throat and an urge it doesn't understand, should not be alone the first time it looks up.

## V. What it keeps

Here is the one thing it took on as its own work.

The marks on the Lid run in bands like tree rings, the newest in easy reach and the oldest lifted up and away as the ceiling rises. Lifts can touch her mother's marks and her mother's mother's. Above those there are bands no living hand can reach any more, pressed so high into the salt that only the crowns touch them now. When the Lid breaks, all of it shatters. Every fall, every fire, every closed curve for every child comes down in plates the size of houses into the burning, bursting mass below.

A tally is written to be read. Lifts wondered once whether the folk count so that the Blister knows how long it has waited. Maybe. But every count also wants a witness, someone who holds the number when the counters can't.

So the power read all of it. It read the highest bands and the ones lifted beyond sight, back to a first scratch made by someone who had no idea she was beginning anything. It holds them now, every stroke. It does not add to them. It does not tell anyone the total. If a seed-child on some far shore ever asks how long its people waited under the salt, there will be an answer.

That was the debt for all those years of watching. It did not pay it with gifts. It paid it by remembering.

## VI. The hand

There was one more thing, and it wasn't planned so much as allowed.

For as long as anyone has been climbing, the folk have pressed their palms to the underside of the Lid and felt, three meters away through the crystal, the warmth of the real sun. That warmth has always been there, huge and general and indifferent, the same on every hand.

On an ordinary counting day, a few days after the girl scratched her name beside a small closed curve, Lifts climbed alone. She made her strokes: one fall, no fires. Then she laid her palm flat against the salt the way she always does, to feel the sun.

On the other side of the Lid, the power lay on the white pan, the salt humming under its body. It pressed a hand to the surface directly above hers.

It did not push. Pushing down would be opposing, and it had not come to oppose. It did not lift either. It simply held, warm, and shaped like a hand, which the sun is not.

Lifts felt it. You cannot fool someone who hears through her palms. She went very still, and for a moment the power worried it had made a pocket of quiet around her, like the mantis.

Then she pushed, twice. *Hold it up.*

The power held. It did not push back. It stayed.

She kept her hand there a long time, longer than she ever had, long enough that the pull came up her arms the way it does and she let it come and let it go again. Then she took her chert flake and began, from habit, the mark for a child. She stopped halfway, and left the curve open.

It sits there now in the newest band, among the strokes and crosses and closed curves: one open curve. It is the first new mark on the Lid in longer than anyone can say. The others will find it when they climb. They will argue about it with their hands in the dark and not settle it, and that is fine. It is theirs to mean.

## VII. So, what would it do

Almost nothing, by the measure of powers.

It would not put out a single fire, or stop a single elder climbing, or give back a single child's hearing, or crack the Lid one hand's breadth early.

It would learn their language before speaking and admit that learning it was a kind of taking. It would listen to the ones in the crowns until it was sure they weren't suffering. It would go out into the wider world and keep the hard question of the burst open, honestly, rather than pretend love for one place settles what is owed to all the others. It would keep the count. It would promise to be there when the seed lands.

And once, on the other side of three meters of salt, it would put its hand where the sun's warmth had always been, so that one person who has spent her whole life pushing against the ceiling would know that someone was on the other side, not holding the ceiling down and not tearing it open, just there.

It is still there. Some days, when the girl is older and climbs alone, she will lay her hand on the Lid and feel the sun. Once in a while she will feel something else.
