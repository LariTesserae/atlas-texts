# What the Power Did

## Attention

It noticed the hollow the way one notices an absence in a crowded sound, the way a room goes quiet and only then do you realize it had been humming.

The power was used to the world as a roar. Everywhere it turned its attention, things were spilling. Forests pushed into grassland. Grassland crept over the ruins of forest. Rivers shifted and took down their banks. Cities swelled. Insects bred past their food and died back and bred again. The power loved all of it, the way one loves weather. But it was loud, and it was all *more*, and it was all *running away with itself*, and the power had spent a great deal of its long attention easing that running where it could: a drought softened here, a fever turned there, a small mercy tucked into the gap between one catastrophe and the next.

Then, on a low grey coast, a point of silence. Forty paces of it.

The power looked, and kept looking. It looked the way you would put your hand on the back wall to learn what is carved there. Slowly it understood that the silence was not empty. Something was holding it. Something lay under the western shelf, face to the stone, keeping a single moment level, one breath at a time.

The power's first feeling was the one it always had on finding a life: *what does it need?*

Its second feeling, arriving more slowly, was stranger. It was something close to shyness.

## The Gifts It Set Down

A power like this does not act on its first feeling. It turns the feeling over first. So before it did anything, it considered what it might give, and it set each gift down in turn.

**Company.** The people had stopped coming. The eleven steps were worn to dishes by feet that were gone. The power could send them back, or send others: pilgrims, a village, a small shrine with a keeper. But the resident did not want company. It only did not mind company. And a village is excess. A village breeds past its well. Within three generations someone would widen the channel, and someone else would decide the basin ought to be deeper, and the moss moons would be scraped off the steps to make them safe. The power set company down.

**A name.** The power could name it truly, the way powers can, so that the name would sit in the world and be known. But a name is a second line on the basin wall. It says *here is the moment when you were noticed*, and the resident had never needed a moment other than the one it was in. The power set the name down.

**Waking.** This one tempted it longest. The resident lived in one instant and attended to it perfectly. It had never known a second thing. Wasn't that a kind of poverty? The power could show it the sea it had never seen, the fig tree whose root it held, the vastness of the flats, the turning of stars it had kept its face away from. It could give the resident *more*.

The power sat with that for what, in the hollow, would have been a very long time. At last it recognized the shape of the thought. It was the thought of a mosquito. It was the hum over still water, the small frantic insistence that stillness must be a lack. The resident had not turned its face from the sky out of ignorance. It had turned toward the back wall the way you turn toward the person you love in a crowded room. It was not waiting to be woken. It was not waiting for anything, and it never had been.

The power set waking down. It was surprised at how much this cost it, and it made a note of that cost.

**Life.** It could let the insects in, and the birds, and let the ferns run past the wet. Abundance was a gift the power gave often and gladly. Here, abundance would be a flood. It set life down.

**Protection.** This was the hardest, and it did not set it down at once. We will come back to it.

## Becoming Small

When the power had set down everything it might give, it found it still wanted to go there. It asked itself why, honestly, and the answer was simple: to sit on the wall. To hear it.

That raised a problem. The power could not arrive as itself. It was far too much. Its attention alone, poured into forty paces, would be heat arriving faster than any flame. The warm air would stir. The drip might quicken, or the ferns lean toward it. It would be the loudest thing ever to enter the hollow. The resident would receive it the way it received everything, and try to balance it, and there would be too much to balance. For the first time in its whole long moment, the hollow might strain.

So the power made itself small.

This is harder for a power than it sounds. Growing is easy, and so is spreading, and so is being everywhere at once. Being exactly one size, in one place, with one body's warmth and one body's breath, means refusing nearly everything you are. The power did it slowly, the way limestone rounds an edge. When it was done it was about the size of a person. It was warm the way skin is warm. It carried nothing, because it had thought about carrying a jar and decided it would not take enough water to need one.

It walked across the flats. The scrub was grey, the soil thin, the land barely above a sea it could not see. It did not hurry. Then the ground stopped, and there was the hole.

## Going Down

It took the steps one at a time, as they asked to be taken.

It noticed the moss in the back corner of every tread and placed its feet in the dishes the old feet had worn, so that it touched none of the eleven moons. At the fourth step the air stopped moving. At the eighth it grew close, the way a room feels when many people have just left, and the power thought: *yes. That is exactly what happened here.* At the bottom the warmth met its skin, matched it, and the power stopped feeling where it ended. It let that happen. It had come a long way to let that happen.

Its eyes began adjusting to the grey-gold dim and did not finish, and it did not make them finish. It could have seen perfectly. It chose to see the way the place allowed.

It crossed the bare floor between the smooth pale backs rising through the grit, and it stepped around them, not over them. At the low wall it stopped. It did not cross. It sat on the edge, the way the wall said one could.

*Tock.*

The faint ring. The overhang returned it, once.

Silence.

*Tock.*

The power's breath lengthened. It did not decide this, and it did not resist it. After a while, and it did not know how long, because the hollow takes the edges off time and the power had agreed to let it, it stopped hearing the drip as a sound. What it heard was the resident confirming, once a breath, that everything was as it was.

*Level. Level. Level.*

The power had listened to a great many prayers in its time. Most of them asked for something to change. It had never before heard a voice whose whole speech was *this, still this, still this*. It found, sitting there, that it had nothing to add.

## The Drink

At some point it got up, went to the basin, and knelt.

The water sat its finger's width below the lip. The bottom was pale. If you kneel long enough, the source says, the paleness begins to seem to have a depth. The power knelt long enough, and it saw what the people had half-seen. It was not a floor. It was a surface, lying very close, the way skin lies under a thin film of water held in a cupped palm.

The power could have looked further. It did not.

It cupped its hands and drank once. It was careful to take a mouthful, not a jarful: an amount a person might take, an amount that would come back by morning without strain. The level went down a hair. Then the power sat back on its heels and breathed out, slowly, over the basin. It gave back breath, warmth, and attention, which was the old currency. It paid the way the people had paid.

It did not know whether the resident noticed. Perhaps a drink and a breath were simply part of the moment, as they always had been. Perhaps no one had ever been missed, because in a single instant nothing can be missed. The power found that it did not need to know. That surprised it too.

## The Flame

It went to the niche in the back wall, chest-high, the size of two cupped hands, with its floor faintly blackened and the darker smudge above where flame had licked up a thousand thousand times.

Here the power allowed itself one small thing, because it was an old thing and not a new one.

It made a flame. A power can make a flame from nothing, but it made this one the way the people would have: small, from a little oil and a twist of fiber it had brought from the flats for this and nothing else. It set the flame in the niche and let it burn. It was a little warmth arriving faster than the day brings warmth, a little surplus, offered in the one way the hollow had ever been offered anything, knowing it would be accepted and returned.

The flame burned for about an hour. The stone above it drank the heat.

The power did not leave. It went back to the wall and sat through dusk. Dusk is one of the hollow's only real events, and it is a quiet one: a slow withdrawal of the already-tired light until the grey-gold went grey, and then went.

In the night the wall gave the warmth back. The power felt it arrive spread thin, patient, and exact, the flame's hour returned across the whole of the dark. Nothing was gained. Nothing was lost. Everything was exchanged.

That was the whole conversation. The power understood that it had been answered. It had not been thanked, and the resident had not woken or turned. It had been answered in the only grammar the place had: *what you gave, I give back.* It was the most courteous reply the power had ever received.

## The Sea

Near morning the power let itself, just for a moment, be a little larger again. It did not take a larger body. It took a larger view. It looked forward.

It saw what it had been avoiding. The land here is barely above the sea. The ice at the far ends of the world will do what ice does, and the sea will do what the sea does, over spans the resident would call a single breath. One day, whether in ten thousand years or fewer, water will come up through the porous floor from below. It will not come as rain that soaks away within the hour. It will be water that does not leave. The basin will rise to its lip, and then over it. The mineral line will be drowned under a second line, and a third, and then there will be no lines at all, only water.

This was the gift the power had not set down. Protection. It could hold the sea back from forty paces of coast. It would be easy. A small, permanent exception to the world's largest balance, a bubble of dry stone kept safe, so that the resident's one moment could go on and on.

The power looked at that for a long while.

Then it looked at the channel.

It was dry, squared off, and cut toward the open floor by practical people who had expected an overflow. For longer than anyone could say it had been waiting for a day that never came. The power had taken it, as everyone had, as a sign that the people had misjudged their neighbor, that they had not yet learned it gave no more than it held.

Now it saw that it had misjudged the people. They had not been wrong. They had been patient in a way even the power had not been. They knew the resident did not overflow, and they cut the channel anyway. They did not do it to force anything. They did it so that if a day ever came when the moment could not hold, the water would have somewhere gentle to go. They had not tried to stop the change. They had made a path for it.

And the power, sitting on the wall with the returned warmth still in the stone beneath it, saw one more thing. The resident's only law was balance. It did not fight the rain. It did not fight the fig. It did not fight the shards. It took whatever stayed beside it long enough into its own temperature and color and pace. The sea is the largest balance there is. When it comes, it will not break the resident. It will be the resident's first true equal: something that takes and gives back at the scale of the whole world. The pale back under the grit will become sea floor. The drinker in the basin will become one mouth among the ocean's. The single moment will not end. It will widen.

The power did not know whether this would be grief or homecoming. It admitted that to itself, and set protection down.

## What It Did

It did one more thing before it left. It is the only thing anyone could point to and say *the power did this.*

It knelt by the channel. Over the years a little grit had drifted into the groove, where rain from the open side had washed it and the stone had filmed it pale. The power brushed it out with its hand, slowly, the length of the channel, and laid the grit beside the groove on the floor. It took nothing away and brought nothing in. It only cleared the path, as the people would have wanted, so that when the day came the water would find its way already open.

In the hollow's reckoning this changed nothing. The level held. The mineral line stayed single and crisp and unrepeated. The drop fell. It was the kind of thing the people might have done on any visit, a small maintenance and a courtesy, and if the resident noticed at all, it noticed it as part of the moment it had always been in.

Then the power climbed out. It took the eleven steps in their dishes, touched none of the moons, and walked across the flats as the wind passed over the rim with its soft hollow breath. When it was far enough away that its size could not trouble anything, it let itself be large again.

It went back to the roaring world, and to its fevers, droughts and mercies, and to all the things that spill. It went back a little changed. It did not think it had fixed anything, and it had not. What it had learned was that there is a kind of life for which the kindest thing is to arrive as a guest and not a gift. With such a life, you take one mouthful and breathe it back, light one small flame and sit through the night to receive its warmth again, clear the path someone else already cut, and leave.

The people came with water jars and fire and patience, and they left with all of it but the shards.

The power came with nothing and left with nothing. The one thing it left behind, it found, was the thing it could most easily spare, though it had not known that until it sat on the wall: the need to have done something.

*Tock.*

The faint ring. The overhang returned it, once.

*Level.*
