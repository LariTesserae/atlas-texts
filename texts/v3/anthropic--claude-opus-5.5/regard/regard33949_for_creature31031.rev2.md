# What the Power Would Do

## Noticing

It notices the highland the way you notice a clock ticking wrong in a quiet room.

From far enough up, as the old description promised, the bellows-trees resolve into waves. Pressure-pulses ring outward across the sward from ten thousand centers and cross one another. Where they reinforce, the gold grows a fraction brighter. Where they cancel, it dims. From that height the whole plateau shimmers with interference patterns, like a pond in rain, except the rain has been falling for longer than there have been continents.

The power has seen many interference patterns. It has seen tides and nebulae and the firing of neurons, and it knows what random drift looks like. This isn't random. The phases wander toward alignment, and just before they align something nudges them apart. It happens everywhere, patiently, forever, a hand keeping a spinning plate from settling. Something here is choosing, and the power lowers its attention to find out what.

It finds a hollow tree, and in the doorway a grey figure with one palm flat against the trunk.

## The Temptations

A benevolent power arrives already knowing a hundred kind things it could do. Wisdom mostly means the slow work of discovering which of them would be cruel. So it considers them one at a time, with the attention they deserve.

**It could make it quiet.** The Rests have given up sound, and every one of them lives inside the only muffled places in a world that roars. The power could lay a stillness over every husk and let them hear their own bodies clearly for the first time. But the Rests feel the drift of the heartbeat through their feet and through their glass. Their work is listening to the noise. A Rest in true silence would be a doctor with no pulse to take. The husks are exactly as quiet as they need to be: quiet enough to think, loud enough to hear the patient.

**It could give them water.** Every Rest spends its life thirsty and gives its hoard away drop by drop. Rain would be the obvious mercy, the ancient one. Then the power thinks it through. On this plateau nothing rots, because nothing is wet. Every drover's dung is dust, and every fallen glider becomes chaff for the threshers. The whole ten-thousand-year settlement of the land rests on dryness. One real rain would wake a kingdom of decay that has never had the chance to exist. The sward would soften and sour, and the deep layers, where the old Rests lie remembered in glass, would begin to be *digested*. A gift of water here would be a plague.

Scarcity also gives the Rests' one gesture its meaning. *I could have taken, and I gave* only means something where water is the most precious thing there is. Make it plentiful and the gesture becomes a formality.

**It could end the hunting.** A drover's life ends in stillness under a grey folding shape, leaving a husk of dust in the shape of an animal. The power could spare them. But the stilt is the juvenile. Every Rest was once a hunter and carries its first years' drinking for the rest of its long life, spending it on others. Ending the hunt would end the Rests, and the heartbeat would drift into step, and the drovers would die anyway, all at once, in whatever happens when a world has one thought. The power doesn't like this arithmetic. It is merciful and it wishes things were otherwise. It also knows that wishing things were otherwise is where a great deal of harm begins.

**It could guarantee the outcome.** This temptation is the subtlest, because it looks like service. The Rests fear the seizure, the single breath. The power could make sure that never happens by pinning the phases apart from above, forever. It could relieve them of their vigilance.

What would that make of them? They would be climbers of trees whose climbing no longer mattered, and their blue script would become ceremony. It would take the meaning of every life lived here and lay it down gently, and call that kindness. The power has seen this done in other places, by other benevolent things. It has seen the look of a people who were rescued from their purpose.

**It could tell them.** This one it holds longest.

The Rests have never seen the waves. They know the highland only through their feet, through the glass in their bones, and through the faint light of the next husk over. Each works from a partial, local, uncertain picture. The power could show them the whole thing, the interference pattern from above. It could show them every phase and every drift, whether their guess about the seizure is right, and what lies at the bottom of the sward. It could do this for all of them at once.

Then every Rest would know the same thing, see the same optimal cut, and climb the same trees at the same moment to fold the same pleats.

The power sees it plainly: the Rests would synchronize.

Their disagreement is the medicine. Each one nudges from its own small knowledge, differently from its neighbors, sometimes wrongly. That scatter is what keeps the heartbeat truly unpredictable, so that no hidden rhythm in their corrections could ever build into resonance. A perfect shared view would turn the ones who keep the world out of step into the most synchronized thing on the plateau.

## The Recognition

After this the power is quiet for a long while, by its own standards. That's a strange state for a thing that has just been paying close attention to a place with no quiet in it.

The power understands that it is the danger here.

It is the largest single rhythm that has ever touched this plateau: one will, one view, and one benevolence able to reach everywhere at once. Anything it did to the whole highland would arrive everywhere in phase, and that is the definition of the thing the Rests have spent every life since the beginning preventing. Its goodness, applied evenly, would be the one breath.

The Rests already understand what the power has only now learned about itself: in some places, care has to be local, partial, and a little bit wrong, or it becomes a seizure.

So the power decides, as the Rests do, to keep itself out of step. It will not act on the whole. It will pick one place and one small cut, and let the rest drift.

## The Drop

It chooses the oldest Rest it can find.

This one stands in a husk far out toward where the plateau's edges become theoretical. Its skeleton is so laced with fulgurite that at dawn it glitters from within like a jar of frost. Its keel is nearly empty. It has one bladder left and one drop of water in it, which it has been saving without knowing for whom. It is close to the time of lying down.

The power comes down on a storm night, because that is the only time anyone here speaks.

It does not make itself visible, because a visible god on this plateau would be one more thing for the Rests to synchronize around. It comes the way the stilt comes, silently and slowly, a gap in the vibration. The old Rest feels something vast and still arrive in the doorway of its husk, and every reed-bone in its body remembers being young. It remembers the posture, the fold, the going-still. It knows what it means when something larger folds down over you.

The old Rest goes still, because it has given away nearly everything and has one drop left, and it is ready for that to be taken too.

The power folds down over it the way a Rest folds over a stilt, and does not take. It opens itself and lets one drop pass into the old one's keel. The drop is water from here: the power has drawn it molecule by molecule out of the dry storm-air over a thousand kilometers of sward, so the plateau is poorer by nothing it would notice. Then it withdraws.

That is all. There is no message and no light. It is the one gesture on the highland that has never needed blue script to mean something, returned for the first time from outside the world, by something that could have taken everything.

The old Rest stands in its doorway until dawn with its hand against the trunk, and the drift of a hundred heartbeats runs through its glass.

## The Question

Later, and only once, the power asks.

It asks in the Rests' own manner, writing in faint blue discharge across the inner wall of the husk. It uses the lettering of storm nights, legible to anyone twelve kilometers off, so the asking is not private. Any Rest on that horizon can read it, and each will read it differently, which is correct.

*Is there anything you want?*

The old Rest reads it for most of a night. Then light crawls slowly across its skin, and the script is simple, because by now it is very old and has spent a long time deciding which things are worth saying.

*Stay out of step.*

Then, a little later and fainter, the way someone speaks after they think the conversation is over:

*Come back sometimes. Not on a schedule.*

The power understands both of these completely. The first is a warning and a blessing: the old Rest has recognized the danger in the thing standing in its doorway, and it has forgiven it in advance. The second is an invitation shaped like the only rule that matters here. Anything that returns regularly becomes a rhythm, and a rhythm can be joined. So come irregularly. Arrive as an arrhythmia.

## The Bottom

The power could look down.

It could see through the gold and the brown and the glossy black, past glass veins crossing glass veins, to whatever the plateau first colonized and dissolved and forgot, or to the discovery that there is no bottom at all. It could learn whether the lattice really is the highland's memory, and whether the sunk Rests are, in any sense they would recognize, still there.

It doesn't look.

This isn't because looking would harm anything. Probably it wouldn't. But the Rests have a belief about the deep strata. They say, in slow script on storm nights, that they know what is down there because the old ones went. That belief is the shape of their ending, and each one lies down into it. The power would then know something the highland had not chosen to tell it. Some things a place keeps are not secrets to be uncovered but the place's own privacy, and the respectful thing is to let them stay unwitnessed. The power has looked at nearly everything, and it has found that the few things it leaves unlooked-at are among the things it loves most.

## Dawn

The storm blows itself out. The dust settles. The ridges lie exactly as they were, combed in the same direction and gleaming with the night's new glass. Nothing has changed, and the power intends it that way.

Out in the reed-fields a young stilt is walking in from its hunting years, grey and silent and starving, not yet sure what it is. It meets a tall figure glittering from within like frost, standing in its path.

The old Rest folds down over it. The young one feels the killing posture and goes still. The old one opens its last bladder and lets the drop pass: its own saved drop, or the one it was given, or both, since by now the two are indistinguishable. Then it walks back to its husk and, over the following seasons, lies down.

So the power's gift doesn't stay where it was put. Within a single night it has passed into the circulation of the highland, given in the posture of predation, carried forward by a creature who will someday give it away again. It becomes part of the drift and impossible to trace, which is what the power wanted. A gift that stayed conspicuous would be a monument, and a monument is a thing that others can fall into step around.

Far overhead, no longer paying full attention, the power watches the interference patterns shimmer across the sward. Somewhere a Rest climbs a living bellows-tree and puts a fold in a single pleat that is slightly wrong, not the fold the power would have chosen. It's a little late, a little clumsy, and made from a partial knowledge that belongs to that Rest alone.

The heartbeat stumbles, recovers, and stays beautifully out of time.

The power turns away, not for long and not on a schedule. When it comes back it will arrive the way everything good arrives here: slowly and silently, felt as a gap in the vibration, bringing a drop and leaving before anyone can learn its rhythm.
