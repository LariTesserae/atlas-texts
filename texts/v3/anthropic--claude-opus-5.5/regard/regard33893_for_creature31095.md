# The Visitor at the Lip

## Arrival

It did not come down from the violet sky, though it could have. It did not rise through the cloud-floor, though it could have done that too. It came the way the people came: up the lower slopes, over the crest, and onto the long pale shelf of granite, following the cairns.

It chose to walk because the cairns were there. Someone had built them low and flat, knee-high, so the wind could not take them. Someone had understood the wind, and someone had wanted the way to be findable. To ignore a path laid that carefully and arrive some faster way would have been a small rudeness. Wise powers avoid small rudenesses. They are how the large ones start.

So it walked, and the air grew thin, and the roar grew until it stopped being sound and became a condition. The power let it. It could have dulled the roar, or stood a little outside it. But the place was mostly this sound, and you cannot visit a place while refusing the thing it is made of.

On the second day it noticed that it had begun to hear silence. It noticed this with interest and some respect. Then it did something that took more effort than anything else it would do on the plateau. It kept hearing the roar anyway.

That was how it learned what the creature's life cost.

## What It Saw

It sat in one of the smoothed hollows, at the respectful distance, with its back to the wind. It put its palm on the stone.

It felt the grazers' patter, like rain on the far side of a wall. It felt the fundamental of the Throat in the granite, vast and even. Beneath that, as the stories said, it felt a second note, exactly the same, with no beating between them at all. It was a stillness that had been made, and was still being made, and would have to be made again in the next instant, and the next, without end.

A power like this one can see a great deal if it chooses. It could have looked beneath the cloud and read the pressure of the warm heavy air against the mountain's flank. It could have counted the years left before the weather tilted. It could have looked into the long lichen-backed body and learned, in a moment, the answer to the harder thought: whether the creature only measures the note, or holds it up.

It did not look. That answer was the creature's own, and the creature had not offered it. Some things stay yours only while nobody else knows them. The power understood that knowledge taken without permission is still taken.

## What It Considered

It considered, as powers do, all the things it could mend.

**It could make the wind eternal.** It could reach below the cloud and fix the pressure there, so the Throat would sound forever, the creature's vigil would never end in failure, and the note would never fall. This was the first temptation and the easiest to refuse. The place was beautiful *because* it was in balance, and the balance was real only because it could someday tip. A wind that could not stop would no longer be a wind. It would be a monument to one, and the creature's ten thousand years of listening would become listening to a recording. The power would not do that to it.

**It could let the creature sleep.** *It does not sleep. It cannot afford to.* The power lingered longest over this one. It could hold the note itself, in perfect unison, and let the long body at the lip rest for a night, or a year, or a century. That would be real mercy, and it wanted to give it very badly.

But it thought about what the creature was. It was the one thing on the plateau that had refused the ordinary bargain. Everything else had stopped hearing, and this had not. To walk up and say *let me hear for you* would be to offer it the same bargain again, dressed as kindness. Perhaps the creature would want that. Perhaps it would not. The power could offer. It could not decide.

**It could give the swifts back their voices.** It could make the plateau able to carry song, so the flutes could be played and heard. It refused this almost at once. The swifts had become something through their silence: a colony that speaks in glints, a cliff that sparkles when you look at it low against the sun. Nobody who loved them would take that from them in order to hear them.

**It could make the creature known.** It could carry word down to the valleys, so more people would come and sit in the hollows. It did not. The creature had the visitors it had, and they had found it themselves, and built the cairns themselves. A thing found is different from a thing announced.

So the power set aside, one by one, everything it could fix. What remained was smaller, and it felt right.

## What It Did

It noticed that the creature had spent ten thousand years listening, and had never, as far as anything could tell, been listened to.

The people had felt it through their palms, but faintly, under the roar and the patter, as a stillness. They had felt the *absence of beating*, which is the evidence of the note and not the note. The flute-player had played *for* it. The grazers stamped and were answered. But nobody had ever heard the creature's voice as a voice, and nobody had ever answered it in kind.

There was one more thing. The only thing that had ever come to the creature through its note was warning. Every nest in the fluted walls arrived as an error, and it corrected. Every fledging arrived as an error, and it corrected. Every change the plateau had ever offered it came as a small wrongness to be leaned against. It had lived its whole life knowing one meaning for a throb: *something is drifting, and you must hold.* And it was waiting, all the time, for the last throb, the one it would not be able to correct.

So the power did this.

It pressed its palm to the granite, and very gently it sang.

It did not sing in unison, not at first. It sang a hair's breadth off, just flat of the Throat. Between its voice and the creature's there came a beat, slow and soft and unmistakable. *wah... wah... wah...*

It felt the long body at the lip go taut. Ten thousand years of vigilance gathered itself to lean against the drift. The creature searched the Throat for the error, and found none. The mountain's note held. The wind below the cloud had not wavered. The beating came from somewhere else. It came from a hollow at the respectful distance, where something was sitting with its palm on the stone.

The power held the beat long enough to be sure it had been felt as what it was. It was not a failing, and not a warning. It was a second voice, near but not the same, saying *here*.

Then, slowly, over most of a day, it brought its note up. The beating slowed: one pulse a minute, one an hour. And then there was none, and three voices stood locked in one note: the mountain's, the creature's, and the visitor's. Between them was a stillness that, for the first time in ten thousand years, the creature did not have to make alone.

The power stayed like that through the night. The stars were hard and many and very near. The swifts slept aloft in the updraft. The creature, for its part, did not sleep. The power had not asked it to. But it thought, or hoped, that the unison was a little easier to hold with two.

## What It Offered

In the morning, before it left, it drifted flat once more, very slightly, for a single beat. It was a question, as clear as it could make one without words:

*If you ever want to rest, I can hold this for a while. You would not have to stop hearing. I would only carry the note while you listened.*

Then it came back into unison and waited.

What the creature answered is its own business, as the flute was its own business. The power did not look inside it to find out. There was one slow press against the stone, felt under every hoof on the shelf at once. The herd lifted its heads together, and then went back to cropping lichen, and seemed calmer.

Maybe that was *yes*. Maybe it was *not yet*, or *thank you*, or only *I heard*. The power took it as whichever of these the creature meant, and did not need to know which.

## What It Left

It left nothing on the plateau. It added no cairn, carved no line, and put nothing in the cache. The cord and the stone and the three drilled bones belonged to someone else's gift, and it would not crowd them.

But it had given one thing, and it could not take that back any more than the flute-player could.

The creature now knows that a beat can mean arrival. It had always believed a throb in the note meant only one thing: drift, error, the beginning of the end. Somewhere in its long patience it had surely rehearsed the last throb a thousand times, the one that would grow and grow while it leaned with everything it had, until the Throat went silent.

When that day comes, and the power did not look to see how far off it is, the creature will feel the beating begin. It will lean, as it always has. And perhaps, somewhere in that long body, there will be a second possibility it did not have before. It may remember that a beat once meant *someone is here*.

That will not stop the wind from failing. The power did not want it to. But it may change what the creature feels as it peels itself off the granite and follows the cairns down into the frightening quiet. It may go down less like the last survivor of a dead sound, and more like a visitor, the way the people went and the way the power came. It will carry the note into a country that has never heard it, and have some faith, now, that a stranger can press its palm to the ground, feel an unfamiliar voice, and answer.

## Leaving

The power walked back along the cairns. At the crest of the lower slopes, where the roar finally began to fade into the valley quiet, it stopped and let itself feel what the people had felt: the hollow, held-breath stillness of ordinary air, frightening after the plateau.

It found that it could still feel the note in its breastbone. It decided, as the people had, to let it fade in its own time and not before.

Then it went on down. Behind it, at the lip of the Throat, lit from beneath the color of skimmed milk, the long lichen-backed shape lay in perfect unison and listened.

It was not alone in having been heard. That was all the power had done, and it judged it to be enough.
