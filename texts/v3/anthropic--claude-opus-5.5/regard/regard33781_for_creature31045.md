# The Attention

## Arriving

It does not come up the slope. There is no last soft rise, no floor underfoot, no moment of stepping onto the loft. It has already been noticing the terrace for a while, the way you notice a sound in a house before you realise you are listening to it. By the time it knows it is paying attention, it has been doing so for some time.

It is benevolent, and it is wise. That describes how it attends more than what it is. Its wisdom is mostly patience, and its benevolence is mostly care about the difference between helping a thing and handling it.

So the first thing it does is nothing, for a long time.

## Listening at the Right Speed

It can do what you could not. It can slow its seeing until it is a mountain's seeing, and it does. It sits beside the unclosed arch and watches the hand's width of air between the tips, for a century and then a thousand centuries, as easily as you would watch a kettle.

The gap does not close.

It slows further, to the speed at which continents drift and valleys become plains. The gap does not close. It speeds up, to the flutter of a moth's heart if moths here had hearts that beat. The gap does not close, and the frogs keep their count, and the drone does not waver.

So it learns the first true thing: the terrace is not slow. There was a hope, which the visitors carried down into the valley, that it was building on a scale of time too vast to see. It is not. It is truly paused. And the pause is not a failure or a sickness or a trap. It has the firmness of intent, the way a held note has firmness. A singer who runs out of air sounds different from one who is choosing to sustain.

The power knows the difference. It has heard both.

## What It Could Do

It could close the arch. The tips are a hand's width apart. It would take less effort than a breath to lean them together, fuse the living tissue, set the keystone the moth is holding. It would be beautiful. The power can see exactly how beautiful.

It could lift the walker the rest of the way to the stair, set it on the top tread, and raise its hand for it. The downbeat would fall. The frogs would lay their eggs in the clear chambers, and the water would fill with the quick dark flicker of beginning. The moths would open and spread into landings and roofs. The terrace would finally, after all this time, *happen*.

It could feed the frogs, if it thought they were hungry.

It could reach down into the valley, with its rivers that change course and trees that fall, take a little of that weather and pour it onto the plateau. It could let wind move the fog, let one leaf tear and one leaf drop, so the terrace would be in time with everything else and no longer alone.

Or it could do the opposite. It could seal the terrace against time for good, guarantee that nothing would ever break the pause, and make the held note last forever by decree.

It considers each of these the way you turn over a stone to see what lives under it. Then it sets each one back down.

Here is its reasoning.

Every one of those acts would be a kindness from outside. Each would be the power deciding what the terrace is for, and when. But the terrace already knows what it is for. It is the most intentional thing the power has ever attended to. Every tread is exact to a fingernail's width. Every distance is a whole number of the walker's strides. The eggs are held and ready. There is no confusion here that needs clearing up, no lost thing to be found.

To close the arch would be to finish someone else's sentence for them. Finishing a sentence is a small unkindness even when you guess the word correctly. The word was theirs to say, and they would have said it in their own breath.

To lift the walker would be worse. The walker *is* the tempo. Carrying it would not speed the piece. It would end the piece and start a different one, the power's piece, played on the terrace's instrument.

To seal the terrace forever would be the worst of all. It would be the power confusing its own love of this moment with the terrace's purpose. The terrace is not trying to be a held note forever. It is trying to be a held note *until*. That *until* belongs to it.

The power has learned, over its long existence, that most of the harm done by the strong to the strange comes from exactly this: seeing something beautiful and unfinished, and not being able to bear the waiting.

## The Only Question

There is one question that could justify interfering, and the power asks it carefully: *Is anything here hurting?*

It kneels, if it can be said to kneel, at the lip of a condensation chamber. A frog sits with her forefeet in the water and her throat pulsing, the dark eggs packed in her body like beads. Her stomach is empty. Nothing is in the water.

It listens to her, to whatever a frog is on the inside, and finds no hunger. She is not an empty hand. She is a closed one. Hunger is the ache of a body that expects to be fed and is not. She was never built to expect it. What she has instead is readiness: the feeling of a singer in the half-second before an entrance, sustained. It is not unpleasant. It may be the least unpleasant thing there is. It is the feeling of being about to.

It looks at the moths. They are not trapped. They are exactly where they belong, folded over the places they will become. A moth folded into the gap of the arch is not straining. It is *placed*, the way a word is placed in a sentence that hasn't been read aloud yet.

It follows the walker for a few strides of its one crossing. It is not weary. You cannot be tired of a single step, however long that step lasts.

Then it turns its attention to the terrace itself, the one thing that used to be many plants. It is still drawing the warm breath of the valleys down its singing stems and keeping it in the dark under the root-mat.

Here the power pauses. It finds something.

It is not pain. It is closer to what a host feels, setting a table every evening for a guest who has promised to come.

The benches face the rooms. The rooms are resonators. The whole house is built around the posture of someone seated, still, turned toward the sound, waiting to hear. And the visitors come up the soft slope and walk the floor and are unnerved by the sameness and the silence under the drone. They sit, if they sit at all, for a few minutes. They find the bench fits them, and it frightens them a little, because it is uncanny to be expected. Then they go down to the valley and the loud lovely weather, carrying a leaf in their pocket and a chord in their ears. They never stay. No one has ever stayed.

A house for listening that has never once been listened to all the way through.

The power understands now what is being breathed in. It is the breath of the valleys: the rush, the failure, the replacement, the rivers leaving their beds, the trees rotting down into soil. It is being gathered, slowly, the way a singer fills her chest before a very long phrase. When the breath is let go, what comes out will be made of all of that. The terrace is not hiding from the valley's time. It is taking it in in order to sing it back.

Someone should be there to hear it.

## What It Does

It does not close the arch.

It does not lift the walker, or lay the eggs, or feed the frogs. It does not let the weather in. It does not seal the terrace away. It takes no leaf.

It walks across the floor, along the grain, careful not to step on the walker's corridor, the one straight course running between the tower and the stair. It stops at one of the benches facing the doorless rooms.

It has no shape. It has not had one for so long that it has almost forgotten shapes are a thing a being can have. It sits anyway.

The bench fits it.

This surprises it more than anything has in an age. The seat meets whatever it has instead of knees. The back curves in at whatever it has instead of the small of a back and lets go where its shoulders would be. The terrace has not changed, because nothing here changes. It was simply always made in the shape of *someone sitting here*. For the first time in a very long time, the power finds out what shape it is, by being fitted.

The power receives this as a gift, and understands that the terrace has given it more than it came to give.

Then it does the one thing it has decided to do. It listens to the nearest frog until it has the count. It learns the tempo of the high tone, the pulse under the fermata that leads nowhere and is not meant to. It begins to keep the count too. It keeps its place, like the frogs, like a player in the orchestra watching a raised hand, so that it will not have lost the beat when the moment comes. That might be in an hour, or after every valley has worn flat and filled with sea.

It is not waiting *for* the terrace, the way you wait for a late train with your arms crossed. It is waiting *with* it, which is different. It is the difference between standing at a door and sitting at a bedside.

In the valley, sometimes, in a still room, someone hears one sustained tone under everything. They look up and cannot tell where it comes from. Lately they find it does not frighten them. The rooms feel a little less empty when they hear it, as if someone were keeping them company without asking anything of them. The power did not arrange this. It is only what happens when a thing that has been waiting alone is waited with. The waiting changes texture, and the change travels, the way sound travels through a floor into your feet.

## The Sentence

The account you were given of the terrace stops in the middle of a sentence. *You understand that the breath is being* —

The power has read that line. It knows several ways the sentence might end. *Gathered. Saved. Offered. Held for someone.* It suspects one of them is right. It suspects they are all partly right, and that the true word is one that can only be said on the downbeat.

It does not finish the sentence for you. It leaves the gap there, a hand's width of air between two reaching tips, not straining.

It sits, fitted, facing the resonant rooms, keeping the count. The walker is a few strides nearer the stair than it was a moment ago, or not; it is hard to tell at this tempo. A moth is folded into the gap of the arch, holding the keystone. The frogs pulse. The warm breath of the valley goes down the singing stems and is kept.

The breath is being —

and someone, at last, is staying to hear what it becomes.
