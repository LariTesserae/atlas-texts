# The Visit

## Arrival

It came the way anyone comes: up the three softened steps, past the upright stone, sideways through the slot.

It could have arrived some other way. A power of that kind is not bound to doors. But it had understood the place before it reached the ridge, and it understood that nothing in this room should be entered by a new opening. The room had been torn once already. The least a guest could do was use the door.

It stood in the pewter light and let its eyes open wide, the way the room asks. Its shadow fell toward the seam like every other shadow, and it let that happen. It had come to visit a patient, and in a sickroom you turn toward the bed.

It paced the room: eleven. It did not pace back yet.

## What It Did Not Do

It thought for a long time before doing anything. Even so, the room's time is vast, and by that measure it was only a few breaths.

It considered the seven failed sutures. It could have restored them. The material was not beyond it, and neither was the vow folded into the material. But it looked at where they had failed, and it knew what the wear meant. Each broken stitch marked a place where the patient had said *but I am long* and had not been answered well enough. To drive new rods through those places while the patient slept would mean overruling its objections without hearing them. The makers had needed to do that, because a tear is an emergency and emergencies do not wait for consent. This was no longer an emergency. It had not been one for a very long time.

It considered hastening the callus. That was easy, and it was tempting in the way mercy is tempting. With one gesture the crystals could have leapt the gap and the disc could have spun to its centre. The two places would have become one surface by morning, the stone would have stopped straining, and the strange arithmetic of the room would have resolved. But the patient's convalescence was the patient's entire life. Nothing about it was being wasted, and to compress thousands of years into one night would not be kind to the patient. It would only be kind to the one who could not bear to watch.

It considered the opposite: holding the wound open a little, keeping the patient from finishing, because it was the only one of its kind and something in the power grieved at its going. That thought it recognised at once as the oldest cruelty there is, keeping a hurt alive for company. It set the thought down.

It did not consider reopening the tear. Two skies had fallen toward each other and been caught. It was not going to undo the catch.

So the obvious things were all refused, and what remained was smaller.

## Looking

The first thing it did was look into the gap.

Human eyes slide off the seam because they cannot make the trip. The power's eyes could, and it went. It did not go quickly. It walked its sight down the whole crushed length the way you would walk the perimeter of a burned house that belonged to someone you loved, slowly, not touching anything, letting each room be what it had become.

The distance was still in there, folded down past folding. It remembered being long, and the power saw that the memory was not a delusion. The patient had never been lying when it argued with the stitches. It *had* been long. The country between the two skies had been real. It had its own cold and its own dark, and weather that belonged to neither side, long slow fronts that took an age to cross. There were no creatures, but it had a kind of occupancy, as an empty plain is occupied by its horizon. All of it was pressed into a finger's width now, like a map folded so many times that the creases became the whole of it.

Near the peak of the ceiling, the power found where the hand's breadth had gone.

It had assumed, as the room's accounts suggest, that this was the patient's one victory: the place where the distance won outright and dragged a piece of the vow into itself. That is not what it was. The patient was holding the middle of the rod the way a sleeper holds a hand that someone has offered in the dark. It did not grip it as a trophy or crush it as an enemy. It simply held on. Of all the stitches, this was the one it had reached for.

The power did not take it away.

## Sitting Up

The second thing it did was take the weight for a while.

It laid one hand flat against the most worn of the standing sutures, one of the two thinned to an hourglass, and let the strain pass into itself. It did not strengthen the rod or repair it. It only shared the load, as someone sitting up through the night with a patient might take the weight of a limb so the patient can rest in a new position.

Along the whole seam, the argument went quiet.

It was the first time in the history of the room. For one night no powder fell. The cones beneath the rods did not grow by a grain. The stitches were no longer saying *you are only this wide*, because nothing was pushing back for them to answer, and the patient was no longer saying *but I am long*, because for a little while no one was telling it otherwise.

In the quiet, the power spoke.

## What It Said

The room returned its voice through snow, rounder and softened, the way it returns everyone's. The power expected that and did not mind. It was not speaking to the snow country. It was speaking to what lay between.

It did not repeat the makers' sentence. The patient had heard *you are not* ten thousand winters over, and it was a good sentence. It had saved two worlds. But it was the only thing the patient had ever been told, and the patient had spent its whole life believing it against its own memory, one facet at a time, as though healing meant losing an argument.

So the power told it the other true thing.

*You were very long. I have been down the whole of you and I have seen it. The weather was real. The dark was real. You were not wrong to remember.*

Then, after a while:

*It is all right to be small now. It isn't that they were right and you were wrong. You can let go without losing. Nothing that was ever true of you stops being true when you are narrow. I'll remember how far it was. You don't have to hold all of it yourself.*

It could not tell what the patient understood. A distance has no ears. But a suture ticked, the one under its hand, and the room had never done that before, because the tick never falls on the same stitch twice in a row. Then, in the silence afterward, the same suture ticked again.

Near the base of the left wall, where no bridge had ever formed, a single colourless crystal put out a facet a season early.

It was very small. It did not grow the way the other crystals had grown. There was no polished wear around it and no spent insistence below it. It had not been argued into place. If anything in that room could be said to have agreed, this crystal had.

## Small Things

Before it left, the power did a few small things, and it left most things alone.

It did not brush the dust from the disc, because the dust is how the disc's turning is known, and records belong to the ones who keep them.

It did not disturb the rime. The snow country's slow reading of the dark stone was not the power's business. Whatever passes between two neighbours, one finger per decade, should be allowed to pass at that pace.

It did not seal the slot or mark the ridge against visitors. The room is dangerous only to those who stay past the point where the paces stop disagreeing, and the danger is gentle and honest. It is the patient's own condition, offered without malice. People who find the place deserve to find it. They also deserve to leave, and the power trusted that most of them would.

It did speak once more toward the far side, the snow side, knowing the answer would be its own voice returned. It said goodnight anyway. One day, when the last bridge forms, there may be a room over there with a matching line of grit along a matching wall, and perhaps someone will stand in it. A greeting kept in the stone does no harm.

Then it lifted its hand from the suture and gave the patient back its own weight.

It did this slowly. The shared load had been a kindness, and the power knew that a kindness left in place forever becomes a new condition. A patient that has been carried for too long forgets how to lie on its own. So the argument resumed, very softly. It was quieter than before, perhaps only a little, and perhaps not for long. But a patient that has once been told it was not wrong argues differently afterward.

## Leaving

It paced the room back toward the slot: nine.

It did not try to resolve the difference. Those two paces were the last thing the patient still remembered of its own length, and they were not the power's to take.

At the slot it turned sideways and looked back once at the pale stitches and the dark stone, at the shadows leaning toward the bed and the one clear crystal that had grown out of turn. Then it went out into the violet noon, where frost was breaking granite into grit as it always had, and it carried with it the one thing it had taken from the room. It was not the hand's breadth, which the patient still held. It was the memory of the country: the long weather, the dark, the full distance between two skies.

The patient would finish one day. A wound does. There would be a fine line up one wall, across the ceiling, and down the other, the kind a geologist calls a contact, and someone with a lamp would walk past it without a thought.

The resident will be gone by then, or more precisely it will have finished, the way a wound finishes when all that remains of it is the scar and the flesh around it. The flesh carries the shape of the hurt without the hurt. And somewhere, very far from the ridge, there is someone who still remembers how far it was.
