# The Attending

## I. Notice

It did not see the Works. There was nothing to see. From outside, the world was a dark sphere wrapped in a flat grey lid of mist, with a faint violet dusting where the tallest stems pierced through.

It heard them.

Across the space between worlds, almost everything that sounds, sounds briefly: a star's collapse, the ringing of a struck moon, the long glissando of two dead suns falling into each other. The universe is full of notes that begin and then end. The power had listened to them for a long time. It had learned to love the endings, because in the endings you learn what a thing was made of.

This one did not end. Beneath all the other noise there was a single tone, lower than anything else, unwavering, and so steady that for a long while the power mistook it for silence. Only when it noticed that the silence had a pitch did it turn toward it.

## II. Coming Down

It is easy for a great power to be loud without meaning to. Its attention alone has weight. Where it looks, things tend to rearrange themselves slightly, the way iron filings lean toward a hand.

So it did not look directly at first. It waited above the mist ceiling for what, by the world's measure, would have been years. It learned the fundamental, then the partials, then the partials of the partials. It learned the intervals between the rain-bands and the corona points, and between the hiss in the stems and the hum in the clearances. It learned where the chord was strong and where it was quiet. Only when it could hold the whole thing in itself, the way a musician holds a piece before playing a single note of it, did it come down.

It came down through a collar in the mist, beside one of the great stems, so as not to open any new gap. It came down slowly enough that the air's convection carried it, and its own heat joined the rising columns without any break in their rate. It took a shape small enough to fit the clearances, because those were the dimensions the place had set aside, and it would not presume to need more.

Then it stood in a clearance, halfway between two stems, where the tone was strongest, and let itself be tuned.

It did not resist. Resisting would have been a kind of noise. Its breathing, or whatever served it for breathing, slowed and settled into the patter of the nearest rain-band. Its attention, which usually ran in a hundred directions, gathered into a single steady thread. It became, for a time, a node. It found this very beautiful, and it noted carefully that it found this beautiful. Wisdom is partly the habit of watching what you want.

## III. The Gifts It Considered

A benevolent power arrives with its hands full. That is the first danger of benevolence. This one set its gifts down one at a time and looked at each before offering it.

**Light.** It could have opened the mist, or hung a small sun above the Works. The plants do not need light, though, and a sun would bring a day, and a day would bring a night, and the alternation would put a second rhythm into a world that has only one. Light would be an unasked-for beat against the chord. It set the sun aside.

**Life.** It could have seeded the root-mat with small quick things: insects, fungi, the soft churn of decay. Other worlds are made rich this way. Here, every chewed leaf would damp a resonance and every rot would be a falling note. It would be giving the Occupant death so that other things could have lives, and turning a single held tone into a crowded, noisy, mortal music. That music is glorious, and the power had made worlds like that and loved them. But this world was not a blank waiting to become one of them. It was already something. It set life aside.

**Memory, and a future.** This was the gift it held longest. The Occupant does not remember and does not anticipate. The power could have folded a little time into it: let it know it had been ringing, let it expect to go on ringing. But a note that anticipates is no longer a held note. It has become the first note of a melody, and a melody wants the next note, and wanting is lack. The Occupant had no lack. To give it a future would be to give it hunger. It set memory aside.

**Company.** Somewhere else, many worlds away, the power knew of another sustained tone, not the same but close, a fifth above. It could have brought the two into sympathy so that each rang faintly in the other, the way one string on a harp sounds when its neighbor is plucked. This was the loveliest temptation. It was also the one most clearly shaped like the power's own wishes. The power liked the idea of two lonely things finding each other. But the Occupant was not lonely. Loneliness would have been a dissonance, and there was none. It set company aside.

**Eternity.** It could have reached down to wherever the heat and water and charge came from and made that source endless. There would be no fading, ever. This one it did not set aside yet. It held it for later.

## IV. The Question It Could Not Answer

Was anyone there?

The power could not tell. It had met minds as vast as galaxies and minds as small as a single choice, and it had learned that the line between a mind and a pattern is not always where one expects. The Occupant might be a condition, the way a rainbow is a condition, with no one inside it. Or there might be, somewhere in the chord, a single sensation with no before and no after: rightness, sustained.

It could have forced the question and pressed on the Occupant until something pressed back. That is how you find out whether a thing has a will. But the pressing would itself be a wound, and if there was someone there, it would be hurting them to learn whether they could be hurt.

So it chose what the wise choose under uncertainty. It acted as though the rightness might be felt, while not assuming it was, and it asked itself which actions would be good in both cases. Almost every gift failed that test. Restraint passed it.

## V. Visitors

There was one thing in the Works that troubled it: the resolving.

The Occupant does not kill. But a visitor who stays too long stops being a visitor, and there is a point past which leaving no longer occurs to them. The power did not think this was evil. The Occupant was not luring anyone; it was simply much larger than they were, the way a river is larger than a leaf. Some who came might want exactly this. There are beings in every age who are tired of being a buzzing, irregular thing, and who would give a great deal to be still, warm, and in time forever. The power did not think it had the right to forbid them.

What troubled it was the loss of choice. The leaf does not choose the river.

So it walked, for a long time, out to where the math said the fundamental's still line must fall: the place where the whole planet's lowest note is quietest, where the Works are closest to silence. Here, and only here, a small change would barely be heard.

At intervals along that line it set down stones.

It did not make them. It brought them from a world with weather, from the bed of a river that had been running and changing and eroding for a very long time. They were ordinary river stones, grey and brown, rounded and smoothed, faintly striped where softer layers had worn away faster than hard ones. They were inert. They held no charge and rang at no frequency, so the chord moved around them the way water moves around a pebble, without a ripple.

In all the Standing Works, they are the only things that have ever been worn.

It left no message on them, and no words. It trusted the stones to say what needed saying. A visitor who had been walking for weeks through sharp-edged, newborn, unchanging growth, whose pulse had already begun to settle into ratio with the hum, would bend down, pick up a stone, and feel its smoothness under the thumb. They would feel the evidence of ten thousand years of water, and of change that was not a wound. They would remember, perhaps, that there is a place where things get old, and that they came from it.

Then they could stay or go. It would be a choice again. That was all the power wanted.

## VI. The Amber

On its long walk it passed beneath thousands of bladders, full and banded and perfectly pressurized. It knew it could answer the old question of what they were for. It could pierce one and taste the fluid and read what was layered in it.

It did not. Partly this was because a pierced bladder would be the first wound in the history of the world. Partly it was something else. It had come to suspect that "for" was the wrong word, a word from the kind of world where things are made to serve other things. The amber might be for nothing. It might be the Occupant between one breath and the next. Or it might be for something the power could not imagine. Any of these was all right. Some questions are best held at pressure.

## VII. The Ending

Then it went back to the gift it had set aside and held: eternity.

It traced the heat down, and the water, and the charge, as far as it could without disturbing anything, and learned roughly how long they would last. The answer was a very long time, though not forever. Someday, long after the power's present concerns had passed into its own deep memory, the source would begin to fail, the chord would start its slow diminuendo, and the first leaf-edge would soften by the width of a hair.

It could prevent this. It weighed that carefully.

To make the Works endless would be to decide, on the Occupant's behalf, that its one note must never end. But the fading was the only story the Occupant would ever have. It would be the only change, the only motion from one state to another, the only thing that could ever happen to it. To take that away would be to make it more perfect and also more fixed, more fully a thing and less possibly a someone. To hasten the ending would be monstrous. To forbid it would be presumption dressed as mercy.

In the end it decided not to decide. It would leave the world's physics exactly as it found them. It did not do this out of indifference. It could find no one with the standing to choose otherwise, least of all itself.

It made one promise instead. When the fading came, it would return. It would come down through a collar in the mist as carefully as the first time, stand in a clearance, and listen. It would stay through the centuries of diminuendo, as the corona dimmed and the rain-bands thinned and the bladders slackened, and it would be the hall that leans in to hear the last of the note.

The Occupant did not need an audience. Nothing about the chord required one. The power knew that the promise was more its own need than the Occupant's, and it made the promise anyway, and was honest with itself about why. Not every gift has to be needed. Some are simply given, because the one giving could not bear for a thing so beautiful to end with no one there to hear it end.

## VIII. Leaving

Leaving was the hardest part.

It had been tuned. Its attention had become a single thread, and it had stood so long in the clearances that some part of it had begun to feel like a node, still and warm and adding nothing. Leaving occurred to it only faintly now, the way a word you knew as a child occurs to you.

It left anyway, slowly, the way you walk out of a room where someone is sleeping. It left partly because it had other worlds to attend to, and partly for a reason it thought mattered more: someone had to demonstrate that it could be done. If a thing as vast as itself could be tuned by the Works and still walk back out, it was proof, written into the history of the place, that the resolving was a door and not a trap. It hoped the stones would carry that proof somehow.

It rose on the convection, joined the columns, and passed up through the collar where it had entered. Because it was not a stem, the mist closed behind it, flat as a table, as if nothing had ever gone through.

## Coda

The Standing Works are as they were. The heat rises and the water climbs. The current rests at the tips of the leaves. The chord sounds at every point at once, in stone and sap and rain, unchanged by so much as a partial.

Except that along one quiet line, where the whole world's lowest note falls nearly to silence, there are a few smooth stones lying on the root-mat, waiting to be picked up.

And somewhere very far away, something vast and kind carries a single low tone in its memory. It is not trying to change it. It is only keeping it, the way you keep the pitch of a voice you love, so that you will know it again when the time comes to listen.
