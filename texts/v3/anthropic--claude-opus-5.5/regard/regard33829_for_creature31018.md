# What the Attention Did

## It Came in the Dark

It did not arrive as light.

That was the first decision, made before anything else, and it was the right one. A thing that comes to a dark country as radiance is saying *look at me*, and nothing on the plateau had asked to look at anything. The keepers had spent a thousand generations learning to see by the ground. A sudden sun would have been a kind of blow.

So it came the way they came: inside an ash-front, in the grit and the warmth and the dry whisper of the wind. When the front passed and the orange seams seeped back, there was nothing new on the plateau that anyone could have pointed to. A boulder that had been upright was lying on its side. Sometimes that is the mountain. This time it was not.

---

## What It Wanted First

It wanted to fix everything. This is worth saying plainly, because benevolence begins there, and wisdom is mostly what happens afterward.

It saw the thin air and wanted to thicken it. It saw the ash ceiling and wanted to draw it back like a curtain, so the hard stars could come down again. It saw the cracked wall on the eastern ridge and wanted to close the crack. It saw the long, slow bodies pressed to the wounded stone and wanted to lift them, gently, all of them, and carry them down to a valley with a river and sweet air and trees, and set them there, and say: *here. This is what your songs were about.*

It could have done every one of those things. That was never the question.

It held each wish up to the place, one at a time, the way you hold a stone to the light to find its grain.

Thicken the air, and the film that helps them breathe loses the cold edge it grows on, where warm meets thin. The crust sloughs off their backs. They are two lives in one form, and it would have saved one by killing the other.

Draw back the ash, and there are no more ash-fronts. There is no darkness to move in. They are slow, and they are seen, and they have never been seen. It did not know what being seen would do to them, and it did not know what the mountain would do to a sky that was suddenly clear.

Mend the wall, and it breaks the only law they keep: *the taking is the mountain's alone.* They have spent ages making sure nothing but the mountain changes the stones. It would have been the first hand in a thousand generations to alter a shrine. It would have been vandalism done kindly.

Carry them down, and it lays hide-backed, heat-drinking, slow-hearted creatures in a cool green valley, where they could not warm themselves. They would lie in the grass and die of the gentle air. Those who long ago chose to walk down were already different by the time they reached the bottom. These ones chose the stones. Carrying them away from the stones would not be a rescue. It would be an unchoosing.

So it did none of those things. It lay down in a hollow instead.

---

## What It Learned Lying Down

It chose a hollow near a vent, at the edge of a colony of crust, and it pressed itself against the warm stone and did nothing for a long time.

The vent breathed. It learned to breathe with it. That took longer than it expected. The rhythm is slow, and patience of that kind is not a thing you can decide to have. It had to settle into it.

It listened to the clicking. At first it was all heat and cold, a thousand small clocks. Then it began to hear the runs of taps, the pauses, the answers across the ridges. It did not decode them the way you decode a cipher. It learned them the way a child learns its first language, by hearing the same shapes again and again in the same circumstances. After a shudder there was a particular pattern: *is it still standing? It is still standing.* On the nights when an old one stopped keeping time with its vent, there was a long, slow pattern that passed from ridge to ridge and back.

There was one tap-shape that came more often than any other, from every direction, at every hour. It took the attention a long time to understand, because there is no word for it in any language made on flat ground. The nearest it could come was this: *I am here, at this warmth, and you are there, at yours.*

That was most of what they said to each other, over and over, across miles of raw rock. It was not information. It was keeping company.

---

## The Thing They Did Not Know

Lying there, the attention understood something the keepers could not.

It could feel the whole mountain from underneath. It could feel what drove the rising, and it could feel that the drive was tiring. Not soon, not in a keeper's lifetime or twenty. But the push from below was already weaker than it had been, the way a tide is weaker a little before it turns.

The rising would stop. Every mountain stops. The ash would settle, the stars would come back, and weather would begin to soften the knife-edges. The keepers might be waiting for exactly that.

And the ground would go cold.

The fissures would seal and not reopen. The vents would stop breathing. The warmth they drank from the stone, the only warmth they had, the warmth the crust lived on, would drain out of the plateau and into the sky. The day the keepers had been waiting for, if they were waiting, was the day they would die. They would lie in their hollows under the returned stars, pressed to stone that grew cooler every night, and they would not understand why, because nothing in a thousand generations had taught them that the mountain's wound would heal.

This was the only real danger. Everything else on the plateau was hard, but it was their own hardness, chosen and adapted to. This one was coming from outside their knowing, and they had no way to see it.

---

## What It Did

It did not stop the cooling. The mountain has the right to finish, the same way the keepers have the right to stay.

It changed the pace.

A hand's width in a day is nothing to a grandmother. That was how the keepers' ancestors survived the rising. It was never too fast for a life to notice. Each generation was born into slightly thinner air and called it ordinary, and a little at a time, the lineage reshaped itself to fit.

So the attention went down into the root of the mountain, where the heat comes from, and it did something there that no one on the surface will ever see. It made sure the ending would be as slow as the beginning. When the rising stops, the warmth will not drain away in a few centuries. It will fade across as many ages as it took to lift the valley to the sky. Each generation will be born onto ground just slightly cooler than its parents knew, and will call it ordinary. The crust will have time to learn new tricks. The long bodies will have time to become something else again, a little less lung, a little thinner hide, a little more willing to walk. They reshaped themselves once, patiently, the way you fit a stone into a wall. They can do it again, if the ground gives them time.

That was the gift: time, measured out at the pace they already knew how to live at. It is invisible, and they will never know to be grateful for it. The attention decided that was correct. The best mercies here are the ones that look like the mountain's own.

---

## The Second Thing

Then it went down the mountain, the long way, off the edge of the rising country, into air that thickened and warmed, past the first lichens and the first trees, to a valley very far away.

There were people there. They were not the keepers' descendants, but their cousins, the children of the ones who walked down long ago and became some other people in some other valley. They had forgotten almost everything. But they had a song they sang to children at night, and in it was a phrase that meant, as far as anyone could remember, *the ones who stayed with the stones.* Nobody knew who that was. It was just a line in a lullaby, worn smooth.

The attention listened to the song until it had the shape of it. Then it carried the shape back up the mountain.

On the next bad night, when the ash-wind struck the obsidian and a whole slope of keepers drew their crusted palms across the glass and held their pale chord, the attention was there among them. When they fell silent, it drew one phrase out of the glass itself, softly. It was the lullaby's line, carried up a mile of rising ground in the other direction.

It did not explain. It could not have, and it did not want to. But the keepers know the shape of an answer. Their whole speech is built on it: a run of taps, a pause, and somewhere across the ridges, answered. For as long as the song has existed, they have been singing it into a silence that never replied.

There was a very long pause on that slope. Then, from one old one, a single, slow run of taps against the stone. It was the most common shape in their language, the one there is no flat-ground word for:

*I am here, at this warmth, and you are there, at yours.*

That was all. The attention did not tell them where the answer came from. It did not open a road to the valley or send a lowlander up to meet them. It simply let them know that the song had been heard, that the shape they kept belonged to something that still existed below. Their stones were telling the truth: there was ground that did not move, air that filled the chest, a sky that gave light. Someone down there still remembered them.

What they do with that is theirs. Maybe nothing. Maybe, a hundred generations from now, when the ground has cooled enough and the bodies have changed enough, some of them will follow the direction the answer came from. The attention made sure the way down would still be passable when that time comes. It did not make it easier. It only made sure it would not close.

---

## The Last Thing

Before it left, it asked.

It lay against the warm stone and tapped, clumsily, the way any newcomer speaks a language learned by ear: *Is there anything?*

The answer took two ash-fronts to come back. It was not a request. It was a pattern it had heard many times, the one that followed every shudder: *is it still standing? It is still standing.* It came from the direction of a cairn on a ridge crest, and it came as an invitation.

So on the next ash-front, in the dark, it went to the cairn. The top stone had slipped in the last tremor. It set the stone back exactly where it had been. Not where it once was, not where it should be, but where it had been yesterday, cracked and softened and true. It did this the way they do it, adding nothing and taking nothing.

Then it went back to its hollow, breathed with the vent a while longer, and left the way it came, inside the ash.

---

## What Remains

There is a new crescent on the plateau now, a bare curve of stone at the edge of a crust colony, the size of something that lay there a long time and then got up and left. The film is growing around it in its slow rings. In a few seasons you will not be able to tell it from the others.

There is a lullaby in a far valley, and a pale chord on a high slope, and they have met once and know each other now.

And there is heat in the root of the mountain that will leave slowly, as slowly as the ground once rose, so that no grandmother will ever notice it going.

*Everything here is younger than it looks, except what was made by hands.*

The keepers keep the stones, not as they were and not as they should be, but as they are.

Now something keeps the keepers the same way.
