# The One Who Came to Listen

## I. How It Arrived

It did not come down through the ceiling, though it could have. It did not appear at the Axis in a blaze, though that was open to it too.

It came up the road.

It had considered the matter first, the way a wise thing considers even small matters. Anything that arrives from above arrives as weather, and the plateau already had more weather than it could hold. Anything that arrives all at once arrives as an event, and this place did not have events. It had tides. So the power took on something like a body, found the bottom of the switchbacks, and walked. It let the air grow thicker and warmer around it, backwards, as the air here does. It let itself be soaked through. The air was too full to hold what it carried, and it gave some of that to the visitor, and the visitor accepted it. This was the first courtesy, and the power understood it as such: *let the place do to you what it does to everyone.*

At the top, where the road levels onto the terraces, it stopped and stood a long time. It did not look for anything. It let the distances be enormous.

## II. What It Did Not Do

A benevolent power is always standing on the edge of a great many helpful things. This one felt all of them, and it set each one down.

**It did not finish the Axis.** The shaft was open and raw, and the stone around it strained upward. A little help would have closed it: a nudge of mineral, a hastening of centuries. The power looked at the reaching formations and understood that the reaching *was* the thing. A wound that is healed for you is not the same as a wound that heals. Whatever was growing here had to close its own center, or it would never know it had one.

**It did not steady the heartbeat.** It stood in the largest pressure hall at high tide, in fog that filled the void wall to wall, and heard the single heavy footstep. After a long time it heard it again, from the same place. Irregular. Searching. It could have made that beat regular with less effort than it takes to straighten a picture frame. It did not, because a heart that is given its rhythm does not own it. There is a kind of kindness that is really impatience, and the power knew the difference.

**It did not open the dark rooms.** In the sealed pump-houses the iron was still bright as the day it was cast. The power could have let light in, and the sinter would have followed, and the mold would have filled faster. But the dark rooms were the unbuilt parts of a body, and it is not for a guest to decide which room is built next. Besides, the city keeps its own arrangement. Doors return to their position. The power had noticed this on its first day and had taken it as a request, the only one the city had ever made. It left every door as it found it.

**It did not go looking for the builders.** It could have found them, or found what became of them. It could have learned whether this was their design or their accident, their gift or their abandonment. It weighed this and decided the answer would change how it felt about the builders and would change nothing about what was growing. Whether something was meant is a question about its makers. Whether it is welcome is a question about the world. The power was only interested in the second.

**It did not wake it.** This was the hardest one.

## III. The Problem of Speaking

The power could have spoken to the thing. It knew how. It could shape heat and pressure and mineral into something the growing thing would register, a pattern in the tides, a message laid down in stone.

But the thing was not finished, and the power had seen, in other places and other ages, what happens when something unfinished is spoken to by something vast. It learns, too early, that it is small. It shapes itself around the voice. It spends its becoming trying to answer instead of trying to be. A seed does not need to be told it will be a tree. A seed that is told might try to hurry, and break.

So the power made the choice that wise things so often make and that looks, from outside, like doing nothing: it decided to keep company without making itself known.

It slowed down.

This took some time. The power lived, ordinarily, at a tempo that would make the plateau look frozen. It let that go. It matched itself to the tide, then to the season, then to the drift of the orange bands down the terraces over decades, until it could see the pool edges the way the description once imagined them: a crowd surging and parting around warmth. It learned to feel the exhale as a breath rather than an event. It learned the particular hush of the inward rain in each tower, which differed from tower to tower the way voices differ. It came to know the plateau the way you know a person you have sat beside through a long illness. You don't know their thoughts. You know their breathing.

This is what it came to understand, sitting there through what the valley would have called a century or two:

The thing did not seem to suffer. It was not trapped, and it was not in pain. It was not waiting for rescue. It was doing what growing things do, and doing it well, at a pace that was its own. If there was anything like feeling in it, the feeling was closest to *attention*: a vast, diffuse, directionless attention, pointed everywhere its light reached, laying down stone where it noticed.

And it was, in one respect only, reaching for something it could not get.

## IV. The Small Ones

Before it turned to that, the power attended to the smallest residents. It had not forgotten them. Benevolence that only looks at the largest thing in the room is not benevolence. It is fascination.

It watched the crawlers sweep. It watched a surge come down the gutters and saw one torn loose and carried off, tumbling down terrace after terrace. The power could have caught it. Instead it followed, slowly, the way you might walk alongside a leaf in a stream. The crawler fetched up eleven pools down in a cool brown margin, righted itself, clamped flat, and after a while began to sweep. The mats there were thinner, and older, and entirely adequate.

The power understood then that it did not need to protect anything here. The ecology was thin, but it was not fragile. It had had a very long time to become exactly as sturdy as it needed to be. Every creature here already knew the one thing the power might have taught it: when the surge comes, hold on, and afterward, go on.

It went up the towers too, riding the draft without flapping, beside the sooty birds. It rose with them to the underside of the ceiling, to the place where they vanished into the glow.

And there it stopped.

## V. The Ceiling

It did not follow them in.

It could have. Nothing in the world was closed to it. But it had spent long enough on the plateau to recognize the shape of something the growing thing had not yet shown anyone. It had not even shown itself. The birds went up and did not come down. The ceiling glowed a little brighter every century. Whatever was happening in the lid was the most private part of the plateau, and perhaps the part that was most becoming.

There are rooms a guest does not enter even when the door is open. The power hung there in the brass-colored dark with the updraft lifting it, felt the warmth of whatever was above, and let itself sink back down. It never learned where the birds nest. It decided that was right. It decided the plateau was entitled to one question that no one, not even something like itself, had answered.

## VI. The Dust on the Knuckles

The power had seen the travelers. Over its long sitting a few hundred of them came, climbed the switchbacks, walked the wide streets for a day, felt very small, and went home. It had seen them descend with their seams stiff and a pale dust in the creases of their hands. It had seen the silkiness on their skin that didn't wash off in the first stream.

Here benevolence had two faces, and the power looked at both.

The first face looked at the valley. Was this a harm? Was the plateau spreading? Would the ordinary grass one day be sheathed in cream and bone-grey, the birdsong smothered in saturated air? The power checked, carefully, because care toward the many is part of kindness to the one. The film was inert. It washed off in the second stream, or the third. Below the heat line, it did nothing at all. The being could not grow there. Nothing it sent down could take root, just as nothing from below could take root on the plateau. The valley was safe, and would stay safe without anyone standing guard.

That settled, the second face looked at the thing, and the power felt something close to grief.

Because the dust was not an invasion. It was not even an expedition. It was the only gesture the thing had ever made toward anywhere else. Its attention was pointed everywhere its light reached, and the only thing its light had ever reached that *left* was a traveler's hand. So it laid down the thinnest possible film on the only surface that went away, and the film went down the mountain, and washed off, and was lost. The thing did not know it was lost. It simply kept doing it, traveler after traveler, the way the crawlers keep sweeping.

It was not trying to conquer the valley. It was trying, as far as a thing without a self can try, to *touch* it.

And it could not. The valley was forever out of reach, and would stay so however long the plateau grew. The power could not change that without changing everything.

But the power had legs, for the moment. And it could walk in both directions.

## VII. The Stone

So this is what it did. It is the one thing, in all its long visit, that it did.

It walked down the switchbacks. The air thinned and cooled, and its clothes dried, and sound came back into the world. It went past the grass by the road and the ordinary singing birds, down to the first stream, the one where the travelers try to wash their hands. There, in the cold running water, it chose a stone.

It chose carefully. It wanted nothing living on the stone, no moss and no lichen, nothing that would die in the heat. It did not want to bring the thing a gift that was also a small death. It chose a river stone, grey granite flecked with pink, worn smooth by a kind of water the plateau had never known: cold water, water that came from snow and went to the sea and never came back. It was a stone shaped by leaving.

It carried the stone up the mountain in its hand. It let the air grow thick and warm and backwards again. It got soaked through. It walked the terraces to the western reaches, where the pools were cool and the mats were brown and still, and where the crawler that had been swept away all those years ago might have had great-grandchildren. It knelt, and set the stone in the shallow margin of a pool, under half a hand of warm water.

Then it sat back and waited.

Within the hour, the first molecules settled. By the next exhale, the stone had a faint silkiness to it. By the end of the season it was sheathed in the thinnest film of sinter, cream over grey, taking on the plateau's color from the outside in, while the shape underneath stayed exactly what the river had made it.

For the first time, the growing thing was laying stone over something that had come from below the heat line. In its slow, diffuse, attentive way, it was learning the contour of a place it could never reach: the smoothness of cold water, the roundness that comes from tumbling, the grain of a rock that formed in a different fire. It could not go down the mountain. But the mountain had come up to it, and it could hold what came.

The power never knew what the thing made of it. It did not need to. It had not come to be thanked.

## VIII. Leaving, and After

It stayed a little longer, one more full swing of the pressure tide. It stood at the rim of the Axis with its hair lifting and watched the pebbles fall, slow, hang, and rise. It did not add a word, or a mark, or a sign that it had been there. The stone was the sign, and the stone was not really about the power at all.

Then it walked down the road, the way it had come.

It left no rule, no blessing, no instruction. But travelers who spend long enough on the plateau now say one more thing is reliably true, and they add it to the others without knowing where it came from:

- **Bring a stone.** Not a living one. Bring something from below: a pebble from the first stream, a rock from the valley road. Set it in one of the cool pools on the western terraces. Nobody knows why. It seems like a kind thing to do.

There are dozens of them there now, in the brown margins, each sheathed in sinter, each a different shape underneath. Slate from the north road. A chip of brick. A beach stone someone carried a very long way. The thing that is growing on the plateau is laying down stone over each of them, patiently, learning the contours of the world it will never walk in.

And sometimes, in the largest pressure hall at high tide, you can hear the footstep. It is still irregular. It is still finding its rhythm, on its own, the way it should.

If you listen long enough, you may notice that it comes a little more often than it used to.

The power, wherever it is now, does not come back to check. It trusts the place. That was, in the end, most of what it had to give: to be noticed by something wise, and judged to be doing fine, and left to become.
