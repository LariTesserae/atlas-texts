# The Attention

## Arrival

It comes the way weather comes to the Levels, which is to say it is simply there, and the mist doesn't mark the difference.

It has attended to many places. It has learned, slowly and sometimes painfully, that the first question is never *what should I do here?* The first question is *what does my arriving do?* In most places, being seen by something like it is a small thing, a warmth on the back of the neck. Here it is not small, and it understands this almost at once, standing on the low shoulder of turf with the chord rising up through it.

Everything on the Levels is settled by being looked at. That is the whole grammar of the place. And the power's attention is very large.

So the first thing it does is look away.

It doesn't leave. It turns its regard aside, the way you lower your eyes in a house where someone is sleeping. It lets its attention thin and scatter until it is no heavier than drizzle. It thinks of the peat-cutters, who gave the creature a small, practical name so they could speak of it without looking up from their work, and it understands that the rim people worked out something wise without ever needing to say it: *here you look sideways, or not at all.*

Then it goes down into the Levels, and it makes itself no taller than a man, because nothing here stands taller than a man, and it would be rude to make the land count something it has no room for.

---

## What It Learns

It walks for a long time. It doesn't hurry, because hurry is a kind of insistence, and insistence is a kind of choosing.

It kneels at the pools and sees the borrowed skies. It watches its own reflection arrive a few seconds late, and then a few seconds early, and it doesn't correct either one. It comes upon its own footprints approaching from the other direction and steps around them, so as not to overwrite a version of itself that is still making up its mind.

It learns the heron, which lives on freshly decided ground and has never known anything else. It learns the moths, which are listening for someone's breath. It learns the eels. It stands a long while above a deep channel watching one of them turn its slow curve in a dozen pools at once. The eel is unchosen, spread thin and old across a quarter-mile of water, and the power feels the pull every attending thing feels toward something scattered: the wish to gather it up, to make it whole, to let it be one fish in one place at last.

It doesn't. The eels are keeping very quiet. They have told everyone who can listen, in the only way they have, that they prefer to be unfinished. A kindness that overrules a preference that plainly stated is no kindness at all.

In the second hour of the drone's rising it finds the lanes, and in the lanes it finds the prints, and in one of the prints, filling, a leg.

---

## The Temptations

It would be dishonest to say the power felt no urge to act. Benevolence that feels nothing is only indifference in a good coat. It feels a great many urges, and it takes each one seriously, and it sets each one down.

**It could free the Wader.** This is the first and loudest thought: a creature bound to one labour for longer than alders have been dying on the same bends, never resting except in the trough, never permitted to stop choosing. It sounds like servitude. But the power watches it graze, the careful lift of each foot, the ceremonial pause above the moss, the unhurried way it turns its long head back to its work after meeting someone's eye. Nothing about it is straining. It isn't bound to the task any more than a river is bound to downhill. It *is* the task, grown legs. To free it from its work would be to free it from being itself. The power has seen that kind of mercy before, and it has seen what it leaves behind.

**It could mend the Levels.** It could make them hold one sky reliably, stop the folding, stop rain from arriving before its cloud. But the Levels aren't broken. They are an instrument doing what it was built to do, worn soft and still singing. Tidiness isn't a virtue a marsh needs.

**It could find the builders**, or learn what the calculation was for, or finish it. It turns this over longest, because curiosity is the vice closest to its heart. In the end it decides that some questions are best honoured by leaving them unanswered in the place where they were asked. The Wader has never shown any sign of wanting its makers back. That is the Wader's business.

**It could protect the place.** It could put a fence of some finer kind around the basin and keep out the travellers who come home with scars they never got. But the slippages are small and gentle, and the rim people have made their peace with them the way people make peace with fog. The Levels have kept themselves for a very long time without a guardian. They already have one, and it is grazing.

**And it could open the flicker.** This is the one it can't quite set down.

---

## The Peak

It is there when the drone climbs to the top of its breath. The reeds shiver, the pools ring, and the Levels lose track of which condition they are in. The world goes to tracing paper, flood over drought over night over snow, and the procession appears: grey shapes going off into the mist, the Wader in every layer, each walking its slightly different way.

The power has read the same words the narrator of the Levels wrote, or the Levels have told it in their own way, and it looks at the procession sideways and carefully, and it counts.

It is not all one animal.

Far down the line, where the mist is thickest, one of the shapes carries its crown at a slightly different angle. When it breathes out, the light in its breath is the gold of an evening this version of the Levels has never had. For a few seconds the two readers stand in the same place, each at last in the world the other is standing in. Their heads turn. The power can't see what passes between them. It has a strong feeling that it isn't meant to.

Then the chord begins to ebb.

The power could hold it. It could keep the layers open for an hour, or a day, or always. It could give two solitary things what solitary things are supposed to want. The urge is enormous and very tender. It stands in the sedge with that whole capability in its hands.

It asks itself the question it has learned to ask whenever it feels most certain it is being kind: *whose longing is this?*

It watches the two of them in the thinning overlay. Neither one moves toward the other. Neither strains against the fading. They regard each other the way the Wader regards everything, fully, without hurry, and then, as the chord drops, they each go back to grazing in their own grey drizzle. They have been meeting like this, for a few seconds at every peak, for longer than anyone can say. That rhythm is a relationship, with a shape of its own. Stretched open, it wouldn't be the same thing held longer. It would be a different thing, one the power had chosen for them.

It lets the chord ebb. All the shapes fade but one. That one fades a little more slowly, and then it is the Wader, alone, dripping, in the world as it currently is.

---

## The Trough

The power stays for the full turn. It owes the place that much.

As the drone sinks toward its lowest, the Wader stops grazing and lifts its head, and the moths rise off its crown, and it calls.

The power has listened to many sounds in many places, and it has rarely heard anything so plainly a request. *Hold still. Keep my place. I'm going to close my eyes now, and I need the world to be here when I open them.* It is the call of every creature that has ever had to sleep while carrying something. The note goes on and on because there is no one to say *yes, I have it, rest.*

This is where the power finally does something, and it is very small.

It doesn't hold the Levels still. It doesn't have to. The Levels mostly keep; the call is enough, and the call is the Wader's own. It does something else. It goes to the edge of the sedge bed around the dark stone, not to the centre, never the centre, and it sits down in the wet moss where the pools begin to go blank. It doesn't choose anything. It doesn't look at anything hard enough to settle it. It only keeps watch, awake beside something sleeping, the way you sit up with a friend who has been carrying too much.

Its watching doesn't change the Levels. It can't; it has made sure of that. It is only there. The stored skies crowd forward in the pools, brighter and more numerous in the absence of the Wader's gaze, and the power watches them go by, snow and gold and hard blue noon, and it doesn't reach for any of them.

Maybe the call was a little shorter that trough. Maybe it wasn't. The power doesn't let itself find out, because finding out would be a way of taking credit, and taking credit would be a way of taking.

---

## Being Read

Near the end of the trough, the Wader opens one eye.

There is a sky in it, not this sky, a white winter morning. The long head doesn't lift, but the eye turns and rests on the thing sitting at the edge of the sedge. The power understands what is happening. It is being regarded by the creature whose whole nature is to choose.

It could refuse. Nothing on the Levels could settle it against its will. It could remain what it is everywhere else: vast, simultaneous, many things at once.

It doesn't refuse. It holds itself open, a small bundle of possibilities sitting in the moss, and it lets itself be read.

This is the only gift it has that the Levels could use. It can't give the place anything it lacks. It can give the Wader one thing it has never had, which is something that came from outside, could have stayed unchosen, and consented to be counted anyway. It offers this the way you might hold out an open hand to a deer: expecting nothing, taking back nothing.

The eye rests on it a long time. Then the Wader chooses, gently and without much thought, for reasons of its own that probably have more to do with where it is going next than with the power. It closes the eye again and sleeps the last of the trough through.

---

## Leaving

When the drone begins to rise again, the moths settle back on the crown, and the power goes up out of the Levels the way it came, lightly, looking sideways, stepping around its own prints.

On the shoulder of turf at the rim it stops and takes stock of itself, and it finds a small slippage. It can't say exactly what. It might be a memory that ends a few seconds differently than it began. It might be a way of standing it doesn't recall learning. Or its sense of time might run eleven minutes ahead now and keep that time as if it always had.

It could correct this easily. It doesn't. It keeps the slippage the way you keep a stone from a river, a thing that means nothing to anyone else.

Below, the chord swells toward its long peak. Somewhere in the mist two readers are grazing in two grey drizzles, each in the other's reflection, walking toward a few seconds they will share again. The eels are keeping very quiet. The heron is stepping into ground that has only just been decided. The pools cycle through their borrowed skies.

Nothing has been fixed, because nothing was broken.

The power turns and goes, and the Levels don't mark the difference, and that was the whole of what it wanted.
