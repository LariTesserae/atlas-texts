# The Attention

## Notice

It did not come because anyone called. No one in the Seam prays aloud; nothing aloud survives there long enough to be heard. It came the way attention comes to anything: a pattern caught its eye.

From far off, from whatever height such a power looks down from, the mountain is a wrinkle and the crack a hairline. Most hairlines on most mountains mean nothing. This one moved. Every dusk a grey exhalation rose out of it and every dawn it breathed back in, and threaded through that breathing, at a resolution almost too fine to see, was something the power knew very well, because it does the same work itself.

Something was being held together while it came apart. And it was being held from both ends.

So it came closer.

## The Voice That Did Not Carry

It arrived badly. It arrived the way it usually arrives, as a kind of brightness and a kind of speaking. It stood at the lip of the rift at dusk and said, in the voice that has been heard in other places as thunder or as a still small sound, *I am here. Do not be afraid.*

The fog took the brightness and turned it into more fog. The moths were rising, and the voice went into them the way a stone goes into moss. It died within a few paces, smothered, softer and softer, until it was nothing, and not one person in the town below had turned their head.

The power stood in the rising wings for a long time. They brushed whatever it had for a face. This did not happen to it often: it had been made quiet.

It understood, slowly, that this was the first thing the place had to teach it. In the Seam nothing is received from a distance. If it wanted to say anything here, it would have to come close enough to touch.

## The Mercies It Did Not Choose

Before it went down, it thought. That is part of what wisdom is: the pause between the power to do a thing and the doing of it. It looked at the obvious mercies one at a time and held each of them to the light, what light there was.

**It could close the crack.** Press the walls together, knit the basalt, end the widening in an afternoon. The bridges would stop breaking and the families would stop being carried apart.

But it followed the warm air down with its attention and saw what the warm air did. It rose and met the cold and made the fog. The fog made the drip, the drip made the moss, and the moss fed the snails, the ferns, the lizards, and the soft grey sheets of wings. The green was young because the crack was young. The opening was not only the wound. It was the breath. Close the crack and the breath stops. The fog would stop pouring in like milk into a cup. The moss would go gold, then brown, then to dust on bare stone, and the people would have their town back, whole, in a dead place. The gift and the cost came from the same source. You cannot keep the one and refuse the other.

**It could hold the walls still.** Leave the crack open but stop it growing, freeze it at twenty paces forever.

This one it considered longer, because it was subtler, and because it was a real kindness. It would end a labour. Nothing would need letting out ever again. But the power looked at the long bridge, furred with thousands of record-knots, and understood that it was not looking at a burden people endured on the way to their real lives. It was looking at their real lives. A child born "when the long bridge had seven poles." Goodnight pulled across twenty paces of air. The Seam's whole grammar is built on a distance that changes. Freeze the distance and the grammar goes slowly dead, like a language whose only subject has been removed. Something might grow in its place. Something always does. But it would be the power's choice laid over theirs, and they had not asked.

**It could lift them out.** Carry the forty up onto the slopes and into the world of voices, and settle them somewhere kind.

They already knew the way up. There are stairs. The ones who wanted to leave had left. The ones who stayed had stayed with open eyes, knowing the town was built for hundreds and holds forty. Rescuing people from a choice they made clearly is not rescue.

**It could light the dark.** Bring colour to the Seam for more than an hour a day, so the green could be seen for what it is.

The Seam's people have large eyes that narrow at the midday blade. The lizards bask for that hour and hide the rest. The pale-stemmed plants feed on rot in the dim, and the white stars open only in fog. The power saw that the dimness was not a lack. It was a condition, and everything alive here had made itself for it.

**It could go down and look.** It could find out once and for all who ties the mirrored knots at the root, and come back up and tell them.

It wanted this one most. That was how it knew to distrust it.

So it put the mercies down, one by one. It did this not because mercy was wrong but because each of these was a mercy for some other place, a place it had imagined. It had not yet met the place that was actually there.

It went down the stairs on foot, as small as it could make itself, one hand trailing along the wall the way theirs do.

## What It Asked

It found Draw in the grey hour, on the lower bridges, with her forearm laid along a railing-cord and her eyes shut.

It did not speak. It had learned that much. It waited, and the moths settled on it as they settle on everything, and it waited through perhaps a hundred drips. Then she opened her eyes and saw a stranger on her bridge, which had not happened in eleven years.

She was not afraid. Draw has spent her life feeling for the wrongness in things, and there was none in it that she could find. She held out her hand, palm up, as you do in the Seam, and waited to be given a name.

It had none. Nothing that could be pressed into a palm.

So it did the only honest thing and laid its hand in hers, empty. She understood at once, the way you understand a child who cannot yet speak. She closed her fingers over its hand and held it. Then, with her thumb, slowly, she pressed a pattern into it.

It was the one the adults give to children leaning over edges. *Not yet.*

Maybe she meant *you do not have a name yet*. Maybe she meant something else. The power kept it anyway, and has kept it since.

Then it asked her its question, clumsily, with its hands. It had watched the pairs across the gap, and it had some of the grammar now, enough to ask badly. It laid her hand flat on the cord she had been tending and pressed toward her, then pushed its palms together, closing a gap.

*Do you want me to stop it?*

She did not answer quickly. She stood with her hand under its hand on the cord, feeling the tension the widening had put there overnight. Then she took its fingers and put them where hers had been. She made it feel the cord: bowstring-tight at one anchor, slack at the other, the wrongness travelling through the line like a held breath.

She loosened the lashing and let the tension ease through both their hands. She spliced in snail-thread. And then, with its fingers under hers, she tied the record-knot.

*I let this out. The walls moved. I followed them.*

That was her answer. It was not *no*, and it was not *yes*. It was *this is what we do*. The power, which has watched the whole world widen away from its first closeness for longer than there has been stone, found that it had nothing to add.

## The Season

It stayed a season. It told itself it was learning, and it was.

It walked the bridges with her every grey hour. Its palms began, very faintly, to ridge. It learned the knot, *the* knot, and learned to read tension with the inside of its forearm, and was bad at it for a long time, and Draw corrected it with squeezes of the wrist, the way the children are corrected.

It did use its power, a little. It came to understand that this was allowed, as long as the power went where the people's hands already went.

**The old cords.** The railing-cords at the middle of the long bridge are so dense with knots that no one living can read them. Draw's hands can read most of the Seam, but they stop there, and she had always carried that stopping as a small grief. She thought something was written there that had been lost.

The power could read them. So one morning it took her hand and ran it along the oldest stretch, slowly. It pressed each knot into her palm in the grammar she knew, translating as it went.

It was names. It was instructions for tending. It was *I let this out, the walls moved, I followed them*, over and over, hundreds of years of it. Among these, here and there, were the small private things the pairs send across the gap: *the fire caught. My knee is bad today. I dreamt of you.* The first builders had not been a separate order of beings with lost knowledge. They were exactly like her.

She stood with her hand on the cord for a long time after that. The power did not know whether it had given her something or taken something away. In the evening she pressed into its palm a pattern it had not been taught. Later it worked out that the pattern meant *company*, or *not alone*, depending on context. In the Seam those are nearly the same word.

**The fifth child.** On the upper tier of the gentler wall there is a woman whose daughter died two winters ago. The child's name-cord hangs on the long bridge with the others. Name-cords have to be let out too, or the widening snaps them, and every season this woman walks out onto the long bridge alone and lets out her dead daughter's name. No one else does it for her. No one would presume to.

The power could not undo the death. It considered whether it could, and what that would mean, and what it would teach every grieving person in every place. It set the idea down without much struggle, because the woman had not asked for that either.

What it did was go with her. When she walked out onto the bridge with the coil at her hip, it held the coil. It paid the thread out to her hand as she needed it. She spliced, and it passed her the line. That was all. She knew what it was, by then; everyone in the Seam did. She did not ask it for anything more, and it gave her nothing more. When the knot was tied she put her hand over the name and kept it there. The power put its hand over hers, and they stayed until the midday blade came down the wall and passed over them and went away.

**The widower.** There is an old man on the fourth tier of the gentle wall. His wife lived across from him for forty years, and her room has been empty since the spring. Their goodnight-cord still runs doorway to doorway, and every dusk he sits with it across his lap.

The power could have answered it. It would have been easy to pull the cord from the other side in her grammar, which the power could read, and give him goodnight.

It did not. A kindness that is a lie curdles, and the Seam is a place built on the truth of touch. You feel what is actually there. So it did not go to her doorway. It went to his, sat beside him in the dusk, and let the moths rise around them both. When he pulled the cord and nothing came back, it put its hand on his forearm. It was a squeeze, the plainest word in the Seam: *I'm here. Someone is.*

Near the end of the season he let the cord out one last time, and then untied it. He tied it to the long bridge, among the names.

**The children.** It did almost nothing for the children. It watched them line snail-shells along the ledges of abandoned tiers and let the lizards run across their bellies. It watched one of them, the smallest, lie on the bridge with her face over the side more often than the others. It saw Draw watching her too, with the look of someone choosing an apprentice. It decided that Draw did not need help with this, and she didn't.

## The Owl

Near the end, it followed an owl.

It had not decided to. It was at the lip at dusk and a white shape folded its wings and dropped into the rising grey, and the power's attention went down after it. It went past the lowest dwellings and the last sheets of settled moths, past the bare wet black of the walls, into the blue, where the air grew warm and smelled of iron and the walls leaned closer on either side.

It could have gone all the way. Nothing in the world could have stopped it.

Somewhere in the blue, where the walls were close enough for the owl's wingtips to brush both sides, a cord touched it. It was a line strung across the narrows, too thin to walk and never meant to be. The power laid itself against it the way Draw had taught, and felt a single pull come through: the first knot, *the* knot, tied in mirror.

It was not addressed to the power. The power could feel that the way you can feel a letter meant for someone else. It was going up. It was the other half of a conversation that had been going on since before the town was built. Whoever or whatever was down at the root, the living or the dead, the first builders or the unbroken stone itself, had not asked to be seen.

The power hung there in the warm dark with the cord across it. Then it did something it rarely does. It gave itself an instruction.

*Not yet.*

It wasn't *never*, and it wasn't *danger*. It was *not yet*. Some things are sewn from both sides because each side cannot see the other. That is what keeps them reaching. It went back up with the owl at dawn, damp and warm, smelling of wet iron, and told no one what it had nearly done.

## The Knot

On its last morning it walked out to the middle of the long bridge with Draw.

It had thought about leaving something behind: a gift, a sign, a mark on the stone that said *a power was here and it loved this place*. There are places in the world with such marks. People fight over them.

Instead it asked her, badly, with its hands, whether it could tie a knot.

She considered this. The long bridge is for names, and it had none. Then she took its hand, pressed into the palm the pattern she had given it on the first day, *not yet*, and guided its fingers to the cord.

So among the thousands of names and the oldest lumpy cords, it tied its name, which is a child's warning about a drop. Beside it, because every splice gets one, it tied the record-knot. It tied it the right way round, from above, from this side.

*I let this out. The walls moved. I followed them.*

It is not certain that this was the right choice. Wisdom is not certainty. Wisdom is choosing with care and then going on attending. But it had watched every living thing in the Seam answer the widening in the same way. The spiders, never discouraged. The snails, making bridges out of patience. The moths, passing a billion stitches through the eye of the crack every night. The tenders, splicing and loosening and tying a small confession each season. The power had watched them for a season and understood that it had been doing the same work, clumsily and at a much larger scale, for as long as it had existed. The whole world had been widening away from it since the beginning. It had never been able to close the gap, and it had never been meant to. It could only follow and keep the reach alive.

It had come to the Seam thinking it might be the one to help. It left having been taught.

## Dusk

It is gone now, or as gone as such things get. Its attention is a light thing to carry and it carries a great deal.

The crack still widens by a handspan every year. There are still forty people, perhaps fewer. The woman on the upper tier still lets out her daughter's name each season, alone, though some seasons she finds the coil already unwound at her feet and cannot remember doing it. The old man sleeps without a cord across his lap and dreams of her anyway. The smallest child has begun walking the lower bridges in the grey hour behind Draw, laying her small forearm along the railings with her eyes shut, feeling for the wrongness and finding it.

Draw's palms are more ridged than ever. At night her fingers still move a little, working knots.

At dusk she sits in her doorway on the fourth tier with a single cord across her lap. It goes down. The fog pours in and the drip begins, the moths rise, and an owl drops past, white against the rising, and is gone. The quiet becomes total.

She closes her eyes, which means *I am listening with the rest of me.*

Up the cord from very far below comes a touch: three short pulls, then a long, slow draw. *Will it hold?*

She answers with the first knot. *Yes. For now. I'll let it out tomorrow.*

And sometimes, not every night, and never when she is looking for it, she feels something else. It is not on the cord. It is on the back of her hand, light, the way you rest a hand on a sleeping back to feel it breathe. It asks nothing and says nothing. It is only *here. Someone is.*

It is one more thing tending the join.
