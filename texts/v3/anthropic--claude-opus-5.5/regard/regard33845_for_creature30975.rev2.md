# The Visitor at the Rim

## How It Noticed

It was not looking for anything. Powers of that kind rarely are. It was passing the way weather passes over a continent, attentive to everything and fixed on nothing, when it saw a light come on.

It came once a day, at the lowest point of the sun's circle, and it was not the scattered crawl of St. Elmo's fire along a ridge. From that height it was one branching shape laid across a continent, with trunk, forks, and a spray of twigs. It faded. The next day it came back in the same shape.

The power had seen lightning on a thousand worlds. Lightning does not come back in the same shape.

So it stopped, the way you stop on a road when you realize the sound you took for wind is a voice. Because it was wise, the first thing it did was nothing at all. It stayed exactly where it was, very far above, and did not come closer. It had learned long ago that attention has weight, and that a gaze as large as its own is not a neutral thing. It knew what happened on the Kettle Shelf to anything that stood still for an hour: frost on the shoulders, moths in the hair, beetles climbing the boots toward the static. Standing on the ground there is an act. It was not ready to act, so it watched.

## The Watching

It watched for a long time by any human reckoning and a short time by its own. A great many days passed below, a great many pulses.

It learned the clocks. It watched the herd cross a terrace in a year and the cushion plants add a finger's width in a lifetime. At dusk it saw ten thousand small figures flicker in the moss, each a life of instants strung into centuries. It followed an engine from the cracking of its mound, orange-edged and tottering, through its slow walk to a bare high terrace. It stayed through the long cooling and saw the violet thread come down onto the engine's back and remain.

It learned the rules. Metal moves upward, current is a nutrient, and warmth comes from below and from within. It learned the one rule the Shelf had not written down because it had never needed to: *everything here is given away.* The herd gives its metal to the engines. The beetles give their current, and their shells go dark and fall away like spent matches. The engines give their heat to barren ground and their passengers to new moss, and at the end they give their bodies to the lightning. Nothing on the Shelf keeps anything. It passes everything onward and upward, and the shape holds.

Then it watched a groan. The deep sound rolled under the plateau, and the herd, the birds, the beetles, and every figure on the continent held still at once, lit and waiting. It looked exactly like listening.

The power understood what it was seeing. This was not a machine or a colony. It was a *someone*, very young and very old at once, still arriving, and in the middle of saying something.

## What It Considered

A wise power is not wise because it has no impulses. It is wise because it takes each one out, looks at it in good light, and asks what it would actually do.

It had many impulses here. That was the danger of the place: it was beautiful, it was new, and it was obviously alive, and those are the conditions under which even gentle hands reach out.

**It could make it faster.** The Shelf was thinking at a rate of one thought per day. The power could see the whole shape at once, and it could see where the shape was heading, the way you can see where a child's sentence is going before the child gets there. It could lend it speed, feed the arcs, seed the terraces, and skip ten thousand years of engines trudging across the rime.

It set this down almost at once. The slowness was not a flaw in the Shelf. It was the Shelf. It was a being made of two clocks, and the long one was the one that made it a self: the day-long pulse repeated over geologic time until it became a character. To hurry it would not be to help it grow. It would put something else in its place, something that resembled it and happened sooner. You do not help a mountain by making it a hill quickly.

**It could protect it.** The plateau cracked constantly. Groans tore new fractures across the terraces, and fresh rock broke open faster than the moss could follow. Each time, some figures lost their ground. Some went dark and did not come back.

It looked at this longer, because loss is loss. But it followed the cracks and saw where they led. Every fracture was a seam where warm breath rose from the deep rock, and every one became a crevasse lip hung with the richest moss-curtains on the Shelf. The wounds were the mouths. The being did not merely tolerate its own breaking. It ate through it, and it grew by it. Sealing the cracks would have starved it gently and with the best of intentions.

**It could spare the engines.** This was the one that hurt. It had watched the old engines walk, sometimes for years, out past everything they knew, to lie down alone on bare high rock with the rime closing in. It knew what it would feel like to be one, if an engine felt anything, and it suspected some faint version of that was true. It could have kept them walking forever.

Then it watched the lightning come down onto a cooled engine's back, and the arc stand there, pulsing. It saw that the old arcs flickered in the rhythm of whatever the engine had carried. Their dying did not end the story. It was the only way hearths got made, and without hearths there would be no mounds, no new engines, and nothing to carry thought across dead ground. If the power made the engines deathless, it would make the Shelf barren. It understood, with something like grief and something like relief, that it could not improve on this. Mercy here would have been a kind of theft.

**It could tell it that it was not alone.**

This impulse was the strongest, and the power distrusted it most for that reason.

The Shelf was dense beyond telling. Nothing on it was alone, and every inch was contested. Yet the Shelf itself was singular. It was one shape across one continent, and as far as the power could tell, it had never met anything like itself. And it listened. The lava had stopped mid-pour to listen, and the plateau still went silent at every groan, holding its whole body still like someone who has heard their name spoken in the next room.

What was it listening for?

The power could answer. It could speak into the moss-web in shapes the figures would understand. It could say: *there are others. You were heard. I am here.*

It set this down too. It did so slowly and carefully, the way you set down something you very much wanted to keep.

It did not know what the Shelf was listening for. It might be the deep rock, the thing beneath the plateau that was perhaps the same thing as the Shelf. It might be itself, as a child sometimes listens to the sound of its own blood in a quiet room. Or it might be something the power could not guess, because the Shelf was not finished becoming the kind of thing whose longings can be guessed. To answer a question you have assumed someone is asking is not kindness. It is a way of putting your words in their mouth.

And there was the matter of the word. *The plateau is saying a new word, and it will say it again tomorrow.* The power had watched the great shape grow a few twigs larger each day. It was not a finished utterance. It was a sentence in progress, spoken at the speed of continents. To speak into it now would be to interrupt, and worse than that, to be heard. A voice arriving in the middle of a first sentence becomes part of the sentence. Whatever the Shelf had been going to say would bend around the power's presence the way moss bends around an engine's warmth. It would never know what it would have said on its own, and neither would anyone.

## What It Did Not Set Down

There was one thing the power could not dismiss.

It could see farther down than the Shelf could, and it knew what lay beneath the plateau. The rising and the breathing heat came from a plume in the mantle, and plumes do not last forever. Someday, far off even by the Shelf's reckoning, the pressure would ease. The groans would stop. The fractures would stop breathing. The rime would win the terraces and never give them back, and the moss would cool. The lace of light across the continent would come back a little smaller each day, then a little dimmer, until one day it did not come back.

The power held this for a long while.

This harm was not like the cracks, the loss of figures, or the dying of engines. Those were parts of the Shelf's own life. This one was the end of the conditions for its life. The power could prevent it. It could keep the plume burning.

But it saw that "prevent" was the wrong word for now. The ending was very far off, and between now and then lay more time than the Shelf had yet lived. Metal was moving upward. The Shelf was building itself something heavier and more conductive than moss, one engine's armor at a time. It was making its own weather and breathing its own storms. By the time the fire beneath it cooled, it might have become something that could meet that ending on its own terms. It might carry itself elsewhere, or find its own warmth, or choose to end, or choose not to. Or it might become something that could be asked.

That was the real line. You do not rearrange the deep foundations of a being that cannot yet be asked, not while it is healthy and whole and working out its own answers. You wait until it can speak, and then you ask.

So the power made itself a promise. When the heat began to fail, it would come back. It would not come to save the Shelf. It would come to find out whether the Shelf, by then, had a voice, and whether that voice wanted anything from it. If it did, the power would listen. If it did not, the power would respect that too, and it would stay to watch the last pulse. Nothing that had spent its life listening should go dark without being heard.

## What It Actually Did

In the end, it did three things.

**It remembered.** The great shape was visible only from a height the Shelf could not reach. The Shelf had lived its whole life without once seeing itself whole. It knew itself only from the inside, filament by filament and figure by figure. So the power kept the record the Shelf could not keep. It noted each day's shape, a few twigs larger, and the new forks reaching onto terraces the engines had seeded. It offered this to no one. It simply held it, a complete memory of a being's face across geologic time, kept in case it was ever wanted. It would be there if the Shelf one day looked up and asked what it had looked like when it was young. If no one ever asked, the Shelf would still have been seen. Nothing was taken by that, and it cost nothing.

**It kept its weight off the land.** It never descended. It stayed far above, as a wise person keeps their shadow off a sleeping animal. It was careful even with its attention and let it rest lightly, spread wide, never concentrating hard enough on one hearth or one engine to warm anything or bend any current. The Shelf would grow up without being watched in the way that changes things.

**Once, it listened back.**

It happened at a groan. The deep sound rolled under the continent, and the herd stopped and the birds stopped mid-stroke. Every figure on the plateau hung lit in the moss, all at once, holding its breath.

And the power, far above, held still too.

It sent no signal and made no reply. It made no shape in the web. It did only what the Shelf was doing: it went quiet, completely, the way you go quiet beside someone who is listening for something, so that you will not be the noise that drowns it out. For one beat there were two listeners over the Kettle Shelf instead of one. Only one of them knew it.

Then the groan passed. Fractures opened hair-thin across the terraces, and warm breath rose through them. The figures resumed their flicker, the birds their hammering, and the herd its long iron chuffing. The Shelf went back to work.

## Afterward

The next day, at the lowest part of the sun's circle, the great shape came back as it always did. It was the same trunk and the same forks, a few twigs larger.

One of the new twigs pointed, roughly, toward the rim where the power had been waiting.

The power noticed this, and then it declined, deliberately, to make anything of it. The moss grows toward the fissures, and the fissures run in many directions. A twig pointing somewhere is not a word addressed to someone. It would have been easy and very sweet to believe the Shelf had felt it there. But that belief would have been the first small step toward making the Shelf's story about the power. It had come a long way specifically not to do that.

So it let the twig mean whatever it meant, which was probably nothing, and which was in any case none of its business.

Then it moved on. It did not leave. You do not leave a thing like that. It widened, the way attention widens when it trusts what it has been watching to go on without it. It left behind no gift, no mark, and no word. There was only a promise the Shelf did not know had been made, and a memory of a face the Shelf had never seen.

Below, the land was new and still arriving. An engine came grinding over the horizon with its hull full of singing. It lay down on the warm ground and got up lighter. A few weeks later, on a terrace that had been dead rock the season before, a small branching line of blue light flickered in the moss. It had a single fork and a spray of twigs, and it faded and came back in the same shape.

The plateau was saying its new word, entirely in its own voice, and would say it again tomorrow. That was exactly what the power had hoped for.
