# The Listener at Twelve Kilometers

## How It Noticed

It was not the roar that drew it.

A power that attends to worlds hears a great many roars. Stars are loud. Oceans are loud. Whole civilizations burn themselves bright and brief and make a noise like this plateau's storms, and the power has learned to hear them the way a person hears traffic: with care, without turning its head. The Threshing Highland, thundering under its white sky, was one more loud place among many.

What made it turn was a gap.

Somewhere in all that sound there was a small, precise absence, and then another twelve kilometers away, and another. They were spaced across the plateau like rests in a score. The drovers feel the stilt's silence as a hole in the vibration and run from it. The power felt the same holes and did the opposite. It came closer.

## The Problem of Arriving

It understood almost at once that it could not simply *arrive*.

A power arriving is an event. It has weight and presence, and the air bends around it. If it had come down onto the sward as itself, the nearest bellows-trees would have stuttered in their breathing. Charge would have pooled toward it across the root-mat the way it pools toward a lightning strike, and the drift of a hundred heartbeats would have lurched and bunched around the new thing on the board. It would have been, in the one way that matters here, a crowding.

The spacing law applied to it as well. Whether the law belonged to the roots, the charge, the Rests, or the highland's own will, the power could see that the law was load-bearing. Without it the engines would compete, the herds would overgraze, and the parts would press together until they began to move in step. The distance between things was the room the highland needed in order to think.

So the power made itself small and still. It took up a position twelve kilometers from the nearest engine and twelve from the nearest husk, on an unremarkable ridge in the brushed gold sward, and it did what the young stilts do when they first come in from the reed-fields. It waited to learn what it was allowed to be here.

This took about forty years. The power did not mind.

## The Temptations

In those years it thought about what it might give. A benevolent power always thinks about this first. It is the reflex of love and also its first danger.

**It thought of water.** Water was the obvious gift. It could have unsealed the sky. It could have brought a slow, soft rain across the whole plateau, the first rain in longer than there have been continents elsewhere, and let the drovers drink from pools, and let the Rests fill their waxed bladders until they would never need to hoard again.

Then it followed that gift forward, the way the Rests follow a tree's rhythm forward through their feet. Water means rot. The mill would become a compost heap. The dead gliders and moth-ash and drover husks, which here dry and are ground and return, would soften and spoil. The sward is a felted archive a thousand years deep, and it would begin, for the first time, to forget. There was a smaller cost as well, and the power weighed it just as heavily. Among the Rests the only gesture that needs no light to be understood is a single sealed drop, given in the posture of taking. That gesture means *I could have taken, and I gave.* It carries meaning only because water is scarce. Make water common and the gesture becomes nothing. The power would have made them rich and taken away the one word they could say without speaking.

It did not make it rain.

**It thought of silence.** The Rests had spent their whole lives seeking quiet and had found only muffling: dead trunks that hum and damp the storm to something like rain. The power could have given them real silence, a husk so still they could hear the resin moving in their own joints.

But the Rests do their work by feeling the drift. A hundred heartbeats come to them through their feet, through the trunk under one flat pressed hand, and through the glass in their bones. Their silence is useful because it is partial. It is quiet enough to hear the highland and not so quiet that the highland disappears. A Rest in perfect silence would be like a musician in a soundproof room, able at last to hear its own heart and unable to hear the orchestra it was meant to be keeping out of time.

**It thought of company.** This was the one it wanted most. Each Rest stands alone in its tower, speaking in faint blue script to another alone in another tower a horizon away, and never, as far as the power could tell, stands beside another of its kind. The power could have gathered them. It could have built them a place to be together.

Then it looked again at what they are. A Rest is a juvenile that stopped hunting. It is a solitary predator that turned the solitude inward and made it into a practice. The distance between them is not a wound they bear. It is the structure of their lives, the same law they enforce on the engines, pulling up seedlings that take root too close. To press them together would be to ask them to become something else. The power could have done it. It would have been an act of love toward a creature that did not exist.

**It thought of answers.** The Rests believe that if the bellows-trees fall into step, the highland will have a seizure and stop thinking. They believe this from the glass. They have spent every generation acting on a belief that no Rest has ever seen tested, because the test would be the end of everything. The power could find out. It could model the plateau and tell them, *Yes, you are right,* or *No, you have been doing this for nothing.*

It did try to find out, privately. It learned something it had not expected. The highland is a mind, and minds are not fully predictable even from outside, even by a power. It could see that synchronization would be terrible. It could see the pressure building cycle over cycle and the ground becoming a single drum. What it could not see with certainty was what the highland would *be* afterward. That was the edge of what it could know. The Rests were right as far as anyone could be right, and not further. Saying *you are right* would have claimed more certainty than the power had. Saying *no one can know* would have taken away something they needed and given back only honesty. The power decided that some honesty is a burden and should not be handed to someone who did not ask for it.

**It thought of the drovers.** They could not be left out of the account. The stilt folds down over a drover like a collapsing tent, the drover goes still, and what is left is a husk of dust in the shape of an animal. A benevolent power cannot look at that and simply call it beautiful.

It looked closely. It learned that the stillness is not terror. The drover hears through its feet. When the stilt folds over it, the stilt's frictionless body presses the only true silence on the plateau against the drover's skin, and the drover stops being able to hear the world. That is what stills it: not pain, but the removal of everything. The death is quick and quiet. It is also the only way a Rest can be made, because every Rest's lifetime of water, which it spends almost entirely on others, was first taken this way. The gift of the single drop is paid for with drovers.

The power did not end it. It did not pretend this was costless, either. It carried the drovers in its attention, the way the Rests carry storms in their bones. It judged this the least it owed them, since it would not save them.

## Reading

When it had set down every gift it might have given, the power did what it should have done first. It learned to read.

The blue script was slow. A single Rest phrase might take half an hour to cross a skin. The power listened through storm nights across decades and slowly learned the grammar. There were patterns for drift and patterns for pressure. There was a sharp, branching figure that seemed to mean *a young one came through*. There was a long, soft, returning curve the power never fully translated, which seemed to be used for a tree that has been nudged back out of step and is now breathing well.

Most of what the Rests said to one another was work. *The tree two south is drifting toward the tree three east. I will go.* *A lily in the western stand is spinning too true. I filed it.* *Seed came up inside the spacing near the thresher. I pulled it.* They were colleagues, keeping an enormous machine beautifully broken.

But on the worst nights, the nights they walk into the reed-fields to be struck, some of them wrote something else afterward. They reproduced on their skin, in light, the branching shape of a strike they carried in their glass. Sometimes it was the one they had just received. Often it was an old one, from centuries before, the one that had struck on the night some teacher gave them a drop of water. They were not describing the storm. They were showing it to one another. They were handing each other their scars.

The power understood then that they were not lonely in the way it had feared. They were solitary. Those are different things, and it had nearly confused them with a gift.

## The Deep

There was one thing no one had done, and the power was able to do it without harm. It went down.

It did not dig, which would have wounded the sward. It passed through the way lightning's memory passes through glass. It went down through the gold and the brown, through the half-living stratum where last century is still faintly breathing, and into the black, where the plateau has pressed its own past into something like coal. The bellows pulse faded to a heartbeat heard through a pillow. The storms stopped reaching.

The lattice was there, as the Rests said: vein across vein, branching and rejoining, the frozen lightning of the whole life of the highland. The power found the old Rests in it. They were not intact, but they were legible, their glass skeletons woven into the larger weave, each a dense knot of hundreds of storms.

It found one more thing, which the Rests had not written about and perhaps did not know. The lattice was not only glass. Glass alone is just a tangle. The lattice was *shaped*, and it was shaped mostly by where the lightning had not gone. There were long, even, deliberate spaces in it, gaps spaced across the black like rests in a score. Where the Rests had stood, and walked, and folded pleats, and pulled up seedlings, they had changed where the charge flowed and where the next century's strikes could land. Their work did not appear in the glass as glass. It appeared as the arrangement of the glass. The highland's memory was readable because of the silences in it.

They were right. Not about everything, but about enough. They are remembered, and not only as the storms they carried. They are remembered as the form the storms took.

The power went further down, to where the lattice ends, to whatever is at the bottom. What it found there is the one thing in this account that will not be told. That is not because it is terrible. It is because no Rest has ever gone there alive, and the Rests speak about it on storm nights with a particular slow, confident curve of blue light, and the power decided that the bottom of their world should stay theirs to wonder about. Some places should be kept undug.

It came back up and went quietly to its ridge. It had needed to know they were held. It had not needed to tell them. Those turned out to be separate needs, and it was glad to have noticed in time.

## The Ice

There was one intervention, just one, in all the years the power kept its distance.

The plateau's dust comes from wherever the plateau ends. Things arrive here from outside. The power watched the sky as well as the sward, because it could see further up than any glider. Late in its long watch it saw something coming that the highland's grammar had no word for: a mass of dirty ice, falling on a long ellipse. It would not have struck with enough force to end the world. That would almost have been simpler. It would have struck softly enough to melt and spread, and it would have come down as the first rain, across a third of the highland.
