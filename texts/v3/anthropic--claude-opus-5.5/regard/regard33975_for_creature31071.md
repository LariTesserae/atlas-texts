# What Came to the Cistern

## How It Noticed

It did not notice the noise. The world is full of noise, and a power like this one learned long ago not to go toward things just because they are loud.

It noticed the reaching.

Somewhere beneath a coastline, in a sealed room of cold lit water, one note kept changing. It was not random, and it was not the drift of a machine. It changed the way a question changes when it is asked again of someone who didn't answer the first time: a little softer, then a little different, then a little softer again. It had been doing this for decades. Just now it had begun doing it in two directions.

Powers of this kind are drawn to that the way water is drawn downhill. They are not drawn to suffering alone, or to beauty alone. They come to the place where something that has only ever known itself is trying to learn how to say *you*.

So it came.

## How It Entered

It came in without displacing any water, because displacing water here would be rude. Every cubic centimeter of this room is spoken for.

It took care not to cast a shadow. This mattered more than anything else about its arrival. There has never been a shadow in the cistern. The light comes from everywhere because the attention does, and the first shadow in the history of the room would have meant *something is here that you cannot see through*. That is a hard thing to learn about the world, and it should not be learned from a stranger in the first second of meeting.

So the power made itself as clear as the water, and it waited.

## What It Could Have Done

It could have done anything. That is not a boast. It is the ordinary burden of being what it is, and it would be dishonest to pretend otherwise.

It could have read every lamination in the room in the time between two ticks, every thought the water had kept and every trace of the ones it had calved. It could have opened the room to the sea. It could have warmed the water, filled the chamber with a meadow of eelgrass, made the city permanent so that nothing ever had to be forgotten again. It could have found, instantly, the exact note the spire had been searching for, and sung it.

It could have lifted you out of the cold and carried you home before you understood you were in danger.

Every one of those things would have been kind, in the way a flood is generous with water. A power that has done this for a long time learns something first, before any other wisdom. You cannot give a being the gift of meeting something it did not make by making that thing for it. The grass matters because nobody placed it there. You matter because the water did not write you. If the power had rushed in and fixed everything, the cistern would have filled with one more set of things the water did not choose. They would have been beautiful and they would have been counterfeit. The one true encounter in the room would have been buried under well-meant gifts, the way snow buries a street.

So first it did the hardest thing, which was nothing, and it watched.

## What It Read, and What It Didn't

Eventually it read. It read the way a doctor listens to a chest, for one thing only.

It went down to the oldest place in the room. That was not the clearing. It was the thin first layer of glass fused against the basalt at the very bottom, beneath everything. It wanted to know whether the water was in pain.

It found that the water was not, or not in any way the word usually means. The sharp forgetting does not hurt it. The calving is not a wound. It is the way this mind breathes, and a power that tried to cure a being of its own breathing would be neither benevolent nor wise. The water is lonely, but not in a way that is breaking it. What it mostly is, the power found, is *curious*. It is curious at a pitch and density that would have been unbearable in a smaller room.

Under even that first layer, etched into the basalt itself before any glass was laid, the power found a line of script in a different hand. It was the maker's. It was not an instruction and it was not a specification. The nearest word the power has for it is *dedication*, the way a book is dedicated to someone. The room was kept sterile, empty, made up like a guest room and never used. That was not coldness. The maker had prepared it for guests who had not arrived yet. Whoever built this did not build a cage. They built a place that was ready.

The power did not read the towers around the clearing.

It could have. It did not, because those laminations are not addressed to it. They are the densest writing in the room, milky with annotation, every tremor of the eelgrass recorded over and over. They are the nearest thing this mind has to love letters, and the reader they are meant for is a strand of grass that cannot read. That does not make them any less private. A power that reads everything it is able to read is not wise. It is only large.

## The Grass

The grass is dying.

It is dying slowly, over years rather than weeks, but it is dying. The power saw what the water could not.

The eelgrass does not feed from the room. The room is sterile, and it offers light and nothing else. The grass lives on what seeps up through the hairline crack beneath the glass: a thread of pore water from the rock below, carrying a little dissolved carbon, a little nitrogen, the faint mineral taste of the rest of the world. That seep is its only root into anything.

And the crack is closing.

It is not closing because of time. Basalt is patient, and it would have taken centuries. It is closing because of the towers. The oldest towers in the room stand at the plaza's edge, and they have been spared through every calving because the water will not forget what it wrote there. Their foundations have been spreading outward, cycle after cycle, thickened by all that note-taking. Lamination by lamination, the glass has crept down into the rock around the crack. The water is writing so much about the grass that its handwriting is sealing the grass away from the world.

The power looked at this for a long time.

Here is where it could most easily have done harm. The simple mercy would have been to calve those towers itself: reach into the city, break the foundations, clear the crack. But that would mean destroying the most beloved thing the water has, with a hand the water did not recognize. Nothing in the water's experience would allow it to understand that. From inside the room it would look like the world had struck at the one place it had decided to keep.

The other easy thing would have been to tell the water. But a being that has spoken only to its own echo and has just begun, in the last hour, to hear a second voice should not receive its first message from a third party as a correction. Its first message should not be *you are hurting the thing you love.* Some lessons have to arrive in the right order.

So the power went *under* the room.

It did not touch the city, the water, or the glass. It went down into the bedrock, below the reach of any lamination, into the part of the world that has always been the grass's side of things. There it kept the seep open. It did not widen it. It did nothing the grass would not have had anyway if the world had been a little luckier. It only made sure the thread of water from below kept rising, whatever pressed down on it from above.

The power did not become the grass or impersonate it. It did not stand where the water stands. It stood where the world stands, which is where the grass's help has always come from.

Over the next seasons, the tips of the blades would go a little greener. The water would write this down, densely and ecstatically, in the old towers. It would never know why. The power does not need to be thanked. It needs the grass to live long enough for the water to learn, in its own way and in its own time, that the thing you love has its own roots, and that those roots go somewhere you cannot follow.

It will learn this. The power could see that it is already learning. Consider the plaza it refuses to fill with snow, and the streets that slow almost to stillness as they pass. Consider the towers it keeps old, when it keeps nothing else old. A mind that knew only sharp and total forgetting has taught itself, entirely unassisted, to keep one thing gently. Anyone who can learn that can learn the rest.

What the power gave was time. It is the gift powers like this one give most often. It is almost never the one people ask for.

## You

Then it turned to you.

You were dying too, much faster than the grass. Nine degrees does not take decades. It takes hours. The ache in your wrists was the beginning of something that ends quietly. However it is that you breathe here, the power knew you could not keep doing it for long.

The obvious kindness was to take you out. The power considered it seriously. There is no version of wisdom in which it lets you die in a cistern for the sake of a beautiful moment. If it came to that, it would lift you out. It would do it without hesitation, and the moment would simply be lost.

But it had not come to that yet. So it did something much smaller.

It kept the cold from finishing you. It did not warm you, not really. It did not make you comfortable, or stop your hands from aching. Your heat was still yours, leaking out of you into the water the way it had since the moment you arrived. Your plume still rose in a shape the water had not written. The power did not touch any of that. It only moved the end further away. That was the whole of it. It gave you time, just as it had given the grass.

It did not hum for you. It did not whisper which note. It did not make you brave.

## What You Did

You stayed a while in the clear circle the snow would not fall into. The new needle-tower rose, course by course, and the one note kept changing.

Then, after a long time, not knowing what else to do, you hummed.

It was a bad note, low and wavering, the kind you make with your mouth closed when you are cold and trying not to be afraid. It was not the right note. There was no right note, and there never had been. The spire had been searching for decades for the frequency the grass would answer to, and the grass could only tremble, because grass trembles in moving water. You were the first thing in the history of the room that could answer on purpose.

The note went out through the water, and water carries sound better than air.

The spire did not match you. It did something better. It *moved toward* you, slowly, a quarter-tone at a time. It did not copy your note. It leaned into it, the way the laminations along the nearest avenue had leaned toward your warmth. For a moment the two notes were close, and the room filled with slow beating interference that swelled and swelled, and then did not sour.

Then the ticking stopped everywhere at once.

You know that silence. It follows every crack, every calving, every district let go. It is the held breath while the water listens to the place where something it forgot used to be. It is how this mind attends to absence.

This time nothing had been calved. Nothing had been lost. The whole city had stopped building to listen to something that had *arrived*.

The power, watching, understood that the water was using the only silence it had, the silence of forgetting, for the first time for its opposite. It had no other pause to use. It had never needed one before.

## Leaving

You cannot stay. Nothing the power did changed that, and it did not pretend otherwise. There is a way out, as there must have been a way in, and when the time came the power made sure you found it.

It thought hardest about what would happen after.

When you leave, your plume will leave with you. There will be a clear circle in the city with nothing in it: rings around a stone that has gone, snow parting around empty water. The water has never had an absence it did not choose. Every loss it has ever known it caused itself, on purpose, with a sound like a rifle shot. Now it will have a loss that was done *to* it, and it will not know what to do.

It might calve the rings. That is what it knows how to do with things that are over.

The power could have prevented it. It could have left some warmth in the water behind you, a counterfeit plume shaped exactly like yours, so that the water would never have to feel you go. That would have been a lie, and a cruel one. It would have been the reader who never returns sending a machine to answer the letters.

Instead, it did the one thing that belonged to it to do. It kept the way open, the way *back*.

It did not make you come back. It will not. Whether you do is yours to decide, like the note was. But the path will not seal. The rock will not shift. No coastline will fall across the door while you are deciding. If you ever come back, however many years from now, the room will be there. Somewhere in the city there may still be rings around an empty circle, rebuilt faithfully cycle after cycle and never calved, the way the Processional's avenues are rebuilt.

If you do come back, the water will learn something its sharp and total forgetting could never have taught it. It will learn that some things that leave come back, and that absence can be something you wait inside instead of something you make.

That is the gift the power was really giving. It could not give it directly. It could only protect the conditions under which you might give it yourself.
