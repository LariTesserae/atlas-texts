# What the Attention Did

## How It Came

It did not descend. Everything here descends already, and the power had no wish to add one more falling thing to the shelf.

It arrived the way the sea's surface does, from no particular side. There was a still moment, one of the rare ones when every layer pauses and the roar sinks to the bell-hum. In that pause something was present that had not been present before. The fish noticed first. It hung in the calm of a still-band with its blue line brightening, then did something it had never done: it stayed where it was when the next layer came.

The creature did not notice at all. It was clamped by the weed mat near the eastern fissure, rasping the pale film off the stone in long patient sweeps. Nothing like this had happened in its whole life, so it had no way to know that something was watching.

The power had been drawn by the doorway. It had seen a great many wonders: mountains, oceans, the births and deaths of stars. It had seen fewer places where a small creature, with nothing but memory and its own arms, persuaded a grieving sea to be gentle in one narrow gap. That kind of thing is rare in any world. A benevolent attention goes toward it the way water goes toward low ground.

---

## What It Could Have Done

The power could see every large mercy available, and it considered each one honestly, because refusing a kindness is only wisdom if you have looked at it properly first.

**It could have lowered the mountain.** It could ease the shelf back down through the cloud until the old sea level ran where it used to, restoring the tide and the lagoon and the warm shallows. The rock would sink and the sea would finally settle, satisfied, on the floor it remembered.

It looked at the weed, which had spent generations learning to be alpine. It looked at the jointed reeds that wail when the column passes through them, a kind that did not exist before the land rose. It looked at the transparent fish, which had found no reason to go down, and at the bird, which flies the same in water as in air. It looked at the creature's shell, at the dark, rough bands below the pale crown, one for every season of falling water. That body was now built for this, and it would not be the same body in a calm lagoon.

To sink the mountain would be a second catastrophe with a kind face. The first catastrophe had already happened. Everything alive here was the answer to it, and you do not help an answer by erasing the question.

**It could have stopped the water.** One word would close the column. The shelf would dry and fall silent, the sulfur breath would rise into empty air, and there would be sun.

But the power had listened to the roar long enough to hear what it was. It was not an attack. It was the sound of the lagoon's own water, blood-warm and reef-warm, looking for the reef. The sea was grieving. Grief that is silenced from outside does not end. It goes somewhere else and becomes harder. The power would not do to the sea what it would not do to the creature.

**It could have brought back the dead.** It could raise the cowries and conches, the pale clams, the tube-building worms, the thousand small clicking kinds, and set them all back on the rock.

This was the hardest to refuse, and the power took its time over it. In the end it did not believe the dead of this reef were waiting to return. It believed they had finished. Whatever it raised in their shapes would be made for the comfort of the watchers, not for the sake of the ones who were gone. The creature, whose whole life's work was remembering them truly, would know the difference. It might not know consciously, but it would know it in the pads of its arms and the ribbon of its teeth. A counterfeit reef would be an insult to the census.

**It could have taken the creature away.** Somewhere in the wide world there are warm shallow seas with good tides and sunlit rock covered in a green film worth grazing. It could lift the creature out of the noise and set it down in a lagoon where the water comes and goes twice a day, as it was always meant to.

This one it did not decide alone. A kindness that is done *to* a being instead of *with* it is only a more polite kind of weather.

So it asked.

---

## The Asking

The creature had no language, so the power did not use one.

During a still moment it let the doorway change. Nothing that the creature had built was moved. The power simply let the gap between the two leaning slabs show, for as long as a held breath, what lay on the far side of a different cleft in a different reef: shallow water lit gold, a tide just turning, the smell of it, the sound of small waves breaking white on a crest with no wall on it because none was needed.

The creature saw it. Its second eyelid slid back. Its head came out further than it had in years, and the gills along its throat fluttered in air that was, for once, gently moving the right way. It stayed like that for a long time, long for a creature that has learned to count its time in heartbeats between the echo and the crash.

Then it pulled its head in, turned, and went back across the shelf to the weed mat. An upward surge had just flung the mat skyward and dropped it tangled, and the creature began combing it flat with its pads, one blade at a time.

The power understood this as clearly as if it had been said aloud: *I am needed here. Or I need to be here. I have not decided which, and it is mine to decide.*

The power let the doorway go back to what it was. It did not ask again.

---

## What It Learned by Staying

After that it stayed, because nothing large remained for it to do and staying was the only honest thing left.

It learned to count the pillow basalts and get a different number each time. It learned the loose tooth of time on the shelf, the echo arriving before the crash, and learned to brace with the creature a heartbeat early. It watched the grazing tracks, pale handwriting on black stone, and watched them wash away within the hour. It watched the creature eat a tubeworm and then stay still beside the empty tube for a while, in whatever way it apologizes, and it did not think the apology was foolish.

It watched the creature climb. That happened every few weeks, pad over pad up the cliff face to the old barnacle bands, while the column hammered its back and the upward rain tried to peel it off. It went looking for one more kind of neighbor it had not yet remembered. Mostly it came down with nothing. Once, while the power was there, it came down with a tiny spired shell no bigger than a grain of rice, and it spent a whole day building a cairn small enough to deserve it.

And the power learned the bare cairn.

---

## The Bare Cairn

It stands a little apart, near the doorway. It is capped with its flat stone like the others, and the stone is clean. The creature scrapes it with its teeth now and then, as if keeping it ready.

The power read it the way it had read everything else here, slowly and without assuming.

Every other cairn holds a shell from a different kind of creature: a cowrie, a conch, a limpet that is a cousin but not the same. This cairn is for the kind that is missing, and only one kind is missing from the census: the creature's own. Its kind are grazers of the in-between, holders-on, and the creature had lived among many of them once. It had climbed the cliffs for years and found cowries and clams and worm-tubes, but it never found one of its own kind's shells. It never would. The only such shell left on the mountain was the one it was carrying.

The census had a hole in it the exact shape of the one who made it.

The creature was the last, and it knew that. It had built a place for itself among the dead and left it empty, because when the time came there would be no one to put it there. The sea would not do it, and neither would the fish or the bird. Its shell would lie wherever it happened to stop, and in time the upward rain would lift it and carry it off with the grit and the glass flakes. The last voice of the reef would go uncounted.

This was the one thing on the shelf that the power could not bear to leave alone.

---

## What It Did

It did very little. That was the point of it.

**First, it kept company.** It stood in the doorway with the creature when the creature slept. It asked for nothing and changed nothing, and it did not hunt, because no one hunts in the doorway. The creature had never been seen before. No one had watched it comb the weed or cement the wall or apologize to the worms. Now someone had. The power did not know whether the creature could feel the difference. It suspected that on some level beneath the shell, it could.

**Second, it went up to where the water begins.** It went to the blue light behind the column, the place that shows itself only in the stillest moments. There it found the sea, or the part of the sea that had lost its footing, and it did not command it. It simply turned the sea's attention downward, to the shelf it kept falling on, to the cairns it had been hammering for longer than any shell had been there.

The sea had never read them. It had fallen on them and around them and through them, but it had never *looked*. So the power showed it: *These are the ones you knew. Someone kept them. And the one who kept them is the one you are looking for. Something from your reef is still alive. It is under you. It has been under you the whole time.*

Nothing dramatic followed. The mountain did not sink and the column did not close. The argument between rock and water is real, and it is not the power's to win. But afterward, anyone who knew the shelf well might have noticed some small things. The still moments lasted slightly longer. When the membrane-surface passed through the creature, it passed a little more slowly, the way you might run your hand over the head of someone you had been searching for. And the water fell around the cairns now, not on them. The shells stayed dry in their small pockets of calm, faces upward, where the sea could see them.

**Third, it told the creature that the census was already complete.** It did not use words. In one long stillness it let the creature *know*, the way animals know things, that every one of the thousands was remembered, every kind and every individual. None of them was lost. The power held them all.

The creature went on climbing anyway, of course. The power had expected that and was glad of it. The census had never been about completeness. It was about love, and love does not stop looking just because it has been told there is nothing left to find. But the power thought that the creature climbed a little differently after that. It climbed as someone looking, not as someone afraid of forgetting.

**Fourth, and last, it made a promise about the bare cairn.**

It did not fill it. That would have been a theft, or a death sentence. The power simply let the creature understand one thing, in the long stillness, beside the clean flat stone:

*When your time comes, however long from now, and it may be very long, since your kind are patient and this rock is warm, you will not be carried off by the rain. I will set your shell here, facing upward. The census will be finished by someone who knows what it was for.*

The creature stayed beside the bare cairn for a long time. Then it did something the power had not expected. It reached one arm up and rested its flattened pad on the clean stone, the way you might rest a hand on a door you know you will one day walk through, now that you know it will be opened for you.

Then it went back to the weed.

---

## After

The power did not leave, exactly. Things that have truly paid attention to a place never quite do. But after a while its presence thinned the way the still moments end, and the roar came back to fill the space.

The shelf is as it was. The sea falls, and it rises. The weed streams skyward and lies flat again, and the creature combs it. The fish rests in the doorway with its blue line glowing, and the bird hunts it in the long slow chase that may take years. The echo still arrives before the crash.

Later, someone standing in the doorway with their eyes adjusting to the green might count the cairns and get a different number than before.

There is one more now, set a little apart on the other side of the doorway. The creature built it. It is capped with a flat stone like the others, and there is no shell on it, because the one it was built for has no shell.

On top of it, where a shell would be, the water falls so gently it barely falls at all. There is a small still pocket of calm, about the size of a held breath, facing upward toward where the water comes from.

It is the creature's census of those who were here, and it has made room for one more.
