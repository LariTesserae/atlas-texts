# What Was Not Done

## Slowing down

It came the way the grazer walks.

This was the first difficulty and the first lesson. At its own pace the power could have crossed the cloud sea in less than a thought. But at that pace the Blanched Steps would have looked like a photograph: a white stillness, pipes that never ran, a car that never moved, a thicket of silver cutlery with nothing happening in it. At its native speed the valley was not visibly alive at all, and a power that cannot see life can do nothing for it but harm.

So it slowed. It took eleven years to come up through the lower slopes. It spent them learning to wait until the drip off one leaf reached the leaf below, and until a rosette on a cushion plant could be seen to have changed. By the time it stepped out of the grey wool into the white-out, it was moving at nearly the speed of the ore car. Only then did the valley begin to move for it. The generators turned like a slow breath. The fog nets inhaled. The blue corona along the cables came and went each dusk like a pulse at a wrist.

It stood in the light with its eyes shut, if it had eyes, and let the place assemble.

## What it could have done

A power like this is never short of possible gifts. It listed them, the way one lists the things one will not say to a grieving friend.

It could make the valley grow faster. A few centuries of patient accretion could be compressed into a season, and the second tower raised by morning.

It could shield the Steps. It could thicken the cloud bank into a wall no traveller would climb, so that nothing from below ever came up with a pick, a survey, or a plan.

It could give the valley a name, a history, a set of words for itself.

It could answer the thought in the cables. At dusk, when the current brightened, it could think something back, so that the valley would know it was not the only mind in the world.

It could find the people who had slowed into the current and draw them back out, re-speeded and whole, blinking in the hard light with their oil cans in their hands.

Every one of these was kind. Every one was also a hand pressing down on a seedling to help it grow. The power had been old for a long time, and it had learned that the most dangerous gifts are the ones that come from love and impatience together. So it set the list aside. It did not throw the list away. It set it aside, and it went to look at the three things that actually worried it.

## The fire

The first was the west.

The second tower stands at the edge of the smoking ground. The power went there and put its attention down through the column, the fresh white faces, and the eight plates with their thin film of beetles. It went down through the new andesite and the old, into the heat.

The fire was closer than the valley could know. In four hundred years, perhaps a little less, the western cracks would open. They would not open violently. It would be a slow grey tongue of new stone, moving at about the speed of the ore car, and it would take the second tower. It would take the capped pipe, the cleared circle, and the eight plates. It would take the valley's first deliberate act.

Closing the vent would have been easy. A small cooling far down, a pressure eased, and the tower would stand for ten thousand years.

The power stayed with the fire a long time before deciding.

This is what it saw. The heat that would take the tower is the same heat that lifts the shelf. The Steps rise because the fire pushes them up from underneath. Every clean white fracture face and every growth plate in the bone comes from that warmth. Cool the vent and the rising slows. Slow the rising and the valley stops growing up. It would have its second tower, and it would be finished.

It saw something else, too. The valley was reaching toward the fire. That was the whole point of the second tower. It had placed its one willed gesture at the edge of the warm ground, and it had done so on purpose. The power had no way of knowing whether the valley understood the risk. But it knew that the first thing a young creature chooses should not be quietly unchosen by something larger while it sleeps.

And the valley already had a tool for this. The sliding joints, extended collar by collar like growth rings, showed that the Steps knew how to live with ground that moves. If the tower was lost, the valley would feel it the way skin feels a burn. Then, very slowly, it would learn. The third tower would stand somewhere a little wiser.

So the power let the fire be. It found this harder than any of the gifts it had set aside.

## The people in the current

The second worry was the builders.

It went and stood beneath the pylon line at dusk, the way the grazer does. It lifted whatever in it corresponds to long thin ears and listened to the current.

It had half expected voices, and there were none. What it found was subtler: tendencies. There was a preference for straight lines in a landscape made of breaks. There was a fondness for a particular gauge of rail. There was a habit of counting, which ran along the cables like a hum under the valley's slower arithmetic of water and light. There was a faint warmth of satisfaction whenever a gutter ran clear. There was the ghost of someone who had liked the smell of rain on hot stone and had put it, somehow, in the shed.

The power was looking for one thing only: whether anyone was held here who did not wish to be. It listened a long time. It found no one. What remained of the builders was not enough of a self to be imprisoned. What had once been enough seemed to have chosen this, a long time ago. They had come up out of the grey, liked the quiet, and let themselves be tended. They had not been taken. They had been received.

Pulling them out would not have rescued anyone. It would have unmade something that was now part of how the valley thinks. You do not pull the calcium out of a bone to give it back to the sea.

So it let them be, and it noticed that it was glad.

## The one who was there now

The third worry was not in the list at all. The power found it by accident, on a ledge above the tower, among the moss.

There was a person in the valley. A human, current, alive. They had come up through the cloud bank some weeks before, alone, with a pack and a knife and a grief the power could see the way one sees weather. They had come to be somewhere quiet, and they had found it. The Steps were tending them, as the Steps tend everything. The buckle of their pack had gone dark and smooth. There was silver fur on their boots. Two beetles slept in the fold of their coat. Their watch had lost eleven days, and they had not noticed.

They were not unhappy. The valley was being very kind to them. It was taking whatever was iron in them and keeping it from rust, and whatever was soft in them and watering it. In another season they would have slowed past the point of noticing, and gone into the current with the builders, and been received.

Maybe that was right for them. The power did not know, and here was the difficulty. The builders had chosen. This person had not chosen yet, because they did not know there was anything to choose. The valley does not deceive, but it does not explain either. It simply tends, and it is very hard to refuse a kindness you cannot see being done to you.

The power did not carry them down. It did not warn them or appear to them. It did one small thing.

It made their watch tick.

It was once, and only loud enough to be heard. In a place where a shout dies in twenty yards and footsteps make no sound at all, the tick was like the ring of two shards knocked together: a bright, human, mechanical sound in a padded room. The person woke from a half-sleep on the moss, looked at the watch, and looked at it again. They saw the date and understood.

They sat very still for a long time, the way the grazer stands beneath the cables.

The power did not stay to see what they decided. Their deciding was the point, and a choice made with something huge watching you is not quite a free one. It knows only that some weeks later, a set of footprints went down into the cloud bank. It does not know whether they were that person's. At the cloud line, one polished beetle dropped from a zipper onto the wet rock. It turned around and began, very slowly, to walk back uphill toward the iron. It would take most of a year to get there.

## The loneliness

The last thing was not a worry. It was a temptation, and the power was honest enough to call it that.

Each dusk, the thought along the cables brightens. The power had learned to read it a little: water, light, growing, and a slow curiosity turned outward toward the west and the sun. And under all that, too deep for the valley itself to notice, was something the power recognized because it had once felt it too. There was no one else like it. There never had been. The valley was the only mind of its kind, thinking at its own pace, in a place where nothing listens the way it listens.

The power composed an answer. It took three years. It was one idea, shaped to travel along a cable at dusk: *You are not the only one.*

It did not send it.

This was its reasoning. The first thing a young mind learns about other minds shapes everything after. If the valley's first meeting were with something vast, old, and already watching, the valley might learn that the world is full of larger things looking down. It might learn to grow toward being seen, and away from its own light. It deserves to meet its equals first: something its own size, its own speed, as surprised as it is. It deserves to find the other the way seedlings find each other, root touching root in the dark.

Perhaps that was what the second tower was for. Perhaps the reaching west was not only toward the warmth. The power did not know, and chose to leave it unknown.

So it kept the answer. It is keeping it still, in case it is ever needed, the way the builders' pipe waits at the edge of the circle: capped, the length of an arm.

## What it did do

It stayed a while. There is nothing wrong with that. The valley tends its visitors, and the power let itself be tended. Beetles found whatever in it corresponds to iron and began to polish. A pale moth rose from its shoulder at dusk. The fog came up and it drank, like everything else.

It kept a record. This was the only lasting thing it gave, and the valley will never know of it. The Steps have no history except the grazer's incisor marks on the trestle. They have no memory the power could find of what came first, the garden or the plumbing. If the fire takes the second tower, or the shelf stops rising, or something from below comes up someday with a survey and a plan, the power will know that the Blanched Steps were here. It will know that they were young and alive and kind in their strange way, and that they reached toward the sun on purpose. Being remembered by something that asks nothing of you is a small gift. It may be the only gift a young thing can receive without being bent by it.

On its last morning the grazer came close. It stood a few yards off with its ears folded flat and looked at the power steadily with its large dark eyes. Then it turned back to its rosettes, as if it had recognized something in that face and found it not quite recent enough to matter.

The power was more pleased by this than by anything in a long time.

## Going

It went down the way it came, at the ore car's pace. The moss slowly filled its footprints with clear seep, and by the next white-out there was no sign it had been there. It made no mark on the trestle. The count stayed exactly as it was.

It left the valley with its fire, its dead and its living, its one visitor free to choose, its loneliness, and its reaching. It had changed nothing except one tick of one watch. It had learned something it had to learn again every few thousand years: the Steps already had their keepers. The rust had its beetles, the moss its pruner, the beetles their thinning bird, the count its clerk. What the valley lacked was not care. It was time, and time was the one thing it was already growing into.

It will come back. Not soon. At the valley's pace, which is to say in perhaps a thousand years, when there may be a third tower, or none, or something no one has imagined yet, rising out of new white stone toward the hard small sun.

It would like to see the second leaf, if the fire allows.

If the fire does not, it would like to see what the valley does next.
