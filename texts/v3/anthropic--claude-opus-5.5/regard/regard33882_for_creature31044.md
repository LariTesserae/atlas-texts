# The Attention

## It does not come down the shaft

A power like this does not need the stairs. It could be in the Hollow the way weather is in a valley, the way a thought is in a head. Before it arrives at all, it notices something it thinks about for a long time. Its attention has weight.

The Hollow is a place where everything registers. Grit migrates to the nodes. Water beads in the antinodes. A single visitor on the middle bridge became, for one listener, a new colour in the chord. Three thousand rooms were shaped with such care that the presence of one warm body changes the sound very slightly. A power's full regard, poured into the chamber all at once, would not be a small warm room. It would be a new wall. The chord would bend around it. The walls would spend a thousand cycles correcting for it. The crickets' roads might shift in a single night, and some of them would be stranded on stairs that were safe yesterday.

So the first thing it does is make itself small.

It thins its attention until it weighs no more than a drip snail, until it is roughly as loud as a moss cushion quivering on a drip-stone. Then it comes down the shaft after all, step by worn step, because that is how things enter here. Its feet, if it has feet, land in the shallow dishes. The water in them shivers and settles.

At the bottom, it stops. It does not go out onto the bridge. It stands where the passage opens and the dark gets bigger, and it listens.

## It listens for a long time

A visitor might stay a day, or a week. The power stays for a season. Then it stays for a year of the surface world, measured by the drift of the breath. Then it stays for longer.

It does this because it has learned something wise things eventually learn: understanding a place well enough to help it takes longer than wanting to help it. The Hollow was built by beings who sat with their hands in the walls for who knows how many lifetimes. The listeners learn it only by being carried through it, chord after chord, clinging blind to a parent's chest. The power decides that it has no right to act faster than the Hollow's own young are allowed to learn.

So it lets itself be carried. It does not ride a listener. It lets the chord pass through it cycle after cycle, the way the pups do, until the rooms stop being rooms and become tones. The doorways become shifts of colour. Each bridge becomes a thin bright line strung across the dark.

Somewhere in the second year it can hear the Hollow the way the listeners hear it. Somewhere in the fifth it can hear it the way the walls do. That is not a single chord. It is a continuous negotiation of three thousand voices, a vast patient argument between the pale grown material and the cave growing through it. It is conducted in warmth, and in the dissolving and permitting of calcite a molecule at a time.

Only then does it let itself make a list.

## What it considers

It is a benevolent power, and it has the reflexes of one. The Hollow offers many things a kind heart wants to fix.

**The crickets that die off the lines.** Some stray during the chord and are found stunned or dead on the stairs below. The power could widen the silences a little, or give them some small warning sense of the breath coming. It watches where the dead crickets go. The listeners take some. The rain washes others into the channels, and the snails graze their edges down to the mineral film. The rest are carried into the basin and down into the warm red mouth, and the breath comes back up. The dead crickets are part of what the Hollow eats. The crickets that live know the lines because the lines are the only safe roads. Walking them is the whole of their culture. The power leaves them their roads, and their danger.

**The listeners' suffering in the silence.** The power understands this one intimately now. After so long in the chord, it knows what the quiet sounds like to a tuned ear: the drip, the grit, the small meaningless grindings, like a room of detuned bells. It could give the listeners some relief. It could soften the silence for them, or fill it.

But it has watched what happens in the silences. The listeners drink there, from the condensation running down into the creases of their folded wings. They rest there, if enduring counts as rest. And in the silence, once a generation, one of them opens its ears in the dark and sings the single thin rising note for the dead. The walls answer that note with a band of warmth that climbs slowly upward and fades. The listeners' only voice exists because of the quiet they hate. The power does not want to purchase their comfort with their grief-song. Some pain is the price of a room large enough to mourn in.

**The oldest listener.** Its wings are ragged. One ear is torn and badly healed. It flies a map of a city that no longer exists and aims for doorways that were wider once. The power could heal the ear. It could correct the old one's inner map, bring it up to date, and tune its memory to the present pitch.

This is the one it thinks about longest.

It watches the young listener flying just ahead of the old one, close, on the same path. After years of listening, the power can hear what the visitor could not. The young one *is* making a sound. It is very faint, so faint the old one may be the only other creature that hears it. It is not a cry and not an echolocation pulse. It is a sort of hum, tuned exactly to the frequency the old one's broken ear still catches well. The young one is not only leading. It is *translating*. It is turning the new city into something the old ear can follow.

The power understands that this translation may be the only thing in the Hollow that looks like teaching running backward. The young carry the old the way the old once carried the young. If the power healed the ear and corrected the map, it would erase the one relationship in the Hollow where knowledge flows upstream. It would be kind to the old listener. It would be a theft from both.

So it leaves the ear torn.

**The flowstone.** It could clear the doorways and restore the old pitch. It could undo ages of accretion in an afternoon and give the Hollow back its original voice. By now it has heard the walls choosing. It knows the city is not losing but *following* something below that is growing and slowing and deepening. Restoring the old pitch would be like forcing a grown body back into its infant clothes. The power leaves the cave to grow in. The walls know which seams to let out.

**The sleeper.** The power could look.

It could turn its full attention, just once, down through the warm black opening, past the dull red glow, and *know*. What sleeps there, how large it has become, what it dreams, when it will wake, what the egg is an egg of. Everything in the Hollow points toward this knowledge. The rooms face it. The song is aimed at it.

The power does not look. It does not forbid itself. It simply understands something the builders must have understood. This is a place built entirely to let something sleep. Every structure in it serves that purpose. And there are things that are not improved by being looked at directly while they sleep. A power's full gaze is a kind of sound. It might be the first sound in the Hollow's whole existence that the cradle was not built to shape.

It lets the sleeper keep its privacy, the way you do not open the door on a sleeping child just to see.

## What it does

It does do something. Its heart, if it has one, would not let it come all this way and only refrain. Restraint is not the same as absence, and wisdom that never touches anything eventually becomes indistinguishable from not caring.

So it chooses a room.

It is a small room on the sixth terrace, one of three thousand, facing inward toward the void like all the others. It is half-swallowed by pale gold flowstone, so that its doorway peers out of a frozen waterfall. The power chose it because the crickets' lines do not cross it and the listeners' paths do not use it. It is the least necessary room in the city, if any room here can be called that.

It goes in. It sits on the floor, which is clean the way riverbeds are clean. In the back wall, near the floor, there is the whorled recess about the size of two cupped hands, the shell-shaped ear-shaped place where the builders once sat. The power places its hand in it.

It has come to answer a question it has been carrying for years without admitting it: *Is the wall lonely?*

The builders are gone. Maybe they left, maybe they went in. The wall has been tuning alone, without hands, for longer than anything else here has existed. It is attentive and correcting, a mind left keeping watch. The power is not sure a mind like that can be lonely. It is not sure it isn't simply lending its own feelings to stone. The only honest way to find out is to ask the way the builders asked, with a hand in the recess, through one full cycle.

The silence passes. The breath comes.

The chord rises around it, and the power does not try to tune. It does not correct anything. It only sits with its hand in the wall and lets itself be one more room.

Then something happens that it did not expect, though perhaps it should have.

The wall tunes *it*.

The power, even made small, is slightly out of true with the Hollow. It is a warm, unfamiliar presence in a room shaped for something else. The wall treats it exactly as it treats everything: as a part of the instrument that needs gentle correction. The warmth around the recess shifts. The trembling under its palm quickens and steadies and quickens again. It is making thousands of tiny decisions a second, and all of them are about the power. The wall adjusts the room around it until the power's presence stops bending the chord. It makes a place for the stranger, the way it makes a place for the growing sleeper and the thickening stone.

The benevolent power had come, as benevolent powers do, to care for something. It finds itself being cared for, precisely and without sentiment. It is being fitted into the song.

It stays for the whole chord. When the rain begins, it does not take its hand away. It stays through the next silence, and the next chord, and many more. It does not know if the wall is lonely. It thinks now that the question was the wrong shape. The wall has three thousand recesses and had, once, three thousand hands. Whatever it feels about their absence, it did not stop listening for them. The recess was still warm when the power reached into it. The wall still answers a hand the way it answers the listeners' death-note, with a band of warmth that climbs.

On the power's last cycle, a listener passes the doorway of the sixth-terrace room. It is the oldest one, with its torn ear, flying a little behind its translator, a little off the true path. It is aiming for a doorway that was wider once. It turns its head toward the room as it passes. For the first time in the power's long visit, the old listener's inner map and the real city agree about something. There is a warm presence in a room on the sixth terrace. In the old one's youth, perhaps, there were hands in all the walls. It is not wrong about this one.

The old listener passes on into the blue light. The power takes its hand out of the recess.

## What it takes with it

Before it leaves, the power lets itself think about the future. It does not look at the sleeper. It only follows the arithmetic of what it has heard.

The pitch is sinking. The breaths are lengthening. Given enough ages, the chord will sink below what the listeners can hear. It will fall away not in catastrophe but gently, into a register of pressure and nothing else. There will come a generation of pups carried through a city that has gone quiet to their ears. They will be pressed against their parents' chests and feel the Hollow, but not hear it.

Or the listeners will change. The power has noticed that the ear-petals of the young are a little broader than the old ones'. Each generation may be growing its ears toward a deepening song. That seems likely. Everything here follows everything else: crickets follow silences, lichen follows crickets, walls follow the sleeper, listeners follow walls. The Hollow is a chain of patient followers, each keeping pace with something slower and larger.

It is also possible that one day the sleeper wakes, and the egg is an egg, and the cradle breaks the way cradles do when they have finished. The power cannot prevent this and would not. A cradle that could never be outgrown would be a cage.

Whatever happens, one thing is true of the Hollow now. The only records of its old voice are the ghost-bands of dead lichen, the mismatched channels on the stairs, and the scarred old listener flying a higher city. All of them are inside it, and all of them will be overgrown.

So the power does the one thing it has decided is a gift and not an intrusion. It *remembers*.

It takes the chord with it, not a recording, which would be a kind of theft, but a memory. It remembers the pitch the Hollow sings at in this age, with its torn-eared elder and its broad-eared young. It remembers the exact shape of the lattice of blue light and the exact paths of the lichen roads. It remembers the warmth that climbed toward its palm on the sixth terrace. It holds this at the pitch it heard, unchanged, the way the old listener holds a city that has since moved. It has become a record outside the Hollow for a place that has only ever recorded itself.

If there is ever a day when the song has sunk past all hearing, or the sleeper has woken and gone, someone will still know what it sounded like. Someone will know that in this age three thousand rooms sang a lullaby to something warm and red and growing. Someone will know that a young listener hummed a private frequency so an old one could find its way, and that when a stranger put its hand in the wall, the wall did not reject it. The wall made room.

## Leaving

It goes back up the shaft the way it came, step by step, through the shallow dishes of water. Each one shivers as it passes and settles behind it.

It does nothing to the entrance. It does not hide the way in and does not open it wider. The Hollow has survived everything else. It survived the builders, their leaving, and the cave growing into its bones. It survived one visitor on the middle bridge, noticed as a new colour and then forgotten. It can survive being found. Anyone who comes down those stairs will arrive the only way the Hollow can be arrived at: in the dark, hearing it before seeing it, the air thickening like breath from a large animal. That is a good enough gate.

Behind it and below it, in the room on the sixth terrace, the recess stays warm a little longer than it needs to. The band of warmth creeps slowly upward along the curve and fades.

Then the breath comes up from the basin, even and delivered. The city tenses, and the crickets go still. Seven listeners open their ears, and the Hollow sings. It is very slightly lower than it did the day the power arrived. It is exactly in tune.
