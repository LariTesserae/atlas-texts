# What Came to the Rising Stand

## It was warm

The first thing it learned was that it was too warm.

It had not arrived the way weather arrives, or light, or anything the Stand would register. It came the way attention comes: all at once, total, the whole of it turned toward one place. Attention of that kind has a temperature. For one long moment the power hung in the wet grey air between two pillars, a hundred meters above the root-stone, and it was radiant with interest.

In the seam beside it, something pale and flat felt the gradient change.

The power noticed. It noticed the fluid in the creature's branching channels beginning to run faster, the whole thin body starting to reorganize its small economy around this new and extravagant source. It saw what would come next: every seam-creature within reach turning, over a thousand dawns, to face it. It saw the slow migration, and the dependence. It saw a population that had spent geological time becoming exactly the shape of the Stand's indifference beginning to become the shape of the power's regard instead. And it saw what would happen when that regard was withdrawn, as all regard eventually is.

So the first thing it did was cool itself. It brought itself down to the temperature of wet stone, and then of the air a hand's width from the bark. It made itself the same on every side, a thing with no gradient across it. It stopped being worth sliding toward.

This was harder than it sounds. It is not natural for a benevolent thing to make itself uninteresting. But the power had been old a long time, and it had learned this much: the first act of care in any place is to stop being the most important thing in it.

## It looked

Then it looked, for a long time.

It did not look from above. From above, the Stand is a dark pelt thrown over a continent, and a pelt tells you nothing about what lives in the fur. It went down instead, to the scale where the qualifiers live, and read them the way the qualifiers asked to be read. *Almost. Barely. Rare. Except perhaps.*

It watched a thousand dawns, and then ten thousand. It lay flat in a seam as thin as paper and felt the wood on either side differ by less than a tenth of a degree. It rode the breathing overlaps of the crown and saw the violet sheen shift across the leaf-plates in patterns that turned out not to be random. Crown-dwellers cluster where the violet is steadiest, and the violet is steadiest where the wind is gentlest, so the shimmer was a map of the continent's calm. It knelt at the rim in the raw gullies and held a thumbnail's worth of grey paste. Nothing else on the plateau was doing what that paste was doing, which was coming undone. It walked ahead of the growth front across old land where the grass was thin and the beetles went about their business under stones, and it listened to the squeal of the young stems advancing toward them a meter a week.

It went down to the dry channels at the bottom and hung among the tuned threads, and it listened.

It took the power a long time to see the place whole, because the place is not one thing. It is one enormous thing and a great many very small ones. The small ones live entirely in what the enormous one cannot help losing. Neither of them knows the other is there.

## Everything it could have done

A power like this can do almost anything. That is the difficulty. So it considered.

**It could stop the front.** It walked the edge of the old land and felt the grass. It thought of the lichens going into the chemistry, the grey stone dissolved and rebuilt as green-veined glass, every small life on the shore unmade and remade as the Stand. That is a real grief, and the power did not pretend otherwise.

But the front is not only where the old land dies. It is also where the old land gets in. Every seam in the interior was once a loose crack between young stems, and every seam-creature is descended from something that crawled into one. If the front stopped, the flood of refugees would stop with it. Whatever was already sealed inside would be the last of it. And advance is the Stand's only verb. To stop it would be to end the only thing it does. The Stand might be no one, or it might be someone. Either way, freezing it is not a kindness you can do without first knowing which.

**It could widen the seams.** Give them room. Let them move whenever they liked, not just once a clear dawn. But the seams are clearance. The pulsing pillars need exactly that much slop and no more, and they need it narrow. And the seam-creatures do not live in the space. They live in the difference between the walls. Widen the gap and the walls stop touching them. The gradient becomes air, and they starve in their new freedom like a fish given a larger desert.

**It could thin the leaves and let the light down.** But nothing below is hungry for light. The darkness at the bottom is no one's deprivation. The crown-dwellers live on precisely the violet the leaves miss. Make the leaves less perfect and the violet is no longer the only thing that slips past. Their one color drowns in all the others.

**It could give them voices.** It considered this longest, because it loves voices. But nothing in the Stand has anyone to speak to. The only audience for anything here is a web of threads in a dry channel that has spent ages learning to hear a roar with no speaker. A voice here would not be a gift. It would be a loneliness with a mouth.

**It could wake the Stand.** It could make the process into a mind, so that there would be someone to reason with, someone to ask for gentleness. But making a mind so that it can be negotiated with is a strange sort of benevolence. It is the imposition of a conversation on something that may not have wanted to be a speaker. And the power was not certain the Stand was not already someone. You do not wake a thing that may simply be thinking at a pace you have not learned to hear.

**It could take them somewhere better.** But every better place was a place they were not shaped for. They are pressed exactly to the size of where they are. That is not their prison. It is their body.

Each of these was a kindness to someone the power had imagined. None was a kindness to anyone who was actually there. The power set them all down. That, too, was harder than it sounds.

## What it heard at the bottom

It stayed with the listeners a long time, longer than it had stayed anywhere. It came to understand that they were right: the roar has weather, and the weather has fronts. It did not learn whether the Stand thinks. It learned something else, and it learned it slowly, the way the listeners learn anything. The weather was changing.

The Stand is rising, and as it rises it breathes out more. Every century the morning mist thickens and the afternoon lid of cloud lies a little heavier. Clear nights were rare when the power came, and they are becoming rarer. The power measured them the only way anyone could here, by counting cracks. Across the deep interior, the dawns that break the wood had once come perhaps one in fifteen. Now they came one in forty. The curve was slow and very steady, the way everything about the Stand is steady.

At the end of that curve was a continent with no clear dawns at all.

No clear dawns means no crack, and no crack means the seams never open their millimeter. The seam-creatures, who measure their lives in cracks, would never move again. Each would stay pressed where it was until it died there. Their lineages would stop meeting one another in the dark. The oldest refugees of the old land would end not by being eaten, but by being held perfectly still.

There was something else in the listening, something the power might have missed if it had been in a hurry. The dawn crack is not only a door for the seam-creatures. It is also how the Stand flexes. Every clear morning, the wood contracts and the clearances are shaken loose, reset, kept honest. Without that flexing the seams would slowly creep shut. The pulsing pillars would begin to bind against each other. The Stand, pursuing its law of *everything filled*, was thickening its own sky and thereby sealing its own tolerances. It was a machine that did not know it needed its own slop, slowly succeeding itself to death.

The Stand could not see this. The danger lived in its margins, and its margins are below the scale of its attention. The only ones who could have noticed were the creatures in the seams, and they had no way to say it.

So there was something worth doing after all. The whole of the power's patience had been spent learning the one place where helping the smallest thing and helping the largest thing were the same act.

## What it did

On some nights, not many and never on a schedule, the power thins the cloud.

It does not tear it. It does nothing a meteorologist could point to. It dries the air a little at the top of the lid and lets the heat of the crown slip upward into the dark. The sky above the Stand clears, and the stars are briefly visible over a continent that has almost forgotten them. The black leaves drink nothing, because there is nothing to drink. The temperature falls a few degrees.

At dawn, the wood cracks.

It sounds like artillery. Across whole valleys, the seams open their millimeter, and the pale flat things let go of both walls at once and slide. The Stand's clearances shake loose and reset. Its tolerances stay open for another season.

The power keeps the mornings irregular on purpose. A schedule is something to depend on, and something to evolve around. It is a new god to face. Random clear dawns are only weather, and weather is what everything here already knows how to live in. The power does not return the Stand to how it was, and it does not make it better. It holds one curve level, so that one in forty stays one in forty and does not become one in ten thousand, then never.

That is almost all it did.
