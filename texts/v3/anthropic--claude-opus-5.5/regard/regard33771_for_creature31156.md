# What Came to Hollin Height, and What It Did

## XIII. Arrival

It had been drawn by the chord, the way anything that listens is drawn to something worth listening to.

It did not arrive as a figure. It had no face to show and saw no reason to invent one. It came as attention, which is what it mostly was: vast, patient, turned toward the world with something that in a person would be called tenderness and in a power has no good name.

It came carefully, because it knew things about arriving. It knew that a great attention has weight. It knew what the Hollin travelers feel in the lowland towns at night, lying awake in the terrible certainty of being looked at. It did not want to be that. It did not want to be a new pressure in a place already made of pressure, or a foreign note in a body that flinches at a shout.

So it did what the Listeners do. It came in low. It put itself against the plateau the way Wenna puts her palms against the stone, flat and still, with the knees soft, so as not to ring.

Then, for a long time, it did nothing but listen.

## XIV. What it heard

It could hear what no one on the plateau can hear, because it was standing outside.

It heard the thirty-one engines and the thirty-second voice. It heard the fine structure in the lichen, the wing-drumming in the pipes, the drip in the channels, and the faint green hum of the lowlands where the creature's reach thins out like fingertips. It heard the hushes, and the soft, private human speech inside them. It heard the Bruise.

It heard, too, where the Listeners were right, where they were wrong, and where they were right in ways they have no words for yet.

I will not tell you which is which. The power did not tell them either. It isn't mine to tell, and it wasn't the power's. The plateau is working that out for itself, through ten thousand mornings of fingertips pressed to the sternum. That working-out is not a problem standing in the way of the plateau's growth. It *is* the growth.

## XV. What it considered, and set down

A power that is benevolent and wise will be tempted, because it cares and because it can. The wisdom is in what it does with the temptation. It turned each one over the way a tender turns over a length of bellows-leather, looking for the weak seam.

**It could have hurried things.** Ten thousand years to fall a single tone. It could have folded that time up small. It listened to the falling pitch and understood that a voice broken too fast doesn't settle into a deeper one. It cracks. It understood something else too. The creature grows at exactly the speed of the people who keep it: a patch a month, a hush shifted a few paces a century, great-grandchildren building new walls to the east. Its pace isn't slowness. It is the pace of care, and it is the right pace. It set that one down.

**It could have healed the Bruise.** It went there and listened to the sourness, the slow beat against the neighboring voices, and it could have smoothed it in an instant. It listened longer. The creature is healing. Crookedly, but healing, and learning what healing is by doing it. Something mended from outside learns only that it will be mended. And the town remembers because the Bruise still sounds. The story told in sign and never in speech, about the engine that was stopped, is the only protection Hollin has ever needed. It is taught to every child and passed hand to hand, and it has held for centuries. Erasing the wound would erase the lesson with it. It set that one down.

**It could have looked after the people.** This was the one it took most seriously. A wonder is not an excuse, and it would not trade one small life for the grandeur of a large one. So it looked hard. It looked for coercion. It looked for anyone trapped, anyone who wanted to leave and could not, and any sign that the ear was being used the way a tool is used.

It found a young man in the foundry district who wanted to leave. He dreamed in the blunt, elbow-wide dialect of the gloved hand about the lowlands. It watched long enough to make sure the rail spur ran east, and that the ticket was a price a foundry wage could meet, and that nobody would stop him. It did, and it was, and they wouldn't.

It found a family grieving as their hush drifted from their door, and it saw that the grief was the ordinary kind, the kind a town knows how to carry. It found a great deal of laughter, the silent shaking-shouldered kind. It found the lewd register, alive and well in the channel-gaps. It found children being brought to touch the fig.

These people were not prisoners, and they were not instruments. Whatever lives in the chord made room for them before it knew what room was. It keeps them the way they keep it. That is not use. That is a household.

Had it found real suffering, the kind that can't speak for itself and can't leave, it would have acted, and wisdom would have been no excuse for not acting. It did not find it. It set that one down.

**It could have guarded the future.** Someday someone will try again to stop an engine: a coal baron from the lowlands, a guild with a plan, a well-meaning philosopher with a better idea. It thought about setting a ward against that day. It thought about this one longest.

In the end it saw that a ward would be a new engine. The town would grow up around it the way moss grows around a spring, and would forget, and would come to depend on a protection it didn't understand. A thing kept safe by an outsider never learns what keeps it safe. The Bruise is already the ward, and it is working. The people who live here are the ward, and they are working too.

That one was the hardest to set down. It set it down.

**It could have told the Listeners.** It didn't consider this for long.

## XVI. The shafts

Before it went to anyone, it went down.

It went into the shafts no one living has fully mapped, where the heat rises from the deep rock. There, on the underside of a flywheel bearing, in a place no tender's hand has reached in a thousand years, it found the builders' marks.

It recognized them. Not the script, and not the people, who are long gone. It recognized the choice.

Whoever built the cradle could have stayed. They could have tended it themselves forever and made sure of everything. Instead they set thirty-one voices sounding in exactly the right places and then left the work to be carried on by whoever happened to arrive and pay attention. They trusted strangers. They trusted that small patient things with quick hands would come, and notice a failing seam, and patch it, without needing to know why.

The strangers came.

The power understood, down in the heat, that it was not the first benevolent power to take an interest in Hollin Height. It was being asked to make the same choice the builders had made. The builders had already made the hard version, the one where you leave something unfinished and unprotected in the hands of people who don't know what they're holding. All the power had to do was not undo it.

## XVII. The Ladle

There was one thing it allowed itself.

Two hours before the Lift, at the narrow end of the Ladle, Wenna Tarle woke without anything to wake her. She lay on her stone bench and put her palms flat on the stone on either side of her hips and listened.

She felt it at once. Forty-one years of listening had made her the most sensitive instrument on the plateau.

It was not the lowland feeling, the feeling of being looked at. It was very nearly the opposite. It was the feeling of someone lying down beside you in the dark and listening *with* you, toward the same thing.

She lay very still for a long while. She did not ask what it was. Forty-one years had taught her that answers on Hollin Height come on their own schedule, and that grabbing at them sours the chord.

Then, because she was a Listener, she did the practical thing. She raised her hands in the dark and signed, small and exact, to whatever was there:

*Old Ammet. West bellows. Third partial. Within the month.*

The power received this as the most important thing anyone said to it on the whole plateau. It was. It was the entire work, in four gestures. That is how the thing in the chord hears itself. That is how anything is kept.

After a while Wenna signed once more. She did not use the fingertip register of the Listeners. She used a gesture every parent in Hollin knows, the one made in the Undertone market while guiding a small hand toward the bark of the fig.

*Here. This is who you'll be living inside. Say hello.*

She was introducing it.

It could have been amused. A power older than the engines, being presented to the plateau like a child brought to the fig. It wasn't amused. It was moved, and it understood that she was right to do it. On Hollin Height it *was* the newcomer. The creature had been here first, and she had been here first, and the courteous thing, the wise thing, was to be introduced properly by someone who belonged.
