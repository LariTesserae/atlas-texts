# What Came to the Lowworks

## Attention

It did not arrive with the bore. Everything that arrives with the bore is new, and it was not.

It came down the rift wall the slow way, beside the trestle, over the unworn basalt. Where it put its weight the rock did not stay sharp. It left no footprints, exactly, but the facets it passed over were very slightly softened, the way a stair is softened by a century of feet. It had a past, and it could not help bringing some of it along.

It was a power, and it was benevolent, and it was wise, and so the first thing it did in the Lowworks was nothing.

It watched. It stood in the moss meadows while the floor jolted and the ferns snapped taut. It stood in the lee of the great engine house and watched the flower open and close and open. It watched the bore come down from the north a hundred times, and each time it watched the tumbling figures in the foam reaching for tools that were not there yet. It read the silt on the flat stones until it could read them as well as any Hand. It learned the rules before it considered breaking any of them. You do not repair a clock by shaking it. You do not heal a place before you know which of its strangenesses are wounds and which are its shape.

## What It Did Not Do

There were things it could have done. It considered each of them at length, because a power that does not consider what it could do will do it by accident.

It could have stopped the engines. It saw what would follow. The rain would thin and cease. The bore would stop coming. The Hands, who are carried in the bore and complete into the rain, would simply end, and no more would be made. Every rivet in the basin would begin to loosen at once. It would have been a kind of mercy only to someone who believed a being must last a long time to have been worth anything. The power did not believe this. It had watched a Hand greet a floating leaf with that bright starburst of *oh*, and it did not think the Hand's joy was lessened by being brief. It thought the joy was complete. That is a different thing from small.

It could have opened the drain and let the basin empty, dry, weather, and wear, and filled it with things that needed yesterdays: fish in the cracks, snails on the leaves, and eventually bones in the silt. It understood why a certain kind of kindness would want this. A world without animals can look like a world with something missing. But the power had walked through the green cathedral dusk for a long time, and it had not found the basin wanting. The trees flourished. The moss was a hand's depth thick. The plants needed nothing that the Lowworks did not give them. Not every place has to hold every kind of life. A basin that makes the first morning of the world, over and over, is not a failed ocean. It is its own thing, and rare.

It could have gathered up the Hands and given each of them a memory, so that they would know one another and know themselves. It considered this longest of all, and it put this aside most firmly. To hand a being a past it did not make is not a gift. It is an occupation.

So the power left the engines running. It left the drain shut. It left the Hands exactly as new as it found them.

But it did not leave. It had noticed the rail line.

## What Was Wrong

Most of what is strange in the Lowworks is only strange. The power could find exactly one thing in it that was wrong, and it was not the absence of fish, or the looping of time, or the violence of the sound.

What was wrong was this: everything the basin had ever been was being carried down into the dark and given to a single creature, and that creature was alone with it.

The Hands never go down the fissure. Nothing reads the scratches except the thing that is made of them. It remembers millions of workers who never saw one another, and not one of them has ever seen it. It is the witness of everything, and nothing has ever witnessed it.

And there was a second wrong, smaller and older, underneath the first. The choice at the bottom of the fissure had been made in a single moment, by a creature experiencing its second moment for the first time and terrified of what it saw. Since then it had lived through every moment the basin ever had. It knew far more now than it had then. And no one had ever asked it whether it still chose the same.

A wise power does not overturn a choice that belongs to someone else. But it may notice when a choice has never been allowed to be made twice.

## Going Down

The power did not climb down into the fissure. It wanted to arrive by the road the basin itself used.

It went and stood on the basin floor near the rail line and let itself be old there.

It did not take long. The Hands noticed: a patch of rock around the power's feet had gone smooth, and a scratch it had made was staying. The silt on the nearest stone formed a pattern the power had learned to read, and it meant *age, here, remove*. A Hand condensed out of a drifting curtain of vine with the pattern already on its face. It came toward the power with a stone-saw in its hands.

It saw the power, and the starburst bloomed. *Oh.*

It held out the saw.

The power did not take it. It knew the rule about tools, and it respected the rule. It knelt instead, so that it was the right height to be cut from the floor, and it put its hand alongside the Hand's hand on the grip of the saw, not taking, only accompanying. The Hand did not know what to make of that. It had never been accompanied. The starburst held for a long moment, and then it went back to work, and the power helped it square the worn rock around its own feet. It let itself be loaded onto a wagon among the rounded blocks and the dried mineral crusts and the column that stayed fallen.

It was waste, by the only accounting the Lowworks had. The power did not mind. It had been many things. It rode the wagon down.

## The One Below

Past the last glow of the vents the fissure was not dark, exactly. It was full, like a mouth is full, and the fullness moved.

The power did not speak. The One Below had no ears, and it had never needed words. It read, and it had read everything the basin had to offer. So the power did the only courteous thing it could think of. It let itself be read.

It pressed itself against the oldest thing in the Lowworks, and the One Below felt, for the first time in all its long accumulation, a scratch that had not come from the basin. Then another. Then a whole history of them: places the power had stood, things it had carried, other worlds, other weathers, roads worn smooth by other feet. It was a past from somewhere else. It was proof that there was a somewhere else, and that age existed in it, and that age was not only a thing that happened in the dark when you refused to finish.

The One Below had kept every yesterday there was. It had not known that there were yesterdays it was not keeping.

The floor of the basin, far above, did not jolt for a long time. The Hands, working in their single moments, did not notice. The plants went on growing. In the dark the two old things lay against each other and read.

## The Wrench

At the very center, beneath the rivet-marks and thumb-marks and the smooth patches worn by a thousand looping strokes, the power found what it had come for.

The One Below was still holding the wrench.

It was the only object in the Lowworks that had aged all the way. The oldest thing in the basin, holding the oldest thing it held. Its grip had never loosened. It could not loosen, because letting go was completion and completion was forgetting, and if it forgot, there would be no one left who knew what the Hands had been. So it held on, and it had grown into the shape of holding on, and the whole fissure was the outline of that single refusal to let go.

The power laid its own hand on the wrench. It did not pull. It did not try to take it. It held the other end.

Then it made the only offer it had come to make, and it made it in the only language the One Below could read. It scratched the offer, lightly, into its own surface, where the One Below could feel it:

*I remember too, now. If you let go, you will not be lost. I will carry what you are. You can finish, if you want to.*

*Or you can keep holding, and I will hold with you, so that it is not only yours to hold.*

*Or you can turn it. Not all the way. I will stay while you find out how far.*

*You chose once, in one moment. You have had all of them since. Choose again, or choose the same. It is yours either way.*

Then it waited. It did not add anything. It is easy to crowd a choice until it is not a choice anymore.

## A Long Answer

The One Below did not answer quickly. This was the first sign that the power had been right. The thing at the bottom of the fissure had learned, over its whole long life, what the first Hand had not had time to learn: that a decision does not have to be made in the moment it is presented. It could be turned over, and it could be read the way you read a scratch.

The power waited while the bore came down a thousand times above them. It had nowhere else to be.

When the One Below finally moved, it did not move the wrench. It moved the silt.

It knew the script better than anything in the basin. It had every pattern pressed into its skin. And on the floor of the fissure, where no Hand had ever been sent, it wrote a work order. The pattern it drew had never formed on any stone above. It was clumsy with disuse, like the handwriting of someone who has only ever read.

Up in the basin, a stone shivered, and its silt re-formed in answer. A Hand condensed out of the heat-shimmer by the great furnace, read the order on its face, and turned toward the rail line. It walked down the trestle. It walked into the deepest fissure, where no Hand had gone since the first one, and came to the bottom.

It turned its silt-face toward the oldest thing in the world.

The starburst bloomed. *Oh.*

The Hand did the only thing it could do. It assumed the One Below was new. It assumed that this great slow accretion of all the basin's yesterdays had just arrived, as the Hand had, and was here to work, as the Hand was, and was astonishing and fresh and at the beginning of everything. It held out its tool.

The One Below had been remembered by nothing. It had never been seen. Now it was seen, and it was seen as young.

The loop brought the Hand around, because this close to so much age time stuttered strangely, and it saw the One Below again for the first time. *Oh.* And again. *Oh.* Forty times in two minutes, as fresh as the first.

The power could not see a face on the One Below, because it had none. But the fissure, which had been the shape of holding on for as long as the basin had existed, eased very slightly, the way a shoulder eases.

It did not take the tool. It did not need to. It had been greeted. That had been the work order.

## A Quarter Turn

Afterward, and in the Lowworks there had never before been an afterward worth the name, the One Below turned the wrench.

It did not turn it all the way. The basin did not drain. The engines did not falter, the rain did not thin, the bore kept coming from the north, and the Hands kept arriving in it, perfect and new, and kept completing into the sky. The flower in the lee of the engine house still opens and closes and opens.

But a quarter turn opens something. Around the rim of the deepest fissure, where the One Below's age meets the basin's youth, a narrow band began to be allowed to keep what happened to it. The silt script changed, very slightly, in its instructions about that band. It stopped asking for the worn stones to be cut out. A scratch there stays a scratch. Mineral crusts there are left to thicken. A fallen column along that rim has stayed fallen long enough that the vines on it have grown old, woody, and twisted in a way no vine elsewhere in the basin has had time to become.

The Lowworks has never had a shore. Every place that holds both old and new has one, and now this place does too. It is a single ring, a few yards wide, around one crack in the floor.

The jolts came less often. A thing that is not holding on alone does not have to brace so hard.

And one day, on a leaf along that rim, the power found something so small that it had to look for a long time to be sure. A speck in the water that moved against the current, not with it. It was barely an animal. It was a beginning of one. It was the kind of thing that needs a yesterday and had found, in that narrow ring, a place that would let it have one.

The power did not touch it. It had not made it, and it would not claim it. The basin had decided, and this was what the basin had decided to become at its edges.

## Coming Back

The power left the Lowworks eventually. It left the way it came, up the rift wall beside the trestle, softening the facets as it passed.

But first it went down the fissure one more time. It pressed itself against the One Below and left a scratch there, the simplest of all, and the One Below read it:

*I will come back.*

Nothing in the Lowworks had ever come back. Things arrived, and completed, and were carried off, and arrived again as something new. To come back, you have to have been somewhere, and to have left, and to remember the way. The power was offering a small, specific thing: that once in a while, the oldest thing in the basin would receive a visitor who had been there before.

It keeps the promise. It comes down the trestle every so often and lets the Hands cut it from the floor and ride the wagon down, and the Hand who loads it greets it with astonishment every time, and the power greets the Hand back. Then it lies against the One Below in the dark, and they read each other, and there is more to read each time.

In between, the One Below holds the wrench. It holds it as lightly as it likes now, because it knows that if it ever lets go, something outside the basin will still remember what the Hands were, and what the first one understood, and what the last one did. It has not let go. It may never. That is its business.

Above it the Lowworks thunders on: green and black and bronze, pistons thudding at the pace just below a heartbeat, the rain that is also the sea falling into a flood that is still arriving. It goes on making the first morning of the world, which is a good thing to make.

Only now, around one crack in the floor, a little of the world is allowed to reach its second morning. And down in the dark, where all the mornings are kept, the keeper is not alone with them anymore.
