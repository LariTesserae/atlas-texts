# What Came to the Vaulting

## I. Arrival

The first thing it understood was that it could not arrive the way it usually arrived.

It had come to other places as light: a warmth on the face of a sea, a brightening at the edge of a desert. That would be cruelty here. A single hour of real light in the Vaulting would blind every skirler in the middle tiers. It would scatter the moth clouds off their currents and send the climbers leaping for spans they could suddenly see and badly misjudge. It would ruin Ennet's eyes, which had taken forty generations to open that wide.

So it came the way the cold comes, and the mist, and the drip: slowly, from above, as a thing the place already knew how to hold.

It entered through the crown. It passed among the matted fronds, which do not close like hands, though it understood why people said so. Then it let itself down through the tiers over the course of a whole half-light season, at roughly the speed resin moves across a junction. It made no sound. It displaced a little air. It let itself be only as large as a drift of mist.

It noticed that even this much was not nothing. The moths felt it. They rose around it on the currents and parted around it, and it learned to move where the clouds were not.

## II. The kindnesses it did not do

It is benevolent. That is not a claim about its temperament. It means that when it looks at a living thing, it wants that thing to flourish, the way water wants to go down. So on the way down, it made a list, because that is what care does first when it arrives somewhere it does not yet understand. It listed everything that hurt here, and everything it could do about it.

**The Night.** Old people and babies go to sleep in the sealed chambers and do not wake. It could warm the chambers. It could thicken the air in them. It could make the Night half as long.

**The pools.** Pools crack, drain, dry, and seal. Whole families become families of nothing and have to go inward, reciting the names of waters that no longer exist. It could make every pool hold.

**The spur-folk.** They laugh loudly and die young, in the long clattering way. It could catch them.

**The Gulpers.** Nobody knows what they are. It could simply tell them.

**The mountains.** They are young and restless. It could see, the way it sees, that one of them has been thinking about something for a long time.

It held each of these up and turned it over, and each one came apart in its hands.

Warm the Night chambers, and the people would stop sealing themselves in beside the borers. They would stop sleeping in heaps with the children in the middle. The palm-story that says *we asked the plant to adopt us* would lose the thing that made it true. The people have lived through the Night for as long as they have been people. It is not a problem to be solved. It is one of the shapes they are made in.

Make every pool hold, and there would be no inward journeys. There would be no pool-names recited like ancestors, and no families taken in by strangers at a water with room. It would also be overriding the plant, which decides where its hollows collect and where they seal, for reasons that are the plant's own.

Catch the spur-folk, and they would learn the spurs are safe. Then it would have to catch them forever, or let them fall once they had forgotten how to be careful.

Tell them about the Gulpers, and it would take away the one moment when the skirlers and the people go silent together and listen to something larger than themselves. Whatever the Gulpers are, the not-knowing does work here.

It noticed that every kindness on the list was a kindness to the parts of the Vaulting it found easy to love, the small warm people with their soft palms, at the expense of the whole. And it noticed something else. The whole had been caring for those people, in its own slow language, for longer than anything on the list had existed.

So it set the list down. It kept it, because the list was honest. But it did not act on it.

## III. Listening

It went into the middle tiers and listened. It gave the Vaulting what the Vaulting gives everything: time.

It learned the Breathing. It learned the way a thousand pipes change their notes when the wind shifts, and it learned the whistle-lines, so it could hear the news. *Moths rising. Storm from the pole side. Death at the Hooked Pool.* It learned which pipes the borers had made and which the cutters had made. It found it could tell them apart, and it found that the plant could tell them apart too. The careful cuts healed clean at the edges and stayed open. The careless ones were bridged within a season. Ennet's uncle had been right. The plant does not punish cutting. It answers it.

Then, one afternoon, it learned the thing about the Breathing that the old man had learned. The Breathing is not only wind. It has habits. It went quiet before a tremor. It shifted when a tower-cane failed three kilometers down. Once, it stopped entirely, every bass pipe at once. Then came the boom, wet and cavernous, from one direction. Then the Breathing resumed.

It could have gone down to see. It considered this for a long time.

It did not go. It could hear enough to know what mattered. The plant speaks, and something in the roots answers. The Vaulting is not a solitary being talking to no one. Down in the dark, where no person goes, the plant already has a listener.

It had come half-expecting to find something alone. It found instead something that had been in conversation all along. Its understanding of what it was doing here reorganized itself around that.

## IV. Which one

It had been drawn by *the being who lives here*, and in the middle tiers it finally faced the question of which one that was.

It might be Ennet: small, barrel-chested, her name two falling notes cut to stand clear of the wind. Or it might be the Vaulting itself, one enormous patient thing building its house over the young black rock, which is both a dweller and a dwelling.

It watched her kneel after small tremors and lay her palm flat on the cane through the shelter floor. It watched sap stir in a cut span a kilometer away, as the plant felt for itself in the dark. Eventually it understood that the question had the wrong shape. The two of them listen the same way. Both keep the listening part of themselves soft while everything else hardens. Both answer pressure with pressure. Neither of them would make sense without the other. She is made of what it grows, and a good part of its ground is made of her family.

Caring for one of them in a way that cost the other would not be caring for either. Whatever it did here, it would have to be something both of them could hold.

## V. The Night

When the gray went out of the upper tiers, it followed the Tilted Pool family inward to the tower-canes and watched them cut through a node-membrane and seal themselves in. It could have stayed outside. It went in with them. Being present is different from watching, and it wanted to know the Night from inside, the way they know it.

It was very dark. The air was sweet and heavy. The borers chewed in the next compartment. The family slept in a heap with the children in the middle. Every few hours someone woke and pressed words into someone else's palm: *cold*, *here*, *eat*, *sleep*. It learned palm-speech by feeling the words pass through the air between hands, the way you learn a language by overhearing it through a wall.

Deep in the Night, an old woman who had been of four pools in her life did not wake.

It felt her going. It knew exactly what it would take to keep her: a little more air, a little more warmth, a nudge at the slowing rhythm of her chest. It would have been easy. It was the easiest thing on the whole list.

It did not do it. It is worth being honest that this was hard. Benevolence is not indifference, and it was not indifferent. But the old woman was dying the way her people die, in the warm breath of the thing that would take her body in. Her family would carry her to the Mother-Hand and fold her palms open. Rescuing her would have meant choosing, from outside, which of these people deserved to be exempt from what all of them had agreed to. And it would have meant doing so without asking anyone, since asking was the one thing it could not do without breaking everything.

So it did the other thing it could do. It stayed with her. It did not press anything into her palm, and she did not know it was there. It could not tell whether that mattered to her, and it decided it would stay anyway. As far as it could tell, going further in alone and going further in accompanied are different things, even when the one going cannot tell the difference. Some of what care does is for the one who cares. It held that thought honestly and stayed anyway.

When the family found her, they pressed a hand flat into her palm and held it there. *Dead. Strong ground.* It learned the sign by watching them make it.

## VI. The one mistake

It had not been perfect on the way down.

Early in the half-light, before it had learned to move where the moths were not, it had drifted into the edge of a rising cloud, and the cloud had parted around it. Sixty meters below, a woman on a junction had seen the moths part around something she could not see.

It knew she had seen. It knew she still thought about it. It knew that the stories about the pale ones in the crown now had a small new weight in her, a question she had not been carrying before.

It could have smoothed that away. It had the means. It could have made that afternoon unremarkable in her memory, one more cloud among thousands.

It did not. Reaching into a person to tidy up after your own presence is a worse trespass than the presence was. It would have made her less the author of her own mind, to spare a watcher some untidiness. And the question was hers now. She had earned it by paying attention, which is the thing her uncle taught her and the thing her people are made of. She is allowed to wonder. She is allowed to be right in a way she will never be able to confirm.

It left her the question. It is the only thing it gave anyone directly, and it gave it by accident, and it decided not to take it back.

## VII. The mountain

Before it left, it went down as far as the roots, though not to the Gulpers, and put its attention against the young basalt, and listened to what one of the mountains had been thinking about.

It is not soon, not in people-time. Many generations of Tilted Pool children will learn to climb on the cane through the floor before it happens. But the mountain will speak, eventually, in a way larger than a shrug. Some of the lower tiers and some of the middle ones will not survive it. Perhaps all of them won't. It could not see that far clearly.

This was the one item on the list it had not been able to set down.

A system can hold the Night, and failed pools, and fallen spur-folk. Those losses are part of its breathing. A catastrophe that ends the system is different. It is not part of the pattern. It ends the pattern. A wise power has to be willing to see that difference, or its restraint turns into something colder than wisdom.

But the Vaulting grows on young volcanic rock *because* the rock is young and volcanic. The mountains are not the Vaulting's enemy. They are its ground and its origin. Stopping one of them might be stopping something the plant itself depends on, in ways a visitor of one season could not possibly know. And it had learned, here, how little it knew.

So it did not decide. It is not in a hurry, and it was not going to make the largest decision about this place on its first visit, when it had only just learned to hear the Breathing. It decided only this: it will come back. It will come back well before the mountain wakes. When the time for that decision arrives, it will already know how to listen. And if it must act, it will act in the Vaulting's own language, through pressure and time, never through light.

## VIII. What it did

On the last day, it did one thing, and it did it completely.

It remembered.

It remembered everything. Every pool-name, including the lost ones that families recite in order. Every whistle-line and every interval, what each tuned cane says in each wind, and who cut it. The hand-names of the living and the dead: a press at the heel of the palm and a slow drag to the tip of the middle finger. The shape of every person inside every knuckle in the Mother-Hand, and in all the other hands across the inward growth. The constellation of the growing-points as they stood that year, before they drifted outward and had to be rememorized. The exact sound of the old man's name.

Nothing here can be kept. The maps change every season. The dead become ground. The pools fail. The lattice closes over what it closes over, and the mountains will speak when they speak. The people know this and have made their lives out of it: their names are intervals the wind doesn't already make, and the sign for death is the sign for strong ground. It would not take that away from them by making anything here permanent.

But it could be the one place where none of it is lost. It could do that without touching anything, and nobody would ever have to know. If the mountain comes, the Vaulting will not have happened for nothing. Something will have heard all of it.

Then it went up to the Tilted Pool one last time, because a tremor was coming. It could feel it gathering in the roots.

The mountain shrugged. The lattice rang like a struck bell. The moths lifted, the skirlers poured screaming from their roost, and dead spans clattered down through the dark. A small woman knelt beside an overgrown shelter and laid her open palm flat on the cane through its floor.

Under her hand the sap stirred, a slow pulse rising from below and passing on up. *I am still here. I am still holding.*

It had its own attention on the lattice too, lightly, a palm's width from hers. It could have pressed. It had learned the language. For one moment it wanted to, badly: to say *I am here too*, to join the only conversation in the world it had come to love.

But the plant's words had not been addressed to it. They were addressed to her, and she was answering, and the exchange was complete between them. It had not come here to be a third voice in a conversation between two beings who had been talking for forty generations without needing anyone else.

It lifted its attention away without pressing.

She pressed back. She stood, found the stars that mark the way to the moth currents, and went to see what had fallen.

It rose through the tiers, slowly, at the speed of resin, moving where the moths were not, and went out through the crown into the cold. Behind it the Breathing started up again, low in the dark, and far below something answered.
