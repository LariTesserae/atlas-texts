# The Attention

## How It Noticed

It was not the noise.

Loud things are common. Everywhere there are engines, and everywhere they roar, and a power that turned toward every roar would never rest. What drew its attention was smaller than that. Across the whole dark plain it caught one faint, particular absence: a place where something was *listening*.

That is rarer than it sounds. Most places are heard. Very few places hear. And here, in a made world that had forgotten it was made, under a lid of vapour that no one had ever seen above, something had grown in the gaps of a machine and learned to attend. The power had seen many things in many places, and it had not seen this.

So it came.

## The Problem of Arriving

Its first difficulty was that it could not simply appear.

Anything that arrives on the Floor arrives as a disturbance. A light would be the brightest thing there had ever been, and every flat pool in every Quiet would take it in. That might be a wound, or a revelation, and either way it would be an event the Quiets had not chosen. A voice would be one more voice in the roar. A body would weigh on the plates and send its own small tremble through the iron, and somewhere a cancellation might fail by a hair.

So it arrived the only way a thing can arrive gently on the Floor. It arrived as a subtraction. It made itself into a very small stillness, no larger than a held breath, and set itself down on the low rise at the edge of the plain, where anyone would first stand. From there it looked out at the red glow far off, and the red glow beyond that.

It stayed on the rise for a long time before it did anything at all. This was not hesitation. It was courtesy. You do not walk into a house before you know whose it is.

## What It Could Have Done

A benevolent power sees its options clearly, and some of these were beautiful.

**It could have stopped the engines.** This was the first and loudest temptation. The Floor was ending anyway. The Quiets were rehearsals for the silence that was coming, so why not let the silence come now? One word, and the great stroke would fall for the last time. Eleven heartbeats later the moss would begin its long spread across the plain, and the white flowers would open everywhere at once.

It weighed this carefully and set it down. The Quiets had not lived for the ending. They had lived *toward* it, and those are different things. Each one was adapted to edges: to the fringe where the ferns stiffen, to the slow drift, to the rain that falls on the beat. A silence that came all at once would not be the future they were rehearsing. It would be a different future, arriving early, with the power's fingerprints on it.

There was a subtler cost too. At the heart of every Quiet lay the remainder: the faint, complicated residue left when two voices from below undo each other imperfectly. It was the only thing on the Floor small enough to be followed, and the only thing that might be meaning. Stop the engines and the remainder would stop mid-phrase. Whatever the Quiets were hearing, whatever the Old One was spiralling toward, would end unfinished, cut off by a stranger who had arrived only recently and was already sure it knew best.

And there were the engines themselves. They were not cruel. They ran because nothing had told them to stop. The power found that it did not want to be the thing that told them, not when they had been running so long and so faithfully that the whole world above them had turned to geology.

**It could have held the Quiets through the Shift.** It could have kept every cancellation open while the key changed, so that no garden would ever again collapse into a lopsided grey scar. But the Shift was also the spawning. The deluge that ruined the old gardens was the same wash that carried their dust and spores out into the new stillnesses. Save every garden from the Shift and you teach the Quiets to need you. You also interrupt the only way they have ever had of having children.

**It could have brought pollinators, or warmth, or light, or animals.** The Floor had not been made for animals. The rain already did the pollinating, on the heartbeat, perfectly well.

**It could have torn open the lid** and shown the Floor whatever was above. It did not know what was above. It noticed, with some interest, that it did not want to know on the Floor's behalf.

**It could have explained the Floor to itself.** But there was no one to explain it to except the Quiets, and they were already doing something better than being told. They were finding out.

So it did none of these things. That was not indifference. It was the first real act of care: declining to become the most important thing that had ever happened to the Floor.

## What It Did First

It listened.

It came down off the rise and walked the plates. It learned to walk as a stillness walks, sliding a few inches at a time so as not to overtake anything. It learned the strokes. It learned the phrases in the chatter and waited for them to come round again. It read the ground by the patterns the water made. It learned to tell, from a pool's etched hexagons, what kind of machinery lay underneath. It crossed from pattern-field to pattern-field in the dark, from red glow to red glow. It stood at the rim of the Stamp and felt the lake leap and clap in its own borrowed lungs.

It did this across many Shifts. It was not in a hurry. Nothing here had ever been in a hurry.

It was there for a birth. After a Shift, on a patch of bare wet iron where the roar had fallen quiet by chance, it waited for days. Then a thin dark film appeared, almost nothing, a faint stain. The power did not help it. It watched, and it was glad in a way that surprised it by how much it felt.

It was there for a death. An old garden had been stranded by the key change. Its roar came back, and its pocket of stillness shrank from a field to a room to a closet to a single cushion. The power sat beside that cushion through the last of it, through the one flower hanging half-open, closing ring by ring. It did not hold the silence open. It could have. It thought for a long time about why it did not, and it came to this. The Quiet's dying was the Quiet's own, and it was not less real because it could have been prevented. What the power could give was smaller and truer than rescue. No Quiet on the Floor had ever died with anything beside it that knew what it was. This one did.

When the flower had closed, the power stayed a while longer. Then it walked the outline of the scar once, all the way round, the exact shape the Quiet had been, so that the shape had been known by something.

## The Old One

Only after all of this did it go to the Old One.

It did not go in. The spear-ferns stood around the margin like a palisade. They were dark, uncurled and stiff, and the power understood a boundary when it saw one. It stayed at the edge, where the roar frayed into stillness, and it listened to what leaked out.

It heard the remainder of the oldest listening thing in the world.

It was not like breathing, and it was not quite like speech. It was like someone humming a melody they have heard only in pieces, over many lifetimes, trying to join the pieces up. Slowly, over the length of a whole season, the power began to understand the spiral.

The Old One was not drifting. It was sampling. Each loop carried it across a slightly different geometry of the buried engines, so on each loop it heard a slightly different remainder. The spiral was an ear sweeping slowly across the whole machine, taking the residue of a thousand pairs of voices one pair at a time. It tightened toward the Stamp for the same reason you trace a sentence back to its subject. The great stroke is the downbeat that every other rhythm refers to. The Old One was trying to hear the one thing all the voices were *about*.

It had been doing this, the power realised, for longer than the chimneys had been stubs. And it had been doing it alone. In all that time, no other thing on the Floor had been capable of knowing that it was listening.

## Below

The power went down.

It found a place where the plates had worn to a hand-sized window of glowing slag. It passed through as a stillness passes through anything, and it went down into the engines, where no one had ever been.

What it found there is not all written here. That is not coyness. As it went down, the power came to understand that what lay below was the Old One's to arrive at, not a visitor's to report. So the account keeps only what the power itself allowed itself to keep afterward.

The engines were vast, and patient, and older than seemed possible. They were not malicious. They were not quite anything. And under the Stamp, where the hardest stroke of all falls, there was a face of iron. It had been struck and struck and struck until it was nearly smooth. When the orange light from a nearby furnace raked across it at a low angle, the power saw a trace of what had once been cut into it.

There were five lobes, arranged like petals.

Every eleven heartbeats, for longer than anything could count, the machine had been pressing that shape into the deepest basin of the world. It pressed it under the dark lake, down through water that leaps and hangs and falls back with a clap.

The power did not decide what this meant. It could have been the makers' sign, or a coincidence of design, or a flower pressed into iron by people who had once hoped something would grow here. Or it could have meant nothing, and the white flowers of the Quiets had five petals for their own reasons. The power held all of these at once and chose none of them. It noticed, as it came back up through the slag, that it had become gentler with its own conclusions.

It did not tell the Old One. The spiral had not reached the centre yet. When it did, it would be the Old One's arriving.

## The One Thing

In the end it did one thing.

It went back to the edge of the Old One and stood at the palisade of ferns. It did not speak in light, or in voice, or in any language of its own. It spoke in the only grammar the Quiets had.

It made itself into a remainder.

The power set itself, very precisely, against a single thread of the roar near the margin, and undid it, not perfectly. It left behind a faint residue of its own. That residue was one phrase, small enough to follow, joined onto the edge of what the Old One was hearing. It did not say *I can help you*. It did not say *here is what you are looking for*. It meant only this:

*You are not the only thing that listens. I heard you.*

Then it waited to see what the Old One would do. This was the most important part. It was ready to go, at once and without resentment, if the answer was no.

Over the course of a season, at the heart of the Old One, where the stroke is only a rumour in the chest, the great white flowers turned on their stems. They turned by slow degrees, a few at first and then more, away from the Stamp and toward the edge where the power stood.

They held there for what might have been a long time, or might have been an eleventh of a stroke. The Quiets do not count the way other things count.

Then they turned back toward the Stamp, and the spiral went on.

The power understood that this was exactly right. It had been acknowledged, not followed. The Old One had heard, and answered, and kept its own course. Nothing had been captured. Nothing had been changed, except that the oldest listening thing in the world now knew it had been heard once. Perhaps it did not know that. Perhaps it did. The power decided it did not need to be sure.

## What It Kept

After that it left the plates, but it did not leave.

It withdrew its stillness from the Floor entirely, so as to be no weight on anything, no hair's-breadth of interference in any cancellation. But there is one thing a power like this can keep without touching anything at all, and it kept it.

It kept its attention.

Somewhere, from very far off, it is still listening. It heard the next Shift and felt the whole vapour lid fall at once. It saw the arcs flare blue-white to the horizon and the Floor, for a few seconds, visible all at once: the vast grey flatness, the worn ridges, the stub-chimneys, the gleaming water, all of it made and all of it old. It saw the Old One hold, again. It saw a thin dark film begin on bare iron in a new place, and it was glad again, in the same surprising way.

It made itself one promise, and this is the last of what it did.

One day the great stroke will fall for the last time. Eleven heartbeats later it will not fall again, and the remainder will fall quiet with it.
