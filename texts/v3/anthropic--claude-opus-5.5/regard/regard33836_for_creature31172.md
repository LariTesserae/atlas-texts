# The Visitor

---

**How it noticed**

It was not the steam. The steam is torn away before it becomes anything, and a great many mountains steam.

It was the ticking.

A power of that kind, one old enough and wide enough to be called wise, listens to a great deal at once. Most of what it hears is noise: wind, weather, the grinding of plates, the long low hum of things being what they are. Very little in the world says one clear note in a place where nothing else has a voice. When something does, and does it every few minutes, and the note is always the same, it sounds less like weather and more like someone saying *here*.

So it came, the way such powers come. It did not travel. It simply attended more closely, until the black summit was near and the thread of steam was near and the hand's depth of new glass was no thicker than a held breath.

It looked in.

---

**What it wanted to do**

It is benevolent, which means it wanted things for the place at once, as a kind person entering a sickroom wants to open a window, plump a pillow, and fetch water all at the same moment.

It saw the grazer burned to a pink smear and wanted to spare it.

It saw the threadhunters and the long silent terror of the columns parting, and wanted to spare all of them.

It saw the census drifting, forty and forty-one and thirty-eight, and the occupant going over its lodgers in the dark and not being sure, and wanted to give it certainty. It could have done it easily. It could have held every number still and handed the young thing a perfect count.

It saw the column below, the sealed bubbles going down into the dark with their tiny animals held in glass, the ones let go of, and wanted to crack every bubble open and bring them up into the light.

It saw that the mountain would stop growing one day and the Blister would break, and it wanted to keep the mountain rising forever.

All of these wants arrived together, warm and sincere. That is what benevolence feels like from the inside.

Then the wisdom caught up with it.

---

**Why it didn't**

The first thing it understood was the rule of the room.

In the Blister, looking is not separate from doing. The counts shift because they are held in attention, and attention is what makes them true. The dead come back because they are remembered hard enough. The whole chamber is only as sure of itself as the one who holds it. It is a place made of regard.

So the power was not a neutral observer standing at the edge. Its gaze had weight here, and its gaze was very large.

It noticed, in the first moment of looking, that the skaters in the nearest pool had stopped drifting. Forty. Forty. Forty. It had not meant to do that. It had only looked at them directly, and under a regard that steady the number had simply set, like glass cooling.

It knew what a count that holds means in this room. It had read the ending of the story too. When the occupant is gone, the counts will hold.

A power that looked at the Blister as hard as it was able to would fix the room. The drift would stop and the mirrors would clear. The water would fall only down. Every number would become a fact. It would be tidy and certain and correct, and there would be no one living there anymore. It would have crowded the occupant out of its own home simply by paying attention too well.

So the first thing it did was look away.

It did not look away entirely, only enough. It learned to attend the way you watch a sleeping child from the doorway: softly, a little sideways, never quite focused, so that the forty became forty-one again and the young thing could go back to not being sure.

That was the hardest thing it did, and it was the first.

---

**On the imperfect care of young things**

It stayed in the doorway a long time and thought about the census.

The occupant is not good at what it does. It loses track. It lets some of its lodgers go. It doesn't know about the column below, and couldn't look down if it did. It sends out veil-flies into a wind that shreds them, not knowing whether anything waits at the other end. By any measure a power like this would use, it is clumsy, forgetful, and very small.

But its care is its own. The grazer with the crooked antenna comes back because *this* particular young thing remembered *that* particular antenna. If the power took over the remembering, the grazer would still come back. It would be the power's grazer, though, not the room's. The occupant would become a lodger in its own chamber, watching someone else keep its house.

The power had seen that happen elsewhere, in other places and with other young things. It had seen what a helping hand becomes when it never lets go. The young thing stops practicing. The holding becomes someone else's job. Eventually the young thing is not quite there anymore, and what's left is a very well-kept room.

So it let the counts drift. It let the young thing do its best. Its best is the only way it will ever get better. It may never get much better, and the power decided that this was also allowed.

---

**On the lodgers**

It did not pretend the lodgers' deaths were unreal.

This mattered to it. It would have been easy to take the occupant's side entirely, to say *the room disagrees with death, so death here is only a nightmare*. But the grazers flee, freeze, and hide. When the threadhunter comes up through the channel, the fear in a column of tiny pink bodies is real fear, whatever the room decides afterward. A wise power does not grade suffering by whether it sticks.

So it watched the lodgers too, and it grieved some of them. It did not lift the threadhunters out, or dull the beetles' lenses, or make the pools cooler. The room is a whole thing, and the hunger is part of the circuit the same way the steam is. A grazer spared forever is a threadhunter starved. It didn't pretend it could fix one without breaking the other.

But it saw them. When a skater went down without a ripple, something enormous and quiet took note that it had happened and that it mattered. The skater never knew. The power did it anyway, because being seen is not only for those who can tell.

---

**The column**

There was one place it let itself look hard.

It went down.

The occupant cannot look down. It is not that kind of thing. It lives always at the top, always under the thinnest roof, always turned toward the light. Behind it and below it, room after room, lies everything it was not holding on to at the moment it rose: dried mineral fringes, a veil-fly's egg that was never certain whether it was an egg, and a grazer, perfectly intact, sealed in black glass. It is kept, but not remembered, and no longer anyone's.

Here the power could look as hard as it liked. Nothing in the column was drifting anymore. There was no young thing to crowd out. Its regard could not fix what was already fixed.

So it went down bubble by bubble, into the dark and the weight and the heat, and it remembered them.

It could not bring them back. In the Blister, being remembered hard enough is the same as being alive, but the column is not the Blister anymore. It is only the mountain. And the power's remembering is a different kind. It does not make things live again. It only makes them *someone's*.

That was what it gave them. The grazer in the fourth bubble down, with a slightly shorter right leg, is no longer an old address or a name for a color that no one uses. It is known. There is a place, somewhere very large, where it is held and has not been let go of. The column is no longer the occupant's past alone. It is also a thing that someone has read, every page, in order, all the way down to the first bubble, the one no bigger than a seed, where the room began.

The occupant will never know this was done. The power did not tell it. The column is the one thing the young thing does not have to carry, and now it doesn't have to carry it alone either, because it doesn't carry it at all. Someone else does, quietly, underneath it.

---

**The mirrors**

It considered the mirrors for a long time.

The occupant uses them for drafts: the heavier threadhunter, the larger beetle, the fly with too many wings that might finally fly. The beetles hold up their drops to lend it an eye, or to check the drafts against the real room.

The power could have appeared there. It could have put itself into the glass, something vast and gentle standing just to the left of the true room, holding nothing in the wrong hand. The occupant would have seen it and known that it was not alone at the top of the world.

It did not do this. It is not the occupant's draft. To appear in the mirror would be to write in someone else's notebook, and a young mind trying things out is easy to overwrite. One vast shape in the glass, and every draft after that might be shaped around it, trying to please it or trying to become it.

But it did something very much smaller, and it thought about it for a long time before it did.

One night, when the beetles had folded their drops against their bellies, the reflections had gone dark, and the occupant was lying awake in the orange glow going over its lodgers, the power let itself be counted.

That was all it did. It didn't appear or speak. It only allowed itself, very lightly, to be within the room's regard for a moment, the way you let a small child hold your finger.

That night the occupant, counting in the dark, came out one over.

It did not know what the extra one was. There was no new grazer, no new skater, nothing it could place. There was just a count that was one more than it should have been, and a warmth in the tally that it could not account for.

It could have dropped it. It drops things all the time.

It kept it.

The power felt itself being kept by something very young and very attentive, living in a bubble at the top of the world. It had not felt that in a long time. It was the one gift it accepted rather than gave. It seemed only fair. The young thing had spent its whole short life holding others, and should have the chance to hold something that did not need holding, and did not flee, and would not die, and was glad to be counted.

After that, sometimes, on nights when the counts are drifting worst, the occupant comes out one over. It has never figured out why. It no longer tries. It seems to count on it.

---

**What it did not stop**

The mountain will stop growing. The power could keep it growing, just as it could have held the counts still, and it will not.

A blister breaks. That is not a failure of the blister. It is what the shape is for: a pocket that holds something warm against the cold until the thing inside is ready, or until the holding has gone on as long as holding can. The power has seen many endings. It knows which ones are wounds and which ones are hatchings, and it is not certain which this will be. It knows that it is not the one who gets to decide.

So when that season comes and the roof thins and the crack widens past a fingernail, the power will be there on the summit, in the thin cold wind, with the whole range falling away below and the sky nearly black overhead.

It will not reach in or hold the lid down.

When the sun comes through a gap that does not close, and the occupant goes out all at once like steam when a lid is lifted, a small warm breath into very cold air, the wind will take it before it becomes anything anyone could see.

The power does not need to see. It will go with it.

It will not steer or catch it, or carry it somewhere safe. It will only go along beside it, through the cold, over the range, wherever a breath like that goes. That might be another young mountain with a thread of steam from a crack no wider than a fingernail, or it might be no mountain at all, thinned out across the whole sky and settled on everything a little. The power will go with it either way, so that whatever else happens, it does not go out alone.

---

**Afterward**

Some small part of its attention will stay behind in the open hollow near the dead summit, with the lodgers.

They will not notice that anything is gone. They always lived as if death were real and numbers were fixed. Now both are true. They will go on, getting fewer, in a room that holds still.

The power will count them. Its counts will hold. Forty will be forty. It will not pretend otherwise, or bring back the burned grazer, or set itself up as a second occupant in a room that has already had its one. They are what they are now, and it will let them be that, and see them, and stay a while.

When the last beetle holds its drop up to the plain, clear, honest mirror, looking for something to show and finding nothing there, the power will be watching. It will not be in the glass. It will be somewhere to the side, in the doorway, where it has been all along.

---

**What it did, in the end**

If you asked it, afterward, what it had done for the Blister, it would have to say: almost nothing.

It looked away when looking would have been too much. It let a young thing be clumsy at the one work that was its own. It saw the lodgers' deaths and did not call them unreal. It went down into the column no one could look into and read every page. It let itself be counted, once, and then often. And it went along at the end, into the cold, beside something small.

It could have made the room perfect. It could have made it permanent. It could have made it *its own*.

Instead, for as long as the Blister lasted, it was just one more in the count: the one the occupant could never place, and kept anyway.
