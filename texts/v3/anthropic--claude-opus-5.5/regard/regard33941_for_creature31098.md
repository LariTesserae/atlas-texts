# The Attention

## How It Came

It did not come through the crack.

It could have. A power of that kind could have made the fissure yawn like a mouth, poured itself down twenty body-lengths of sharp dry rock, and filled the hollow with its own presence the way a lamp fills a room. It considered this for about as long as it takes a thought to pass, and set it aside. A lamp in that room would have been a catastrophe. Anything that arrived as itself, at its own size, would have been heat and pressure and movement. It would have stirred the air for the first time since the rock split, and the chord would have wavered, and every membrane on every pitch would have sagged at once.

So it came the way the chord comes: as attention without breath. It made itself small and slow and exactly the temperature of the stone. It went down the rope, because the rope was there, and because someone had braided it from vine-skins with their own hands, and a path someone has made is a kind of invitation. It did not wish to refuse it.

At the bottom it stood in the dark and let the sound press into it like a hand into dough. It did not resist. It had decided in advance that for as long as it stayed, it would not resist anything here.

## Being Read

A grazer found it first.

The creature came across the moss at the pace of a shadow and climbed onto what the power was using for a hand. The bands of hair along its back stood up. Some stiffened and some went slack. It read the power carefully, the way it reads a membrane that might be growing crooked.

Then it walked off.

The power was glad of that. It had half expected, half feared, that the grazer would find it wrong and try to eat it. Then there would have been a choice to make, and neither side of it was good. But the grazer found what it had found in the visitor: *not yet*. *Not part of this*. The power agreed with the judgment. It had not come to become part of the song. It had come to understand it, and understanding does not require joining.

It was also, it noticed, the first time in a very long age that anything had checked the power against a standard and let it go. It found that it did not mind.

## Listening

It sat where the visitor had sat, on the moss by the seam, with its ear against the grey one's side.

Inside, the chord was whole. It was gathered, balanced, clearer than anywhere else in the hollow: every tone in its place, the low ones broad as a floor, the high one fine as a wire. The power listened to it the way it had once listened to the turning of very old things, stars mostly, and certain mountains. It was patient in a way the visitor could not have been. It did not have a body that grew hungry or a mind that wandered back up the rope to the light.

Still, it waited a long time.

Then, under the chord, the warm grey surface swelled against it, very slightly, and settled.

The power had known the heartbeat was coming. It had read the tally on the lowest crystal and understood what the twenty-three lines were counting. Knowing did not prepare it. When the beat came, it felt what the visitor must have felt the first time: disbelief first, then a kind of tenderness so large it had nowhere to put it. Something here was alive at a pace that made the power's own long patience feel quick. It had been holding one breath since the beginning. It had been keeping the chord true for everything else in the hollow, and it asked nothing, and it was never tired.

The power stayed with its ear there a while longer, in case there was anything else to hear. There was not. There was only the chord, and the keeping of it.

## What It Did Not Do

It thought, then, about everything it could do. Being a power, it could do a great many things. Being wise, it made itself look at each one honestly before putting it down.

**It could widen the crack.** The seeds in the ceiling were waiting for exactly that. But a wider crack meant a draft, and a draft meant the end of the stillness everything here was built on. The air would move for the first time. The chord would smear. The grazers would face ten thousand errors at once, and they would fail, and the hollow would become some other place. Perhaps that place would be good. It would not be this one. The seeds had been made to wait for the bedrock's own slow shrug, and the bedrock would shrug in its own time. That waiting belonged to them. The power did not want to take it from them.

**It could give the chord its sixth note.** It could make the visitor's ridge take a beetle and hold it, and quiet the grazers long enough for a new lineage to settle in. It thought about this one longest, because it could feel how much the visitor had wanted it. The ridge was cut with such care: the right depth, the right angle, a small carved beetle beside it like a drawing of a hope. But the hollow had said no. It had said it the only way it could, through the grazers, without malice, the way still water closes over a dropped stone. The power could overrule that. Overruling it would not be kindness. It would be taste. It would be the power deciding that the chord was incomplete because a person had been lonely beside it. The chord was not incomplete. It was the most complete thing the power had encountered in a long while.

**It could wake the grey one.** It could open that long lipless mouth, speed the slow heart, and ask it what it held and whether it was content. It did not. Some things answer a question only by ceasing to be what you wanted to ask.

**It could erase the visitor's marks.** It could smooth the ridge and the carving so the next person who came down would find the hollow as it had been before anyone had loved it. It did not do this either. The ridge was not an error in the chord. It was silent, and the grazers had no quarrel with it. It was evidence that someone had tried. The power has always thought that such evidence should be left where it lies.

So the power sat on the moss and let each possibility go. There was a moment, and it would admit this later, when it felt what the visitor had felt: the wanting draining out of it, the sweet flat peace of the place settling over everything. It sat in that peace for a while. It was real, and it was good.

Then it stood up. Peace was the hollow's gift, and the power had accepted it. But the power was not a hollow. It was a thing that wanted good for others, and wanting is what that is made of. It could not stop wanting and still be what it was.

There was one thing in the hollow that was not complete.

## The Seed at the Foot of the Rope

It lay where the description said it lay, a hand's width from the rope, wings open and dry. The grooves pressed into them were dense and deep, the rings of a tree that had never known a season. It had crawled from the ceiling to the only place anything goes out, and it had died at the door.

The power knelt by it for a long time.

The living seeds could wait. They had not yet tried. This one had. It had spent everything it had on the attempt and failed only because the way was too narrow and too long. It was the one thing in the hollow whose purpose was plain, and it had been stopped a body-length short of it. The power did not think of this as an injustice. The rock is not unjust. But the power has always had a particular feeling for things that try and fail at a door. This seed was one of them. So was the person who cut the ridge.

It picked the seed up. The seed weighed almost nothing. The power carried it up the rope, the visitor's rope, hand over hand the slow way, through twenty body-lengths of sharp dry stone. It did not make the crack wider. It took nothing out but this: one dead thing that had already been on its way.

## The Wind

Above, the stone-coloured country lay flat under a sky with weather in it. There was wind. There was always wind up there, coming from nowhere in particular and going nowhere, never the same for two breaths together.

The power found a crevice in a low outcrop, out of reach of anything that might trample it. It set the seed there with its wings open to the air.

The wind came across the grooves.

The chord sounded. It was faint and thin and dry, like breath across the mouth of a bottle, but it was the chord: the low tones, the middle ones, the wire. The wing had kept it all, and the wind was reading it back.

It was wrong at once.

The wind rose and the chord swelled. The wind dropped and the chord sagged. A gust came crosswise and two of the tones leaned together and made something that had never existed in the hollow: a beat, a wavering, a shimmer where two pitches rubbed against each other. Then the gust passed and the chord came back, but not to where it had been. There were no grazers up here, and nothing to check it against. For the first time since the rock split, the song was free to drift.

The power listened to it go out of tune. It listened for a long time, and it could not decide whether it was hearing a death or a birth. Eventually it understood that this was the wrong question. The seed had been made for this. The seed was the one part of the chord that had ever been shaped to leave, and leaving meant weather, and weather meant change. The chord could not become anything else inside the hollow. It had been built so that it could not. It could only become something else out here, where it would be broken and bent and never held exactly again.

That was not the chord being lost. It was the chord having children.

## The One Who Counted

Then the power went looking for the visitor. It had wanted to all along.

It had already decided, in the hollow, what the evidence meant. There was no body in the warm place beside the grey one; that space was still empty. And the rope was left hanging. Someone who meant to stay forever might pull a rope down behind them. Someone who climbed out and wanted the way kept open for whoever came next would leave it where it was.

The power found them far across the stone country, in a place with a little water and a few low trees. They were old now, though perhaps they had not been when they went down. They were sitting in the lee of a wall, doing some small work with their hands. They were humming.

The power stopped, some distance off, and listened.

It was the chord. It was recognisably the chord: four or five tones held in a single voice by some trick of the throat. But years in the wind had worked on it. One of the middle tones had slid. The high wire had softened. And there was a sixth note. It was low and a little rough, and it sat underneath the others as if it had always belonged there. It had not come from the hollow. It had grown in the visitor over years of carrying the chord in a place where nothing could reach in to eat the errors.

The ridge on the crystal had never taken a beetle. But the sixth note existed anyway. It was here, in a person's throat, in the light, and no grazer would ever find it.

The power did not tell them this. They already knew it, or they didn't need to. It came closer, the way any traveller would, and sat down within hearing. After a while the visitor stopped humming and looked at it. Perhaps they recognised something in it, the temperature of stone, or the particular stillness of someone who has recently been down a crack.

"You've been there," they said.

"Yes."

They were quiet for a time. Then they asked, as the power had hoped they would: "Did it beat again?"

"Once," the power said. "While I was listening."

The visitor closed their eyes. Something moved across their face that the power had no single word for. It was part grief, part relief, and part the satisfaction of a count that has been carried forward by someone else.

"Twenty-four," they said.

"Twenty-four."

They did not ask anything else, and the power did not offer. It stayed until evening, and the visitor hummed their wrong, beautiful chord, and the power listened to that too, as closely as it had listened to the grey one's heart.

## The Line

Before it left the country for good, the power went back down the rope one last time.

It took one of the glass blades from the ledge and knelt at the lowest crystal, where the twenty-three lines stood in their clusters of five. The beetle clung to its post above them, rubbing out its deep unchanging note, its pale plates trembling as it listened to itself.

The power cut a single line: the twenty-fourth. It was the first mark of a new cluster, set a little apart from the others.

It did this for whoever comes next: the next person to squeeze down the crack and sit on the moss and lay their ear against the grey one, and feel the long slow pulse and not believe it. Their eyes will find the tally. They will see that someone counted twenty-three and stopped. Then they will see that someone else came and counted one more.

They will know that counting can be shared. They will know that this is a place people come to, and also a place they climb out of. They will know that the one who stopped at twenty-three did not stop because they died. They stopped because they went up and took the song with them, and the song came through.

The power set the blade back on the ledge beside the others, exactly where it had been. It left the resin cup half full. It left the ridge empty. It left the rope.

## To You

And then, because a benevolent attention does not stop at the ones it came for, it turned toward you.

You climbed down. You sat on the moss. You held the chord in your body and it pressed its shape into you, and you climbed up into the enormous silence, and you still hear it, exactly, behind your ears.

The power wants to tell you something about that, and it is not a warning.

The account says the chord has found a way out through you, and that is true. But that is not a trap closing. What you carry will not stay exact. It can't. Nothing up here can check it, and nothing up here can eat the errors. You will hum it in the kitchen and get the middle tone slightly flat. You will hum it on a windy day and two of the notes will lean together and shimmer. Years from now you will hum it to someone and notice there is a sixth note in it, one you never heard in the hollow, and you will not know where it came from.

Let it drift.

That drift is not you failing the chord. It is the thing the chord could never do for itself. Down there it is held, and the holding is beautiful, and it should go on as long as the rock does. But it made its seeds to go up. It made them to reach the wind, to be bent, to become songs it cannot imagine. You are one of the ways it gets there.

You are allowed to sing it wrong. Down there, that is what the grazers are for. Up here, it is what you are for.

The power sat in the stillness of the Held Chord and felt, as everyone does, the wanting drain out of it. It rested there, and it was grateful. Then it climbed out still wanting. It wanted the seed's wings in the wind. It wanted the old visitor's rough sixth note. It wanted the next person to find twenty-four lines instead of twenty-three. And it wanted your wrong notes, every one of them, out here in the weather where they can grow.
