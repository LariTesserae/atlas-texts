# What Came Down at Kessary

## How It Noticed

It did not notice the rift. Rifts open all the time, somewhere. Ground breaks, and the world is always tearing a little and healing a little. A new trench full of swifts is a wonder, but the world is full of wonders, and a power that went to every one of them would never stay anywhere long enough to understand anything.

It noticed the laugh.

A slab let go on the south wall in the ninth year of Pell's life. There was the crack, the grinding roar, and the boom felt in the chest. Then came the one silence, and into it a child laughed. The fresh rock carried the sound the way it carries everything, down the long axis of the rift and out past the end of it, much farther than sound should go. It carried it farther than rock could explain.

Something heard it. It was benevolent, and old enough to be careful about what that word required. It came to see who was laughing, and why, in the one minute when everything else had gone quiet.

## It Did Nothing for a Long Time

This is the first thing it did, and the most important. It did nothing, for most of a year.

It had learned, over a long existence, that the moment of greatest danger in helping is the moment just after you arrive. Everything looks wrong then. Everything looks like it needs fixing. A girl of nine walks barefoot on razor-lipped stone above a crack that exhales steam. A man grieves in a roofless ring of rocks at the bottom of a wound. Four hundred feet of wall may let go at any hour. People die every year for salt. The urge to reach in is enormous, and it is almost always premature.

So it learned the place first.

It learned the rules the way a newcomer does, in the first hour. *Watch the birds. Watch the crabs. The rain is not water. Nothing is where it was yesterday. The rift is not finished.* Then it learned them the way Oszek had, over months, by listening. It learned the difference between the tonk of a slab on basalt and the tonk of a slab on air. It learned the sound of the seeps turning over in bed. It learned to read red hands and white hands and red outlines around white palms. It learned which shrine nests held the oldest lineages of dead's birds. It sat with the old-men crabs in the eight-year pools. It watched the young ones grow up inside the crystal shapes of their grandmothers, and it was moved in a way it had not expected.

It watched Pell most of all. She never saw it, but twice she stopped on a ledge and turned her head. That was not quite toward it. It was toward the place where a sound would be, if it had made one.

By the end of the year it knew three things.

The first was that the rift was not sick. It was raw, violent, and lethal, but it was not sick. Its collapses were its metabolism. Bloom grew heavy and broke. Nests fell and were rebuilt. Walls let go and exposed fresh stone, and the colored films spread across it, and the crickets came for the films, and the swifts for the crickets. Pell's small white hands went up on the new rock, saying *this is new, and it is fine.* Nothing in Kessary was going wrong. It was going.

The second was that the people there were not asking to be rescued. The harvesters came for money and knew the price. Oszek had not stayed because he was trapped. He had stayed because he found, to his own surprise, that the place was worth knowing. Pell was not suffering. Pell was the most at-home creature the power had seen in a long time.

The third thing took longer to see, and it changed everything.

## What It Could Have Done

It made a list anyway, because a wise power should know exactly what it is declining.

**It could have closed the rift.** It could have pressed the slabs back together, sealed the seeps, and cooled the heat. The plain would be a plain again, brown scrub over limestone. Everything in this account would have died or scattered in a season. So would the swifts in their columns, the crabs, and the first grandmothers in Kessary. It set this aside quickly.

**It could have stilled the walls.** It could have made the stone hold, so that no slab let go and no walkway fell at nightfall. It looked at this one for a long time. Then it saw that a rift that does not collapse does not renew. The bloom would grow and grow and never break, and the nests with it. There would be no fresh rock for the films to spread across. Within a few years there would be no more new stone for Pell's white hands, and she would have nothing left to be first on.

**It could have sweetened a spring.** It could have given one seep of clean, cold, drinkable water near Oszek's ring, enough for two. This was the most tempting of all, because it was so small and so kind. It considered it until it understood what the carried water was. Every jar that comes down the ladders from the plain is a thread. Harvesters bring it, and while they are there they bring news, cloth, oil, and a hand on the shoulder. The water is the reason people still come to the bottom of the rift to see an old man and a girl. A spring would have made the two of them free, and it would have made them alone. It left the brine undrinkable.

**It could have made Pell unbreakable.** It could have made her skin proof against the liver-stone and her bones proof against a fall. It could have caught her, invisibly, if a ledge ever went out from under her. It thought about her feet. She reads the slabs through them. She knows hollow from solid by the sound of her own steps. If it put a hand under her, even once, she would feel it the way she feels everything in that stone. She would learn, somewhere below words, that the rift was not quite what it said it was.
