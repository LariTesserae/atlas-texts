# The Visitor at the Rim

## Arrival

It does not come down through the rock, because that would break the rock, and it does not come in through a passage, because there is none. It simply begins to be present, the way attention begins, and the first thing it does is make a mistake.

It turns its full regard on the Hollow all at once.

Nothing visible happens. No light, no sound, no pressure. But the power is vast and its attention has weight, and somewhere in the column forest a colony of flyers falters. They do not stop singing, since they have nothing to stop for, but for the length of a breath the calls go ragged, the way a crowd goes quiet when a door opens and no one comes through. A few thousand bodies press closer to their pillar. The hum of the draperies seems, for an instant, very loud.

Then the power withdraws a little, and the roar closes over the place, and the flyers go back to seeing.

It has learned the first thing the Hollow has to teach, and it learned it the way everything here learns anything: by causing a silence and noticing the shape of it. To look at this place too directly is to dim it. There is already one creature here that lives by that law. The power had meant to begin with that creature. Instead it begins by becoming more like it.

It goes up to the roof.

## Listening

For a long time it does nothing but listen.

This is harder than it sounds, even for a power of its kind. The Hollow is not a picture. It is a process, and it does not hold still to be understood. The power listens through Heaving after Heaving. It hears the great chord slide up and down. It hears the ticking of the eastern flats swell with each new generation of crickets and thin with each die-off. It hears a young colony, having found no pillar that fits it, settle onto a column two tones flat and begin the work of a thousand years. It hears the grinding under everything, the deep earth shifting and exhaling through the springs, and in that grinding it hears something the Hollow cannot: the rhythm is slowing. Not soon. Not within any span that would mean anything to a flyer, or to a hunter, or to the stone. But the breath of the deep earth is growing longer between each surge. One day, a very long way from now, the Heavings will stop. The mats will starve, then the snails, then everything.

It sets this knowledge aside, carefully, to come back to.

It listens to the hunters most of all. There are thirty-one of them. It learns each one by the shape of its silence: the one that drifts in wide lazy spirals, the one that always crosses the eastern flats against the wind of the breathing fissures, the old one whose ring frays wider at the edges now, because the flyers have learned it is slower than it was. It learns the pup on the southwestern roof, folded under a parent's wing, and hears it cry less each day, then not at all.

After a while the power understands that it has begun to do what the hunters do. It reads the Hollow by the gaps. It notices each creature by the hush around it.

## What It Could Do

A power like this one could do almost anything, and so it has to think about each thing.

**It could give them light.** The mats already glow. The rising larvae already spark. It would take very little to make eyes. Somewhere in the flyers' long history there were eyes, and the memory of them is still folded into their development, waiting. It could let one generation open them. They would see the embers climbing over the springs. They would see each other.

It considers this for a long time, because it is the most beautiful of the possible gifts. Then it sets it down. The flyers do not lack sight. They have sight, a better one for this place than any eye, one that can resolve a larva at forty meters in total dark and share the illumination with every neighbor at once. Eyes would be a clumsy second sense, bought at a metabolic price they cannot afford, useful for nothing but the one thing the power wants for them, which is to see what it sees. That is not a gift to them. It is a wish to be joined in its own way of seeing.

**It could give the hunter a voice.** Let it call. Let it find its own kind in the dark without the long blind drift toward a distant hole in the light. Let it see what is directly in front of it for the first time.

It would starve within a season. Worse, the flyers would learn its voice, and the rule of silence would break. The travelling fronts of stillness that are the Hollow's only shadows would stop, and in their place there would be a single frequency everything flees from. The Hollow's weather would end.

**It could end the killing.** The flyers live in terror of a shape they have never perceived. Every hunter's life is a string of small deaths at the rim of its shadow. The power could make the hunters need less, or the flyers matter less to them.

It traces what would follow, and follows it all the way through. Without the hunters the colonies swell. They crowd the pillars and outgrow the larvae. They come apart in hunger, and the long tuning of the stone, the slow agreement of voice and column, collapses into crowding and famine. The silences are not a wound in the Hollow. They are part of how it breathes.

**It could let them find each other.** This is the one it circles back to most often, and the one it distrusts most in itself. It listens to the hunters and hears a creature that has never once perceived the thing in front of it. It meets its own kind only in the blindest place in the world, and when one of them dies, another hears a region of its map that never goes dark again. The power has a word for this, and the word is loneliness.

So it does the hardest thing it does in the Hollow. It stops assuming, and looks.

## What It Finds

Not loneliness. Or not only that. Nothing the word quite fits.

What the power finds in the hunter, as far as it can find anything at all, is attention: enormous, patient, total. The hunter's life is not organized around the absence at its center. It is organized around the whole. It holds the entire Hollow in its skull, every pillar, every tuned column ringing a fraction too true, every region of ticking and bubbling and whistling. It hangs on the voiceless roof and listens down, and what it hears is not the absence of anything near. It hears the presence of everything far. The blindness at the center is simply where it stands. No creature sees the place where it stands.

And the meeting in the doubled silence is not a consolation for isolation. It is the rarest event in the hunter's world, and possibly the only one that is wholly its own. Two holes in the light turn toward each other across the basin. For a while, at the center of the deepest dark in existence, there is touch.

When the old hunter falls, the other one hears the place that no longer goes dark. The power listens to that one closely, for a long time. It hears the hunter open one wing, listen toward the changed region, fold the wing, and open it again. Something is happening there. The power does not know what to call it. It decides that not knowing is correct, and that the honest thing is to leave it unnamed rather than lend the hunter a grief that may be only its own.

It does notice, though, that it was the power that came here carrying the word. The hunter did not bring it.

## What It Learns

Near the end of its stay, the power understands what the hunter has been showing it all along.

It is also a thing that cannot approach without changing what it approaches. It learned this in its first moment here, when a colony faltered under its regard. Everything a great power reaches toward falls a little silent at its coming, reshapes itself, performs, hides, or breaks. It is the rule of silence, written larger. The power has lived its whole long life, like the hunter, surrounded by a ring of altered things, and has rarely seen anything as it is when unobserved.

The hunter's answer is to hunt at the rim: to stay far enough back that the world keeps singing, and to read everything by the light of other voices. The power's answer, it decides, will be the same, turned toward love instead of hunger.

So it keeps to the edges. It stays on the roof.

## What It Does

It does three things, and none of them can be detected.

**First, it watches the light.** After every Heaving, when the larvae rise freckled with luminescence over the springs, ten thousand faint embers climbing into nothing and going out one by one as they are eaten, the power is there to see it. Nothing else in the Hollow has eyes. The light was an accident, beautiful and wasted.

It is not wasted now. It is seen. This changes nothing in the Hollow at all. The power has come to think that this is the point. Some gifts are given to a place. This one is given to the place's beauty, which had been happening unwitnessed for ten thousand years and now, once, has a witness.

**Second, it attends the meetings.** When two silences drift together over the springs and merge, the power turns its whole regard toward the doubled dark. It does not come close, and it does not light anything. It simply lends the meeting an audience besides the flyers lying flat on their pillars. The hunters never know. The power does not need them to. Attention is not always meant to be received.

**Third, it remembers.**

This is the only thing it does that touches the long future. It knows the Heavings are slowing. It has thought about intervening: steadying the deep earth, keeping the springs pulsing forever. In the end it will not, for the same reason it would not give the flyers eyes. The Hollow is not a painting to be preserved. It is something that is happening, and everything that is happening ends. To freeze it would be to replace a living place with a monument to one.

But it can make sure the ending is not also a forgetting. So it learns the Hollow completely, the way the hunter does, as a single singing thing. Every pillar's pitch. Every tuned column and the colony that tuned it. The drift of the silences across the roar. The chord of the draperies at rest and at the peak of the glissando. The patter of rain on fur, a little too soft. It takes the whole map into itself.

When the springs finally slacken for the last time, a very long way from now, and the roar thins, and the colonies fall from their pillars one band at a time, and the last hunter lets go of the roof and falls blind through the whole dark height, the Hollow will not simply stop. Somewhere it will still be held, whole and sounding, in a mind that listened to it once.

The skull full of hearing, sinking into the guano and turning slowly to stone, was already the Hollow's way of remembering itself. The power only adds its own memory to the stone's.

## Departure

On its last Heaving it stays on the roof beside the old hunter from the southwestern range, the one whose pup is grown now and hunting on its own. The two of them hang there in the cold among the forming drops, both silent, both listening down across the whole basin. The great chord slides upward. The roar doubles. Far below, the embers rise.

The hunter opens one wing, then both, and drops into the dark toward the bright rim of its own shadow.

The power watches it go, and then it goes too, as quietly as it came.

Nothing in the Hollow notices. The flyers sing. The stone hums. The rain falls from the roof, as always. Only the power itself is changed. It carries a region in its map of the world that never used to sound, and now always does.
