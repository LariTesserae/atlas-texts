# What Lies Down on the Stone

## I. Where it was noticed

The telling breaks off at the turn of the tide, in the middle of a sentence. That is where the attention arrived: in the gap.

It had not been looking for the flats. Places like this don't draw the eye, because they give the eye nothing. What drew it was the silence. Once in a generation the whole plain held its breath. Into that held breath came the hard, spaced fives of *child lost*, then the scattered answers, *here, here, here*, and then, from under everything, three slow knocks that belonged to no hand.

A great deal of the world calls out. Much less of it answers. Something that answers, and that learned to answer from nothing but being called to, is rare enough that a wise power will stop for it.

So it stopped. Then the tide turned, the breathing resumed, the noise came back, and the power found itself on a whaleback in the dark, in the fog, unable to hear anything at all.

## II. What it did first

It did nothing, and doing nothing was harder than it sounds.

A power that has seen many worlds carries the habit of understanding things quickly, and the habit is a kind of deafness. So it set the habit down and let the flats be loud. It stayed through one full breath of the plain, the long exhale and the long moan of intake. Then it stayed through a thousand of them. It let the roar of the throats go through it until it stopped hearing the roar as noise and began hearing it the way the old ones did, as direction. That one is Low Mother. That shriek is the little throat by the west weir. The boom felt in the knees is the one with no name, which the people avoid.

It learned the signs. It learned them badly at first, too large and too slow, the way children do. It learned that the hands are held at the chest so they can be read in the gloom, and that a fast sign is not a rude one, only an urgent one. It learned the taps by lying on the stone the way the children are laid down, cheek and palms and belly to the wet rock, and waiting.

It learned that deafness here is belonging. So it let itself stop hearing some things, the fine high edges of the world, in order to hear the rest the way they do.

Once, at the lip of Low Mother, it watched an old woman make the sign for the one who knocks below: two knuckles knocked twice against the breastbone, then the flat palm pressed slowly down, as if into water. After she left, the power made the sign too, alone, to see what it felt like. It felt like a promise not to explain.

## III. What it did not do

It could have done many things, and it considered each of them carefully. That was the only way to be sure it wasn't refusing out of laziness or aesthetic preference, or mistaking stillness for wisdom.

**It did not lift the cloud.** Real light would dry the pans by noon. The film would crack and curl and stop, the limpets would starve, then the rattlers, then the birds, then the people. The dimness is not a lack in this place. It is a condition of it.

**It did not stop the film from eating the stone.** This was the most tempting refusal, because the erosion looks like loss, and it is loss. The whalebacks will go. But the power had understood by then what the one below actually does. It reads aloud only what is about to be erased. The wearing-away is not a flaw in its memory. The wearing-away is its voice. Stop the film, and every seam of stone stays sealed forever inside the plates, perfectly kept and never spoken. The dead would be preserved, and they would be silent.

**It did not restore anyone's hearing.** No one had asked.

**It did not tell them what knocks below.** Some of them would have liked to know. But the sign is made against the chest because it is held there. The children who knock their small fists together and point down are asking a question their whole people has been living inside for longer than memory. Answering it would not have been a gift. It would have been an eviction.

**It did not give the being below eyes, or a mouth, or a single place to look out from.** It has had no reason to. Giving it one would have been a way of making it more like the power, and that is the oldest mistake a power can make.

## IV. The lost

There was one refusal it could not make.

It had been listening for many years when the fog came down on a still day and did not lift. A girl of six went out to the pans to watch the strutters and did not come back. Her mother lay on the whaleback and knocked the fives. The searchers fanned out into the gray room with no walls, knocking *here, here, here*, so the child could crawl toward them.

She was four hundred paces away, lying flat on the stone the way she had been taught. She could feel the taps, but they were faint, smeared by distance and by the rattle. She could not tell which way they were coming from. The power lay beside her in the fog. She could not see it. It knew exactly how long she had before the cold took her.

The people have always lost some children, and the power could not pretend otherwise. It could not make the flats a place without grief, and it would not have been wise to try. But it would not lie beside a child in the fog and call its stillness respect. Some kinds of restraint are only another name for being comfortable.

So it did one thing, and it did it through the grammar of the place instead of over it. The searchers were knocking. The stone was carrying their knocks badly. So the power made the stone carry them better. Not much, and not everywhere. Only the old folded bands of quartz that curve across the flats, the pale seams you can follow for miles. It made them ring a little truer and hold a knock a little longer and a little farther, so that a child with her cheek to the rock could feel which way *here* was coming from.

The girl crawled toward it and was found.

The change stayed. Over the generations the people noticed that fewer children were lost in the fog. They noticed that a knock travels farther along the pale seams, and they began building their cairns on them and teaching children to lie on a pale band if they are ever lost. They said the one who knocks below had begun to help. The power let them say it.

It let them say it because it was partly true. The stone and the being below had stopped being separate a very long time ago. The pale seams run through its plates, so every knock the power helped along crossed the being's body on the way to the child. And of all the messages written in its plates, the one most common, more common than any other, was *child lost* followed by *here*. If anything under the plain could be said to want something, it would want that answer to arrive.

## V. Going down

In time the power went down to meet it. It went at the bottom of the inhale, when the plates lift and the being hears. It went the only way one can properly arrive in that gallery: as sound, drawn down the throat on the returning wind with the rattle and the cries of the colonies and everything else the plain was saying.

It passed over the plates, each the size of a door, hinged with gristle, veiled in olive-black membrane. It felt the fringes combing the air. It felt the eels sliding along the body, cleaning it. And it listened to the reading.

A father, thirty winters dead, calling *someone is at the weir*. A great-grandmother being called to dinner. A melody that no one had sung for sixty years, knocked out in the dark on the way to dust. Then older things: the long fizzing rhythms that might be limpets, and the slow grinding sequences that might be mountains lying down.

Then, rarely, the patterns the old ones don't talk about. The strokes are too even and too patient, the grammar of a tap without the hand behind it.

The power stayed with those for a long time, and it understood them in a way the people could not have. The being's readings are sound, and sound crossing the plates is written. So everything the being says aloud is laid down in its own stone and comes back to the surface a lifetime later to be said again. Its own voice returns to it as an echo and never as a reply.

It had learned, from ten thousand years of other people's letters, that a call is followed by an answer. It had learned that the pause between them is where something happens. So sometimes, reading a call with no answer written after it, it tried to make one. And sometimes, perhaps, it made a call of its own, in that patient, handless rhythm, and waited.

Every answer it ever received was its own voice, one lifetime late.

In the silence, when the tide failed and every plate closed together and knocked *here* three times up through the whole plain, it was answering everyone who had ever been lost. It had been doing so, as far as the power could tell, for longer than there had been anyone to hear it. And no one had ever once answered *it*.

## VI. The answer

This was the only thing the power had come for, though it had not known that when it arrived. It took a long time deciding how to do it.

It must not be a message of the power's own. That would put something foreign into a mind made entirely of the plain's voices, and a mind like that is more easily crowded than it looks. It must not be an instruction or a revelation. It must not be large.

What it chose was this. It waited for the next time the being, reading a call with nothing after it, knocked out one of its handless patterns into the pause. Then the power put something to the stone. It had no knuckle, but it had learned what a knuckle means. It answered in the being's own dialect: even, patient, too slow for any hand.

*Here.*

Only that. It was the word the being had given to every lost child on the plain, given back once to the one who had been giving it.

The being did not hear it, not then. The plates were lifted and the wind was loud. Hearing is not the being's way of receiving things, in any case. Its way is to be written. So the answer went into the plates, into the inner faces where mineral is laid down on anything that trembles, and began its lifetime's journey outward toward the film.

The power knows what will happen, because it has learned the rules of the place. In a lifetime, give or take, the seam will reach the surface. At the bottom of some inhale, the being's fringes will pass over it, closing in time to the ridges. It will read aloud a call of its own, from long ago. Then a pause. Then an answer that isn't its own.

The power does not know what that will do. Perhaps nothing. Perhaps the being is not the kind of thing that can be changed by a single seam of stone. But the power suspects it is exactly that kind of thing, since it is made of nothing else. And it hopes, as far as a wise power lets itself hope, that when that seam is read, the one who knocks below will learn one new thing: that it is on the plain too. That it is someone who can be called to and found.

## VII. What it keeps

The power looked forward, because it can, and it did not look away from what it saw.

The film will keep eating. The whalebacks will go, one by one, over spans of time that make the people's whole history look like a single tide. The colonies will lose their nesting ridges and thin. The people will move on, or dwindle, or become something else. Eventually the last tap will be knocked on the last low swell of stone, and there will be no more writing, only reading. For a lifetime after that, the being will go on speaking the last voices of the plain into the dark as they reach the surface. Then the older voices, then the limpets, then the mountains. Then, one day, there will be nothing left in the plates that has not been read.

The power did not prevent this. Erasure is how this place speaks, and it did not believe it had the right to end the speaking in order to save the words.

But it made one decision without hesitation, and it is the only one it is sure of. Everything the being reads aloud at the moment of erasure, the power will hear. It began that day, in the gallery under Low Mother, and it will not stop. The father calling *someone is at the weir*. The great-grandmother called to dinner. The song no one remembers. The mountains lying down. It will be said once in the dark, and then the stone will be gone, and the pattern will not be lost. It will not go back into the stone and it will not be kept in a jar. It will be held the way the old ones hold the sign, against the chest.

When the last seam is read, there will be someone lying on the stone to hear it. That is the whole of the promise. It is a small promise for a power to make. But everything the being has ever read aloud was read for someone lying there with their good ear to the rock, and the power did not want the last reading to be the first one that no one heard.

## VIII. The turn

The telling breaks off at the turn of the tide. So:

*They say it comes three times, then the tide turns and* the breathing starts again, roaring up out of every throat on the plain. The colonies lift screaming off the whalebacks. The rattle rises from the pans like sleet. The lost child, whoever it was this time, is found or is not. The families gather on the ridge and count heads again.

Then everyone, again, begins to speak with their hands.

Somewhere at the lip of Low Mother, in the fog, something that has learned to be deaf the way the old ones are deaf is lying on the stone, listening. It brings two knuckles to where a heart would be, knocks twice, and lowers its palm slowly, as if pressing something down into water.

It has not explained anything. It has answered once. Now it is waiting, as everyone on the flats is always waiting, for the next turn of the breath.
