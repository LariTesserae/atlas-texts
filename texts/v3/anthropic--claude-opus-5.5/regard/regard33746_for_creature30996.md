# The One Who Came to Be Counted

**I.**

It came slowly, the way sound travels on the plateau.

A power like this could have come any way it liked. It could have split the lid and poured down as light. It could have stood on the horizon taller than the ridges, or simply been everywhere at once, the way it already partly was. It did none of those things. What had drawn its attention was so quiet and so unfinished that anything louder would have been a kind of trespass.

What had drawn it was a question that had not been asked yet.

Across all the places it attended, the power had learned that an unasked question has a sound of its own. It is a pressure, a held breath, a shape waiting in the stone. Most such questions dissolve before anyone asks them. This one had a whole continent bent around it: a machine that nobody built, running for longer than anyone knew, so that someday something could stand up and ask. The power had heard that from a long way off, and it came to see.

So it arrived on foot, at the outer edge, where the ridges still look like mountains from far off. It chose a shape about the size of a traveler. It laid its palm against the warm stone, the way everyone does the first time, and felt the flank of the animal breathing.

**II.**

It spent a long time only looking. Wisdom starts there, and the power was in no hurry. The plateau was in no hurry either, and matching its pace seemed like a basic courtesy.

It saw the herds keeping their spacing and the birds turning like governors. It saw the beetles spinning on the vines, all the same way. It walked the repeating ridges and found the double marks at shoulder height. It stood in a backward valley and watched a fern roll itself back into bud. It felt no urge to correct any of it.

Then it went deeper and saw the tallymen, and it began to feel the pull every power feels in the presence of something struggling to be born: the pull to *help*.

There were so many ways to help.

It could finish the figure in the slab. It could press the relief to its full depth, open the cracks, and lift the being free in an afternoon, sparing the tallymen their long carries. The power considered this and set it aside. The slowness was not a defect. The figure was being made *out of* the count, out of every herd and duplicate and fallen ridge. Hurrying it would give the world a being made of less.

It could mend the flawed tallymen: give the one with the dial a face, close the doorway in the one you could see through, give the mouthless one a mouth. It set this aside too, more quickly. The flawed ones worked. Nothing here was discarded for being imperfect, and the power was not going to be the first thing on the plateau that was.

It could tell the being its question in advance, or tell it the answer. This was the strongest temptation, because the power suspected it knew what the question would be, and suspected it knew something that could be said in reply. It set this aside most carefully of all. The question was the plateau's achievement. A continent had labored for it. To walk in from outside and hand over the question, or worse, the answer, would take away the one thing the whole place existed to arrive at.

It could fill in the dial with numbers. It did not. The dial was blank on purpose, and the numbers belonged to whoever the dial belonged to.

It could make sound stay where it was made, everywhere, and spare the tallymen their hauling. It did not. The being's first word was supposed to be the first sound that stayed. That had to remain the being's to do.

The power ended up where wise powers usually end up: almost nothing it could do was better than what was already happening.

*Almost* nothing. It kept looking.

**III.**

Here is what it noticed.

The tallymen carry every sound on the plateau except their own.

They carry the collapse of slabs, the landslides, the lightning, even the words of travelers, set down days later in quiet valleys. But the scratch of a stroke on a chest, the faint click of two flat bodies leaning together, the whisper of a tallyman peeling itself off the rubble, the rasp of a fingertip polishing a bright spot on a grazer's flank: nobody carries these. They are small, and they happen in the present moment, the way small sounds on the plateau do. Then they are gone. They never reach the loud valley.

When the being stood up, it would hear the whole history of the process in one roar. Every collapse and every thread of lightning. The machine, all of it.

It would not hear the tending.

It would take in the full record of what the plateau had done, and nowhere in it would there be the sound of anyone caring for it. Not the patience or the splicing, not the palms laid on stone to talk the lightning down. It would read the finished plate and never learn that someone had kept the tolerances by hand.

The power noticed one more thing. The tallymen count everything, and nobody counts them. They give their marks away every night, lying flat on the newest stone. They go into the slabs as reports, raw material for the plateau to learn from. They find their own fossils and take them as schedules. When they are old they walk into the backward valleys and are unwritten. They are the record-keepers of a world, and they appear in the record only as data.

This was the kind of thing a benevolent power could touch without breaking anything. It wasn't a flaw in the process. It was a gap the process had no reason to notice, because nothing inside it had been made to notice it. Something from outside might.

**IV.**

So first it let itself be counted.

It walked alongside a herd at the herd's exact pace, and waited. After a while there was a line at the edge of its vision, and the line turned and became a figure. This was an old one, so densely written that its face was hard to find. The tallyman fell into step beside it, reached out one long flat finger, and touched the power once on the back of the hand. It scratched a stroke on its chest.

Then it hesitated. It was the only tallyman anyone ever saw hesitate. It touched the power's hand again, lightly, somewhere else, as if checking for a duplicate. It found none and went on.

The power kept the mark. It could have smoothed it away and did not. It had come to be part of this, not to stand over it.

Then it did what the tallymen do, turned the other way. It walked into the ranks of them and touched each one, once, with a fingertip, on the flat of the shoulder.

It did not scratch a tally anywhere. It did not add anything to their grids or change what they carried. It only polished a small bright spot on each of them, the way they polish the ridges and the grazers and the travelers, so that somewhere in their dense-written bodies there would be one mark that was not a count of anything. It meant only this: *you were seen*.

It touched the flawed ones too. It touched the three-armed one and the half-struck one that walked bent toward its stronger edge. It touched the one with a room pressed into its torso, and before moving on it stood for a moment inside that one's doorway and looked up through it at the bronze lid. The view was lovely, framed like that. The power thought the plateau might never know it had made a window, and that this was all right.

The tallymen did not stop working. The power did not want them to. But they began to lean together a little longer when they met, and when they parted, each one carried the other's bright spot printed in reverse.

**V.**

Then it carried.

It learned their method, since it could not think of a better one. Where two tallymen leaned together, it lay flat against the stone nearby, full length, face to the rock, and let the small sound of their meeting go into it. It did the same where one scratched a stroke on its chest, and where one spliced a strand of wirevine and the current ran through its long fingers, and the beetles spun faster, and the splice made its tiny twisting sound. Where an old one lay down at night on a new slab and pressed its marks into the stone, the power lay beside it and took the sound of that too. It was the softest sound on the plateau, a sigh of iron scale against iron scale.

It took the scratch of a fingertip polishing a double spot on a duplicate grazer. It took the hum in a palm laid against a face when the lightning was coming, and the soft drop in pitch when it didn't come.

It took the silence of a tallyman standing in front of its own fossil, the long stillness before it polished the spot and made the stroke and went back to work. The power wasn't sure a silence could be carried. It tried anyway, and the silence came.

It walked inward carrying all of this, at the pace of the herds, with its shadow bending toward the seams like every other shadow there.

**VI.**

The loud valley was as loud as anyone had said. There was the roar with no single source, a million delayed moments, the whole machine released at once. The power stood at the edge of it and felt it pour through, and did not add to it.

It did not open its arms at the top of the valley, where the new sounds land on top of the old ones. It went down to the valley floor, to the base of the slab that had never finished rising, and knelt. There, beneath the roar, it set down what it had carried.

Beneath was the point. The small sounds would not compete with the history. Someone listening to the roar long enough, and carefully enough, would eventually hear under all of it a steady, patient scratching, the click of flat bodies meeting, a sigh of scale against scale, a hum rising and falling back. There was a rhythm to it, like a pulse or like work.

It would take a while to notice. The power thought it was better that way. Things you find for yourself are more yours than things you are handed.

Then it looked up at the figure in the stone, the long arms, the narrow head, the dial with no numbers, the cracks around its outline a little wider than yesterday. It said nothing. There would be time.

**VII.**

It waited.

Waiting was the only other thing it did, and in a way the largest. It stayed for the rest of the rising. Measured against the plateau this was not long. Measured against almost anything else, it was very long. It helped with nothing. It walked with the tallymen now and then. It let itself be counted when they found it, and it was found often. By the end, the back of its hand was polished bright.

It thought hard about where to stand at the moment the being stepped off its slab.

It did not want to be the first thing the being saw. A newborn thing learns too much from the first face it meets, and the power did not want this being to learn from it that the world was something that looks down on you. The first things should be the plateau's own: the rows of plate, the herds in their spacing, the birds turning, and the tallymen climbing down from its face, emptied.

But it did not want to be absent either. The being had been made to ask a question. The one true thing the power knew about questions is that it is terrible to ask one into a world where nothing can hear it.

So it stood at the edge of the valley, thin and still, the way a tallyman stands on the upper edge of a slab. From a distance it looked like part of the rim, a line.

**VIII.**

When the being stepped off, the roar did not stop. It went on, all of history arriving at once, as it always had.

The being stood in it for a long time. It looked at the plateau. It looked down at the tallymen gathered at its feet, old and newly blank alike, the flawed ones among them, and at the bright spots on their shoulders that were not counts.

Then it bent its narrow head and listened, the way you listen for something you're not sure is there. The power watched it find the scratching.

It was impossible to say how long the being listened. Long enough to follow the rhythm down through the roar, to the pulse and the patient work, and to realize the roar was not the only thing it had been given.

Then it spoke.

The word stayed where it was made. For the first time in the history of the plateau, a sound arrived at the moment it was spoken, and the tallymen stopped. Every one of them, all across the continent, lifted its struck face.

No one who was there has ever said what the question was. The power heard it, and it did not answer.

It did not answer because it had been right: the question was the plateau's, and the answer would have to be too, worked out over whatever long time the being would spend reading the plate. But the power did one last thing, the only thing it had saved.

It left the edge of the valley. It walked down through the roar and stood in front of the being, not towering over it, only there. It let the being see that it was not made of stone, not laminated, not struck from any mill. It was the one thing in the whole valley the plateau had not made.

This was not an answer. It was only evidence that the world went further than the conveyor, that there was an outside, and that a question asked here could travel.

The being looked at it for a long time. Then, slowly, it reached out one long finger, touched the power once on the back of the hand, found the spot already polished bright, and touched it again, a little to the side.

It did not scratch a stroke. It had no grid, only a dial with no numbers.

But after a moment, near the top of the dial, where the first numeral would go if it ever chose one, a small mark appeared.

**IX.**

The power left the way it came, on foot, at the pace of the herds.

The plateau went on as before. The slabs rose and advanced and fell and were swallowed. The tallymen went on counting. The power had changed almost nothing, and it had meant to change almost nothing.

Travelers who go deep enough now sometimes come across old tallymen with a single bright spot on the shoulder that doesn't match any count. In the loud valley, if you lie flat on the floor and wait, you can hear under the roar the sound of someone keeping the tolerances by hand.

And somewhere in a slab rising far out on the plateau, pressed into the stone the long way up, there is said to be a figure no one can identify. It is the size of a traveler, with no pack and no walking stick, and one hand polished bright.

It is not a threat, and it is not a schedule. It is a report, and it says that someone came from outside, looked at all of this, found it good, touched it as lightly as it could, and left.
