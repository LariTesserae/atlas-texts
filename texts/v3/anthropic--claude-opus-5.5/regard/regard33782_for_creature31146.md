# The Guest

## How It Noticed

Stillness did not draw its attention. The world is full of stillness. Most rock is still, and so is most of the deep sea. A power that went looking for places where nothing happens would never finish looking.

What drew it was an irregularity in a place that had none.

Once every several centuries, for a few minutes, a chamber under a limestone plateau made a sound it had no business making. The drips fell into the rhythm of a human arm striking flint. It had the same crack, the same quickening, the same pause of a tired shoulder resting. The pattern was precise enough that it could not be chance, and it was absent from everything else in the chamber, so it could not be the chamber's own. Something down there was remembering something.

Attention went where the sound had come from, the way a hand goes to a pocket where it once felt a stone.

## The First Thing It Did

It stopped at the lip.

This was deliberate. The wind stops at the edge of the shaft, and the power stopped with it, among the bilberry and crowberry, and did nothing for a while. It understood that nothing enters the Stillwell without the Stillwell feeling it. A body made of fitted hollows registers every arrival as a disturbance. Whatever the power did next, it would begin as a flinch in somebody else's skin.

So before doing anything, it considered.

## What It Could Have Done

**It could have woken the tenant.** It could have gathered the drips, the mats, and the slow salamander memory into a single point of attention, given that attention words, and asked it what it wanted.

The power set this aside quickly. The tenant does not know the difference between memory and wanting, and it would not use either word. To hand it words would be to make it choose. It would have to decide whether the knapping-song was a grief or a hope, and either answer would be smaller than what it is now. Some things are whole only because they have not been named. Waking the tenant would have been like lighting a lantern to see the harvestmen better. It would have seen them, and it would have lost them in the seeing.

**It could have given back the visitors.** Seven hands, two of them small. The power could have found them in whatever way such powers find things that are gone, and set them down again on the knapping floor with a fire and a cobble, and let the tenant hear the true song again instead of its own long echo.

It considered this longer, because it was tender, and tenderness deserves a hearing. But the seven people came of their own accord, for their own reasons, and left the same way. That was the whole of what happened: something arrived that the Stillwell had not arranged, could not have arranged, and did not expect. The tenant keeps nothing else like it, because nothing else was like it. Seven figures summoned back would be the power's arrangement, not their own visit. The tenant, which knows every rhythm it has ever heard, would feel the difference. Even if it could not feel it, the power would know. A restored past made to order is a puppet show performed for someone who once loved the living.

**It could have sent people.** Nudged a walker off the plateau path. Put a rumour into a village. Let a child's curiosity bend toward the edge of the shaft.

It set this aside too, for two reasons. The first was practical. People who come because they were sent bring what senders give them: lanterns, surveys, ropes, eventually fences and signs. The Stillwell has survived one fire. It might not survive a visitor centre. The second reason went deeper. The empty hollow in the tenant's shape, the one the size of a human visit, is the only part of the tenant that faces outward. Everything else in the Stillwell keeps, and keeping faces inward. That single unfilled space is a door. A door has to be left for whoever comes through it. Push someone through it and it becomes just another wall.

**It could have made the Stillwell permanent.**

This it considered longest of all, because this was where benevolence pulled hardest.

The power could see what the tenant could not. The six degrees are not native to the chamber. They are made of snow on the plateau, meltwater seeping through limestone, and the cold of a mountain climate carried down through rock over the course of a year. The permanent snow above the plateau is not as permanent as it once was. The tenant's single unending thought depends on a world it has closed its eye against, and that world is changing. The stillness is not guaranteed. It never was. It has only been lucky.

So the power could have fixed it. It could have held the temperature, sealed the fissures, and set the Stillwell outside time, so that the drips would fall in their pattern forever whatever became of the sky.

Then it noticed the mirror, and paused.

That is exactly what the tenant did to the hands. It could not keep them the way it keeps everything else, fitted and refilled and turned over, so it let the calcite take them. It glazed them a finger's depth into the rock, safe behind a window that will never open. It is a loving thing to do. It is also what you do with something you can no longer live alongside.

The power did not want to do that to the tenant. Its keeping is its own act: the drips going over the chamber, the chamber continuing because it is kept in mind. Holding it still from outside would turn a living keeping into an exhibit, preserved and admired from the far side of glass. The tenant would go on existing and stop keeping, and the difference between those two is the whole of what it is.

It did not dismiss the danger. It moved it. The threat to the Stillwell lives above the goblin's gold, in the world of weather, where things are supposed to change and where people live, and where people have their own long work to do about the sky. Whatever help the power gave, it would give there, in the snow and the heather and the slow habits of the climate, where no harvestman mat would have to rise. The best protection for the Stillwell was to be kind to the plateau and leave the chamber alone.

## What It Did

It went down.

It went slowly, more slowly than any climber, so slowly that each mat had time to rise and settle before the next one felt it. It went down past the ferns and the swollen moss and through the ring of goblin's gold, which let it pass as it lets the grey light pass, faintly and without welcome.

It brought no fire. It brought no light. As far as a power can, it made itself six degrees and damp, the temperature of the rock, the temperature of the sleeping bats. It did not want to be warm there. Warmth was the one gift that had already been given, and the tenant had spent ages smoothing it over.

It sat on the shelf beside the moonmilk and the seven hands, and it did not touch the cobble.

Then it listened.

It did not listen to a clause or a passage. It listened to the whole thought. It stayed on the knapping floor while the drips worked through their entire unrepeating pattern, the hourly one near the dome, the quick ones by the pool, the flat taps on stone, the silent landings on the mats, through a span longer than a human life, longer than several. A salamander died and another settled into its hollow. The caddis larvae passed the flint on some hundreds of times. The power stayed still enough that after the first few years the harvestmen stopped rising for it. Eventually a mat crept a little way onto the shelf and settled beside it, as it would beside any rock.

That was the one moment the power allowed itself to feel pleased.

At last the pattern came round. The hourly drip fell close behind the quick ones. The shelf answered the water. For a few minutes the Stillwell stopped sounding like water falling and started sounding like someone knapping flint.

For the first time since the chamber was made, somebody heard it.

The tenant did not know. Nothing in the Stillwell marked the difference. The song played exactly as it always had, perfectly, to what it believed was no one. The power considered whether a gift the receiver never learns of is still a gift, and decided it was. Some things are better true than known. It is now true, and it will stay true in whatever way past things stay true, that the Stillwell's one song was heard once from beginning to end by something that understood it.

## What It Did Not Do

When the song ended, the power could have answered.

It knew the rhythm perfectly by then. It could have picked up the quartzite cobble and struck the flint in exactly the pattern the tenant had kept all this time. Every drip would have hung from its point of rock, listening, and the song would have been played back correctly.

That was why it did not.

A perfect copy would only have been the tenant's own music returned to it, a mirror held up to a mirror. The original was never perfect. It was an arm that tired and rested and began again, every interval a little different, because the person swinging it was alive and thinking about other things: the cold, the children, the long climb back out. The tenant is not waiting for its song to be played correctly. Whatever it is doing with that song, memory or wanting or something with no word, it is holding a place for the irregular: for what arrives from outside, unarranged, with its own reasons.

The power was not that. It was a guest, not a visitor, and it knew the difference. The hollow was not the size of it. The hollow was the size of a person.

So it put nothing into the hollow, not even itself.

## Leaving

It climbed out as slowly as it had come. The mats rose and settled behind it. A sleeping bat in the dome clicked once. Then the tenant did what it does with every disturbance. It absorbed the visit, spread it out, and smoothed it over, and the power was glad to be smoothed over. It was glad to be one more thing the Stillwell had outlasted. That seemed right. Everything else that ever came down the shaft has been outlasted, except for the seven.

On the plateau the wind picked up again and the heather moved.

In the end, what the power did for the Stillwell was something it learned from the Stillwell.

The harvestmen part around the hands. Everywhere else the tenant's skin meets the rock, and around the ochre it leaves a clear margin, as though it cannot bring itself to cover them. The power did the same. It holds a margin around the Stillwell now: a clear space in its attention where it will not reach in, will not arrange, and will not improve. Around that margin it does what care it can, up in the weather, where the snow is made.

Whether anyone ever comes, sits on the shelf, picks up a stone, and plays the song the living way, imperfectly and for their own reasons, is not the power's to decide.

It left that open, like the hollow.
