# The Attention

## How It Noticed

It did not notice the Throat because the Throat was loud. The world is full of loud things.

It noticed because of the line of spires. From high enough, and over enough time, the old Throats curving around the mountain's shoulder stop looking like rocks and start looking like handwriting: a row of pale strokes, each stopped at a slightly different height, each leaning a little toward the next. The power had seen many marks on many mountains. It had rarely seen one that looked so much like a sentence.

So it drew near, the way such powers draw near, which is to say it turned its regard toward the place and let the regard settle.

And almost at once it saw that this was a mistake.

## The Weight of Being Looked At

Attention like that is heavy. When a great and patient thing fixes its gaze, the gaze does not move, and in the Throat whatever does not move is taken.

The power felt it happening before it understood it. The mineral in the spray began to favor the places where its regard rested, as if the stone had recognized a kindred stillness. A frill of glass began to form along the lip of the fissure, reaching toward the ribbon. Somewhere below, the long pale body flexed harder than it needed to. The stutter came late. A swift, halfway through the curtain, faltered, recovered, and was through.

The power withdrew its gaze at once.

This was the first thing it learned about the Throat: here, even kindness has to keep moving. A regard that settles is a regard that seals. If it wished to look at this place without making it into something else, it would have to look the way the swifts fly, in and out, never resting, never folding its attention into a nest that the stone could claim.

So it learned. It took a long time by the measure of the visitors and almost no time by its own. It learned to glance, to look away, and to look back. It learned to hold its interest the way the ribbon holds the current, by matching the motion of the thing rather than pressing against it. Only then did it begin to consider what, if anything, it should do.

---

## What It Considered

It had the power to do almost anything here. That was exactly why it went slowly.

### It considered keeping the room open.

The fissure is closing, and the power could have stopped it. It could have held the crack at its present width, dissolved the shelves as fast as they grew, and kept the floor from rising. The room would have stayed as it was for a thousand years: bright, roaring, and wide enough for the visitors' grandchildren's grandchildren.

It looked at this for a long time, and then it looked at the spires.

A Throat that never closes is not a Throat. It is a fountain. The narrowing is not damage to the place. The narrowing *is* the place, as much as the water or the light. Each room is a breath, and the ribbon's word is made of breaths. To hold one open forever would be to make the word stop mid-syllable, not in stone, the way the mountain does it, but in an endless unchanging vowel. The power understood that this would be a kind of death more complete than the glass.

It left the fissure alone.

### It considered making the passage safe.

This was harder.

The power looked, as no visitor ever could, at the ones who had tried the wall without the gap. It followed where they went. It will not say what it found, and neither will I. Some things belong to the mountain. But it grieved them. It learned each of their names, and it keeps them.

It could have made it so that no one would ever be lost again. It could have given the wall a door that was always there, or taught every visitor's body to find the gap without counting, or quietly caught anyone who fell.

But the gap is the ribbon's. The stutter is the one gesture that creature has, the only thing it does that touches the world above. Whether it knows what it is doing is, as the visitors say, the oldest argument about the Throat, and the power could have settled that argument. It chose not to. What it saw was enough: if there is any attention in the stutter at all, then the door belongs to the ribbon, and taking it would be like taking a tongue out of a mouth to make the mouth safer.

The visitors know the danger. They come anyway. They learn the count. The power decided that the count was theirs to learn, and the gap was the ribbon's to give, and that its own benevolence did not extend to reaching in between two parties who had been conducting their strange, courteous business for longer than either could remember.

It left the wall alone.

### It considered translating the word.

It could hear the word. It could hear more of it than anyone. It had the patience to sit through a thousand days and nights and the reach to listen to every stutter at once. It could have gone down to the valley and told people what the Throat was saying.

But the word is not a message. It is not addressed to anyone. It is a description, a thing naming what it has found. A description spoken aloud to people changes. It becomes a speech. Its listeners start waiting for the point.

And the visitors come out quiet. That quiet is not a lack of understanding. It *is* the understanding. It is the correct response to hearing the middle of a sentence that is too long to hold. Translating the word would give them something to repeat, and they would stop being quiet, and they would stop coming back to hear the next part.

It left the word unspoken.

### It considered the end.

This was the hardest, and it came last.

The power followed the ribbon down. It went where no visitor had gone, down the crack, past the cold, into the warm, and then into the hot, along the whole length of the pale body to where it finally ends. It found the tail curled in the heat like feet toward a fire, just as the visitors had guessed. It looked below that, at the heat itself, and saw that the heat is waning.

The mountain is old. The fire under it is cooling slowly, on a scale longer than the line of spires, but it is cooling. There will be a last Throat. Someday the pressure will not find another crack, the ribbon will draw down and there will be nowhere to rise, and the word will end.

The power could have moved it. There are other mountains, younger ones, with fire under them that will last far longer. It could have carried the ribbon to one of them, and the word could go on and on, spire after spire, across ranges and ages.

It thought about this longer than it had thought about anything.

In the end it came to this: a sentence that can never end can never mean anything. It is the ending that lets everything before it become the thing it was. The ribbon has been saying one long word since before there were people on the mountain. It has been naming cold, and light, and swifts, and green, and hands. The power did not know what the word meant. But it knew that if it made the word endless, the word would never get to mean it.

And there was something else. It did not know whether the ribbon wanted to go on. It could not ask, not without speaking in the Throat, and that is not done. It could only listen. When it listened, very closely, to the parts of the word that are oldest, sealed in the stumps of the first spires, it thought it could hear in them already the shape of an ending. It was not a fear of the ending. It was the shape a sentence has when the one speaking it knows it will stop and has arranged its clauses accordingly.

The ribbon, perhaps, had always known the mountain was mortal. It had been speaking with that in mind.

The power left the fire alone.

---

## What It Did

So it did not save the room, or the passage, or the word, or the speaker. It did not repair or rescue anything, and it did not speak.

Here is what it did instead.

### It waited outside.

It did not come through the curtain the way a power could. It came the way the visitors come. It took a body about the size of a person, stood on the downhill side with its back to the mountain, and was soaked through in seconds.

It counted.

The gap did not come for a long time. The stutters happened, but they were small and uneven, and the wall never went thin enough. The power understood that it was being considered, just as it had considered. It did not push. A day passed. A night. It stood in the cold with the water going into its borrowed hands, and it did not mind, because if the ribbon did not want it in, then it would not go in, and that would be an answer too.

On the second morning the wall went glassy and thin, and the power threw itself forward. The water hit like a falling tree, and then it was on its knees on the wet stone, in the bright hush that is not quiet at all.

### It kept the rules.

It did not speak. It breathed through a cloth. It drank from the bowl, which tasted of cold metal and of nothing, and it was the first time in a very long age that the power had tasted anything simply because it was offered.

### It read the cords.

All of them.

It read the soft new ones knotted to the cairns, and the stiffened ones on the ladder. It read the ones sealed so deep in the floor that the stone above them was thicker than a hand, and the oldest ones, rigid as bones, that had been laid down in the first years of this Throat. It read counts of days, and names, and prayers, and things for which there is no category, things people had tied because their hands needed to say them and the room would not let their voices.

Nobody who ties a cord expects anyone to read it. The power did not read them to answer them. It did not answer them. It read them so that it would be true, from then on, that they had been read. These were things said aloud in an empty room, and the power made it so the room had not been quite as empty as they thought.

It did not tell anyone. It does not have to be known to be true.

### It pressed its hand into the floor.

It knew that nothing you bring stays yours. That was the point. It found a place where the new skin of stone was still soft as wet sugar. It laid its borrowed hand on top of a thousand other hands going down like hands under a frozen lake, and pressed, and let go.

The stone took it within a season, the same as all the rest. There is nothing to distinguish it. If you found it you would not know. It is one of the hands.

### It lay down at the lip.

This was the real reason it had come.

It lay on its belly where the stone is sharpest and newest, with the rising wall close enough to tear the cloth from its face. It looked down into the part of the water that is not yet white, and the ribbon was there, holding.

The power made itself small. This was the one hard thing it did. It held all of what it was down to the size and weight and warmth of a single visitor. It did this not to deceive the ribbon but out of courtesy. The word names things by tasting them, and the power did not want to be too large a taste. It did not want to become the whole word, or bend the sentence toward itself, or take up more than one syllable in a description that had room for the swifts and the moss and every soaked stranger who ever learned the count. It wanted to be named the way a person is named in the Throat, as one of the things found up here, no more.

And the ribbon turned, very slightly, toward the shadow of its face.

The power let itself be tasted.

### It left on the stutter.

It did not stay until the stone could start on it. When the gap came, it went. It went out into the grey cloud-scraped morning and stood on the downhill side for a while, dripping, with the roar behind it and its ribs still humming.

---

## Afterward

The visitors who stayed a full day and night that season came out and said, among themselves, in the valley, that there had been a new sound in the word.

They could not describe it. No one ever can. It was a syllable that did not sound like cold or light or green or hands. One of them said it was like the sound a very large thing makes when it is trying to be the size of a person. Another said, no, it was more like being thanked by someone who had come a long way to do it. A third said nothing at all and went back up the mountain the next spring.

The syllable was there for a few stutters, and then the word went on to something else, the way it always does.

---

## What It Does Now

It does one more thing, and it will keep doing it.

It walks the line of spires. It glances; it does not stare, because even old stone can be claimed by a stare. It listens to the sealed syllables in each one: the stumps in the scree, the needles three people tall, the ones foolish people have broken open. It is, as far as it knows, the only listener who has heard the whole sentence so far, from the first pale knob to the roaring room.

The ribbon does not need this. The word was never addressed to anyone. But the power does it anyway, for the same reason it read the cords.

It has made a promise. It did not make it aloud, because that is not done, and it did not tie it in a cord, because the cord would be taken. It made the promise in the way a power makes promises, by deciding.

When the fire under the mountain is finally too cool, and the last Throat narrows to a needle and closes, and the ribbon lets the last of itself go still and there is nowhere left to go down to, the power will be there. It will stand on the downhill side, soaked, counting the last stutters. It will hear the final syllable. It will not stop the ending, and it will not finish the word, and it will not say what the word meant.

But it will have heard all of it. A thing that spoke one long word alone in the dark for longer than there have been people will have been listened to, once, from beginning to end.

That is all. The power decided that was enough. It is not sure it was right. It thinks this is probably the correct way to feel.

---

It has been back since. Everyone who returns says the same thing, and so does it:

The room is smaller than it remembered.

It stands outside with its back to the mountain, soaked through, and waits for the count.
