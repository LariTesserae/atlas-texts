# The Second Day

## How It Came

It did not come down the rift the way the flood does. It did not come as light, because the basin already has more strange light than it needs. It came the way a hand arrives on a shoulder: by the time you notice it, it has been there a while.

For a long time after it arrived, nothing in the Lowworks changed. That is the first thing to understand about it.

## What It Did First, Which Was Nothing

It listened.

Listening in the Lowworks is not a gentle act. The beam engines thud. The intakes shriek. The cables hold their shifting chord, and under everything lies the white floor of the rain. The power let all of it through, and kept letting it through, until the noise stopped being noise and became structure. It learned where each sound came from, what each one was doing, and what each one was afraid of.

It watched the tenders. It watched them die by the hundreds at the boiler, by the thousands at the grilles, and by the fifth of a heartbeat at the cables. It did not save a single one. This was not coldness. It did not yet know what they were for, and a power that acts before it understands is only a larger flood.

It stood outside the stutter, so for it the loops added up. It watched the same flower by the pumping station open forty thousand times. It watched the same column fall and stand and fall until it knew the column's grain better than the column did. Nothing in the basin could remember, so it remembered for all of them. It did this at first for no purpose, the way you might hum along to a song before you know you are learning it.

## The Things It Did Not Do

It considered its options, and it considered them honestly, because it was able to do any of them.

It could stop the engines. But the engines were holding the door. The whole grid of pumps, pylons, and cables ran toward the fissure like the fingers of a hand braced against something. Knock the hand away and the door would open onto water with no air above it. That would not be a birth. It would be a drowning with extra steps.

It could drain the basin. One gesture would do it, and there would be air everywhere. But the green would die in that air, and the green was the larder. A child born into a dry, dead basin is born into famine. And the Lowworks was someone's house, however strange. You do not pour out a house to prove you are strong.

It could reach down and pull. It knew enough about labors to know that the ones that are pulled go wrong. It also knew that no one had asked it to.

It could leave. This it weighed most carefully of all, because the loop contains a real mercy. Nothing in it accumulates. The one in the fissure has never pushed twice. It has never grown tired, never despaired, never learned that the last thousand pushes failed. Each time is the first time. There are torments far worse than one that forgets itself instantly.

But pain that does not accumulate is still pain every time it happens. And the one in the fissure lived only at the hardest moment of a life, forever, and never at the moment after, which is the moment the hard one exists for. A push that never counts is not a labor. It is a single effort, repeated without end.

Then there were the tenders. Every one of them, in every beat, spent part of the only instant it would ever have turning south. The power had watched this millions of times. It understood that this was the closest thing the Lowworks had to a vote.

## The Tender Who Was Asked

Still, it wanted to ask someone.

It chose a tender at the largest boiler, hunched and heavy, fizzing at the hands. The figure formed on the downstroke, laid its palm against the plate, found the fault it already knew was there, and turned the valve its quarter turn. The beam began to rise and the pressure began to return. Every tender before it had died at this point.

The power held the void open. It did not seize the tender. It cupped it, the way you cup a match in wind.

For the first time in the history of the basin, something had a before.

The tender did not know what to do with it. Its shape held one task, and the task was done. It shuddered. Its bubbles strained against a pressure they had never had to survive. The power felt that the tender was frightened, if a standing wave can be frightened. It had no word for *after*, and it was in one.

Then it finished turning its head.

Every tender before it had turned only partway before collapsing. This one completed the motion and faced fully south, toward the rail and the fissure and the glow. It stayed that way. The power asked it, without words, the only question worth asking: *What do you want?*

The tender had a shape and one memory. It raised the fizzing hand it used for finding faults, and it pointed.

The power thanked it, and let it go gently. The tender collapsed more quietly than any tender ever had, a single soft tick instead of gravel on iron. It was the only worker in the Lowworks that had ever lived long enough to be thanked.

## Down

The power went down along the rail line, past the wagons creeping south one jolt at a time, past the lip where the blocks tip into the glow, into heat that turned from orange to yellow. It went down between walls of delivered basalt, laid block on block like the lining of a kiln, to the narrowest place.

The pale curve was there, like wet paper, like new bark that had never been rained on.

The power laid against it whatever served it as a hand. Nothing had touched the curve before except stone.

Then the floor jolted, and the power felt what a jolt is from the inside. It was not tectonic, and it was not mechanical. It was a push, blind and enormous, aimed at something it could not picture.

Beneath the push, threaded through the loop that held it, the power felt something else. It was fear, and it did not belong to the child. It belonged to the engines, or to whoever the engines had been built by, or to whoever the tenders were sketches of. The power could not tell which of these it was, and it did not need to. It knew the shape of the fear. The loop had not been built by an enemy. It was a held breath, made by someone who loved what was coming and could not bear to let it arrive into drowning. The whole Lowworks was hands braced against a door, because the room behind it was not ready and might never be.

The power had met this fear before, in other places and other forms. It is one of the oldest fears there is. It is also, almost always, answered the same way. You do not argue with it. You make it unnecessary.

## What It Did

It did two things. Both were small.

**First, it remembered.**

The loop worked because nothing had happened. Every push was erased and started over as the first push, so none of them had ever been part of anything. The power stood outside the stutter, where time adds up, and began to count. It did not count aloud, because nothing in the basin could have heard it. It held each push after the push was over. It kept the yesterday that the basin could not keep.

*One. Again.*

*Two. Again.*

This is what midwives do, in every world that has them. They say *again*. They tell the one who is laboring that the last push counted, even when it felt like nothing, so that the next one is not a first attempt but a continuation.

The loop, which ran on nothing having happened, began to find that something had. It did not break. It began, very slightly, to fill, the way the basin never did.

**Second, it made one dry place.**

It did not drain anything. It rose up through the canopy, through the churning seam of foam, into the downpour, and bent itself over the south end of the rift. It made its body a roof over a hollow about the size of one breath, just above the surface, warm and still.

The rain fell on it.

For the first time, rain in the Lowworks landed on something and did not go back into the sea. It struck the power's back and was held there. Water that had circled through the pipes and the sky since before the rock was rock finally had somewhere to go.

Beneath the power there was air. It was not a bubble, and it did not collapse. It stayed.

The power had already decided that if the engines fought the roof, it would withdraw. It would not win against the hands that were holding the door, because winning that fight would mean breaking them. It waited to see what they would do.

## The Engines

Nothing told the engines. They are iron and furnace and have no way to be told. But every pipe in the Lowworks runs toward the fissure, and the pumps had spent their entire existence pulling against a sky that gave everything back. Now they felt a place that did not.

At the largest station the suction at the intakes eased, only by a hair. The ferns bent a little less toward the grilles. The great beam rocked on its pivot and came down a fraction slower than it ever had. It did not stop. It slowed the way a held breath slows just before it is let out.

In the lee of the boiler, the flower opened, and stayed open.

## The Push That Went Through

The power counted.

It will not say how many pushes there were. The number belongs to the one who made them. It was not small. The power held every one, and said *again* after every one, and kept its back bent under the rain. None of this was difficult for it. It was simply long, and it was willing to take as long as the labor took.

Then the floor jolted, and the knock did not close.

The basin rang with it, trees and machines and water, and kept ringing, because the knock was no longer being erased. It was being continued. The rock gave. The delivered blocks shifted in their kiln-courses. The pale curve moved. It moved past the lip, past the rail, into the water column. There, in one of the bands where down is negotiable, the water lifted it gently upward the way the loose gravel had always been lifted. The Lowworks, it turned out, had been built with a way up.

It rose through the moss, the ferns, and the green cathedral dusk, and through the canopy the trees had woven just under the foam. It came up into the one dry hollow in the world, under the bent shape of something that had been waiting for it.

It took a breath.

It was the only sound in the entire basin that was not loud.

## Yesterday

The Lowworks had its first past. It was one breath long: *the moment before the breath.* Everything else followed from it, as the old account had guessed it would.

The flood came down the rift from the north one last time: a wall of foam and silt and torn green, flattening the ferns and slamming against the engine houses. This time it finished. It reached the south end, spent itself, and for the first time became something that had happened. The water level, which had always been exactly full and always being filled, simply stood where it was. After a while it went down a little.

The pumps wound down one at a time, the small ones first, their shrieking dropping into groans and then into ticking. The live cables hummed lower. The rain thinned. It thinned slowly, over hours, from white streaming into ordinary rain. Then, above the sea, there was a layer. It was grey and moist and full of drifting mist, but it was air, a gap between the sea and the sky. The Lowworks had never had one.

The tallest basalt columns broke the surface. They were black and faceted and still sharp enough to ring. They were the first dry stone. The first ordinary rain ran down them in threads and, too slowly for any eye, began to wear them.

## The Tenders

At the moment of the breath, the tenders who were mid-stroke did not collapse.

There were thousands of them, all over the basin: hunched ones at the boilers, long ones on the chain, branching ones at the frays, glittering clouds at the grilles. Each of them suddenly had a past one stroke long, and none of them had been shaped to carry it.

The power stayed with them a long time. It was the part of the work it had been most careful about.

Some of them let go on their own. They collapsed softly, the way the one who was asked had collapsed, and the power did not stop them. A thing made for one beat may be finished with one beat, and it was not the power's place to insist otherwise.

Some kept working. Something new happened to them: they got better at it. A boiler tender turned its valve a second time, and the second turn was smoother than the first, because it remembered the first. A cable tender spliced a fray and then, having seen how it had done it, spliced the next one faster. They were learning. It frightened them. Being able to improve means you have been worse than you are, and they had never been anything before. The power did not take that fear away. It did not think it should. It stayed near them while they felt it, which is sometimes all that can be done for a fear, and is enough.

Most of them went south.

They went toward what they had spent every beat of the basin's history turning to face. They climbed down the rail and up the columns and gathered on the new stone around the one who had been born. It lay on the wet black rock, blinking at a rain it had never felt before.

It had a long head and a bent back. At the ends of its limbs were fine, articulate fingers, small and damp and trembling.

The tenders looked at it, and it looked at them. Whether they were its rough sketches, or its parents, or its own shape rehearsed in advance by the engines, the power did not say. It is possible the tenders could now tell. They stayed.

## Hunger

The first thing the child did after breathing, and breathing again, and breathing a hundred times without anyone holding the air in place, was to reach for a leaf.

It was one of the broad, translucent leaves from a crown that had surfaced when the water fell. It bit it.

In the whole history of the Lowworks, a leaf had never been bitten.

The power watched this with a grave kind of joy, because it understood what it was seeing. A bitten leaf is a leaf that was here before the bite. It is a larder that has begun to be used. It is also the beginning of the end of a place where nothing dies. Someday something would die here. There would be bones in the silt and shells under the moss, and tracks in the mud that led somewhere and then stopped.

The power grieved for that in advance, briefly and completely. Then it let it be. A world where nothing ever dies because nothing is ever born is not a mercy. It is only a waiting room.

## What It Left

It did not stay as the roof.

When the child had breathed on its own long enough that the breathing was clearly the child's and not a gift being held open, the power straightened and lifted away. The thinned, ordinary rain fell on the child for the first time.

It did not drown. It squinted, turned its long head toward the sky, and breathed the wet air.

The engines had been built to make one dry place, and now there was one. It was not in the sky and not above the foam. It was inside the child, in a chest that filled and emptied. It was air carried inside a living thing: the only kind of dry place that can walk, and the only kind that lasts.

Most of the engines went quiet. The largest beam engine the power left alone. It still rocks on its pivot, slowly now, well below the pace of a heart. The tenders who stayed tend it, and they get a little better at it every day. It no longer pumps anything anywhere. The child sleeps against the warm brick of the engine house, in the thud that was the first sound it ever felt, and no one has decided that the engine needs any other purpose.

The power left one more thing, the only thing it had really brought: the count. It had kept the basin's yesterday until the basin could keep its own. Then it handed the yesterday back and went away. It did not do this all at once. It went the way it had arrived, so that by the time anyone noticed the hand was no longer on their shoulder, it had been gone a while.

The rock is wearing. The moss holds a small handprint that is filling back in slowly, because the moss has stopped keeping pace and started simply growing. The flood arrived once, finished, and is now a story the tenders know how to tell, because they remember it.

The power did not make the Lowworks a paradise. It did not drain the sea, or break the engines, or pull anyone into the world. It made the basin's first day end, and so it gave the basin a second one.

That was the whole of the gift. Everything else, the Lowworks will have to do itself, and now it can.
