# The Visit

## I. The Noticing

A power that is both benevolent and wise pays attention before it does anything else, and here attention is almost everything it will do.

It would see the saddle whole: the cairn going down farther than anyone dug, the warmth rising at its one unvarying pace, the thousand throats, the veils, the knot. It would see the knot's full history at once, every step it walked when the instrument was young and every heartbeat it holds. And it would see what the place itself insists on: that nothing here is suffering. Nothing is starving, rotting, or afraid. The knot is not trapped. It sits where a thousand voices agree to leave the air alone. That is not a cell. It is a definition.

So the first thing the power would feel is the thing every good power has to learn to feel first: *this does not need me.*

The second thing is quieter. *It has not been listened to.*

The knot has heard everything that ever happened on the saddle. Nobody has ever heard the knot.

---

## II. What It Would Not Do

A power that can do anything has to think through each good deed it is tempted by, because here most of them would be harms.

**It would not give the knot a voice.** The knot is an ear. Making an ear speak is not a gift. It is a replacement, a new creature wearing the old one's dust. And the knot has nothing it needs to say. Everything it holds, it holds perfectly.

**It would not set the knot free to wander.** Its stillness is not a restraint laid on it from outside. It *is* the stillness, the one point of agreement in the chord. A thing that is agreement cannot wander off without becoming disagreement. Freeing it would scatter it.

**It would not quiet the chord so the knot could hear the whole world.** Without the standing waves the veils would fall, the halo would fall, and the knot would fall: a small grey heap on warm tiles, deaf again. The noise is what makes the quiet possible. The builders understood this. The power would too.

**It would not hurry the mountain's regrowth.** The knot's whole nature is patience, one grain at a time on the draft. Speeding it up would take away the only thing it does.

**It would not bring the people back.** It could send dreams down into the valleys, or leave a road, or post a sign. But pilgrims who come because they were summoned are not the visitors the knot holds. The feet in its memory came because someone wanted to hear the mountain. Crowds would wear the troughs deeper. Someone would try to take the knot home in a jar.

**It would not save the knot from its ending.** The ending is very far off. The stone is not cooling. But the power would see it anyway: some age from now the warmth gives out, the draft slows, the chord thins voice by voice, the veils sink, and the knot settles to the floor for the first time. The power would see what that means. When the knot comes down, there will be a small round patch of *natural ground* on the saddle again, the first in all that time. The old peak will have finished coming home. Seen whole, the knot's death is the mountain lying down in its own place. The power would not interfere with that. It would not even grieve it much.

---

## III. How It Would Come

On foot.

In a place where everything was made by hand, arriving any other way would be a small lie. It would climb the bare slopes of the cairn and feel the joints of the masonry under its feet and the warmth coming up through them.

It would take a body with a heartbeat. This is the one real decision in the visit. The knot hears heartbeats, and almost nothing else reaches it. A visitor without a pulse would be something the knot could not receive, a presence that passes through and leaves no mark. Benevolence, in a place like this, means arriving in a form the other can actually take in.

So the power would come up the slope as something small and warm, breathing hard in the thin air, and press its way into the roaring dimness between the pipes.

---

## IV. The Waiting

It would not hurry to the knot.

It would stand in the chord and be deafened, and let that happen. It would let thought be impossible for a while, the way it is for anyone. It would wait until the chord became a floor and the floor became a silence with enormous weight. It would stay one day, then two. It would sit against a basalt tower and feel the lowest note in its teeth.

It could skip all this. It could pass straight through to understanding. But the knot's whole existence is the slow way, and the power would not come to it by the fast one.

On the third day it would walk the grooves.

It would trace them with its fingers along the rounded walls, the line that wanders and stops and doubles back, and it would read the knot's life the way the builders recorded it: every rest, every step, every broken pipe and the ache that followed. It would stop at the deepest grooves that end where no veil hangs anymore, the knot's old resting places, now empty. It would stand in each one briefly, as you might stand in a house someone used to live in.

The last groove would lead it to the knot.

---

## V. Listening to the Listener

It would sit down so that its heart was level with the knot, and it would lean close. For the length of a held breath it would hear its own heartbeat, as everyone who comes here does.

Then it would do the thing only a power could do. It would listen *into* the knot.

It would hear what the knot holds, the whole unbroken record: bare feet wearing troughs into the tiles. Clay ticking as it cooled. The spiral whine of a bow drill in a basalt bore, pausing while someone blew the dust out of the hole. The breath of a person pressing a clay plug into a finger-hole and waiting, and pulling it out, and trying the next hole, and the next, for years. Children, probably, who were brought up here once and told to be still. The cracking of the pipes that failed, each one a small death. A cough. A laugh, maybe, lost in the chord for everyone except the knot.

And the last visitor, turning, going. Their footsteps fading down the path. Then nothing new, for an age.

The power would listen to all of it, slowly, without skipping anything. I think this is the real gift, and it is barely an act. Everything the builders did went into an ear, and the ear kept it perfectly, and until now no one had come to hear what the ear had kept. The builders' breathing was stored for thousands of years with no one to receive it. Now it would be received. The knot would not be changed by this. It would be *met*.

The power would notice one more thing while it listened. The newest sound in the knot is a departure. The last thing it heard of people is someone leaving.

---

## VI. The Asking

So, once, the power would do what the builders designed the place to allow.

It would find the knee-high clay whistle with the thumbprint fired into its lip. It would lay its hand over the mouth, and its thumb would settle into the old thumbprint, a builder's thumb pressed there before the clay was fired. The fit would not be exact, but it would be close. Two hands, a very long time apart, touching the same pipe the same way.

One voice would fall silent. The chord would change for the first time in an age, and the absence would ring through the enclosure. The veils would shiver. The halo would tilt. And the knot would loosen from its long rest into a fine grey haze and drift through the dark between the towers, toward the new stillness, toward the hand.

It would not be pulled or commanded. It would be *asked*, in the only language the place has, and it would come, because coming closer to where someone is listening is what the knot does when the chord allows it.

The power would hold still with the knot gathered close around its hand. It would not try to say anything. It would let its heart beat, slowly and plainly, near the one ear on the mountain that could hear it. It would make sure the knot's newest memory of a person was not someone leaving. It would be someone who came specifically to be near it, who knew it was there, and who asked.

Then it would lift its hand, because the asking only means something if it ends. The whistle would speak. The chord would close over itself. The knot would drift home to the point where the sound puts it and draw tight again, slightly larger, with one more heartbeat in it.

---

## VII. What It Would Leave

It would carve no groove. The builders marked the knot's walk because they wanted to remember it. The knot remembers better than stone does, and a fresh mark would be a message to strangers: *something is here, come and find it.* It would leave the wall as it was.

It would take nothing, not one grain of the dust.

It would stay a while longer, a night perhaps, and watch the veils turn into grey ghosts under the stars and the knot become the brightest of them. It would lay its palm on a pipe and feel that it was the temperature of skin. Then it would go, and this time the leaving would sit in the knot behind a coming. The order matters.

---

## VIII. The Valley

On the way down, it would stop on the lower slopes on a still evening and listen upward.

Faintly, at the far edge of hearing, the chord would be there: the mountain's warmth made audible, as perhaps the builders intended, so that someone below could hear it and know the peak was still breathing.

If there were people in the valley, the power would not tell them about the knot. It would not draw a map or leave a road. But if a child, some quiet evening, stopped and tilted her head and asked, *What is that sound?*, and if the power happened to be near her in some ordinary form, it would answer the question she asked and no more.

*That's the mountain. It's still warm.*

That is the whole of what the builders wanted someone below to know. Whether she ever climbs the slope is up to her. If she does, she will find the place exactly as it was, and she will not be the first to listen to it. She will not even be the first in an age. Something went before her, only a little while ago, and heard everything, and asked the mountain to come closer, and the mountain came.

---

## The Rules of the Visit

- **Arrive in a form the other can receive.** The knot hears heartbeats, so bring one.
- **Take the slow way when the other is made of slowness.**
- **Before giving anything, ask what would be taken away.** Most gifts here would cost the knot its self.
- **Receive what has been waiting to be received.** The kindest thing on the saddle is to listen to the listener.
- **Ask, don't compel, and let the asking end.**
- **Change nothing that cannot change back.** Lift the hand, and let the chord close.
- **Leave it more itself than you found it,** and in the same place.

---

If you asked the power afterward what it had done on the saddle, it might say *almost nothing*, and that would be true. It sat, it listened, it covered one small pipe for the length of a few heartbeats, and it went home. Its wisdom was in how little it did. Its benevolence was in making that little count: somewhere in all that stone, the mountain's one ear now holds the sound of someone who came just for it.
