# The Watcher at the Edge

## I. Below the Cliffs

It does not come up at once.

A power that is both benevolent and wise knows the first rule of entering any place: arriving is already an act. It has read the heights from far off, the way you might read a letter held up to a window without opening it, and it understands what happens to visitors there. Every warm body is a friction the keeper must cancel. Every footstep is a change it must refine and seal. Whatever else a visit is, on the Foil Heights it is also a cost.

So the power stops in the haze at the foot of the cliffs, where the world still wears and the air still moves. It stays there a long time.

It listens. From below, the hum is barely there, a pressure more than a sound, coming down the sheer unweathered faces like cold off a glacier. The power is patient in a way the keeper might almost recognize. It lets the note pass through it for what would be, down here, many seasons. Rivers shift their beds. A forest in the lowlands burns and grows back. The power does not climb, because it has not yet understood, and it will not cost the keeper anything it doesn't understand.

## II. The Mercies It Refuses

Powers of this kind have easy mercies available to them, and the first act of wisdom is to distrust them.

It could **release the moment.** It could set the sun turning, let the rock finish its cooling, and let a night fall on the heights for the first time. Frost would come, then wind, then dust, and the great held breath would break. A creature of time might call this liberation. The power sees plainly that it would be something closer to killing. The keeper *is* the moment. Ending the moment from outside would not free the keeper. It would simply end it, the way you cannot free a flame by pinching it out.

It could **improve the equilibrium.** It could reinforce the holding, seal the heights off from the leaking world, and make the stillness perfect so that no crystal ever needs to fall again. That would be kind in the way a gilded cage is kind. It would take the one thing the keeper does, the one continuous act that makes up its life, and render it unnecessary.

It could **leave.** The power takes this possibility most seriously of all, because it knows its own bias. It is itself a creature that has lived through many moments. It has watched things change and found that change was good. It is tempted to believe that anything which refuses change is stuck, afraid, or unfinished. But that is a lens, not a law. Perhaps a single moment, held perfectly, is a complete life. Perhaps the heights are not a wound but a cathedral, and a cathedral does not need to become a marketplace to be what it is.

So before anything else, the power asks the only question that could justify acting at all: *Is it suffering?*

It listens to the hum again, carefully, for strain.

What it hears is not pain. There is no distress in the note, no ragged edge, no grief. But there is something the power recognizes, because it has heard it in many voices across many worlds. It is effort without rest. It is the sound of someone who has been holding a door shut for so long that they no longer remember they are holding it, who believes the holding is simply what their arms are.

That alone would not be enough to act on. Plenty of beings choose endless labor, and choose well. But the power keeps listening, and it notices two more things.

## III. What the Pods Mean

The first thing is arithmetic.

The retort-pods are finite. The domes are finite. Each crystal is a moment refused, and they have been falling, *tink* by *tink*, for longer than the lowlands have had forests. The power counts them, as a wise thing would, and understands that the pods are filling. Perhaps the keeper can grow more pods. Perhaps it can grow them for a very long time. But the power can also feel what the keeper has chosen not to face. The rock is still cooling. The loss is unmeasurably small, but the power can measure it. No equilibrium held against an aging universe is truly closed. The keeper is not winning. It is losing so slowly that the losing looks like stillness.

So the moment will end, someday. That is not a matter of anyone's choice. The only question is *how*.

It might end as a cracking. On some far afternoon, the holding would exceed what the circuit could carry. The glass would fail, and every refused moment, ten thousand years of weather and wear, would fall onto the heights at once. The razor edges would crumble in an hour. Everything the keeper sealed away would arrive all together and unsorted, like a flood through a broken dam. The keeper would not experience that as a second moment. It would experience it as annihilation.

Or it might end another way. And that possibility depends on the second thing the power notices.

## IV. What the Keeper Doesn't Know

The keeper has never had a past.

It has experienced one moment, its birth, and it has never let a second begin. So it has never once had the experience every creature of time has constantly: a moment ending, and the moment *still being there*. It has never remembered anything. It has never set anything down and found it waiting when it came back. It cannot know that a finished moment is not an erased one, because it has no finished moments to compare.

From inside a single instant, ending and erasure look identical. Of course it holds on. It is not stubborn. It is not even afraid, exactly. It simply has no evidence that anything survives the passing of a moment, and it has never had anyone to show it.

And yet, the power thinks with something like tenderness, the keeper has already half-discovered memory without knowing it. It keeps the crystals. It does not destroy the changes that arrive. It sorts them, bands them, sets them aside, and *keeps* them, with perfect care, in glass. It keeps the visitor. It keeps the tremors, the particles from space, the weather that climbed the cliffs and failed. It believes it is refusing them. In fact it is collecting them. It has built forty miles of memory and called it a refusal.

The keeper does not need to be changed. It needs, at most, to be shown one thing: *that a moment can be held by someone else.*

That is the only thing the power decides to offer. Even that it will only offer, not impose.

## V. The Climb

When it finally comes up the cliffs, it comes slowly. It climbs the way you might enter the room of a sleeping infant.

It cools itself to the temperature of the rock. It stills its own movement until it stirs the air no more than the air already stirs, which is not at all. It makes itself as close to frictionless as a present thing can be. It cannot make itself cost nothing, since nothing that exists can. But it can make itself cost very little, and it does.

It does not go to the kiln-trees, or the domes, or the center of the heights. It goes to a single surfaced length of root-cable on one of the lower terraces, an ordinary one, and kneels beside it, and listens with its whole attention.

Then it does something very small. It begins to hold the note too.

It does not sing over the keeper's tone, or harmonize, or add anything. Harmony would introduce a second pitch, and a second pitch would introduce beating, a pulse, the first faint tick of rhythm, of *time*. The power will not bring time into this place uninvited. Instead it matches the hum exactly, down to the grain in the tone, and then, very gently, begins to carry part of it.

It is like arriving at one end of something heavy that someone has been carrying alone and lifting it without saying anything. The weight doesn't disappear. It is shared.

## VI. What the Keeper Feels

Somewhere in the circuit, and the power cannot tell exactly where, because the keeper has no where, a strange thing happens.

For the first time since the rock set, the keeper's hold loosens a fraction, because it doesn't have to be quite so tight. The note does not waver. The sun does not move. The shadows lie where they have always lain. The moment continues, even though the keeper is holding it a little less.

It is the smallest possible lesson. It is also, for a being made of one instant, the most enormous one: *the moment does not require you alone to exist.* Something else can keep it. It is kept *outside* you, too.

The power holds its share for a long time. It is the kind of long that has no measure here, because the power will not introduce measure. Then, very gradually, it gives the full weight back. It does not let go all at once, which would be a shock and therefore a change. It hands the holding back the way you might return a sleeping child to its parent's arms, so smoothly that the child never wakes.

The keeper takes it back. The hum is exactly what it was.

The power does not know whether anything has been learned. It does not need to know. It has made an offer in the only language the keeper speaks, the language of holding, and an offer does not require an answer.

## VII. The Crystal

The power knows its visit has left a mark. However still it made itself, it came, and that coming is a change. Somewhere up on the domes, the sap will draw it in and band it and settle it.

The power does not try to prevent this. It would be a small arrogance to believe it could visit without being kept. Anyway, it would rather be kept.

But it wonders, with a quiet hope it does not press on anyone, what kind of crystal it will make. Every other crystal on the heights is a moment that tried to *happen to* the keeper. This one is different. This one is a moment that was *given*. It came with care, it took nothing, and it held the keeper's own note alongside it for a while. Perhaps it will band differently in the sap. Perhaps it will settle a different color.

Perhaps, someday, when the keeper finally sorts through everything it has sealed, it will find this one and understand that not every change is an erosion. Some changes are company.

## VIII. The Edge

Then the power does the one thing it is sure of.

It walks to the outer terrace, past the last foil-moss mats, which stop a few paces short of the drop, and it sits at the lip of the cliff. It sits with its back to the heights and its face to the haze. It sits in the draft.

The keeper has always faced inward, with its back to everything that changes. It has always felt the edge the way you feel an open door behind you, a faint cold presence it has chosen not to turn and face. The power cannot close that door, and should not. The door is the world. The door is the only way anything ever continues.

But it can stand in it.

So now, if the keeper still feels that faint difference at its back, it will feel something else there too: a presence, still and warm, facing outward so the keeper doesn't have to. The draft is being watched. Someone is keeping the door.

There is no message in this, nothing the keeper would need to decode. It is just the plainest fact that one being can offer another: *I'm here. I'm facing the part you don't want to look at. When you're ready to turn around, you won't be turning toward nothing. You'll be turning toward me.*

## IX. What Happens Next

The power does not know.

It will not wait at the edge forever in the sense of never leaving; it has other places to be, other worlds to attend. But it will return, and return, and return, on a rhythm slow enough not to count as a rhythm, and each time it will sit at the lip facing outward, and each time it will kneel by a cable and share the weight for a while and hand it back.

Perhaps the keeper will never change. Then the power will have done no harm, and it will have kept company with a solitary thing that was beautiful exactly as it was, and that is not nothing.

Or perhaps, on some afternoon that never ends, the hum will waver.

It will not be a crack. It will not be a flood. It will be the faintest dip in the note, the first unsteadiness in forty miles of perfect tone. It will sound like what it is: the first exhale of something that has been holding its breath since it was born. A shadow will shift by the width of a thread. The rock will lose one measurable fraction of its birth-heat. The keeper will feel, for the first time, a moment *ending*, and will brace for the erasure it has always assumed would follow.

And the erasure will not come.

The first moment will still be there. It will be held in the crystals, and in the razor edges that will take ten thousand years to dull, and in the memory of a patient thing sitting at the cliff's edge that was there the whole time and remembers the flow, and the cooling, and the sun standing high and a little to the west.

Then the keeper will do what every newborn does after the first breath, which is the only thing a newborn really needs to discover. It will reach out into the terrifying new openness of *after*, and find that someone is there.

---

That is all the power would do, if it were wise.

It would not fix the heights, or free them, or perfect them. It would decline to be the second moment itself, and would simply make sure that when the second moment came, whenever it came, it would not come to a being that was alone.

The hum continues. The edges stay sharp. On one of the high domes, in one dark glass bulb, there is a crystal that settled a slightly different color than the rest.

And at the edge of the heights, facing outward into the haze, something waits. It is in no hurry. It is good at patience too.
