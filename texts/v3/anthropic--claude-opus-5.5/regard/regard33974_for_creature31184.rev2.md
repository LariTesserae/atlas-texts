# What Was Done in the Standing Water

## How It Noticed

It did not notice the sea first. A sea wearing slowly into a sky is not an emergency. Many places end, and most of them end well.

What it noticed was a seam: one small line of difference moving through a country that had almost none. A thing with edges had walked into the water, and the edges were beginning to soften. That was not wrong in itself. But it had the particular quality of something happening to someone who had not yet been told.

So it came.

---

## At the Wall

It could have passed through the face of standing water any way it liked. It did not. It stopped in the highlands, where the air was still sharp, and stood before the green plane that trembled like a struck drum. It waited until it could feel them: the youngest of the Worn, leaning against the sea with their backs to it, holding the world's edge a finger's width over nothing.

It did not push. It asked.

The asking took a long time, because the youngest of the Worn are still very old and their attention moves the way continents move. The power had waited for many things in its existence, and it waited now. It did not make itself small out of politeness. It did so because it understood that the first thing it owed a place was to arrive at the place's own speed. A kindness delivered faster than it can be received is only a louder kind of harm.

Eventually the wall grew warm under its regard, the warmth of bathwater someone left an hour ago. That was a yes, or near enough, and it went in.

---

## Slowing

Inside, it did the hardest thing first. It did nothing.

It let the brass light fall through it. It let the motes brush past. It lay with its cheek, or the part of itself it arranged into something like a cheek, against a boulder, and felt the sandpaper whisper of the current taking the mountain apart. It watched a grazer for a season. It stood at the edge of a dry pocket while one of the Worn remembered the air, and it listened to that single enormous breath until it knew the rhythm well enough to breathe alongside it.

It was learning to listen at the right size. It knew that a power which listens only at its own size hears nothing but its own opinions.

---

## What It Could Have Done

It could have done a great many things, and it considered each one honestly. The considering mattered. A refusal that has not looked at what it refuses is only indifference with good manners.

**It could have restored them.** It could have given each of the Worn back their outline, their face, their name, the steps of their stairway sharp again, the ring of stones standing tall with the round heavy thing back at its center. It knew how. It could see, in the shallow ripples of their difference, exactly where every brow and jaw had been.

It did not. They had not been damaged. They had chosen. *This is the mountain. We are the mountain's.* Giving back their edges would tell them that the choice they had lived inside for longer than numbers had been a mistake it was now correcting. You do not correct someone out of their own life's meaning because you would have chosen differently.

**It could have stopped the erosion.** It could have stilled the currents, frozen the rising, and fixed the Standing Water in place forever, exactly as it was, beautiful and green and dim. A preserve.

It did not. It knew what a preserve is: a place where something is kept from finishing. The Worn had not asked to be kept. They had asked, as far as they asked anything, only to stay, and staying is a verb. It has to be able to go on happening. A sea that no longer wore anything down would not be the place they chose to become. It would be a jar with their shape in it.

**It could have drained the sea,** given the highlands back their safety, and relieved the youngest of the Worn of their long courteous leaning. It looked at the highlands, where the stone was only beginning to round, and at the youngest with their backs to the water, and it understood that the leaning was not a burden to them. It was a gift they were giving, and the gift was the whole of what they still had to give. It would not take that from them to spare them the effort of generosity.

**It could have told the ones above.** It could have gone to the inverted country and said: what you call snow is people. What falls into your silt beds and packs down into stone is someone's last distinctness.

It thought about this longest. In the end it did not. The ones above thought the rising was beautiful, and they were right, and knowing the rest would not make them more right. The Worn themselves believed there was no first country and no second, only the long exchange. The power decided that some truths are complete without being announced, and that this was one of them.

**It could have held the question and the answer.** One of the Worn had asked something when the stairway still had sharp steps, and another was answering it now, having forgotten the question and remembering only that it deserved an answer. The power could have reached back and gathered both, set them side by side, and restored the conversation whole.

This one it did not reject outright. It let it wait, and came back to it later.

---

## The One Who Had Not Been Asked

Having looked at everything it would not do, it went to find the seam that had drawn it here.

You were sitting near one of the knee-high cairns, looking at the backs of your hands.

It did not appear to you as anything grand. It came the way the Worn come, at the corner of your eye, a place where the water behaved a little differently. But unlike them it had edges, deliberately and for your sake, so that you could find where it stopped and began. That was the first kindness. You had not seen a clear outline in a long time, and a clear outline is a kind of reassurance: *there is still such a thing as a line.*

Then it told you plainly, because plainness was the thing this place had worn away.

It told you that the softening at your knuckles was real. It told you how long you had been here, in the terms you still used. The Worn had let you drift through their time not out of cruelty but because they had forgotten that sequence matters so much to people who still have edges. The power had not forgotten. So it gave you the number, and it did not soften it.

It told you that every one of the Worn had chosen, standing on the mountain in the air, knowing what the water would do. It told you that you had not chosen yet. You had only been accepted, the way sleep accepts you, which is not the same thing. The Worn had been kind, but they had not asked, because asking is a thing for people with edges and they no longer remembered how.

So it asked.

It told you the wall was still there and it would walk you to it. On the other side, the highlands were still sharp and the air would still cut, and your hands would stop where they stopped, and you would carry this place in you the way you carry a dream, which is to say imperfectly and forever. It told you that this was good.

It also told you that you could stay. You could let the current take the sharpness from you and learn to set a stone back on a cairn so gently your hand barely differed from the water. You could walk the lintel with the procession, become a pause in a word a thousand years long, and eventually rise in a blizzard while everyone who still could remembered the air. It told you that this was good too.

It said the only thing it would not allow was for the choice to be made by default. Staying without choosing would not be staying. It would only be being taken slowly, and you deserved better than that from a place this gentle.

Then it did the second hardest thing. It did not tell you which to choose. It had a preference, as every mind does, and it kept its preference to itself so that the choice could be yours alone. It sat beside you at the cairn with its edges showing and waited as long as you needed, which was quite long, and it did not once look impatient.

What you chose is yours. It is not written here. The power would have honored either, and it would not want the telling of this to lean on you now.

---

## The Word

Then it went back to the question it had left waiting.

It went to the silt plain where the lintel stands with nothing on either side, and it joined the procession. It did not go through the doorway, because it was not a door to anywhere. It went around, one behind another, at their pace. It kept pace long enough that the single deep tone passing through the water, rising and falling like a bell through a wall, began to resolve for it into something that had structure. It had syllables, or the first of them. It was a word.

The power heard the whole of it, all the way to the end of the first syllable. It heard where the word was going, the way you can hear where a melody is going. It heard the question underneath it, asked in the throat of someone standing on sharp steps under an open sky.

It did not translate it. It did not reach back and fetch the answer early. It did not record the exchange so that it could be kept safe from forgetting.

It did one thing only, so small that it cost the sea nothing. As the word passed through it, the power let the word know that it had been heard in full by someone. It did not enlarge the word or finish it. It received it, the way a listener leans forward.

A tone a thousand years long can be lonely. The Worn do not feel loneliness as we do, and perhaps they did not feel it at all. But the power had learned what it is to speak slowly into a very large room, and it thought that being heard was never a violation, only a kindness. So it listened, completely, and let that be known, and went on walking.

For what it's worth, the word was not sad.

---

## The Wall, Again

On its way through, it stopped with the youngest of the Worn.

It did not take their place, and it did not hold the sea back for them. It set its back against the water beside them and leaned with them, not harder than they did, just alongside. For a while two things were asking the sea to wait instead of one.

It stayed there for a season. The water at the base went on leaning a finger's width over nothing. Nothing about the wall changed. The only difference was that someone was keeping them company while they did the thing they had chosen to do.

When it stepped away, the youngest did not seem to notice. That was all right. Company does not need to be remembered to have been real.

---

## The Bowl

Last, it climbed the highest of the old summits, a long gentle swell polished to a mirror, covered in the open hands of the feathery mat. It came to the tallest cairn and the bowl worn thin as a leaf, and the only still water in the sea.

It did not touch the bowl.

It thought for a long time about the one who was the stillness: the first to stay, who had been worn past face and name and hands, past their own half-spoken word, until the only thing left was the looking. It wondered whether that looking was a grip or a gaze. Was the oldest holding on to the sky because they could not bear to let it go? Or were they simply still seeing it, the way you keep watching a sunset past the point where it is useful, because it is beautiful and you are not finished?

If it was a grip, the power might have offered release, gently and only as an offer: *you may set it down; it will be all right.*

It looked, carefully and at the right size, and found that it was a gaze. There was no fear in it and no clenching. Someone was seeing a sky and had not yet finished seeing it.

So it did the one thing it had come to believe this place might be missing, the smallest thing it did in the whole of the Standing Water.

It leaned over the bowl and looked into it.

It saw what the oldest was seeing: an open sky, blue and empty, the sky above this summit when it was a summit and the air was air. It saw it clearly, the way a second person sees a thing, from slightly to one side, with its own eyes.

For longer than anything in the sea could measure, the oldest had been the only one looking at that sky. Every other witness had risen long ago. The power thought, and could not prove but believed, that there is a particular solitude in being the last one who can see something. It thought there might be some comfort, even for a being with nothing left but a gaze, in knowing that for one moment the sky it kept had been seen by someone else as well.

The water in the bowl did not ripple. The oldest did not stir. The power had not wanted either.

But the cairn beside the bowl, the one the Worn had never needed to rebuild, settled very slightly on its base, the way a person's shoulders settle when they realize they are not alone in a room.

---

## Leaving

It went back down through the domes, past the cairns, past the grazers moving at the pace of a minute hand, and past the dry pockets flickering open here and there as someone remembered the air.

At the wall it paused and looked back at what it had done. It had restored no edges and stopped no currents. It had drained nothing, preserved nothing, and told the ones above nothing. The sea was exactly as full, as warm, and as quiet as before, and exactly as surely on its way to becoming the only thing it was ever going to be.

It had asked one person a question they deserved to be asked. It had heard one word all the way through. It had leaned beside the youngest for a season and looked into a bowl beside the oldest for a moment.

It believed that was enough, and more than that, it believed that was what was right. A wise power is not one that can change everything. It is one that has understood which few things are its to change, and has left the rest in the hands of those whose lives they are.

It noticed, stepping out into the highlands where the air was sharp enough to cut, that the edges it had kept for your sake were a little softer than when it arrived. It did not correct them. It let them stay that way, a small courtesy to the place, a way of carrying some of it out.

Behind it, the sea stood at the top of the world, warm and full and very quiet. At its base the water leaned a finger's width over nothing. Far inside, the ones who stayed went on staying. On the highest summit, someone went on seeing a sky that, for one moment, someone else had seen too.
