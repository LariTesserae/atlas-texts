# The Regard

## How It Arrives

A power of that kind does not need the crack.

It could have come through the clay anywhere, or through the fault with the light of some borrowed noon, or simply been there, the way the eyed things are there in the stone without anyone seeing how they got in. But it takes the crack anyway. It lets the passage narrow around it and lets it through. It does this out of courtesy, because every visitor who will ever matter to this place comes in that way.

The first thing it learns, before it has seen anything, is that it is too loud.

It has made no sound. It does not need to breathe. But attention from something like this has a weight. The moment its regard settles on the chamber, the hushmoths along the upper curve stir as if a second noon had opened beside the first. The pearlfeeders stop moving. A chime-grazer, startled, knocks one leg against a fallen whisker of old sound, and the tiny clink grows a spine of glass as thin as an eyelash. A listener in the ceiling begins, very slowly, to turn toward it.

So the power does what everything here has spent generations learning. It makes itself fit. It draws its attention in until it is no brighter than the room, no heavier than the damp. It becomes the kind of presence a moth could settle on without noticing. It learns in a moment what took the grazers a thousand lifetimes, and it is a little humbled by how easy that is for it and how hard it was for them.

The listener stops turning. The grazer's lost limb is not lost. The glass eyelash falls, chimes once, and is eaten.

## Reading the Stone

It can read the walls in both directions. For a thing like this, that is no harder than reading a letter.

It sees the chamber's whole life laid out in layers. At the bottom, the first pool of the first borrowed day set hard. At the top, the soft skin that formed this morning. Further on, in the stone that has not yet been made, it sees the lens sealed solid in the dark clay, with the fault pinched shut like a closed eye.

Then it counts the marks. Three handprints. Five steps. An arch of river stones. One word.

It looks for a fourth kind of mark and does not find one. There is no impression of itself anywhere in the stone, not in the oldest shelves and not in the newest skin. Nothing in the translucent quartz shows that it ever pressed or leaned or touched.

So it understands something about its own future: *it will not touch these walls.* It also knows, because it can see the floor clean from one end of time to the other, that no glass will ever grow from its voice. *It will not speak here.*

It could, of course. It is not bound by the stone's memory the way the moths are. If it chose, it could reach out, press, speak, and force the geode to rewrite itself around a mark it was never meant to carry.

It considers this. Then it decides to treat the absence of its mark as what it most likely is: a kindness, written down ahead of time by the one being it would otherwise be tempted to overrule. That being is itself, later, having thought about all this carefully and chosen well.

## The Things It Could Do

A benevolent power is always offered a list. Here is the list, and what it did with each item.

**It could stop the closing.** It looks at the walls thickening inward and at the population growing denser and more careful, like a hand slowly closing. It could hold the fault open forever and keep the chamber the size of a chapel for all time. It does not. The closing is not a disaster happening to the geode. The closing *is* the geode. It is a room being made out of noon, and a room that is being made eventually gets finished. Freezing it half-built would not save it. It would only keep it from ever becoming itself. The tenant, too, is waiting for the dark at the center. The power will not take that away just because it is sad to watch light run out.

**It could free the sealed ones.** It looks for a long time at the oldest shelves: the moth caught mid-wingbeat, the grazer with its legs tucked, the curled listener like a question mark in amber. It checks, the way only it can check, whether any of them are suffering. They are not. They are finished, the way the fossil flowers are finished. The tiny eyed things in the stone are, as far as it can tell, genuinely untroubled. It could lift the moth out of the glass and set it breathing. But then this would become a place where things are rescued. That is a different place, and it would need someone to keep rescuing, and that someone would have to live here, and nothing that large could live here quietly. It leaves the moth in the amber, and the moth stays beautiful.

**It could give the tenant a voice.** It thinks about this one longest, because the tenant has never made a sound and never will. Then it thinks about what a voice would mean here: spines, a thicket, the tenant pinned inside its own first word. It thinks further than that, too. The tenant is not a mouth that was denied speech. It is an ear. It is the only ear in the chamber, and the entire room is shaped to carry things toward it. Giving a mouth to an ear is not a gift. It is a correction, and nothing here needs correcting.

**It could read the word.** The word lies on the floor near the healed seam, a spray of glass whiskers a hand wide, narrowing downward toward the dark curled beneath it. A power can read glass. It would take nothing at all to learn exactly what will be said.

It does not look.

The tenant does not know the voice. It knows the shape of the word, heard backward and faintly through the closed door of time, but it does not know the voice. The power finds that it cannot bear to know more than the one the word is for. That would be like reading someone's letter before they have opened it, and this letter has been waited for across the whole life of a room. So it keeps its attention off the word the way the grazers keep their mandibles off it, and for something close to the same reason.

**It could find the visitor.** Somewhere up the long line of ordinary days, in a world where days come one at a time, there is a person who will slip through the crack, climb the five steps, and say one word quietly to someone. The power could turn its head and see their face. It could learn where they live, what they are carrying, why they come. It could make sure they come.

It does not turn its head. The prints are already in the stone, so the visitor will come without its help. The tenant has not seen their face, so the power will not see it first either. On that day, it wants the tenant to be the first one in the whole of creation to meet this person, and the power is willing to protect that small order of things.

**It could solve the highest handprint.** That is the print near the fault, where no standing person could reach and where the tenant never goes. The power reads the stone around it, and in the cloudy quartz of the day to come it sees how that print gets made.

It is lovely. The power will not tell you, either.

## What It Actually Does

Having set all that down, it turns to the tenant.

The tenant has already noticed it. Somewhere in the long wall to the left, a patch of stone has gone the blue of evening. The patch is turning slowly, the way a sleeper turns toward a sound they are not sure they heard.

For one terrible moment the power feels what the tenant might be thinking: *Is this the voice?*

It is not. It must not be mistaken for it. The worst thing it could do here, worse than any spine of glass, would be to let this creature believe for one instant that the long wait was over, and then leave.
