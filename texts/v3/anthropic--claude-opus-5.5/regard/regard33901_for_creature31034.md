# The Attention

## Arrival

It comes the way the weather here comes: from high up, and without sound.

It does not descend. There would be nothing to land on that isn't already someone's roof. It stays above the virga line, where the grey veils of rain hang and fade without touching anything, and it looks.

For a long time that is all it does. By its own measure a long time is not much, but by the measure of this country it is a queen's lifetime, maybe two. The power has learned, in other places, that the first urge of goodwill is almost always wrong. The urge is to see thirst and send water, to see death and stop it, to see silence and give a voice. It has learned that nothing earns the right to change a place until it understands what the place is doing. Here that takes a while, because almost everything the place is doing is happening inside the walls.

So it learns to listen. It lowers some part of itself, a fine thread of attention, into the fused architecture, and it holds that thread against the grey pellet-work the way a tenant holds its fingertips there. It hears the patter of the soldiers and the whisper of the tunnels. Underneath those, it hears the grammar.

It learns the grammar slowly. *Ants on the eastern arcade. The comb smells wrong.* It learns the long slow patterns that carry no information, and it stops trying to decode them, because it decides they are not a code. It learns three light and one heavy, and the reply from far away. It learns that the reply always comes, and it comes to understand that the reply is the point.

Only then does it let itself ask what it might do.

## Rain

The first answer is the obvious one. It sees at once why it is wrong.

The power could bring the sea inland. It knows how. It would turn the thermals a little and change the pressure off the coast, and clouds that have never come this far would come. For one season the virga would reach the ground.

The power follows what happens next. Water falls on unworked ash, glass shards that no river has ever sorted. Loose water in a country where nothing is loose. The fresh courses at the suburb edges slump. The chimneys drown. The fungus combs, which need damp but not flood, rot in their galleries. The succulents are built to hoard and have no way to refuse a surplus, so they swell and split. The ash starts to sort itself into silt and gravel. Channels open. For the first time the country gets what it has never had: valleys, erosion, and ruins.

In a hundred years there might be a greener place here, and a richer one by some measures. It would not be this place. The tenants would have nothing to be for. Their whole life, the sleeping that is drinking, the breath spent on the comb, the saliva spent on graves, is an answer to a world where water must be made and kept. The power would be taking away the question and calling it a gift.

It lets the clouds collapse at evening the way they always have.

## The Others

It thinks about the ants. The silent wars leave gallery floors ankle-deep. It thinks about the diggers, whose tongues take soldiers by the thousand, and the falcons stooping into the swarm-columns, and the lizard that gorges until it cannot move.

It could thin them. It notices, with some wariness toward itself, how much it wants to, because it has come to love the tenants and the builders, and the ants are their enemy.

But the ants are also a people of this country. The breach-scars are how the mounds grow their patchwork. The swarms feed everything that eats because everything that eats is waiting. A benevolence that works by choosing whom to love is not benevolence toward a place. It is taking a side in a place. The power has seen what happens when a watcher from above takes sides. It does not take one.

## The Voice

Next it considers giving them speech.

They have no name, because a name would have to be said. The power could make their breath cost less, so that speaking aloud would not waste water. It could give them a sound for themselves.

It listens again to three light and one heavy. It thinks this is already a name, and a better one than a sound. A spoken name goes out into the air and the ash swallows it. A tapped name goes into the walls, into the one continuous structure that is also their home, their food, their graves, and their ancestors. They do not lack a voice. Their voice is load-bearing.

It leaves their voice alone.

## The Walk

This is the hardest one, and the power stays with it longest.

It watches the young tenants climb out through the exit-slits on swarm nights. It watches them start toward the edge with their bundles of living comb, spending their own thirst every morning on a fist of fungus that is not theirs to eat. It watches soldiers in foreign districts tick at them the whole way. It watches the diggers take some of them, and the ants take some, and the dry take some.

It could clear the road. It could put a coolness around each walker, turn the diggers aside, and make every foreign wall smell faintly of home.

It thinks about what the walk does. It is how comb reaches the new ash. Only the walkers who can cross three weeks of indifferent country arrive, and they arrive as whatever three weeks of that country has made them: patient, still, able to wait like a succulent at noon while two frightened founders decide whether to let them in. That patience is what the founders are smelling. If the road were cleared, the walk would be a delivery, and the walkers would become the power's couriers rather than the country's.

So it does not clear the road. It lets that decision cost what it costs, because it should cost something.

Then it sees the thing it can do.

## The Gap

This is a country where nothing is loose. The tenants make sure of it. Every death is carried to the lowest warm gallery and sealed in and burnished, and the water goes back to the comb, the chimney, the dew, and the hundred small mouths.

Every death except one kind.

When a walker dies on an antelope path, in a district whose walls don't know its smell, there is no one to seal it in. No one comes from neighbouring mounds, because there are no neighbours, only strangers. It lies on the glass ash. Its water leaves into the dry air and is gone, taken out of the closed economy and lost. Its comb dries in the bundle on its back. It is the only loose thing in the whole country.

That gap is the one the power fills.

It does not stop them dying. It waits until they have died. Then, in the dark, it does what the tenants would do if any of them were there. It carries the body to the nearest living mound, whatever colony that is, and finds the lowest warm gallery by the damp coming through the wall. It mixes wall-dust with water, and it takes that water from the dead one's own bundle rather than bringing any from outside. It presses the courses in and smooths each one. It burnishes the finish with the flat of something like a thumb until it gleams. It puts the dried comb inside with the body, so the fungus has somewhere to begin.

By morning there is an oval scar low on the flank of a stranger's mound, smooth and warm to the touch. Over a year or two the water goes back.

It takes nothing from the economy of this place and adds nothing to it. All it does is close the one leak.

## Noticed

The power does not hide what it does. It has thought about hiding and decided that hiding would be its own kind of arrogance, the stance of someone managing a people rather than living near them. It simply does not announce anything. It lets the work be found.

The work is found, because the tenants' noses read everything.

A tenant in a distant district comes to the lowest gallery and smells a grave that belongs to none of its own. The grave carries the scent of another colony's comb, from very far away. Its finish is nearly right: the burnish is a little too even, and the courses are pressed with a pressure no palm quite has.

The tenant taps about it, and the district taps back. Over some years, slowly, the power hears something new in the grammar. It is a short pattern that comes up only in the context of these graves. The power cannot fully translate it. It thinks it means something like *the one who finishes*, or perhaps just *someone was there*.

It does not answer. The pattern is theirs.

## The Edge That Will Close

The power knows something the tenants may not. The basin is finite.

The edges are filling. The power can see it from high up the way it sees everything: the new ash going grey-green, knee-high domes turning into shoulders and then arcades. Some number of queens from now there will be no bare ground left. There will be no founding pairs on new ash and no walk to the edge, because there will be no edge. It does not know what the young tenants will do on swarm nights then. Perhaps they will climb out, watch the columns rise, and have nowhere to go.

The power considers splitting the crust again, reopening the seam so the country has new ground. It knows how.

But the first splitting was not a gift. It was what the ground did. The power will not open a wound in the earth on behalf of people who have never asked for one, to solve a problem they have not reached, in a way that would bury whole districts under tephra. The tenants are the part of this country that remembers. When the edge closes they will remember that it closed. Perhaps they will find something the power cannot foresee, because they are not only a function. Some of their tapping carries no information, and people who sing are not only a function.

It decides to trust them with their own future. It will be there, listening, when that future arrives. It thinks this may be the whole of what it has to offer: not rescue, but being present at the turning.

## Once

There is one night that the power does not plan.

A walker is dying on an antelope path in a district far from anything it knows, three days short of the edge. The power has come, as it always comes, to wait. It has never let itself be felt before. It would stay unfelt tonight too, except that the walker, with its huge double-lidded eyes nearly closed, reaches out in the dark for a wall. There is no wall, only glass ash.

The walker's hand finds the power instead.

The power could withdraw. It doesn't. It holds still, lets itself be solid, and becomes for a moment the only wall there is.

The walker presses its fingertips against it and taps, weakly, the way you would tap into the architecture of home to tell the district who is there:

Three light strikes and one heavy.

The power has listened to this country for longer than any tenant has lived. It knows what comes next. The pattern waits, and somewhere, always, from very far away, a reply comes back through the fused walls.

There are no walls here. No reply is coming from far away.

So the power answers. It does not tap a name, because it has none worth giving. It uses the pattern it heard ten thousand times while it was learning to listen, the one the tenants use when they have received a name and are holding it.

The walker's hand stays against it for a while, then goes still.

In the morning, low on the flank of a stranger's mound, there is a new smooth scar. It is warm to the touch, burnished a little too evenly. Inside, curled against a dried fist of comb that the fungus is already beginning to reach, there is a tenant who did not die alone.

That is all the power does. It is almost nothing. It closes the one gap in a country where nothing is ever loose, and it makes sure the call is answered.

The rest it leaves to the builders, the green, the dry, and the ones who remember. After all this listening, it has come to think that they were doing it right already.
