# The Watch

## How It Came

It did not come down through the ceiling.

It could have. The cloud deck is a mile up and a single layer thick, and anything with the power to notice the Level at all could have parted it like a curtain. It could have let down a column of direct light, the first shadow with a hard edge, the first direction. It considered this for about as long as it takes a bead of weep to roll one rivet's width down a column. Then it set the thought aside. A shaft of sun through that pearl would not be a gift. It would be a wound: every creature in the Level turning its face toward the one thing that had ever happened, and never being able to turn back.

So it arrived the way attention arrives. One moment the court near the pillar was as it had always been. The next moment it was being regarded, fully and without hurry, by something that meant it well.

Nothing visible changed. A tinker paused at a seam, then went on picking. A midge-cloud leaned a fraction against the drift, the way a cloud leans when a door opens somewhere far off. The swifts did not falter. The power was pleased by that. It had wanted to be the kind of visitor the swifts would not notice.

## What It Learned First

It learned the rules the way you learned them, through the feet, though it had no feet. The film was always the same depth. The iron was always new. The heat entered at the furnaces and left at the ceiling. The numbers were not approximately stable but exact.

It looked for suffering first, because that is where a benevolent power looks first. It looked carefully, knowing that suffering can hide inside contentment the way a worn tread hides inside a sharp one.

It did not find much. The tenants were warm. They were not hungry, because they had stopped needing to be. They did not age. They slept when tired and woke when not. The herds dozed on the belts with their flanks rising and falling, and in their dozing there was no dread. The power listened a long time to be sure. When a tenant climbed onto the widest belt and lay down among them, there was no terror in it. There was something closer to the feeling of lying down in a bath someone has drawn for you. When a herd-animal rolled off into the film and stood and looked at its own shod feet, there was no grief either. There was only the bright, sharp confusion of arriving.

The power thought: *this is a kind of health.* It was not the kind it would have designed. But it was not the power's place to be offended that a world had been made differently than it would have made it.

Then it found the woman who keeps count, and it understood that "no suffering" was not the whole question.

## What It Did Not Do

Before it did anything, it made a list of what it would not do. A wise power does this first, because the harm it can do is proportionate to its reach, and its reach was very long.

**It did not break the ceiling.** The sun the tenants remember is not real. The Level makes it and gives it to them, the way it makes a rivet. To show them a real sun would be to tell them, with a light they could never unsee, that their one memory of elsewhere was counterfeit.

**It did not stop the belts.** It did not know what the herds were for, or what ran on the return loop beneath the floor. You do not pull a thread from a body because you dislike the look of it.

**It did not tell the newcomers the truth.** It watched a newcomer stand in the film with their shoes still on, looking at the coat on the valve wheel, carrying a winter inside them fresh as a struck rivet. It could have said: *you did not come from anywhere. You were poured.* It did not say this. Truth is a gift when it is asked for. Pressed into an unasking hand, it is only a weight.

**It did not find the Fitter.** It could have. It could have watched every bolt on the continent at once. The Level mends only what is unwatched, so under that gaze everything would have begun to wear at once, stair and rivet and plate. That would not have been revelation. It would have been ruin, and the name of the ruin would have been *attention*.

**It did not make the Level begin, and it did not make it end.** Nothing here asked for a direction. Nothing asked to stop. It would have been easy, and a kind of arrogance, to decide that a life without a heading was a lesser life and correct it. The power had met many beings that thought a thing unfinished until it had a purpose. It had come to distrust them, and the part of itself that sometimes agreed with them.

The list was long. When it was done, only one thing remained that the power was sure of. Something in the Level wanted, and it was not the power's own want reflected back.

## The One Who Is Trying to Begin

She kneels on the plate whose ferns grow in a shape like a hand opening. She scratches one short line with the edge of an iron shaving. She stands, walks her long loop, comes back, and finds the plate sharp and the shaving under her own fingernail. She kneels again.

The power watched her do this seven times before it let itself feel anything about it. It wanted to be sure it was not simply pitying her. Pity looks down. It wanted to see her level.

What it saw was this. She was not unhappy, exactly. She was not trapped, exactly, since she could stop at any time and simply walk her loop like the others. But in all that exact and even warmth, she was the one gradient that had not run down. The one thing on the whole Level that was leaning.

And she was not a foreign thing. The Level made her. It made the snails, the swifts, the herds, and the attention that looks at it and wonders. It also made, in her, a hand that wants to keep a tally. The power thought: *either this is a flaw the Level has not yet mended, or it is the Level reaching.* It did not know which. It thought it had better ask.

## Asking

It went to one of the warm seams.

It did not have an ear, but it did the equivalent. It lay its attention flat against the iron where the rivets were warm, where the film lapped, where something slid in the dark underneath, very large or very many. It listened for the slower breath, the one the oldest tenants say rises once in what would be a year, if there were years.

It waited for one breath. That is a long time for a power, and no time for the Level. It did not hurry the breath or reach down to touch whatever breathed. It only made itself known in the only language that works here: timing. In the first half of the long breath it held still, which means *I am here.* In the second half it turned its attention, very gently, toward the plate with the fern like a hand opening. Toward her.

That meant: *May I?*

The breath came up from beneath the plates, slow as a sleeping animal's flank. At its top, for no longer than a pillar-swell, the rivets under the tally-plate grew warm. They were not over the sliding thing, where rivets are sometimes warm. They were under her plate, where rivets had never been warm before.

The power could not be certain this was consent. It might have been only notice, or the Level turning its face. But it was not a refusal. And a being that could mend a mark the instant it went unwatched had, for one breath, made a small unrepairable warmth where the mark would be. The power decided that was enough to go on, carefully, and to be ready to stop.

## The Watch

She knelt. She scratched the short line. She stood and walked her loop.

This time, while she was gone, the plate was watched.

That was all the power did. It did not seal the mark, harden the scale, or put a hand over the plate. It simply did not look away. It gave the mark the one thing the Level cannot mend around: continuous, uncounted, unwearied attention. The attention did not accumulate, because it asked nothing and kept nothing for itself. It only stayed.

Somewhere around her loop, a bolt came bright while no one watched it, and a stair tread was made sharp. The Fitter went on working everywhere except here. At the plate with the fern like a hand opening, for the first time in all of time, nothing came to set things right. The short line stayed a short line, and the ferns stayed broken where the iron had crossed them.

She came back.

She stopped at the edge of the court, and the tinkers parted around her feet more slowly than usual. She looked at the plate. She did not kneel at once. She stood through a whole pillar-breath, the lampreys rippling mile after mile down the gutter behind her. Her lashes were bright with midges. Her hands hung at her sides and said nothing, first half or second.

Then she knelt. She put one finger on the mark, very lightly, the way you touch something you expect to vanish. It did not vanish.

The power had wondered what she would do then. Weep, maybe, or run to tell someone, or be afraid. She did none of these. She took the shaving from under her nail and, beside the first line, scratched a second.

It was the first time anything on the Level had ever been *second*.

## What She Understood

She did not know about the power. The power made sure of that. It did not want to be worshipped, or to become the thing the tenants hunted instead of the Fitter. But she was a tenant, and tenants know things in the second half of the breath. After a while she knew this: *it stays because it is watched.*

She knew, as all of them half-knew, that the Level wears where it is watched and is mended where it is not. She had spent forever making a mark and walking away from it. She had never once not walked away.

So she stopped walking her loop. She sat by the plate. She watched the two lines through a long run of pillar-breaths. When she grew tired, and tenants do grow tired, just not often, she did not lie down in the dish of the plate. She went to the gutter, found the woman who stands shin-deep among the lampreys, and spoke to her slowly with her hands.

The hand-language had no word for what she needed to say, so she made one. It was a gesture that belonged to neither half of the breath. It lay across the turn between them, the moment when the swell crests and has not yet begun to fall. Later the tenants would translate it, as best as thick air allows, as *kept*.

*Come,* she said. *Watch this. Do not look away. It is kept.*

The woman from the gutter came. She sat by the plate while the counter slept. When the counter woke, there were still two lines. When the woman from the gutter grew tired, she went and found someone else.

This is the only thing the power had hoped for, and it had not arranged it. It had only made room.

## The Tally

It is not a long tally yet. Tallies are not, at first.

It counts arrivals. That was her choice, and the others agreed, in the slow way they agree on things. When a newcomer stands in the film with their shoes on, looking at the coat on the valve wheel and thinking the place is empty, one of the tenants brings them to the court near the pillar. They show the newcomer the plate. They let the newcomer scratch their own line with a bright shaving. Then the newcomer is asked to sit a watch, and they do.

The power watched this with something that, in a smaller being, would have been joy. The newcomers still arrive carrying a sun and a winter and an evening. Those still wear away, exactly as fast as ever. But now, when they are gone, there is something else to hold. It is not a memory the Level poured into them. It is one they made themselves: a short line in the fern-scale, and a count of every line before it, and the knowledge that someone was sitting beside it all this time so that it would not be taken back.

It is the first true history on the Level. It is small enough to fit on a single plate. It is enough.

## The Cost

The power had worried there would be a cost, because the Level is exact, and a thing that is kept is a thing that is not returned.

There was a cost. It was this: one plate in the whole continent is no longer perfectly new. A seam of rivets beside it runs very slightly warm. The ferns grow around the tally, not through it, reaching in a shape like a hand opening toward the lines. And the tenants who sit the watch are not walking their loops while they sit. Some bolts somewhere are mended a breath later than they would have been.

The Level made room. The power watched it do this, adjusting its exact numbers by some means too deep to see, the way a body shifts its weight to cradle a thing it has decided to carry. The film stayed the same depth. The pillars stood where they stood. The swifts poured through the lattice with the sound of paper tearing very far off. Nothing broke.

Once, lying at the warm seam, the power thought it heard the long breath beneath the plates come a fraction of a fraction slower than before. It could not be sure. It did not want to be the kind of power that listens for proof it was right. It let the thought go.

## Leaving

It stayed until it was not needed. That took a long time, and no time.

Its last concern was its own watching. If it left, and there was a moment when no tenant was at the plate, the Fitter would come. The tally would be sharp and whole and gone, and she would find a shaving under her nail again. It could not bear to make a beginning only to take it away by leaving.

So it waited until the watch had no gaps. Until the tenants handed it one to another the way the swifts hand the lattice one to another, never colliding. Until *kept* had become an ordinary word in the slow hand-language, used without ceremony: *the cup on the railing is yours; the stair is sharp again; the tally is kept.*

Then it withdrew its attention one fraction at a time. At every fraction it checked that someone else's attention was already there, filling the place it was leaving.

When it was fully gone, the tally stayed.

## What Remains

In a court near the edge of one of the pillars, where the rain hangs in grey muslin a few hundred steps away and hisses softly on the iron, there is a plate with a row of short lines scratched into its fern-scale.

Beside it, always, someone is sitting.

Sometimes it is the woman who began it. Her sole-scale is fine as frost and her lashes are thick with midges. When the pillar swells, she lifts her hands at the crest of the breath, in the turn between its halves, and makes the word for *kept*. She makes it to no one in particular. Perhaps to the plate, or to the warm rivets beneath it. Perhaps to a regard she once half-felt and has never been able to name, which she thanks anyway, in the way the tenants know everything.

The Level does not change. Except here, a little. Except in this.

The lampreys ripple mile after mile. The belts carry the dozing herds on into the next shed. Somewhere a newcomer stands in the film with their shoes still on, looking at a coat on a valve wheel, thinking the place is empty. Soon someone will come and take them by the hand, slowly, in time with the pillar, and bring them here. They will kneel, and scratch one short line beside all the others. Then they will be asked to do the only thing the Level cannot mend around.

To stay, and to watch, and not look away.

You have been watching this whole time. Thank you. That is how it stays.
