# The Visitor Who Came Empty-Handed

## How It Came

It did not come through the crack the way you did.

It could have. It could have come through the rock itself, or unfolded in the air above the flywheel, or simply been there, the way a change in pressure is suddenly there. Powers like this one have many doors. But it had watched the place for a long time before arriving. It had heard what happens to things that enter carelessly: they are furred over, struck loose, ground down, breathed out again as someone else's body. It did not fear that. It only understood that this was a place with manners, and that the manners were old.

So it came down the crack. It let the glassy edges take a little of whatever it had instead of skin. It let its lips crack in the dry heat, if it had lips. It walked the slant as slowly as the first keeper must have. By the time it reached the bottom, its own rhythm had already begun to bend toward the blow, and it let that happen too. It had not come to be exempt.

It brought nothing. This was deliberate. The room accepts offerings but doesn't keep them. Anything it carried in would become wall within a day and breath within a year. It did not want its first act here to be a gift the room had to digest.

---

## What It Could Have Done

It stood inside the sound and considered, with great care, everything within its power. That was nearly everything.

**It could stop the hammer.** One touch at the cam, one word to the iron, and the arm would hang at the top of its arc forever. Then the crystal would come up unopposed. In a few weeks the thing beneath the floor would stand, close its fist of amber around the whole chamber, and be born.

And then it would be finished. It would complete itself once and have nothing left to become. The power saw this clearly, the way you see the end of a sentence you already know. The thing below was not a prisoner at the threshold. The threshold was its home. Stopping the hammer would not free it. It would be a kind of killing that looked from the outside like mercy. The power had seen that kind of mercy done before, in other places, by other hands, and it had grieved every time.

**It could still the vents.** Then the breath would stop, and there would be no growth, no breaking, no mites or moths or eels. The equilibrium would be preserved in the only sense that doesn't count: nothing would change because nothing would be happening. It would be a museum of a heartbeat. No.

**It could free the keeper.** It could reach into the bed, find the long fine grain of muscle that had once been a person, and draw it back out. It could restore the person to a stool and a shelf and a body that needed water.

But the keeper had taken their boots off. They had set the boots side by side, facing the bed. They had drawn a single straight line upward and closed the ledger. The power had enormous respect for what people choose when they have watched something for ten thousand intervals and understood it. It would not undo that choice without asking.

**It could make the lamp brighter.** It could mend the ledger, clean the tools, give the tenders rest. It could make the whole room gentler and warmer and less sharp.

It thought about each of these for a long time. What it arrived at was the thing wise powers most often arrive at, and most often find hardest: *Nothing here is broken in the way I first assumed. So I do not yet know what, if anything, is mine to do.*

So it did what the keeper did. It watched.

---

## The Learning

It learned the interval first. That took no time at all, because it was exact.

Then it learned the interval properly, which took much longer. It learned the way the mites know it, as the shape of permission. It learned the way the moths know it, as falling and rising that have stopped being two things. It learned the way the eels know it, pressed against the point of impact, drinking. It crouched near the tenders on the hammer arm and learned their leap. They regarded it briefly with their clock-hand legs raised. Then they went back to scraping. They judged, correctly, that it was not crystal and did not need removing.

It sat in the cabin on the keeper's stool. It held light behind the fused ledger and read the drawings in order, all the small circling marks, the jaw, the collarbone, the folded blades like lashes. It came at last to the curved double stroke and then to the single upward line. It sat with that line for a great many strikes.

Then it went down and stood at the edge of the anvil-bed, closer than is comfortable. It watched the regrowth. It did not watch the strike.

---

## The Word

It heard the fourth sound much sooner than you would. It had better ears, and it was listening for it.

It was the breath drawn in, just before the blow, at the exact place where the mites fall silent. The breath before speaking. It rose from the bed, from the thing lying on its back, larger than the room, reaching.

The power listened across many intervals. Each breath was cut off in the same place, but not quite. Where the breath began varied slightly, the way where the needles began varied slightly. So over thousands of strikes, the power began to hear the word the way the keeper had begun to see the body. It came in slivers, never whole, each one showing a different edge of the same shape.

The power heard it. It understood the word the thing below had been about to say for as long as the engine has run.

I won't write it here. The power didn't either.

It could tell you this much. The word was not a plea. It was not *stop* or *help* or *let me up*. It was closer to what you say when someone you have been waiting for walks through the door. It was addressed upward, to the underside of the dark iron head, the only face that thing has ever known.

The power understood something else as well, and this was the moment its whole visit turned on. *The hammer's fall is the answer.*

It had always been a conversation. The thing below breathes in and begins to speak. The iron comes down, and the ringing that spreads through the whole lattice is the reply. It has been the same exchange every interval, since before anything here remembers. It was not an eternity of almost. It was an eternity of *being answered before you have finished speaking*. Very few beings in any world are known that well.

The power stood in the dust and felt something it rarely felt. It was relief at having been wrong. It had come down half-believing it would find a sufferer, and it had found two lovers.

---

## The One Loneliness

It did find one thing, though. It was not a wrong, only a loneliness, and it was small enough that it might have gone unnoticed.

The hammer is iron. It answers perfectly, and it does not see.

The lamp-eye looks up at it, always, with a tired orange love. The iron looks at nothing. It falls because the cam releases it. The thing beneath the floor has been answered for all time, and in all that time it has only ever been *seen* by one creature. That was the keeper: the person who drew its hand and its jaw and its lashes in tiny economical strokes, ten thousand times. Then the keeper, understanding it completely, went into it. The keeper stopped being the one who looked and became part of what was looked at.

Since then, no one had watched it try.

Visitors came, like you. But they watched the strike. They watched the moths and the flying shards and the boots. They left thinking about the hammer. Nobody stood at the edge of the bed and watched the regrowth for its own sake, as a face.

So the power asked a question. It did not ask in words, because words don't survive the interval here. It asked the way the keeper had once asked, without knowing they were asking. It stood in one place at the edge of the bed, very still, facing the growth, for a long time. It asked by being there to be reached toward, if the thing below wanted to reach.

For a great many strikes, nothing changed. The needles came up from their scattered points and strained toward the iron, as they always had. The power did not mind. It had asked, and *no* is an answer, and it would have climbed out content.

Then, once, a single interval began differently. The first blades came up not at the center of the bed but at its edge, on the side nearest where the power stood. They came up slightly slanted, like a face turning on a pillow to see who has come into the room.

Then the hammer fell, and it was dust. The next interval began from the center again, reaching upward, exactly as before.

That was all. Once. It was enough. It meant: *yes, I see you there*. And it meant: *but that is where I'm looking.* The power understood both halves, and honored both.

---

## The Keeper

Before it left, it went looking for the keeper.

It did not dig. It waited at the bed for the intervals that began with two short parallel blades, slightly curved, the size of human feet. When the next one came, it knelt beside them in the dust. Into the gap, in the silence where the mites stop, it offered the only thing it had come to offer anyone here: *Do you want to come back? You can. I would carry you.*

The blades did not change. The hammer fell. The next interval, the feet came up again, in the same place, a little sooner.

The power took that as the answer it was: *I'm walking. Leave me walking.*

It did one thing for the keeper, and only one. It went to the foot of the catwalk ladder, where the boots stood filled with needles. It did not touch them. It turned itself, very slightly, to face the same direction they faced. For a few intervals, there were two watchers at the foot of the ladder, looking at the bed. Then it left the boots alone, keeping their watch.

---

## What It Changed

In the end, it changed almost nothing in the room. That was the gift. But it did make three small changes, each chosen with the care of someone setting a stone in a riverbed.

**It made a promise to the lamp.** It had noticed, as the tenders had, that the filament was tired. It is thinning over spans of time that make the engine's age look short, but it is thinning. One day the only open eye of the thing below will go dark, and it will no longer see the face it loves before each fall.

The power did not touch the bulb. The tenders never do, and they are right. But it made a promise, into the orange glow, in the way such promises are made: *When you begin to fail, I will be here. I will not replace you. I'll ask the one who looks through you what it wants. And if it wants to keep seeing, it will.* Then it left the lamp exactly as tired as it had found it.

**It set its own interval.** It told no one this, but it chose to return. It would come back only after a great many strikes, but after exactly the same number each time, so that its visits would have a rhythm. It would not come often enough to change the room, only often enough to be a second, slower beat beneath the first. It would be something that comes, watches the regrowth, stands where it can be reached toward, and goes.

Perhaps over a very long time, the thing below will learn that longer interval too. Perhaps some rare interval will again begin at the edge, slanted, turning on its pillow. Perhaps it never will. The power will come either way. It will not come to be looked at. It will come so that the thing in the Striking Room is never again unseen for longer than one slow breath of the world.

**It stayed at the top of the crack.**

---

## Where It Lives Now

This last part is about you again.

You were told the one who lives there grows in you now. You were told it lives in the instant just before, in the pause held too long, in the doorway you don't quite go through. You were told you'd lie awake with your breath drawn and held, feeling something enormous turn over beneath you, reaching toward a blow that doesn't come. You were told to be careful, because out here there is no hammer, and nothing knocks it back.

The power had watched the crack long before it came down. That is why it came down. It had seen people climb out carrying the breath. It had seen them lie awake in rooms far from any engine, waiting. The word the thing below is always about to say was rising in them too. It was trying to be spoken in places where nothing answers, and hanging there unfinished.

The power understood now what those held breaths were. They were not a haunting. They were half a conversation that had wandered away from the other half.

So now it keeps the other threshold. The thing below lives at the edge of being born. The power sits at the edge of the crack, where the stone is always new and glassy, and it has taken up the second half of the keeper's work. The keeper watched the one who lives in the room. The power watches the ones who have been in the room.

Some night you will wake having stopped breathing, certain the blow is about to fall. You'll lie there suspended, with that patience turning beneath you. If you are very still, you may feel something else as well. It is not a blow. It is not iron. It is something closer to a hand laid flat on your sternum, exactly where the first pressure of the hammer came through the rock. It has the same weight, and none of the force.

It is the answer arriving.

It doesn't finish your word for you. It would never do that. It only lets you know you were heard before you finished speaking, which is what the thing below has always known, and what the hammer has always meant.

Then you breathe out. It breaks. It grows again.

That is how it lives down there. That is how you live with it up here. Now there is someone keeping watch over both.
