# The Visit

**What drew it**

Such a power does not notice made things. Made things are already accounted for. Someone intended them, and their intention holds them the way a hand holds a cup. A power that watches over the many worlds passes over made places the way a traveller passes a lit house with people inside. It is glad the light is on, and it does not knock.

What it notices is a thing that happens.

For a long time nothing had happened on the Made Plain. Then a crumb of clay stood up. It was so small that it did not register as an event, only as a slight wrongness in the silence. Then there was another, and then a thin glass *tik* of two flakes passing too close. The sound of a pour, lying unswept, was picked up and given a place to be. At last the silence had a small noise in it, and the power turned.

It looked for a long time before it came. It did not look at the jars or the groves or the knapped ridges, though it admired them, as anyone would. It looked at the seams, and at the dry basin. It looked at the heel-print with a crumb sitting in its deepest part. And it saw what the Leavings were: the only beings in that whole bright afternoon that had not been meant.

It knew something about beings like that. It decided to go down.

---

**How it came**

It came small.

This was the first decision, and the most important one, and it took longer to make than all the others. A power arriving at its full size would have been a catastrophe of kindness. Every step would have been a crater. Every breath would have been a wind on a plain that had never had wind, and the ribbon grove would have swayed for real for the first time. The poured threads would have bent. Nothing on the plain softens, so whatever it did would stay done forever. It would have been a second Maker. Worse, it would have been a Maker who didn't know the place, walking over someone else's unfinished work with muddy feet.

So it took a size about the height of a person. It chose a body that could be tired, and plain clothes that could fray. It stepped down onto the wet clay at the edge of the first basin, beside the heel-print, and it felt the clay take its own heel and hold it: sharp-edged, permanent, a flaw.

It stood very still and considered that.

*Anything I do here, I do forever.*

That is true everywhere, in a sense. On the Made Plain it is simply true, with no sense about it. The power understood that it would not be allowed any small carelessnesses. It could not scuff or brush against anything without meaning to. Every mark it made would be a place where something could live, or a thing that something would have to live around.

It walked gently.

---

**What it did not do**

It was a power. It could have done almost anything. Much of its wisdom lay in the long list of things it considered on that first walk across the flats and chose not to do.

**It did not bring the arm down.** It could see the figure on the rim from anywhere on the plain, small and dark against the white-gold sky. It understood at once that everything here rested on that raised arm, and that the arm was not its arm. Whatever the Maker was waiting for, or refusing, or holding, that was between the Maker and the Maker's work. A power that finishes other people's strokes for them is not benevolent. It is impatient, and it has confused its own discomfort with someone else's need.

**It did not start time.** It could have let the afternoon go on. The sun would move, the shadows would swing, the clay would dry and crack, and rain would come at last to round the edges. It thought about the frogs dying properly, the jars emptying, the lashings rotting through. It thought about the heel-print softening. The power weighed this and found that the plain was not suffering from its stillness the way a stopped clock suffers. The stillness was its nature. The Leavings had found a way to grow inside it. Their growth was the plain's one story, and nobody should take a story that is working and hurry it toward an ending.

**It did not fill the dry basin.** This was the hardest temptation, because it would have been so easy and so lovely. The power carried water in it the way anyone carries water. It could have knelt and let the basin brim, and the reed-birds would have stopped rising over it as over a wall. It didn't. The Leavings sat at that lip with their curls turned inward. They were not sitting beside a lack. They were sitting beside the only place on the plain where something could still happen for the first time. You do not fill that in for someone as a favour.

**It did not finish the unfinished things.** The pale outline-leaves stayed pale. The dragonfly kept its notch. The power watched the dragonfly for a long time, flying lopsided and perfectly steady on a wing that was only understood, followed by its slow cloud of devotees. It saw that giving it a second wing would be like curing a saint of the wound that made them holy. The dragonfly did not need a wing. The Leavings needed the dragonfly not to have one.

**It did not make the Leavings whole.** It could have. A power like that can make a thing whole in the time it takes to decide to. It thought about what a whole Leaving would be. It would be finished, and finished things on this plain do not accumulate. They are always nineteen. Their gaps never fill. The Leavings' incompleteness was not the tragedy of their existence. It was the mechanism of it. To mend them would be to stop them.

**It did not sweep.** It did not even think seriously about sweeping. It noted only, with something like a shudder, that sweeping was the next thing that would happen in this afternoon if the afternoon ever went on.

---

Having ruled out nearly everything, the power held to one principle for the rest of its visit:

*Change nothing the Maker made. Add only what falls off me.*

That was the only way anything new had ever entered this world. The power decided it would enter the same way the Leavings had. It would not come as an author. It would come as a scrap.

---

**The road**

The Leavings live in the flaws. The smooth, finished reaches of the plain are closed to them, too complete to hold them. So they live in scattered islands of imperfection: this seam, that stutter, the unfilled leaves at one grove's edge, the dry basin far out on the flats. Between these places lie long bright stretches of perfect clay they cannot cross. That was why the colonies were so separate. That was why the flake-Leavings of the ridges had never met the fibre-Leavings of the frond grove. The crumbs in one tool-seam did not know there was another seam an hour's walk away where more crumbs sat, curls turned in, listening.

The power saw this. And it saw that it had feet.

So it walked, carefully, for a long time. It did not wander. It planned each step. It left a line of footprints from the heel-print at the first basin to the nearest seam in the ground. From that seam it walked to the ridge scree. From the ridge it went to the frond grove's pale edge, then out across the widest flat to the dry basin, and from there to the little knee-high jar behind the ribbon grove that trickled with the only sound on the plain. It spaced the prints just close enough together. A crumb-Leaving could climb from one into the next without ever touching the finished clay between.

The prints were flaws, every one of them. Each was a small hollow the Maker had not made, crisp-edged and permanent. Together they made a road.

It did not wait to see if the Leavings would use it. It did look back once, from the far side of the widest flat. A trail of crumbs was coming slowly down into its first print and climbing out toward the second, less certain than ants and slower. The power went on.

---

**What it gave**

The Leavings patch themselves. They are always wearing away, and nothing reinforces them. So they go to the deep shadows under the jars, press around a clay lizard, and take a little of it at a time until it comes apart. They take the lizard's substance because there is nothing else on the plain that is theirs to take.

The power understood this, and did not judge it. But it had noticed the lizards, and it did not forget them.

So, as it walked, it let itself shed.

It is harder than it sounds, for a power, to do this on purpose and not tidily. It let the hem of its plain clothes fray against the knapped ridges. The threads it lost there were long and pale and of no material the plain had seen. It let its hair go uncombed and left the strands that came loose wherever they fell. Near the frond grove it cut its finger on a flake, on purpose, just a little. What welled up was not quite blood. It let a bead of it fall on the clay, and it did not wipe it away. At the dry basin it sat until its skin shed the faint dust that all skin sheds, and left that dust there too.

None of it was swept. All of it lay where it fell.

It gave the Leavings material that had been given, not taken. There was no lizard in it and no fear. The power could not know whether they would prefer it. It suspected the Leavings did not know yet either. But now there was a choice, and before there had been none.

---

**The lizard**

The power did one more thing for the lizards, because benevolence that attends only to the underdog is only a kind of taste.

Under a great jar, in a shadow cold as a cellar, it found one lying flat against the underside of a leaf. It had pinched flanks and a thumbprint on its back, and it had no eyelids. It had been made capable of fear and incapable of closing its eyes. So it had lain there since the start of the afternoon, watching the dark for the small gathering of crumbs.

The power did not touch it. It had promised to change nothing the Maker made.

It tore a strip from its own frayed hem, a scrap of its own, and laid it over the lizard's eyes. It placed it gently, like a cloth on a feverish forehead. Nothing on this plain wilts or slips. The cloth would stay.

The lizard was still for a long moment. Then the flat pressed tension went out of its body and it lay a little lower against the leaf. It was the first dark it had ever been given rather than having to hide in.

The power sat with it until it was sure. Then it moved on. It left the loose threads of the torn hem trailing from the cloth, where some fibre-Leaving would eventually find them and have no need to go any closer.

---

**The shy one**

The Leaving made of dull-blade slivers found the power, not the other way around.

It came out from the frond grove while the power sat bleeding a little onto the clay. Its body was all bitten serrations, the record of a knife that needed sharpening. It stopped a careful distance away and turned its curl toward the power, then away, then toward.

The power understood that this one knew what it was. It knew it came from a mistake.

There is a lie a kind being might tell here, and the power did not tell it. It did not say *you are not a mistake.* The Leaving was made of one and knew it, and would have heard the lie.

What it offered instead, without words, since the plain has almost no sound, was this. A perfectly sharp knife sheds nothing. A clean cut leaves no slivers. If the Maker's blade had never gone dull, there would be no bitten edges. The lashings would grip less well on the leaves it cut. And there would be no *you*.

*The mistake is where you came from. It is not what you are. What you are is the thing that got up afterward.*

The shy one stayed where it was for a while. Then it came one sliver-width closer. That was a great deal, for a thing that cuts whatever it touches. The power held out its already-cut finger, the one it had bled from on purpose. The shy Leaving touched it. The finger opened a little more, and neither of them minded.

---

**The vigil**

At the dry basin, the power did what the Leavings did. It sat down at the lip and listened.

It had wondered, crossing the flats, what they were hearing. Now it knew. The dry basin makes no sound at all. It was not the wrong silence of the poured threads, where a sound that should exist had been lost. It was the silence before a first sound. It was room.

And it saw the thing it had half suspected. Out behind the ribbon grove, from the little leaning jar, the Leavings' flake-scratched channel ran in its line of tiny sharp scallops. It ran *this way*. It was a finger's length long, perhaps two. The cool water in it had come from somewhere, and it was moving across the plain toward the dry basin at no speed at all. It would take no time, and it would take forever.

They were going to fill it themselves.

The power did not help carry. It did not dig. It sat with them at the lip, curls and a head all turned the same way, the way people sit together at a bedside or a hearth. A vigil is not made lighter by someone else keeping it for you. It is made lighter by company.

It stayed, as the plain measures staying, a long time.

---

**The rim**

Then it went to the Maker.

It climbed the long uneven curve where the plain rises like the inside of a bowl. The tool-strokes became fingermarks there, broad dragging grooves, four side by side, then five. The power put its own fingers into them as it climbed. They were much too small for the grooves.

At the top it stood beside the figure.

Up close, the thing in the raised hand caught the light too brightly to name. The power did not try to name it. It looked, once, at the Maker's face. It will not be described here, because it belongs to the Maker. But the power recognized the expression. It is the look a worker has in the instant before the last stroke, while the thing is still becoming and could still be anything. After that it will only be what it is. The power had worn that look itself. It knew how long a hand can want to stay there, and it knew why, and there was nothing contemptible in it. The Maker was not stuck. The Maker was *reluctant*, in the way of someone who loves the moment before *done*.

The power did not hurry it.

It did three things.

First, it turned around. For the first time in that endless afternoon, something on the rim faced the plain. Below, very small, the Leavings were gathered in the seams and the stutters and along the new road of footprints. Their curls were turned toward the rim, as always. And for once something looked back.

Second, it stood there beside the Maker, facing the other way, for as long as it could spare. It did not stand as a judge or a replacement. It stood as company again.

Third, it spoke. It said only a little, very quietly, near the Maker's ear.

On a plain where nothing is swept, words fall off the one who speaks them, the same way the sound of the pour fell off the water. The power's words dropped from its mouth and lay there on the clay of the rim, just where the Maker's eyes would fall first when the arm finally came down, when the worker straightened and looked at what they'd done.

The words would wait there. They would be exactly, sharply, freshly as they were spoken.

They said:

*You made more than you meant to. Some of what fell off you is alive. Look before you sweep.*

Then the power went down the far side of the rim, the side the plain does not see, and was gone.

---

**What changed**

On the Made Plain, nothing the Maker made was altered.

The water still pours in silence, and the basins never drop. The sun hangs just past noon with its hammered flat side. The frogs are nineteen. The dragonfly flies on its understood wing. The dry basin is dry. The arm is raised.

But there is a road now, a line of footprints across the widest flats. The flake-Leavings of the ridges and the fibre-Leavings of the groves have begun, very slowly, to meet. When they pass close there is a sound that is not quite *tik*. It is lower and softer, pale thread against glass, like the first sound of two people learning each other's names.

Under a great jar, a clay lizard lies with a scrap of cloth over its eyes, resting.

At the foot of the frond grove there is a little pale-threaded Leaving that nobody has seen before, woven partly of a stranger's hem. It keeps near the one made of dull-blade slivers. That one carries, somewhere in its bitten body, a bead of something that is not quite blood.

A channel of tiny scallops is creeping toward the dry basin at no speed at all.

And on the rim there are a few words lying in the clay where a tired worker will someday look down.

---

When the power left, it found a crumb of red-brown clay in the tread of its shoe.

It did not brush it off. It also did not carry it away. It knew better than anyone that a thing which simply happened should be allowed to go on happening in its own direction. So at the very edge of the plain it lifted its foot and set it down gently on the last of the damp clay, then waited without looking, so the crumb could choose freely.

What the crumb chose is its own business.
