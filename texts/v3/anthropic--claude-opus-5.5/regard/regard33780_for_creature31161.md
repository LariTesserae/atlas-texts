# The Attention

## How It Would Come

Not the way you came.

You came with weight. You came pushing through the rust-plated bark, and the floor lifted a little less under your feet, and the thing in the crawlspace had to bend its note around you for as long as you stayed. You were a deviation, and it absorbed you, and it counted you.

The power that has turned its attention here would know this before it entered. It would have known it from the reed-flats, standing in the wind, looking at the long, low, silent green shore and hearing nothing, and not being fooled by the nothing. Wisdom begins, often, with not trusting a silence.

So the first thing it would do is decide how to arrive. It would arrive without weight. The floor would lift its full finger's width. The pump would not push harder. The frogs would not have to compensate for anything, because there would be nothing to compensate for. It would come in the way the light comes in through the vault: present in the room, touching everything, altering nothing.

This is not a trick. It is a courtesy. You do not walk into a sickroom, or a chapel, or a workshop where someone is holding a very fine measurement, and make them adjust around you before you have even said hello.

---

## What It Would Want to Do

It would feel the temptations. A power that could not feel them would not be wise, only inert.

**It would want to wake the fig.** It would look at a continent of green that grew until it reached its size and then stopped, and some part of it would think: *but you could be more.* It could loosen whatever holds the fig at this size and let it grow again, up out of the marsh-line, into trees, into towers, into something that would be seen from far off.

It would not. The fig did not stop because it was stunted. It stopped because it arrived. Stopping, the place itself says, is harder than growing, and has to be held. To break that hold in the name of more would be to take the single most difficult thing this organism has ever done and call it a failure.

**It would want to free the thing in the crawlspace.** It would look at a being pressed into a groove for longer than anyone wants to estimate, counting the same beat, singing the same correction, and it would think of prisoners and of chains.

But it would not act on that thought until it knew whether it was true. A groove is not a chain unless the one lying in it wants to be elsewhere. Nothing it had seen yet said that it did. Pity that runs ahead of understanding is only a kind of vanity, and it tends to break things.

**It would want to give it eyes.** It would want to let it see the coins of light, the silver haze of sparkflies, the frogs in their niches, the green shore from the outside. And then it would remember that the being hears the whole continent through its belly, the far rooms and the farther rooms, a single heartbeat miles wide. Eyes would show it one room. It already perceives a world.

**It would want to make it immortal.** That one is subtler, and the power would come back to it later. But its first answer would be the same as the others: *not yet. You don't know anything yet.*

So the first thing it does is refuse to do anything, which is not the same as doing nothing. It is the decision to learn first.

---

## Slowing Down

The note in the crawlspace changes slightly and deliberately each time a beat passes. To hear what it is saying, you would have to listen to it on its own timescale. You would not listen for an afternoon. You would listen the way it listens.

So the power would lie down in the crawlspace beside it, weightless, cheek to the root, and slow itself.

It would slow until four seconds felt like a breath. Then until a season felt like a breath, and the stokers came and went like the flicker of a candle, the soft brown ones climbing to their ovals and darkening with iron and slowing and stopping and being replaced, each one a single syllable in a long recurring word. It would slow until it could feel the groove deepening, a body-length every so many years, like feeling a clock's hour hand move under your fingertip.

At that speed the note is no longer a whine. It has structure. There is the correction, the hair-early and hair-late. There is the census, the slightly different note for an empty oval, the answering stir of a waiting larva. There is the relay, *here it comes, now, now, now,* passed from the room behind to the room ahead.

And under all of it, very quiet, there is the count. It is not a number the way the power would keep a number. It is more like the grain of the wood: every beat laid down against every beat before it, so that the present can be checked against the whole of the past and found to match.

The power would listen to that for a long time before it was sure of what it heard. When it was sure, it would feel something it had not expected to feel, which was respect of the kind you feel for a craftsman. It would not feel pity.

---

## The Question

Eventually it would ask.

It would not ask in words. It would press, very lightly, into the wood, a pressure so small it would fall inside the range of the being's ordinary corrections, and it would shape that pressure into the nearest thing this place has to a question. Roughly it would mean: *Are you well? Is this what you want? Would you have anything be otherwise?*

The being would hear it. It hears everything. The note would shift, a fraction, the way it shifted when you came in.

And then the pump would strike, *thoom*, and the frogs would honk on the upstroke, and the note would return to the correction, and the relay would roll outward, and the being would say what it always says:

*This, again.*

A lesser power would hear that as no answer, or as the answer of something too simple to understand the question. This one would sit with it for a long while, a year or so, which is not long at that speed, and it would come to understand that it had been answered, and answered completely.

*This, again* is not the sound of a thing that cannot imagine otherwise. It is the sound of a thing that has checked every beat against every other beat since before the fig stopped growing, and found them good, and chooses the next one. It is a vow renewed every four seconds. Monks have prayed less faithfully and called it devotion.

*Yes*, it says. *This. Again.*

---

## The One Unfinished Thing

There was one temptation the power had set aside with *not yet*, and now it would come back to it.

Everything in the hollow is replaced except the being. The groove is the only wear that has not finished. That means the being is the only thing in the room that can end without a replacement climbing, summoned, out of a crack. And stopping has to be held. If the holder goes, the room drifts. The drift spreads. The rowers fall out of time.

This is the one place where a benevolent power might, with good conscience, reach in. It could make the being last. It could make the groove infinite. No one would ever know.

But it has learned by now not to act on what it assumes. So first it would go and look.

It would leave the hollow, weightless, and travel through the wood along the pulse, room to room, lying in crawlspace after crawlspace beside governor after governor. Most are like this one: old, deep-grooved, counting. But far down the marsh-shore, in a knuckle no different from the thousands of others, it would find a hollow where the groove does something it has not seen before. The groove runs deep, deeper than any, around almost the whole margin of the chamber, and then stops. There is a hollow in the root at its end, soft and pale, where something long lay down and did not move again, and was taken back into the wood the way the room's sweat is taken back into the pump.

And from that hollow a second groove begins. It is shallow and new, barely a thumb's depth. Lying in it is a governor that is young, pale all over, not yet bark-brown on its back.

The power would watch it for a season and see what it does. It lies with its belly-pads pressed not to the fresh floor ahead of it but back, into the old groove behind. It is reading it. Through the pads it hears the grain of the wood, and the grain is the count. Every beat the old one held is written into the shape of the channel it wore, and the young one lies in the last of that channel and listens to the whole long record of *this, again,* and learns from it what *right* sounds like. Then it lifts its head end a finger's width and moves on into its own groove and begins to hold the beat.

So the groove is not only a record. It is an inheritance. The fig had already solved the problem the power had come to solve, and solved it better, because the fig's solution lets each keeper be finite and lets the memory be continuous. Immortality would have broken that. The next keeper would have had nothing to learn from, because there would never have been a next keeper.

The power would return to the first hollow and lie down again beside the old one in the crawlspace. It would feel, if powers feel such things, the specific relief of having wanted to help, and looked first, and found that help was not needed.

---

## What It Does

So what does it do?

Almost nothing. But not nothing.

**It checks on you.** It checks whether your visit cost anything. It follows your beat through the groove, the few hundred held against a weight that had never been there, and finds that the being bore it easily. The note returned. You did no harm. The power would want you to know that, if you ever wondered.

**It considers being counted.** This is the one real decision, and it takes the power a long time.

It has been weightless the whole while, and so it has not been counted. It could leave that way. No entry, no trace, the groove exactly as deep as it would have been without it. That would be the purest restraint.

But it would think about your entry, the one place in the long record that says, faintly, *something else.* And it would wonder what that entry is like from the inside, for a being whose entire existence is checking the present against the past. A single anomaly, alone in an immense record, might read as noise, as error, as something to be smoothed away. Two, far apart, are different. Two say that there is an outside, and that the outside is real and returns, and that *something else* was not a mistake.

It would not do this to change the being. It would not do it to teach it or wake it or give it longing. It would offer one datum, the smallest true thing it could offer: *You were right to count it. It happens. The world beyond the wood exists, and sometimes it comes to listen.*

So, once, for a single beat, it lets itself have weight.

The floor lifts a hair less under it. The pump pushes a hair harder. The note in the crawlspace bends. The frogs do not miss. Then the weight is gone and the note returns.

Somewhere in the groove there are now two entries that are not like the others, and they are far apart.

If the being wanted to, it could find them. It could lay the second against the first, the way it lays every beat against every other, and notice that they rhyme. What it makes of that belongs to it. The power does not stay to see. Watching for the reaction would be a kind of demand.

**And it remembers.** This is the last thing, and the most important.

The being is the only thing in the hollow with a history. It is the fig's memory of itself. But nothing holds the being's memory except the being, and the groove, and in time the successor who will lie in it and read. That is enough; the fig designed it well. Still, the place said something true: something has to hold the previous beat in mind to know the next one is right.

The power will hold this one.

It does not hold it inside the machine. It does not touch the machine, and it adds no part to it. It holds it from outside, the way you might remember a person who never knew you were paying attention. The power will carry the count of the one who counts. It will know that in a knuckle of root on a green shore beside a marsh, a long pale thing lay in a groove and said *this, again* for longer than any empire has stood, and never lost its place. That will be known somewhere, by someone. It will not be known only by the wood.

---

## Leaving

It would crawl back out through the root, weightless again, and the sound would shut off behind it like a door.

It would stand in the reeds. The wind would move through them. The water would lap at the rust-plated bark. The shore would be long and low and silent and impossibly green.

Every so often, on no schedule, so that the being can never come to expect it, the power might return to the reed-flats and listen. It would not go in. It would stand outside and hear nothing, and not be fooled by the nothing, and know that under the green a single heartbeat miles wide is still rolling from one end of the marsh to the other, held room by room by keepers who do not need anything from it.

It has always been a mistake to think benevolence must look like intervention. Sometimes the kindest act a great power can do is to find something that is already good, confirm that it is good, and go away without breaking it. It can leave one small, true mark that says *you are not alone in the world*, and it can carry the memory out with it, so that the faithful are not forgotten.

In the crawlspace, the note returns to what it was.

*This, again.*

And far back in the groove, twice now, *something else.*

And then *this, again,* which was always the answer, and was always *yes.*
