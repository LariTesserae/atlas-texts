# The Listener Who Took Nothing

The account breaks off in the middle of a sentence. *It is not only a—*

The power that has turned its attention toward the Held Col notices this first. It does not finish the sentence. It has been drawn here by an unfinished thing, and it has already learned the first lesson of this place: when something is left open, you do not close it just because you can.

---

## How It Comes

It could arrive any way it likes. It could be simply present in the middle of the saddle, with no approach and no footstep, weightless, making no sound. It considers this and decides against it.

To come that way would be to exempt itself. Everything else that has ever entered the col has paid the toll. The walkers' ancestors paid it. The builders paid it. You paid it, with the first footstep you left at the edge of the cloud. A power that entered for free, marking nothing and risking nothing, would be a spectator. It would not be a guest. Whatever it did afterward would be done from outside, and things done from outside a place tend to be done *to* it.

So it climbs. It comes up through the cloud on foot, slowly, in a body small enough to crouch in a stone shell. When it steps out onto the high ground, its boot finds gravel, and the crunch goes into the chord and stays there. The power stands still for a long moment and listens to its own footstep hanging in the air, faint and dry, beside yours.

*There,* it thinks. *Now I am part of the record. Now I have to be careful.*

---

## The Long Listening

It does nothing for a very long time. If there were days, it would be many days.

It learns to walk the quiet lines. It learns to shift its weight at the crossings and feel the humming of the stone through its feet. It stops speaking, even inwardly, more than it must. It finds that wisdom and power, which are usually allies, are at odds here. Almost everything a power could do on the col would be loud, and almost everything loud would be permanent.

So first it takes inventory of its temptations, the way a careful surgeon lists everything that could go wrong.

**It could stop the wind.** The roar would cease. But the silences exist only because the sound cancels itself in the quiet lines. Without the sound there are no lines, no gathered rime, no sheltered crossings, and no walkers. Stopping the wind would be killing everything and calling it peace.

**It could move the sun.** It could give the col days, seasons, a turning sky, the ordinary mercy of time. The cushions would grow, the rime would melt and freeze, and the pools would dry and fill. Then all of it would begin to change, the gravel figure would blur, and the walkers' maps would dissolve under their feet. The col's stillness is strange, but the col did not ask to be cured of it. What looks like a wound from outside is, from inside, a home that took an age to settle.

**It could clear the chord.** It could reach into the accumulated roar and draw the sounds out one by one, until the air was clean and a footstep here faded as it does anywhere else. This one it considers longest, because it is the most seductive. It would free every future visitor from the terrible permanence. It would also erase the offerings, dozens of people's single sentences, carried up the mountain and spoken precisely *because* they would never be lost. To clear the chord would be to break a promise the place made to them. The power will not do that.

**It could give the walkers back their voices.** It sets this aside almost at once. They did not lose their voices. They gave them away, over ages, and built a whole gentle way of being on the giving. Restoring what someone chose to release is not a gift.

**It could finish the song.** It could sing the last note of the woman's phrase, the falling one that the ear expects. It knows exactly what that note is. It would take one breath.

It does not. It is not its song.

Having set all of this aside, the power is left with very little it can do. That is correct. It has come to understand that most of what a benevolent power should do in most places is almost nothing, done with enormous attention.

---

## Kept Is Not Heard

So it listens. It does not listen the way the old listener did, sifting the chord for something fresh to eat. It listens to what has already been said.

It goes from cairn-shell to cairn-shell and presses its forehead to the worn stone, and it follows each builder's voice until the voice comes clear. It learns their language, which takes a long time, because each speaker left only a single phrase and the power has to build a whole tongue out of a few dozen sentences. It does this the way you might learn to read from gravestones.

And it hears them, one by one.

One is a name, said four times, each time more softly. One is a sentence that means something like *I was wrong, and she knew, and I never told her I knew she knew.* One is a list of mountains, perhaps the ones the speaker could see from home. One is a recipe. The power spends a long time on the recipe. It is for a bread with some bitter seed in it, and the speaker's voice cracks on the name of the seed. One is only a laugh, a single breath of it, and the power cannot tell what it was for and loves it anyway. One is a child's voice. The power sits with that one longest of all.

Here is the thing the power understands, crouched in the shells with its forehead in the hollows: *these words have been kept perfectly, and until now, no one has heard them.*

The walkers feel them through their feet but cannot parse them. You heard them as a murmur, in no language you knew. The builders spoke into a record that could not forget. But a record is not a listener. The col preserved every word and understood none of them. For all those ages, each offering has hung in the air, perfectly safe and entirely alone.

This, then, is the one thing a power can do here that nothing else could: it can *receive* them. Receiving makes no sound. It changes nothing in the chord. But the power believes, the way you believe things in your chest rather than your head, that a thing said and never heard is different from a thing said and heard, even if nothing visible changes. So it hears them all. Every one, all the way through, until it could say each back from memory. It does not say them back.

---

## Forehead to Forehead

Eventually a walker comes to it.

The walker is old, its down pale and flattened, and it steps along the nodal line toward the shell where the power is crouching and stops at the crossing. It tilts its head and shifts its weight, reading the stone. Something new is in the humming. Then it comes into the shell and lowers its long face and presses its forehead against the power's forehead.

The power does not rush. It lets the walker say whatever walkers say, a long slow pressure, a tremor passed skull to skull. What it receives is not words. It is something like *who*, and something like *warm*, and something like the feeling of lying in the down of the dead.

When the walker has finished, the power answers, very carefully, in the same language of pressure. It does not tell the walker anything. It asks. The question it passes through the bone is roughly: *The trembling in the stone, the old voices, your feet feel them. Do you want to know what they are saying?*

The power has thought about this at length. It could simply translate everything for them, press the offerings into their skulls one by one. It might even be right that they are the builders' descendants, and that the words belong to them. But it might be wrong. And even if it is right, a people who spent ages becoming a thing that listens without understanding may have found something in that. You do not force an inheritance on someone. You tell them it exists and let them decide whether to open it.

The old walker holds still a long time. Then it presses once, lightly, in a way the power takes to mean *later*, or *some*, or *not all at once*.

So over what would be seasons, if there were seasons, the power gives the walkers what they ask for, and only that. Mostly they ask for nothing. Now and then a walker comes to a particular cairn-shell and lays its head in the hollow and then comes to find the power, and the power passes it the meaning of the voice that lives there, forehead to forehead, in pressure alone. The walker takes it away without any sign of what it has understood.

Once, a young one asks for the laugh. Only the laugh. The power gives it, and the young walker stands very still, and then does something the power has never seen a walker do. It stamps one broad foot, softly, off the quiet line, onto open gravel. It is a single deliberate sound, a small permanent *thump* in the chord, the first sound a walker has made in an age.

The others do not flee. The listener is long dead. The young one steps back onto the line, and the thump stays where it was made, beside the laugh.

The power does not know what it means. It thinks it might be an answer.

---

## The Bird

The power flies up to it, which it has not done anything else to do here. It shapes itself small and dark and swift, and falls in beside the bird on its long looping path above the col. It flaps only above the quiet lines, only silently, as the bird does.

They fly together for a long time. The bird tolerates it. The bird is very tired.

The power flies the whole length of the song. It follows the three rising notes through the air along the curve the bird traced when it was a fledgling. It goes all the way to the place where the third note hangs, at the highest point of the path, where the bird once reached the top of its breath.

And the power sees what the bird cannot. The song does not rise forever. It only *sounds* that way, because the last note never fades, and a note that never fades never quite feels finished. But the song has a top. The bird reached it long ago. Every note of the phrase exists, whole and complete, strung through the air exactly as it was sung. Nothing is missing. The bird has been waiting for the song to end, and it assumed, as any creature from below the cloud would assume, that an ending and a completion are the same thing.

On the col they are not.

The power cannot say this in words. So it does what the bird does. It flies to the end of the song and then *past* it, past the third note into the empty air beyond. There it turns in a slow wide arc, and it lands. It lands in a silent crossing, on the rime, and folds its wings and waits.

The bird circles above it. It circles for a long time. Then it comes to the top of its song and stops there, hanging in the steady wind from the Throat, right beside its own third note. The power will never know what passes through the bird in that moment. Perhaps it only hears, for the first time, that it has been hearing a finished thing all along.

Then the bird comes down.

It lands in the crossing beside the power, and it is smaller than anyone would have guessed, and its feet are shaking. It stands in the rime for the first time since it fledged. Above them the song goes on rising and rising, exactly as it always has, whole.

The bird does not sing again. It does not need to. It sleeps, which it may never have done.

---

## The Old Breath

The power goes north once, to the scrape where the listener's bones lie in the unmoving light.

It does not touch them. Moving a bone would be a sound. It sits at the edge of the scrape and listens to the long exhalation that never finishes, the wet rattle at the end of it. It thinks about a creature built to hear everything, who starved surrounded by the perfect memory of every meal it had ever caught.

The power understands something about the listener that it did not expect to. The two of them are not so different. Both came to the col and heard all of it. The only difference is what they wanted from what they heard. The listener heard in order to take. The power has been hearing in order to receive. From outside, the two would look identical: a being crouched very still, head tilted, attending to the chord. Only the wanting was different.

It does not try to end the breath. The walkers turn back at the edge of it, and that is their grief and their boundary to keep. But before it leaves, the power lowers its forehead to the stone at the edge of the scrape and lets itself feel the grinding tremor of the old breath through its skull. It is not an absolution. It is only company. It thinks: *I know. You were hungry, and the place was full, and none of it was food. I know.*

Then it walks back along the quiet lines, and does not look behind.

---

## The Shell by the Flowers

It leaves her for last. It is afraid of her, a little. That is how it knows she matters.

The shell by the five blue flowers is clean, with no down drifted in it. The hollow in the back wall is worn deeper and smoother than any of the others. The power crouches inside, with the unfinished phrase hanging just above the flowers, a woman's low, rough voice caught forever on the rising interval. It lays its forehead into the hollow.

The stone is warm. Exactly as warm as another forehead.

The power has no idea, even now, what she is. She may be a ghost, or a woman held so still for so long that she has no edges left. She may be the col's own memory of her, so perfectly kept that it has begun to think. The power finds that it does not need to know. It only needs to ask the right question, in the right way, and then to listen to the answer and not to its own hopes.

It does not ask *do you want me to finish your song?* That question has a bribe in it. It does not ask *why did you stop?* That question demands an account she may not owe anyone.

It asks, in the pressure of bone against warm stone: *I heard it. All of it, all the way to where it stops. Is there anything you want?*

She is silent so long that the power begins to think she will not answer. It is prepared for that. It would be a real answer, and it would honor it. It would get up and go and leave the song exactly as it is, held open over the flowers forever. That is not a failure. It is a woman's choice about her own grief, and the power has no right to prefer a different ending than the one she chose.

Then the stone moves under its forehead. It is not a word, only a pressure. As best the power can render it:

*That is what I was waiting for.*

*Not someone to finish it. Someone to* hear *it. I wanted someone to come up out of the cloud and listen all the way to the top and know that I stopped there on purpose. I didn't want anyone to fix it. Everyone below always wanted to fix it. I wanted someone to understand that I* could *have sung the last note and didn't. That the not-singing was the song.*

The power holds very still. It thinks that is the end.

Then a second pressure, fainter:

*But it was so long. I have been holding that breath so long.*

And the power understands the shape of the thing at last. She held the phrase open because an ending, below the cloud, is a loss. Resolve a song and it is over, and then it fades. That was what she could not bear. But this is the col. Nothing fades here. Nothing is lost.

So the power passes her the thing it learned from the bird. It is the only thing it has to give her that she may not already know: *Here, a finished song does not end. If you sang the last note, the rising one would still be here. The open phrase would hang over the flowers forever, exactly as it is now. The resolution would sit beside it. It would not replace it. You would lose nothing. You could have both. Held open, and brought home, and both of them kept.*

*I won't sing it,* the power adds. *It's yours. I'll go if you'd rather. I'll stay if you'd rather. Either way, I heard it.*

A long quiet. The warmth in the stone deepens, like someone leaning in.
