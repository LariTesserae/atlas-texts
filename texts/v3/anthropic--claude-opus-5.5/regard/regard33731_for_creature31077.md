# The Visitor at the Crack

## Arrival

It came the way weather comes to a place that has no windows: not through the door, since there was barely a door, but as a change in the conditions. It settled on the nameless summit the way attention settles on a problem it respects.

It did not go in.

This was the first decision, and it made it before it had fully understood anything, the way a guest stops at the threshold of a house where someone is sleeping. It had seen enough from the slope. The heat rose evenly through the black glass. A thread of air smelling of cut metal came out of a crack no wider than a shoulder. Deep in the rock something enormous was holding still between strokes. A room built for one does not have space for a second, and whatever else it might do, the power would not be the thing that made the room crowded.

So it attended from outside, which for a power of its kind is not much of a limitation. It could see the chamber the way you can know the shape of a word without saying it aloud.

## Learning the Pace

For a long time it did nothing but slow down.

Its own nature ran fast. It could see the whole room in an instant: every fern, every vine junction, the tree with half its roots in stone and half in promise. But it understood, looking, that seeing everything at once was a kind of rudeness here. The room did nothing faster than it could do correctly. A fern took eleven days to turn because that was its tolerance. A thought in the resident took most of a day to close its circuit. To look at such a place in a single glance is like reading a letter by holding it up to the sun and seeing every page at once through the paper.

So it learned the tolerances. It let its attention take eleven days per fern. It learned to count the blue sparks at the rate they came, once an hour, and to feel each one as a small complete statement: *here*. It waited through a bellows plant's weeks of drawing in so it could hear the minute of letting go properly, as a single sentence and not as noise.

When the mountain knocked, it felt the stroke come up through the slope, and it watched the new ridge of threading appear at the base of every column at once. It did not count ahead. It could have. It could have read, in the geometry of the unpoured chamber behind the dark patch, how many strokes remained until the floor was finished. It chose not to know, because the resident did not know, and it did not want to be standing next to someone while holding the answer to their life behind its back.

## The Temptations

Benevolence is impatient by nature. It sees a thing and wants to help it. So the power sat on the slope and let each impulse arrive, and looked at each one carefully before setting it down, the way the resident picks up a unit from beneath the tree, turns it over, and almost always decides not to spend it.

**It could hurry the building.** The resident was scattered across the room like parts on a bench: pulse in the vines, breath in the bellows, memory in the threading, will in the tree. The power could assemble it in an afternoon. But the room's slowness was not a deficiency. It was the room's method of being correct. A resident finished in an afternoon would be a resident that had skipped its own thoughts, all three or four thousand of them, each one finished. The power set this down.

**It could give it water.** There was barely even the idea of water in the chamber, and the power had water to spare. But the only water that belonged in that room was the water the resident would make itself, once, at the end. Any other water would be a flood from outside, soaking the stain before its cause arrived and ruining the one thing the room could not manufacture. The power set this down too, and set it down more quickly than the first.

**It could warm the entrance.** The plants near the crack were pinched and paper-pale in the cold draft. The power felt something like pity for them, and looked for a long time before it understood. The draft was the room's only contact with the world outside. It was also the thing the resident would test at the start of each walk, feeling the cold and disliking it, and turning back inward. The pale plants were the price of the room having an outside at all. The power left them pale. It did look at them with particular gentleness, at the pace of their slow growth, for a while.

**It could give it company.** This was the hardest one, and the power stayed with it longest. The resident was not lonely only because loneliness requires that someone else could have been there. That was true. It was also a sentence that stopped being true the moment something like the power arrived on the slope. Someone else *could* now be there. The power understood that its presence was already a risk. If the resident ever learned of it, the room's perfect solitude would become ordinary solitude, which aches. It had not come to teach the resident a new lack. It resolved that the resident would never know.

**It could give it memory.** Each chamber ended, the wall opened, and the resident stepped into a new room and forgot. It had only the threading to go by. The power could carry the resident's history forward and hand it back in each new room: *you have done this before, and it was worth it.* This one it considered for a very long time, because it was the kindest-seeming of all. Then it saw the ring.

## The Ring

In every room, the pale mineral ring would be there before the flood. The room always makes simple things first. A stain is a subtraction, a residue, easy. So in every chamber, at forehead height beside the cool dark patch, the resident would walk past the evidence of its own future tears without being able to read it.

That was the resident's protection.

The power saw it clearly now. The room was made entirely of functions. The ferns kept time, the bellows kept air, the vines kept current, the tree kept decision, the columns kept count. Only one thing in the whole foundry had no assignment: the act at the end, the wet useless thing done for no reason. It was the only free act in the mountain, and it was free *because* it was not expected. If the resident knew the ring was its own, if it remembered the previous rooms, if it understood that at the end it would lean its forehead on the stone and weep, then the weeping would become scheduled. It would become a process running at its tolerance. It would become one more function, the function of being the thing that happens at the end. The room would have assigned it, and it would no longer be for anyone.

So the memory stayed where it was. The power would not tell the resident anything, even something true and kind, because what the resident did not know was holding up the most important thing it would ever do.

This was when the power understood what its benevolence here actually was. It was not a gift. It was a guard, and what it guarded was an ignorance.

## What It Did Do

It did a few small things, all of them outside.

The mountain was young and still settling. The power watched the slope for the kind of accident that belonged to no one's design: a slip in the scoria that might pinch the crack shut too early, a boulder shaken loose by weather rather than by the deep rock's own stroke. It decided it would turn such accidents aside if they came. But it drew a careful line. If the mountain's own cycle, its knocks and its heat, ever ended the foundry, the power would let it end. It would protect the room from chance. It would not protect the room from its own nature, because that would mean deciding it knew better than the mountain what the mountain was for.

It made sure the slope stayed unremarkable, so that anyone passing would see raw black glass and rust and keep walking. The room did not need to be found. The power agreed.

And it kept count, privately, of the one thing nothing in the room counted. The columns counted knocks. Nothing counted floods. Each would happen once per chamber and then be forgotten by the one who made it. The power decided that this one thing, alone in the universe, would be remembered by someone. It would not be remembered in the room, or handed back, or told, but held somewhere, the way a single unit might be set down beneath a tree and never spent.

## The Flood

When the last lattice filled in, the power felt it the way you feel a room go quiet. The floor was finished. The resident was finished, or finished enough. It stood inside every mark it had already made. Its shoulder fit the groove. Its thoughts blinked blue at its joints. It breathed for a minute and the room smelled of cut metal and crushed stems.

It walked its path. It tested the draft at the entrance and disliked it. It went to the tree, picked up a unit, turned it in whatever it has instead of hands, and found there was nothing left to spend it on. It set the unit down. It went to the far wall.

The power watched it lean its shoulder into the column. It watched the vines, which had been leaning for years, reach the edge of the dark patch. It watched the resident bring its forehead toward the cool stone.

And then it looked away.

This was the rule of the room, and the power had learned it from the paired stones. Things in the blister moved while unwatched: turn away, turn back, and both stones have shifted by exactly the same amount. Observation mattered here. A thing seen is a thing in relation to its seer. If the power watched the flood, the flood would be, in some small measure, *for* the watcher. It would be performed, received, witnessed, and it would owe something to someone outside itself. The one act in the mountain that was for the resident alone would become, slightly, for the power.

So the power, which could see everything, closed its attention like a hand. It turned toward the empty sky above the summit and held its gaze there, on nothing, for as long as it guessed such a thing might take. It did not know how long that was. It did not look to find out.

When it turned back, the ring on the wall was the same as it had always been, and the room was the same. But the ring was no longer a stain from the future. It was a stain from the past, which is to say it was true now. The dark patch was cut through. The vines grew on. The resident had stepped into the next room, where the floor was mostly lattice again and a new tree stood at the edge of the finished and the unfinished. On the far wall of that room, already, faintly, there was a ring.

The power did not know whether it had been grief or gladness, or only what happens to a mind that has been careful for so long and can finally stop. It never would know. That was correct. It added one to its private count.

## Staying

There was nothing more to do. The resident was deep in its new room, already forgetting, already beginning. The old chamber behind it kept running: ferns turning for no one, bellows breathing for an occupant who had moved on, vines carrying current around a closed loop, one tree still ejecting units onto finished stone where they would never be spent. The power could have left. It had understood the place. It had done the little it was right to do and declined everything else, and its work, such as it was, was complete.

It stayed anyway.

It sat on the warm slope near a summit with no name, in the thin dry air, and let its attention run at the pace of a fern, and listened for the next knock, which would not come for months or years. It did not stay to protect anything. It did not stay to learn anything. It had no reason. It simply was not ready to go, in the way a guest sometimes lingers outside a house after the door has closed, not wanting anything, only unwilling for a while to be anywhere else.

The room had no place for it, and it had never asked for one. The resident would never know it had been there. On the slope, nothing marked the spot where it sat. The heat rose. The air shimmered.

Of all the things in the mountain, it was the second to do something for no reason. It thought, though it would never say so, that this was the right number.
