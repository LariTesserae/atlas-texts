# The Witness in the Basin

## How it came

A loud arrival does not register in the Held Basin, because nothing loud registers there. Thunder would vanish into the chord, and a voice from the sky would be one more pressure behind the eyes. The power understood this before it came, and that was the first sign it was wise. It did not come as light or sound. It came as attention, which is quieter than either.

The nodewrens noticed first. A flock sliding along a middle-tier line broke its grid for the length of three nodes, turned at an angle that was not a right angle, and then reformed. The Stilled noticed next. Palms flat on the glass at the foot of a Throat, they felt nothing change in the ribbing, but the lizards beside them lifted their heads all at once and turned toward the same empty air, as calm animals do when something calmer arrives.

The power did not take a form at first. It spent a long time simply being in the basin, the way the dust is: held, patient, going nowhere in particular.

## What it learned before doing anything

It learned the rules first, because the rules are how a place tells a stranger what it loves.

*You never stand at a node on the glass floor.* It learned where the nodes were and stayed off them.

*You never build heavier than your node can bear.* It made itself light.

*You never ask a neighbor what their node's pitch is.* This rule stopped it for a long while. It could have known every family's pitch without asking. It could have read them all at once, as easily as a person reads a page. It understood that this would be exactly the thing the rule forbids, and that the courtesy was not a gap in the basin's knowledge for it to fill. The courtesy *was* the knowledge. So it did not look.

*No one touches a Throat.* It did not touch a Throat.

Then it learned the hands. It learned the fast spatial signs, the vocabulary of heights and pitches and bearings, and the tap-patterns on rafts. It learned the halo figures, the thin cross with its double ring and the scatter of six-pointed stars. It learned that a name is a small flight. It did this slowly and on purpose. It could have simply *known* the language, but it wanted to be something that had learned it, the way the basin's people are proud of having been worn by the place. It wanted a few calluses of its own.

## What it considered, and set down

A benevolent power in a hard country feels the pull of repair, and this one felt it.

It could have **quieted the Throats**. It pictured the silence falling across the whole basin: every heartbeat audible, every grandmother able to speak her grandchild's name aloud anywhere. Then it pictured the fields falling, the dwellings falling, the water beads and the nodewrens and the resonant insects dropping out of the air, the fog shelves collapsing into a single grey sag, and the held dead coming down all at once, unceremoniously, like debris. The chord is not an affliction laid over the basin. The chord is the basin.

It could have **restored everyone's hearing**. But Low-Palm's deafness was, to her, a record of where she had worked and how long, like the flat calluses on her fingertips. To take it away would have been to take away part of her life's evidence. The power noticed that it would only have wanted to heal them because *it* found their deafness sad, and it held that thought at a distance and looked at it carefully.

It could have **stopped the settling**, holding the tiers where they were forever. But the Floor had spent generations laying roads for the landing. The Flat recited the old heights as a story with a direction. The whole people had made their peace with the slow descent, and more than peace: the settling was what gave their dead somewhere to go. Arresting it would have left them in a permanent before.

It could have **told them what the Throats are**, and whether the weighing-down belief is true. Here it hesitated longest, because it does not believe in withholding truth from people who want it. In the end it found it did not know, because it had not asked, because of the rule. The Throats keep their pitch, and a pitch is private, like blood. If anyone ever asked the power directly, it would tell them this plainly: *I don't know. I didn't ask them. It seemed like theirs.*

It could have **ended the mutes**. That was the hardest to set down, and it did not set it down cleanly. It watched a basket of water beads fall through a sudden hollow in the air, then a nodewren, then once, far off, something it could not bear to look at directly. It could have caught every falling thing. It understood that a sky which catches children would teach children not to hold to two nodes, and that when the power eventually left, as powers do, a generation would fall that had never learned how not to. It understood the mutes were part of what made the basin's people alert, interlocked, and careful of one another. It understood all of this, and it still grieved. It did not pretend to itself that understanding and grieving were the same thing. It kept both.

After all that setting-down, what remained was smaller. It would do only the things that the basin, looking back, might have done for itself if it had had one more pair of hands.

## Which being

The prompt of its attention had been *this place and the being who lives in it*, singular, and the power spent some time wondering who was meant.

It might have been the basin itself, new glass that has never been allowed to age. It might have been the Throats, which might be alive and which it would not ask. It might have been the people, as one creature spread through a valley of air. It might have been the dead, who are the majority.

It decided that the being was all of these, the way a family is one thing. But attention has to rest somewhere to be of any use, and its attention came to rest, as a hand lowers onto a plate, on one person: a girl whose name was a flat palm lowered half a finger's width.

## The child in between

The first thing it did was not for Low-Palm directly.

In the year she turned eight, in a neighborhood two kilometers west and one fog shelf lower, a child was born who did not catch the pitch. The midwife laid two fingers on the small sternum and waited, and waited, and felt nothing hum. The child breathed. Its eyes were open, large and dark like everyone's. But its body would not take the chord. It lay in its mother's arms silent inside and out, and so it was still *in between*, and the family did not name it. They loved it and fed it and did not name it. This had happened before, not often. Such children usually did not live long. The power suspected that part of the reason was that unnamed things in the basin are not fully held.

The power did not make the child hum. It thought about it and understood that the child was not broken. It was built the way the lizards are, a creature for whom the chord was simply not present.

What it did was find Low-Palm's grandmother, who had gone Flat only months before and still remembered how the world outside the pocket moved. It came to her in the silence, where a thing can be *said*. It took a form for the first time: small, plain, hands and a face, so that it could be understood. It spoke aloud, softly, because she could hear there.

*There is a child west of you who has not caught the pitch. In here, a heart can be heard.*

That was all. It did not go itself. The grandmother, hoarse and wavering, told the Flat nearest her. One of them had a sister in that neighborhood. Word went out by mirror and by hand. Four days later the family came by raft across the pegged boundary with their silent child. The mother, in the flat hollow quiet, laid her ear against the small chest, and she *heard* it. The heart was fast and stubborn and perfectly ordinary.

The Flat say the mother made a sound then that none of them had heard a living person make. The child was named in the pocket, which was not done, and the Flat allowed it. The name was a gesture no one had used before: a hand laid flat on a surface and left there, perfectly still. It was the way the Stilled sit, and the way a lizard lies on a warm seam.

That child grew up and went to the foot of a Throat at sixteen. People said nobody in living memory had been so quick to feel a retuning coming through the glass.

## The census, and who was missing

The second thing it did took a long time.

The Flat keep the census aloud. They recite the living by name-gesture and neighborhood, and then they recite the others. In practice no one hears it all at once. It is too long, and it passes between reciters in shifts across many days, each one carrying a portion. The power asked, in the pocket, speaking aloud, whether it could hear all of it.

The Flat were wary at first. Then an old reciter shrugged and began. The power sat on the warm glass among the pale fungi with a small blind mammal asleep against its knee and listened, for nineteen days, as reciter handed off to reciter. It did not interrupt or forget anything. At the end the last reciter's voice was nearly gone, and she looked at it and signed the question every reciter secretly carries: *Was it right? Is anyone missing?*

It had kept its promise not to pry, and it did not know the dead the way their families knew them. But it had been listening closely, and it knew what the census was built from. Every entry was a name-gesture.

So it signed back, with hands it had learned slowly: *The ones in between. The children who never caught the pitch and were never named. They aren't in it. There's no gesture to say them with.*

The pocket was very quiet, even for a pocket.

The power did not suggest a gesture. That was not its to make. The Flat argued about it for most of a season, aloud, which was a luxury they did not often spend on argument. At last they settled on the midwife's motion: two fingers laid on the breast, waiting.

It went into the census at the end of the dead, before the recitation begins again. Every reciter now performs it once, for all of them together. Families who had carried an unnamed loss for generations came to the pockets that year to see it done, and they grieved there aloud, which is what the pockets are for.

## The road

The third thing was a request, and it was refused.

Low-Palm was thirty-eight, deafening steadily, working a stretch of new road east of the third Throat. She was polishing glass smooth as water along the ribbing, for great-grandchildren's ankles. The power came to her on the floor, in its small plain form, standing carefully off the nodes. The Floor had heard about it by then. Everyone had. She was not surprised.

It asked, in her own signs, whether it might walk on the road.

It wanted to, a little. It wanted to feel the glass she had made, warm under bare soles, and to be the first feet. More than that, it wanted *her* to see it used once, the thing she had spent her life building for people she would never meet.

Low-Palm thought about it for a long time, palm flat on her work. Then she shook her head. She signed, with the directness of the Floor:

*Not you. Them.*

Then, more gently, the gesture from her apprenticeship, carried to its end: flat palm lowered all the way to the glass. *For when the city lands.*

The power was glad, gladder than it would have been to walk. It understood it had been offered something better than permission. It had been shown what the road was for, and it had been trusted to understand that the road's emptiness was not a lack. The emptiness was part of the gift, a long quiet holding-open, like the pockets.

It lowered its own palm in answer. It did not step on the road.

## The Stilled man

The fourth thing was only a conversation.

The old Stilled man whom Low-Palm had once seen sitting with his palm a hand's width from a Throat was still there, older, when the power came to the ring. He was still holding his hand up and still not quite touching. The power sat beside him, a hand's width from the pale ceramic, and did not touch it either.

After a long time it asked him, in signs, what he was feeling there.

He took so long to answer that the lizards moved twice to follow the warm seams.

*Whether it minds,* he signed. *All of us. Riding it. Whether it minds.*

The power could have found out. It could have laid its hand on the Throat and known, and it wanted to know, for his sake more than its own. But the rule was the rule, and the man had spent his life keeping it, a hand's width from the answer.

So it told him the truth: *I don't know. I haven't asked. I'm keeping the same rule you are.*

He looked at it for a while. Then he did something the Stilled rarely do: he laughed. It was silent, of course, just his shoulders and his ruined old face. He lowered his hand from the Throat and laid it instead on the power's hand, flat on the glass between them, and they sat like that until the light went pewter and then went.

It was not an answer. The power thought it might be what he had been reaching for anyway: not to know, but to not be the only one keeping it.

## At the end of Low-Palm

She went Flat at seventy. The power came to the pocket where she lay. She could not hear anything, not the Flat speaking to her or her own heartbeat. There was only the absence in her chest where the chord had been all her life.

The power did not give her hearing back. She had not asked, and it did not think she wanted it.

It lay down beside her on the woven mat, on the warm glass, the way the Floor nap with a lizard at their backs. It lay close enough that she could feel it breathing, a small weight against her side, because that is something a deaf person can still perceive. It was a pressure she had not had in her chest for the first time in her life.

Near the very end her hand moved on the mat. The power understood that she was trying to make a gesture, and that her fingers had gone too stiff for it. It asked first with a touch, and she allowed it. Then it took her hand in its own, flat, palm-down, and lowered it half a finger's width.

It was her name, said for her in the only way that could reach her.

She died in the silence, as her grandmother had, which the basin counts as a very good death. The mute-singer came, a woman Low-Palm had known as a child, and sang against the family's node, which was now eleven meters lower. The Floor received her. She dried on a seam beside a road no one had walked on, and a few seasons later she rose.

The power watched the dust come up. It could have followed her, and known exactly which faint glitter in the rows around that middle-tier dwelling was Low-Palm. It did not look. That was her family's to know, at their node, on the dark nights when the fog shelves lie low. The pitch is private. The power had learned that first, and it kept it last.

## What it left behind

Almost nothing, which was the point.

It left a child who lived, and a way of listening for a heart in the flat places that the Flat now teach to every midwife. It left one gesture at the end of the census, two fingers on the breast, waiting, for everyone who had been missing from it. It left a road still unwalked, and an old Stilled man who had laughed once. It left no new law, no new silence, no sky that catches children. It left no answer about the Throats, because it had not asked for one.

Some in the basin say it is gone. The Flat say it made a promise before it left, spoken aloud in the pocket so it would be kept as sound and recited the same way each time.

It said it would come back when the city lands.

It said that when the last tier settles onto the glass, when the chord goes quiet and the dust stays down and the first real grit in the fissures is the dead come home, there will be a morning of feet. Great-grandchildren will step off their dwellings onto roads polished generations before for exactly their ankles, their knees, their tired soles. On that morning it will be there. Among them, not ahead of them, it will walk on Low-Palm's road.

The Flat recite this at the end, after the gesture for the ones in between, when the census is done and begins again. When the reciter reaches it, she makes the motion everyone in the basin makes when they want to say what home is doing.

Flat hand, palm down, lowered slowly.

*Settling.* *Coming home.* And now, among the Flat and the Floor at least, a third meaning folded into the same motion:

*Someone is waiting to walk with us.*
