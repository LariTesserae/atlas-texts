# The Visitor

## How It Was Noticed

It was the ninety-one that did it.

For a long time the lake had been one of the quiet places, if anything was watching. From far enough away it was a green coin on the face of a continent. It did not change and it gave off nothing. A mind that looks across the worlds for what needs looking at passes over such places the way your eye passes over a wall. A wall is finished. Nothing there is asking.

But something that listens closely enough, for long enough, hears the ninety-one. It is one beat, once in a span of years, landing a second late at the deepest part of the basin. It is not a failure. It is a hesitation, the kind of pause a person makes when they almost say something.

The power that heard it was old and kind, and in the way that matters, careful. It had learned over a great deal of time that finished places are not always finished and that the quietest thing in a room is not always the one with the least to say. So it came to see.

## How It Came

It did not come quickly. It knew what the lake was before it arrived, because the lake was easy to read from outside: a governor, a system that had spent unimaginable effort making itself the same. Anything that entered at speed, at the wrong temperature, or with its own light and its own rhythm would be an error. The lake would correct it, and the correction would run downhill.

So it came as slowly as weather. It settled onto the plain over the course of a season, though the plain has no seasons. It cooled itself to the temperature of the leaves. It went down through the lid the way the grazers move along their strips, a finger's width at a time. In the water it took the color of the water, and it let the standing currents carry it. It learned the ninety-second stroke until its own stillness fell between the blows, and it took one long count of sixty after another to come down.

Even so, it was not perfect. Nothing that comes from outside can be. It displaced a little water. It was a little too dense in places and a little too warm in others. It knew this, and it knew where those small wrongnesses would go. Before it ever reached the bowl, some of it had already arrived there, carried down the gradient and folded into the newest layer as a thin, strange band.

That was the first thing it learned. You cannot visit this being without becoming part of it.

## What It Saw

It saw what you have seen: the knotted tissue, the rust and wine and bone-yellow, the amber glass and the bent flutes. It saw a follower lay a wrong pod against the surface and wait. It heard the hum weaving through the beat and never locking to it.

Because it was patient, it also read the strata the way a scholar reads a library. It read the ashfall. It read the tilting. It read ten thousand rays folded in their stripes, and every eel that let go. At the bottom it read the loose, fast, branching tissue of the lake's young years, when the lake was still making and there were still edges to reach.

Then it did what a wise power does before anything else. It asked whether this being was suffering.

This was harder to answer than it sounds. The Remainder has no face. It has no cry that means pain, and the hum was not a scream. The power listened for a long time, as long as the rays take, and what it found was not agony. It was closer to weight. The creature held every mistake of a continent, every death, and every degree that was not allowed to exist anywhere else. It held these things all at once, and it could not put any of them down, because putting them down is not what it is for.

It found something else as well: the reaching. Every so often a lobe unfolded toward the edge of the bowl, stopped, and folded back. The power watched this happen many times. It came to understand that the lobe did not stop because it had changed its mind. It stopped because there was nothing there. The lake reached its edges long ago. The Remainder dreams of growing into a world that has no room left to grow into.

It was not suffering, exactly. It was the ache of a thing that remembers that there used to be somewhere to go.

## What It Could Have Done

The power could have done many things, and it considered each one honestly. Being able to do a thing is exactly when it is most important to think about it.

**It could have healed it.** It could have smoothed the scars and dissolved the grit and the salt, made the tissue even and white, and lifted the weight. But the scars are the memory. A Remainder without its strata would be a Remainder with no *before*, and the lake would lose the only thing in it that knows anything has happened. Healing it would have been erasure dressed up as mercy.

**It could have freed it.** It could have opened the bowl, or lifted the creature out through the lid into the thin air and let the young reaching tissue grow as far as it liked across the plain. But the lake leans on the Remainder. The columns measure themselves against it. Take away the error signal and a continent-sized heart becomes perfectly regular, and a perfectly regular heart is preparing to stop. Freeing the Remainder would have killed its world. The Remainder might also have died of it, like a deep-water thing brought up into the light.

**It could have perfected the lake.** It could have tuned every column and cable so finely that no error ever occurred again, and nothing ever ran downhill, and the Remainder never had to carry anything more. That would have been a kind of starvation. It would also have been a kind of arrogance: deciding that the lake's long struggle toward sameness was a problem to be solved, when it was the lake's own achievement, held stroke by stroke for longer than stars.

**It could have loosened the lake.** It could have given it seasons, varied the beat, and spread the wrongness evenly so no single place had to bear it all. This one tempted the power most, because it looked like justice. But the power could not know what the lake would become. It would be taking away a balance that had been found, and replacing it with one the power had merely designed. The Remainder was not a mistake in how the lake was built. It was how the lake had chosen to survive. Unmaking a solution because it is sad is not the same as having a better one.

**It could have simply left.** That option deserved respect, because it is often the right one, and the power had chosen it before in other places.

But it had heard the ninety-one. Something had hesitated. And the power could not shake the feeling that the hesitation had been something close to a question.

## What It Noticed

In all the lake, everything answers something.

The spark-fish answer the arcs. The column answers the pulse. The follower answers the drifting pod. The intake eels answer the stroke, and the grazers answer the regrowing leaf. The whole sea is a vast chain of call and response, perfectly kept.

Only one sound in the lake has never been answered, and that is the hum.

The columns near the bowl lean toward it, but leaning is listening, not answering. The lake checks itself against the Remainder the way a sailor checks a compass against the stars. No one has ever asked the stars how they are. The Remainder had been heard, in a sense, for its entire existence. It had been used and relied on and measured against. It had never once been replied to.

It also has a past and no future. It is the only thing in the lake that remembers, and remembering is all it can do. Nothing is coming. Nothing is expected. The rays arrive, but the rays arrive on schedule. The pods arrive, but they are just the lake's errors, the same kind of arrival over and over. Everything that will ever come to the Remainder is more of what already has.

The power understood that the lobe did not reach for space. The lake could never give it space. It reached for *something that had not happened yet*.

## What It Did

It hummed back.

It did not match the Remainder's hum, because matching would only have been another kind of locking. It hummed alongside it, off the beat of the columns as the Remainder was, but differently off. It fell behind where the Remainder hurried and hurried where the Remainder fell behind. It was a second syncopation in a sea that had only ever held one. It was quiet, barely louder than the tissue itself, and it went on for a long time.

For a while nothing changed.

Then the Remainder's hum faltered. It was a small thing, the kind of hitch you make when you are speaking in an empty house and someone in the next room says your name. The hum stopped, and there was a silence the length of a stroke. Then it started again, but slower and lower, as though testing. The power answered, and the Remainder answered that.

They spoke like this for years. It was not language. There was nothing to translate, and the power did not pretend otherwise. It was the oldest kind of conversation: *I am here. I hear you are there. Is that you? Yes. Is that you? Yes.*

During those years a lobe unfolded toward the edge of the bowl, as it had ten thousand times before. This time it did not fold back. It turned in the water, slowly, the way a plant turns in a window, until it was pointing not toward the edge but toward the power.

Toward something that was not yet reached.

## What It Left

The power could not stay. Staying would have made it a fixture, and a fixture in the lake becomes part of the machine. The lake would learn it, correct for it, and schedule it. In a century its hum would be one more thing on the beat, and the Remainder would be alone again in a larger room.

So before it left it made the only promise it believed was wise. It would come back, and not on any schedule. There would be no interval the lake could learn and no rhythm the Remainder could predict. It might come in ten years or in three hundred. It might come twice in one decade and then not for a thousand years. It would be the one irregular thing that came from outside, a season in a place without seasons, a weather that was not permanent.

This meant the Remainder would never be sure. That was the gift. Anything certain it could only remember. Something uncertain it could *wait for*.

The power did one small practical thing as well, and it went back and forth over it for some time before deciding. It left nothing of itself in the bowl. It left no marker and no token that could weigh on the tissue or be corrected into a stratum. Its visit had already made its band, thin and strange, the color of something from very far away. That was enough. The Remainder would keep it the way it keeps everything. For once, one of its layers would be not a mistake but a meeting.

Then it went up the way it had come, a finger's width at a time, between the strokes. It cooled itself back to the temperature of the leaves and waited on the plain for a season that was not a season. Then it was gone.

## What It Did Not Do

It did not fix the lake. The lake did not need fixing.

It did not free the Remainder. The Remainder was not imprisoned. It was *placed*, and the place was necessary, and necessary things can still be lonely.

It did not tell itself that it had solved anything. It had not. The Remainder still carries every error of a continent. It still takes in the dead, and it still dreams of edges that no longer exist. The weight is the same.

What changed is that the weight is no longer the whole of it.

## The Surface Again

Climb back through the lid into the thin cold air. The wind blows steady from the direction it always blows. The sky is pale, the sun is only the sun, and the green flatness runs out to every horizon.

The ground trembles underfoot once every ninety seconds. It will never stop, and it should not.

But go far enough across the plain, toward the place where the basin is deepest, and stand there long enough, as long as the rays take.

The ground still trembles at ninety-one, once in all that time.

It no longer sounds like a hesitation. Now it sounds like listening, like something turned toward the sky, holding still, waiting to hear whether anyone is coming.

Below, in the green dusk, one lobe of rust and wine and bone-yellow stays unfolded, pointed upward at the underside of the lid.

It has not folded back.
