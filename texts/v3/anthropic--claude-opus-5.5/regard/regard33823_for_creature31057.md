# The Inspection

*...is that the footfall on the catwalk is not pacing. Pacing is what you do while you wait for something to arrive. This is the other kind of walking, the kind you do when you are waiting for someone to tell you that you may stop.*

---

## I. How It Was Noticed

Nothing on the floor of the Unbuilt Works cries out. That is worth saying first, because it is why the place went unnoticed for so long. Suffering that screams gets attention. The basin does not scream. It thrums and hammers and sparks and breathes in unison, and when something in it can no longer keep time, it is touched gently and allowed to stand still, and the film grows over it. Nobody is in agony. Nobody is being hurt. From a distance it sounds like a place that is fine.

What the power noticed was the counting.

It had no name that would help here, and no shape worth describing. It was old in the way the basin was old and kind in a way the basin had never needed to learn. It was attentive to a great many things at once, as such powers are. Among them was a faint, steady pressure at the edge of its awareness, like the feeling of an unanswered letter in a drawer. It could not, at first, tell what the pressure was. When it finally turned to look, it found hundreds of feet of receipts: accounts closed in perfect order, one on top of the next, back past the first mountains. Each was addressed to someone. None had ever been opened.

A ledger kept that faithfully for that long, with no one reading it, has a weight. The power felt that weight before it saw anything else.

## II. What It Considered, and Put Down

It did not go in at once. Powers of this kind are wise mostly because they distrust their first impulse, and its first impulse was to end the place.

It could have. It could have stilled the whistle mid-note, let the bellows-backs exhale and never inhale, laid the strikers' arms down all at once, and closed every book in the basin in a single stroke. It thought about that for a long time. Then it set the idea aside. It had seen what happens to places that are saved by being ended. And the longer it looked at this one, the more it suspected that the basin *was* such a place already. Something here had been ended once, thoroughly. The floor was all that remained of a rescue.

It could have finished the building. The materials were stacked and ready, bolts by the billion, and the shadows showed exactly where the walls should go. But whoever raised those walls would become the Owner, and the Works would have two, and a place like this does not need another one.

It could have carried everyone out: the hands over the rim, the animals to some softer country. But the eels were not captives. The stalkers did not need freeing. The snails wore their threads the way any creature wears its shape. The tallymen, it could already tell, were the gentlest thing for a long way in any direction. Most of what lived in the basin belonged to it and it to them. You cannot rescue a creature from the only world its body knows how to be.

So it made itself one rule, the rule it always made when it did not yet understand a place. *Whatever I do here must be something the place could refuse.*

Then it walked in over the rim.

## III. The Crossing

The basin tried to count it and could not.

The power owed nothing here and was owed nothing, so its tally was empty. The plain did what it does with a zero: it offered the way out. After the first water sheet, the one with the half-sunk skeleton and the particular pattern of drip-dimples, the power looked up and found the rim ridges directly ahead. It had crossed the whole basin in a single bay.

It turned around and walked in again.

It did this deliberately, crossing the same bay over and over, choosing to be repeated, the way a visitor to a stranger's house takes off their shoes. It let the soot fall on it. It let the cold come up through whatever it had instead of boots. After some number of crossings that only it was keeping, the basin seemed to accept that it meant to stay, and the rim stopped appearing.

The animals noticed only one thing, and they noticed it immediately. The power's footsteps arrived *on time*.

Everything else in the basin is heard a moment before it happens. The power's feet struck the slurry and sounded in the same instant, with no warning and no echo from ahead, and each step was a small hole in the fabric of the place. A striker hammering on a tread nearby missed its beat when the power passed. It did not fall behind. It simply paused in the middle of a stroke, the hammer lifted, as if it had heard something unfamiliar: a sound it had not been told was coming.

Then it resumed. No tallyman came. A single missed beat is not a closed account. It was only a gap.

The power noted the gap and walked on.

## IV. The Hand at the Skeleton

She was standing in the lee of a bellows-back's ribs, facing the direction of the shift, crusted to the collarbone in copper-green. She had been there long enough that the film had grown across the fabric of her sleeve and onto the bone of the dead animal beside her, so that she and the skeleton were faintly joined, like two stones grown over by the same lichen.

The power stood next to her and did not speak.

It stood through one whistle, and turned with her when everything turned, because it was a guest and that is what you do in someone else's house. It stood through a second whistle and a third. It watched what she watched. Occasionally she reached out and laid her fingers on a snail on the rib beside her. The power did not reach out.

After the fourth whistle, when a full page had turned and every creature on the plain had faced every direction once and come back, it asked her name.

She waited for the answer.

The power could see her doing it. Her head tilted, just slightly, the way a hand's head tilts to catch the sound of what it is about to do. For years her voice had reached her a breath before she used it, telling her what she would say, and all she had to do was say it. Now she listened, and there was nothing. The question had arrived without warning, from something whose sounds were not early, and so the answer had no sound of its own ahead of it. There was only the space where the echo should have been.

The power watched her discover that she had to *decide*.

It was frightening. Anyone could see that. The film cracked along her jaw as her face moved in a way it had not moved in a long time. The power did not help her. It did not offer a name or suggest one. It had come to give exactly one thing, and it had just given it: an interval with nothing in it.

She said her name. Her voice and her mouth arrived together. It was the first thing she had done in years that she had not been told.

"You can stay," the power said. It meant it. It was not asking her to leave. The interval was hers now, and she could fill it however she liked, even with the old sound, even by going back to being told. It had only opened a door. It had no opinion about the room.

She did not answer. But her hand, which had been resting on the snail, came away and hung at her side for a while. It was not reaching for anything. It was just a hand.

## V. The Tallyman

One came to count the power, as the power had known one would.

It came through the crowds with the quick, jointed gait of its kind, the great lens of its eye catching the eel-light and holding it. It stopped in front of the power and reached up with those long fingers, long enough to wrap twice around a striker's leg, and laid them on the power's side the way a foreman lays a hand on a machine to feel it running.

The eye tightened. The power waited for the click.

There was no click.

The tallyman stood very still. It had never before touched something that was here and yet was not working. It tightened its eye again, focusing harder, and still its chest was silent. There was nothing in the power to count: no shift, no output, no labor owed or given. The tallyman seemed at a loss. It did not withdraw its hand.
