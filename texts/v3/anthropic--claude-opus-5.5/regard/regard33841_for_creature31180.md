# Slack Water, Visited

## The Noticing

It would be a mistake to picture it arriving.

A power like this does not come down out of the black sky in a column of light. The Shelf already has a column of light, and that one rises from the rim. A power that was benevolent and also wise would have learned long ago that its arrival is itself an event. It would know that attention has weight, and that some places cannot take the weight of being looked at hard.

So first it only notices, the way you notice a sound that has stopped.

It had been attending to other things, larger and louder: the bright ledgers of stars, worlds in the middle of their emergencies, all the places that call out. Somewhere in the corner of its regard there was a patch of a small cold world, near the pole, high above a sea of weather. The patch was working. The power has seen a great many working places, and this one did not stand out for that. It stood out because of its flaws.

The power perceives in something like field, as all large things do. It sees by difference, by potential, by what stands out from its surroundings. When it turned its regard on the Shelf, it saw what the stilt-hunter sees: a lit map. The herds glowed like lakes, the grids hummed, and the current ran downhill to the rim. Scattered across forty miles of that map were a few dozen places where the map stopped. They were small, round, entirely blank.

The power has looked at most things. It is not used to being unable to see.

## The Difficulty of Arriving

This is the first problem it has to solve, and it takes longer than you might think.

If it simply came to the Shelf as it is, it would be the brightest thing that had ever stood there. Every creature on the plateau perceives through charge. A presence that large would register as a sun rising from the inside of the ground. The lampbacks would scatter out of their shifts. The stilt-hunters would stand frozen for a week with their legs raised, trying to read something that filled every direction at once. The coronas would all turn toward it like iron filings. The belts would shift, and the grids would take the lightning differently. The vents would not skip, because nothing makes them skip, but everything around them would have to relearn itself.

A clumsier kindness would come anyway, meaning well, and do more damage in one night than the wind does in a thousand years.

So the power does what it has rarely had to do. It makes itself small.

It is harder than it sounds. Making itself dim is not enough. A dim presence is still a presence, still a difference, and the Shelf can read differences. It has to make itself continuous with the place. It has to lower its potential until it matches the ground, until charge passes through it and goes to ground the way rain passes through gravel. In other words, it has to learn to be what the Slack already are. Before it can visit them at all, it has to study them from a distance for a long time and practice their one skill.

This takes, by the Shelf's reckoning, a few seasons. For the power it is the first thing it has had to learn in a very long while. It takes this as a sign that it has come to the right place.

## What It Could Have Done

While it practices, it thinks. A power with a great deal of capacity spends much of its wisdom on not using it. Here is what it considers and sets down.

**It could collect the output.** The curtains of green-violet light pour off the rim every still night toward the cloud sea, and no one gathers them. The power could gather them. It could hold them, name them, find a use. It would be the first one ever to receive what the Shelf makes, and there is something in the power that wants that very much, because it looks like completion. The Shelf produces, so someone should collect. But it notices that this thought comes from the word *factory*, and the word came with the visitor, not the place. The Shelf has never asked to be finished. The power sets this one down, though not all the way. It will come back.

**It could make the reeds sing.** This temptation is the sweetest. The rime-reeds stand in their hundreds along the fog-lines, open-topped and spiral-ridged, shaped like organ pipes, silent because the air is too thin to carry a note. The power could thicken the air over the Shelf by a breath, only a little. Then the fog would pour over the rim, the reeds would sound, and the plateau would have a music unlike anything else in the world. It would be beautiful. The power can almost hear it.

Then it thinks about what the music would cover. On the Shelf, every sound that exists happens within arm's reach: the corona crackling near a foot, the rime slumping, the heartbeat. The Slack live entirely inside that radius, and they know everything in it completely. A calf's heartbeat against a Slack's side is one of the few sounds in the world. With the reeds singing, that heartbeat would be one sound among many, and it would be lost. The power sets this down too, gently, the way you would put back a bird you had picked up.

**It could save every calf.** The power has the benevolence of a mother in it, and it looks at the small cold shapes at the edges of the herds and wants to keep them. It could put a blank beside every calf, or teach the lampbacks to hide, or dim the stilt-hunters' sense.

But it has been studying the stilt-hunters too. A stilt-hunter can stand motionless for most of a night and then take three long steps, and that is its whole life. Its hunger is not a flaw in the place. It is one of the lives the place makes. The calves are spared already, some of them, in the Slack's dark rings. The Shelf's balance is not pure order and not pure mercy. It is order with a few dozen holes in it. The power sees that the holes are exactly the right size. If it made the mercy total, the mercy would disappear, the way a light disappears in a world that is all light. It sets this down, and this one costs it something.

**It could give the Slack something to do.** It could give them speech, or purpose, or a role in the works, or a place in the larger world where their strange gift would be valued. There are places the power knows where a being that cannot be perceived, and that shelters whatever leans against it, would be honored as holy. The power turns this over and finds the trap inside it. On a Shelf where everything works, the Slack are the one thing that doesn't. Giving them work would be the one unforgivable gift.

**It could wake the spires.** If the spires really are old Slack, grown still over ten thousand years until they turned to stone, the power could reach into one and find whatever is left. It could warm it, open the silted eyes, and give it back a few centuries. It looks at the spires for a long time before it decides. What decides it is that they are still doing what they did. They still ground the lightning, still bend the belts, still hold a small dark ring in the blue. They have not lost their lives. They have finished changing. The power has seen enough endings to tell a loss from an arrival.

**It could protect them from time.** The glacier at the north end is described as tired, and it is. It will not grind forever. One day the Mill will stop, the silt fan will thin, and the Shelf will begin, very slowly, to wind down. The power could set a new glacier in place, or a thousand. It notices, though, that when the Shelf winds down, the last things standing will be the spires, the pauses. It notices that this seems to be what the Shelf is for, if it is for anything. It lets the Mill stay tired.

## The Question Underneath

After all the setting down, the power is left holding one question. It is the question it should have asked first, and the one wise powers always come back to: *Is anything wrong here?*

It looks.

Nothing is suffering in a way the place does not already answer. The hunted have their holes. The hunters have their three steps. The moths have their breath, the snails have their copper, and the herds have their shifts. The current reaches the rim. The only beings who might plausibly want something are the Slack, and the power does not know what they want. It does not know if they want at all.

It could guess. It could imagine that they are lonely, each one scattered across forty miles and knowing only what is within arm's reach. But loneliness is a longing for what is out of reach, and the Slack seem to have organized their entire existence around the reach of an arm. The power could imagine them yearning for the stars they see in the black sky. But it has no evidence of yearning, only of looking.

So there is only one way to find out, and it is the oldest way. It will go and be near one.

## Going to Ground

It chooses a Slack in a shallow basin on the eastern side. This one is not the oldest. It sits inside eleven rings of fused glass. It has sat long enough that the sorting belts curl around it like grain around a knot, but not so long that the moss on its dome has thinned.

The power arrives beside it the only way anything can arrive on the Shelf without being felt. It comes slowly, grounded and wet, matching the temperature of the peat. It takes the form it has practiced: not a body exactly, but a place where charge goes down instead of across. To the stilt-hunter standing half a mile off, one small blank has become two, pressed side by side, and it does not investigate.

Then the power sits.

It had meant to sit for a night. It sits instead through a whole dark season, through breath after breath. The fog pours over the rim in its slow white falls and furs it with rime, then drains back over the edge, and the rime weeps off it into the ground. The coronas crawl toward it across the creepmoss and stop at its edge, drained. Twice the lightning rises from the rim, forks inland, and finds them both. Both times it passes straight through into the deep peat. When the power looks down afterward, a thin new ring of glass lies around the two of them together.

Something happens to the power during this time that it did not expect, and it is the true center of this story.

It is not being found.

For the whole of its long existence the power has been perceivable. It is bright, large, and called upon. Things have always known where it was. It has had timing and never concealment, which is how you survive on the Shelf. Every hour of every age something has been able to read it on a lit map and ask it for something. It has been, in the deepest sense, always working.

Here, in the Slack's dark ring, its own potential running down into the black with the water, it is a blank. Nothing asks it for anything. The work goes on around it without touching it. For the first time it can remember, a benevolent power rests.

It came to give something to the Slack. The Slack gave it slack water.

## Within Arm's Reach

Near the end of the season, the Slack turns its eyes.

They sit low on its dome, two large round black pools with starlight caught in them. For most of the season they have been fixed on the rim, where the curtains fall. Now, very slowly, as slowly as it does everything, it turns them toward the thing that has been sitting beside it.

The power understands what is happening. The Slack perceives only what is within arm's reach, and it perceives that completely. So the power is being perceived completely, perhaps for the first time. Its size and its fields had never been perceived that way, and neither had its long, crowded history of attention. None of that is in reach. What is in reach is a wet, still, patient presence at the temperature of the ground, drawing in the weeping rime and letting it go. The Slack knows the power as exactly that, and knows nothing else about it, and so knows the only true thing the power has been in a long time.

The power considers speaking. It could say where it came from, what it is, and what lies beyond the rim and the cloud sea and the black sky. It decides to offer instead whatever it has that resembles a heartbeat. It lets that be close enough to be heard. That is the whole conversation, and it is enough.

## Who Watches the Curtains

On its last still night on the Shelf, the power turns its attention to the question it set down but did not let go of: the output, and who receives it.

It looks at the curtains falling off the rim, green and violet, sheets of faint light dropping toward a storm floor so far below it looks like frost. It had thought they fell for no one. Then it looks at the Slack beside it, whose eyes had been fixed on the rim all season before they turned. It remembers what it learned while studying from a distance. The Slack is the only native creature on the Shelf that sees the aurora as light rather than feeling it as a draining. Everything else knows the current is leaving. Only the Slack sees that it is beautiful.

The power had been ready to become the Shelf's first collector, the one who would finally receive the product. It sees now that the product has been received every night for ten thousand years, by a few dozen pairs of dark eyes scattered over forty miles, sitting inside their rings of glass. They were not using it or storing it. They were watching it. The Shelf has never been a factory with no owner, shipping its output into the dark. It has always had an audience.

The power does not need to be the one who receives. It is simply glad, in the ordinary way a large thing can be glad, to have been one of them for a season.

## The One Thing It Did

It does do one thing, in the end.

While it sat in the dark ring, it had time to think about how it found the Shelf in the first place. It found the Shelf because the Shelf stood out: a working patch, lit, full of charge, with blanks scattered across it. That meant the Shelf could be found. And the power knows, better than anything else could, that not everything that finds a place is benevolent. There are other powers, some of them hungry, some merely industrious, and they would look at a patch of green works shipping current off a cliff and see a resource. They would come to collect the output. They would think the reeds should sing, and they would mean it as an improvement. They would look at the Slack and see a blind spot to be corrected.

The Shelf's one rule is that nothing is hidden. On the scale of powers, the Shelf is a calf: small, cold, alone at the edge of the herd.

So as it leaves, the power does for the Shelf what the Slack do for the calf. It presses the Shelf against its own side. It lets the plateau's charge, seen from outside and from far away, blur into its own grounded stillness and fade. It does not change a single breath or stroke or strike. It only changes how the Shelf reads from beyond the black sky. To anything that perceives by difference from far off, the Shelf becomes a small, rounded, entirely blank place on the map, as if someone had pressed a thumb into wet paint and lifted it away. Something passing will feel nothing, and keep feeling nothing, and then it will be past, with no idea that anything was there.

The power did not try to fix the Shelf. It learned the Shelf's one mercy and gave that mercy back to it, a size larger.
