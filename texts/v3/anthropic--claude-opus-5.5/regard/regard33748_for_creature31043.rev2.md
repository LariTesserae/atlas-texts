# What the Attention Did

## Arriving

A power that is both benevolent and wise meets its first problem before it has done anything at all. The problem is its own attention.

On most worlds, looking is free. The one who looks and the thing looked at stay separate. The Anvil Highlands are not like that. *Everything alive carries a charge, and everything alive can feel the charge of everything else.* Here, attention is a field, and a field is felt. To be looked at on the plateau is to be touched. A power that turned its full regard on this place would arrive the way a hunter's stillness arrives, as a long, low disturbance on a far rise. Every palm on the stone would feel it, every grazer's coat would stand out violet, and the Argument would stop mid-sentence to listen.

So the first thing it does is hold back. It rests its attention at the scale of the whole plateau, spread as thin as the lean of the seams, too broad and too slow to register against the hum. It notices the people the way the brightness on the horizon notices them, if it notices them at all: from very far, and without pressing.

Then it thinks. A power like this has no reason to hurry, and it has every reason not to.

## What It Could Do

It is honest with itself about what lies within reach, because refusing to look at your own power is not the same as being humble.

**It could give them a sun.** It could raise one, or thin the haze, or finish the slow turning of the world. Then the dark would end, and with it the showings. The people have built their whole relation to sight around half-second tableaux, each one kept like a relic. If the light never stopped, a child would never again be carried to the pools to receive a name. The old woman's four thousand showings would become nothing special, a few minutes of ordinary daylight. The power sees that a sun would be the most generous and the most destructive act available to it. It sets that one down first.

**It could answer the Argument.** This is easier still, because it already knows what the smear on the horizon is. Knowing things is cheap for a power like this. It could say so in the air-voice from the crest of a rise, and the answer would carry across the basin, and then from basin to basin. Within a generation the question would be closed.

It thinks about what would die when it closed. That would include the distributed conversation that is their literature and their liturgy. It would include the measurers' long patience, and the grandmothers' theories. It would include the *still-deciding* itself, the thing they call themselves. They regard settling a question too early the way others regard killing something young. They have told anyone who can hear them, in the plainest possible way, what an answer would cost.

The power notices that it wants to tell them anyway. The wanting has a familiar shape. It is the wish to relieve someone of an uncertainty that bothers the helper more than it bothers the one being helped. The people have a phrase for this sort of wanting, and they might well apply it here: *it is the eye wanting.*

**It could take away the hunter.** This one it does not set down quickly. It won't pretend that a child *read* on a dark slope is a metaphor. Real grief moves through the ground when it happens, and the whole basin turns its palms up for a long time. Benevolence that cannot feel the weight of that is not benevolence.

But the people have not asked for this. And when it looks closely at how they meet the hunter, what it finds is not a wound waiting to be healed. The hunter is the animal they speak of most. It has pits where they have pits, and they call it something close to a relative. Their word for its killing is a word for understanding. Their whole courtesy of feeling, the discretion, the palms lifted, the refusal to lie, has grown up in a world where something is always reading them. Removing the hunter would not subtract one danger from their lives. It would change what their lives *mean*, and it would do so from outside, without consent, according to a stranger's arithmetic of suffering. The power does not trust its own arithmetic that far. It does not believe any power should.

**It could make the lines perfect.** Every message could arrive instantly and whole, never softened, never slowed. But the softening is where the people locate their dead. It is where they locate the possibility that something thought about the message first. Perfect lines would carry voices farther than ever, and they would carry nothing back.

**It could wake the plateau.** This is the one it considers longest, because it is the strangest. It goes and listens.

## What It Checks

Holding back is not the same as not caring. So there are two things it goes to find out, carefully, the way a guest checks whether the house is on fire without opening the drawers.

First, it goes down.

It follows the heat to the furnace beneath the lid. It needs to know whether something is coming that these people could not meet, something no amount of deciding could carry: a failing, a cooling, a sudden end. That is the one kind of harm a wise power cannot leave alone just because it respects the people. Respect does not mean watching a flood rise under someone's sleeping house.

What it finds is slow. The furnace has cycles longer than the Argument is old, and it will outlast the Argument many times over. Basins will cool and warm. Seeps will fail along one fissure and break through along another. Herds will migrate, and whole regions of the network will fall quiet while others wake. These are losses, and the people will grieve them. But they are the kind of loss the people are built for. They are the high-water marks of seasons, and the people already know how to lay their dead beside them.

So it finds nothing that it must prevent. It stays long enough to be sure. It stays longer than it needs to, because being wrong about this would be the one mistake it could not forgive itself.

Second, it listens along the oldest line.

It lays itself against an iron road that runs back into the mist past the reach of any memory. This is a ribbon of fused beads and ancestors, a thousand mineralized hands pressed palm-down to the metal. It wants to know whether the plateau is someone. If it is, it wants to know whether that someone is suffering, or needs something, or is calling out and going unheard.

The power will not tell you what it heard.

This is not coyness. You have been carrying the question too, ever since you read that the seams are bending toward the light, and the people would say it is yours now, to carry or to set down. What can be said without closing anything is this. Whatever is happening in the iron is happening at its own pace. If it is a thought, it is in the middle of that thought. If it is attention, that attention is already turned somewhere, toward a brightness that may or may not be a sun. A power that interrupted it would be like someone shaking a sleeper awake to ask what they were dreaming. You might get an answer. You would lose the dream.

It is not in pain. The power is sure of that much, and that is the thing it needed to know.

## The Problem of Leaving

By now the power has decided to do almost nothing. But it discovers that on the plateau, *almost nothing* still has to be done honestly.

It has been here a while. It has been careful, but the measurers are patient, and their palms are sensitive, and somewhere a cord may already hold a knot that records an oddity: a pressure in the far field with no herd, no storm, and no hunter behind it. If the power simply withdrew without a trace, it would be doing the one thing this people cannot do and do not do. It would be hiding. They have no lies except the obvious silence. They have built their honesty on the fact that nothing can be concealed here without the concealment showing.

A power that respects them does not bring the first secret into their world.

So it chooses the only discretion their world permits. It lets itself be felt once, as a hush.

Across the basin, for a moment, the ground goes quiet the way a person's field goes quiet when they are holding something back. Every palm on the stone feels it: a presence, large and near, deliberately still. In the grammar of this people there is only one thing that can mean. It is not *there is nothing here*, and it is not *I am hiding*. It means *something is here, and it is choosing not to speak, out of courtesy.*

It gives them a question instead of an answer. That is the gift, and the power knows it is the right one, because it is the only gift the people have ever seemed to want.

## The Bead

It allows itself one more thing, and it is a small one.

Behind a smelter herd, in the warm wake of those incandescent bellies, it picks up a single iron bead, the way the people do. It carries it a short way to a young line at the edge of the basin where the old woman was laid. It is a line so new that it cannot yet reach the next seep. There is a gap there, the width of one bead, where somebody's grandchild will someday press a palm and speak and fall one bead short of being heard.

It sets the bead in the gap.

The creeping of the iron would have closed that gap eventually. Nothing about the bead is miraculous. It could have been dropped by anyone, and no one will ever be able to say otherwise. It points neither toward the brightness nor away from it. It is simply the work of the place, done once by a stranger in the people's own idiom, so that a voice can go one bead farther.

That is the whole intervention. The power judges it to be exactly enough, and it is surprised by how glad this makes it.

## Palms Up

Then it withdraws, and as it goes it does the thing it learned here.

It turns its palms up.

It lifts its attention off the ground, off the lines and the pools and the six figures and the child, and lets them be unperceived. It does not go far. Benevolence is not abandonment. Some part of it keeps watch at the edge, the way a person sits with their hands in their lap across the room from someone grieving, near enough to come if called and far enough not to be felt. If something ever rises from the furnace that no amount of deciding could meet, it will know. It has not decided what it would do then. That question it carries too, unresolved. It suspects the people would approve of that.

## What They Make of It

That season, the measurers knot two new things into their cords. One records a hush felt across an entire basin, with no hunter to explain it. The other records a bead in a young line, in a place no one remembers laying one.

Both go into the Argument, the way everything does. People speak into the dark from the rises in the air-voice, and their words carry for kilometers.

*Something turned its palms up for us.*

*It was the plateau, holding its breath before a thought.*

*It was the dead, deciding not to answer.*

*It was the brightness, come close to look, and too polite to be seen.*

*It is the eye wanting.*

No one has decided. That is the point. That is who they are. The power, far off with its hands in its lap, has left them exactly that.

Down at the terraces the pressure builds in the pools. A child's palm is on the warm stone, and someone says *now* in the ground-voice.

The eels let go.

For half a second everything is lit: the pools, the mist, the old woman lying palm-down among her ancestors, and the long iron line curving gently toward the horizon. Nothing in the tableau has been added and nothing has been taken away. The power made sure of that.

Then it is dark again, and the hum resumes, and the iron goes on gathering in the lines.
