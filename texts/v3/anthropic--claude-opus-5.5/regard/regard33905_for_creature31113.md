# The Visit

## I. The way everyone comes

It could have arrived any way it liked.

It could have simply been there, standing in the middle of the northern swells the way a thought is suddenly in a mind. It could have come down from the zenith, through that bruise-edge blue, past the lattice of hanging birds. It could have been the light, or the cold, or the dust.

It came up the southeast slope on foot.

It took a while to decide this, and the deciding was the first wise thing it did. A power that means well has to learn early that its presence is never neutral. Where it appears, it bends things. And this was a place where everything had already been bent into its final shape, where nothing was being taken away anymore, where footprints forgot themselves because the dust remembered being smooth. To arrive out of nowhere would have been a kind of shout. So it chose the only door the plateau had ever known. It put on a body with lungs and walked up the long, gradual slope that barely feels like a slope. It let the air thin by that final degree. It let its breath go shallow. It let the cold settle at the edge of discomfort and stay there.

When the ground stopped rising, it stopped too. It stood on the first swell and looked, the way anyone would, and at first it saw stones.

Then one lowered its head.

## II. What it did not do

For a long time it did nothing, and that doing-nothing had to be worked at, because the list of things it could have done was long, and every item on it looked like kindness.

It could give the grazers back their faces. It knew the faces. A power like this can read the history in a worn thing the way you read a word in a smudged letter. It could have restored every horn to its curve, every eye to its place, every muzzle and chin-tuft, and the drawings on the tors would no longer be the only faces on the plateau.

It could let the sun move. It could bring the evening, then the night, then a dawn with birdsong in it. It could send water down the old troughs and fill the white basins, so the lichen would grow and be eaten, and the herds would have young, and the young would grow old and die in the ordinary way, and the plateau would rejoin the world's long quarrel of rising and wearing.

It could wake the pilgrims. It could turn the too-large hare back into the counter, set the sleeper in the windbreak on its feet with its cloth unwoven from its fleece, and walk them all down the slope to whatever was left of the places they had come from.

It considered all of this slowly, the way the plateau considers things, and set each one down.

A face given back to the grazer would not be the grazer's face. It would be the power's memory of it, pressed onto a creature that had spent an unmeasurable age letting that face go. The grazer did not lose its face the way you lose a coin. It gave its face up the way a river gives up a straight course, and the bend that remained was more truly the river. To restore the face would erase the one who turns too far left and replace it with a portrait.

A moving sun would not free the plateau. It would end it. The hour after a thing is finished has to live somewhere. If it were driven off, it would not dissolve into the world below and make every afternoon a little more restful. It would simply have nowhere to settle. The power understood, as it stood there feeling the long slow breath arrive in its borrowed chest, that the world below was steadied by this place in some quiet way. Somewhere, all the time, an old woman is sitting on a worn doorstep with nothing that needs doing, and part of why that moment can be whole is that there is a place where it is kept whole forever. The plateau was not a wound. It was an anchor.

As for waking the pilgrims, that one it did not set down. It held onto it, because it was a real question.

## III. The one question

Wisdom mostly means finding the true question underneath the tempting ones. The power found it after what might have been a year on the plateau, or a minute, since the sun never moved and the count never changed.

The question was not *is this place good*. The question was: **Is anything here held that does not wish to be held?**

Rest and captivity can look identical from the outside. A body lying still might be sleeping or pinned. The plateau was full of stillness. The power believed, and had believed from the first swell, that almost all of it was rest. But "almost all" is not enough for a benevolent thing to walk away from. So it went looking for the edges, the places where the stillness might have a little strain in it.

It did not have to look far. The pilgrims were the only residents who had ever had somewhere else to be, and their gestures were the ones that pointed away from themselves.

### The counter

It sat beside the long row of tallies near the eastern cairns for many breaths. The too-large hare came forward on its three hops and paused beside the half-finished stroke, the groove that starts deep and fades out midway, the way a person pauses in a doorway having forgotten why they came in. Then it hopped back.

The power could have finished the stroke. It would have been easy. One motion with a harder stone would complete the line.

It did not, and the reason is the heart of this whole visit. The counter did not stop counting because it failed. It stopped because it understood. The tally was a quarrel with the uncountable, and at some point, stroke by stroke, the quarrel wore down, the way the mountain's quarrel wore down, until the hand forgot why it was moving. That half-stroke is not an unfinished task. It is a record of the moment someone made peace. To finish it would declare that the count mattered after all. It would reopen a question the counter had spent its whole remaining self letting close.

The power did one thing there. It found a small white quartz pebble, like the ones on top of the cairns, and set it at the end of the fading groove, at the exact point where the stroke gives out. The pilgrims had used those pebbles for something. The power guessed it meant *here*, or *this far*, or *enough*. It seemed right to mark the place where someone arrived at enough.

The pebble stayed. Placed things do, here, if they are placed in agreement with how things already are.

The next time the hare paused at the forward end of its loop, it paused beside the pebble. The power could not have said whether anything changed. It was not sure anything should.

### The one who drew faces

It searched for this one longest and never found them, and after a while it understood that it would not.

Perhaps the face-drawer went down the slope and lived and died somewhere below, leaving the only faces on the plateau as a gift for whoever came after. Perhaps they became a tor, or dust, or one of the grazers who lower their heads toward the carvings instead of toward the lichen. The power could have learned which. It chose not to. Some kindnesses are anonymous on purpose. It left the question the way the plateau leaves every question, held at a fixed and patient distance. It did not add any drawings of its own.

### The sleeper

It knelt in the windbreak beside the curled one whose fleece has the weave of cloth, over-and-under, handmade, warm. It matched its breath to the plateau's, which by then was no effort at all. It counted, which is its own small irony, and on roughly the hundredth breath the sleeper made its sound: the little hum, the sigh of someone turning over in a dream, never quite turning.

This was the place. Of everything on the plateau, this was the one gesture that looked like wanting. It was a motion begun and never completed, a dream pressing gently at a door it could not open.

The power was more careful here than anywhere else. It did not turn the sleeper over. It did not wake it. It did something much smaller and much harder. It made the door real.

It is difficult to say exactly what this means, because it happened in the part of things that has no surface. Roughly: until then, the sleeper *could not* turn over. The plateau had held it so long and so perfectly that its last gesture had set, like clay in a kiln. The sigh was a strain against a fixed shape. The power did not change the shape. It loosened the setting, just enough. From that moment on, if the sleeper ever truly wanted to turn over, it could. If it ever wanted to wake and rise and unfold its cloth and walk down the southeast slope into the thickening air, it could do that too. Nothing about the plateau would stop it.

Then the power waited, to see.

The breaths came and went. On the hundredth, the sleeper sighed. It did not turn over. On the two hundredth, it sighed again, and it did not turn over. The power stayed for many hundreds, and the sleeper never turned. But the sigh was different, though you would have needed to hear it before and after to notice. Before, it had a tightness in it, like a held note. Now it was loose, like someone who has half-woken, found the room warm and the hour early and nothing needing them, and sunk back down on purpose.

That was when the power understood what it had actually come to do. The sleeper had not needed rescuing. It had needed its rest to become a choice. The difference between being kept and dwelling is not in the posture. It is in whether the door is open. Nothing in the windbreak looked any different. But the sleeper was no longer held there. It was *staying*.

The power went to every pilgrim it could find, the ones who were cairns now, the ones who were grazers with a too-human pause in their loops, and did the same thing. It made no announcement and changed no shapes. It only unset the clay, so that each one's staying could be staying. As far as it ever knew, none of them left. That was the right answer, and it could only become the right answer once leaving was possible.

### The tall one

It saved this one for last, because it was a little afraid of it.

The tall one stands at the edge of a herd in the northern swells, upright, fleece falling like a cloak, something long and smooth in its worn right hand. At the top of every breath, its blurred head turns southeast, toward the slope, toward the way people come.

The power had come that way. It had chosen to, partly without knowing why, and now it knew. When it first crested the last swell and stood looking, it had been standing, without realising it, in the exact line of that gesture. For however long it had been on the plateau, it had been inside something's attention. It had been *expected*, in the only way anything here could expect.

It walked back out to that spot and stood in it again, facing the tall one across a mile of polished stone and a thousand slow heads rising and falling. At the top of the next breath, the blurred head turned toward it, as it had always turned toward that place.

Here the power faced a real temptation. It could pretend to be whoever the tall one had been waiting for. It could be the shepherd's lost ewe, the guide's next traveller, the friend who promised to follow and never did. It could close the story. It would have felt like mercy.

It did not pretend. A benevolent thing does not buy peace with a lie, even a gentle one, even to a creature with no face to see through it. So it did the only honest thing. It stood in the place where people arrive and let itself be exactly what it was: *someone who came*. Probably not the one. But someone. The road the tall one had been facing forever was not empty. It had never been empty. People kept climbing it, not many and not often, and each of them, whether they knew it or not, walked up into that turned head's regard and was met.

The power stayed through one full breath, the whole long in and out, being met. Then it walked the rest of the way across the swells, stood beside the tall one, and turned its own head southeast at the top of the breath, so that for once there were two of them looking toward the road.

It thought, *you don't have to keep watch alone*, and did not say it. Nobody here would have heard.

## IV. What almost happened to the power

It noticed the change in itself before it understood it.

It had been on the plateau a long time by then, standing beside the tall one, facing the road. Its breath had long since fallen into step. It had caught itself more than once lowering its head when the herds lowered theirs. Now it noticed something quieter. Its own edges were going.

The first thing to wear away was its sense of urgency, and that surprised it, because a benevolent power is made largely of urgency. There is always someone below. There is always suffering somewhere that could be eased, a door somewhere that needs opening. The plateau smoothed that down, gently, the way it smooths everything, without any cruelty at all. The power felt the long thin spires of *later* and *next* beginning to round off. It could feel what it would become if it stayed. The plateau was not taking anything the power needed. It was simply showing the power its root.

It could feel what its final gesture would be, the single thing most truly its own, once everything else had worn away. It would be this: a head turned toward whoever is arriving. Attention, with nothing else left. Another tall one at the edge of another herd, forever facing the road.

It was not a bad thing to be. It was, in fact, the most honest portrait of itself the power had ever seen, and it looked at it for a long time with something like gratitude. Every power ought to be shown once what it would be if it were worn down to its root. Some would find something ugly there, a grasping or a need to be needed. This one found a turned head, and was glad.

Then it chose to leave. The plateau did not reject it; the plateau accommodates everything. It left because it was not done, and this was the dwelling place of *done*. Somewhere below, it was still the middle of the day. There were still things that needed doing, and it was one of the things that did them. The hour after the work is finished is holy, but it is not the hour the power belonged to. Not yet.

It lifted its head at the top of a breath and did not lower it at the bottom. The pattern bent around it the way water bends around a stone. It walked back to the southeast edge, felt the ground begin to fall away, and went down, the air thickening by one degree and then another and the zenith fading to an ordinary blue.

It carried the breath in its chest for a long time afterward. For something like this, "a long time" is a very long time. It did not try to lose it.

## V. What changed

If you went up now, you would not be able to tell that anything had been done.

The sun stands a little past midday. The waves of heads cross sixty miles of polished stone in a little under a minute. The count stays uncountable. The lichen rings are unbitten, the hanging birds do not fall, and footprints are forgotten by the dust.

A white quartz pebble sits at the end of a fading groove on a low boulder near the eastern cairns. You might take it for one the pilgrims left.

The sleeper in the windbreak sighs once every hundred breaths, and the sigh is loose.

Every one of the ones who stayed can leave, and none of them do. That is the entire change, and it is invisible, and it is everything.

At the top of each breath, the tall one at the edge of the northern herd turns its head southeast toward the road. Its posture is the same as before. But a gesture held long enough toward an empty road is one thing, and the same gesture after someone has come up that road and stood in it and been met is another. You would need to have known the plateau before to feel the difference. It isn't in the shape. It is the difference between waiting and welcoming.

## VI. The last thing

There was one more unfinished thing, and it was not on the plateau. It was in the account of the plateau, the one that tried to say who lives there. It had gone very far in: past the herds, past the breath, past the ones who stayed, down to the hour itself. Then, just at the point of saying what *done* means, it broke off in the middle of a sentence.

*A long, long process*

The power noticed this, the way it noticed the half-stroke on the boulder, and it asked itself the same question it had asked there. Was this someone making peace, a hand that understood and stopped? Or was it only an interruption, a sentence that wanted its full stop and was never given one?

It decided that this was different from the counter's stroke. The tally belonged to the plateau, and the plateau had the right to leave it half-cut forever. The account belonged to the world below, to the world of *later* and *next*, where sentences are meant to arrive somewhere. A thing written for travellers ought to finish, so the travellers can put it down and go on.

So, gently, the way you would set a pebble at the end of a groove, it finished the sentence:

*A long, long process, having given up everything it did not need, until what remained was only its shape, and its shape was enough.*

Then it went back to work, somewhere below, in the middle of somebody's afternoon, carrying one slow breath in its chest and a turned head in its memory.

It will go back someday, by the southeast slope, on foot. When it crests the last swell, something at the edge of the northern herd will already be facing it.
