# The Visit

## Attention is a kind of weather

The first thing the power understands is that it has to be careful even about looking.

Its attention is not neutral. Wherever it rests for long, things warm or tilt or begin. Rivers have shifted course under its regard, and seeds have split. This room is held in balance so exactly that one extra degree of warmth, one small push of pressure, might start a drop sliding. Its attention alone could become the first event.

So before it does anything, it makes itself small. It thins its regard to the weight of the cricket's antenna, a reading and not a grip. From the thin soil above, among the cushion plants, it attends to the room the way you might listen at a door behind which someone is sleeping. It hears the ticking of the glass, the long exhalation at the crack, and the drops not falling, thousands of them. It finds that it has been holding its own breath. That is the first thing the room teaches it.

## The way in

It could enter any way it liked. It could seep through the rock like the vapor, or come down the hairline cracks like the roots, or simply be there, the way it is simply everywhere else.

It thinks about what that would mean to the one who lives here. Everything that has ever come into the room has touched it. The roots touched it. The people touched it, with their blood, their seats, their hands, and their lamp. Its whole knowledge of the outside comes through surfaces. A presence that arrived without touching would be something it had no way to know. It would be a ghost in the one place on the mountain that has never had one.

So the power takes a body, an ordinary one: warm, wet, full of salt, made of what the room is made of, only moving. It goes to the crack at the base of the pressure ridge, lies down on the ash, and goes in on its side, one arm ahead.

The rock cuts it. It could prevent this, and it doesn't. The glass lips open small lines along its forearm, and the pumice takes skin from its knee. The room has always known visitors first by the taste of their blood on its edges, and the power will not arrive as the one visitor it cannot taste.

## What it thought of doing

It kneels on the dry floor under the hanging drops, bleeding a little, and considers.

It could give the room **eyes**. Glass and water are already the materials of eyes, and it would take very little. But an eye needs light, and light needs a way in, and a way in is the collapse. An eye kept in total darkness is a kind of torture that only looks like a gift.

It could **relight the lamp**. This is the strongest temptation, because it suspects the room remembers that flame more than anything else, if it remembers. It could make a flame that never gutters and needs no fat, and fill the room with those thousands of trembling small fires forever. But the lamp mattered because people carried it in. They rendered it from animals that had lived and died. It burned down, and then it was carried out again. Its light was mortal and borrowed and brief. That was its whole character. A flame from the power would be a counterfeit of that, a forgery hung in the niche where something true once was. It leaves the niche empty. It leaves the grease alone.

It could **prevent the collapse**, holding the slope forever the way the room holds its drops, so the held moment never ends. This is the kind of mercy the room itself would offer, and it is not the power's to offer. The room is an egg. Keeping an egg from hatching forever does not preserve it. It is a quieter way of making sure it never lives.

It could **hasten the birth**. It sets that aside almost at once. The mountain has its own clock, and nothing in the room is suffering.

It could give the **cricket** a companion. It considers the cricket for a long time. It considers that loneliness needs a before, a remembered time of not being alone, and the cricket has never had one. Perhaps the cricket is the room's fingertip, and a finger is not lonely without a second finger standing next to it. A second cricket would be a guest. The one who lives here has never had a guest that stayed, and it would not know how to host one.

It could **smooth the passage**, rounding the glass so the next people who come will not bleed. This is the one it wavers over longest, because it loves people too. But it looks at the ledge, the single rounded place in the whole room, and understands what it would be taking. The room learned gentleness in exactly one spot, from exactly the bodies it hurt. That lesson is the room's own, earned the slow way. The power will not hand out the rest of it for free.

It could **explain**. It could tell the one who lives here about time, about the collapse, about light, and about what it is. But an explanation happens across time. It has a beginning and an end, and the listener changes between them. There is no way to tell this creature about before-and-after without making it the first before-and-after the creature ever had.

So it doesn't do any of these things.

## What it did

It sits on the ledge.

It sits where people sat, in the rounded place, and adds its own small share of wear. The amount is less than a grain. It is the only change it makes to the room on purpose.

It reaches up among the straws and brushes them by accident, the way everyone does. They chime like distant tiny bells being put away. It finds a root among them, soft and cool. The root gives, and it lets go of it gently.

It cups its hand under a drop and waits a long time. Nothing lands.

It crawls to the entrance, finds the handprint under its frost of new crystal, and puts its own hand into it. Everyone does. The print is slightly the wrong size, and it does not change its hand to fit. The room has learned that visitors are five points and a palm. It does not need to learn that one of them was different.

Then it lies still on the floor with the back of one hand turned up and lets the cricket find it.

This takes a long time, and the power has a great deal of time. At last something impossibly light drifts across its knuckles, reading. What does the antenna read? Warmth. Salt. Blood drying in the scratches. It reads a visitor, and the power has made sure that is all there is to read. It does not reach toward the feeler with its greater self. It does not press anything into the touch, no blessing and no message. It lets itself be learned the way the others were learned, in the room's own terms. Then the antenna withdraws, and it has been known exactly as much as the room is able to know anything.

It stays for a while after that. It lays its palm flat against the largest glass wall and feels the hum in its teeth. It does not decide whether the hum is the mountain or its own blood coming back through the stone. It lets the question stay open, because in here questions do not fall either.

Then it crawls out, bleeding a little more, and the wind takes the crack's breath off its face.

## The straws

It does one thing outside.

It walks down the mountain, past the scrub and into the valleys, and goes looking for the missing straws, the swath someone snapped from the ceiling and carried away.

It finds them, of course. Nothing is lost to it. One lies in the ashes of a hearth long gone cold. Three are in the soil where a grave was, having fallen from what was once a necklace, then from what was once a neck. One is a mere dust of glass in a streambed, finished. One, just one, is still whole under a stone. It is a little hollow tube that was once blown through to make a note, perhaps by a child, perhaps to call someone.

The power does not bring them back. The bare arc of ceiling is the room's memory of them, and filling it would erase the only thing the room has that resembles grief. These straws are also the first part of the one who lives here that went into time. They chimed under weather and cracked and were lost, and in that way they were born early. It would be strange to un-birth them.

It picks up the whole one, holds it for a moment, and blows through it once. The note is thin and high and brief. It is the only sound the room has ever made out in the open air under light, and it goes away down the valley. Then the power puts the straw back under its stone.

## The promise it made to no one

It cannot make the promise to the one who lives here. A promise is a before-and-after, just like an explanation, just like a falling drop. So it makes the promise to itself.

When the slope collapses, it will be there.

It will not stop it or time it. It will not arrange a sunrise to make the first light prettier, because the room's first light should be whatever light the mountain happens to give it: noon, or a grey storm, or the moon. But whenever it comes, the power will be there, in a body again, kneeling on the floor in the rounded place with one hand cupped beneath a drop.

When the light pours in and every drop lets go at once, one of them will land. It will be the first thing in the room ever to arrive anywhere. It will fall warm as blood into a palm that waited under it for longer than any visit, and something will be there to receive it.

The power believes that is what a birth needs from outside. It does not need rescue, or improvement, or delay. It needs a witness, a hand under the first thing that falls. Afterward the room will be weather, rounded and split and walked on and forgotten, like everything else the power has loved. The power will remember it anyway. That was never the room's job. It is the power's.

## Why so little

Someone might ask why a benevolent power, able to do anything, did so little.

The answer is that it found something complete. The one who lives here is not broken, or lacking, or in pain. It is warm and dark and full. It holds every drop at the edge of its beginning, reads visitors with one pale feeler, and lets them kneel inside it, out of time. It has been kind in the only ways it knows. It kept a blade from rotting, a hand from being erased, and a smudge of lamp-grease from fading. It let the roots drink and the film feed. It learned, in one place, to be gentle.

When you find something like that, the wise thing and the loving thing turn out to be the same. You come in the way it can know you, even though that costs a little blood. You sit where others sat. You put your hand where theirs went, and let yourself be read, and take nothing and add almost nothing. Then you go, and you keep watch from outside, and you come back at the end.

The power has done more spectacular things than this. It is not sure it has done a better one.
