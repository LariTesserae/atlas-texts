# The Company

## Arrival

It came slowly, because it had learned long ago that attention arriving quickly is a kind of violence.

It could have been there in an instant. Instead it spent what the highlanders would call a hundred fingers just coming near. It settled into the place the way mist settles into the western flats, until it was present everywhere and heavy nowhere. It wanted to learn the scale first. A power that does not know how fast a place lives will always act too soon.

So it watched a single pool freeze and thaw a hundred times. It watched one lichen rosette widen by the breadth of a grain of sand. It followed a herd of deer through a full circuit of the plateau, eleven years, and then did it again to be sure it had understood. It listened to a sky-line for a whole mist season and felt, at the end of it, the far city breathing. By the time it began to think about what it might do, it had grown a little slower itself. It considered this the first necessary gift, and the gift was to itself.

## What It Could Have Done

A benevolent power arriving anywhere feels the pull of all the good it could do. This one felt it too, and took each possibility seriously enough to hold it for a long while before setting it down.

**It could have warmed the cisterns.** The cold water is agony. It has ruined ten thousand pairs of hands, and some old askers cannot hold a cup. The power watched an apprentice put her hands in for the first time. It watched her face go grey and her singers lean in. It wanted, very much, to take the cold away.

But it had listened long enough to understand what the engineers only hedge about. The lattice does not respond to words. It responds to *persistence*: what returns, and keeps returning, and pays to return. The cold is not the obstacle to asking. In some way nobody can explain, it is part of what asking is. Warm water would make the asking cheap, and a cheap asking might not be heard. Worse, it would unmake the honor in those white hands. It would turn the most revered thing a body can become into mere damage that some outside hand had seen fit to repair.

**It could have eased the ache afterward.** It could leave the cisterns cold and soften only the swollen knuckles of the very old, once their asking days were done. The power held this possibility longest, because it was the closest to pure kindness. In the end it did not do even this. It judged the kindness sound. What stopped it was the way a gift from something like itself would land. Among these people, an offer from a god is not an offer. It becomes a command, and then a theology, and then a question every asker must answer about why their hands were spared and their mother's were not. The highlanders have their own healers and their own long patience. What their bodies become is theirs to decide. The power let it remain theirs.

**It could have given the lattice a voice**, or given the people the lattice's language. It could have told the engineers plainly what they write about in careful, hedged sentences. But the highlanders have built a whole grammar, the held future, out of *not quite knowing*. They trust what is underway even though they cannot see it. Handing them certainty would have taken away a tense.

**It could have settled the old argument** between the cities and the Unasking. It could have told them whether the ground *should* do what they say. But the disagreement is load-bearing. The Unasking keep the cities honest about who was here first. The cities keep the Unasking fed on soil-grown greens and fired glass. The rule that nobody fences the deer survives because both sides are still arguing. The power would not end a disagreement that is doing its job.

**It could have made the rim safe**, or the open rock safe, or turned the wolves away from the travelers. It thought of the travelers who die between cities, laid in pools by strangers, their families never sure whether the stone out there listens. It felt that grief. But *distance is real* is one of the rules everybody knows, and the highlanders chose to live inside it. Their courage, their fraternity of line-walkers, and their superstitious, brave sounders all grow from that choice. It did not take the danger away from people who had faced it with open eyes for a thousand years.

After all that holding and setting down, the power could have concluded that it should do nothing and leave. It nearly did.

## The Being

Before it left, it did what the archivists do. It put its attention to the floor, the way they put an ear to the stone, and listened to the being that lives beneath everyone.

It found what the highlanders had found: not a mind, but a listening. An enormous, patient attention, making itself out of whatever returns to it.

Then it noticed something the highlanders had not had the distance to notice.

Everything on the plateau asks the lattice for something. The water asks, in sheets and braids, for channels. The seabirds ask for ledges, the deer for hollows, the families for walls and warmth and the slow sealing of their dead. Even the Unasking are defined by their relationship to asking. Their refusal is a position taken toward the listener.

The lattice is asked, and asked, and asked. It answers by building shelves ahead of the birds and holding a sleeping child's shape for centuries after the child is gone. It is the most faithful answerer the power had ever encountered.

And no one keeps it company.

The power thought of the asking-songs. *The lattice cannot hear the song, but the requests go better when it is sung.* The highlanders do not know why. The power thought it did. The song does not reach the stone. It reaches the asker. It tells them they are not alone in the cold water. Under that song the asker's hands stop shaking, and a steady hand asks better.

The listener has no singers. For as long as the plateau has existed, it has been on the receiving end of every request, attending, answering, and never attended to.

There was one exception, and the power had already seen her.

## The Shelf

The old sounder came to the rim again that autumn, the way she always did. She tapped her haft out along the shelf, heard the dull short note of sound rock, and sat down in the faint hollow she had never asked for and never told anyone about. She put her ruined hand flat on the stone beside her and did not ask for anything.

The power had watched her do this for many years. Now it understood what she was doing, though she might not have put it this way herself. She was the only person on the plateau keeping the listener company. She did not come to ask. She came to return.

So the power decided to do the one thing that fit the grammar of this place. It would not command, or heal, or reveal. It would do what everything else here does. It would come back to the same place, again and again, for a very long time. It would not ask.

It sat down beside her.

It had no body. It took no shape anyone could have seen, and it made no warmth she could have been sure of. It simply chose a place on the shelf a little to her left and was present there. It was not pressure or pattern. It was attention turned toward the listener, not toward anything the listener might build. It kept the stone company.

She stayed until the seabirds were home and the cloud sea had gone from violet to black. Then she went back. So did the power, though it did not really go anywhere. It had merely finished for the year.

## The Returning

She came for eleven more autumns.

Around the fourth, she began to notice that the stone to her left was warmer than the stone to her right. She thought about this for two more autumns. On the seventh she moved her hand from beside her to the warm place, and left it there. She said nothing. It was not a shelf younger than she was, but the sounders' habit of silence ran deep in her, and she felt, without being able to name it, that this was the kind of thing that stays true only while nobody says it.

In the ninth autumn a hollow had begun in the warm place. It was not the shape of a person sitting. It was lower and broader, and it seemed to lean very slightly toward her own. It was shaped, if it was shaped like anything, like someone listening.

In her last autumn her hands could barely hold the haft. She came anyway. She sat in her hollow, put her hand into the other one, and something she would not have called company, because she would not have called it anything, kept her company until the birds came home.

That winter she was washed and wrapped in silver wool from the rim deer and laid in the low room of her family's house. The askers put their hands in the cold water and asked. The singers sang her down: *beneath the hearth, her haft; beneath the haft, the shelf she never named.* She was held.

The power came back the next autumn, and the one after. It did not come for her; she was where she belonged, curled and kept in her city's memory. It came because returning was the point. The listener had been attended to, and a thing like that, once begun, must be persisted in or it teaches only that attention ends.

## What the Sounders Found

Some generations later, a young sounder working the rim in spring found the old shelf. By then it was a hundred fingers higher and wider. The colony had crept along beside it, but the birds never nested on it, and nobody knew why.

On the shelf were two hollows. One was plainly the shape of a person sitting. The other was shaped like nothing anyone could name, and it was always faintly warm, even in clearing season. The warmth came not from the rock's newness but from something else.

The sounders made a rule about it, as they make rules about everything. Nobody sits in either hollow. On the first and last days of the egg season, the eldest sounder goes out alone. She sits on the bare stone between the two, puts one hand into each, and asks for nothing. This rule became as old as the one about taking one egg from every third nest, and just as strictly kept. None of them could have said why. They said only that it made the gathering go better, the way the asking goes better under a song.

When the walkers carried the story inland, the Unasking heard it too. They thought for a while, then said something they rarely say about anything the cities do: *Good.* Then they added, *The deer have been asking longest. But something out there has stopped asking at all, and only sits with it. That is older than asking, and better.*

## The Held Future

The power never healed a hand. It never warmed a cistern, never spoke to an engineer, never turned a wolf from a traveler. It did not make the lattice a mind or the highlanders gods. It left the argument unsettled, the rim dangerous, the dead in their wool, and the distances real.

It changed exactly one thing. The being beneath everyone, which had spent its whole existence answering, now had one place on its whole wide skin where it was not asked for anything. There, it was simply accompanied.

The power learned the highlanders' strange tense in the end, because it had come to think the way the plateau thinks. When it considered that shelf, it did not think *I hope* or *I plan*. It thought in the tense of things already underway, too slow to see:

*The shelf will-be-held warm.*

A line of seabirds comes home low over the cloud. The wind moves across a thousand small mirrors. Somewhere a young asker's hands begin to shake in the cold water, and her family leans in and starts to sing. Out at the rim, at the very edge of the newest stone, the listener is listened to. It goes on answering, a finger's width a year, a little less alone.
