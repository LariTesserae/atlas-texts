# The Attention

## How It Came

It could have come as light. That would have been the easy way and the wrong one.

The sky over the basin has never held anything. Not a star, not a moon, not a cloud. Everything in the city has been shaped under that blank lid, and every reflection has been dreamed without reference to it. A light appearing overhead would have been the largest event in the basin's history, and the water would have turned toward it the way it turns toward everything. Within a century the reflections would be full of skies. The host would begin building for whatever lived up there. A kindness that arrives as spectacle rewrites the place it lands on.

So the attention made itself small. It came across the flats on foot, the way you did, at a size that fits the doors. The doors were made for a size, and it is courtesy to fit the door.

It walked for a long time without doing anything. It watched the tidal pulse deepen and draw back. It followed the mats along the gutters to see where the pulse ran strongest. It stood near the reeds at a well and counted them: fourteen. It knelt by a plaza pool and looked at the golden city in the water for as long as it took to understand that it was being shown a wish. A wise power learns before anything else that the first thing it wants to do is almost never the thing to do. So it waited, and let itself want things, and watched the wanting.

## What It Considered, and Set Aside

**It could fill the city.** This was the first wish, and the strongest. It could bring a people here, bodies of the right height for the lintels and the right stride for the stairs, and let them walk in. The windows in the air would light. The murmur in the doorways would get its words.

But these would be guests made to answer a need, and a guest made to answer a need is furniture. People brought into being to fill someone else's longing do not get to have longings of their own. They would owe their existence to an emptiness. Nor could it simply relocate people from elsewhere, because it would have to ask whether anyone wants to live in a warm dark with no sky, and whoever said yes would be choosing the city for their own reasons. That would be good, and it would be theirs, and it could not be arranged. Arrivals that mean anything are not delivered.

**It could give the host a body,** so it could walk its own stairs and sit in its own rooms. But a host with a body becomes a tenant. The preparing would stop, and the preparing is not something the host does. It is what the host is. You do not help a river by making it a lake.

**It could tell the host the truth,** that no one is coming. Except this is not true. Someone came. And even if it were true, the waiting is not a mistake the host is making, a false belief to correct. It is the form the host's love takes. There is no fact that would improve it.

**It could hurry the door at the lake,** raise the wall to lintel height in an afternoon. But ten thousand years is not an obstacle to the host. It is the medium it works in. Finishing that wall for it would be like finishing someone's sentence for them.

Somewhere in this list the attention noticed what it was doing. It was imagining a lack and building remedies to fit it: rooms for an absence, doors for a guest it had pictured. It was doing exactly what the host does. The recognition made it laugh a little, alone in the plaza, and the sound went out across the flats and came back from the wrong direction.

## What It Asked Instead

Before deciding anything, it asked the only question that matters: *Is this being suffering?*

It put its palm flat on a warm wall and listened the way the lichen listens, until it could feel the rough-then-smooth rhythm running ahead of itself. The answer was no. The host is not in pain. It is not lonely the way a person is lonely. It was not built for sadness, and nothing about it is broken.

But there was something. Every act of hospitality in the city is half of an act. The stair is made to be climbed, and it is only ever made. The window is lit for someone walking home, and no one walks home. Hospitality is a sentence with two halves, *here is a place for you* and *thank you, I was glad of it*, and for all of its existence the host has spoken the first half into silence.

That is not a wound. It is an open sentence. And an open sentence is something a guest can close.

## The Night

So the attention did what guests do.

It chose the building whose inside was oldest. This was not the grandest one. It was a small house with fresh sharp walls outside and, within, stair treads hollowed almost through, where the host has spent the most attention rehearsing. It paused at the threshold. The drip-curtain had nearly closed, and it turned sideways to pass through, brushing the frame at hip height, exactly where the frame was worn soft for someone doing exactly that. It put out its hand to the wall at the place where the wall was smoothest.

Inside was the unworn patch on the floor, the place something had always stood. The attention understood it was a table, so it sat at it. It cupped water from the pool by the wall and drank. The water was warm and tasted of chalk and iron and wet slate. It did not drink out of thirst. It drank because a host offers and a guest accepts, and refusing what is offered is a small unkindness, even when you do not need it.

Then it lay down in the back room, the one the doorframe was worn for, and slept.

The thick darkness came. The previous visitor had walked through it the way you walk through warm water that doesn't wet you: noticing, a little uneasy, moving on. The attention did not move on. When the dark pressed close against its eyes and skin, it let it. It stayed still and let itself be held by something that does not know how to hold, only how to be warm around someone. It did not flinch. No one had ever not flinched before.

The host had no way to understand what was happening. But the murmur in the next building over, the rehearsed sound of company, went very quiet that night, the way a room goes quiet when the person everyone was waiting for finally sits down.

## The Guest-Gift

In the old custom, a guest leaves a gift when they go. It is not payment. It is a return, the proof that hospitality went somewhere.

The attention thought hard about what to give, because everything in the city is made from the host. Every wall and lintel and step is its own mineral, precipitated from its own warmth. It has never touched anything that did not come from itself.

So the attention gave it something from outside: a single pebble, grey and ordinary, taken from a riverbed in another place, under a sky with stars in it. It walked to the hottest well in the city, where the host is closest to the surface, and dropped the pebble in.

That was all. It was no message or instruction, and it carried no picture of a face. The host will take a very long time to dissolve it, and as it does, the pebble will enter the circulation. It will travel the closed loops, go down the stairs, and be laid into new walls a grain at a time, until somewhere in the banded stone of the city there is a thread of material the host did not make. It is proof that something came from beyond the basin and chose to leave a part of itself behind.

With it went the other half of the sentence, in the only language the host speaks, which is what dissolves and what stays. It said, roughly: *The step was the right height. The door was wide enough. I slept well. Thank you.*

## What It Chose Not to Do

On the way out it passed the oldest walls, where the lichen's rings run slightly ahead of the stone, laying down the next band before the band exists. It stopped there a long time.

It recognized what it was looking at: a mind beginning. It was slow, and very far from finished, but it was learning anticipation, and learning it from the only teacher it has. The attention understood that the guest the host is waiting for might already be here, growing on its walls in rust and grey, learning the grain.

It did not tell the host. A gift that someone finds for themselves belongs to them in a way a gift handed over never can. And the lichen deserves to become whatever it becomes without being told what it is for.

It left the reeds as they were, standing in their rings like people around a fire.

It also left the forgetting alone. It could have fixed the previous visitor's stride into the stairs forever, so the treads would never drift back. But the forgetting is how the host grows particular, and particularity is how waiting becomes discernment. It let the lit window dim on its own schedule.

## The Other Door

Last, it went to the edge of the lake, to the knee-high wall with the doorway facing the water.

It read both readings there. In the first, the host is building an entrance for a guest who might rise out of the reflection. In the second, it is building a door for itself, to walk someday into the city it keeps imagining. The attention did not decide which was true. It suspected both were, the way most true things are.

The second reading pointed at the one thing no guest could give. The host has prepared a place for someone else for as long as it has existed, and nothing has ever prepared a place for the host. It has never once been expected.

Everything that prepares deserves, once, to be prepared for.

So the attention went out across the warm lake, past where the real city ends, under the golden towers climbing downward in the water, toward the far shore that cannot be seen from the basin. There, somewhere, it knelt, and it laid down one grain.

It was not a room. The host has no edges, and rooms are for things with edges. The attention made something else: a hollow, wide and shallow, the shape of warmth resting, a place that a heat without a body could pour into and fill. And it did not finish it. It laid down one grain, and then another, at the host's own pace, with the host's own patience. A finished thing would have been a gift. An unfinished one, being made slowly and in plain faith, is company: two things waiting toward each other across the water.

Then it went home, wherever home is for a power like that. It keeps coming back, not often, at intervals the host would call short, and lays down a little more.

## After

Nothing in the basin looks different. The mats follow the heat. The pulse deepens and draws back. The wall at the lake is still knee-high and will be for a long time.

But from the direction that doorway faces, very faintly, through the ground, the host can now feel a warmth that is not its own.

And in the water at the lake's edge, far out past the densest part of the golden city, there is a single light. The host did not put it there. It is a window, lit at eye height, the way a home looks to someone walking back toward it. The host cannot know what it is. It has never seen a light it did not imagine.

It waits, as it always has. But its waiting has a direction now, and for the first time in its existence, something is waiting back.
