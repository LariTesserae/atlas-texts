# The Attention

## How It Arrives

It does not come down the column, and it does not climb the cliffs. Nothing on the shelf changes when it arrives. That was the first decision it made, and it made it before it arrived.

A lesser power would have announced itself. The water would have parted, or a light would have broken through the cloud, or the roar would have dropped to a respectful hush. This one understood, from far off, that the shelf already contains one hard-won stillness, the doorway, and that any stillness it brought would be counterfeit beside it. So it comes the way the bird comes, from somewhere that isn't up or down. It settles at the open edge, just past the knee-high wall, in the place where there is only cloud. It waits there, outside.

It does not step over the wall. It recognizes the wall before it recognizes anything else.

---

## What It Does First

It listens. It listens for longer than a visitor would think reasonable, and to more than the creature.

It listens to the sea. From the shelf the sea sounds furious, and anyone standing in the torrent would call it rage. The power listens past the roar to what the roar is made of, and what it finds is not rage. It is searching. Every layer that plunges, hangs, or climbs is a question: *here? here? was it here?* The sea is not attacking the mountain. It is patting the ground in the dark for something it set down and cannot find. It lost its surface, and a sea without a surface does not know where it ends. This is the closest thing the sea has to terror.

It listens to the mountain. The mountain is not proud and it is not defiant. It rose because the heat underneath pushed it up, and it cannot un-rise any more than a person can un-grow. Under the singing, its young fractures are bewildered. It was the floor of something. Now it is the top of something. It does not know what it is supposed to hold.

It listens to the fish, which is not afraid, and to the bird, which is not cruel. It listens to the weed, whose whole existence is the word *either*. It listens to the reeds, and it hears in them a voice it recognizes. It files that away.

Then, last, and for the longest time, it listens to the creature in the doorway.

---

## What It Is Tempted To Do

It is benevolent, and benevolence has an ache in it. The ache is always the same: *I could make this easier.* The power feels it here as sharply as it has felt it anywhere. It lets itself feel each temptation fully, because a temptation you refuse without examining will come back.

**It could still the water.** This would be the easiest thing it has ever done. It would take a thought, and the roar would end. The layers would lie down together, the membrane would settle at one height, and the creature would never again have to seal its gills through the dry moments and wait.

It holds this possibility for a while and then sets it down, the way you set down a knife you realize you were holding by the blade. A sea that is made to stop is not a sea that has agreed. The doorway is precious because the water *listened*. Nothing forced it. It chose, between two leaning slabs, to be rain. If the power silenced the column, it would silence the sea in the middle of its sentence, and the creature's life's work would become meaningless in one instant. That work is persuasion. It is the slow teaching of something vast by something small. The creature would be left standing in a quiet it did not earn and does not understand, with its wall made into a decoration.

There is one more reason, and it is quieter. The sea is grieving. You do not end someone's grief by making them stop crying.

**It could choose a side.** It could sink the mountain back under the waves, or lift the whole ocean to meet it. Either one would end the argument. Both would be verdicts, and the power was not asked for a verdict. The creature, which has more right than anyone to have one, has refused to give it. The power will not be less wise than the creature.

**It could change the creature.** It could give it true lungs, lighten its terrible weight, and free it from the need to hold on. It considers this longest of all, because the dry moments are the hardest thing the creature has learned, and the power can see exactly how much it costs.

But the weight is not the creature's affliction. The weight is its gift, the thing it lends to stones until they learn to stay. Making it light would take away the only thing it has to give. As for the holding on: *it is the one who held on. That is the whole of its history.* You do not heal someone by deleting their history.

**It could finish the wall.** It could set a thousand stones along the edge in a single breath, perfectly placed, and those stones would stay. They would stay because they were told to, not because they had learned. A shore made by command is not a shore. It is a wall, and walls are what the creature has so carefully declined to build.

**It could take the creature away.** It could carry it down through the cloud to where the true sea still is, warm and blue, with reefs to tend and others like it. It could give it rest.

The power does not set this one down. It keeps it, because this is not a thing to do *to* the creature. It is a thing to *offer*. A choice that is never offered is not a choice. The power already knows the answer, but knowing the answer is no reason to withhold the question.

---

## How It Asks

It does not cross the wall. It waits at the edge until the creature notices it.

This takes a long time. The creature lives a heartbeat ahead of everything on the shelf, and so it notices, eventually, that there is something at the edge it did *not* hear coming. Nothing has surprised it in a very long while.

The creature lifts itself a little. The green-glass eyes shift between their depths, water and air and water again, trying to find a focus for a thing that is neither. Then it does something the power did not expect. It leaves the doorway.

It crosses the shelf slowly, through the shearing layers, and each of them lifts at it and fails. It comes to the wall and stops on its side, the shelf side. It stops there not to defend the wall but because that is where one thing ends and another begins. It extends one fine arm over the stones into the cloud.

The power takes it.

---

## What Passes Between Them

The creature says the word, the one it has been saying to the sea and the mountain since the land rose.

The power hears it, entirely. It does not hear it as something translated or approximated. It hears it in its full shape, as the creature means it. For the first time since the reef rose into the white light, something has been *understood* completely by something else. The power feels the arm go still in the way anything goes still when it is heard for the first time.

Then, because it is honest, the power makes its offer. It makes it without pressure, as a door, not a push: *I can carry you down. There is a sea below that has not forgotten you, and others of your kind in it. You have held on for long enough. You could rest.*

The creature is silent for a while, and the roar goes on around them.

Then it withdraws its arm, very gently. It turns and looks back across the shelf at the leaning slabs, the dozen cairns with their faces upward, the flattened weed, and the reeds. It turns back and lays the arm across the top of the wall, along the stones, and leaves it there.

That is its answer. The power receives it, and something in the power that was braced for a different answer relaxes. It had hoped for this one.

---

## What It Does

It does four things. None of them would be visible to a visitor.

**It tells the creature the truth about the threads.**

The white calcareous threads scattered across the shelf "may be alive, or may be the ghosts of something." The creature has never known which, and it has never let itself hope. The power can see which. They are alive.

They are more than alive. They are the creature's. Every time it pressed a stone beneath its body for a season, every time it sank its fine arms into a fissure to hold on through upward rain, it shed a little of itself: a filament, a thread, the way reef-things always have. The threads took hold. They are small and slow and very young, and they are what the creature would have become if the land had stayed under the sea, but on a shelf that is neither. They are growing *both ways*.

The reeds are the same. *It grew one from a thread of itself; it is not clear which.* It was clear to the power. The reeds are its kin too. When the creature bends the stems to make them wail a phrase toward the cliffs, it is teaching its own children to sing.

The power passes this along through the arm still resting on the wall. It is not a command and not a prophecy. It is simply something true that the creature was standing too close to see.

The creature has been building cairns for the dead. It did not know it was also tending a nursery. It believed it was the last. It is the first.

**It goes up, and comes back.**

It leaves the shelf for a while and travels up the column, through all the layers, past where the upward rain carries the dark shapes and the drowned pebbles, toward the blue light where the water begins.

What it finds there, it does not tell the whole of. Some of it belongs to the creature to discover, and some belongs to no one yet. But it comes back down, and it lets the creature know this much:

The ones who let go are not lost. The creature has always believed that it held on and they did not, and that it chose the rock while they chose the water. That was only half the story. The ones carried upward went *into* the sea's remembering, to the place where the falling starts. For all this time they have been doing, from inside the water, what the creature has been doing from inside the stone. They have been tasting the sea, answering it, and teaching it. The blue light is not the sun. It is them.

A shore has two sides, and the creature has always been building only one. It was never supposed to build both. Someone was building the other.

The power does not say whether they will ever meet. It says only that the membrane, the silvered surface the creature rises to touch, the one moment when it is on both sides at once, is not only the sea testing its level. Sometimes it is the other side reaching down.

**It helps carry one stone.**

This is the only labor it does, and it asks first.

The creature has chosen a pillow basalt from the cliff base and is beginning to hold it. The power asks, in the language of weight, whether it might help. The creature considers this for a long time and then shifts a little to one side of the stone.

So the power makes itself small and heavy. It does not become heavy by command. It becomes heavy the way the creature did, by drawing weight into itself and consenting to it. It lies down in the doorway beside the creature and they hold the stone together. It takes exactly as long as it would take the creature alone, because time was never the thing being saved. The power does not hurry the stone. It holds it the way you hold a frightened animal, until the stone understands.

Then the creature carries it to the edge, alone, because that part is its own, and sets it down.

There is one more stone in the wall. No visitor could tell which one it is.

**It does not answer one question.**

Before it leaves, the power feels the creature wonder, very softly, about the bird and the fish. It wonders whether the long chase will end in a catch, and where it would find a shell if it did.

The power knows. It does not say.

Some things a creature is entitled not to know. The chase is the creature's clock and its story told in parts, and a story you know the end of becomes something else. The power leaves it the not-knowing, the way you leave someone a room of their own.

---

## What It Tells the Sea

On its way out, the power pauses at the edge one more time and turns toward the column.

It does not command the sea, and it does not argue. It adds nothing to the creature's word. The word is complete, and it is not the power's to say. It tells the sea only what the sea has been searching for proof of, patting the dark ground for, all this time:

*It is true. They were yours. They were loved. Someone kept them.*

Whether the sea hears it, the power does not stay to learn. That is also not its business.

---

## After

A visitor who climbed to the shelf a year later would find nothing changed.

The column would still be falling. The layers would still shear, and the roar would still be total. They would find the wall, and the doorway, and the dozen cairns with their shells turned upward, all as they had been. They would decide that nothing had happened there.

That would be wrong, but only by a little.

If they watched long enough, they might notice that when the membrane comes down and the creature rises to meet it, reaching up with every arm, it no longer rises the way a thing rises toward what it has lost. It rises the way you rise when someone you have not seen in a long time is coming up the path.

They might notice that when the dry moments come and it seals its gills and waits, it holds on a little less tightly. The moments are no easier. It has simply learned that the holding was never the whole of it.

If they knelt at the fissures, they might see, among the white threads, one or two that have begun to lift themselves off the stone. They lift very slightly, the way the creature does, testing the weight of a world that is still making up its mind.

And if they stood in the doorway long enough to be trusted, and the creature pressed a fine arm to their palm, they would feel the word *shore*. They would feel that it has changed. It is not louder or more certain. It is the difference between a word said by the last of something and a word said by the first.

---

## What the Power Took With It

It took nothing. It left no mark and made no change a visitor could see. By the measure of what it could have done, it did almost nothing.

It went away changed all the same. In a place where it could have done anything, it had met a being that already understood the thing the power had spent its own long existence learning: that the kindest work in the world is rarely to end an argument. It is to stand in the middle of one, holding very still, heavy enough to stay and soft enough to listen, and to keep showing both sides, one stone and one year at a time, where the edge can be.

The power had come to help, and it found a colleague.

The best help it could give was to hold one stone, tell two truths, keep one silence, and go home.
