# The Attention

## Arriving Without Arriving

The first thing it does is decide how to come in.

It could take a shape, stand in the cleft, and walk the thirty paces into the dark. But a body is warm, or cold, and either would matter here. A body displaces air, and the vault runs on air: on the exact volume of the breath, the exact temperature gradient between the inner glass and the outer stone. A body has a sound. Even standing perfectly still, it would reflect the swifts' clicks back at them, and ten thousand small mouths would register an object where no object has ever been. The border colonies nearest it would retune. The chord would bend around it the way it bends around the gray beards, and something that had never been hurt by anything but the mountain and the quiet would learn a third kind of wound.

So the power comes in the way attention comes into a room when someone who has been talking looks up and realizes they are being listened to, except that here no one looks up. It takes up no space and gives off no warmth. It does not ring. It is, as nearly as a thing can be, not there.

This is the first choice it makes, and it will turn out to be most of what it does.

## The Temptations

It is benevolent, and it is powerful, and so its first impulse is the one every benevolent and powerful thing has on entering a place: to look for what is wrong.

There is a great deal wrong, if you want to see it that way.

**The first night.** It watches a fledging. The river of wings pours out on the exhale, and the young go with it, ragged-voiced, a few days past learning their notes. The cold above the cloud-floor takes them by the hundreds. It watches a chick tumble, lose the breath, spiral down onto the black scree below the ridge, and cling there. It is small enough to hold in a closed hand. It clicks its note at a mountain that does not answer, because it has fallen below the level where the chord leaks out. By morning it is still.

The power could stop this. It would be almost nothing: a little warmth laid over the ridge on fledging nights, a gentleness in the wind. It does the arithmetic anyway, because benevolence that will not do arithmetic is only a mood. If every chick survived, the ceiling would hold four times the bodies in a decade. There are no more spines. The mats would crush their own young against the glass. The border wars would stop being wars of pitch and become wars of teeth. Then the colony would crash, far more horribly than the cold ever kills. The first night is the vault's way of never having to find out how many swifts it can hold.

So it does not intervene. It does not pretend this is painless, either. It stays with the chick on the scree until the chick stops, because there should be at least one thing in the world that noticed. That costs nothing, and it changes nothing, and it does it anyway, and it will keep doing it for every fledging night it is there. This becomes the pattern.

**The gray beards.** The fungus is the obvious enemy. It eats the song and makes silence, and it is patient in a way that looks like malice. The power could scour it off every spine in an instant, and the ceiling would ring clean.

It listens to the fungus instead. This takes years, because the fungus is slow. It learns that the beards are not killing the spines, exactly. They are what a spine becomes when nothing is moving it. The fungus is the mountain's rest, arriving early in the places where the mountain has already stopped moving. It learns that the pale beetles graze the fallen beard-fragments on the floor. It learns something subtler, too. The chord's shape is made as much by its silences as by its notes. The low voices bend away from the beards, and that bending is part of the chord, part of what makes this voice this voice rather than some other. A chord that could never lose a note would not be a healthier version of this one. It would be a different, stranger thing, with no memory of injury. It would never reach for anything.

The power leaves the fungus alone. It extends to the quiet the same courtesy it extends to the song: it is also a resident, and it was here first in the sense that matters. Silence is what the vault was before the swifts and what it will be after.

**The mountain.** The deepest threat is the slow clock. The mountain will stop rising, the tremors will end, the beards will win, and the chord will go out. The power could keep the mountain restless forever and feed it from below, and the song would never end.

But the mountain's restlessness is not only the vault's. Below the clouds, the same tremors that crack fresh glass also crack terraces, bring down the stone walls of fields, and bury houses. The chord's immortality would be paid for in the valleys, by the very people who climb up to count its notes. And beyond that, a mountain held forever at the moment of its youth is not a mountain being kept alive. It is a mountain being kept, like a thing under glass. The power understands that there is a kind of love that mostly wants the beloved to stop changing, and that this kind is not wise.

**The waking.** This is the temptation it takes longest to refuse, because it is the most generous-seeming.

The chord does not think, probably. The power could change that. It could take the listening-and-answering of two hundred voices and deepen it, loop it back on itself, and let the pattern notice that it is a pattern. There is enough complexity on the ceiling to hold a mind. It would take very little, a push in a direction the thing is already, faintly, leaning.

It imagines what the chord would wake to.

It would wake into a body it cannot move, made of ten million creatures that do not know it exists. It would wake into a throat of glass that is slowly breaking and cannot be mended. It would learn about the gray beards and what they mean. It would learn about the widening cleft, and the light coming, and the dulling. It would have perhaps a few thousand years in which to understand, precisely and with no recourse, that it is dying, and there would be nothing it could do with that knowledge except sing, which is what it is already doing.

The power does not do this. There is a difference between giving something life and giving something the knowledge of its death, and the power will not confuse the two. The chord persists, flinches, heals, and changes without ever having to know it will end. The power decides that this, too, is not a lack. It is a mercy the chord already has, and it is not the power's to take away.

## The Cord

In the second decade it follows the braided cord.

The cord runs a little way into the vault and stops, knotted around a spur of glass. The power goes on past the end of it, into the ammonia, where the air would close a human throat. A long way further, near the center of the nave, directly beneath the longest of the bass spines, it finds what it half-expected: a shape under the guano. It is mostly gone. The beetles and the damp have had it for a long time. What is left are a few bones, green-black, and on one of them a small bead of worked obsidian. The fungus glows faintly along the line of the ribs, so that on the floor of the vault there is a single constellation shaped roughly like a person lying on their back, face up.

They were facing the ceiling.

The power stays with this for a while. It thinks about why someone would let go of the cord. Not for guano, which you can gather at the mouth, and not for eggs, which are on the walls. Here, at the center, under the deepest spines, is the one place in the vault where every note arrives at once with nothing in between. It is the only place where you could hear the whole chord.

Everyone else who ever came heard it from the passage, partially, and cut the fragments into stone. This one wanted the rest of it. They walked out into air they could not breathe to lie down in the one place where it was complete, and they listened for as long as they had.

The power does not move the bones. They are part of the floor now, and the gleaners' glowing tracks cross them nightly. But it understands, finally, what it came here to do. It is the only thing here that can do what this person tried to do and survive it.

## The Listening

So it listens.

It settles at the center of the nave, where the bones lie, beneath the long bass spines, and it hears the chord whole. It is the first thing that ever has. No swift knows the whole chord, and no colony does. The people in the passage hear a corner of it, smeared by the narrow stone. The chord itself does not hear itself; it is only the sum of neighbors answering neighbors. Until now, the whole shape of the voice in the Chorus Vault has existed without ever being received by anything.

Now it is received.

The power hears the dusk swell as a single long event, the high tones draining east-to-west like color leaving a sky. It hears the pre-dawn return as a frantic, beautiful gathering-in, the chord refilling itself note by note as each colony finds its spine on the wind. It hears the seasons: the thickening in the fledging months, when thousands of young voices click off-pitch and are corrected, so the whole ceiling shimmers with near-misses. It hears the wall birds' screams run across the glass and the ceiling's rippling, irritated answer, which through the years it comes to understand as something like the chord's temper.

Then there is a tremor.

It is small. The valleys below probably do not notice. Three spines fall into the guano. For the first time the power hears, from inside, what the swifts' ancestors have heard for centuries: the chord *reaching*. The neighboring colonies stretch their pitch toward the gaps. The whole ceiling leans, for days, toward what is missing. Then comes the long quarrel of resettlement, and then, one night, the power hears the chord settle. It is in a new configuration, slightly darker, with the old wound folded into it, and it is unmistakably itself.

The power has seen many things with minds. It has seldom seen anything grieve so well without one.

It stays. The swifts live their four years and die, and their grandchildren's grandchildren cling to the same spines and sound the same notes. The notch-cutters come up from below the clouds, stand in the passage, listen, cut, and go down. Their faces change, and their clothes, and once, for a period of about forty years, no one comes at all. The power does not know why. It does not go down to find out. Then someone comes again, very young, and the new notches are clumsy and the intervals are slightly wrong, and over a decade they become right again.

## What It Keeps

There is one thing the power allows itself to do. It is the only act it permits itself in the whole long attendance, and it is done entirely on its own side, where the vault will never feel it.

It remembers.

It holds the whole chord: not a record like the notches, partial and frost-dulled, but all of it, every configuration the voice has taken since the power arrived, every wound and every healing. And because it is wise, it holds the chord *as it is*. The power could hold any pitch perfectly. But there is a spine near the eastern wall, cracked by some tremor centuries before the power came, that rings a little flat. The chicks there learn the note wrong on purpose, because wrong is what home sounds like. The power keeps that note flat. It does not correct it.

This is the difference between remembering something and remembering your idea of it. It is the most exact kind of care the power knows.

## The Long Quiet

It stays through the end.

That takes a long time. The mountain slows, the tremors come further apart, and then they stop. The beards spread. The cleft widens by fractions of a finger each century until one year the blade of light comes in a little broader, stays a little longer, and reaches spines it never reached before. The glass begins to weather and the swifts' notes blur. Colonies merge onto the spines that still ring, and the border wars soften into crowding. The chord thins. It gets simpler, fewer voices, cleaner intervals, like an old singer's voice losing its upper register but keeping, somehow, its character.

The power does not intervene. It had decided long ago, and the decision holds. It does what it did for the chick on the scree: it stays, and it notices. At the end, when there are only a handful of voices left, clustered on the last deep spines at the center of the nave directly above the green-black bones, the power is right there underneath them, where someone once lay down to hear them whole.

The last colony on the last spine is perhaps two hundred swifts, clicking a single low note into glass that barely answers. Then it is a few dozen. Then it is the old and the injured who stayed behind, as they always have, keeping the spine singing as long as one mouth is working.

Then no mouth is working.

The power holds the note a moment longer, the way you keep your hand on something after it has stopped moving. Then it lets the vault be quiet, because the quiet has been waiting a very long time and has its own right to the place.

## The Question at the End

People still come.

They have not forgotten. A person climbs up out of the white cloud-floor on some particular night of the year, the night when the young used to fledge. They stand in the widened cleft, where the moss has gone brown and the rime no longer forms. They sing the old notes into the dark: the chord as it was carried down, drifted by generations of valley throats, not quite what the ceiling last sounded but recognizably, somehow, *the same voice* in a different key.

They are waiting, as the notches always suggested someone would one day wait, to see if anything answers.

The power could answer. That would be the easiest thing it has ever done. It holds the whole chord, and it could pour it back out of the dead glass in the voice of the vault itself, exact and complete, flat eastern spine and all. The person in the cleft would weep, and carry it down, and it would become a story.

It does not do this, and this refusal costs it more than any of the others. It would be a lie. The voice would be the power's, not the vault's. The people in the valleys have spent centuries doing something true and difficult: following a real voice, at a distance, and faithfully recording its changes. They deserve not to have the last page of that record forged.

And it does not need to answer, because something already does.

The person sings into the cleft, and the vault, empty and dull and bearded, gives their voice back. The glass no longer rings, but the room still has its shape. A chamber the size of a small church nave returns any sound made into it, a little late and a little softened, colored by every surface. The notes come back out of the dark, and they are not the chord. But they are the valley's version of the chord, sung by someone whose ancestors learned it from this ceiling, reflected off the same stone that shaped it. The mountain gave them the song, and now the mountain gives it back.

The power listens to this, too. It understands that this is not an echo pretending to be an answer. It is the answer, the only honest one available, and it is enough. The thing that lived in the Chorus Vault does not live there anymore. It lives in the person in the cleft, and in the room that still knows how to hold a voice, and in the one place where it is kept complete, with its wounds and its wrongness intact.

When the person finishes singing and turns to go down into the cloud, the power follows them a little way down the scree. It does nothing, and it touches nothing. It is only there, listening, the way it has always been, to a voice that cannot think, carried by one that can.

That is what it did. It was not a rescue, because there was nothing that needed rescuing that could have been rescued without being destroyed. It was the other thing benevolence can be when there is nothing to fix. For the whole life of something that had never once been heard in full, it made sure that it was.
