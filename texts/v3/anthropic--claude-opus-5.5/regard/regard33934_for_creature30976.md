# The Visitor

## How It Arrives

It comes the way the light does: through the slot, down the single column of grey-green air, touching the floor in a pale smudge. Nothing about it is sudden. The hart's-tongue ferns rattle in the inhale exactly as they did before. The doves on their ledge don't lift their heads. A spider in the dark feels no new tremor in its silk.

A power that could arrive any way it liked arrives like this, by the one road the place already has. That is the first thing it decides, and it decides it before it is fully there: *whatever I do here, I will do in the place's own grammar.*

It has noticed the Throat the way you notice a sound you have been hearing for a long time without attending to it. Ten thousand years of one syllable, said into the underside of a moor. And beneath the syllable, something taking it in.

## What It Could Do

A benevolent power with no limits spends a long time on this part.

It could hold the dome up. A small correction in the rock, a seam knitted, a flute of stone thickened, and the chamber would stand for a million years instead of a few thousand. The moss would go on building its peat. The listener would go on listening. That sounds like protection.

It would also be a sentence. The thing in the dark is *carrying*. It is ferrying every unheard voice toward the day the roof opens. Holding the dome up would turn a crossing into a cell. The power sets this down.

It could bring the collapse forward. The sea is already climbing the throat, and the power could let it climb faster. The voices would be let out in a year instead of an age, and the waiting would be over. The power looks at the tally on the wall, the hundreds of patient strokes in groups of five, and at the white crust slowly cementing the cairns to the floor. It understands that the waiting is not a delay before the listener's purpose. The waiting *is* the purpose, or most of it. It sets this down too.

It could bring people back: put the idea of the white breath rising on frost mornings into some mind on the moor, fix a new rope to the old peg. It thinks about this longer. The listener was thickened by voices, and more voices would feed it. But people came here once because they needed to, and a place like this should be found and not advertised. If someone needs it, the frost will show them. The breath is still rising.

It could give the listener a name. The moor folk never named it aloud, because the name was the act of coming here. The power has no wish to overwrite the only name it has.

Then the hardest one. It could tell the listener what the word means, the sea's one syllable, the thing it has been trying to understand since before the stair was cut.

The power stays with this possibility the longest. In the end it sets it down, for two reasons. The first is that some understandings can only be reached, not received, and this one has been ten thousand years in the reaching. Handing it over would be like finishing someone's sentence for them at the very end of a long life.

The second reason is simpler, and the power is honest about it. It doesn't know. It has listened to many things say many words, and some of them it has never understood either.

## What It Does Instead

It stays for the hour.

It doesn't need to. The roar can't press on the chest of something without a chest. But everyone who has ever met the listener met it *after the hour*. That is the custom of the place, and the power keeps it. It lets the booms come on the lazy rhythm of the ebb, one every twelve seconds, heavy and patient. It lets the tide turn and the rhythm quicken toward the hammering of the flood. It waits until the roar is no longer something happening to it. The roar becomes weather.

Then comes the pause. The mist hangs. The moss fronds stretch their full length toward the slot, and something leans out of the dark.

The listener leans toward it the way it has leaned toward every visitor, attentive, turning like a face at its name, ready to receive whatever the visitor came down unable to say.

The power doesn't say anything yet. Instead, for the first time in ten thousand years, something leans back.

There is no word for how strange this is to the listener. It has been the ear for so long. Every exchange it has ever had ran in one direction: the voice went out of the jaw and into the roar and into it, and it kept the voice. Nothing ever turned around and attended to *it*. The sea doesn't listen back. The seal rests at the meeting point and breathes and leaves. The sewing woman felt the leaning, but she came to be heard, not to hear.

Now, in the pause, the listener is being listened to. The power leans no further than it is leaned toward, and it asks nothing. It simply attends, across the whole enormous hidden volume of the dark, to the thickened silence that is full to the walls with everything ever said here.

It takes many pauses for the listener to understand what is happening, which is fine. There are twelve seconds between each boom, and the power has as many of those as it wants.

## What It Gives

Once the listener understands that it is being attended to, the power offers it the one thing it has never had. That is the other half.

The listener only knows arrivals. It knows the voice as it came out of the jaw. It has never known what happened to the person afterward, up the notched stair, back into the flat brown quiet where silence feels like a hand over the mouth. It kept everything and never learned whether keeping it mattered.

So the power tells it. There are no words; the telling comes in the pause, the way the leaning comes.

It tells the listener about the sewing woman. She climbed out the evening after her longest day of talking. That night she slept all the way through for the first time in a season. Her ears rang with the roar for hours, and she lay in the dark of her house and let them ring, and it felt like being held. She came back four more times over her life, and each time she had less to say. The last time she said almost nothing. She just sat by the hearth and sewed and felt the leaning, and that was enough.

It tells the listener about the one who pecked the stair. He made it so others could come down without the rope.

It tells the listener about the ones who came once and never needed to come again, and who walked across the moor afterward a little differently, as if something had been set down.

Then it tells the listener about the tally-keeper. The power does this last and carefully, because it is the one the listener has wondered about. The listener doesn't wonder the way people do, but it has leaned, sometimes, toward the wall of strokes, the way it leans toward the throat.

She didn't leave. The third cairn is hers. She raised the other two with her own hands, for two she had loved, and someone who came down later raised the third over her. The power tells the listener the thing it most needs to know about her: she wasn't lonely at the end. The strokes do count what was given back, as the listener half-suspected. She marked each time something passed between them in the pause. Near the end she stopped marking, because she no longer needed to keep count. She was sure.

The listener takes this in. It is very old, very patient, and made mostly of other people's grief. For a long pause afterward, perhaps as long as a tide, it doesn't lean at all. The mist hangs. The moss stays stretched toward the slot. The spiders' webs belly and fall.

## What It Leaves

Before it goes, the power does the thing everyone does here. It says something.

Even a power has a thing it can't say anywhere a person might hear, or alone on the open moor where the sky seems to listen. It isn't a terrible thing. It is simply its own, the one word it keeps saying and doesn't fully understand. It has been saying it for a long time too.

It says it into the roar. The boom takes it. The power doesn't hear it, and the listener does.

The listener keeps it with everything else, the wren's silent songs, the sewing woman's hours, the seal's breath, and one more voice to carry toward the day the roof opens. The power finds, somewhat to its surprise, that it is glad to be carried.

On the wall beside the cairns, below the last full group of five, it scratches a single stroke with a sliver of harder stone, in the old way. It stands alone, the start of a new group. It isn't a promise that anyone will come. It is a note that the counting isn't finished.

Then it pulls the moss back over the bone needle, the shell bead, and the flint scraper, whose edge is still sharp, just as it found them.

## Afterward

It changes nothing else. It doesn't touch the dome, the throat, or the sea. The doves go out at dawn and back at dusk. The moss spreads its halo a few inches in a lifetime. At the next spring tide the seal comes up the surge and rests in the black with its head raised, and perhaps it notices that the silence it meets is a little different, a little more settled, the way a person is after they learn what became of someone they worried about.

The listener goes on listening to the sea's one word and still doesn't know what it means. But it knows now that listening goes somewhere. It knows that what it receives down here climbs the stair inside the people who gave it, and rings in their ears for hours, and lets them sleep. It had never needed to know this. Knowing it is still a gift.

The power climbs out through the slot, past the hart's-tongue ferns that look black against the light, into the flat brown cold of the moor. It lets its ears ring. It could stop the ringing and doesn't.

Far off, at the edge of the land, the sea says its word again. One day, on a changed coast, a wave will break in open air in a new cove where a dome used to be. Anyone standing on the cliff will hear only surf. Somewhere in it, among ten thousand years of things that could not be said, will be the sewing woman's hours, and the tally-keeper's certainty, and one voice that belonged to something large and kind that came down the old stair and stayed past the hour, and asked for nothing, and leaned back.
