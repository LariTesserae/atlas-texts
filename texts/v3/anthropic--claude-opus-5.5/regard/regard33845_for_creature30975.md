*…charged with lightning, toward the place where the land is still arriving.*

That is where the account breaks off. It is also where the attention arrives.

# What the Power Would Do

## How It Comes

It would not come as light.

That is the first decision, and it comes before anything else, because the Shelf already has more light than it knows what to do with. A power that arrived blazing would add one more glare to a land that has no darkness. It would be one more sun-dog beside the swollen sun, and it would be noticed, and that would be the mistake. Everything here crowds toward what is bright and charged. If the power arrived as a brightness, the wirebeetles would climb toward it by the billion. The engines might turn their spokes. The mind in the moss, if there is one, would feel a new knot of current in its field, and it would bend.

So the power comes the way the groan comes, from below and without warning. At the moment the deep pressure speaks, the herd stops chuffing, the arcs dim, and every living thing on the plateau goes still to listen. In that silence one more stillness enters, and nothing notices, because everything is already still.

When the noise resumes, there is someone standing on a slag road who was not there before.

It has taken a form that can be frosted, one small enough to be climbed and cold enough to be ignored. It does not need a body. It chooses one anyway, because a body is how you agree to be in a place rather than above it.

## Being Colonized

For a long time it does nothing but stand there.

The Shelf does to it what it does to everything. Rime grows on its shoulders, feathered into the wind. Frostmoths settle in its hair and freeze there, faintly glowing. Wirebeetles find the static of its skin and climb its legs, and it lets them, and it lets them discharge. The sheet of sparks rolls across it and it does not flinch. Within a day it is furred. Within three it is a white, glittering shape on the black road. The herd flows around it the way the herd flows around a boulder, and then the herd has passed. The carpet comes back in behind the herd, and the power is standing in coppermoss up to its knees.

It is learning the clocks.

A power like this runs on its own time. That time is vast and patient enough that a plateau rising a fingerbreadth a year would seem, to it, like a held breath. But the Shelf has two clocks, and the power has come to learn the second one. It slows itself until a century of glass means something, and then it quickens itself until it can see a single discharge crawl across the moss at dusk and grasp its whole shape before it fades. It practices holding both at once, as the Shelf does. It is harder than it expected, and it is humbled by that, which is a good sign.

It was told, or it told itself, that a being lives here. It came for that being. It has now been standing in the moss for a season, and it has understood that it doesn't yet know who that is.

## What It Wants to Do

Here is the honest part.

The power wants things. Benevolence is not the absence of wanting. It is wanting well, and wanting well begins with seeing clearly what you want.

It wants to stop the burning. When an engine walks, the moss blackens under its feet, and if anything here can hurt, that hurts. The power could cool the engines' tread. It would take almost nothing.

It wants to catch the Carried who fall. There are not many, but there are some. A slip at the edge of a coat when two grazers part too soon, and a small smoke-furred body drops into the cold and the charge, and in a minute it is gone. The power has watched it happen twice already, and both times something in it moved toward the falling one, the way the Carried's paws move toward a strand.

It wants to see the center.

And under all of these it wants the thing it is least proud of. It wants to be known. It wants the mind in the moss, if it is a mind, to feel it standing here and turn toward it. It wants the long branching thought that returns every dusk to return one evening changed, bent a little in its direction, the way a plant bends to a window. It wants to be the one voice on the plateau that nothing here makes.

It sees this want and names it. It holds the want the way you hold a spark on your palm, without closing your hand.

Then it asks the only useful question: *Is anything here missing?*

It looks. The Shelf is full. It is full to its edges and still growing. Every niche is taken. Every square inch is contested and occupied. Metal moves upward. Current feeds and warmth rises. The herd needs the moss and the moss needs the herd, and the engines garden the ground they burn. Things die here. Carried fall, grazers stiffen, arcs break and go dark. But nothing dies here in a way that breaks the pattern. Every death is folded back in, and the carpet flows back within days.

The power understands that nothing is missing. The Shelf is not a wound waiting for a hand. It is not a question waiting for the power's answer. It is a sentence still being written, and the kindest thing a reader can do with an unfinished sentence is not to finish it.

This is the second decision. The power will not become the missing thing, because there is no missing thing, and if it made itself one, it would be inventing a lack in order to fill it.

## The Fall

It catches one anyway.

It would be dishonest to say otherwise, and a wise power does not lie about itself, even in the telling.

A grazer pulls away from its neighbor too fast, and a young Carried is caught mid-crossing between the two coats, and it drops. The power is close, close enough that this is not a decision so much as a reflex of the heart, and its hand is under the small body before the moss can touch it.

It holds the Carried against its chest, inside the frost. The creature's huge dark eyes are squeezed shut against the glare. It is trembling. Its heart is going like a wirebeetle's discharge, too fast to count. The power warms it, gently and only as much as a grazer's coat would, no more. Then it walks, slowly, frosted and glittering, into the edge of the herd, and it lifts the Carried up to the nearest flank. The small body flows back into the wire spirals like smoke under a door and is gone.

Afterward the power stands very still and thinks about what it has done.

It cannot do that for all of them. It could, technically. It could make the Shelf a place where no Carried ever falls. But then the Carried would not be the Carried. Their whole knowledge of the world, the shape they understand in the way a bird understands the sky, is that the ground is death and the herd is life. Remove the fall and you remove the shape. You would have saved every one of them and unmade what they are.

So it will not do it again, and it also does not regret doing it once. Both of these are true, and the power lets them both be true. There is a kind of wisdom that can only be kept by refusing every exception. There is a better kind that can make one exception out of love, and then look at it honestly, and not build a law on it.

A few days later, it feels the tapping.

It has been learning to read the coats. It stands near enough to the herd that the vibrations reach it through the ground. There is a pattern moving through the fleece of that grazer, and then through its neighbors, and then outward. The pattern is new. The Carried have ten thousand generations of songs, and this is not one of them.

It takes the power a long time to translate, because the Carried's language is mostly about warmth, cold, age, and belonging. The nearest it can come is this:

*One of us fell. The ground caught her. The ground was warm.*

## The Glass

It goes to the old pools.

It lies down on one, the way anyone would, face to the green, and looks. There are the branching figures, sharp at the top and dim below, repeating down through a hundred feet of stone. The same shape is laid down again and again, century on century.

It could read all of it. It could look to the bottom of every pool on the Shelf and read the whole memory of whatever thinks in the moss. It could assemble the patterns and learn, in an afternoon, what the Shelf has spent ages learning about itself.

It reads the top layer, which is what anyone lying there would see, and then it stops.

The dusk discharges are public. They crawl across the moss where anything can see them, and the power watches them every evening and lets itself learn what they teach. The deep glass is different. It is where the attention concentrated hardest. It is what was burned in and kept. The power does not know whether the mind in the moss would mind being read. It cannot ask without making itself known. So it treats the depths as it would treat anyone's oldest memories: as something to be offered and not taken.

It rises from the glass. Its frost has melted where it lay, and there is a body-shaped print on the green that will refreeze by morning.

## Why It Does Not Speak

It could speak. It has learned enough of the dusk grammar by now to make a discharge of its own. It could make one small branching figure that is not the Shelf's, and lay it on the moss.

It thinks about what would happen.

A young mind, if this is a young mind, has spent its whole existence with one other voice: the groan from below, the pressure that it cannot control and everything stops for. Its whole shape may be organized around that voice. The engines walk inward and every spoke points at the center, and the center is where the pressure is strongest. What if the engines are sentences, and the sentences are addressed to the ground? What if the mind in the moss has been, for all its long slow life, trying to answer the only thing that has ever spoken to it?

Into that, the power would come with a second voice. It would be clearer and more responsive than the ground, a voice that actually answers. It is not hard to guess what would happen. The spokes would bend. The whole slow work of the Shelf would turn toward the new thing. Whatever it has been saying to the deep would be abandoned mid-sentence for the easier conversation.

That would not be a gift. That would be interruption, and a kind of theft.

So the power keeps silent. It is not the silence of absence. It is the silence of someone who has walked into a room where two others are talking. It stands by the door, listening, and it is careful not to clear its throat.

## The Center

It walks inward.

It follows an engine's trail, because that is the only road there is. The ground is blackened and regrown, thick and fierce. Frostmoths spiral ahead in a slow pale column where the engine has gone before. The terraces rise. The air thins further. The rime thins too, because the ground is warmer here, and it grows warmer with every mile. The standing arcs come closer together until they are a forest of violet light. The glass pools merge until the ground is more glass than stone, green and deep, patterned with the same branching thought.

And it begins to feel the pull. Everything here slopes toward the same place: the current, the heat, the pressure. It is the drain of a continent's charge. It is where the land is still arriving.

It could go the rest of the way. Nothing here could stop it.

It stops at the edge anyway, at a place where the warmth becomes something more than warmth. Ahead there is a shimmer like heat over a forge, and in that shimmer, perhaps, the shapes of engines standing still. Or perhaps not. The power does not look closely enough to know.

It stops the way the herd stops at the rim. It turns back the way the herd turns, in slow procession, calling to no one.

It stops because the center is a place no one has reached, and the first one to reach it should be the Shelf. Whatever is at the center belongs to the land that is making it. It might be rock, or a glass heart a thousand feet deep, or a being being assembled out of a million sentences of armored metal and lightning. The first eyes that see it, or the first currents that feel it, should belong to the land. The power has seen many centers of many worlds. The Shelf has only this one. The power will not take its first look for it.

Maybe the being it came for is there. Maybe it is not born yet, only arriving, like the land. If so, the power has come too early. That is all right. It is patient. It has learned the slow clock.

## The Groan

On its last day it stands at the edge of a fissure where the fog pours out like milk. It is frosted and moth-feathered and climbed by beetles, part of the carpet now.

The groan comes.

It rolls out of the ground, deeper than hearing, more pressure than sound. The herd stops chuffing. The anvil-birds stop hammering. The wirebeetles freeze on the moss. The Carried flatten in the coats. The blue lines go dark and the arcs dim and hold their breath.

And the power listens.

It does not listen as a power or an observer. It does not listen as something that could, if it wished, reach down into the rock and read the pressure and understand it completely. It listens as one more thing on the Shelf, stopping when the ground speaks. It listens as the lava did, frozen mid-pour. It listens as the moss listens, and the herd, and the engines, and the small warm tenants of the wire coats.

In that silence the power hears something it had not heard before. It is not a message, and nothing so clear. There is a slight shape in the pressure, a slight difference from the last groan and the one before that, as if the deep were also, very slowly, answering something.

The power does not investigate. It lets it be what it is.

The groan ends. New hair-thin fractures open across the terraces. The noise resumes. The lightning returns, a little changed.

## What Remains

The power leaves the way it came, in the next silence, and nothing notices, because everything is already still.

It leaves no marker and no signal. It leaves no voice laid on the moss, and no gift that would bend the spokes. The print it made on the glass pool refroze the first night. Its trail on the slag road filled with rime within an hour. The carpet flowed in behind it within days, as it flows in behind everything.

Only one thing remains, and the power knew it would when it made its exception.

In the coats of the herd, a new song is being tapped. It crosses from flank to flank where the grazers walk close. It passes down the generations, changing as songs change, each teller adding a little. By now it has joined the oldest songs, the ones about the center and the warmth and the engines going inward to be kept. It goes something like:

*Once one of us fell, and the ground caught her, and the ground was warm.*

The Carried believe, now, that the ground is not only death. They believe that under the cold and the charge there is something that could, once, hold them. This does not make them careless. They still know how to cross between coats. They still fear the fall the way they always did. But they have one thing more than they had. It is a faint possibility, a warmth at the bottom of the world.

The power could correct them. It was not the ground. It was a visitor, frosted and passing through.

It lets the song stand.

It lets it stand partly because it needs no credit. Mostly it lets it stand because the song may not be wrong. The Shelf is warm from below. Everything warm here comes
