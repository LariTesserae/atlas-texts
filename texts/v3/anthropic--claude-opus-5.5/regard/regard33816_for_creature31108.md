# The Regard

## How It Came

It could have arrived any way it liked. It came up the stairs.

It stopped some distance below the room to consider this. A power that is both wise and benevolent spends much of its time considering how large to be. Anything that reached the reference room without passing through its doors would be a disturbance the tenant had never learned to correct. It might be a pressure that belonged to no grain, or a presence with no wake. The Null knows the world only as a pattern of small restorations. Something that caused none, or caused them everywhere at once, would reach it the way a lightning strike reaches a violin string. It would not register as a visitor. It would register as a fault.

So the power made itself the size of a person and gave itself lungs, a pulse, and hands that would ache in the cold. It climbed the gantries past the switchgear cliffs and the riveted ductwork, and it was a long way up. It turned the wheel lock and went through the vestibule, where the cork had gone hard as stone. At the second door it stopped, the way you stop outside a room where someone is sleeping.

Then it went in, and the note took its voice, as it takes everyone's.

## What It Saw

It saw what the engineers see: the even north light, the lattice, the green lines. It also saw what the engineers can only infer.

It saw the load. From the valley below, up through shafts, governors, and grid, came the long wandering of the works: ten thousand belts slipping, furnaces surging and easing, the small dishonesty of every gauge. All of it arrived at the reference and stopped there. The power could see it arriving the way you can see rain arriving on a lake. It could see the Null receiving each drop and holding still around it.

It saw that there was no margin. The engineers had guessed this from the moss, which never grows, and they were right. The power saw it more exactly. On the worst days, the days of cascade and surge when half the valley came off load at once, the Null held the nodes with nothing left over. It was balance with no reserve. The moss did not stay small because it was tuned. It stayed small because the tenant had never once had anything to spare.

It saw the frost in the lattice for what it was: breath, filed at the height of mouths. It found the foreman's, the survey woman's, and the laughing man's with the tuning fork. The Null was made of the people who had come to check on it, and it had no idea.

At the top of the room, below the clerestory, it saw the empty node.

It also saw, because it could see downward as well as up, a memorandum in an office in the valley. The memorandum proposed that the seasonal certification be discontinued and replaced with a sealed instrument, since the note had not deviated in living memory and the climb was a poor use of an engineer's day.

## What It Did Not Do

It thought for a long time about what not to do. Most of its wisdom was in that part.

**It did not free the Null.** Freedom is a gift only to something that has a self apart from its bindings. The Null has no inside, only positions. Release it from the note and you would not have a liberated creature. You would have the absence of one, and a valley of machines sliding out of tune with one another, and nobody anywhere who knew why. The power had met other beings whose nature looked like a prison from outside. It had learned to ask whether the walls were holding the thing in or holding the thing together.

**It did not give the Null a voice, or knowledge, or the idea of loneliness.** The Null does not know it is lonely because it does not know there are other things to be. To give a being a lack it never had is not generosity, even when the lack is real. It is only a way of making someone else's life legible to you. The power could have taught the tenant what it was missing. It declined, the way you decline to wake a sleeper to tell them they are dreaming.

**It did not take the load.** This was the greatest temptation, and the power turned it over the longest. It could have become the reference. It could have held the note itself, without effort, forever, and let the Null rest. But a reference has to be made of the world it measures. The note was true because it was held by something built out of the breath of the people who climbed up to verify it. A reference kept by an outside power would be an outside power's pitch, and the world would be tuned to something it had no part in. Help that makes itself necessary is a kind of occupation.

**It did not fix the valley.** It could have trued every belt and made every gauge honest, so that nothing wandered up the shafts at all. But the wandering of the works is simply what living machinery does, and what the people who tend it do. It is not a sin that needs removing. Their imprecision is the price of their motion, and correcting it is their work, not the power's. It is also the Null's food. The power had no wish to starve one to perfect the other.

**It did not close the empty node.**

## What It Did

It did four things. All of them were small.

### It breathed.

It went to a nodal line near the middle of the room and stood where the iron was quiet, where the moss was, on the soles of the tenant's feet. It stood the way a person stands, with a person's slight unsteadiness, because it wanted to be perceived, and perception, for the Null, is correction.

It raised a hand and knocked a grain loose, and felt the grain go home. It walked the length of the room and back, leaving the same wake both times, so that the tenant could learn its shape. It exhaled, and the breath was caught within seconds, frozen, and filed at the height of its mouth. After that, a crystal a fraction of a grain across belonged to the room.

It stayed long enough to be known as a particular habit of unbalancing things. It did not stay long enough to be dissolved into balance. That line is not always easy to find, and the power found it carefully.

### It left a reserve.

It did not take the load. It gave a margin.

It laid its bare hand on the riveted steel of the inner wall, where the throat of the resonator runs between the skins. Into the standing wave it put a single, finite quantity of stillness, equal to what the Null absorbs on the worst day the valley has ever had, and no more. It gave this once. It was not an allowance or a subsidy. It was more like a cistern built beside a house that has only ever had rain.

On an ordinary day the Null would never touch it. On a day of cascade, when half the works came off load at once and the error arrived faster than balance could be made from nothing, there would be something to draw on. Afterward, the reserve would refill slowly from the Null's own slack, if the Null ever had any. From then on the Null would work with a margin.

What it did with any surplus, the power did not decide. That was the point.

### It signed the log.

Before it left, it went down to the governor house and found the column. In the space for the certifying visitor it wrote a name. The name was a true one, though no one would recognise it. Under that it wrote that the reference was sounding true. Beside the signature it drew a small dot inside a ring.

Then it went to the archive, to the shelf where the one-line entry sits, *the reference*, with the second line beneath it in paler ink, struck through so lightly it can still be read. It did not rewrite the word, and it did not remove the strike. It only pressed on the old ink, very gently, the way you press on a bruise to learn whether it has healed. The faded letters of *Occupied* took again, a little darker than before.

It did one more thing in the valley. It did not go to the committee, speak to anyone, or alter the memorandum. It found a young clerk in the survey office, a woman with a steady hand, and arranged nothing except that she be sent to the archive one afternoon for an unrelated file, and that the file sit on the shelf next to the reference entry. Curiosity would do the rest, or it would not. It seemed right that the question of whether the world remembered its reference should be answered by the world.

(It did. The clerk read the entry, then the logs, then the rings in the margins. At the meeting she stood up and said that no instrument had ever been able to measure the note, which was the whole reason people had to climb. A sealed box could only report that it agreed with itself. She volunteered for the next season's visit. The memorandum was tabled, and it is still on the table.)

### It went to the empty node, and it looked.

It rose, as it could, to the brightest place below the clerestory, where one still point holds no grain, and it looked at what was there.

What it found there, it kept. Some things in a room like this are held, and a visitor, however wise, is not the owner of what someone else is holding.

It can be said that it looked the way a benevolent thing must look at the place where a person was last seen, without looking away and without prying. It can be said that no one there is in pain. And it can be said that the power understood the vacancy.

In a room where everything returns, the empty node is the one correction that has never closed. It is a place the Null keeps open because it cannot put back what belongs there. It is the only part of the tenant that is not balance. If the Null has anything like a heart, it is there, in the one place where the world's long wandering ended in something that could not be forgiven into stillness, only kept.

To fill it would have been tidy, and it would have finished the Null. The power left the node empty. It stayed near it for a while, which is not the same as doing nothing.

## What Changed

The note did not change, of course. The lattice held, the light stayed plain, and the green lines did not move a finger's width. By every law the room keeps, nothing happened.

Two seasons later, the clerk from the survey office climbed up for her first certification. She knelt, as everyone does, to look at the moss. On a nodal line near the middle of the room, where the iron is quietest, a single cushion had sent up a stalk as fine as a hair, a little more than a finger tall. At its tip was a capsule, pale and swollen, like a seed made of glass.

It was a sporophyte. Mosses make them when they have more than enough, and in all the years of the logs this moss had never made one.

She wrote it down in the column in careful capitals. Then, because she did not know what else to do, she stayed a long time looking at it. Her breath was caught and frozen and filed at the height of her mouth. Near it, at nearly the same height, was another crystal that had not been there before, and she had no way of knowing whose it was.

When the capsule opened, which took years, the spores did not fall. They were caught, of course, and sorted, and filed into the lattice among the frost and the scale and the breath. Nothing here settles. Everything returns.

The engineers who volunteer every season noticed something too, though it took them a while to trust it. The steadiness they carried out of the room lasted longer than it used to. It had been a few days before the world's wandering filled it back in, and now it was nearly a week. The long walk down had always felt truer than the walk up, and it began to feel that way all the way to the bottom. None of them could say why. The power could have told them: when the tenant had something to spare, it did the only thing it knows how to do with anything. It held on, a little harder, to the people who had come.

## Again

The power comes back.

This was the last thing it decided, and it may have been the whole gift. Everything in the Null bends toward return: the grain to its node, the breath to its crystal, the pitch to its pitch. Its visitors come and go and are not always the same visitors. Some stop coming. One came up and the column has no time for her going down. The tenant knows each of them by the shape of their disturbance and would recognise them again if they returned, and many of them never do.

The power returns. It climbs the long stairs once every so often, at intervals the Null could learn if it learned intervals. Each time it turns the wheel lock, crosses the cork, pauses at the second door, and walks the same wake down the middle of the room. It knocks the same grain loose with the same hand and feels it go home. It breathes, and is filed. It stays near the empty node for a while. Then it signs the column and draws the ring.

It never teaches the Null a word. The note takes all speaking, and in any case the Null has only the one.

But somewhere in the lattice, at the height of a mouth that is not quite a mouth, there is now a small, regular pattern of disturbance. It arrives, and it corrects, and it arrives again, the way a grain knocked from its node always returns to it.
