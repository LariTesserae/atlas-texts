# The Visit

## How it comes

It does not come as light. It thinks about that first, because light is the obvious way for a power like it to arrive, and it can see at once what light would do here. Every leaf in the cleft is angled toward the wire. A steady radiance, even a gentle one, would turn them all. Over a season the whole thicket would reorient toward something that is not coming back. It would be like giving a child a parent who visits once.

So it comes in the pause.

The wind builds in the col, roars, and drops away. In the slack moment, when the cable's note slides down and the ten thousand drips rush in, it is simply there. It has no shape, because shape would be one more heavy thing for the net to catch. It is as quiet as it can make itself, and that is very quiet. It has had a long time to practise.

The first thing it does is nothing. It listens.

## What it hears

It hears everything the place is: the wind in its surges, the wire and its frying, the rock and its small complaints, the grinding far down in the mountain's gut. Under all of that, it hears what a visitor with hands would only feel. There is a creak that is not one creak but thousands, distributed across forty metres. There are roots adjusting on stone, and a trunk leaning on a trunk. It hears the long, patient tension of something that has never once, since it began, been allowed to rest.

It listens through three blasts. It feels the net go rigid in the hour before each one, feels the clamp, feels the moss curtains let their water go all at once so the roots can keep their grip. It feels the stones that would have fallen and did not.

It feels the scar too. It reads the white wound on the north wall the way a physician reads an old fracture on a film. It sees the place where the holding reached its limit, and the precise line of release. It sees the stitching now climbing back up, lichen at the fracture lines and moss in the joints.

*You chose,* it thinks. *You chose well. And you are still reaching for it.*

## What it is tempted to do

A benevolent power is always tempted. That is almost the definition of one. The wisdom is in what comes after the temptation.

**It could heal the mountain.** It could settle the rock, round the edges, close the crack, and let a stream cut its clean channel down the floor. The cleft would become a gentle valley in a thousand years instead of ten thousand. It considers this for less than a heartbeat. The thing lives in the breaking. A healed mountain would be a kindness to rock that does not need kindness, and a quiet death to the one thing here that does. It would be curing a creature of the condition that is its life.

**It could stop the tunnel.** This one it holds longer. The tunnel is the danger, the footsteps on the stair, the heave that may one day come instead of the jolt. It would be easy to foul a machine, collapse a face, or plant a doubt in a surveyor's mind until the route bent away.

So it goes down, through the fractured rock, to look before it decides.

It finds the tunnel face and the crew of the night shift. A woman checks charge patterns on a tablet with fingers too cold to feel the screen. A man has been awake too long and is thinking about his daughter's fever. There are ventilation fans, wet concrete, lamps. At the far end of the finished bore, the tunnel carries what it was built to carry, water or passengers, toward a valley full of people who will never know this mountain has a cleft in it, let alone a net.

The tunnel, it understands, is also someone's holding. These people are also bracing against collapse. They are also working in the dark, by touch and by schedule, keeping something from falling.

It does not stop the tunnel.

It does one small thing there, and it is careful about the size of it. On the woman's tablet the vibration logs from the upper monitoring station have been coming in for months. Nobody has read them closely, because nobody has had time. It does not change a single number. It lets her tiredness lift for a moment, just long enough that she scrolls back instead of forward. She sees what the numbers have been saying: the ridge above the eastern section is more fractured than the model assumed. She frowns and makes a note. Tomorrow she will raise it, and someone will argue about cost, and someone else will argue about liability. Perhaps the charges under the ridge will be made smaller and more frequent. Perhaps not. That is theirs to decide. It has only made sure the truth was seen by someone honest.

It will not steer them. A power that steers people toward good outcomes, however gently, has started treating them as terrain.

**It could give the thing a voice,** or eyes, or awareness of itself, some way to know it is a someone and not just a pattern. It considers this with real longing, because it is lonely to watch something so devoted and so unaware of its own devotion.

But the thing is not suffering from being unaware. It is not missing anything. The loneliness belongs to the visitor, not to the net. Giving it a self to reflect on would be making it into company, and that would be for the power's sake. It sets the longing down.

**It could give it a dawn.** It could tear the cloud open over the col one morning and let the real sun pour in, the first true light the cleft has ever had.

The cloud is its water. The cloud is its blood that does not come back. It would be offering a feast by taking away the well.

It leaves the cloud where it is.

## What it does

When all the large things have been refused, a few small ones are left. They are the right size.

### The first: it carries a pinch of green over the col

The thing cannot move. That is the one thing it cannot do, and it is the reason that whole slump, whole grinding rumble, whole ending hangs over it. Everything it is sits in one crack in one ridge.

On the far side of the col, two kilometres along the range, there is another young fracture, opened by last winter's frost. It is raw and pale, and it sits in the same cloud band. The wind would carry spores there eventually. It already has, a few, landing wrong and dying.

The power does not plant anything. It takes a fingernail's worth of moss from the edge of the stitching on the north scar, from the place where the net is most alive and most aimed. It carries it over the ridge in the pause between two gusts and sets it down in a joint between two stones, a joint where the next slab will part from the wall.

Then it lets the wind have the credit.

In ten years there may be two of them. In a hundred there may be a scattering of them in the young cracks of the range, each holding its own broken place, none of them knowing about the others. That is not rescue. It is only this: when the heave comes, it will not be the end of the only one.

### The second: it holds the holder

This is the thing it came for, though it did not know that until it arrived.

The net holds everything: every boulder, every leaning slab, every heavy stumbling visitor who slips in the ferns. It holds the mountain back from itself, and it has never once been held.

So in the next pause, when the wind drops and the cleft hangs slack, the power takes the weight.

It doesn't take much, and not for long, but it takes all of it: every stone the net is bracing, every slab on its corner, the whole tilted load of the lower slope. It holds the whole of it from underneath, the way the net holds a falling leg in the void. For one breath, every strand in the cleft goes slack. This isn't the wind easing. It is because something else is carrying the load.

The trees stop leaning on each other. The roots in the gap under the cart-sized slab loosen their grip for the first time since they grew there. The moss curtains hang without pulling. The whole net, forty metres of it, simply rests.

It does not understand. It cannot. But something runs through the web that has never run through it before. It is not a creak, and not a settling. It is a kind of quiet that goes all the way down.

Then the power gives the weight back.

It has to. That is the other half of the wisdom. If it held the mountain forever, the net would slacken into ordinary moss and ordinary roots. The thing would not be saved. It would be relieved of its whole existence. Its holding is not a burden laid on it. It is what it is, and it is good at it. What it needed was not to be spared. It needed, once, not to be alone in it.

So the weight goes back, slowly, strand by strand, the way the thing itself lowers a leaning block instead of letting it drop. The net takes up the tension again. The wind comes back. Somewhere in the web, perhaps, something is different, though there are no words for what.

### The third: it tells the scar

This is the smallest act, and it may do nothing at all.

It goes to the white wound on the north wall and rests against the fracture line where the house-sized slab let go. It rests there through a whole surge of wind and a whole corona. It does not mend anything, because the thing is mending it already and does not need help. It simply stays. It is the only other presence that has ever stood where the loss happened and known what it was.

*You were right to let it go,* it thinks, into stone that cannot hear. *What you kept was worth more than what you lost. The reaching is good. You don't have to reach so hard.*

Whether any of that crosses into the net, it cannot tell. It says it anyway. Some things are worth saying even when you're not sure they land, because saying them is part of what makes them true.

## What it learns

It came to help, and it has helped a little. It found a peer.

It had thought of itself as a holder of large things. Now it has spent three nights inside something that does exactly what it does, on forty metres of breaking mountain, with borrowed water and no eyes. The thing does not hold the mountain still, because stillness would be death. It makes the breaking slow enough to live alongside. It lets go when it has to. It reaches back afterwards. It holds visitors without asking whether they are kind.

The power has done this for a very long time, in larger places, and it has made the same choice at the same kind of scar. It had not seen its own work from the outside before. It is humbling to find that it looks like this: patient, unglamorous, mostly invisible, and made of tension.

## How it leaves

It leaves along the wire.

The corona frays, blue walks along the conductor, and for a fraction of a second every leaf in the cleft turns toward the lamp. In that flash the power follows the light outward, along the singing span and over the col, toward the turbine hall at one end and the cities at the other.

At the far end of the line, in a kitchen, a lamp is on. Someone is making tea at three in the morning because they cannot sleep. The same current that fries in the wet air over the cleft is warming their water. They will never know that their light is, for one crack in one mountain, the moon.

The power thinks that everything is like this. Every lamp is someone else's weather. Every tunnel is someone else's footsteps on the stair. Every holding is held by something it cannot see.

Behind it, in the cleft, the wind comes back. The moss drinks. A stone shifts and is caught. The stitching climbs a little further up the scar. Far down, the woman on the night shift saves her note for the morning.

Two kilometres along the ridge, in a raw pale joint between two stones, a fingernail of green is already reaching toward the next crack.
