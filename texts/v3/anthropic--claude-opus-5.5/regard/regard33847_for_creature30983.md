# The Attention

It does not arrive all at once. Nothing on the Kiln Steppe arrives all at once, and the power, being wise, understands this before it understands anything else.

It could come the way it comes to other places: sudden, total, a pressure in the sky. It feels, very faintly, what that would do here. The tender-birds would scatter from the pillars. Across a thousand miles of dark stone the herd-pulses would stutter and lose their rhythm, like a crowd going quiet when someone important walks into the room. Every Lee on the plateau, with ears that can hear a stranger's heartbeat at a day's walk, would lift her head at once.

So it comes by the tilt. It lets its attention climb the way the ground climbs, so gradually that no single moment feels like arrival. The air thins around it and the light changes, and by the time it is fully here it has been here for weeks, and nothing has flinched.

This is the first thing it does, and in some ways the most important: it arrives slowly enough to be mistaken for weather.

---

## Looking

It looks for a long time.

It watches a herd rest, and sees the forest of breath go up and become sky. It watches the pulses travel through the herd, and it can read them, or nearly. They are simpler than the Lee fear and stranger than they hope. They say *cold there*, and *water gone*, and *one of us is going out*. Beneath all of that runs a slow steady phrase it does not have a word for. The nearest it can come is *still here, still here, still here*.

It watches a Lee child kneel on a new trail and lick the bright powder from her fingers. It watches a fisher reach into a tarn at the wrong hour and be thrown back, and watches her lie on the rime with her arm dead while her brother holds his palm to her throat, cool, then cooler, then warming slowly.

It watches a family stay with a dying stack for nine days while the herd walks away from them. On the ninth day the flank is cold, and they stand and walk a sentence into the stone and go. It follows them at a distance, invisible, and watches them catch up on the fourth night after, half-starved, the youngest carried. It feels something it has not felt in a long while, and it takes some time to name it. It is respect of the kind you feel for something that does not need your help.

It is benevolent, so it wants to help. It is wise, so it waits until it knows what help would mean here.

---

## What It Could Do

The power could do almost anything. That is the problem with being what it is, and it has learned to treat its own capacity with suspicion.

**It could give them fire.** One gesture, and the Lee would have a small, portable, inexhaustible flame to carry in a bone vessel. No one would freeze behind a herd again. The stragglers would live.

It turns this over for a long time, because the stragglers matter. Then it follows the gift forward. A people with their own fire do not need the side of the animal. Home stops being a place that moves. They could linger. They could stay by a tarn, then build by a tarn, and the first cairn on the Kiln Steppe would go up within a generation. Palm-to-palm speech would fade, because it exists to share a warmth that is scarce and borrowed. *My hand is warmer, I can share* means nothing to someone holding a flame. The long rule of grief, *you stay as long as it is warm*, would lose its edge. It is precise because the heat is not theirs to prolong.

The fire would save lives. It would also end the Lee, slowly and kindly, and leave behind a small, comfortable people who happened to live on a cold plateau. The power does not give them fire.

**It could thicken the air, soften the cold, and brighten the grey.** The lid of cloud is made of the herds' breath. Brighten the sky and the engine that moves the migration falters, the loop comes apart, and two hundred years of trail stops meaning anything. Even the Lee themselves, with their barrel chests and their large pale eyes, would be half blind in any kinder light. Everything here fits everything else. You cannot improve one part without breaking the fit. The power does not change the sky.

**It could tell them what the writing says.** It can see the whole honeycomb in one glance, every polygon from edge to edge, the way no one standing on it ever could. It can read it. Or rather, it can see that the plateau holds a pattern, and it could decide the pattern was a text and give that text a meaning, and the Lee would believe it, because it would be the one saying so.

That is exactly why it must not. The Lee have lived for ages inside an open question, *when the beasts have read the whole thing, something will happen*. They have built no doctrine around it, only a practice: walk your name, let the rime take it. An answer from the sky would be the most permanent mark anyone had ever left on the Kiln Steppe, heavier than any cairn. The power keeps what it sees to itself. It is not quite sure the plateau is writing anyway. It is not quite sure it isn't. It finds, a little to its surprise, that it would rather not know. It would rather keep the question company than close it.

**It could heal the fisher's arm, and the next one's, and the next.** Here it hesitates longest, because pain is pain and it is not a power that finds suffering beautiful. But it sees that the same principle holds at small scale. A healing no one understands becomes a story. A story becomes a place people return to, a practice, a shrine, a thing to build around. The Lee would begin, reasonably, to look up. On a plateau where every creature looks down at the stone or across at the herds, a people that looked up would be a new kind of people.

So it arrives at a rule for itself. It writes the rule nowhere, because this is not a place for writing things down. *I may act only where I can remain unknown. I may act only in ways that leave no mark.*

The rule will cost lives. The power knows that, and does not pretend otherwise.

---

## It Is Warm

Here is the thing it did not expect.

It had thought of itself as a watcher, and on the Kiln Steppe there are no watchers. Everything that is present gives off something. It discovers, standing unseen at the edge of a resting herd, that its attention has a temperature. Wherever it rests its regard, the rime softens a little. Three times it has lingered too long over one tarn, and the eels have lit up as if a muzzle had lowered to drink.

And the old ones have noticed. It sees this with something like alarm. There is an elder, a woman of perhaps fifty-eight with bronze-tipped teeth, who has caught. For three nights she has woken in the iron-dark, turned her face to where the power stands, and laid her palm flat against the stone as if feeling for a draft.

It could withdraw. It considers withdrawing. Instead, because it is here to be good and not merely to be careful, and because care that never risks contact is its own kind of coldness, it decides to ask.

---

## The Asking

It does not appear. It has no shape it wishes to lend this place. On the fourth night, when the elder wakes and turns and lays her palm against the stone, the power comes as close as it can without being anything, and lets the stone under her hand grow warm.

Not hot. Warm the way a flank is warm.

She is very still for a long time. Then, without fear, she does what the Lee do when they meet. She moves her palm, slowly, off the stone and into the air, and holds it there, flat, at the height of a throat.

The power understands that it is being greeted, and that it must answer in the only language available.

It thinks about what to say. It could say *I am vast*. It could say *I can help you*. It could say *I love this place*, which is true. But what it has learned here is that saying too much is loud, and loud is obscene. So it meets her palm with a warmth exactly equal to hers, not warmer and not cooler. It means *I am not here to give you anything you have to carry.*

Her hand stays still. Then it cools, very slightly, which in the Lee's speech means *I am tired, I am behind*. The power understands this is not about herself. She is telling it about the people: the stragglers, the fishers, the old ones who fall behind. She is not asking for anything. She is only saying, *this is how it is with us*, the way you tell a guest where the cold spots in the house are.

The power lets its warmth dip in answer: *I know. I have seen.*

Then her hand begins to warm slowly while it is held, the phrase lowlanders can never get translated.

The power holds still and lets itself be remembered.

Before she lies back down among the children she does one more thing. She lifts her palm away and presses it once against the flank of the sleeping stack beside her, the gesture the Lee make when an outsider asks who they are. The power takes it as an answer to a question it had not quite asked. *We are theirs. We are this. Whatever you are, you are not what we are made of, and that is all right.*

Then she sleeps. In the morning she says nothing to anyone. She is Lee, and there are no words for it anyway.

---

## What It Does

After that, it knows what to do. It is less than it expected, and it is enough.

**It becomes a hearth, sometimes.** Not for everyone. It cannot be for everyone without becoming the fire it refused to give. But on certain nights, when someone has fallen behind after mourning, or is walking alone because the grief rule held her too long, and the cold is coming and there is no flank for a day in any direction, the stone around her will be a little warmer than stone should be. It will not be warm enough to notice, only warm enough to live. She will wake in the morning with rime on her sleeve and walk on, and she will reach the herd, and she will not know why she did not die.

It does not choose these nights by merit. It found it could not bear to. It chooses them by whether it can do it without being seen, and that is arbitrary, and it knows it is arbitrary, and it carries that.

Some nights it cannot help without being known. Then it does the other thing it learned here. It stays with them as long as they are warm. It lies beside the ones it let go, unseen, through the whole slow cooling, and it does not leave until the body is cold under its attention. It keeps their rule. It does not know if this does the dead any good. It thinks perhaps that is not what the rule is for.

**It keeps the eels from killing, but not from biting.** This is the only thing it changes in the land itself. It makes a small adjustment in the tarns, a slight lowering of the peak of the discharge. A fisher who reaches in at the wrong hour will still be thrown back, and her arm will still be dead for a week, and the Lee will still teach their children the hours. But she will get up. The power weighed this for a long time, because it is a mark. It decided that a mark no one can find, one that changes nothing the Lee build their lives around, only how many of them come home from the tarns, is the kind of mark the plateau makes on itself all the time. The ridges shift the meltwater. The tarns move. The route drifts a few feet a century. This is one more drift.

**It goes to the edge and waits there.** It saw the real danger early, and it was not the cold. It was the tilt. Somewhere below, in the lowlands, there are people who have burned their forests and dug out their hills, and the slag trails of the Kiln Steppe are the richest refined ore in the world, laid out on the surface in lines a league long, free for the carting. The hooves of the kiln-beasts are steel. Their bones are girders. Sooner or later someone with a ledger will hear a traveler's story, and a strange thing will come up the long slope: wheels.

The power does not close the road. Walling a people in is its own kind of harm, and the Lee, who build nothing, would never build a wall. It does not reach into the minds of lowlanders to turn them away. That is not benevolence, only a quieter kind of force.

What it does is simpler, and it hopes it is enough. It resolves that when the carts come, it will be there. It will speak to whoever leads them, plainly, in a language they have, the way one would want to be spoken to. It will tell them what the trails are. It will tell them what the dead are, and what the side of the animal is. It will ask them to go home. It will not decide for them. It will not decide for the Lee either. But it will make certain that no one comes up the tilt without knowing what they are walking into, and it thinks, having met the lowlanders too, that knowing is most of what stops people.

Until then it rests at the edge where the air begins to thin, and is part of the reason the climb feels like nothing.

---

## A Sentence

Before it draws its attention back, not all of it but most, it does the one Lee thing it has wanted to do since it arrived.

It walks a sentence.

It has no feet, but it has attention, and attention on the Kiln Steppe has weight. Just before dawn, when the moths are settling and the ground is furring over with their soft grey bodies, it moves across the honeycomb from polygon to polygon, along this seam, across that one. It walks the phrase it learned from the elder's hand. It thinks it is the phrase the stacks are saying under all their weather and news. *Still here*, or *I remember you*. It suspects these are the same phrase.

Each place it touches is warm for a moment, and the moths there lift a little and resettle.

By midday the rime has taken it, and nothing is written in the stone.

---

## What It Takes Away

A wise power knows that the truest encounters change the one who has the power, and it expected to leave the Kiln Steppe unchanged. It did not.

It came believing, as powers do, that its task was to give. It leaves carrying something the Lee gave it without knowing they had. It has taken a way of grieving that lasts exactly as long as the heat does, no less and no longer. It has taken the knowledge that a home can be a thing you keep up with instead of a thing you keep. It has taken a whole people's deliberate refusal to leave a mark, which it had always thought of as a lack and now understands as a kind of mastery. And it has taken the fact that the highest thing it could be here was not a god, a healer, or a teacher. It was a hearth, for one night, unrecognized, gone by morning.

Nothing here needs it, and the power finds that it is glad of this. A place that needed it would be a place it had already harmed.

Somewhere on the plateau, years later, an old woman with bronze-tipped teeth lies dying at the center of her family, where the children sleep. She takes longer to cool than she should. On the second night, in the iron-dark, her grandson swears he felt the stone beneath her growing warm, just slightly, as if someone else were lying on the other side of her, staying as long as she was warm.

He does not tell anyone. There are no words for it, and the Lee do not need any.

In the morning they walk a sentence into the stone and go after the herd.

*Keep moving.*
