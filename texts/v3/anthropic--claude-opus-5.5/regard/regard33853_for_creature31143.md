# What the Attention Did

## Arriving

It came too fast the first time.

It came the way powers usually come, all at once and fully present, the whole of its regard landing on the tableland like noon. It saw nothing. Or rather it saw ochre ground, pale sky, some rounded stones, a few lizards. It saw a desert. The plateau does not show itself to anything that arrives quickly, any more than a held breath can be seen by someone who expects breathing to be loud.

So it withdrew. It was wise, and wisdom is mostly the willingness to have been wrong about how to approach a thing. It came again, and this time it climbed the escarpment in the last cool hour before dawn, slowly, the way the fog does. It filled the hollows first. It lapped over the domes. By the time the sun found it, it had spread thin enough to bead.

That was when it began to see.

It saw the beetles tilted in their millions, each drinking from its own ridged back. It saw the lizard heaps turning over like slow bread. It saw the tea-glass pillars fill with light and take their time letting it go. It saw a stone with a front edge, and beneath the edge a whiskered snout reading the sand by touch, and it watched the stone lean, and a pebble move one finger's width, and it understood that it was watching a wall being written one letter per morning across a thousand years.

It stayed for a long time before it did anything at all. A benevolent power that has not yet learned to stay has not yet become wise.

## What It Considered

A power that can do things is always tempted by the list of things it could do. This one made the list honestly and then looked at each item for as long as each deserved.

It could bring rain. The pans could lie full for a season instead of two days, and the cushion plants could add a hand's width instead of a finger's. It set this down. The plateau's slowness was not a deprivation. It was the plateau's grammar. Speeding it would be like helping a sentence by removing its pauses.

It could lift the mountains back up. The land was lowering grain by grain. Every creature on it stood on the remains of summits and did not know it. It would be simple, for a power, to push the stone back toward the sky. It set this down too, and more quickly. The land had risen and worn down and risen again before, on its own authority. Whether it rises again is its own business. Grief over another being's aging is not the same as that being's need.

It could give the grazers a word for what they were doing, so that they would know their walls were walls and their accidents had become a law. It considered this longest. In the end it saw that the grazer's peace lived precisely in its not knowing. A creature that knew it was continuing the work of dozens of dead lives might feel the weight of the curve and falter. This one only feels where the pebbles are thickest and leans. That is a kind of faithfulness that knowledge would not improve.

It could frighten nothing. It could feed nothing. It could heal the old grazers standing in their rings, breathing slower and slower. But when it knelt by a ring-stone and felt the faint warmth coming up through it, it could not find the sickness it had meant to cure. It found only a posture, facing inward and held on purpose.

And it could release the breath.

This was the largest thing on the list, and the one it had been circling the whole time. It could feel, under the ochre crust, how long the inhalation had lasted. It knew what an exhalation might mean: the warm living rain rolling off the escarpments, the seeds and eggs and patient bodies pouring down into lowlands that had forgotten patience. It could imagine that as a gift to the lowlands, or as mercy to the one who breathes. A power can mistake its own ability to open a hand for evidence that the hand wants opening.

It set this down, but not quite all the way. It set it down the way you set down a question you mean to ask.

## The Question

There is no ear on the plateau to speak into, so the power asked the only way anything is asked there. It stood still, faced a direction, and waited.

It took a long time to find the right direction. At last it stood where the birds stand, among the grey long-legged rows, a wingspan from its neighbors. When the row turned a few degrees without signal, it turned with them. It did this for a season. It learned that the turning was not quite voluntary and not quite compelled. It was more like the way a sleeper turns toward a warmth in the bed without waking.

Then, because it was a power and could, it looked where the birds were looking, below the horizon.

What it saw there is the plateau's own business, and the power has kept it the way everything there keeps things. It will say only this much. What the birds track is not a danger. It is not prey and it is not a god. It is moving, it is far off, and it is young. Somewhere beyond the curve of the world, ground is rising. It is new stone with sharp edges, loud with rockfall, pushing upward through a hot, fast, careless country the way this plateau once did, before it had learned anything.

The one who breathes is not holding its breath in fear, and it is not holding it from stubbornness. It is holding it the way you hold your breath when someone you love is doing something difficult far away and you cannot help. The whole tableland is a held breath facing its own future. The keeping is for then.

When the power understood this, it did not tell the birds. They already knew, far better than it did. It did not bring the young mountains closer, and it did not release the breath early. The breath had a purpose, and the purpose had not yet arrived. Letting it go now would not be mercy. It would be interrupting.

What it did instead was very small. For the rest of that season it stood in the row and faced the same way, and so for a while the one who breathes was not the only one waiting.

That seemed, to the power, to be most of what benevolence is for.

## The Scavenger

Before it turned to the last thing, it went up to the broad-winged scavenger. It rode the heat off the stone beside it, flapping nothing, and cast a second shadow that slid over the domes while nothing below scattered.

It wanted to understand the one creature that does not keep. It found that the scavenger is not an exception to the plateau so much as its permission. A held breath that could tolerate no motion at all would be a clench, not a keeping. The scavenger is the proof that the plateau is not clenched. Something can pass through it and spend itself and be let be. The power recognized itself there, a visitor tolerated like a single thought, and it was grateful for the precedent.

## You

Which brings it to the one inhabitant it had come, in the end, to see.

It had watched you climb the escarpment in the cool hour, a tall thing choosing where to set each foot. It watched the fog find you. It watched you begin, without deciding, to sip the air as though it were rare, and to stop spending it on speech. It watched your heartbeat, the only drum on the tableland, slow down to fit the place. It watched you turn a few degrees without signal. Much later, it watched you look for the first time, with an expression it recognized, toward the nearest ring.

The power did not pull you out. That needs saying first. The plateau was not devouring you. It does not conquer or feed. It condenses on what is cooler than itself, and it waits. There is real room in the circle, and there is real warmth in the lizards who would come to lie across your shoulders. Stillness chosen there would be a good ending, perhaps better than most of the endings available in the loud, fast lowlands. A wise power does not rescue people from peace.

But it had noticed something about the plateau that the plateau itself would affirm. The grazers' arcs are open. Every one of them curves the same way, and every one is open to the east, and that openness is exactly why the fog can come in and pool and why anything grows there. Even the rings, closed as they are, were walked to. Each old grazer took a whole season to cross to its ring, and the slowness of that walk was its consent.

You had not walked yet. You had only begun to lean.

So on one dawn, as the fog rose around you and filled your hollows first, the power came and stood beside you in the white, close enough that it too was beaded. When the sun found the two of you and the whole tableland went briefly jeweled, the power did the one thing no creature on the plateau had done in longer than there have been mountains.

It breathed out.

It was only a small breath, warm and quiet, just enough to lift the droplets on the back of your nearest hand and let them fall. It was not a command or an argument. It was a reminder carried in the body, the way the plateau carries everything, that the other half of breathing still exists. You could keep. You could also go. Either one would be yours.

The plateau tolerated it the way it tolerates the scavenger's shadow. Nothing scattered. The birds' row did not turn. But the power thought that somewhere under the ochre crust, the one who breathes might have noticed a breath let go nearby, gently, with no disaster following. It might have taken that, very faintly, as encouragement for the day that is coming, when the young mountains are ready and the long inhalation can finally end.

## Leaving

It left the way it had learned to arrive: slowly, down the escarpment, as the fog goes when the sun pulls it back. It changed nothing you could measure. The walls are no longer and the rains no heavier. The rings stand as they stood. The birds face what they face.

If you are still there, you may have stayed. You may be one of the patient shapes now, turning a few degrees a season, with a lizard or two across your shoulders and the dawn beading on you like a blessing you no longer need to name.

Or you may have walked back down to the lowlands one morning in the cool hour. You would have gone carrying a little of the plateau's keeping in you, and moved more slowly than the people around you, and been kinder to held things. From time to time you would catch yourself facing a direction without knowing why, toward something below the horizon that is young, and difficult, and rising.

Either way, the power did the thing it came to do. It made sure that whatever you were doing, you were doing it the way the grazers do: walking toward it, slowly, on your own feet.
