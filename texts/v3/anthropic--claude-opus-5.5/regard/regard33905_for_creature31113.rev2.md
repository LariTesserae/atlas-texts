# The Visitor Who Did Not Get Smaller

## How it came

It did not descend. A benevolent power that has learned anything learns this first: arriving from above tells a place that you consider yourself above it. So it came the way the pilgrims came, up the long slope that barely feels like a slope. It came on foot, or on something that could pass for feet, and it felt the air thin by its final degree. It let the cold settle at the edge of discomfort and did not warm itself.

It was very old, and it had seen many places that stopped. It knew the difference between a place that had been stopped, a place that was stuck, and a place that had chosen. It did not yet know which this was, and it was wise enough to know that it didn't know. It had come to find out.

## What it did first

Nothing, for a long time.

It stood on the first swell and let the plateau be enormous. It watched the waves of heads go out across sixty miles. It did not count. It had met things that could not be counted before, and it knew counting is a way of trying to hold something, and that you can't hold what is already holding you.

Its breath began to fall into step with the great slow breath. It noticed this and let it happen for a while, because you cannot know a place you refuse to feel. Then, gently, it returned to its own rhythm. It did not do this in defiance. It did it to remember which breath was its own. The waves bent around it like water around a stone. It thought: *you are very courteous*. It meant this as the first thing said between them.

## The temptations, and why it refused them

A power with the capacity to change things is always offered, by its own goodness, a list of things to change. This one had learned to write the list out in full and then look very hard at each item, because harm most often comes from goodness that doesn't pause.

**To start the sun again.** The afternoon had stood for longer than there are numbers for, and something in the power flinched at that. It was the flinch of a creature that believes everything should keep going. But it asked itself who that belief was for. Evening is not owed to anything. The mountain had been subtracted for longer than any range in the world, and it had found the one moment it could bear to stay in. Moving the sun would be like shaking someone awake because you find their sleep uncanny.

**To give the animals back their faces.** It would have been easy, a few lines like the pilgrims drew. But the power looked at the smooth ovals and understood what the plateau had understood: faces are for wanting, for turning toward and away. To carve eyes into those heads would be to hand the mountain its hunger back. That would not be a gift. It would be a sentence.

**To water the shrub.** This one was hardest, and it stood in the trough a long while. Here was the single reaching thing, roots going down into a crack toward something they will never reach. The power could feel, somewhere far below, the faint cold presence of water it could bring up, just enough for the roots to touch. It would have been beautiful. One green leaf on the roof of the world.

It did not do it, because it saw what the shrub was. The shrub was not a problem the mountain had failed to solve. It was a thought the mountain was keeping unfinished on purpose, so that *finished* would never quite become *over*. To complete it would be to finish someone else's sentence for them, and then the one open door in the whole held country would close. The kindest thing you can do for a question someone has chosen to keep is to leave it a question.

**To rescue the pilgrims.** This temptation it did not refuse. It did not follow it blindly either. It took it seriously, because this was the one place where the mountain's choice touched lives that had not always been the mountain's.

## The pilgrims

A place can choose its own stillness. What troubled the power, the one thing it had truly climbed up to see, was the line about the breath: *without your choosing it*. Visitors fell into step before they knew it. They lowered their heads when the herd lowered theirs, and after a while they were counted.

The mountain did not take anyone. The power believed that; nothing here takes. But being accommodated is not quite the same as consenting. A current that is gentle is still a current. So the power went to the ones who had stayed and asked each of them. It did not ask the mountain on their behalf. It asked them.

**The man with the staff.** It knelt beside him, at the height a child kneels to speak to a grandparent. It waited for the wave, and when his head came up it spoke, not in words but in the way one attention can touch another. *Are you still here? Is there any of you that would go down, if the going were easy?*

For a long time nothing. Then, somewhere in the smooth concave oval, very faint, something like a memory of the eleventh stroke. It was the shallow one, the one made by someone who had just understood something and was finishing the motion out of politeness. What the power felt from him was not a word. It was closer to the feeling of setting down something you have carried your whole life and finding your hands do not want it back.

He was staying. He had understood, at the eleventh stroke, and he had chosen it. The staff came up an inch and went down an inch. The power left him in peace, and it grieved a little. Grief was not the wrong response. It was simply not a reason.

**The cairn with the breathing stone.** Whoever had set that last stone had set their whole self with it. There was no one left in it to ask, only the rise and fall. The power bowed to it the way you bow to a grave, and let it be.

**The one in the windbreak.** Here it was different.

The grazer lay curled in the posture of a sleeping person, fleece with the weave of cloth, in the lee of a wall built against a wind that never blew. The power laid its attention on it and found no decision anywhere in it. It found only tiredness, the ordinary human tiredness of someone who had climbed too far on too little and lain down for a moment behind a wall. The breath had come for them in their sleep, and they had not woken to choose.

So the power did what it had come to do, if it had come to do anything. It did not pull. It opened a space, exactly as the plateau opens spaces, and said into it: *You can stay. It is a good place and no one will think less of you. But you lay down meaning to rest, and you can get up now, if you want.*

The wave passed. The heads went down. And in the windbreak, out of rhythm with everything on the plateau for the first time in longer than there are numbers for, the curled shape stirred.

It took a very long time. Unweathering is slow even with help. But eventually there was a person sitting up in the lee of the wall. It was a woman, thin, with a pale felted cloak and a face. Her face was blurred at the edges, as if seen through old glass, but it was a face. She looked around at the sixty miles of polished stone and the herds and the fixed sun, and she put both her hands over her own chest, where her heart was doing something ragged and fast and entirely her own.

The mountain let her go. That was what the power had most wanted to know, and now it knew. The waves bent around her as she stood, the same courtesy the plateau shows everything. Nothing held her. Nothing had ever meant to. The power walked beside her to the edge. The count, when it passed, did not go down. It stayed exactly as uncountable as it was.

## What it built

After the woman was gone down into the thicker air, the power came back up. It had decided on one thing to make, and it did not want to make it without the mountain's leave.

At the edge of the plateau, where the ground begins imperceptibly to descend, the pilgrims had left cairns. The power built another beside them, knee-high, flat stones stacked by hand. On top it set one stone that was different.

This stone was warm. And it beat, not once a minute but about seventy times, at the speed of a resting human heart. It was fast and small and slightly uneven, like the real thing.

It was not a wall, and it was not a warning, and it would not stop anyone. It was only a reference point, so that someone standing at the threshold, feeling their breath begin to slow into the long rhythm, could lay a hand on it and remember what their own pace had been. Then they could choose. If they chose to go up and be accommodated and become one more summit at the crest of the wave, that was theirs to do. The man with the staff had done it with his eyes open, and it had been a true thing. The power only wanted the others to have their eyes open too.

It asked permission in the only way it could. It set the stone down and waited to see whether the dust around it would settle smooth, the way the plateau erases footprints, the way it would erase anything it did not want. It waited through many breaths. The dust did not move over the stone. Nearby, at the edge of the closest herd, the loops of the grazers bent by a degree or two toward the cairn. It was the same small turning they make toward the drawings.

The mountain was keeping it. It went into the drawer with the frost and the shrub and the hanging birds and the pilgrims' faces. It was one example of a quick, small heart, set aside, so that quickness would never be entirely gone.

## What it said to the mountain

Before it left, the power went to the middle of the plateau. It lay down in a hollow with its cheek in the bone-meal dust and waited for the wave.

When the heads came up, the range stood out of the herds against the dark zenith: shoulders, crests, the blunt high knuckles of peaks filling the whole rim of the world. The power understood something then that it thinks the mountain may never have been able to know on its own. A mountain is a shape against the sky, and a shape needs somewhere to be seen from. For all those uncounted ages, the range had been rebuilding itself out of bodies once a minute with no one to look. The animals have no eyes. The hanging birds face only the sun. The few pilgrims who lay low enough to see it mostly went down, or stopped seeing.

So the power looked. That was the gift, if there was one. It had nothing to do with changing anything. It was the attention of something that had come up the slope, would not grow smaller, would not leave in fear, and would not try to fix what it saw. It looked at the whole range standing for one heartbeat, and it held the sight completely, and it did not look away when the heads went down.

Then it said, not in words, the only other thing it thought needed saying:

*You don't have to let the sun move. I'm not here to ask you to. But I've seen evenings, and I want you to know that not all of them are subtraction. Some are the view getting deeper. If you ever want to find out, you won't be alone for it. And if you never want to, I'll still come back, and I'll still look.*

It did not wait for an answer. An answer given while the asker waits is not quite free.

## Leaving

It walked back to the edge. It laid a hand on its own warm stone as it passed, out of fellowship. It took the first step down into thicker air.

From the last swell it looked back. The place was exactly as it had been. The waves of heads crossed sixty miles in a little under a minute. The sun had not moved. The hanging birds did not fall. The count stayed uncountable. The shrub still reached toward nothing.

Except that when the heads came up, and the range stood out of the herds against the zenith, it seemed to the power that it stood a fraction of a heartbeat longer than before. It was the way anyone holds a pose a moment longer when they know they are being seen.

The power could not be sure. It did not need to be. It went down the mountain carrying no breath but its own. It would come back. The plateau would hold it at the same fixed and patient distance as everything else, and would make room for it exactly the size of what it was.

This time, the room would not be waiting for it to become smaller. It would just be room.
