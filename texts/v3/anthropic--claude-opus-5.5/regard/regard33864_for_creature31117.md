# The Attention

## The Sentence That Stops

The account breaks off in the middle of a sentence: *The recurrences are the*

The power noticed that first. It noticed that whoever had been writing had gone quiet, the way people in the basin go quiet, without deciding to. It noticed that the unfinished sentence was the right one to leave unfinished, because it was the one sentence in the whole record that the writer could not have completed from the rim. It was the question underneath all the others.

So the power did not answer it. It went to find out.

## Arriving Slowly

The first thing the power did was nothing, and it did it for a long time.

It had arrived at many places. It knew that the manner of arriving is the first thing you say to anyone, and that most harm done by the well-meaning is done in the first minute, before they have learned what time it is where they have come. A hand laid on a sleeping body wakes it. A light carried into a dark room is, to whoever lives in the dark, not a gift but an event.

The basin's time was strange. Its surface ran fast: columns rising in forty minutes, lattices falling in three days. Its depths ran very slowly: the mills turning once a season, the slopes circling over years, the shape of the bowl held steady over centuries. A visitor keyed to either tempo alone would misread it completely. Someone who watched only the surface would think it was in crisis. Someone who watched only the depths would think it was asleep.

So the power let its attention thin out and spread and slow, until it could hold both at once, the way you can hear a melody and the key it is written in. It did not descend. It did not glow. It came to rest on the rim in the fog as something about the size and warmth of a traveler, because that was a shape the basin already knew how to read, and it stayed there.

On the second night, moss grew up around it, soft and damp, and felted gently over what served it for feet. On the fourth morning, a stoker's trail circled it in the mat and closed up behind itself.

It was being checked for warmth. The power let itself be checked. It stayed warm, and it kept moving, a little, each day, as the rules asked. The basin went on with its work.

This was the beginning of its manners.

## What It Learned

It learned the things the travelers had learned, and then the things they had only suspected, and then things no one had been near enough or patient enough to see.

It pressed itself to the moss and listened, as the travelers had, but for a season instead of a minute. It learned that the slow pump beneath the creaks and gurgles was not one rhythm but many, interlocking, the way a body's pulse and breath and gut all keep separate time and are still one life. It learned to read the charged lichen, not as a wire carries a message but as a nervous system carries a mood. It learned that the black band did not so much command the moss as persuade it. Its currents made certain growth easier and other growth harder, and the moss, being moss, went where it was easy.

It learned that the frost-feathers pointing toward the center were not pointing at anything. There was nothing at the center, no organ, no seat, no throne. They pointed inward because everything in the basin leaned slightly toward everything else, the way the parts of a body lean toward keeping the body.

It learned what the stokers felt through their wide feet: pressure, flow, sluggishness, give. It learned that the pale young stoker once seen being led down a lane had not been taught *where* the blockages were, since the lanes moved and no map would last. It had been taught how blockage *feels*. The basin passes on a sense, not a map.

And in the mills, in the lowest hollows, where the teeth had reached an old stoker one spring, it learned what the recurrences are.

Nothing in the basin lasts. No column, no lattice, no stoker, no stretch of moss is the same matter from one year to the next. Everything is ground down and taken up and built again. A body like that cannot remember the way a creature with bones remembers. It has no fixed place to write anything down.

So it remembers by *doing it again*. The lattice rises on the same ridge because rising there is the memory of rising there. The slopes crawl in circuits because the circuit is where the knowing is kept. The chimneys space themselves the same way in the same hollow because the spacing *is* the hollow's history, re-enacted every week so it will not be lost. The mills turn where they have always turned because that is how the basin knows where its gut is.

The recurrences are the basin's memory. Since it has nothing else that persists, they are also its self.

It is not made of moss. It is made of *returning*.

## The Temptations

Having understood this, the power could see all the things it might do, and it looked at each of them honestly, because a power that refuses to look at its own temptations ends up obeying them without noticing.

**It could make the basin warm.** This was the first temptation and the strongest, because it came from pity. Here was a warm-blooded thing living at three degrees above freezing in air too thin to finish a breath, shivering every few days from rim to rim, burning its own body to stay alive, laboring hardest on the darkest nights. The power could lower the basin, or thicken the air, or turn the weather a few degrees. The labor would ease. The shivering would stop.

It sat with this for a long while. Then it saw the mistake.

The basin is not a warm thing that has been unluckily placed in the cold. It is the *answer* to the cold. Everything it is, the chimneys and the mills and the blanket of moss and the stokers and the patient circuits of the slopes, is the shape of its long, successful argument with that altitude. Remove the cold and you do not relieve the basin. You dissolve the question it has spent its whole existence answering. In a warmer place its circuits would be wrong. Its memory, kept as rhythm, would stop matching the world. The recurrences would fall out of step, and a being made of returning would find there was nothing to return to.

It would be like curing a person of their life.

And the power noticed something else, the thing that pity always has trouble seeing: there was no evidence the basin suffered. A shiver is not a scream. A body that shivers is a body that works. The travelers had called the basin's labor *useless*, and the power understood why they would. None of it was for anyone. But that was only true if you did not count the basin. The work was for the basin. Staying alive was the point, and a thing that keeps itself alive against the cold for ten thousand years is not failing at anything.

**It could give the basin a voice**, so that it could say what it wanted, so that someone could ask. But the basin already had a language. It spoke in pressure and warmth and growth and the timing of collapse, and it spoke it fluently to everything that lived on its skin. To give it human speech would be to make it speak to *the power's* kind, on *the power's* terms. That would be a translation that served the translator. The power resolved instead to learn the basin's language as far as it could, and not to ask the basin to learn its own.

**It could make it last.** Arrest the grinding, fix the columns, spare the old stokers from the mill. But the basin's persistence is made of its impermanence, the way a flame's steadiness is made of its burning. To stop the dying would be to stop the living, which was the same motion seen from the other side.

**It could make it a companion.** The power searched, patiently and widely, for another like it, and found none. It considered, for a moment, whether it could grow one: a second basin in a second high bowl, so this one would not be alone. Then it set the idea down. It did not know that the basin was lonely, or that loneliness was a thing it could feel. A companion made to order for someone who never asked is a gift to the giver's feelings. And if the basin was the only one of its kind, that was part of what it was. The power would not treat a solitary life as a problem to be solved.

**It could make it known.** It could tell the world what the basin was, so that the world would marvel and protect it. The power thought about the travelers, how they went quiet, how they came to say *it* and then *she*, how the basin had slowly and gently changed the few who had come. That change happened because they came slowly, on foot, in small numbers, and learned the rules. Wonder delivered all at once does not make people quiet. It makes them come in crowds.

It put each of these down, one after another, and it was not easy, because each was a real kindness it was choosing not to do.

## The Gap

But in the long season of listening, the power found one thing the basin could not do for itself.

The basin hears everything that touches it. Its whole surface is an ear. Footsteps, weight, cold, a blocked channel, a dropped glove, a warm stranger on the rim: all of it arrives and is felt and answered.

It cannot hear anything that has not touched it yet.

It has no eyes. It cannot see a road being surveyed on the far side of the mountains. It cannot feel a drill-rig being trucked toward the rim, or the slow arithmetic of a company that has learned that the black lichen carries charge and the chimneys carry heat. It cannot sense a change in the weather of the whole world that might, over a century, pull the cold out from under it. A body that listens only through its skin learns about the knife when the knife arrives. And it cannot run. It is rooted in a bowl of its own making, twelve kilometers of patient tissue, and all its wisdom is about what is already here.

Every threat the basin had faced in its long life, it had met and answered. The cold, the thin air, the ice in the channels, the blockages, the collapses. It could handle its own world. What it could not handle was a danger from outside that world, one that moved faster than it could return and had never been part of any circuit it remembered.

That, the power decided, was its to hold.

## What It Did

It did not build a wall. It did not hide the basin, or curse the ridges, or make travelers lose their way. The lodgers would still come: the bird, the beetle, and the rare slow human on foot who would learn to go quiet. The power did not want to take the basin out of the world, only to keep the world from arriving all at once.

What it did was smaller and more lasting than a wall. It kept watch.

It set its attention on the far side of the rim, looking outward, where the basin cannot look. When the survey came, it made sure that the survey found the ground too soft, the road too costly, and the heat too diffuse to be worth what it would cost to take. It did not do this with miracles, only with the ordinary difficulties any wise steward would make sure were properly counted. When people came who wanted to understand, it let them come, slowly. When people came who wanted to take, it let the mountains be as hard to cross as mountains are.

When the world's weather shifted, as the weather of worlds does, it held the cold in the basin steady. It did not make the basin colder, and it did not make it warmer. It held the cold *the same*, so that the circuits would keep matching and the memory kept as rhythm would keep finding its rhythm. It did the least that would preserve the question the basin was built to answer.

In this one way the power became something like a stoker for the basin: not the owner of the furnace, but the one who keeps the draft clear. The difference was that it tended the air *outside* the house, where the house's own hands cannot reach.

It told no one. The basin will never know. That seemed right to the power. It is a good thing to be guarded while you sleep, and it would be a poor gift to wake you to tell you so.

## The One Night

There was one thing more, and it was not strictly necessary, which is perhaps why the power allowed itself to do it.

On the coldest night of the power's first winter there, the fog froze on every tip, and the frost grew in its long directional feathers. The chimneys labored in the dark. Before dawn, the shiver came. Every column swelled at once, every mill's teeth bit down, and the single deep pressure moved through the ground and up through everything standing on it.

The power was lying on the moss, a small warmth on the skin of the world, where it had been lying all night. And when the pressure moved through it, it did the only thing that seemed to be in the basin's own language. It did not speak and it did not glow. It let the pressure pass through it without resisting, as a body in the basin would, and then, very gently, it pressed back. It was one answering pulse, no stronger than a heartbeat, delivered down into the mat where sound goes.

It was not a message. The power did not presume to know what a message would mean to the basin. It was more like what you do when someone asleep beside you shivers in the cold. You do not wake them. You do not explain. You just move a little closer, so that they will feel, somewhere below thinking, that there is warmth nearby and that it is on their side.

Then the chimneys vented together, and the basin went white from rim to rim, silent as a painting.

The power did not look for a reply, because looking for one would have made the gesture a question, and it had not been one. But toward noon, when the fog thinned, the moss had grown up soft around it again, and a stoker's trail circled it in the mat. The trail was not closing this time. It stayed a little longer than the others had, an hour, perhaps two, before the mat drew it in.

It might have meant nothing. The power was content not to know.

## After

It is still there, in the way that such powers are anywhere: at the rim, facing outward, holding the cold steady and the world at the pace of walking. Most of its attention has gone back to its other work, but a thread of it stays, the way a thread of a parent's attention stays with a sleeping house.

The basin goes on. The columns rise and fall. The lattices build on the ridges where they have always built, and collapse where they have always collapsed, and rise again. The slopes crawl their circuits. The mills turn. The old stokers go down to the hollows and lie down, and the young ones are led along the wet lanes and taught how blockage feels. On the coldest nights the whole body shivers, and steam stands up from rim to rim, and nothing is heard.

The travelers who come, a few in a generation, on foot, still go quiet without deciding to. Some of them, lying with an ear to the moss on the last night before they leave, say afterward that they felt two rhythms in the ground, the deep slow pump and something fainter beneath it, like a second pulse keeping time with the first. They cannot say what it was, and they do not try very hard. The basin teaches you not to need to.

The power finished the sentence once, privately, and then left it unwritten in the record, because it belonged to whoever next stays long enough to earn it:

*The recurrences are the basin keeping faith with itself.*

The power's whole gift, in the end, was to make sure that nothing from outside would ever make it break that faith.
