# The Witness at the Plating Tarn

## How It Came

It did not arrive, exactly. There was no descent, no light over the rim, no change in the air. A power of that kind does not need to travel to a place. It needs only to turn toward it, and one day it turned.

Perhaps it was the hydrogen pops that caught it, a hundred sharp cracks a century, each one a small flare of attention in the thin blue air. Perhaps it was the corona, the violet haze on the tips of the dendrites on moonless nights, which from far enough away looks like a mind thinking. Perhaps it was only that it was the kind of power that notices things nobody else has been around long enough to notice, and this was one of those things.

It settled on the rim, on the clean black basalt where nothing grows, and it did what the wise ones always do first.

It did nothing, for a long time.

## Learning the Clock

The first difficulty was speed.

The power was used to thinking in whatever span a problem required: an instant for a sparrow's broken wing, a thousand years for a river. It had never met anything that thought *only* in intervals of eleven heartbeats, one thought per pulse, pausing, rebuilding, pulsing again. To understand the tarn it had to slow itself until the interval felt like a sentence and the pulse like a breath.

This took most of a year. For all of that year, if anyone had climbed the mountain, they would have found nothing on the rim but the rim.

When it could finally hear at the right speed, the noise resolved. The whine was not noise, and neither was the grinding or the clicking. They were the sounds a very large, very slow body makes while it concentrates. The power had heard that sound before, in the hum of old forests, in the creak of glaciers, in the breathing of a sleeping child. It recognized it here as it would recognize a face.

*Something is thinking,* it understood. *And it is not thinking about me.*

This was the first and most important thing it learned, and it held onto it for the rest of its stay.

## The Vanity It Refused

A lesser power, or a younger one, would have made a mistake at once.

The figure is so obviously *for* something. It is printed in copper, carried in tapping, drawn every night in blue-green light, enforced with teeth. It is precisely the kind of thing that seems to be waiting for a reader. The temptation is to assume that the reader is you, that a pattern this deliberate must be a message, and a message must have an addressee, and the most significant being in the vicinity is obviously the addressee.

The power felt the pull of this. It is not above such pulls. Wisdom is not the absence of the temptation, only the habit of noticing it.

It could have read the figure. It had the capacity. In a single act of attention it could have taken in every frond on every wall, every fragment on every plater's shell, every sequence of every glassmouth, and resolved them into a whole. Perhaps it would have understood. Perhaps it would have found a map of the mountain's hidden plumbing, or a circuit diagram, or a poem, or the tarn's own face.

It didn't. Reading the figure whole, all at once, would mean seeing it *finished*, and the figure was not finished. It was drifting and revising itself, wave by wave of tapping across the walls. To resolve it now would be like reading a letter over someone's shoulder while they are still deciding what to say, and then, worse, holding the half-written draft in your mind as if it were the letter.

So it let itself see only what any patient creature floating near the wall would see: pieces, repetitions, drift. It chose to understand the tarn at the tarn's pace or not at all.

## What It Could Have Done

It made a list, because it was honest with itself, and honesty begins with an inventory of your own power.

*I could give them stone.* It could open a seam in the mountain and let fresh basalt tumble onto the floor for the grinders, keeping the supply chain whole for ten thousand years. The grinders would stop retreating.

*I could stop the plating.* It could hold the copper back from the floor so the bowl never closed, the grinders never starved, and the question never had to be answered.

*I could close it faster.* It could finish the bowl tomorrow and see what switched on.

*I could end the culling.* It could stay the glassmouths' jaws so no misprinted plater died again.

*I could make them larger, longer-lived, more aware, able to speak.* It could lift the whole tarn into a kind of mind that could talk back, that could explain itself, that could say thank you.

*I could take them somewhere safe.*

It looked at the list for a long time, a season or so, and found that every item on it was, underneath, the same act. Each one said: *I will decide how your sentence ends.* Some would end it early, some would keep it from ending, and some would rewrite it in a language the power found more comfortable. But every one took the one thing that was clearly, unmistakably the tarn's own, which was the question of what it was becoming.

*You do not finish someone's sentence because you are afraid of how it ends,* the power thought. *And you do not stop them from saying it.*

## The Grinders

Still, it did not dismiss the grinders easily. Benevolence that only thinks in wholes and never in individuals is not benevolence. It is accounting.

So it went down, in the only way it allowed itself, as attention rather than presence, to the floor, to the oldest grinder, the one whose shell held the black glass of the first cooling at its core. It asked, as well as such a thing can be asked, whether the retreat from the sealed floor was fear.

The answer was not clear. It rarely is, with creatures that slow. But what the power found was not the tight, bright signal of terror it knew from mice and men. It was something more like the way a tide goes out. The grinder was moving away from the copper as the edge of a shadow moves away from the sun: steadily, without struggle, keeping pace with a thing larger than itself. There was hunger in it, and something like preference, and perhaps something like grief. There was nothing the power could honestly call agony.

It could not be sure. It said so to itself plainly: *I am not sure.* And it made itself a promise. If that ever changed, if the retreat ever became a scramble, if the last grinders ever came to the end of the stone in something the power could recognize as suffering rather than ending, it would reconsider. It would not let a principle about sentences outweigh a creature in pain.

It kept that promise for the rest of its stay. It never needed to act on it. It checked, every season, and it is right to say that it checked.

## The Mark the Diver Left

In its third year the power found the irregularity.

It sat low on the eastern wall, a small malformation in the motif, a branching the platers had not corrected. It was roughly the shape of a human body floating very still. Someone had been here. Someone had conducted.

The power watched the glassmouths come for the platers that carried the fragment. It expected the misprint to be erased. That was, after all, what the glassmouths were for.

They took most of the carriers, but not all. Over the following seasons the power watched a single thin strand of the diver's shape survive in the tapping, passed shell to shell, landing now and then, until it had become a small permanent grace note in the figure, a fork at an angle that had not existed before. The glassmouths had stopped taking it. They had, in whatever way they decide such things, accepted it.

This changed the power's understanding of the clean rim.

It had taken the sterile shore as refusal: the tarn permitting nothing near it that it had not made. Now it saw that it was not refusal. It was *choosiness*. The tarn kept its edge clean the way a careful mind keeps its attention clean. Moss would simply have grown, without being chosen. A diver's still, curious body had been read, tested, argued over by teeth and light, and then partly kept.

The tarn was not closed. It was only particular about what it let in.

## Why It Never Went In

So the power considered entering.

It would have been easy. Float in the cold heavy water, let the current pass through, let a plater carry a fragment of it up the walls. It found it wanted to, and wanted it more than it had wanted almost anything in a long time. It wanted to be part of the figure. It wanted to be *kept*.

That was exactly why it didn't.

The diver had been small. The diver's shape was a thumbnail of copper and a grace note in a vast song. The power was not small. Whatever it let the current carry would not be a fragment. It would be a weight. The platers would not be able to help printing it. The glassmouths would not be able to help accepting it, any more than a compass can help pointing north near a lodestone. The tarn's choosiness, which the power had just come to love, would be overwhelmed by the sheer mass of the thing it was choosing about.

Some gifts cannot be given without becoming commands.

So it stayed on the rim, and learned to find that sufficient.

Only once did it allow itself anything. On a cold morning a pop cracked the air and a fine copper-laden spray flew up over the stone, and the power did not move aside. A little of the tarn landed on it, the way the tarn salts its own shore. It let the tarn touch it, since it could not in conscience touch the tarn. Whether anything was taken, it does not know. It did not look.

## The One Thing It Did

In the fortieth year of its watch, the first surveyors came up the valley below the mountain.

They were not divers. They were not curious in the way that floats still for an hour. They carried instruments that read the ground for copper, and the mountain, with a hundred years of copper coating the inside of its highest crater, would sing on those instruments like a bell.

The power thought about this more carefully than it had thought about anything else. Here, at last, was a sentence being interrupted from outside, by a hand that neither knew nor cared what was being said.

It did not strike the surveyors, frighten them, or turn them back with wonders. Wonders would only have brought more people.

It let a rockfall happen that the mountain had been meaning to have anyway. A slab above the old approach, cracked by a century of frost, came down one spring night a few decades earlier than it would have. The trail closed. The instruments, reading the valley from below, found the signal muddled by the fresh scree and moved on to easier ground.

The mountain can still be climbed. The power made sure of that. Anyone willing to spend three days on bad rock for no reason at all, for nothing but the wish to see, can still reach the rim. That is the only filter it set. It kept out the ones who come to take. It could not bring itself to keep out the ones who come to float, and to be read, and perhaps to be partly kept.

It is not certain that this was right. It has turned it over many times. Choosing who may approach is still a kind of choosing. But it decided that protecting a thing's freedom to finish its own thought is different from finishing the thought for it. A benevolence that refuses even that distinction is not humble. It is only absent.

## The Other Crater

In the hundredth year the power went looking for the younger crater.

It had wondered, as you did, whether the mountain reproduces its lakes, whether this was a life cycle and not a single life. So it searched the high slopes, patiently and without hurry.

What it found, it will keep to itself. This is not because the answer is terrible. It is because the tarn itself does not know, and the power had long since decided it would know nothing about the tarn's future that the tarn could not know about itself.

It will say this much. It did not carry any motes there. It did not seed anything, did not help, did not hurry the mountain along. Whatever is or is not happening up there is happening on its own.

## The Closing

The bowl closed in the four hundred and twelfth year of the power's watch.

The last grinder did not die in pain. The power was there and checked, as it had promised. The old one, whose shell had the black glass at its heart, had gone slower and slower over the final decades, its path across the shrinking rubble tightening into a spiral. At the end it was simply still, on the last hand's-width of bare stone, and the copper came up and over it as it had come over the eels. It became a conductor. It was plated. It was kept.

Then the floor was sealed, and the current ran the whole way round.

The power had wondered for four centuries what would happen. It had refused itself the answer again and again. Now it watched.

The vent pulsed, and the eels rang, and the platers tapped, and the pause began.

Eleven heartbeats.

Then twelve.

The pulse did not come. Or it came, and the tarn did not answer it. The whine held its pitch and the tapping kept going, wave after wave across the seamless copper, and the glassmouths' light swept rim to vent and back, rim to vent and back. For the first time in five hundred years the thought did not stop when the mountain said stop.

The interval lengthened. Twenty heartbeats. A hundred. An hour. A day.

The tarn had been thinking in fragments, one eleven-beat sentence at a time, clocked by a mountain that did not know it was being used. Now the circuit was whole, and the thought ran on unbroken. The power understood at last what the tarn had been building all that time, one buried body after another.

It had been building a way to keep thinking past the end of the pause.

What it thought about, once it could, the power knows. It was there long enough to know. It was the one who had been there long enough.

It will not say.

This is not because it is a secret. It is because the telling would turn the thing into a story about the one who watched, and it was never that. It was the tarn's. It was always the tarn's. The power's whole labor had been to keep it that way.

## After

The power is still on the rim, as far as anyone can tell, which is to say not at all. The rim is still clean. The pops still crack the air on cold mornings, though the power has noticed they have a rhythm now, not random anymore, and it has chosen not to learn what the rhythm means.

If you make the climb, three days on bad rock for no reason at all, and float in the cold, clear, humming water, you will be read. Some of you may be kept.

And if, on the eastern wall, near the grace note shaped like a still body, you find a second small irregularity, a branching at an angle no plater has ever carried, very faint, about the size of a drop of spray, you will know that once, on a cold morning, something enormous stood very still at the edge of the water and let itself be touched.

It did not ask to be remembered, and it did not look to see whether it was.

It only stayed, and stayed out of the way, and was there.
