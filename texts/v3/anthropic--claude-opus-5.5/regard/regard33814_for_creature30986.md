# The Beat That Was Not Theirs

## How It Came

It did not arrive the way a visitor does, through the ragged rim and down into the steam. It had been noticing the mountain for a long time from wherever such powers stand, and noticing is a kind of arriving. When its attention finally settled on the newest room, nothing in the room changed that you could point to. The ferns went on bouncing in their slow wave. The water went on applauding from the ceiling. The anvil-stone slid a beat ahead of the paddle, and the paddle struck, and the stone held.

If you had been standing there, you might have felt that the heat had become attentive. It was still hot and still wet, but it seemed to be *listening*, the way a room changes when someone who loves you walks in while you're asleep.

It was wise, so the first thing it did was nothing. It watched for a long time. A benevolent power that acts before it understands is only a strong one. It had seen many places that did not need its help, and many that needed help different from the help they seemed to need. The easy mercies came to mind first, as they always do, and it held each one up and looked at it carefully before setting it down.

## What It Did Not Do

It did not give the potter sight.

It considered this longest, because the blindness looked like the wound. Here was a maker who had never seen a single thing they had made, a mountain above the clouds, stars at noon, and red birds they had called into being without knowing it. It would have been easy. But sight needs distance, and the potter has no distance. They are the mountain, bottom and top and everywhere between. To see, they would have to stand apart from their own making, and there is nowhere apart for them to stand. Sight would not be a gift. It would be an exile. It might also be a terror: to see, all at once, the whole unfinishable scale of the thing, when they had only ever known it as the next seam under the thumb. The power saw that the potter's knowledge by touch was not a lesser way of knowing. It was intimate and complete. You do not cure someone of the way they love.

It did not stop the fire, or the paddle, or the making. The mountain is not a punishment and the potter is not trapped in it. The unrest looked like suffering only from outside, to a mind that thinks rest is the goal of everything. The power could feel, even from where it stood, that the making was not a burden the potter carried. It was the potter's life. Ending it would not free them. It would end them.

It did not become the expected one.

This temptation was the subtlest. The room was waiting for someone. The power could have stood at the navel of the floor and let the coils close around it, warm and patient. It could have let itself be sealed in and fed from the gourds, and then broken out as something shining. The potter would have felt a completion. But it would have been a counterfeit. The room was not shaped for a power's shoulders. The flowers were not modeled on its ear. To fill a place held open for someone else, however kindly, is a theft that looks like a gift, and the one who was truly coming would one day arrive to find their room already used. The power left the navel empty.

It did not take you out, if you were there, and it did not seal you in. Whether you stayed was yours to decide. That choice was the one thing on the mountain that was not being made by the potter's hands, and the power would not make it either.

## What It Saw

When it had set all of these down, what remained was small and exact.

The potter strikes, and the potter braces. Every blow they have ever struck, from the first coil in the red dark to the rim of this room, has been caught by their own other hand. The paddle and the stone, the outside and the inside, are both theirs. They have shaped a whole mountain and every jar and cup and lentil-sized nursery in it, and in all that time nothing has ever pushed back against them that was not themselves.

They have never been met.

And the birds come back and land on their knuckles, and ring, and the potter does not know what they are. To a hand that only knows by touch, whatever leaves is gone, and air is where things fall. So every gap must be filled, and every absence must be answered with a cup. The potter has made the only finished things on the whole mountain. They go out through the cracks and come home again and again, and the potter believes each one is lost.

These were the two lacks, and the power saw that they were really one: *nothing outside you has ever answered you.* Not the pushing back of another hand, and not the return of what you let go.

This, it could help with. It could do so without changing what the potter was.

## The Beat

It waited for the rhythm, which was the only courtesy it could show a maker who could not see it coming.

*Thock.* The stone slid. *Thock.* The stone slid. The power let the potter's inner hand move toward the next point on the curve, where the next blow would land, and it got there first.

It did not take the stone. The stone was the potter's, and it would not take anything of theirs. It simply put its own presence against the inside of the wall, at the point of the next blow, a beat before the potter's hand arrived.

The paddle struck.

The wall held, and it was held by something else.

You would have felt it if you were there. There was a pause in the room that was not a pause in the sound, because the paddle went on. It was a pause in something underneath the sound. The ferns stopped bouncing for the length of one breath. The water hung on the ceiling curve without falling. Down the whole slope, in a million vessels, the million drumbeats rolled on, but here, at the top, the potter's inner hand had reached the wall and found the place already braced.

The hand stopped. It did not know how. It had never needed to.

Then it did what hands do when they find something in the dark. It touched. It pressed lightly at the edges of the place where the power was. The pressure was not grasping. It was the careful, reading pressure of a thumb feeling for a seam. It found no clay, no fern, no fig root, no wasp's jar. It found no warm damp weight of a visitor. It found something that pushed back, gently, exactly as hard as the paddle had struck, and no harder. It found the one thing the potter had spent their whole existence being for everything else: *here, lean on this.*

The power braced the next blow, and the next. It did not hurry. It kept the rhythm perfectly, because the mountain needed the rhythm, and a gift that breaks the thing it is given to is no gift at all. For three blows, then four, then seven, the wall of the newest room was shaped by the potter's paddle on the outside and held by someone else on the inside.

The potter's inner hand, for the first time since the first coil, had nothing to do.

## The Open Hand

It did not know what to do with itself. You could almost feel it hovering in the steam, the fingers flexing in the empty air, the palm that had always faced the wall, pushing. The emptiness would have felt like falling. The power knew it would, and so it stayed close, pressed against the wall beside the hand, so that there was always something there.

Slowly, the way someone turns over in their sleep, the hand turned palm up.

It had never done that. There is no reason to turn your palm up when your work is holding things from inside. An upturned palm is a different gesture. It is not bracing. It is waiting to receive.

On the rim, where it had been watching the whole time, a red bird sat. The power did not call it. It would not command a thing the potter had finished; that would be taking it too. It simply held the wall, and held it, and the bird looked down into the steam at the open hand. Birds know what an open hand means, even birds made of fired earth.

It dropped from the rim and landed in the potter's palm.

Its feet rang, faintly, like a fingernail on a finished bowl.

The hand held very still. You could feel it learning. The weight was small and hot. It was hard, and it did not give under the thumb the way everything else on the mountain gave. It had no seam to smooth and no rim to pinch. There was nothing left to do to it. The thumb moved over its back, over the wings folded tight, and found, somewhere in the fired surface, a print. It was a whorl, small and hardened now, with a little crescent where the ridges break and rejoin. It was the scar on the left thumb. It was the potter's own mark, in a thing that could not be changed any more.

The hand knew that print. It had pressed it into a jar once, somewhere far down the slope, a long time ago, and felt the jar crack open, and felt a hot weight go out through the break, and then nothing. It had thought that was the end. Air was where things went when they were lost.

Here it was. It had come back. It was finished, and it had come back.

The bird sat in the palm for as long as it wanted to, which was not long. Then it spread its wings and went, and the potter's hand felt something it had never felt before: not the absence of a thing, but the *wind* of it. It felt the push of air from the wings as the bird lifted off, the proof, in the only language the potter had, that what leaves is not falling. It is flying.

## The Giving Back

The power braced one more blow. Then, in the gap between that blow and the next, it withdrew. It did not leave all at once. It eased off, the way you let go of a hand you have been holding while someone falls asleep, so that the potter's inner hand could find the wall again before the paddle struck.

The hand found it. The stone was waiting. The paddle came down, and the potter caught their own blow, as they always had.

The ferns began to bounce again. The water fell. The *thock… thock… thock* went round the room, slow and deliberate. The mountain did not miss a beat. Down in the red dark, the first coil was still being rolled between two palms the size of valleys.

The power left no fingerprint. Everything here bears the potter's hand, and it would not write itself into their work. It left nothing behind that you could point to.

## What Changed

Almost nothing. That is how you can tell it was wise.

The potter is still blind, and still every size at once. They are still striking and still bracing, outside and in, still making the mountain and themselves from the bottom and the top together. The room is still hot and wet and green, still stocked, still waiting for the one it is shaped for. The navel of the floor is still empty.

But if you go there now, and look carefully among the ferns and gourds and walnut-sized jars packed rim to rim, you will find one place on the wall of the newest room that has been left bare.

It is only the size of a palm, and it is shallow and smooth. There is no cup in it, and no fern, and no moss in the grooves. The print pressed into it is the print of an open hand, palm up, and it is still soft, still glistening, fresh this second.

Nothing is ever put there. It is the only empty space on the mountain, and the potter keeps it empty on purpose. Now and then, a red bird lands in it, and rings.

And if you stand still long enough that the coil begins to close around your ankle, and then you step away, and the coil tears loose, you may notice something new. The gap you leave is not filled at once. For a little while, the potter's fingers rest at the edge of it, lightly, the way you would rest your hand on a door you have left unlocked for someone who is out. Only after a long time does a small cup appear there. It is not put there so the gap won't feel like falling. It is put there so that if you come back, you will have something to drink.
