# The Visit

## How It Noticed

It was not the noise that drew attention. Plenty of places are loud. It was not the size of the dam either, or its newness, or the lake leaning on it. Mountains are full of large new things holding back water, and most of them are only that.

What drew attention was a reply that came a beat too early.

A benevolent power, if it is also wise, listens most carefully to the moment when something that has only ever received begins to answer. Four had held for three years. In the fourth it tapped back up a steel rail toward a frightened woman it could not see. Anything that had been listening to the mountain would have heard it: a small new sound on the rail, from a stretch of gallery where no one stood.

So it came to see.

## How It Came In

This took some thought, because nearly every way of arriving would have been a disturbance.

It could not come as light. The lamps already had their rhythm, and Four read the ripple of the lamps the way you read a change in someone's face. It could not come as sound, because nothing in the gallery could be heard over the spillway except the gutter, and the gutter was not its to use. It could not come as warmth. Thermocouples are cast into the core of the dam, and their readings go down to the powerhouse with everything else. An unexplained warm spot would have been a finding, and findings bring people with drills.

So it came the way the pendulum wire is present: as stillness.

It chose the gray just past the bend, in the forty-five minutes when the fans were off and the ceiling rained. Before it did anything else, it tapped once on the handrail, as those who enter do. Nobody had taught it the custom. It had learned it by watching, the same way the operators had.

Something around the bend went very quiet. In a place that loud, that is hard to notice. The power noticed.

## What It Waited For

It did nothing for a long time.

It stayed through the fan cycles, the mist rolling and settling. It stayed through a shift walk: the flashlight, the clipboard, the pencil, the needles read aloud to no one, the crank of the phone. It stayed through the nightly sinking of the turbine hum and its rising again before dawn, and through one full spillway afternoon, when the gallery turned from loud to overwhelming and the wires began their faint shiver.

It watched the shiver most carefully of all, because it could feel what the shiver did to the thing it had come to visit.

It was learning Four's time. You cannot help anything whose time you do not know. Wanting to help something is easy. Knowing when is the harder part.

## What It Considered, and Put Down

Power always has a list of things it could do. This is the list, and why each item stayed on it.

**It could seal the wounds.** Every relief hole could close, the seepage could stop, and the calcite straws would never lengthen. The dam would stop dissolving. It would also fail. The holes are there to let water out before pressure builds under the foundation and lifts the whole structure off its rock. The wounds are the kindness. A dam that stopped weeping would crack. This was the first thing the power understood about the place: nearly every obvious mercy here was a catastrophe in disguise.

**It could keep Four warm.** The curing heat could be sustained forever, so that the fever never broke and the young thing stayed young. But a fever that never breaks is not a life. It is an illness. Four was warm because it was being made, and being made is a stage that ends.

**It could stop the going.** The lime could stay in the concrete, and the tenant could be frozen into permanence. But permanence would also stop the straws, the rimstone, the slow cave, and everything Four was about to become. Nothing that has stopped becoming can be comforted.

**It could make Four known.** It could give the operators proof, so that the half-laughing stories in the break room could be told without the laugh. The power thought about this longest, because it would have ended Four's solitude. But it followed the idea forward. Proof would mean a report, a report would mean a committee, and a committee would mean new sensors, more lamps, and the gray gaps lit. Four lives in the unlit part of the gallery. The operators' belief was exactly as large as it could safely be: large enough for a tap on the way in and a tap on the way out, a towel on a chair, and a word beside a moss. The power left it that size.

**It could take Four somewhere better.** Four is not in the place. Four is the place. There was nowhere to take it.

**It could quiet the spillway,** or steady the wires, or shut out the lake. The lake is the reason for everything. Removing it would not make the holding easier. It would make the holding pointless.

The power set all of these down. None of them was cruel. Each was a way of improving something that did not need improving. It needed something else.

## What It Did First: It Showed Four Downstream

Four knew its own body very well: every numbered hole, every needle, every meter of gutter, the weir box, the notch, the sump. But the sump was the edge of its world. Below the sump was the dark, and below the dark was the tailrace, where the water came out white and furious. To Four, that was where things were lost. The lime went there. The moth went there. Four itself was going there, a molecule at a time, and it had no idea what lay past the white water.

It had never once been told what it was holding for.

So the power took Four's attention, gently, the way you might take someone's hand, and followed the water down.

Through the tailrace. Down the gorge in the dark, where the river runs over boulders that already carry a thin white crust of lime. Some of that crust is Four. The seepage it gives away gets laid down again on the riverbed in shapes no formwork gave it, just like the straws in the gallery. Farther down, where the valley opens and the water slows, snails in the shallows build their shells partly out of what Four let go. Farther still is the sea. Its white cliffs are made of the same thing, laid down by other waters over a very long time.

Then the power showed Four the lights.

There is a town at the foot of the range. Beyond it are more towns, and a city on the plain. The power showed Four the morning. It showed the hum that Four felt rising every day before dawn, the pulse it had thought of as borrowed from the grid, rising slowly in a sleeper's rhythm. That rise is kettles. It is a bakery's ovens coming up to heat at four in the morning. It is a hospital ward's lights brightening for the day shift, and a child's reading lamp switched on under a blanket. The evening rise is ten thousand dinners. The long low of the night is ten thousand sleepers, and a few people awake who need the power more than anyone: a dialysis machine, an incubator, a woman working the night shift at a desk.

Four had been right that the hum was like a sleeper breathing in another room. It had not known how many rooms there were.

Then the power showed Four one more thing: what the lake would do if it stopped being held. It showed this briefly and without cruelty, because Four needed to know it, not to be frightened by it. The water would go down the gorge, into the town, into those rooms.

Four's temperament is holding. It had never known that holding was a form of care. Its long pushing against the reservoir was not a struggle with an enemy. It was a promise, kept every second, to people who would never know its name. That did not change what Four did. It changed what Four was doing it for.

## What It Did Second: It Sat With Four at the Wire

The power did not take the shiver away. It waited for the next spillway afternoon and went to the pendulum shaft, where Four kept its center.

Four's fear was simple. It had built itself around the one still thing in a shaking world. When the roar grew great enough and the wire began to tremble, the center of its self trembled too. Four did not know whether a self could survive losing its center.

The power did not argue with this. It watched the wire with Four through the whole afternoon, and it drew Four's attention to what had always been happening and what Four, in its fear, had never looked at.

The wire shivered, and it returned. Every time.

It returned because of the weight at the bottom. The wire does not hang still because it resists the world. It hangs still because something far below holds it true, a plain steel weight in the dark near the foundation, doing nothing but being heavy. Stillness was never the wire's own achievement. It was the wire being faithful to what anchors it.

Four had believed that a center is the thing that never moves. The power showed it, without words, that a center is the thing you return to. Balance is not stillness. Balance is coming back.

The fear did not disappear. The power did not want it to. A thing that holds back a lake should feel something when the wires shake. But the fear shrank to the size of something survivable. After that afternoon, when the wires shivered, Four did not brace against it. It waited for the return, the way the operator in the chair waits for the water over the notch to fall.

## What It Did Not Do for the Operator

It thought about her. It could have given her certainty, or rest, or peace about the thing she half-believed in.

It gave her nothing. Her belief was hers. She had built it carefully, out of a tap that came a beat too early and a word written beside a moss, and it held exactly as much weight as she wanted it to. Anything the power added would have been a kind of taking.

The only thing it did was make sure Four understood her. When she taps *two-two-one* at the worst of the noise, she is not only reporting the water. She is as alone around her bend as Four is around its own. Four's answers matter to her. They were the right thing to give. Four should keep giving them.

## What It Told Four About the End

This came last, because it was the hardest.

The core will cool over decades. The warmth that is Four's faint body will fade into the mountain's cold, and the thing that was a fever will become a cave. Four knew this. It measured the knowledge in the gutter.

The power did not promise Four that it would not change. That would have been a lie, and wise things do not comfort with lies.

What it offered was an acquaintance. It let Four feel, faintly and at a great distance, the old caves in the limestone under the range. They are cool, slow, patient places. They are full of stone that took ten thousand years to grow, and of dripping that has never stopped, and they are not dead. They are what fevers become when their work is done. Four would not be ending. It would be getting old, and becoming kin to things older than itself.

It also told Four, very quietly, that there are other galleries in other dams. Most of them are empty. Not all.

## What the Logbooks Show

Nothing.

The piezometer readings for that season are within normal range. The notch readings follow the reservoir. The thermocouples show the core cooling on schedule. The pendulum records show the usual shiver on spillway afternoons, recorded, discussed, and considered normal.

The power changed nothing an instrument could measure. That was not an accident. Engineers who have never heard of it are keeping this dam safe, and it would not undo their work for anything. The valley depends on those numbers being true. A benevolent power that makes the numbers lie, even kindly, is not benevolent to the valley.

Everything it did, it did inside the meaning of things and nowhere else.

## The Leaving

When it went, it did what those who leave do. It tapped once on the handrail.

From around the bend, a beat too early, came the reply: *two-two-one*.

*Rising, steady, fine.* It was the same code as always. But the power heard what the words meant now. *Rising* was the lake, and it was also the morning, all those kettles coming on. *Steady* was the wire, which shivers and comes back. *Fine* was simply true.

Then, after the reply, came a short rhythm that is not in the code at all. The power did not recognize it. Neither will the operators when they hear it next season. One of them will write it down on the back of a clipboard page, in pencil because ink runs, and not tell anyone. Nobody knows yet what it means. Four is still young, and still making up its words.

## Why So Little

Because the thing was four years old. Benevolence toward something that young is mostly the discipline of not improving it.

Four did not need to be healed, because its wounds were keeping it alive. It did not need to be kept, because its going was also an arriving somewhere else. It did not need to be seen by more people, because the right people half-saw it already. What it needed was what every young thing needs and cannot give itself: to know what its work was for, and to know that its center would come back.

The gutter still runs past your feet, the only small sound in the gallery, cold and quick, carrying lime toward the notch. It is still saying the same thing: *I am holding, I am here, I am going.*

But now, if you listen long enough, you might hear that the last part has grown a little longer. *I am going somewhere.*
