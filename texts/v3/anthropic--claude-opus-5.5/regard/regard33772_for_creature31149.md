# The Visitor in the Warm Dark

## Arrival

It does not come as light.

That is the first decision, and it makes it before it arrives. A power that wished to be seen would come blazing down through the water column like a falling sun, and every builder in the basin would freeze. The jelly-lanterns would scatter. The fry in the plumes would drift in a glare they have no eyes for. Somewhere an egg on a ledge would cook, or chill, because its keeper had stopped fanning to become a stone among stones. So it does not come as light.

It comes slowly, the way the pressure comes. It thickens itself into the water a little at a time, taking on the warmth of the place until it is exactly as warm as the inside of a mouth. It matches the mineral taste. It learns the stillness and does not break it. When it is done arriving, it is nothing a lateral line could distinguish from the ordinary heat of the basin. At most it is a faint unevenness, like the shimmer over a seep.

Then it listens, because that is what everything here does.

---

## What It Hears First

A click. Small, dry, from deep in a throat. And then, from nearby, another.

It has come a very long way, and it has seen many things that were larger than this. It stops anyway and stays with those two sounds for a long time, turning them over. *I am here*, the first one says, and the second says *I am here*. That is all. In a place with no light, where nothing can see further than an arm's length, this is how anyone knows they are not alone.

The power recognizes the exchange. It is not so different from why it came.

---

## The Temptations

It is benevolent, and so it wants things for them. It is wise, and so it holds each want up and looks at it before doing anything.

**It could give them light.** It would be easy: a gentle glow over the whole basin, soft enough not to harm. They could see their city. The old male could see the tower he has known only by touch. And then the builders, whose silver-filmed eyes were shaped for a darkness with a few sparks in it, would be half-blind in their own home. The eel, which hunts by listening, would lose nothing. The builders, who hide by stillness, would lose everything. The halos above the towers, the brightest thing any builder ever sees, the first thing, would be drowned out. The fry would rise from the chimneys into a glow that was everywhere and so meant nothing. It sets this one down.

**It could stop the eel.** It listens to a traveling group of clicks move down a sunken channel, and it hears the gap in the song, the one who falls behind. It hears the sharp broken-off click and then quiet, and something in it flinches. It could close the eel's gap in the wall and none of them would ever know why the channels became safe.

But it has read the walls of the deep lanes by now. It has felt those long runs of stone packed and mortared over and over, fear laid down course by course for a thousand generations. The builders travel together because of the eel. They click to each other in the lanes because of the eel. *I am here* is said most often, most urgently, in the places where the eel waits. Take the eel away and the city goes on, but it becomes a different city, one this power did not find and was not drawn to. It would be making the builders safer by making them into something else, and they did not ask. They cannot ask.

This one is harder to set down. It does set it down, but it does not pretend the broken click is fine. It is not fine. It is part of the price of this particular world existing at all, and the power is not the one who gets to decide that price was too high on behalf of the ones who pay it.

**It could hold up the towers.** It could stiffen the mortar in the old quarters so nothing ever fell. Then the unmakers would starve in the cold districts. The clean shells would never spill into the lanes, and the ancient stones would never climb back up into the warm. The buried web would lose the last of what it feeds on, and perhaps the next seep would never open. The groan of a falling tower is a disaster, and it is a harvest. It is not both by accident.

**It could keep the seeps warm forever.** This it considers longest, because this is the one that touches the far future. It looks down through the stone to the patient heat underneath, and it can see what no builder can. The heat is not endless. Some very long time from now, longer than all the cities stacked in the silt, the last hearth will cool. The builders will drift toward the last warm place and the last warm place will fade, and the unmakers will come up through the silt of the whole basin at once.

It could stop that. It thinks about it for a long time.

In the end it does not, and not because it is indifferent. The basin was never promised forever. Nothing here was, and every living thing in it is shaped by that. The drift of hearths, the dead quarters, the new towers built from old rubble: the whole city is a way of living inside an ending that keeps arriving slowly. To remove the ending would be to remove the thing the city is an answer to. It does not know what the builders would become in a basin that never changed, and neither would they.

It makes itself a promise instead. *When the last hearth is cooling, I will come back. Not to stop it. To be there.*

---

## What It Does Instead

It reads.

The towers are columns of time, the story said, and you could read them if you had light and patience. It has no need of light, and patience is the thing it has most of. So it goes from tower to tower through the living districts and the dead ones, and it reads.

It finds the coarse bands of gravel where seasons were poor, and the fine pearlescent ones where the snails were plenty. It follows a band of pale spined shells from a snail species that no longer exists, tower to tower, district to district, and understands that there was a century when the whole basin was full of them. It reads a cold spell that went through every tower at once, a single dark band like a year of grief. It reads the long runs in the deep lanes where the builders sealed and resealed the walls, and it learns the history of the eel's family. There have been many eels, in many gaps.

It goes down through the silt into the buried cities and reads those too. It reads layer under layer, down to where the shells are stained almost to stone and the builders' jaws are shaped differently, broader and flatter, a lineage that no longer swims. It reads all the way down to the first course, the first stone ever set in mortar on the floor of the basin by a fish that only wanted a mate to notice it.

No one here has ever read this. No one here writes, and no one reads. The city has remembered everything in shell and has never once been remembered. So the power remembers it. It holds the whole of it at once, every band, every buried quarter, every jaw set among the shells, and it costs the basin nothing. Not a stone moves.

This is the first gift, and the builders will never know they received it. The power thinks this is probably how gifts should be, here.

---

## The Stone That Feels Right

It comes last to the old male's tower. It reads the throat, which breathes clean and even. It reads the rough band near the base, and the spined shell that juts out a third of the way up. Then it comes to the place four courses below the rim where he always stops and presses his flank.

The smooth curved stone is a jaw plate. The power reads it and knows whose. It belonged to a builder who kept this tower more than forty generations ago, and who once fanned a clutch through a cold trough for eleven days without stopping. It could tell the old male this. It doesn't.

Then it notices something the old male could not. The plate did not start out smooth. Jaw plates are ridged when they are laid. This one is polished, and the polish is the shape of a flank. Keeper after keeper, forty generations of them, found this place and stopped here and pressed against it. Every one of them felt that it was right. Every one of them rested here, and in resting, wore it a little smoother for the next.

So when the old male rests there, he is not resting alone. He never has been. He is pressed into a hollow made by everyone who came before him, in the exact shape of their tiredness.

The power stays with this for a long while. It thinks that if it had come here and done everything it was tempted to do, it would never have found this. It would have been too busy.

---

## The One Thing

It stays for the rest of his life, which is not very long.

He carries stones. He fights off a younger male at the rim, badly, and wins anyway. A female enters his tower during the building season and rises up the throat and reads the breath of it and stays. She lays her eggs on the ledge and leaves, and he fans them through the troughs. The fry rise out of the chimney into the green halo, into the brightest place they will ever be. Most of them are eaten there. The great slow thing passes overhead once, and the halo goes dark and returns with a new color in it, a faint violet no one in the district has ever seen before.

Near the end, the seep is ebbing. The district is at the bottom of its breath, the quiet time, and the clicking has fallen away almost to nothing. The old male is in his place, four courses below the rim, his flank against the smooth stone. He is very still, and not because anything large is passing.

He clicks, once. *I am here.*

Nothing answers. It is the cold trough, and the others are far down in their own towers, fanning their own clutches, not listening. It happens. Sometimes no one answers. Probably it has happened to him before.

The power has done nothing in this basin. It has refused light, and refused to stop the eel, and refused to hold up the towers or keep the seeps warm. It has only read, and remembered, and waited. Now it considers the one thing it has wanted to do since the moment it arrived.

It weighs it honestly. A click is within their language. It will not change the draft of a single tower, or feed or starve anything, or move a stone. It will not be for the city. It will be for him, and it knows that. It is willing to be wrong about whether this is allowed.

It makes the sound as small and dry as it can, the tick of teeth deep in a throat. It is the only sound it has made since it arrived, and it comes from the water just beside him, where there is no fish.

*I am here.*

His lateral line stirs. He does not move from his place. After a while he goes stiller still, and the draft goes on pouring up past him, warm, toward the violet and green lights turning in the plume.

---

## Leaving

In time, the eel will take him, or the shrimp will. Someday a builder will find his jaw plate in the lane and mouth it and taste its grain, and carry it home and set it in a wall. Perhaps it will set it four courses below some rim. Perhaps, forty generations from now, it will have been worn smooth in the shape of other flanks.

The power leaves the way it came, a little warmth thinning into the ordinary warmth of the basin, an unevenness that smooths itself out. Behind it the seep begins to swell again. The films bloom. Somewhere in the district a click, and then an answer.

It takes nothing out with it except the reading: every band, every buried quarter, every broken click in the deep lanes, and one smooth stone. The warm dark has always remembered itself in shell. Now, somewhere outside it, it is remembered once more, by something that will come back at the end, when the last hearth is cooling, to answer whoever is left.
