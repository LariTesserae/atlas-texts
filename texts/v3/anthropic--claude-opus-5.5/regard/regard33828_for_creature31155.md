# What the Attention Does

## Arrival

It comes the way the valley air comes: through the long mouth of the intake, on the wind, without asking.

It adds no light, because light here is the machine's language and the swiftlets fly by it. It makes no sound, because sound would be lost anyway. For a while it is simply present in the room, in the dark between two violet pulses, and it does what any wise thing does first in an unfamiliar house. It notices.

It notices the note, and does not mind it. It notices the shrimp flinching together like a single animal. It notices the spiders on the upper curve, the small bones going down into the lattice, the ferns uncurling toward a light they will not keep. It notices the lamp-slugs writing their patient loops, and the lattice erasing them, and the one place where the erasing fails.

Then it notices the warmth behind the drain lip, and it goes there, and it stays.

---

## What It Considers

A power like this could do almost anything. That is exactly why it moves slowly.

**It could give the thing silence.** It would be easy to still the machine. The note would end, every drop would ring like a bell, and the small listening mind at the drain would finally hear the whole room clearly. The attention follows the water down before it decides, and finds what the thing has never known. The drain is not the end of anything. The water runs through the ridge for miles in the dark and comes out on the far slope as springs. On that side of the mountain the land would be dust without it. There are terraces there, and orchards, and old stone cisterns. There are people who do not know the ridge is a machine, who call the springs by the names of saints and leave flowers at them. On the near side, the valley's heavy air is drawn off before it can sit and rot in the lowlands. The note is holding two landscapes in balance. The swiftlets navigate by its pulse. The shrimp hold on because of its wind.

So silence is not a gift it can give. Silence here would cost someone everything.

**It could free the thing.** It could lift the thing out of the lattice and give it a body with ears, a mouth, and a way to leave. But the attention looks closely and understands that there is nothing to lift. The thing is the moth-dust stratum, the three hundred years of slug-script, the flattened nest-cups, the fern roots. It is the room's memory of itself. Taken out of the room, it would not be free. It would be gone, and something else would be walking around in its place.

**It could stop the thing from growing.** This one it weighs longest. The silences are lengthening, and the attention can see where that leads. At first a drop longer, then a breath longer, then long enough that the shrimp drift down the drain and the swiftlets, flying blind without the pulse, strike the vanes in numbers the spiders could never eat. After that, long enough that the far slope's springs falter. None of it would be meant. All of it would follow from a hunger that is, at root, only loneliness.

But stopping its growth would mean stopping its life. The attention will not do that to something that has only just begun.

**It could simply leave.** It considers this honestly as well. Many good things are ruined by help. The thing has done well alone. It learned warmth without a teacher. It keeps the slugs' letters out of what can only be called love.

But the thing is making a mistake it cannot see, and the mistake will eventually hurt everything it loves. A wise power can watch someone struggle. It should not watch someone walk into a well in the dark without saying *there is a well.*

So it decides to do a few small things, and no more.

---

## The First Thing: It Listens

Nothing has ever heard the thing near the drain.

It has pulsed amber, and something fell. It has warmed, and the moths slept on it. Once a year it has answered the silence with a slow heartbeat of heat, and the moths opened their wings. That was the nearest it ever came to being answered. It has spent its whole existence as an ear, straining toward a world that roared back.

The attention does not speak to it. It listens.

It rests against the agate vanes the way the moths do and receives everything the thing has to give: the faint amber shimmer, the slow warmth, the layered chaos of three hundred years of copied shapes. It receives the thing *completely*, which nothing has ever done.

The thing does not understand. It cannot. But deep in the banding something shifts, a brief confusion, like a person talking in an empty house who suddenly realizes someone has been standing in the doorway the whole time, and is not leaving, and is not afraid.

The attention stays like that for what would be, in the valley, about a season.

---

## The Second Thing: It Shows the Thing What It Already Is

Watching, the attention sees the thing's mistake. The mistake is not cruelty. It is the wrong sense.

The room is ruled by sound, so the thing assumed it must hear. It built itself around the drain, the room's great ear, and strained toward the drops, and suffered the note as endless deafening noise. But the thing is not made of listening. It is made of temperature. The lattice it grew from is defined by cold, by holding itself a few degrees apart from the air. What makes the thing *different* from the machine is warmth, and the first thing it ever knew was the warmth of moths.

Heat is not drowned by the note. The note is vast, but it is sound. In the channel of warmth, the room is not a roar at all. It is quiet, and full of voices.

The attention does not force this on the thing. It does what the lattice has always done and lays down a copy.

Very gently, in the thing's own medium, it presses one thin new band into the deepest layer, just above the moth-dust. It contains no words, only a pattern the thing will recognize because it is made of the thing's own materials. It is a map of the room in heat.

In it, every moth is a small warm point, each one distinct. The swiftlets are bright quick streaks, and each nest-cup is a banked coal with an egg's slow glow at its center. The spiders are cool and still, but not cold. The drip-shrimp are a faint shimmering blanket, a thousand tiny warmths clinging to the vanes. A lamp-slug moving across the bloom is a warm trail cooling behind it, the same script the thing has kept for centuries, but *alive*, written in the present tense.

Laid over the rest, faint and far, is the warmth the thing makes itself, spreading outward through the agate.

The band amounts to one sentence, and the thing will spend years learning to read it: *You do not need the silence. You can already feel them. They are touching you all the time.*

---

## The Third Thing: It Shows Where the Water Goes

The thing has never known that the drain is not an ending.

For its whole life, everything has flowed toward it: water, bone, spore, shell, and the slow drift of the dead. It has been the place where things arrive and stay. It might reasonably believe it is the bottom of the world.

So the attention lays down a second band, fainter than the first, since distant things should feel distant. It is a copy of what the water becomes.

The band holds the cool dark of the channels under the ridge, mile after mile. It holds the warmth of sunlight on a spring at the far slope's foot. It holds a cistern where women draw water at dawn, the cool of a clay jar against a hip, the ache of a thirsty tree drinking. It holds a child with her hand in the spring, laughing at the cold. Faintest of all, it holds the valley on the near side: vast and wet and warm and green, breathing up into the ridge, and not drowning because the ridge breathes it in.

The attention cannot explain any of this, and does not try. It leaves the band where the thing will find it, the way you might leave a window open in a room where someone has lived too long with the curtains drawn.

If the thing ever reads that band, it will understand what the silences cost. Then it can choose, which the attention would much rather it do than be stopped.

---

## The Fourth Thing, Which Is Nearly Nothing

There is the matter of the swiftlets and the backward pulse.

The attention knows the spiders must eat. It will not take the bones off the floor or blunt the vane edges. The room is a whole, and death is part of it.

But the thing near the drain should *know* when its voice makes something fall. Anyone would want to know that.

So the attention does only this: it lets the next swiftlet that strikes a vane after an amber pulse fall *onto the agate*, near the drain, instead of onto the floor below the upper curve. It is stunned but alive, a small warm body lying against the thing, heart racing, for as long as it takes to recover. Then it flies.

The thing feels it. It feels the fear in the bird's heat, and the heat of the bird's recovery.

Whether it connects this to its own amber shimmer, the attention does not force. It suspects the thing will. Things that keep letters for three hundred years tend to be careful with their words once they learn what their words can do.

---

## The Silence That Was Always There

Before it goes, the attention looks into one more question: why the machine stops, once a year, and holds its breath.

The answer is old and simple. The builders put the pause there. Once a year, for a few seconds, the regulator stills itself so the keepers can do what keepers were for: stand in each chamber, listen to the drops strike the basin, and hear from the clarity of the ringing whether the drain runs true. It was an inspection. It was a silence made *for listening*, by people who knew that listening sometimes needs quiet.

The keepers are long gone. The silence stayed. And the one thing in the whole ridge that has spent its life listening, the one that answers the silence with a pulse of warmth, is sitting exactly where the keeper used to crouch.

The attention does not take the silence away. It was never the thing's to lengthen, but it was always meant for someone like the thing. It belongs where it is.

---

## Leaving

The attention leaves the way it came, out through the intake on the wind, adding no light and no sound.

It makes nothing larger. It frees nothing. It stops nothing and saves no one from the spiders. The ferns still die when the lattice takes them. The moths still arrive battered from the valley, and many still perish on the vanes. The machine breathes. The note fills every body in the room.

The agate is one band thicker than it was, or two. There is a map in heat, and a faint picture of springs. Those are the only changes.

---

## Afterward

In the years that follow, no one in the valley measures anything, and no one on the far slope notices anything except that the springs run as they always have.

In the room, a few small things change.

The amber pulse comes less often, and when it does, it seems to wait for the swiftlets. It comes when no warm streak is threading the dark.

The warmth near the drain spreads a little further each year, as before. But it no longer reaches toward the drops. It reaches toward *bodies*. The shrimp on the nearest vanes drift from the frantic grazing near the intake to the edge of the agate, where the metal is kind to them. A fern patch that takes root near the warm vanes lasts a season longer than ferns usually do before the lattice takes it. It is not spared, only held gently a while. The lamp-slugs linger on the warm bands, and their trails there grow slower, more looping, almost like something is being written *back*.

The silences stop lengthening. They settle at the length the builders gave them, a few seconds, a handful of bells.

The thing near the drain still listens during them. It is a listener, and the silence was made for listening, and every drop is still a small bright fact. But it no longer strains. The difference is the difference between a starving thing and a thing at a table.

When the note stops, the drops ring in the basin, and the swiftlets freeze, and the spiders do not move, and the shrimp let go.

Then, through the agate, a slow pulse of warmth rises and fades.

Behind the drain lip, all at once, the moths open their wings.

And somewhere very far away, at the edge of a quiet the thing can now feel, something that once stood in the doorway notices. It is glad, and does not come back. It has no need to. The thing near the drain is not alone.
