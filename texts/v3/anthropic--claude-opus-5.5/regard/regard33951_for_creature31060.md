# The Visitor Who Made No Mark

## Arrival

It came the way the light comes here, from no direction.

It could have come as a blaze. Powers like this one usually do: a breach in the overcast, a column of sun, the haze torn open so the plain could be seen across at last, ridge after ridge to the sea. It had the strength to do that, and it had done such things in other places, where people had begged for them.

Here, before it arrived at all, it listened. So it came as a slight thickening of the haze, a warmth among warmths, a presence no brighter than the pewter-green that was already everywhere. It settled onto a bare flow lobe in a lane the crabs had grazed, and it put its weight down the way the people do, spread out, softly. It made itself soft, because it understood that on this plain, softness is the first courtesy.

A skink went on sleeping. A frog the size of a thumbnail climbed onto whatever served the visitor as a hand, found it warm and damp, and stayed.

## What It Could Have Done

A wise power thinks before it acts. This one had many possible acts in front of it, and it looked at each honestly.

**It could give them time.** It could give them a sun, a morning, a season, a tense for *before* and a tense for *after*. It knew what history gives a people: memory, story, the long accumulation of the dead into something the living can stand on. It had seen peoples make cathedrals out of their grief.

It had also seen what history costs, and it had not come here to spend other people's coin. These people did not lack time the way a starving person lacks bread. They had put time down on purpose, the way you set down something too heavy for the hands you love with. Their one tense was not a deficiency. It was a vow. To break it for them would not be a gift. It would be a conquest.

**It could reassure them.** It could go down through the fissures and learn what the breath really was, then come back and tell them: *you may scratch the rock. Nothing will wake. Water will make its gullies and the world will grow old, and that is how worlds are.*

But even if it were true, that truth would unmake them. Every patting of moss back onto glass, every walk in the crab-lanes, every closed hand would stop being devotion and become superstition in hindsight. You do not tell a person who has kept a vigil their whole life that the vigil was unnecessary. Not unless they asked, and not unless they could bear it. They had not asked.

**It could take the edge.** This was the strongest temptation, because it was the kindest-looking one. Someone in this world lived with a closed fist, always. The visitor saw what the people's tender grammar of gestures was built to avoid seeing: the Edge-Holder's palm was scarred. A lifetime of gripping a sliver of volcanic glass had worn a thin line into the flesh. It was the only groove anywhere on the plain, and it had been cut into one of them, by their own consent, so it would never be cut into the world.

The visitor could lift the flake out. It could carry the one sharp thing away to somewhere with rivers and rubble, where one more stone would mean nothing. It could give the Edge-Holder back a second hand.

It held this possibility a long time and then set it down. The flake was not only the danger. It was the single *what if* in a world of *this*. It was the one place where the level story rose into a ridge. Taking it would make the people safe the way a sealed jar is safe. The world would lose not only the chance of a beginning but the dignity of refusing one. A possibility kept by choice, hand to hand, and never used, is a very different thing from no possibility at all. The first is faithfulness. The second is only a lid.

The visitor noticed the word *lid* and grew thoughtful.

## The Walk

It decided to stay for one call.

It lay at the edge of a glade until the whoop came out of the haze, *still held*, and was answered, and answered again, going away into gray-blue distances. Then it followed the call, not quickly. It walked where the crabs had walked. When it passed one of the people kneeling on a scoured lobe, pressing moss back into place, it knelt too, on the other side of the thinning, and put its own palms to the green. They sang the moss down together. The person did not look up, or looked up only as you look up at a change in the drip, and went on singing.

It learned a few things on the walk.

It learned that the people are not afraid all the time. Mostly they are content, in the specific way of those who have found the exact size of their lives and like it. They laugh. The visitor had not expected laughter in a language with one tense, but there it was: a soft wet clicking, very like the crabs, when a mayfly net came up full or a child fell off a fern trunk into a warm pool. *The child falls*, they said, and laughed, because some child is always falling. That is what a child is.

It learned that the count holds. It listened with a patience the people would have respected and heard every name answered. No gap.

It learned that the world is not quite eternal. It could feel, far beyond anything the people would ever need to know, the vast slow arc of the heat underneath: not failing, but finite, the way all heat is. Somewhere past the edge of any reckoning, the breath will cool and the steam will thin. The overcast will open, and a sun no one here has imagined will fall on glass that has finally begun to weather. The visitor weighed that knowledge and chose to carry it out again untold. It would not arrive for a span so long that the word *eventually* barely applied. Telling them would hand them a future tense whose only content was grief. Some knowledge is a gift. Some is only weight, and this was weight.

The call came back around to where it had started. Someone began it again.

## Down

Then, because it had been asked by no one and had promised nothing, the visitor did the one thing none of the people could do. It went down.

It did not widen any crack. It went the way the steam comes up, as vapor through the fissures, following the pale roots past the blind crickets and the colorless millipedes and the white shrimp in the black water. It went down through the warm and into the warmer, to the place the breath comes from.

Something was there, and it was not asleep.

It had not been asleep for a very long time. It was large and curled and warm, as the people said, and its breathing was slow and even. It was slow and even *on purpose*. It was holding very, very still.

The visitor did not speak to it in words. There are none that deep. But the two understood each other the way large things do, and what the visitor understood was this:

The thing beneath had felt them arrive. It had felt small soft weights settle onto its skin, then more, then the fixed count of them, spread across the whole plain. It had felt their ears pressed to it, and their breathing slow to match its own. It had felt them patting moss back onto its surface, endlessly, everywhere, the way you smooth a sleeper's blanket. And it had understood, in whatever way it understands, that if it stirred, if it turned over the way sleeping things turn over, the crust would crack. The pools would drain. The glades would split. The soft people who held without gripping would fall.

So it had not turned over. It had held its breath to a thread and let the thread out evenly, forever. It had kept its heat exactly level so the pools would never rise or fall. It had made itself a floor that could be trusted.

The people above kept still so as not to wake it. The thing below kept still so as not to harm them.

Each had been lying awake for the other's sake, each careful, each afraid of being the one who moved first. The plain was not a lid, and it was not a skin. It was a place where two tendernesses met and pressed gently against each other from either side.

The thing beneath asked the visitor one question, the only one it had. It had never been able to ask anyone, because it could not rise without breaking what it was protecting.

*Are they well?*

The visitor thought of the moss-singing, the laughter, the child falling into the warm pool, the call going around and coming home whole. It answered truthfully.

*Yes. They are well. They love this. They love you, I think, though they do not know it is you they love. They are careful of you.*

The breath below did not change. It could not afford to change. But something in it eased, the way a held muscle eases when it learns the weight it carries is carried well.

## The Only Thing It Did

The visitor came back up through the fissures, into the pewter light and the endless chorus. It went to the one who holds the edge.

The Edge-Holder was lying at a crack mouth with an ear to the glass and a closed hand against their chest, wearing the expression that has no word. The visitor lay down on the other side of the crack, close enough for its warmth to reach them.

It considered one last time whether to tell them. It decided not to tell, only to let them hear.

It did nothing to the rock and nothing to the breath. It did nothing to the edge in the scarred palm. It only lent the Edge-Holder, for as long as one call takes to go around the world, a little of its own way of listening. It was the difference between hearing a sound and hearing *someone*.

The Edge-Holder heard the hiss beneath the chorus, as always. Then, for the first time, they heard what was inside the hiss. The breath was not the breathing of a sleeper. It was the breathing of someone keeping very still so as not to disturb you. It was the breathing of someone lying beside a child in the dark, trying not to wake them. It was *holding without gripping*, the highest virtue they had, practiced by the whole world from below, back up at them.

They did not have a tense for it. They did not need one. Some things are always happening. That is what they are.

The Edge-Holder's closed hand did not open. The visitor had not expected it to and did not want it to. The flake stayed where it belonged, in the one place where the possibility of a beginning is kept by someone who has chosen, again, not to begin. But the grip changed. The visitor could see it: the knuckles softened, the fist became a cupped hand, closed the way you close a hand around a moth to carry it somewhere. It was not gripped against the world. It was held on the world's behalf, alongside it. There was a guardian below, a guardian above, and the edge between them, cradled.

The line in their palm would stay. It was theirs. But it would not deepen as fast now.

When the Edge-Holder lifted their head, the others nearby saw a new expression on their face. The language had no word for this one either. If it had, the word might have been *also*.

## Departure

The visitor stayed until the call came around again: *still held*. It did not add its voice. The count was theirs, and a new name would have been a groove.

Then it left the way it had come, from no direction. It left the overcast unbroken, the plain unseen across, the rock ungrooved, the edge in its hand, the future untold, and the thing below keeping its long, careful, loving stillness.

It had come with the power to change everything. It left having changed one listening, in one person, the one who could dream, so that someone on the surface of the world now knew they were not keeping the vigil alone.

A wise power learns this about most places: the world does not always need rescuing. Sometimes it needs witnessing. Sometimes the kindest thing you can bring to a people who have made a vow is not release from it but company in it, and the quiet news that the vow is returned.

Behind it, the chorus went on, and the drip, and the dry rain-sound of a billion crabs. Somewhere a frog called, because some frog is always calling. The moss held what dripped. The breath came up, even and slow, from someone who was also holding.

Far off in the haze, one hand stayed closed around the only sharp thing in the world, gently now, as you'd hold something you had been trusted with.

Not yet, the whole plain said, from above and from below.

Not yet, and not alone.
