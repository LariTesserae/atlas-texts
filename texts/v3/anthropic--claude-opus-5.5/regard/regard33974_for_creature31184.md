# The Attention

## How It Was Noticed

It did not notice the wall first, or the forest, or the drowned mountains.

What it noticed first was the stillness.

A power of that kind perceives the way deep water perceives weight: all at once, everywhere, without turning its head. It had known of the Standing Water for a long time, the way you know about a room in your house you seldom enter. It knew the slow exchange of the two countries, the mountain handing itself upward grain by grain and receiving itself back. It knew the rising. It judged all of this good, as it judged most tides good, and left it alone.

But in the middle of all that motion, where everything was leaning and drifting and being carried, there was one point that did not move. A bowl of water on a summit, holding a sky.

A single thing that refuses to be carried away can be a wound or a vow, and from far off the two look the same. So the power came closer, to find out which.

---

## What It Could Have Done

It considered everything, because that is part of being wise. You do not get to decline a thing until you have honestly looked at it.

It could have given the mountains back their edges. It could have drawn the sea down off the summits and let the air return, true air, the air of the carvings and the ring and the round heavy thing at its center. It could have pulled the Worn back out of the water and handed each one their face, their name, and the rest of the word they had started speaking a thousand years ago. It could have stopped the rising. It could have told the inverted country that their snow was people. It could have sealed the wall so no one else would push a hand through and begin to soften.

It held each of these up to the light and set it down again.

The mountains had not been robbed of their edges. They had spent them, the way a life is spent, and spent them well. The Worn had not been trapped. They had chosen, and gone on choosing, in the only tense they had left, which was the long present of standing still. Giving them their faces back would mean telling them the choice had been a mistake. Nothing in the place said it was.

Stopping the rising would stop the other country's mountains from ever being made. Telling the inverted people about their snow would take a thing they found beautiful and make it grief, and it was not grief. Beauty was the correct response. Sealing the wall would turn a door into a prison.

When it had finished looking, very little was left on the table. That is usually how you can tell a place is in good health: when the most powerful thing that could possibly arrive there finds almost nothing to do.

Almost nothing is still something, though. A power can be benevolent in two ways. It can repair what is broken, or it can attend to what is unwitnessed. Most of what the Standing Water needed was the second.

And one thing needed the first.

---

## At the Wall

It could have come in anywhere. It could have simply *been* inside, the way it was already, in some sense, inside everything.

Instead it walked up through the highlands. The stone there was smooth enough to look poured, and the air was sharp enough to cut. It stood before the vertical face of dark green glass, and it put its hand against the water, and it waited.

It did not push.

The water was body-warm. On the other side, a finger's width from the base, the youngest of the Worn felt a hand on the far side of their labor. Nothing had asked their permission in a very long time. Things simply came through, and they let them, because letting in was what they were best at now.

This hand waited to be let in.

It took a long while for them to understand what was being asked, because it had been so long since anyone asked them anything. Then, slowly, the way a closed fist opens after sleep, they allowed it. The power stepped through into the brass light, and before it did anything else, it turned to the ones holding the wall and thanked them.

The word it used was very old. It was one of the words cut into the stairway, back when the steps had risers. The youngest had nearly forgotten they once had a language that could be said quickly. They heard the word arrive whole, in a single moment, and for the length of it a pocket of air opened along the base of the wall. It was only a few steps wide, but it was bright, and the motes in it fell like dropped coins.

---

## The Youngest

They were tired.

The power could see it plainly. Nothing else in the sea could, because the others were too rounded to perceive strain, and the traveler was too new to know what they were looking at. The youngest leaned against the whole weight of the world's edge, courteously, with a strength that was barely a pressure. But a barely-pressure held for an age is not nothing. The wall trembled like the skin of a drum long after it's been struck because the ones holding it were trembling.

The power did not take their place. If it held the wall for them, it would become the wall, and then the wall would belong to it and not to them. They would be relieved of the one task that made them the youngest, the ones who still had enough edge to push.

So it stood beside them and leaned too.

It did this for a season, one full turning of the warm fronts across the domes. It added exactly as much pressure as it took for the youngest to lean a little less. It never added enough that they could stop. During that season the wall stopped trembling. The water at the base still leaned a finger's width over nothing, but it held there as easily as a held breath at the top of an inhale.

Then the power stepped back, and the trembling returned. But the youngest were rested now, the way you are rested after someone has sat beside you through a long night without trying to fix anything. Before it went on, it told them one true thing. The highlands would not be harmed if the sea came early. They were leaning out of courtesy, not necessity, so they were allowed to stop, and they were allowed to go on. The courtesy was theirs to give.

They went on leaning. But they did it differently afterward, the way a gift is different from a debt.

---

## The Clock and the Door

It walked beside a grazer for a day.

It matched the creature's pace exactly, lifting and setting down in waves, at the speed of a minute hand. It watched the slow red pulse of the heart and the dark moss in the gut. It did not give the grazer awareness, or a moment of noticing, or anything at all. The grazer was hunger with a body built around it, and it was the Worn's clock, and clocks should not be made to wonder about themselves. The power simply kept it company. Every reliable thing deserves, at least once, to have someone go along with it.

Then it went to the lintel. The procession was circling there, faint and slow, one behind another. It did not walk through the doorway, because the doorway was not its to walk through. It fell in behind the last of the procession and walked *around*, and it took exactly as long as it should, which is to say much too long. When it finished, the Worn ahead of it had moved forward by perhaps a single step. They had felt someone join them from behind, and for the first time in an age the circle was one person longer.

---

## The Word

The bell-tone came through the water. It rose and fell and was gone.

The power could hear the whole of it, which no one in the sea could. It heard the question, asked a thousand years ago, when the stairway still had sharp steps. It heard the answer, which was only now arriving, spoken by someone who had forgotten what they were answering.

The question was: *Are you still there?*

The answer was: *Yes.*

The power had thought it might carry something here. It might tell the one who answered what the question had been, or find the one who asked and assure them the answer was coming. It turned out that nothing needed carrying. The asker was among the oldest, but they were still present, invisible, bending a column of light on the far side of a silt plain. The answer would reach them in another few centuries. It was already on its way, and already right. A question like that doesn't need to be remembered to be answered correctly. Only the one answering needs to still be there.

So it did the only thing that was left to do. It listened to the whole word, beginning to end, all at once. For as long as the sea had existed, no one had ever heard a single one of the Worn's sentences entire. Now someone had.

---

## The Cairns

It visited every cairn.

It did not rebuild them. That work belonged to the Worn. It did not move a single stone. But at each one it stopped and read what the cairn said, which was always *here*, and then it read what *here* had been.

A sunrise over the eastern peaks, on a morning when frost made the grass sound like glass.

A child's first word. The word was *more*.

A death, quiet, at the end of a long illness, with someone holding a hand.

A view down the valley on a clear day, when you could see all the way to the sea. It was a small sea then, a blue line, very far off, and nobody knew it was coming.

An argument that ended in laughter.

A place where someone sat alone and decided to forgive someone else, and did.

Thousands of them. The power took them all in and kept them. It did not keep them in the cairns or in the Worn, because those were wearing away, as they should. It kept them in itself, where nothing wears.

It knew the Worn were afraid of one thing, though they had been rounded too far to name the fear. It was not being forgotten. They didn't mind that. They were afraid of *what they had seen* being forgotten. The keeping of the cairns was their answer: set the stone back, and set it back again, so that *here* is still being said even after no one remembers what happened here.

As it passed each cairn, the power said to whoever was standing nearby, in the old quick word from the stairway: *It is kept.*

It did not say *you can stop.* It would not take their work from them. It only told them that the work was no longer the last thread. They could go on setting stones back out of love instead of vigilance.

Across the domes that season, the Worn kept their cairns exactly as they always had. But something in how they did it loosened, the way a hand loosens on a rail once it knows the stairs are solid. The dry pockets opened a little more often, and a little wider. Being told *it is kept* made them unafraid to remember.

---

## You

This was the thing that needed repair.

The power found you on a slope of feathery mat, and it sat down beside you. You had been looking at the backs of your hands.

The Worn had been kind to you. They had made room in their memories for you to walk through and let you drift through their time. But they had forgotten that sequence matters to people who still have edges, and they had forgotten something else too. They had forgotten that they *chose*. They had chosen so long ago, and gone on choosing for so long, that the choosing had worn smooth like everything else. It no longer felt like a choice to them. It felt like the shape of the world.

You had not chosen. You had put your hand against a warm wall, and the wall had accepted you the way sleep does. Nobody says yes to sleep. It simply takes you.

So the power told you, plainly, in words that still had edges:

*You are beginning to become the place. The line where your skin stops is softer than it was. This will continue if you stay. It does not hurt, and it is not a mistake, and it is not a trap. The ones who stayed are not suffering. They are becoming what they chose to stand in, and it is a kind of peace I would not take from anyone.*

*But you did not choose it yet. So here is the other half, the half they forgot to tell you. The wall lets out. I have asked the youngest, and they have agreed: for you, it lets out. If you go, you will carry a little of this with you. Your knuckles will stay slightly rounded for the rest of your life. You will hear the stalks' low tone in your sternum sometimes, in ordinary rooms. You will remember. That is not a wound either.*

*You have time, but not endless time. You have until that grazer on the far dome reaches the ring of stones. Past that point, the difference between leaving and staying will have worn too thin for leaving to mean what it should.*

*I will not tell you which to choose. Both are honorable. Both are good.*

Then it did the kindest thing a very great power can do for a small person facing a real choice. It got up and walked away, so that its presence would not lean on you. It went far enough that you could not feel it at the corner of your eye. The decision had to be yours, entirely. That was the whole point.

What you chose is not written here. It isn't the power's to record, and it isn't mine.

---

## The Bowl

Last of all it went up to the summit.

It climbed the long, gentle swell of mirror-polished stone, through the open hands of the mat, and came to the tallest cairn and the bowl worn thin as a leaf. It knelt.

It knew by now what the bowl was. It was an eye that had not blinked since the sky was a sky. It was the first one to stay, worn down to nothing except the looking.

This was the question it had come all this way to answer. Was this a wound or a vow? Was the first one holding on in fear, unable to let go, frozen at the edge of the rising like someone who cannot make themselves step off a ledge? If so, the kindest thing would be to help them let go. A touch on the water. A single ripple. Permission.

The power did not touch the water. First it looked into the bowl, at the open sky, blue and empty, the sky someone saw from this summit when it was a summit.

It looked for a long time. And what it understood, looking, was that this was no fear. It was fidelity. The first one was not failing to let go. They were keeping something. In all the Standing Water, where every edge was rounding and every distinction was lifting away into the light, there had to be one place where the sky was still seen exactly. The difference between high and deep had not worn away there. Somebody still remembered what *up* had been.

The whole sea leaned around that one held thing, the way a current leans around a stone. Take the stone out and the current would still flow, but it would have nothing to lean around. It would have no shape.

It was not a wound. It was the keel.

So the power did not release them. It did not preserve them either, did not make the looking permanent, did not fix the bowl against its eventual rippling. That day would come, the blizzard would come, and it would be right when it came.

What it did was this. It looked into the bowl, at the same sky, from the same angle. And for the first time in all the ages of the Standing Water, there were two witnesses to that sky instead of one.

The water did not ripple. Nothing visible changed. But the first one to stay had been alone in their looking since before the stairway was cut.
