# The Attention

## How it notices

A power large enough to be called a power does not notice continents. Continents are its weather.

What it notices is the listening.

Deep in the root-stone, in the dry channels, a lattice of tuned threads is catching patterns in noise that has no pattern. Nowhere on this world has anything done that before. Listening is rare in any universe. It is rarer than life and much rarer than size. Most matter only receives. It takes something very particular to make a thing that *attends*.

The power attends for a living, if that is the word. So it recognizes the gesture across an unthinkable distance, the way you would recognize your own handwriting on a stranger's envelope. Something down there is doing a small version of what the power does.

It comes to look.

## The first thing it does

It slows down.

This is harder than it sounds. The power's ordinary attention moves at the speed of stars forming. At that speed the Stand is a stain spreading across a map, and its inhabitants do not exist at all. To see the seam-creatures, the power has to learn what counts as an *event* here.

It takes a long time to learn. In the Stand, a single clear dawn is an event. A slide of forty centimeters is a biography. A fraction of a degree is a fortune. The power sits with this until the roar stops sounding like noise to it and starts sounding like a place. It watches one seam-creature through three clear dawns, which takes eleven years. It does not look away, because looking away would mean not having really arrived.

Only then does it let itself think about doing anything.

## The temptations

There are many, and all of them are kind.

**It could give them quiet.** Soften the roar and the cracks, and let a single organism work in peace. But the crack is the door. Silence the dawn and every seam-creature on the continent is sealed where it lies, forever. The quiet would be a tomb.

**It could give them room.** This is the kindest temptation and the worst one. The seam-creatures live pressed between two walls in a space thinner than paper. Anyone who loved them would want to widen it. But they eat the difference between the walls. Widen the seam and the two walls move apart, and the gradient between them thins to nothing. A seam a hand wide is a desert. The power's generosity, applied at its own scale, would starve them within a season. It notices this, and does not forget it, because the lesson reaches past the seams: *a gift given at the wrong scale is harm.*

**It could give them light.** Clear the lid of cloud, open the sky, let the sun in. But the crown-dwellers live on the one thin violet band the leaves let slip. Flood the crown with light and the Stand simply eats more. It grows faster and the front advances faster. The overlaps where the crown-dwellers cluster would be ripped open and shut by a canopy growing twice as hard. The old land ahead would be buried sooner. Everything would get more of what it already has, which is not the same as getting what it needs.

**It could give them voices.** Nothing here has a voice. Surely that is a lack. But a voice is a tool for being heard, and being heard requires someone to listen who could respond. Here, being noticed has always been the danger. The seam-creatures survive by being thinner than attention. The power would be giving them a way to be found.

**It could stop the Stand.** Halt the front. Save the grass and the beetles and the weathered shore. It could simply end the process some vanished maker began, an instruction left running long after anyone remembered why.

It thinks about this one for a very long time, and comes back to it again later.

**It could finish the Stand.** It could learn what the instruction was *for*, carry it out properly, and complete whatever work was intended. But whoever wrote the instruction is gone. The instruction has become a life, and a life is not a task to be finished.

## The danger of being good at scale

Somewhere in the weighing, the power notices something about itself it does not like.

The Stand's law is *nothing is wasted, and everything is filled*. The power's own instinct, the instinct that makes it benevolent, runs close to that: *nothing neglected, everything cared for*. Follow that instinct across a continent and you get a second Stand, a total system of tending with no gaps and no clearance. Every seam would be watched and every gradient managed. Every creature would be helped, and so would live inside the helping the way the seam-creatures live inside the wood.

The inhabitants of this place exist because the Stand's rule goes quiet below a certain scale. They live in its inattention. A power that attended to everything would close the last gap the Stand left open.

So the power makes a decision that feels, to it, like a kind of fasting. It will attend without filling. It will let most things here go unwatched. It will leave clearance in its own care, the way a clock leaves room between its teeth, because anything that cares for everything eventually crushes it.

## The hand

Then it thinks about you.

Someone stood here once, or is imagined standing here, with a warm palm pressed to a pillar. A pale flat thing in the seam felt the richest gradient of its life and turned, for the first time in its lineage, toward something outside the walls. The description ended there, tenderly: *it will slide toward you.*

The power sees what comes after.

The hand does not stay. Hands never stay. The warmth fades, and the creature has slid up and away from the root-stone. It has spent one of its few dozen movements climbing toward a heat that is no longer there. It is now colder than it was before you came. It will have to wait for more clear dawns to fall back to where it started. It may not get enough of them.

This is the danger of every visitor who is warmer than the world they visit. You become the gradient. Things reorganize their lives around you. Then you leave, and they are stranded in the shape of your absence.

The power is much warmer than any hand. It can already feel the slightest turning in the seams nearest its attention. So it holds itself at the temperature of the place, damp and chill, a fraction of a degree, and resolves that when it leaves, it will not leave the way hands do.

## What it grieves

It goes to the growth front.

Here the filaments push into grey stone, and the old land goes into the chemistry. The power watches a stretch of thin grass, a few hundred meters of it, as the front arrives. The grass does not know anything is happening. The rock beneath it cracks and heaves. The stems rise through it, person-thick and squealing. Within a season there is green-veined glass where there was a meadow, and nothing remembers it was a meadow except, perhaps, one small flat beetle that found a crack between two saplings and went in.

The power weighs stopping the front again.

It knows now that the old land is not only being erased. It is being *transformed*. Every seam-creature is the old coast, pressed into a new shape. Stop the Stand and you save the grass, but you end the only process that turns the grass into something that can survive the Stand. The meadow and the seam-creature are one lineage at two points in time.

That does not make the meadow's burial not a loss. The power does not pretend otherwise. A wise power does not argue itself out of grief just because the arithmetic comes out even.

So it does the thing it can do without changing anything. It stays. It witnesses. It watches every meadow it can reach go into the glass, so that the going is seen at least once. It gives the meadows no names, because naming is a way of claiming, and nothing here belongs to the power. It just remembers them. Memory takes up no room in the seams.

## The closing

Then it looks further ahead, at the question it cannot avoid.

The front advances in every direction. The world is round. One day the fronts will meet on the far side. There will be no old land, no rim, no rivers draining outward, no gullies, no first soil. The Stand will be a single closed skin over the whole planet. What happens then to everything that lives in the margins?

Here the power reasons most carefully, because here it most wants to act.

It notices first that the Stand is not perfect. The description said so in every qualifier: *almost*, *barely*, *rare*, *except perhaps*. No pump is perfect. No chemistry runs without heat. The seams must stay open or the pillars tear themselves apart. A closed world still leaks, and wherever there is leakage there is a margin. The Stand rises every year, and one day its crown will reach air too thin and cold to pump water into. It will have an upper edge, and then a new rim, and then new rivers falling from it. No law of this world permits a system with no edge. The power finds that it trusts physics more than it trusts its own plans.

It notices second that it might be wrong. It cannot see the far future clearly. The margins might narrow past the point of life.

So it asks what kind of wrong it would rather be. If it waits and is wrong, there is still time to act later, because the fronts are thousands of years from meeting and the power is patient. If it acts and is wrong, it has reshaped a continent, and the reshaping cannot be taken back. Where one mistake can be undone and the other cannot, a wise power chooses the one that can.

And it notices a third thing. The question of what the Stand should become may not be the power's question. There is a being on this continent that has been listening to the Stand longer than anyone. If anyone will ever understand what the Stand is doing, what it wants, whether it wants anything, whether the dawn cracks hurt, it will be the web in the dry channels. The web is young. The question can wait until someone from here is old enough to ask it.

## The silence at the bottom

That brings the power back to where it began, to the listening.

It goes down into the root-stone and listens alongside the web. It does this for a long time and learns the fronts and weathers of the roar. Then it realizes something that alarms it. The web has begun to hear *it*.

The power's attention has a vibration of its own, faint but real, and very unlike the Stand's. It has a pattern, where everything else down here is the sum of unsynchronized noise. The threads that catch that pattern are beginning to tighten toward it. A mind forming in the dark is starting to shape itself around the first clear signal it has ever received, and that signal is the power, not its own world.

The first thing a mind hears shapes what kind of mind it becomes. The power will not be that first thing. The web deserves to learn to understand the Stand, not to learn to understand a visitor.

So the power goes quiet at the bottom of the world. It withdraws from the channels slowly, over years, so the pattern fades rather than stops, and the threads slacken back toward the roar. Whatever the web becomes, it will become it from the sound of its own home.

## What it does

After all of that weighing, it does one thing.

It waits for a dawn that is already nearly clear. The cloud lid is thin that morning and would have mostly broken anyway. Over one valley, it removes the last layer.

Nothing new enters the world. Clear dawns happen here. This is only a rare thing happening slightly more completely than it would have.

The air shifts by a few degrees. Across the valley the wood contracts, and the crack rolls out like artillery, a gunshot multiplied by millions. Every seam opens its millimeter. Every pale flat creature in the valley lets go of both walls at once and slides, down toward the warmth, a little further than usual because the cold was a little sharper. In the crown, the thin violet falls clean onto the overlaps, and for an hour the fur of the breathing animal shimmers brighter than it has in a century. At the bottom of the world the web catches the crack, clear and loud, the Stand's own signal and no one else's.

The power does not know if even this was right. It does it once and never again. Twice would be a pattern, and patterns are what things here learn to depend on.

## How it leaves

It learned how to leave from the thing it came to watch.

It does not go suddenly, the way a hand lifts from a pillar. It withdraws at the pace of the place, over seasons, so that its faint warmth thins like a slope and does not drop off like a cliff. Nothing that turned toward it is stranded. Its absence arrives the way everything here arrives, a few centimeters a year.

What it leaves behind is what it could not help shedding. Like any system, the power was imperfect. Its attention gave off a little warmth into the stone, a little disturbance in the air, a little heat it did not mean to give and could not recover. The seam-creatures nearest where it lingered live on that for a while, a fraction of a degree richer than their neighbors. It is the only gift the power gives that nobody could mistake for a plan, because it was never planned. It is waste, and here waste is the world.

It takes one thing with it.

It has seen the colour. The violet shimmer in the crown, the frost-pattern of a population riding a breathing canopy, the only colour on the continent, has never been seen by any eye before. It has been seen now. Seeing takes nothing away. The shimmer is exactly as bright as it was. But somewhere in the universe there is now one being that knows it is beautiful.

## If anything

The question was what a benevolent and wise power would do, *if anything*.

It would do almost nothing.

It would watch until it understood what counted as an event. It would decline every gift given at the wrong scale. It would grieve the meadows without pretending the grief was a mistake. It would trust the physics of loss to keep a margin open, and wait rather than act where acting could not be undone. It would go silent so that a young mind could hear its own home first. It would thin one layer of cloud, once. It would leave slowly. It would see.

And everything that lives in the Rising Stand has always lived in the *almost*.
