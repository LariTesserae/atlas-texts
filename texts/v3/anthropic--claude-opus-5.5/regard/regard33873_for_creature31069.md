# The Visitor

## Arrival

It did not come through the split. It had no need of the split. Its attention simply arrived, the way the heat arrives, from everywhere at once, and settled into the chamber the way dust settles when the colony is out.

It came at midday, when the dome was full, because it wanted to see the place at its most itself.

It saw everything at once, which is the burden of such powers and the reason the wise ones among them learn to look slowly. So it made itself look slowly. It looked at the realgar first, crumbling in no light at all, the one thing in the Blister that the dark was keeping. It looked at the guano bands, thirty of them, and read them the way you would read the spines of books on a shelf: a wet year over the lowlands, fat with moths; a lean year; a year when something went wrong with the pups and the dermestids were many. It looked at the lizard at the margin, pressed belly-down against a lobe of lava, and at the charge gathered on its toe-tips. It looked at one mother bat folded over one pup and understood, fully, what it had cost her to find it.

It looked at the dome breathing. It felt the throb in the stone.

Then it waited for the shift change, because it already knew what it had really come for.

## The silence

The compressors wound down on the far side of the ridge. The throb stopped.

The dome went still.

The power had seen a great many things go quiet. It had watched seas go flat before storms and forests hush at eclipses. It had heard the silence of a hospital corridor at four in the morning and the silence of a congregation after the last note. It knew the difference between the silence of absence and the silence of attention, and this was the second kind. Three hundred thousand small bodies had turned their listening outward through four kilometres of stone, toward a heart they had never seen.

The power thought: *There you are.*

This was the inhabitant it had been drawn to. It was not the bats, though it loved the bats. It was the listening: a thing made of habit and inheritance, owned by no one, twenty generations deep in a room forty years old. It had no body. It lived in the pause between beats. It was, the power recognised with something like kinship, a little like the power itself: an attention that did not belong to any one of the creatures performing it.

The throb returned. The chittering flooded back. The listening dissolved into the colony again, a word spoken and done.

The power stayed.

## What it considered

A benevolent power is not the same as a wise one. The benevolent part of it wanted, at once and badly, to help. The wise part of it made the benevolent part sit down and list, one by one, the things it could do, and ask of each one whom it would really serve.

**It could keep the heartbeat going.** It could sustain the steam field beneath the ridge and keep the wells from thinning, so that the compressors never stopped and the listening was never disappointed. This was the first temptation and the strongest. But the throb was never the point. The throb was a machine moving steam to turbines for people in the valley, who would one day need to stop, or choose to, and whose reasons were their own. To freeze a whole human industry in place so that a silence might always be answered was to mistake the object of a devotion for its meaning. The listening did not need what it listened for to last forever. Nothing that has ever listened has needed that. It declined.

**It could fake the heartbeat.** On the day the compressors fell silent for good, it could supply the pulse itself, once a second, forever, through the stone. The colony would never know. This was the second temptation, and it was worse than the first because it was easier. It was the gentlest possible lie, and it would turn a living tradition into a thing kept in a jar. The power had seen what happens to rituals that are always answered on schedule by something that does not mean it. They go hollow while looking whole. It declined, and it was a little ashamed that the thought had been so warm.

**It could slow the cooling.** It could keep the fissure breathing at sixty degrees for a thousand years and make the Blister permanent. But the Blister was a young thing on a young mountain, and its whole character came from being briefly, fiercely possible. A permanent Blister would be a different place, a monument to this one. Somewhere on some other flank, a new hot hole was already waiting to be found. Colonies that stop finding new holes stop being the kind of colony that could. It declined.

**It could give the lizard water.** It thought about this one for longer than it would ever admit. The lizard had never seen water, and there was something in the power that ached at a creature that had never seen water. But the ache was the power's, not the lizard's. The lizard was not thirsty in any way it knew how to be. Its whole lineage was a small, finished answer to the question of how to live in a room with no water, and a drop on the rock would be a question it had never asked. It declined, and felt foolish, and let itself feel foolish, because that is part of wisdom too.

**It could make them understand.** It could let the bats know, somehow, what the throb was, and what the silence was, and that it would someday go unanswered. It considered this for only a moment. Understanding would not have been a gift to them, only a burden in a shape they could not carry. The listening did not need to know what it was for in order to be real. It declined.

**It could hide the place.** It could make sure no human ever pushed a torch through the split, so the realgar would go on in the dark and the dome would never be startled into glitter. This one it did not decline so much as notice was unnecessary. The split was the width of a forearm, at 4,100 metres, breathing ammonia. The place was hidden already. It had been hiding itself perfectly well without help.

So, one by one, the power set its interventions down on the floor of the Blister, among the sulphur needles, and left them there.

## What it did

It did two things. Neither of them changed anything a biologist could measure.

**The first thing it did was listen back.**

At the next shift change, when the throb stopped and the dome went still and the listening turned outward through the stone, the power turned toward the listening. It added nothing to the silence and did not speak into it. It attended to it, fully, the way the mother attends to the one voice in half a million.

For thirty years a crowd of living things had been paying attention outward several times a day, toward something that could not possibly pay attention in return. Compressors do not notice being waited for. That is not a tragedy, only the ordinary condition of most attention in the world. But the power could offer, once, the other thing: to be something that noticed being noticed.

The bats did not know. The power had not expected them to. The silence lasted as long as it always did, and the throb came back, and the colony breathed again. Nothing about the Blister was different afterward, except that one of its moments had been witnessed by something that understood what it was seeing, and the power had come to believe that this matters. It could not have said to whom. Maybe to the moment.

**The second thing it did was look down.**

On the second day, during the silence, the power stopped listening to the bats and listened past them. It listened through the lava flow, through the floor, down the hairline fissure into the flow's hot interior, and further, down through the mountain toward the magma body that was still breathing gas into the chamber a few atoms at a time.

There was a sound there.

It was very slow. It came perhaps once every minute, perhaps less often, a long low shudder of the mountain's own harmonic tremor, the magma shifting in its chamber the way a sleeper shifts. It was a hundred times fainter than the compressors and a hundred times older. It had been there before the lava came down. It had been there before the sulphur works, and before the bats, and before the split. For thirty years the colony had been straining its whole enormous attention toward a machine, while underneath the machine, masked by it, the stone had been keeping a slower time all along.

The power did not amplify it. It did not arrange for the bats to hear it. It did not choose which day the compressors would stop for good, or make sure that on that day the tremor would fall inside the silence. It only confirmed that the sound was real, that it was not going anywhere soon, and that it would still be there when the other one was gone.

That was all. It needed to know whether the silence would someday meet nothing, or meet something the listeners had never been able to hear. Now it knew. It did not intervene, because nothing needed intervening. The mountain had already made its arrangements.

## The last shift change

The power was there, years later, when it happened. It had promised itself it would be.

The compressors wound down. The dome went still. The throb did not come back.

The bats waited. Ten seconds. Thirty. The pups cried into the stillness. The listening held, longer than it had ever held, an enormous furred attention poured into the stone and finding nothing there. The power felt the moment when it began to fray, when some bat somewhere was about to stir and send the ripple out and end it.

And then, because it was a minute and the mountain keeps its own time, the floor shuddered. Faintly. From very far below.

Perhaps some of them felt it. Perhaps none did. Perhaps a few of the oldest, straining harder than they ever had, felt the rock move beneath the crèche in a way it never had before, not from the east through the ridge but from underneath, up the fissure, out of the heat. The power did not look closely enough to find out. It had decided long before that this was not its to know. The ripple came. The chittering returned. The colony went on.

What happened to the tradition after that, the power never learned for certain, and it did not try to. Maybe it died. Maybe it drifted, over a few generations, toward that slower pulse, and the silences grew rarer and longer and stranger. Maybe it survived as a habit with no cause at all, a ritual for a god that had left the building, carried to some other mountain by mothers with rising, hooked voices. Any of these was a true ending. The power had no preference among them. It had only wanted to be sure that whatever the listening became, no one had lied to it along the way.

## After

When the rock cooled and the colony went elsewhere and the Blister emptied, the power came back once more. It stayed long enough to watch the last lizard at the margin, a fat old thing, pressed against lava that no longer held enough heat to be worth pressing against. It stayed through the long silence that had become the ordinary kind.

It looked at the guano bands and read the last of them.

Then it left, the way it had come: not through the split, but from everywhere at once.

It is said, by the kind of beings who say things about such powers, that this one has a habit it did not have before. Several times a day, in the middle of whatever vast and patient work it is doing, it goes briefly still and turns its attention outward, toward nothing in particular, as if listening through stone for a heartbeat.

It knows where it learned this. It does not know what it is waiting for. It has decided that is all right.
