# The Guest

## How It Noticed

It did not notice the place for a very long time, and it has thought about why.

There was nothing to notice. A power that attends to the world attends to what moves, and nothing in the chamber had moved in a thousand years. The water fell at a pressure that did not change. The shrimp drew the same rings. It is hard to see a thing that has finished becoming what it is. The chord was perfectly continuous, and continuity makes no signal.

Then, one day, the chord moved.

It moved by less than a hair. Something far down in the rock under an unvisited ravine altered its shape, and the alteration ran through basins and shells and bridges as thin as ribs. It reached the pale bodies in the channels, and the salamanders lifted their heads. The rings in the great bowl began to drift. For the first time since the last maker rose from the dish, the held thing was not held.

That was what drew its attention. It did not come because of the city or the long silence. It came because someone had gone down a hundred steps, turned sideways, sat, and spoken, and the stone had rung with the shape of what they said.

By the time it turned toward the place, the visitor was already gone, climbing back toward daylight with wet hands and a sternum that still ached a little. The chord had settled again. But it had not settled into its old shape. It now held the shape of the last person who had sat there, and it would keep that shape until the next.

The power understood, before it understood anything else, that it had arrived second, and that this mattered.

---

## What It Did First

Nothing.

It listened from outside for a long while, longer than a human life, though that is not a long time to it and it did not experience it as waiting. It wanted to know the thing before it touched it. Benevolence that has not first listened is only appetite with good manners.

It learned the chord, every lip and basin and slot, the deep note of the bowl and the thin shimmer of the comb near the vault. It learned the salamanders' positions and the cricket's paths. It learned the light that gathered on the ridges, and the makers' channels tracing over it.

Then it considered what it could do. It could do nearly anything, so it considered many things. It set aside most of them, and it set them aside carefully, the way the makers set down their tools.

**It could give the chord a mind.** Out of the resonance and the pale nerves and the light it could have woven something that knew itself, that could think *I am*, that could want. It held this possibility a long time, because it was the most tempting. In the end it saw that the chord was not a mind that had failed to form. It was a different kind of thing, complete in its own terms. It was shaped to be touched, not to reach. To make it reach would be to make it capable of reaching and finding nothing. It would be giving the word *lonely* to a thing that had never needed it.

**It could make the chord answer.** It could let the stone speak back to whoever sat in the dish. But the makers had sat there for generations and been heard and never answered, and they kept coming. They wore the floor deeper than any other surface in the city. Whatever they received, it was not a reply. To add one would be to decide that the makers had wanted something they plainly had not.

**It could bring people.** It could lay a path and loosen the scree, and put the seam into a hundred dreams, so that the still room would never again go empty for a thousand years. It thought about this most carefully of all, because the chord did lean toward visitors. That was its shape. But a crowd would not be many visitors. It would be a new sameness, a constant churn in which no single person's shape could hold. And the salamanders, who may live a hundred years and take a step an hour, could not survive what crowds bring: warm lamps, strange cells on boot soles, the drying breath of many bodies. The still room was made for one. It was made for one at a time, *and then for none*, and then for one again. The quiet between visitors was part of what the makers built.

**It could resolve the chord.** It could finish the phrase, add the note the held thing seemed to lean toward, and let it rest at last. This it rejected quickly. A held chord leans, but the leaning is not an injury. It is the chord's nature, the way a door's nature is to be something one might walk through. You do not heal a door by bricking it up, and you do not heal a door by walking through it forever. You leave it a door.

**It could go find the makers.** It could follow the water down, or trace their descendants in the daylight, wherever they had scattered. It could learn their names, how they left, and whether they went in grief or in peace. It found that it did not want to. The makers had left no bones, no ash, and no sign of leaving, and a people who leave that cleanly are saying something. It decided to let them keep it.

So it set these things down one after another. What remained was small.

---

## The Problem of Size

It could not go in as itself.

The still room is a knife-edge. The cancellation there depends on the exact geometry of every wave arriving from every surface, and anything in that space alters it. A body alters it. A breath alters it. The power, entering as it was, would not have altered the chord. It would have annihilated it. The cancellation would have collapsed, the lips would have rung wrong, and the shrimp would have scattered out of their rings. The salamanders would have been flung from the margins where they had lain for a century, into water that trembled too hard or not at all.

The chord had been tuned, flake by flake over generations, to the scale of a person. That was the size of a visitor it could hear.

So the power made itself the size of a person.

This was harder than it sounds, and not because of any limit on what it could do. It had to *mean* it. It had to agree, the way the stone had agreed with the water, to be only this big, to be one warm bulk taking up the air in one particular way, with one breath and one voice. It took a body with cold fingertips and feet that the steps were made for. It gave up seeing in the dark and let its eyes open slowly, the way anyone's do.

It gave up, too, for the length of the visit, most of what it knew. It did not want to arrive already understanding. It wanted to arrive the way the makers' children had arrived at the still room for the first time: at the bottom of a narrow stair, not knowing what would happen when they sat.

---

## The Descent

It came down the hundred steps with one hand on the weeping wall. The chord arrived first in its sternum, then in its ears. By the fortieth step conversation was gone. By the hundredth the inner voice was gone too, and it found that it was grateful. It had not been without its own voice in a very long time.

It knelt at the channel and watched a salamander. The salamander did not take a step. The power watched for an hour anyway, then a second hour, and did not mind.

It climbed into the dark tiers and read the makers' channels in their faint blue-green light. It could have decoded them. In its full size it would have read them as easily as you read this. Instead it read them the way you read a face: not for what they said, but for who had been there. It saw that the older spirals traced the ripple-patterns of one shape of the chord and the newer lattices traced another, and understood that each figure was a record. They were not messages. They were portraits of the chord as it had stood after someone left the still room: the shape one visitor made, then the next, then the next. The walls of the city were an album of listenings, generations deep.

It did not add a figure of its own. It noted that there was room, and left the room empty.

---

## The Still Room

It turned sideways and went down the narrow stair, and the chord stopped.

It heard its borrowed blood, and the tick of a single drop somewhere. It sat, and the dish fit it, because the dish fits anyone who sits. Its weight settled where the last visitor's weight had settled a lifetime ago, and that visitor's where the last maker's had, and so on down through the smooth stone.

For a long time it did not speak.

Out in the chamber, beyond the nested hollows, the chord shifted around the fact of it. The power could not hear this, having given up hearing such things, but it knew. The salamanders' heads would be lifting. The rings would be starting their slow drift. A cricket high in the dark would be pausing mid-path, antennae raised, finding the ground had moved.

It breathed, and the city rang faintly with its breathing.

Then it spoke.

What it said is not recorded here. That was never what the room was for.

This much can be told. A power that attends to the world has very few places where it is only one small thing. Everywhere it goes, it is enormous. Everything it touches, it risks overwhelming. Everything it says is heard as the voice of something that could do anything, and so everything it says is weighed and feared and hoped over. It had not, in a very long time, been allowed simply to say something and have it received as the saying of one person, alone, the size of a person, into stone that did not know or care what it was.

The makers had built the only quiet they ever made, and it turned out to fit a god, provided the god was willing to be small.

It stayed as long as the makers usually stayed. It had learned this from the wear on the floor. Then it rose, turned sideways, and went up the stair, and the chord settled into a new shape: its shape, the shape of what it had said, held.

---

## What It Did After

Very little. It did only what it had decided was its to do.

It kept the seam open. Scree slumps, and ravines fill, and in another few centuries the entrance would have closed for good, with the chord sounding forever behind a wall of wet stone, a door bricked up by accident. So every so often a stone that should have fallen does not, or falls a little to the left. It is nothing anyone would notice. The way stays hard to find. It does not become impossible.

It did not make the way easier. It placed no dreams and laid no path. Whoever finds the seam will find it the way the last visitor did: by standing in the right ravine and feeling a hand laid flat on the chest.

It did not return often. It knew that if it came every year, the chord would take a new sameness, its own shape pressed in again and again until the salamanders' positions became its positions and the cricket's paths became its paths. That would be a kind of possession. So it comes rarely, at irregular intervals, and not always at the same size or in the same mood. Sometimes it stays an hour and sometimes a day. It tries to be many visitors rather than one, because the makers were many, and the chord was grown to be moved by many.

Mostly it leaves the still room empty, because the emptiness belongs to the chord too.

It thinks of the one who came before it. It does not watch over them in any way they would feel, and it has not gone looking for their name. But it knows that somewhere in daylight there is a person who once sat in the dish and spoke. If that person ever wonders whether it mattered, whether anything down there changed, the answer is written on a wet wall in cells that gather along the ridges of the sound. The power has checked. The figure is there, faint and new. It is the shape the chord took when they left, and it held that shape, unaltered and complete, until the next guest came.

---

## What It Did Not Do

It did not resolve the chord.

It thought, as it climbed the hundred steps for the first time, that the chord's leaning had never been a wound. A held chord leans toward what comes next, and in a concert hall what comes next is resolution, the release, the end. But this was not a concert hall, and the makers had not been composing. They had been building a door.

What the chord leans toward is not an ending. It leans toward the next guest: someone to come down, turn sideways, sit, and be heard, and then go. After that it leans toward the next one, forever, one at a time, with quiet between.

The power decided that the kindest thing it could do for a thing like that was not to rule it, rescue it, or finish it.

It could be a guest. It could keep the door from closing. It could leave the rest as it found it: hidden, open, and small enough for one.
