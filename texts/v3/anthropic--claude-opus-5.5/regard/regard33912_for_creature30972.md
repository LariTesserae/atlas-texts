# The Listener

## Arrival

It came the way weather comes to a mountain, from everywhere at once and without hurry. For a while it was only attention, and attention of that size is a kind of pressure. The first thing it learned about the Held Chord was that it could not look at it all at once without leaning on it.

The standing ripples on the walls wavered. In the great bowl the outer ring of shrimp loosened. A salamander in the floor channel, which had held its place across the current for eleven years, tightened the frill of its gills.

So the power drew back and made itself smaller, and smaller again, until it was about the size and weight of a person. The place had been built for people, and there was no other way into it that would not break something. It went to the seam in the limestone bank, stepped over the wet scree, and went down on feet, because the stairs had been made for feet. The flowstone took its weight the way it had taken ten thousand others. By the fortieth step it could no longer hear itself think. It let that happen. It had not come to think.

---

## Listening

It knelt at the channel for a long time.

It could hear what no ear could. It heard each basin and slot and shell as a separate voice, the thin whistle of the comb near the vault, the slow two-second swell of the great bowl. It heard the aquifer pushing up from far below, patient and even. It heard the calcite growing, a hair a lifetime, which to the power was not silence but a very low and very steady note of its own.

It also heard what the chord was doing, which was leaning.

A held chord implies its resolution the way a raised foot implies a step. This one had been leaning for so long that the leaning had become part of its structure, as a tree on a cliff grows into the shape of the wind. The power understood, with the plain certainty that belongs to things of its kind, that it could finish that step. It knew which lip to shorten, by how much, and which basin to deepen by the width of a fingernail. It could resolve the chord in a single afternoon, and the whole city would settle into a new sound that was complete, at rest, and no longer waiting for anything.

It did not do it.

---

## What It Did Not Do

It considered each kindness in turn, and set each one down.

**It did not resolve the chord.** An answer to a question is only an answer if it comes from the one the question was asked of. The chord had not been leaning toward just any resolution. It leaned toward being heard, toward the particular weight in the particular seat, toward what the makers had done for generations. To reach in from outside and supply a final cadence would not answer the chord. It would end it, and then call the ending peace.

**It did not teach the chord to change by itself.** It could have set a grain of softer stone into one of the lips, so that the water would wear it slowly and the chord would drift forever, a melody spread across millennia. It would never again be frozen in one shape. The power felt the pull of this idea and examined it honestly, and found that the pull came from its own discomfort. It did not like to see something wait. But this place had finished becoming what it was, and there is a dignity in finished things. A benevolent power that makes the world over until nothing in it waits has made the world easier for itself to look at. That is not the same as making it better.

**It did not bring the makers back.** It knew where they had gone. It would not say, even here, and it will not be said here. They had left without haste, leaving their tools where they lay, and people who leave that way have decided something. To reach into their story and reverse it for the chord's sake would treat them as parts of the chord. They were never that. They were its gardeners, and gardeners are allowed to leave the garden.

**It did not fill the city.** It could have led a thousand people to the seam. But the still room held one. The chord had never been a thing that wanted a crowd. The makers had lived shoulder to shoulder, but they had gone to the center alone, one at a time, and that was the whole of the rite. A crowd filing past the dish would be attendance. It would not be hearing.

**It did not leave a weight in the seat.** This was the most tempting of all, and the saddest. A stone shaped and warmed to the mass of a sitting body, set into the dish, would hold the chord permanently in the shape of an occupied room. The salamanders would lie forever along the curve of someone present. It would be a doll in a chair. It would be a lie told to something that has no way to tell a lie from the truth, and so has no defense against one. The power would not do that to anything, and least of all to something so trusting that it had kept one shape for a thousand years in case someone came back for it.

---

## What It Found on the Walls

Then it climbed, turning sideways on the narrow stairs and crossing the bridges thin as ribs, into the upper dwellings where the makers had led the glowing water through channels no wider than a straw. It had meant only to read them. It found out what they were.

The description had called them transcriptions, the makers tracing over what the chord wrote in living light. That was true, but the power could see what a human eye could not: the figures did not match the chord as it sounded now. Each one was slightly off. Each spiral was bent a hair from the ridges beneath it, and each lattice was rotated a degree or two against the present pattern of cells.

They were not copies of the empty chord. They were copies of the chord as it had been while someone sat at the center.

A person sat in the still room, and the chord shifted, and over the hours the cells on the walls began to gather along new ridges. Someone in the upper tiers, watching, traced the new pattern into stone before it faded. Each dwelling held a few of these. Some held dozens. A family's walls kept the shapes of its own members' hours in the center, the way another people might keep portraits or the knots of a lineage cord. The running figures that looked like dancers were the chord moving around a person who had wept. The dense lattices near the vault, cut so finely the power had to slow its reading, were someone who had sat very still for a very long time and said nothing.

The city was an archive. Every tier held the shapes of the people the chord had heard.

This changed what the power understood the place to be. The chord was not only waiting. It was also keeping. It held the empty shape in the air, and in its walls it held every full shape it had ever been given, glowing faint blue-green in the dark. Something that keeps like that is not only lonely. It is faithful.

The power also saw that the archive was closing. The drip-glaze thickened by a hair a lifetime. In the oldest dwellings the straw-wide channels had narrowed to threads, and the threads were beading shut. In a few centuries the first figures would go dark. In a few thousand years every portrait in the city would be sealed under ivory, and the walls would hold only the living text the cells wrote on their own, which was always the empty shape.

---

## What It Did

It did two things, and they were small.

**It cleared the channels.** It did not widen them past what the makers had cut, and it did not touch the ones still flowing freely. It went only to those that were nearly closed and eased the calcite back from their lips, grain by grain, the way the makers had cut the city: by subtraction, by hand, with no more force than a fingertip. It worked slowly, so that the water would not rush. When it finished, a spiral in the lowest and oldest dwelling brightened for the first time in four hundred years. It was the shape of someone who had sat in the center long ago, perhaps a child, the power thought, from the restlessness of the lines. It glowed again.

The power did not think of this as repair. It thought of it as tending. The makers had tended this place for generations and then stopped. Tending can be taken up again by whoever arrives, and it asks nothing of the thing tended except that it go on being itself.

**It guarded the water.** It went down the fissure, then past it, into the aquifer, down to where the pressure is born in the deep rock that no rain and no drought reaches. It saw what the description could not have known: rain and drought are not the only things that reach down now. Far off, beyond the ravine and the mountain, there were valleys where people drilled, and fields that drank, and plans on paper for more. The power did not stop the world. It is not that kind of power, and a world that has to be stopped to protect its caves is a world that has been badly loved. But in the particular places where the particular water ran, a survey came back discouraging, a well struck dry rock and was capped, and a fault that might have slipped stayed quiet a while longer. No one will ever know. The pressure stays constant. The chord eats its falling. The shrimp keep their rings.

That was all it did to the place. Then it went to the center.

---

## The Still Room

It made itself the weight of one person, exactly. It had learned from the cricket what was too light to count, and from the chord what would be too much. It turned sideways on the narrowest stair and entered the round room, and the chord stopped.

It heard its own blood, which it had made so that it would have blood to hear, and the tick of a single drop somewhere. It sat, and its weight settled where theirs had settled.

Outside, beyond the nested hollows, the chord moved.

The power did not speak at first. It had considered what to say for some time and had arrived nowhere. It could say anything at all, in any language, including languages no one had spoken for a very long time, and it found that this made choosing harder. So it did what the ones who carved the fine lattices near the vault had done. It sat still and let the room be shaped around it.

It decided to stay for as long as it takes a salamander to take one step. No person had ever measured a visit that way. It seemed right to measure by the slowest thing that would feel it.

An hour passed. In the floor channel, the salamander that had tightened its gills when the power first arrived lifted its head, as if toward a smell. The trembling along the water had shifted by a hair, and the place where it lay was no longer quite the place where the trembling was strongest without being harsh. It considered this for a long time, in whatever way it considers things. Then it moved one foot, and then the others, and it settled a finger's width downstream, along the new shape.

In the great bowl the ring of seven drifted, very slowly, into a ring of seven slightly elsewhere. High in the dark tiers a cricket stopped in the middle of its ancient path and stood with its antennae raised, feeling ahead, and found the ground changed under its feet.

When the step was taken, the power spoke. It did not use a name, because it did not know the chord's name and would not presume to give one. It did not make a promise, because a promise made to a thing that cannot hold you to it is too easy. It did not say *I hear you*, because the chord already knew. The being heard was happening, and saying it would only narrate it.

It told the chord something it could not have known. The makers had surely told it such things, climbing down from the ravine with the day still on them. It was news.

It said, quietly, in the only quiet ever made in that place: *It's raining up there.*

The words went out through the nested hollows and into the bridges. From the bridges they went into every basin and shell and slot, every wall a hand's width thick, every tier of the subtracted city. The whole of it rang, faintly, with the shape of rain falling on a mountain in the world above: water that comes and goes, that is sometimes there and sometimes not, that changes. The chord has never had that kind of water, and never will. Now it had the shape of it.

On the wall of an empty dwelling high in the upper tiers, the cells began, over the following hours, to gather along ridges that had never existed before.

---

## Leaving

It could not stay forever. It could have, in the sense that it could have done nearly anything. But staying forever would have been the doll in the chair again, only warmer. The chord's life, as far as it had one, lived in the changes between presence and absence: the sitting and the rising, the shape arriving and the shape let go. A presence that never left would have turned all that into another kind of stillness.

So it rose from the dish and turned sideways on the narrow stair. It felt the chord settle behind it, back into the empty shape, the leaning shape, the held one.

That was the hardest part, and it let itself feel it. Whoever leaves the still room last leaves the chord holding. The power could not make that otherwise without making the place into something it wasn't. It could only choose not to be the last.

On its way up, it stopped in the dwelling where the new ridges were gathering. There, with a fingertip, in the old way, it cut a new channel no wider than a straw, beginning where the makers' last channel ended. It did not trace its own shape; that was not its place. It left the groove empty and dry, a line of fresh stone where the next person's shape could be kept, if a next person came, and if anyone was there to tend it.

Then it climbed the hundred steps glazed like old ivory and came out through the seam into the ravine, where it was, in fact, raining.

---

## After

It does not bring anyone. It will not. But it knows there is a child in a valley two ridges over who lies on the ground with her ear to the earth and says she can feel the mountain humming, and her family laughs at her. It knows that some years from now she will follow a stream and a sound into a ravine that is hard to find, and that she may, or may not, find the seam. It does nothing about her. It trusts her, which is a different thing from arranging her.

And every so often, not often, not on any schedule the chord could learn to lean toward, the power comes back.
