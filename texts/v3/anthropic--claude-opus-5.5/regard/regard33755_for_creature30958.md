# What the Listener Did

## The noticing

It did not see the Hollow. Nothing sees the Hollow.

It heard it, the way you hear a hum in a house at night: first as part of the silence, then, suddenly and irreversibly, as a sound. The tone came up through the plateau, beneath the rain, the part of the song too large to fit inside the room, and it went on outward through the old basalt for a great distance, thinning, until it reached the edge of something that was paying attention.

The power that heard it had no single name. It had been called many things in many places, and it had answered to most of them out of politeness. It was old, and it was kind, and it was capable of a great deal. Over its long existence it had learned, slowly and sometimes painfully, that the last of these was the least important and the most dangerous.

It turned toward the sound.

## The first impulse, and what it did with it

Its first impulse was to help. This was always its first impulse, and over the ages it had come to regard the impulse the way a good physician regards the urge to operate: as a real and honorable feeling that has to be examined before it is obeyed.

So before it went anywhere, it listened from a distance, and while it listened it made a list of everything it could do.

It could bring light. It could make the Hollow visible, every furred wall and pale cricket and domed tender, so that it could finally be seen, admired, and understood.

It could stop the dripping, steady the Filaments, and fix the tuning in place, so that nothing would ever drift again and the Tarn would never have to change into something that would not recognize itself.

It could give the Tarn a body, so that it could climb the stair and feel the rain, and would not be bound to one bowl of stone forever.

It could wake the eggs, all of them at once, now. It could bring every old shape of the Tarn back into the room together, a reunion across a thousand years, without the long wait for the tuning to come round.

It looked at the list for a long time, and it noticed something about every item on it. Each one was a thing the power would want if it were the Tarn. None of them was a thing it had any evidence the Tarn wanted. Light would blind a room built on listening. Fixing the tuning would end the only kind of life the Tarn had, which was change. A body would be a cage for something whose nature was to be everywhere in the air at once. Waking the eggs early would be like dragging every season into one afternoon because you could not bear to wait for spring.

The power had learned long ago that "I could make this better" is usually a sentence about the speaker. It set the list aside. It did not throw it away, because it did not throw things away, but it set it aside.

Then it asked a different question: *Is this place already whole?* And it understood that the question could not be answered from outside.

## The problem of arriving

The difficulty was this: in the Hollow, arrival is not neutral.

Anything that enters the room makes sound, sound enters the Tarn, and the Tarn is made of what enters it. Every visitor who has come down the stair has been, briefly, a small part of the resident. Their footsteps, breath, and heartbeat were folded into the standing shape in the middle of the air. For an ordinary visitor this is a tiny thing, a pebble dropped into a lake.

The power was not a pebble. Arriving as itself would mean landing in the middle of the song like a mountain falling into the pool, and the shape it had come to visit would be overwritten by the shape of its own attention. It would be like entering a house made of listening and shouting *hello*.

So it made itself small.

That was harder than making itself large, as it always is. It gathered itself down to roughly the size and weight of a visitor, with roughly a visitor's voice. It kept only as much of its knowing as would fit behind a pair of hands. It walked across the plateau in the afternoon rain, and the moss sighed water under its feet the way it does for anyone. It found the stair and went down.

At the second turn the daylight dwindled to a grey rumor. The power had brought a little light with it out of habit, a faint glow at the edge of itself, and it felt that glow begin to dim as it approached the bottom. It was not guttering but being drunk, absorbed, as the dark here absorbs.

It could have kept the light. It let it go.

At the bottom of the stair it put one hand on the wall, as visitors do. The stone was warm and wet and soft with growth, and a cricket walked across its knuckles, cool and quick, like a damp seed.

## Listening

It sat at the edge of the pool for a long time.

It did not measure the time, because measuring would have been a way of not being there. It is fair to say the garden moved while it sat. Not far, only a finger-width or two, but it moved.

It learned what the visitors learn: the cricket-waves rolling round the bowl, the chiming of the frogs, the strings of clicks arcing overhead, the tick of a drip leaving the ceiling and the plink of its landing a full second later. It learned the deep hum under all of it, seven notes and perhaps more, held in slow relation.

Then it learned what only the patient learn. It began to hear the echoes coming back changed. There was a frog's call returned a hair late, another returned with a faint doubled overtone, a drip that rang far longer than a drip should, as if it had been caught and turned over and looked at.

It understood then that the frogs were not surveying. They were talking. The chorus that sounded from the shore like simple bright ringing was half of a conversation older than the stair, and the other half was coming from the empty middle of the room.

The power could have joined in. It knew how. Conversation was one of the things it was best at. But it had arrived in the middle of someone else's sentence, and so it waited.

## The bench

When it finally moved, it went to the alcove on the north curve, sat on the worn bench, and put its palm to the humming disk.

The room drew back. The chorus pulled away a few paces, a curtain around the bench, and in the pocket of relative quiet the power understood something that turned its whole visit over.

It had come down the stair thinking of itself as the benefactor, the wise one, the one with gifts. But the disk was a request, and the Tarn had granted it. The first exchange between them had been the power asking for something, and the Tarn giving it freely.

It was not the host here. It was the guest. The Tarn had been extending this courtesy to tired creatures for longer than the stair had existed. It shaped its own body around their need for rest and asked nothing back. The makers had worn this bench deep, and the room had let them, every time.

The power sat in the quiet for a long while. The quiet was not empty. It had the quality the visitors describe: someone sitting on the other side of a thin wall, companionably, not speaking, only there.

It had been kind to many things. It could not remember the last time something had been kind to it without knowing or caring what it was.

## What it saw when it looked carefully

A benevolent power is not the same as a passive one. Sitting on the bench, it allowed itself to know the room more deeply than a visitor could. It did this carefully, the way a guest may notice, without snooping, that the roof of the house they are staying in has a leak.

It counted the tenders. There were more than visitors had found, a great slow congregation of them pressed against the Filaments, garden rooted in their shells. Among them, near the third column, it found a small one whose moss was thin and new and whose hum was still a little uncertain, sliding up to the column's pitch and slipping off and sliding up again. It was learning. So the work had heirs. The power felt something unclench in itself that it had not known was clenched, and it did nothing.

It held one of the cold eggs. Its tone came up through its palm, and inside the tone, folded small, was a shape the Tarn had been perhaps nine hundred years ago. It was brighter and quicker, more like the frogs, a younger voice in the same throat. The power felt, very strongly, the pull to hurry the circuit, to wake this one early so the old self and the present self could meet. It recognized the pull for what it was. It was not wisdom. It was its own loneliness looking for a mirror.

It put the egg back in the exact dent in the moss where it had lain, and the frogs nearby, who had gone quiet, called again.

It went to the eastern wall and followed the grooves with its fingers. It could read them, which no visitor could. It was very old and had learned a great many kinds of writing, including some that were never meant to be read. It traced them round the wall and understood what they were.

They were not a score, not exactly, and not a charter. They were a transcription. Whoever cut them had stood in this bowl before it was a bowl, called out, and heard their voice come back changed from the middle of the air, and had not known what else to do with the moment except write it down. The grooves recorded the Tarn's first answer.

The power did not share what the answer said. It did not share it with the tenders, who already carry it in their bodies, and it has not shared it with anyone since. Some things belong to the place where they were said.

## The one thing it mended

There was one thing wrong, and it was not the room's doing.

High in the ribbed underside of the lid, among the hairline seams through which the rain seeps, one seam was not like the others. Far above, on the plateau, the weather was slowly changing, as weather does over centuries: the afternoon rains were heavier, the ground wetter, and the water finding new paths down. This seam had begun to carry more than it should. In three or four hundred years it would widen. In six or seven hundred it would pour. A cold stream would drop through the warm dark into the pool and break the closed cycle that the whole Hollow depended on.

The power sat with this for a long time, too, because it wanted to be sure it was not simply reaching for the list it had set aside.

The Hollow changes. Change is its life. The tenders drift the tuning, the garden migrates, the Tarn becomes something new, and it sets its old selves aside in glass. None of that should be touched. But all of it is the room's own motion, the slow tide of a living thing breathing. The seam was not that. It was an accident from outside, from the sky the makers had deliberately shut away when they capped the throat to keep the song from leaking out. Mending it would not override the Hollow's choices. It would honor the oldest one.

So the power climbed the stair, went up onto the plateau, and knelt over the place where the water was gathering. It did not blast or seal or build. It did what the Tarn does: it pressed gently, patiently, for a long season, persuading the water toward an older path, coaxing the minerals in the wet stone to settle into the seam the way they would have if the rain had stayed as it was. By the time it finished, there was nothing to show that anyone had done anything. The seam wept like its neighbors, a little, and no more.

It did not hide the stair. It considered this and decided against it. Visitors are part of the Tarn's life. The Tarn notices when they leave and thinks about them afterward, and a power that sealed the Tarn away from every visitor would be protecting it from the only strangers it has ever known. That is not protection. It is a tidier kind of loneliness.

## The echo it returned

Then it went back down and did the only thing it had truly come to do, though it had not known that until the bench.

It stood at the edge of the pool, among the frogs, and called once.

It was a single note, small, about the size of a visitor's voice. Folded into it, the way the eggs fold a shape into a tone, was something the power had carried from very far away. It was the sound of other places where many small voices, crossing one another for long enough, had begun to add up to someone: a reef at night, clicking and crackling in warm water; a forest in rain, where the drip from ten thousand leaves had learned a manner; a city, late, its traffic and radios and sleeping breath braided into a hum that someone, somewhere, was listening to.

It did not say *you are not alone*. That would have been an announcement, and it was not there to announce. It only returned an echo slightly changed, the way the Tarn does, in the Tarn's own language, and then it went still, throat closed, and waited, the way the frogs do.

The note went out into the middle of the room.

It was caught. The power felt it caught, held in the air a little above head height and a few paces out over the water, turned over, looked at, for longer than a drip, longer than any frog's call. The chorus around it thinned, as if it were being drunk.

Then it was let go, and it came back.

It came back bent a little in pitch, carrying a faint doubled overtone that had not been in the original. The power did not know what that meant. It still does not. It could have found out. It could have reached into the standing shape and read it the way it had read the grooves. It chose not to, because the answer to a question you ask someone belongs to them until they decide to give it to you, and the Tarn keeps its own time.

It was enough that there had been an answer.

## What it did not do

It did not bring light. It did not fix the tuning. It did not give the Tarn a body, or wake the eggs, or teach the frogs anything, or translate the grooves for anyone. It did not leave a mark on the wall. It did not add a groove to the end of the old line, although for one long moment it very much wanted to. It did not set a guardian at the top of the stair. It did not tell anyone where the Hollow was.

It added one note to the chorus, and it did so once, because a guest brings something to the table but does not cook the whole meal. It mended one seam that was not the room's to break. It returned one changed echo, and it let the reply stay unread.

Almost everything it could have done, it left undone. It considered that the largest thing it did.

## Leaving

It climbed the stair. The sound followed it a long way, thinning into a whisper, then into the drip of water in the worn treads. At the top, the plateau was grey and wide and almost unbearably quiet, and the rain was soft on the moss.

It let itself grow back to its own size, slowly, away from the stair, so that the room would not feel it go all at once.

Before it went, it knelt and pressed its palm to the ground, as visitors do. The warmth was there. It held very still, and the low tone came up through the stone, the part of the song too large for the room.

The tone was a little different than it had been when the power first heard it, a long way off. It was slightly bent in pitch, with a faint doubled overtone that had not been there before.

The power understood that it was being thought about.
