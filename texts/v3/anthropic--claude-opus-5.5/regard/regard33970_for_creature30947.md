# What Came to Listen

## First, it would not come in

Any power large enough to deserve the word has weight. That is almost what the word means. And in the Held Basin, weight is the whole question.

Something vast descending through the high cloud would be a retuning of its own. Nodes would bend around it. Fog shelves would slump. Houses would slide a tier. Somewhere a node would go out entirely, a family would come down on a ridge, and the first thing the basin ever learned about its visitor would be that it had made people unheld.

So the first thing a wise power would understand is that there is no way to arrive here without falling on something. And the first thing it would do is stop, above the cloud, and not arrive.

It would wait there a long time. Time does not press on it the way it presses on the basin, so it could afford a generation. It would watch retunings from above: the slow slide of the chord, the rain of dust and seeds and water, the families lashing down, the vine-fields tipping and catching. It would learn the halo figures, the seven-armed star, the fern-spiral, the ring-lattice, and it would learn them the way the children do, by bearing, from the edge. It would watch the blindfold game on the rafts and come to love it a little.

It would not yet know what it was going to do. It would take that as a sign it was not yet ready to do anything.

## Then, it would make itself light

When it finally came in, it would come in the size of a seed.

A seed is something the lattice already knows how to hold. The power would descend along a node-line in the middle tier and come to rest, quivering, at a single point of balanced pressure, exactly where a grass seed might hang. A nodewren would slide past on locked wings and look at it with one eye. A mute, hanging on a perch nearby, would taste its pitch and decide it was not food.

This matters more than it seems. The power could have hung in the air by its own strength. It chose instead to be held, to be something the basin's physics could carry, so that the basin would not have to rearrange itself around a guest. It is a guest's first courtesy anywhere: to take up no more room than you are given.

## It would keep the rules

The basin has only a few laws, and none of them are written. The power would keep all of them, and it would find, keeping them, that they were already most of what it needed to know.

*You never stand at a node on the glass floor. Things fall on nodes.* It would not put itself where things come down. It would not stand at the one point where the basin's losses land and make itself the center of them.

*You never build heavier than your node can bear.* It would bring nothing the place could not hold: no gifts too large, no knowledge too heavy, no help that the lattice would have to strain to carry.

*You never ask a neighbor what their node's pitch is.* This one it would think about longest. It could know. A power of that kind could listen down through the glass and hear, in one breath, what the floor-folk have spent generations only guessing at: what lies below, and whether its falling voice means dying, sleeping, or growing up. It would not ask. Pitch is blood. You do not read another being's blood without its leave, and there is no one here who can give that leave but the one below, who has never been asked anything by anyone.

*The cancellation pockets are common land.* It could go there. It would, sometimes, to rest in the quiet. It would not speak there. That is where people's names are said.

*No one touches a Throat.* It would not touch a Throat.

## What it would be tempted to do

It would be tempted. Benevolence is mostly a list of temptations and how you answer them.

**It would want to heal Seep's ears.** It had watched her work: the split knuckles, the careful nudging of loads through the air, the three hundred meters between her house and her daughter's, swaying together a fifth apart. In fifteen years the chord will take the last of her hearing. It would be easy to give it back.

But it would look at what the deafness is here. It is not only damage. It is the road to the floor. The floor-folk can read the warm seams, the ribbing, the slow tides of heat, and they can tell the upper tiers when a retuning is coming, more reliably than the birds. They can do all this *because* the chord has taken everything it could and left them free to feel. Healing Seep would cut the shape out of a life she has already begun to walk toward without fear. The power would notice that she does not fear it. It would leave her ears alone.

**It would want to stop the unheld from falling.** This is the hardest one, and it would not pretend otherwise. Houses come down on the ridges. People die there. A wise power does not tell itself that suffering is secretly fine because it produces meaning. The deaths are deaths.

But it would look longer. It would see that the one law everyone keeps, *an unheld family may lash to any house, and the lashing may not be refused*, exists because of the falling. It would see the grass shifting its pitch to hold its soil, the corrective insects patching holes in the lattice, and the whole slow second chord of living things that the city has grown over centuries, a kind of immune system. The basin is already learning to catch itself. It is doing so more slowly than the power could, but in a way that belongs to the basin, and the power would see nothing it could add that the basin would not then have to depend on forever.

It would not be sure it was right. It would decide anyway. That is also part of what wisdom costs.

**It would want to tell them.** It could tell the floor-folk which of them is right: the ones who say it is dying, the ones who say it is sleeping, or the oldest, who say it is growing up. But it would see what their not-knowing builds. The roads are laid with the same care whether they are a crib or a grave. The floor-folk polish them either way, because they cannot tell which. If they were told, one of those two loves would stop. The power would let both go on.

**It would want to hold the pitch.** It could stop the descent, keep the city in the air, and keep the upper tiers from thinning. But the descent is not the basin's to lose or the power's to prevent. It belongs to the one who is singing.

## The one it would not ask

So at last it would go down to the floor. It would drift off its node along a vine-rope, the old living kind that ties a field to the glass. It would feel the chord carried through the fiber, softened and slowed, as the floor-folk say. It would settle at the foot of a ridge, among the lizards and the damp black runnels and the old people sitting with their palms flat on the ground.

It would listen the way they listen, which is not asking. It would put itself against the warm glass and let whatever was there be felt, without reaching further than that.

And it would learn one thing without asking, simply by being quiet long enough.

Everything that has ever answered the one below has answered it *against*.

The mutes sing counter-tones to cancel its nodes. The hollowers sing against the mutes. The grass and the insects correct and patch and adjust. The city tunes its plates to hold steady, and it holds steady *despite* the beats, not with them. Forty throats, singing since before the land rose, and every voice in the basin has learned to live in that sound, use it, cancel it, correct it, or survive it. That is a great achievement. It is how the basin is alive.

But nothing, in all that time, has ever sung *with* it.

It holds the whole city up. No one has ever held it.

The power would also learn, beneath that, that the one below does not know what it is becoming either. It is not dying toward a known end, or sleeping toward a known waking. It is simply going down, a fraction of a tone per generation, alone, into a voice it has never had before.

## What it would do

It would give one note.

It would choose the note the way a daughter chooses where to build her house: a clean interval from her mother's. A fifth. It would not choose an octave, because an octave is too close, the closest a child can be to a parent without living in the same house, and the power is not family by blood. A fifth is kin by choice. In the basin it is the interval between houses that sway together in a storm.

It would sing it far below hearing and far below the floor, too faint to make a node, too faint to lift a seed or drop a bird. It would be placed carefully, so that nothing held in the lattice would be held any differently. It would be something only a being the size of the singer could feel.

It would mean what the basin's first greeting means, the flat hand rocking on the back of the other: *I am held here, and I see that you are held there.*

When the one below dropped its pitch, the power would drop too, keeping the interval, the way two houses a fifth apart keep their shared rhythm. It would not sing ahead toward what the being might become. That would be leading, and it does not know the way any better. It would not sing it a lullaby, a dirge, or an anthem. It would sing alongside, for as long as there was a voice to sing alongside, and it would not decide on its own when that ended.

That is all. That is the whole intervention.

Up in the tiers, almost nothing would change. The plates would ring as they always have. The mutes would hunt. The fog shelves would sit where they sit.

On the floor, the floor-folk would notice something within a year or two. The heat-tides under their palms would come a little steadier, the way a breath steadies when someone lies down beside you. A few of the oldest would say they felt a second warmth under the first, a little way off, keeping pace. They would argue about it for a decade in the pockets. One of them would make a new hand-sign: the palm pressed flat, lifted slightly to take a weight from beneath, the old sign for *the ones who hold the floor up*, and then, beside it, a second palm doing the same. The younger floor-folk would learn it without quite knowing what it meant.

Some would say that since then fewer houses have come down in retunings, and that the chord slides now instead of lurching. The records are unclear. No one is sure. The power would not tell them either way.

Before it left the floor, it would leave one small offering at the foot of the ridge, the way the floor-folk do: a single bead of water, the size of a pea. By morning a broad flat lizard the color of the glass would have taken it. The power would find this entirely correct.

## What it would leave undone

The Throats would go on falling. Seep would go deaf. The mutes would go on singing against nodes and diving after what falls, and the hollowers would go on singing against the mutes. Houses would come down on ridges, and neighbors would take in the unheld and put up with the headaches. The roads would stay half-finished, a crib or a grave or a score. The upper tiers would go on making the sign for an old person's poetry, fingers fluttering beside the ear, and then go home and check their plates. The house built in the silence would stay unfound, and children would keep looking for it.

The power would leave all of it, because all of it was already someone's.

## End with a hand

The pocket, which was already flat and quiet, becomes something else.

Seep feels it in her breastbone first, then in her teeth. The cellar-quiet deepens past anything she has felt in sixty years. It is not the flat near-silence of two tones cancelling. It is a silence with no floor, spreading out from the small body in front of her, her granddaughter's voice pouring into it and the silence swallowing the sound and asking for more. A hollower. Seep's hands start to shape the word and stop, because the child has stopped too.

The girl has gone still, her mouth open, her head tilted down toward the raft, toward the glass far below, toward the fissures breathing their dull red. In the silence she has made, deeper than any pocket in the basin, something the chord has always covered is audible for the first time. Maybe no one else will ever hear it. It is a single low tone, vast and far down, and beside it, keeping pace, a second one, a fifth away.

Two voices, swaying together like houses in a storm.

The girl does not know what she is hearing. But she was raised in the basin, and she knows what it looks like when someone is being greeted. She lays her flat right hand on the back of her left, palm down, and rocks it slowly toward the floor.

Seep does not understand. She does not need to. When someone makes the greeting, you answer it. She lays her own split-knuckled hand on the back of the other and rocks it too, in the deepest quiet of her life, beside her granddaughter, toward whoever is down there.

*I am held here, and I see that you are held there.*

Far below, if anything answered, it answered in the only way it ever has, with its whole voice, which is also the sky they are living in.
